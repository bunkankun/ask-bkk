# KR6l Division Summary: Abhidharma Treatises

## Overview

Division KR6l of the Kanripo corpus comprises 45 catalog entries spanning the core of the Chinese Abhidharma (*Āpídámó* 阿毘達磨) literature, from the canonical "six feet" texts of the Sarvāstivāda school through the *Abhidharmakośa* and its extensive Tang commentary tradition. It is one of the most technically demanding and philologically significant divisions in the entire KR6 Buddhist corpus.

## Structure and Contents

The *Mahāvibhāṣā* (KR6l0010, T27n1545) is the centerpiece of Vaibhāṣika scholasticism, recording Kaśmīri interpretations of the *Jñāna-prasthāna*. The *Śāriputrābhidharma* (KR6l0013) is uniquely important as the only complete non-Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma text surviving in Chinese.

The *Hṛdaya* trilogy (KR6l0015–0017) represents the lineage of condensed Abhidharma manuals that parallels the *Kośa* tradition and was foundational for Central Asian and Chinese Abhidharma study.

The *Abhidharmakośa* (KR6l0023) is the single most important text in the division and one of the most influential works in the entire Buddhist canon. Vasubandhu's synthetic mastery of Sarvāstivāda doctrine, combined with his incisive Sautrāntika critique, made the *Kośa* the standard Abhidharma textbook throughout East Asia.

Saṃghabhadra's *Nyāyānusāra* is the fullest surviving defense of orthodox Vaibhāṣika doctrine against the *Kośa* critique and remains among the least-studied major texts in the Taisho.

The "three commentaries" (*sān shū* 三疏) — 普光's *Jì*, 法寶's *Shū*, and 圓暉's *Sòng Shū* — represent the mature Tang scholarly response to Xuanzang's translation and were all transmitted to Japan as the foundation of the Nara-period *Kusha-shū* 倶舍宗 school.

## Key Scholarly Findings

1. **Translation date data**: Colophons and prefaces confirm specific dates for several translations: KR6l0002 (T1537, colophon: 顯慶四年九月十四日 = Sept 14, 659), KR6l0008 (T1543, 前秦建元十九年 = 383 CE), KR6l0010 (T1545, 顯慶元年 = 656 – 龍朔三年 = 663).

2. **Dual Vasubandhu problem**: Person notes document the ongoing debate about whether the author of the *Kośa* and the Yogācāra works are the same person or two distinct *Vasubandhus*.

3. **Sthiramati's lost translator**: The *Jùshě lùn shíyì shū* (KR6l0030, T1561) identifies its author as 悉地羅末底 (Sthiramati, 安慧) but carries no translator attribution — an anomaly in the well-documented Taisho corpus.

4. **Jiǎ Céng's preface**: The preface to 圓暉's *Sòng Shū* (KR6l0036) by the official 賈曾 is a rare non-monastic witness to the Tang Kōsha commentary tradition and explicitly documents the transmission chain from Xuanzang through 普光 to 圓暉.

5. **Japanese Kōsha tradition**: Six texts (KR6l0024–0027, 0043–0044) are Japanese Kōsha studies with no source files in the KRP tree, reflecting the division's dual Chinese/Japanese scope.
