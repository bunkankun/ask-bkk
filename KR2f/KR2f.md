# KR2f — Zhàolìng zòuyì lèi 詔令奏議類

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR2f is the *Zhàolìng zòuyì lèi* 詔令奏議類 (Imperial Edicts and Memorials), one of the fifteen subdivisions of the *Shǐbù* 史部 in the *Sìkù quánshū* taxonomy. Within the Sìkù scheme the category is itself bisected into two *zhī shǔ* 之屬: *zhàolìng* 詔令 (top-down imperial pronouncements — edicts, proclamations, rescripts, *shèngxùn*, *shàngyù*) and *zòuyì* 奏議 (bottom-up officials' memorials — *zòu* 奏, *yì* 議, *shū* 疏, *biǎo* 表, *zházǐ* 札子). [[KR2f0001]]–0011 cover the *zhàolìng* side, [[KR2f0012]]–0041 the *zòuyì* side. The division thus collects the two halves of the Chinese chancery-documentary tradition: what the throne sent down, and what its officials sent up.

The category as Sìkù defines it is younger than its contents. Bibliographically, *zhàolìng* anthologies were generally classed under *biéjí* or *zǒngjí* in the Tang–Song catalogs, and individual officials' *zòuyì* under *biéjí*; only with the *Sìkù* did the documentary-administrative function of these texts win them an autonomous subdivision under the histories. The chronological span of the works collected runs from the Hàn (whose edicts are extracted retrospectively in [[KR2f0011]] *Liǎng Hàn zhàolìng*) through 1786 (Qiánlóng's [[KR2f0041]] *Yùxuǎn Míngchén zòuyì*); the date of compilation, however, is concentrated in three peaks — Northern Sòng (the Sòng officials' own *zòuyì*), late-Sòng to early Míng (the great classified anthologies of [[KR2f0038]] and [[KR2f0039]]), and Kāngxī-to-Qiánlóng (the Qīng imperial *shèngxùn* / *shàngyù* / *zhūpī* program). Wilkinson 2018 treats the genre principally in §47.5 (Hàn documents and chancery typology), §61.3.7 (Táng *zhàolìng*), §62.3 (Sòng *zòuyì* and *míngchén* anthologies), and §65.3.7 (Míng–Qīng edicts and memorials); the per-text notes in this directory anchor their references to those sections.

The principal transmissional fact about KR2f is that it preserves what the *shílù* 實錄 and *qǐjūzhù* 起居注 have not. The Táng veritable records are entirely lost; the Sòng *shílù* (except for the fragmentary [[KR2f0003]]) are lost; the Yuán *shílù* is lost. For each of those periods, the surviving body of imperial-edict text and ministerial-memorial text — i.e. the actual documentary record of court business — has descended chiefly through the anthologies collected here.

## Important texts and text clusters

### Imperial edicts (*zhàolìng*) — pre-Qīng anthologies

- *Táng dà zhàolìng jí* 唐大詔令集 [[KR2f0010]] — Sòng Mǐnqiú's 1070 collection (original 130 *juàn*, surviving 107) is the principal extant body of Táng-period imperial pronouncements, organized in 30 functional categories. Indispensable for Táng institutional history.
- *Liǎng Hàn zhàolìng* 兩漢詔令 [[KR2f0011]] — Lín Fú's *Xī Hàn zhàolìng* (preface 1109) and Lóu Fǎng's *Dōng Hàn zhàolìng* (preface 1222) joined as a single Sòng anthology, with Hóng Zīkuí's *zǒnglùn* essay on the typology of imperial documents.
- *Tàizōng huángdì shílù cán* 太宗皇帝實錄殘 [[KR2f0003]] — anomalously placed here: the only surviving fragment of any Northern Sòng *shílù*, edited by Qián Ruòshuǐ for *Tàizōng*'s reign.

### Imperial pronouncements (*zhàolìng*) — Qīng imperial program

A tightly coordinated Kāngxī-to-Qiánlóng compilational program treating the dynasty's own ancestors as canonical authors:
- *Tàizǔ Gāohuángdì shèngxùn* 太祖高皇帝聖訓 [[KR2f0001]] (Nurhaci, ed. 1686);
- *Tàizōng Wénhuángdì shèngxùn* 太宗文皇帝聖訓 [[KR2f0002]] (Hóng Tàijí, ed. 1687);
- *Shìzǔ Zhānghuángdì shèngxùn* 世祖章皇帝聖訓 [[KR2f0004]] (Shùnzhì, ed. 1687);
- *Shèngzǔ Rénhuángdì shèngxùn* 聖祖仁皇帝shèngxùn [[KR2f0005]] (Kāngxī, ed. 1731);
- *Shìzōng Xiànhuángdì shèngxùn* 世宗憲皇帝聖訓 [[KR2f0006]] (Yōngzhèng, ed. 1741).

These five — and the contemporary *shàngyù* and *zhūpī* anthologies that flank them — function as the dynasty's self-canonization. The two *shàngyù* compilations, *Shìzōng Xiànhuángdì shàngyù bāqí* 世宗憲皇帝上諭八旗 [[KR2f0007]] and *Shìzōng Xiànhuángdì shàngyù nèigé* 世宗憲皇帝上諭內閣 [[KR2f0008]] (159 *juàn*), preserve the day-to-day Yōngzhèng pronouncements to the Eight Banners and to the Grand Secretariat. The crown jewel of the cluster is *Shìzōng Xiànhuángdì zhūpī yùzhǐ* 世宗憲皇帝硃批諭旨 [[KR2f0009]] — 360 *juàn* of Yōngzhèng's personal vermilion rescripts to provincial secret-memorial submitters, the largest single-emperor documentary monument in the Sìkù, and a foundational source for eighteenth-century Chinese administrative, fiscal, and frontier history.

### Memorials (*zòuyì*) — individual Sòng officials

The Northern-Sòng generation of *míngchén* whose memorials founded the genre as an independent literary form: *Fàn Wénzhèng zòuyì* 范文正奏議 [[KR2f0012]] (Fàn Zhòngyān, the *Qìnglì* reform memorials), *Bāo Xiàosù zòuyì jí* 包孝肅奏議集 [[KR2f0013]] (Bāo Zhěng), *Jìnyán jí* 盡言集 [[KR2f0014]] (Liú Ānshì), *Dǎnglùn jí* 讜論集 [[KR2f0015]] (Chén Cìshēng). The Southern Sòng is represented only by *Zuǒshǐ jiàncǎo* 左史諫草 [[KR2f0016]] (Lǚ Wǔ). These are the principal documentary witnesses to the policy debates of the *Qìnglì*, *Xīníng*, and *Yuányòu* eras.

### Memorials (*zòuyì*) — individual Míng officials

The single largest cluster in KR2f: seventeen *biéjí*-style anthologies of individual Míng officials' memorials, from the *Tiānshùn*/*Chénghuà* generation (Shāng Lù, Wáng Shù, Mǎ Wénshēng) through the *Wànlì* and late-Míng (Pān Jìxùn, Lù Zhōnglì, Zhōu Qǐyuán). The cluster reads as a documentary commentary on the principal political debates of the Míng — the *Dà lǐ yì* 大禮議 (Yáng Tínghé [[KR2f0022]], Hé Mèngchūn [[KR2f0023]], Hú Shìníng [[KR2f0021]], Zhāng Yuán [[KR2f0026]], Xià Yán [[KR2f0027]]), the Hé Lǎotài 賀老菜 / *Dōnglín* 東林 opposition (Lù Zhōnglì [[KR2f0032]], Zhōu Qǐyuán [[KR2f0033]]), Yellow-River management (Pān Jìxùn [[KR2f0030]], [[KR2f0031]]), and military policy on the northern frontier and against Wokou pirates (Tán Lún [[KR2f0029]]). Of these, the two Pān Jìxùn memorial collections ([[KR2f0030]], [[KR2f0031]]) are the indispensable primary source for late-sixteenth-century hydraulic engineering.

### Memorials (*zòuyì*) — individual Qīng officials

Three early-Qīng *biéjí*-style memorial collections selected by the Sìkù as exemplary: *Zhāng Xiāngzhuàng zòushū* 張襄壯奏疏 [[KR2f0034]] (Zhāng Yǒng, frontier-military), *Jìn Wénxiāng zòushū* 靳文襄奏疏 [[KR2f0035]] (Jìn Fǔ, Yellow-River management — Pān Jìxùn's institutional heir), *Huáyě shūgǎo* 華野疏稿 [[KR2f0036]] (Guō Xiù, Kāngxī-era reform).

### Memorials (*zòuyì*) — the great classified anthologies

The four monumental thematic collections that define the *zòuyì* anthology as a Chinese bibliographic genre:
- *Wǔcháo míngchén yánxíng lù* 五朝名臣言行錄 [[KR2f0037]] — Zhū Xī's 1172–1188 biographical-anecdotal anthology of Northern Sòng moral exemplars, the foundation of the *míngchén* canon and one of the principal Sòng *dàoxué* textual operations.
- *Sòng míngchén zòuyì* 宋名臣奏議 [[KR2f0038]] — Zhào Rǔyú's 1186 collection of 1,630 Northern-Sòng memorials by 241 authors, organized in 12 thematic divisions and 114 sub-categories. The principal classified anthology of Northern-Sòng administrative documentation, and the site of the famous Zhū Xī / Zhào Rǔyú by-person-versus-by-theme editorial debate.
- *Lìdài míngchén zòuyì* 歷代名臣奏議 [[KR2f0039]] — Yáng Shìqí and Huáng Huái's 350-*juàn*, ~8,000-memorial Yǒng-lè-era anthology (1416), the largest single memorial-anthology in pre-modern Chinese bibliography. Indispensable for Sòng and especially Yuán *zòuyì* (the Yuán *shílù* being lost in toto).
- *Yùxuǎn Míngchén zòuyì* 御選明臣奏議 [[KR2f0041]] — Qiánlóng's 1781–1786 imperial selection of Míng memorials, organized by author. The Sìkù's own canonical synthesis of Míng *míngchén* memorial-literature.

To which the *Míngchén jīngjì lù* 名臣經濟錄 [[KR2f0040]] (Huáng Xùn, *c.* 1540s) — a Míng-period thematic compilation of *jīngjì* (statecraft) memorials — is a fifth, narrower-scope member of the same family.

## Important persons

- [[宋敏求]] — Sòng Mǐnqiú (1019–1079). Compiler of [[KR2f0010]] *Táng dà zhàolìng jí*; the principal mediator through whom Táng chancery prose has descended to us.
- [[林虙]] and [[樓昉]] — joint compilers, separated by a century, of [[KR2f0011]] *Liǎng Hàn zhàolìng* (Lín 1109; Lóu 1222); through them the Hàn edict-corpus survives reign-organized.
- [[朱熹]] — Zhū Xī (1130–1200). Compiler of [[KR2f0037]] *Wǔcháo míngchén yánxíng lù*; the architect of the *míngchén* canon as a *dàoxué* moral-cultivation resource.
- [[趙汝愚]] — Zhào Rǔyú (1140–1196). Compiler of [[KR2f0038]] *Sòng míngchén zòuyì*; the principal pioneer of by-theme classified-anthology method for memorial-text, in explicit methodological opposition to Zhū Xī's by-person approach.
- [[范仲淹]] — Fàn Zhòngyān (989–1052). Author of [[KR2f0012]]; founding figure of the Sòng *jīngshì* (statecraft) memorial tradition.
- [[包拯]] — Bāo Zhěng (999–1062). Author of the memorials in [[KR2f0013]]; cultural archetype of the upright remonstrating official.
- [[劉安世]] — Liú Ānshì (1048–1125). Author of [[KR2f0014]] *Jìnyán jí*; the *Yuányòu* censor whose famous omission from Zhū Xī's *Yánxíng lù* is treated in both [[KR2f0014]]'s and [[KR2f0037]]'s *tíyào*.
- [[楊士奇]] and [[黃淮]] — Yáng Shìqí (1365–1444) and Huáng Huái (1367–1449). Joint editors-in-chief of [[KR2f0039]] *Lìdài míngchén zòuyì*; Yǒnglè cabinet ministers and the senior literary administrators of the early Míng.
- [[商輅]], [[王恕]], [[馬文升]], [[楊一清]], [[胡世寧]], [[楊廷和]], [[何孟春]], [[夏言]], [[譚綸]], [[潘季馴]] — the principal Míng *zòuyì* authors collected here, each representing a major mid-Míng policy domain (palace administration, Censorate reform, fiscal-military integration, the *Dà lǐ yì* controversy, frontier defence, hydraulics).
- [[聖祖]] (Kāngxī, 1654–1722), [[世宗]] / [[世宗胤禛]] (Yōngzhèng, 1678–1735), [[高宗]] / [[高宗弘曆]] (Qiánlóng, 1711–1799) — the three emperors who as commissioning editors define the Qīng share of KR2f. Yōngzhèng additionally appears as commissioning *author* of the *Zhūpī yùzhǐ* [[KR2f0009]].
- [[允祿]] — Yǔnlù (1695–1767). Compiler of the two great Yōngzhèng *shàngyù* anthologies [[KR2f0007]] and [[KR2f0008]]; the principal early-Qīng compiler-prince.
- [[靳輔]] — Jìn Fǔ (1633–1692). Author of [[KR2f0035]]; Pān Jìxùn's institutional successor in Yellow-River administration.

## Topics

- **Genre typology of imperial documents.** The classical typology — *cèshū* 策書 (mandate documents for the highest ranks), *zhìshū* 制書 (administrative orders), *zhàoshū* 詔書 (general edicts), *jièchì* 誡敕 (admonishing instructions), *cèshū* 冊書 (investiture documents), with later additions of *shàngyù* 上諭 (Qīng imperial pronouncements) and *zhūpī* 硃批 (vermilion rescripts) — is laid out programmatically in Hóng Zīkuí's *zǒnglùn* prefixed to [[KR2f0011]] *Liǎng Hàn zhàolìng*. Wilkinson 2018 §47.5.7 is the standard modern treatment.
- **Memorial sub-genres.** Within the *zòuyì* side, the principal sub-genres — *zòu* 奏, *yì* 議, *shū* 疏, *biǎo* 表, *zházǐ* 札子, *qǐ* 啓, and the Sòng-introduced *zòuzhé* 奏摺 — each have distinct formal and rhetorical conventions. The Sòng officials' *biéjí*-style collections ([[KR2f0012]]–0016) tend to be mixed-genre; the Míng and Qīng collections increasingly specialize. The Qīng *zòuzhé* (secret memorial), preserved in [[KR2f0009]], is the institutional climax of the genre — a confidential channel between provincial officials and the emperor personally, bypassing the regular bureaucracy.
- **By-person versus by-theme.** The single most consequential editorial-methodological debate in the division is the explicit Zhū Xī / Zhào Rǔyú disagreement preserved in the *tíyào* of [[KR2f0038]] (with the *Sìkù* editors decisively endorsing Zhào's by-theme approach). The downstream history of the genre shows both methods in active use: [[KR2f0037]] and [[KR2f0041]] organize by person, [[KR2f0038]] and [[KR2f0039]] by theme.
- **The anthology as documentary archive.** Because the Táng, Sòng, and Yuán *shílù* and *qǐjūzhù* are almost entirely lost, the *zhàolìng* and *zòuyì* anthologies collected in KR2f function as the principal *de facto* state archive for those dynasties. [[KR2f0038]] preserves Sòng court documentation; [[KR2f0039]] preserves Sòng and Yuán; [[KR2f0010]] preserves Táng. The *Sìkù* editors are explicit about this archival function and frame it as the category's principal scholarly justification.
- **Imperial self-canonization.** The Qīng *shèngxùn* / *shàngyù* / *zhūpī* program ([[KR2f0001]]–0002, 0004–0009) treats the dynasty's own ancestral pronouncements as canonical texts in the manner of the Confucian classics, complete with thematic divisions modelled on the *Zhōu lǐ* and the *Zhōngyōng*. This is a specifically Qīng innovation in imperial-document compilation; earlier dynasties did not aggregate ancestral pronouncements at this scale.
- **The *míngchén* canon.** The lineage [[KR2f0037]] → [[KR2f0038]] → [[KR2f0039]] → [[KR2f0040]] → [[KR2f0041]] traces the construction and re-construction of a canonical list of "famous officials" across six centuries, with each successive compiler revising the canon to reflect his own moment's political-moral concerns (Zhū Xī's Daoxue moralism; Zhào's policy-pragmatic by-theme classification; Yáng Shìqí's Yǒng-lè-era universalism; Huáng Xùn's mid-Míng statecraft turn; Qiánlóng's late-imperial selective consolidation).
- **The *Dà lǐ yì* and the Míng cluster.** A disproportionate fraction of the Míng *biéjí* collections ([[KR2f0021]], 0022, 0023, 0026, 0027) consists of memorials submitted around the Jia-jìng accession's Great Rites Controversy of 1521–1524 — the defining political-ritual crisis of the mid-Míng, and one of the principal scholarly uses of the KR2f Míng materials.
