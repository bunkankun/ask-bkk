# Shèngzǔ Rén-huángdì shèngxùn 聖祖仁皇帝聖訓
*Imperial Instructions of Shèngzǔ, the Benevolent Emperor*
by [[世宗]] (敕編)

## About the work
The largest of the Qīng dynastic *shèng-xùn*, in 60 *juàn*, compiling the pronouncements of the *Kāngxī* 康熙 emperor (Shèngzǔ 聖祖, 1654–1722, r. 1661–1722) over his unprecedented 61-year reign. The work was edited by his son and successor the *Yōngzhèng* 雍正 emperor (Shìzōng 世宗) with a preface dated *Yōngzhèng* 9 / 12 / 20 (1731), as the companion volume to the 1722–1731 imperial compilation of the *Shèngzǔ Rén-huángdì shí-lù* 聖祖仁皇帝實錄 in 1000 *juàn* (the longest *shí-lù* of any Chinese dynasty). A second imperial preface by the Qián-lóng emperor, dated *Qiánlóng* 6 / 3 / 24 (1741), was added at the time of printing.

### Tiyao
*This work has no formal* 提要 *block in the WYG frontmatter — the text begins immediately with the Yōngzhèng and Qiánlóng prefaces and proceeds directly into the table of contents.*

### Abstract
The *tíyào* function in this case is performed by the Yōngzhèng emperor's own preface of 1731, which states the editorial method explicitly: "the *Veritable Records* are an annalistic book … but our *Imperial Father* responded to the times and dispensed transformation in the manner of Heaven's way; in each item of his teaching, looking up to one *juàn*, the sage plan stands unalterable through the ages. Of the Six Classics and the histories of all transmission, each has both warp and weft; in His Majesty's divine merit and sage virtue I have observed in heart and body — taking the *Veritable Records* as warp and the *Sage Instructions* as weft. In the chronological annals one sees the appropriateness of times; in the categorical arrangement one sees the essence of each matter." The 60 *juàn* are arranged into a substantially expanded set of categorical headings reflecting the Kāngxī reign's complexity: governance, moral cultivation, court ritual, military campaigns (the Three Feudatories war, Galdan, Tibet), examination administration, water-control, foreign relations, and the major scholarly projects (the *Kāngxī zì-diǎn*, *Pèi-wén yùn-fǔ*, *Gǔ-jīn tú-shū jí-chéng*). The compilation is thus the principal categorically-arranged precis of the Kāngxī reign and was relied upon by all subsequent Qīng *zhèng-shū* compilers. The Qián-lóng preface of 1741 emphasizes that the editing under Yōngzhèng was an act of filial *jì-shù* 繼述 — continuation and exposition — paralleling Yōngzhèng's compilation of his father's *Tíng-xùn gé-yán* 庭訓格言 ([[KR3a0103]]).

## Translations and research
*No substantial Western-language translation located.* For Kāngxī-period state documentation generally see Jonathan Spence, *Emperor of China: Self-Portrait of K'ang-hsi* (1974) — based primarily on the *Shèngzǔ shèng-xùn* and *Tíng-xùn gé-yán*; Pei Huang, *Reorienting the Manchus* (2011); Pamela Crossley, *A Translucent Mirror* (1999).

## Other points of interest
At 60 *juàn* this is by far the most extensive of the Qīng *shèng-xùn*; it dwarfs the 4-, 6-, and 6-*juàn* compilations for *Tàizǔ*, *Tàizōng*, and *Shìzǔ* respectively. The disproportion reflects both the length of Kāngxī's reign and the great volume of his preserved pronouncements in the *qǐ-jū-zhù* 起居注 — the "diary of activity and repose," which Kāngxī was the first Qīng emperor to have systematically kept in both Manchu and Chinese.
