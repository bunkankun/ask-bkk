# KR3h 藝術類 — Arts

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3h sits inside the *Sìkù quánshū* Masters Branch (子部) as the *Yì-shù lèi* 藝術類 — the "Arts" — and gathers the works the Qiánlóng editors classed as the practice and connoisseurship of the literati arts: calligraphy 書 and painting 畫 (which together account for roughly three-quarters of the division), antiquarian *jīn-shí xué* 金石學, music for the *qín* zither, chess and Go, seal-script and seal-cutting, and one major treatise on garden design. Following Wilkinson (§2.9 on calligraphy as a fine art, §2.10–§2.12 on stone inscriptions, inscribed landscapes, and seals, §32 on painting and especially §32.3 on the literati theoretical tradition and §32.11 on collection catalogs, §33.4 on imperial parks and private gardens, and §34.2 on ancient music), the division is the single best-documented Chinese genre-cluster for what the Western tradition would call the "fine arts" — and the editorial point of the *Sìkù* *Yì-shù lèi* is the consolidation, under one heading, of what earlier *sì-bù* schemes had distributed across *zá-yì* 雜藝, separate calligraphy- and painting-catalogs, and the *qín* and *qí* subdivisions of the Masters Branch.

The chronological span runs from the Wèi-Jìn games-anthology *Yì jīng* 藝經 [[KR3h0099]] (c. 200–221, Hándān Chún) and Cáo Pī's *Tán-qí jīng* 彈碁經 [[KR3h0102]] (c. 220–226) — the earliest received Chinese writings on parlour-arts — through to the high Qiánlóng court, where the imperial *Shíqú bǎojí* [[KR3h0063]] (1744–45) catalogs over ten thousand secular calligraphies and paintings in the inner-palace collection and the *Xī-qīng gǔ-jiàn* [[KR3h0089]] (1749–55) catalogs 1,529 antique bronzes. Three transmissional traditions structure the reading. (1) **The calligraphy tradition**, from the Wèi-Jìn *Cǎo-shū zhuàng* [[KR3h0091]] of Suǒ Jìng and the *Sì-tǐ shū-shì* [[KR3h0092]] of Wèi Héng through the Liáng *pǐn*-criticism of Yǔ Jiānwú [[KR3h0002]], the Táng theoretical canon of Sūn Guòtíng [[KR3h0005]] and Zhāng Huáiguàn [[KR3h0006]] and Zhāng Yànyuǎn's encyclopaedic *Fǎshū yàolù* [[KR3h0008]], the Northern Sòng connoisseurship of Mǐ Fú [[KR3h0022]]–[[KR3h0024]] and the imperial *Xuānhé shū-pǔ* [[KR3h0026]], to the late-imperial syntheses of Yuán Zhèng Jiǎo's *Yǎn-jí* [[KR3h0038]], Míng Xiàng Mù's *Shūfǎ yǎyán* [[KR3h0050]], and the 420-juan Qīng compendium *Liù-yì zhī yī lù* [[KR3h0070]] by Ní Tāo. (2) **The painting tradition**, from Zōng Bǐng's foundational *Huà shān-shuǐ xù* [[KR3h0095]] (c. 420–443) and Xiè Hè's *Liù-fǎ* in *Gǔ-huà pǐn-lù* [[KR3h0001]] (c. 480–510) through the Táng art-historical synthesis of Zhāng Yànyuǎn's *Lìdài mínghuà jì* [[KR3h0009]] (847), the *yì-pǐn* 逸品 elevation of Zhū Jǐngxuán [[KR3h0010]] and Huáng Xiūfù [[KR3h0016]], the Northern Sòng *xiè-yì* 寫意 / *qì-yùn fēi shī* doctrines of Guō Ruòxū [[KR3h0017]] and Guō Xī [[KR3h0018]], the imperial *Xuānhé huà-pǔ* [[KR3h0025]], and Mǐ Fú's *Huà-shǐ* [[KR3h0021]], through the Yuán biographical-dictionaries of Tāng Hòu [[KR3h0037]] and Xià Wényàn [[KR3h0041]], the late-Míng connoisseurial compendia of Zhāng Chǒu [[KR3h0055]]–[[KR3h0059]] and Wāng Kěyù [[KR3h0060]], to the imperial Qīng catalogs *Pèiwénzhāi shūhuà pǔ* [[KR3h0061]], *Mìdiàn zhūlín* [[KR3h0062]], and *Shíqú bǎojí* [[KR3h0063]]. (3) **The antiquarian *jīn-shí* tradition**, from Liáng Táo Hóngjǐng's *Gǔjīn dāojiàn lù* [[KR3h0083]] and Chén Yú Lì's *Dǐng lù* [[KR3h0084]] through Lǚ Dàlín's *Kǎo-gǔ tú* [[KR3h0086]] — the founding work of empirical Chinese antiquarianism, c. 1092 — and the imperial *Chóng-xiū Xuān-hé bó-gǔ tú* [[KR3h0087]], to the high-Qīng *Xī-qīng gǔ-jiàn* [[KR3h0089]]. Smaller but established subclusters in the catalog cover *qín* zither (Zhū Chángwén's *Qín-shǐ* [[KR3h0073]], Yán Chéng's *Sōng-xián guǎn qín-pǔ* [[KR3h0074]], etc.), Táng music (Nán Zhuó's *Jié-gǔ lù* [[KR3h0079]], Duàn Ānjié's *Yuèfǔ zá-lù* [[KR3h0080]]), seal-script and seal-cutting (Wú Qiūyǎn's *Xué-gǔ biān* [[KR3h0077]], Zhū Xiàngxián's *Yìn diǎn* [[KR3h0078]]), Go and chess (Yàn Tiānzhāng's *Qí jīng* [[KR3h0081]], Liú Zhòngfǔ's *Qí jué* [[KR3h0082]], and the Six-Dynasties anthological fragments [[KR3h0098]]–[[KR3h0102]]), and the unique Míng treatise on garden design, Jì Chéng's *Yuán-yě* [[KR3h0090]] (1631–34). Across all of these the editors' principal hermeneutic problem is the same: how to discipline a body of work composed largely of *pǐn*-grade aesthetic verdicts, eyewitness colophons, and connoisseurial anecdote into the evidential format the *Sìkù tíyào* demand — which they accomplish through the same regime of textual-critical scepticism they apply to [[KR3g]] *Shù-shù lèi*, treating pseudepigraphic Wèi-Jìn brush-treatises (*Bǐ-zhèn tú* [[KR3h0093]], *Bǐ-jīng* [[KR3h0094]]) and reassigned colophons (the *Shānhú mùnán* / *Tiěwǎng shānhú* doublet [[KR3h0044]]/[[KR3h0045]]) with the same documentary respect-with-scepticism the cosmological pseudepigrapha receive next door.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The Six-Dynasties theoretical foundation.** Three works compose the conceptual armature of Chinese painting criticism. Xiè Hè's *Gǔ-huà pǐn-lù* 古畫品錄 [[KR3h0001]] (c. 480–510) articulates the **Six Methods** (*huà liù-fǎ* 畫六法) — *qì-yùn shēng-dòng*, *gǔ-fǎ yòng-bǐ*, *yìng-wù xiàng-xíng*, *suí-lèi fù-cǎi*, *jīng-yíng wèi-zhì*, *chuán-mó yí-xiě* — that govern every subsequent Chinese pictorial criticism through the Qīng. Zōng Bǐng's *Huà shān-shuǐ xù* 畫山水敘 [[KR3h0095]] (420–443) is the founding metaphysical statement of landscape painting, with the geometric-perspectival argument and the doctrine of *wò yóu* 臥遊 ("recumbent travel"). Yáo Zuì's *Xù huà-pǐn* 續畫品 [[KR3h0003]] (540–552) is the second-oldest painting catalog and the principal correction of Xiè Hè's grade-assignments. Liáng *pǐn*-criticism trio: Yǔ Jiānwú's *Shū-pǐn* 書品 [[KR3h0002]] (c. 530–551), Liǔ Yún's *Qí-pǐn* 棋品 [[KR3h0101]] (505–517), and Xiè Hè's *Gǔ-huà pǐn-lù* together form the late-Liáng *pǐn*-discourse triplet that anchors the aesthetic-critical vocabulary of all the *yì-shù* fields (paralleling Zhōng Róng's *Shī pǐn* in poetry).

**The Wèi-Jìn calligraphic-foundational layer.** Suǒ Jìng's *Cǎo-shū zhuàng* 草書狀 [[KR3h0091]] (270–303), Wèi Héng's *Sì-tǐ shū-shì* 四體書勢 [[KR3h0092]] (270–291), and the pseudepigraphic *Bǐ-zhèn tú* 筆陣圖 [[KR3h0093]] and *Bǐ-jīng* 筆經 [[KR3h0094]] (attributed to Wèi Shuò / Wáng Xīzhī, in fact 4th–8th-c. composite) furnish the earliest stratum of Chinese calligraphic theory and the source of the "battle-formation" stroke-imagery and the *wén-fáng sì-bǎo* (Four Treasures of the Studio) literature.

**The Táng systematic synthesis.** Sūn Guòtíng's *Shū-pǔ* 書譜 [[KR3h0005]] (687) is the canonical Táng theoretical treatise on calligraphy and the principal organising statement of the field by script-genre (*zhēn / xíng / cǎo / lì*); Zhāng Huáiguàn's *Shū-duàn* 書斷 [[KR3h0006]] (720–740) is the model for all later Chinese calligraphic biographical-rank treatises; Dòu Jì's *Shùshū fù* 述書賦 [[KR3h0007]] (754–758) with Dòu Méng's commentary anchors the *fù*-form treatment of calligraphy and the earliest collector's-seal documentation. The two great Táng art-historical syntheses are by Zhāng Yànyuǎn: *Fǎshū yàolù* 法書要錄 [[KR3h0008]] (847–855) — the single most important preserver of Six-Dynasties and early-Táng calligraphic theory — and *Lìdài mínghuà jì* 歷代名畫記 [[KR3h0009]] (847), the earliest fully extant systematic art history in any language and the model for every subsequent Chinese art-historical work. Zhū Jǐngxuán's *Tángcháo mínghuà lù* 唐朝名畫錄 [[KR3h0010]] (840–846) introduces the **untrammelled grade** (*yì-pǐn* 逸品) — the conceptual move that, once Huáng Xiūfù [[KR3h0016]] elevates *yì* above *shén-pǐn*, becomes the theoretical foundation of the literati-painting tradition.

**The Northern Sòng painter-biography tradition.** Liú Dàochún's *Wǔdài mínghuà bǔyí* 五代名畫補遺 [[KR3h0014]] (1059) and *Sòngcháo mínghuà píng* 宋朝名畫評 [[KR3h0015]] (1086–1100) are the earliest systematic records of Five-Dynasties and Northern Sòng painters, seeding the Jīng-Guān monumental-landscape genealogy. Huáng Xiūfù's *Yìzhōu mínghuà lù* 益州名畫錄 [[KR3h0016]] (1006) registers Shǔ-court painting and gives the theoretical elevation of *yì-pǐn*. Guō Ruòxū's *Túhuà jiànwén zhì* 圖畫見聞誌 [[KR3h0017]] (1074–80) — translated by Soper as *Experiences in Painting* — supplies the *qìyùn fēi shī* 氣韻非師 ("vital-pneuma cannot be transmitted from a teacher") doctrine and the Northern / Southern landscape-lineage scheme that anchors literati art theory. Guō Xī (with Guō Sī's editing) in *Línquán gāozhì jí* 林泉高致集 [[KR3h0018]] (1117) articulates the *Sān-yuǎn* 三遠 doctrine — *gāo-yuǎn* 高遠 (high distance), *shēn-yuǎn* 深遠 (deep distance), *píng-yuǎn* 平遠 (level distance) — the standard typology for spatial recession in all subsequent Chinese landscape practice. Hán Zhuō's *Shānshuǐ chúnquán jí* 山水純全集 [[KR3h0027]] (1121) supplies the most-cited Northern Sòng source for the technical vocabulary of *cūn-fǎ* (texture-stroke methods) and adds three further "distances" to Guō Xī's three. Dèng Chūn's *Huà-jì* 畫繼 [[KR3h0030]] (1167) is the principal bridge text between the late-Huīzōng academy and the early Southern Sòng.

**Mǐ Fú and Sòng connoisseurship.** Mǐ Fú's three short connoisseurial works of 1086–1107 — *Bǎozhāng dàifǎng lù* 寶章待訪錄 [[KR3h0023]] (1086), *Huà-shǐ* 畫史 [[KR3h0021]] and *Shū-shǐ* 書史 [[KR3h0022]] (both c. 1100–07), and *Hǎiyuè míngyán* 海嶽名言 [[KR3h0024]] — are the foundation of Chinese connoisseurship literature: the earliest detailed first-person records of private collections, the earliest systematic descriptions of seals and mountings, and the canonical sceptical rejection of metaphor-laden criticism. Dǒng Yǒu's two paired colophon-collections *Guǎngchuān shū-bá* 廣川書跋 [[KR3h0028]] and *Guǎngchuān huà-bá* 廣川畫跋 [[KR3h0029]] (1115–27) found the colophon-essay form (*bá* 跋 / *tí-bá* 題跋) that dominates Yuán-Míng-Qīng connoisseurial writing — and, on the calligraphic side, advance *jīn-shí xué* into systematic connoisseurial appraisal.

**The Northern Sòng imperial catalogs.** *Xuānhé huà-pǔ* 宣和畫譜 [[KR3h0025]] (1120) — translated by Ebrey 2008, with McNair 2023 arguing for the eunuch Liáng Shīchéng as actual editor — and *Xuānhé shū-pǔ* 宣和書譜 [[KR3h0026]] (1120–23) are the foundational documents for the Huīzōng imperial collection, ten-fold subject-classified for painting (Wilkinson §32.11 reproduces the breakdown: 6,396 scrolls under 231 painters) and the canonical model for all subsequent imperial collection-catalogs. Zhū Chángwén's *Mòchí biān* 墨池編 [[KR3h0019]] (1066–80) is the great non-imperial calligraphic compendium of the period.

**The Southern Sòng anthological consolidation.** Jiāng Kuí's *Xù shū-pǔ* 續書譜 [[KR3h0031]] (1200–08, translated alongside Sūn Guòtíng in Chang and Frankel 1995) is the principal Southern Sòng systematic calligraphic doctrine, organised by aesthetic category. Yuè Kē's *Bǎozhēnzhāi fǎshū zàn* 寶眞齋法書贊 [[KR3h0032]] is the largest family-collection catalog of the era and the only premodern source preserving the *È-guó chuán-jiā tiè* of Yuè Fēi's correspondence. Chén Sī's *Shū-yuàn jīnghuá* 書苑菁華 [[KR3h0033]] (1234–59) and *Shū xiǎo-shǐ* 書小史 [[KR3h0034]] (1259–67) — 521 figures — together provide the systematic biographical reference set for calligraphers from antiquity through the Five Dynasties; Dǒng Gèng's *Shū-lù* 書錄 [[KR3h0035]] is a rare prosopographic survey of Sòng-dynasty calligraphers, one of the few medieval Chinese calligraphic prosopographies systematically including women.

**The Sòng antiquarian / *jīn-shí* cluster.** Lǚ Dàlín's *Kǎo-gǔ tú* 考古圖 [[KR3h0086]] (1092) is the founding work of empirical Chinese antiquarianism — Wilkinson (§2.10) treats it as the systematic point of departure of *jīn-shí xué* — establishing the *yǒu yí zé quē* ("doubt-then-leave-blank") evidential principle. Wáng Fú's *Chóng-xiū Xuān-hé bó-gǔ tú* 重修宣和博古圖 [[KR3h0087]] (1107–23) is the imperial counterpart, in 30 *juàn* with over 800 vessels. Wáng Qiú's *Xiào-táng jí-gǔ lù* 嘯堂集古錄 [[KR3h0085]] (1130–70) supplies the *kuǎn-shí*-with-gloss format that gives the earliest surviving Sòng readings for many Shāng-Zhōu inscriptions. Together with Ōuyáng Xiū's *Jí-gǔ lù* (in [[KR2k]] not here) and Zhào Míngchéng's *Jīn-shí lù* (also outside KR3h), the *Kǎo-gǔ tú* / *Bó-gǔ tú* / *Xiào-táng* triplet is the canonical Sòng *jīn-shí* primary-source set.

**The Yuán reference works.** Lǐ Kàn's *Zhú-pǔ* 竹譜 [[KR3h0036]] (1300–19) is the only Chinese treatise combining a complete bamboo-painting manual with a systematic botanical catalog, foundation of *mò-zhú* practice. Tāng Hòu's *Huà-jiàn* 畫鑒 [[KR3h0037]] (1322–30) is the principal Yuán connoisseur's manual; Zhèng Jiǎo's *Yǎn-jí* 衍極 [[KR3h0038]] (with Liú Yǒudìng's commentary, 1324–28) is the principal Yuán calligraphic-theoretical treatise; and Shèng Xīmíng's twin compendia — *Túhuà kǎo* 圖畫攷 [[KR3h0039]] and *Fǎshū kǎo* 法書考 [[KR3h0040]] (1330–49) — are the earliest topical (rather than chronological) systematic references on painting and calligraphy. Xià Wényàn's *Túhuì bǎojiàn* 圖繪寶鑑 [[KR3h0041]] (1365) becomes, after the dynastic transition, the standard biographical dictionary of Chinese painters and is reprinted through the Míng and Qīng.

**The late-Míng connoisseurial complex.** The richest sustained cluster of the division. Dū Mù's *Yùyì biān* 寓意編 [[KR3h0042]] (c. 1495–1525) and Zhū Cúnlǐ's *Shānhú mùnán* 珊瑚木難 [[KR3h0044]] (1495–1513) open the late-Wú connoisseurial mode. Yáng Shèn's *Mòchí suǒlù* 墨池璅錄 [[KR3h0046]] (1530–59) and Fēng Fāng's *Shū-jué* 書訣 [[KR3h0048]] (1523–63) carry the calligraphic-theoretical thread through the mid-century Zhào Mèngfǔ revival. Xiàng Mù's *Shūfǎ yǎyán* 書法雅言 [[KR3h0050]] (1580–1600) supplies the explicit calligraphic recovery of the Wáng Xīzhī tradition — the calligraphic parallel to Dǒng Qíchāng's *Nán-běi zōng* 南北宗 painting theory. The compilation *Tiěwǎng shānhú* 趙氏鐵綱珊瑚 [[KR3h0045]] (1600–20, attributed to Zhū Cúnlǐ but rejected by the *Sìkù tíyào*, with Zhào Qíměi as actual editor) and Sūn Kuàng's *Shū-huà bá-bá* 書畫跋跋 [[KR3h0047]] (1590–1613) extend the colophon-collection mode. Zhào Yíguāng's *Hánshān zhǒu-tán* 寒山帚談 [[KR3h0051]] (1600–25) is the principal doctrinal statement of the late-Míng *cǎo-zhuàn* (cursive seal-script) innovators. The three terminal compendia — Zhāng Chǒu's *Qīnghé shūhuà fǎng* 清河書畫舫 [[KR3h0055]] (1615–16) with its tabular companions *Qīnghé shūhuà biǎo* [[KR3h0057]], *Shūhuà jiànwén biǎo* [[KR3h0058]], *Nányáng shūhuà jí* [[KR3h0059]], and the *Zhēnjī rìlù* [[KR3h0056]]; Yù Féngqìng's *Shū-huà tí-bá jì* 書畫題跋記 [[KR3h0054]] (1634–44); and Wāng Kěyù's monumental 48-*juàn* *Shānhú wǎng* 珊瑚網 [[KR3h0060]] (1643) — together form the terminal record of Jiāngnán connoisseurship before the dynastic collapse, alongside Táng Zhìqì's *Huì-shì wēi-yán* 繪事微言 [[KR3h0049]] (the principal late-Míng systematic landscape-painting compendium) and Zhū Móuyīn's *Huà-shǐ huì-yào* 畫史會要 [[KR3h0053]] (1631).

**The Qīng imperial-catalog programme.** Sūn Yuèbān's *Yùdìng Pèiwénzhāi shūhuà pǔ* 御定佩文齋書畫譜 [[KR3h0061]] (1708, 100 *juàn*, with 1,844 sources cited per-entry) is the principal single Qīng reference work on Chinese calligraphy and painting up to the late Kāngxī, and stands as the Qīng equivalent of the Sòng *Xuānhé* catalogs. The Qiánlóng-court imperial catalogs follow: *Mìdiàn zhūlín* 祕殿珠林 [[KR3h0062]] (1744, Buddhist and Daoist works in the inner-palace collection), *Shíqú bǎojí* 石渠寶笈 [[KR3h0063]] (1744–45, the more than ten thousand secular pieces — the most ambitious catalog of any premodern Chinese imperial art collection, and the principal modern provenance reference for surviving post-Sòng masterworks in Beijing and Taipei), and Liáng Shīzhèng / Jiǎng Pǔ's *Xī-qīng gǔ-jiàn* 西清古鑑 [[KR3h0089]] (1749–55, 40 *juàn*, 1,529 antique bronzes — the closing imperial antiquarian compilation). Around them, Biàn Yǒngyù's 60-*juàn* *Shūhuà huì-kǎo* 書畫彙考 [[KR3h0068]] (1682) is the largest single private compendium of calligraphy-and-painting records before the *Pèiwénzhāi*; Sūn Chéngzé's *Gēngzǐ xiāo-xià jì* 庚子銷夏記 [[KR3h0064]] (1660), Gāo Shìqí's *Jiāngcūn xiāo-xià lù* 江村銷夏錄 [[KR3h0067]] (1693), and Lì È's *Nán-Sòng yuàn-huà lù* 南宋院畫錄 [[KR3h0069]] (1721–45) are the principal scholar-connoisseurs' catalogs of the Kāngxī-Yōngzhèng-Qiánlóng cohort.

**The Qīng calligraphic-and-painting handbooks.** Féng Wǔ's *Shūfǎ zhèngchuán* 書法正傳 [[KR3h0066]] (1707) is the principal Qīng compilation of regular-script theory; Ní Tāo's *Liù-yì zhī yī lù* 六藝之一錄 [[KR3h0070]] (1700–60, 420 *juàn*) is one of the longest single-author *kǎo-zhèng* compendia in the entire *Sìkù*, effectively a one-volume library of pre-Qīng calligraphy literature; Wáng Yùxián's *Huì-shì bèi-kǎo* 繪事備考 [[KR3h0065]] (1691) is the standard Qīng painter-dictionary bridging Xià Wényàn and the *Pèiwénzhāi*; Jiǎng Jì's *Chuán-shén mì-yào* 傳神祕要 [[KR3h0072]] (1707–27) is the first and principal Chinese treatise on portraiture, elevating *xiě-zhēn* 寫真 from artisanal to *shì-dài-fū* status; and Zōu Yīguì's *Xiǎo-shān huà-pǔ* 小山畫譜 [[KR3h0071]] (1756) is the principal Qiánlóng-court theoretical handbook on flower painting in the *mò-jīng* (boneless) tradition.

**The qín, seal, music, and games subclusters.** *Qín-shǐ* 琴史 [[KR3h0073]] (Zhū Chángwén, c. 1080) is the first systematic history of the *qín* zither and the earliest substantial Chinese music-historical work; the *Sōng-xián guǎn qín-pǔ* [[KR3h0074]], *Sōng-fēng gé qín-pǔ* [[KR3h0075]], and the bilingual Chinese-Manchu *Qín-pǔ hé-bì* [[KR3h0076]] are the major received Míng-Qīng tablature collections. Wú Qiūyǎn's *Xué-gǔ biān* 學古編 [[KR3h0077]] (early Yuán) and Zhū Xiàngxián's *Yìn diǎn* 印典 [[KR3h0078]] (Qīng) are the principal pre-modern Chinese treatises on seal-cutting and seal-script; Wilkinson (§2.12 on seals) treats them as foundational to the late-imperial *zhuàn-kè* revival. Nán Zhuó's *Jié-gǔ lù* 羯鼓錄 [[KR3h0079]] and Duàn Ānjié's *Yuèfǔ zá-lù* 樂府雜錄 [[KR3h0080]] are the two principal Táng records of court music and entertainment, indispensable for the Tang music section that Wilkinson treats at §34.2. Yàn Tiānzhāng's *Qí jīng* 棋經 [[KR3h0081]] and Liú Zhòngfǔ's *Qí jué* 棋訣 [[KR3h0082]] are the two principal Sòng received writings on Go. Together with the Six-Dynasties fragments [[KR3h0098]]–[[KR3h0102]] (Yóu Zhào's *Rú-qí gé*, Hándān Chún's *Yì jīng*, the Five-Dynasties / Táng *Qí-shǒu shì*, Liǔ Yún's *Qí pǐn*, and Cáo Pī's *Tán-qí jīng*), these constitute the canonical *qí* primary-source set; Liǔ Yún's *Qí pǐn* [[KR3h0101]] in particular completes the late-Liáng *pǐn*-criticism quartet.

**Bronze-and-blade antiquaria.** Táo Hóngjǐng's *Gǔjīn dāojiàn lù* 古今刀劔錄 [[KR3h0083]] (502–536) is the standard premodern Chinese source on the historiography of blade-weapons; Yú Lì's *Dǐng lù* 鼎錄 [[KR3h0084]] (557–569) is the earliest surviving Chinese register of ritual tripods — predating Lǚ Dàlín by some five centuries. Lǚ Zhèn's *Xuān-dé dǐng-yí pǔ* 宣德鼎彝譜 [[KR3h0088]] (1428) is the principal document of the 1428 Xuāndé imperial bronze-recasting programme.

**Garden design.** Jì Chéng's *Yuán-yě* 園冶 [[KR3h0090]] (1631–34) is the foundation of Chinese garden theory and the single most influential premodern Chinese treatise on landscape-garden design, articulating the doctrines of *yīn* 因 (working with the site), *jiè* 借 (borrowed scenery), and *tǐ-yí* 體宜 (appropriate scale). The work is the only pre-modern Chinese architectural treatise to give a systematic poetics of practice; rediscovered by Edo-period Japanese architects and re-introduced to Chinese scholarship in the 1930s. Wilkinson (§33.4) treats it as the canonical theoretical reference for the Sūzhōu and Yáng-zhōu private-garden tradition.

## Topics

1. **The Six Methods (*huà liù-fǎ* 畫六法).** Xiè Hè's six-fold framework — *qì-yùn shēng-dòng*, *gǔ-fǎ yòng-bǐ*, *yìng-wù xiàng-xíng*, *suí-lèi fù-cǎi*, *jīng-yíng wèi-zhì*, *chuán-mó yí-xiě* — is the conceptual armature of every subsequent Chinese pictorial criticism through the Qīng. The principal interpretive problem the tradition negotiates is the supremacy and untranslatability of *qì-yùn shēng-dòng* and its relationship to teachability: Guō Ruòxū's *qìyùn fēi shī* doctrine [[KR3h0017]] is the canonical reply, asserting that the supreme aesthetic quality cannot be transmitted from a teacher and so must be cultivated through the painter's personal *xiū-yǎng* (self-cultivation). See Acker (1954–74), Bush and Shih (1985), and Vinograd's "Classification, canon, and genre" essay in Powers and Tsiang's *Companion to Chinese Art* (2016).

2. **The *yì-pǐn* 逸品 elevation.** The Táng grading-categories *shén-pǐn* 神品 (spirit grade), *miào-pǐn* 妙品 (subtle grade), *néng-pǐn* 能品 (competent grade) are inherited from Xiè Hè's six-grade scheme. Zhū Jǐngxuán's *Tángcháo mínghuà lù* [[KR3h0010]] introduces *yì-pǐn* (the untrammelled grade) outside the existing scale; Huáng Xiūfù's *Yìzhōu mínghuà lù* [[KR3h0016]] elevates *yì-pǐn* to the top of the scale. This is the conceptual move that founds the literati-painting tradition's theoretical superiority over technically-trained court / academy painting.

3. **The *xiè-yì* 寫意 vs. *gōng-bǐ* 工筆 polarity and the *Nán-běi zōng* theory.** The principled distinction between the spontaneous-suggestive literati hand and the meticulous court / professional hand crystallises in Northern-Sòng colophon-criticism (Sū Shì, Mǐ Fú in [[KR3h0021]], Guō Ruòxū in [[KR3h0017]]) and culminates in Dǒng Qíchāng's late-Míng *Nán-běi zōng* (Southern / Northern school) theory of painting lineages — paralleled on the calligraphic side by Xiàng Mù's *Shūfǎ yǎyán* [[KR3h0050]]. The polarity is the principal organising distinction of late-imperial art-historical writing.

4. **Pseudepigraphy and textual-criticism in brush-treatises.** The *Sìkù tíyào* exhibit the same textual-critical scepticism here as in [[KR3g]]: the brush-treatises *Bǐ-zhèn tú* [[KR3h0093]] and *Bǐ-jīng* [[KR3h0094]] are exposed as pseudepigraphic notwithstanding their Wèi Shuò / Wáng Xīzhī attributions, the *Tiěwǎng shānhú* [[KR3h0045]] is rejected as a Wàn-lì-period editorial fabrication built on Zhū Cúnlǐ's [[KR3h0044]], and the *Tán-qí jīng* [[KR3h0102]] is treated as a composite anthology rather than a single Cáo Pī work. The *tíyào* preserve a characteristic stance: the pseudepigraphic ascription is repudiated, the document's technical-and-historical value preserved.

5. ***Jīn-shí xué* 金石學: from inscriptions to evidential history.** Wilkinson (§2.10 on stone inscriptions, §2.12 on seals) treats Sòng antiquarianism as the systematic origin of Chinese evidential / *kǎo-zhèng* method. The triplet *Kǎo-gǔ tú* [[KR3h0086]] / *Bó-gǔ tú* [[KR3h0087]] / *Xiào-táng jí-gǔ lù* [[KR3h0085]] — in conjunction with Ōuyáng Xiū's *Jí-gǔ lù* and Zhào Míngchéng's *Jīn-shí lù* (both outside KR3h) — establishes empirical antiquarianism in Northern-Sòng China, with the *yǒu yí zé quē* principle. The Qing high-imperial *Xī-qīng gǔ-jiàn* [[KR3h0089]] is the closing imperial expression of the same tradition; the gap between the two registers the lapsed-and-revived character of the *jīn-shí* tradition Wilkinson treats at length.

6. **Imperial collecting and the catalog as state document.** The transformation of the painter's-eye record into the imperial-bureaucratic catalog runs from the Tang *Zhēn-guān gōng-sī huà-shǐ* [[KR3h0004]] through *Xuānhé huà-pǔ* / *Xuānhé shū-pǔ* [[KR3h0025]]/[[KR3h0026]] (which Ebrey 2008 and McNair 2023 treat as cultural-political instruments) to the Qing *Pèiwénzhāi* [[KR3h0061]] and *Shíqú bǎojí* [[KR3h0063]]. The imperial catalog is simultaneously inventory, legitimation, and aesthetic-canonical declaration — and its provenance information is the principal modern basis for authenticating surviving pre-Qing masterworks.

7. **The colophon (*tí-bá* 題跋) as critical form.** Dǒng Yǒu's pair [[KR3h0028]]/[[KR3h0029]] founds the colophon-essay, which the Yuán-Míng-Qīng connoisseurial compendia ([[KR3h0042]] through [[KR3h0060]]) carry as their dominant form. The colophon is the calligraphic-pictorial equivalent of the *bǐ-jì* 筆記 form: episodic, dated, signed, frequently dialogic with prior colophons on the same piece — and structurally inseparable from the physical-object practices Wilkinson treats at §32.3 (the *shuāng-kuǎn* signature-plus-dedication form, the *tí-shī* picture-poem).

8. **The late-Liáng *pǐn*-criticism quartet.** Within a single generation (c. 505–552) the Liáng / Chén court produces Zhōng Róng's *Shī pǐn* (poetry, in [[KR4d]]), Yǔ Jiānwú's *Shū-pǐn* [[KR3h0002]] (calligraphy), Xiè Hè's *Gǔ-huà pǐn-lù* [[KR3h0001]] (painting), and Liǔ Yún's *Qí-pǐn* [[KR3h0101]] (Go). This is the foundational moment of Chinese aesthetic-critical *pǐn*-discourse, in which the four major non-Confucian literati arts simultaneously acquire systematic ranked criticism.

9. **Forgery, authentication, and the social life of objects.** Late-Míng connoisseurship is acutely aware of forgery: Mǐ Fú's bipartite *mù-dǔ* / *dí-wén* model [[KR3h0023]] is the canonical methodological response, and the late-Míng compendia of Zhāng Chǒu [[KR3h0055]] and Wāng Kěyù [[KR3h0060]] organise themselves around the eyewitness-record distinction. The notorious Fēng Fāng [[KR3h0048]] embodies the period's productive ambivalence around forgery: a substantial calligraphic theorist who was also one of the era's most famous forgers. Park (2025, on Zhāng Tàijiē's *Bǎo-huì lù*) treats this strain in late-Míng connoisseurship; Wilkinson (§32.9) treats Chinese forgery historically.

10. **East-Asian circulation.** *Yuán-yě* [[KR3h0090]] survives because Edo-period Japanese architects collected and preserved a corpus the early Qīng allowed to fall into neglect; Japanese rediscovery in the late nineteenth century re-introduced the work to twentieth-century Chinese architectural scholarship. The bilingual *Qín-pǔ hé-bì* [[KR3h0076]] (Yáng Lún's Míng *qín*-manual with Hé Sù's Qīng Manchu translation) registers an interior East-Asian musical-cultural translation. Both works register the longue-durée cross-border circulation of Chinese art-knowledge that Wilkinson sketches at §34.2.5 on early musical interactions.
