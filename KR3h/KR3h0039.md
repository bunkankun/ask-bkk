# Túhuà kǎo 圖畫攷
*Investigations on Painting*
by [[盛熙明]] (Shèng Xīmíng, *fl.* 1330–1349, 元, *zhuàn* 撰)

## About the work

A late Yuán theoretical-historical compendium on painting, in seven *juàn*, presented to the throne (likely under Shùn-dì). The work is the companion-volume to Shèng's [[KR3h0040]] *Fǎshū kǎo* (eight *juàn*, on calligraphy) and was produced as part of the same project of imperial reference. The chapters as listed in the SBCK Mokuroku: *juàn* 1 *Xù-gǔ* 敘古 (sources of painting, including *Shù-yuán* 述原, *Xīng-fèi* 興廢, *Guī-jiàn* 規鑒, *Tú-míng* 圖名, *Shī-zhuàn* 師傳); subsequent *juàn* cover technical methods, materials, and exemplars. Shèng's own preface explains the work as a systematisation of the painting tradition, drawing on the precedent of the Táng-Sòng masterpieces and on imperial patronage from Shāng Gāo-zōng's portrait-search down to the Yuán *Jiàn-shū-huà bóshì*.

### Tiyao

*No tiyao found in source.* The Kanripo base for KR3h0039 is the *Sìbù cóngkān* SBCK reprint and lacks the Sìkù *tíyào* (the Sìkù version is the parallel [[KR3h0040]] *Fǎshū kǎo*; the *Túhuà kǎo* circulates separately). Shèng's own *Jìn Túhuà kǎo xù* serves as the substantive preface and is preserved at the head of the SBCK text. It opens with a long mythical-historical framing — from Fúxī's *Hé-tú* and the eight trigrams, through Cāng Jié's character-invention, the painted official robes in the Wèi palace, the painted bronze cauldrons of the Three Dynasties, the portrait-paintings of the Hàn *Yúntái* and Táng *Língyān* halls, the *zhuàn-lì-zhōng-Wáng* canonical line of calligraphy, the Táng Tài-zōng's establishment of court painting, the Sòng *Jiàn-huà bóshì* office, and finally the present sage dynasty (Yuán) under whom Shèng now presents his book. The preface concludes with Shèng's self-deprecating modesty as a "former *yìwén-shēng*" and the formal presentation language. The seven-*juàn* structure is given immediately after.

### Abstract

Shèng Xīmíng 盛熙明 (active *Zhì-zhèng* 1340s) was a Yuán court literatus of Central Asian (probably Khotanese) descent, fluent in multiple languages of the Yuán court and well known for his unusual cultural-bridging position. His [[KR3h0040]] *Fǎshū kǎo* (in 8 *juàn*) is the better-known companion volume; the *Túhuà kǎo* in 7 *juàn* is its painting counterpart. The two together constitute the most systematic late-Yuán imperial reference works on the visual arts, designed as practical manuals for the imperial *Jiàn-shū-huà* office. Where the Sòng [[KR3h0017]] *Túhuà jiànwén zhì* and [[KR3h0030]] *Huà-jì* gather painters chronologically by reign, Shèng works topically: he provides separate dedicated chapters on origins, transmission, materials, ranks, technique. The work is the earliest such fully-systematic Chinese painting reference and the model for the comprehensive imperial compendia of the Míng and Qīng.

## Other points of interest

The *Túhuà kǎo* and *Fǎshū kǎo* are the earliest Chinese visual-arts reference works compiled by a Central Asian author for the multi-cultural Yuán court — a unique testimony to the cosmopolitan character of late-Yuán imperial patronage.
