Yú Dé-lín 兪德鄰 (also written 俞德鄰; 1232–1293), *zì* Zōng-dà 宗大, *hào* Pèi-wéi-zhāi 佩韋齋, native of Yǒng-jiā 永嘉 in Wēn-zhōu 溫州 (Zhè-jiāng). He passed the *jìnshì* in the *guǐ-yǒu* group of Sòng Xián-chún 9 (1273), only three years before the dynasty's surrender. When the Sòng fell in 1276 he refused all Yuán recruitment and retired to Jìng-jiāng 京口 (Zhèn-jiāng, Jiāng-sū), where he lived as a private literatus for the next seventeen years. The Xióng Hé preface of 1312 to his collected works reports that "with rank and emolument in front and the executioner's pot behind" he had refused Yuán enrollment in the camp at the moment of surrender. His surviving works are the literary collection *Pèi-wéi-zhāi jí* [[KR4d0398]] in sixteen *juàn* (compiled posthumously by his son Yú Yōng 俞庸) and the *bǐ-jì*-style *Pèi-wéi-zhāi jí-wén* [[KR3i0026]] in four *juàn*.
