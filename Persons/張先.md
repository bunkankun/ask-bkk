Northern-Sòng *cí*-poet, *zì* Zǐ-yě 子野, of Wū-chéng 烏程 (Hú-zhōu 湖州, modern Zhèjiāng). *Jìnshì* of *Tiān-shèng* 8 / 1030; held a long series of provincial appointments (Sù-zhōu 宿州, Wú-jiāng 吳江, Jiā-hé 嘉禾, Yǒng-xīng 永興, etc.) and reached *Tài-cháng bóshì* and *Dū-guān láng-zhōng* before retirement to Hángzhōu. Famous for his longevity (lived to the age of 88 *suì*, dying in 1078 by Chinese count) and his late-life associations — notably with Sū Shì 蘇軾, who composed several poems for him in old age — and for the literary nickname *Zhāng sān yǐng* 張三影 ("Zhāng Three-Shadows") earned from three favourite *cí* lines containing the word *yǐng* 影 (shadow). With Liǔ Yǒng [[柳永]] he is one of the principal early-Sòng pioneers of the *màn-cí* 慢詞 (long-tune *cí*), expanding the form beyond the *xiǎo-lìng* of Yàn Shū [[晏殊]]. His own *cí*-collection survives as the *Ān-lù jí* [[KR4j0004]] (1 *juǎn*, WYG). Wrote the preface (now lost) to Yàn Shū's *Zhū-yù cí* [[KR4j0001]]. Biography: *Sòng shǐ* j. 444 (under "Wén-yì" 文藝); CBDB.
