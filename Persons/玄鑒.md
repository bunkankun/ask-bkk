Buddhist monk of the *Dà-cháng-hé-guó* 大長和國 (902–928, the kingdom that succeeded *Nán-zhāo* 南詔 in Yúnnán). Self-identified in the head-cartouche of [[KR6v0074]] as "*Yúnnán Dà-cháng-hé-guó nèi gōng-fèng sēng, Chóngshèng-sì zhǔ, yìxué jiào-zhǔ, cì zǐ shāmén Xuán-jiàn* 雲南大長和國內供奉僧、崇聖寺主、義學教主、賜紫沙門玄鑒" — i.e., resident-imperial-attending monk; abbot of *Chóngshèng-sì* 崇聖寺 (the principal royal monastery of Yúnnán, with the famous Three Pagodas at *Dà-lǐ*); *yìxué jiào-zhǔ* 義學教主 (master of doctrinal-studies); recipient of the imperial purple. Compiled the *Hùguó sī-nán chāo* 護國司南抄 [[KR6v0074]], a sub-commentary on Liángbēn 良賁's *Rénwáng hùguó bōrě bōluómìduō jīng shū* 仁王護國般若波羅蜜多經疏 (T33n1709), in *Ān-guó* 安國 6 (= 908 CE). Hóu Chōng's careful re-examination of the colophon and the use of the title *yìxué jiào-zhǔ* (rather than the previously misread *mì-xué jiào-zhǔ* 密學教主) places Xuán-jiàn in the *yìxué* (doctrinal-exegetical) rather than the esoteric tradition of Yúnnán Buddhism — a correction with significant implications for the history of *Nán-zhāo* / *Dà-cháng-hé* Buddhism.
