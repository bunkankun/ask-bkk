Wú Lóng-hàn 吳龍翰 (1233–1293), *zì* Shì-xián 式賢, *hào* Gǔ-méi 古梅 (after an old plum at his Shè-xiàn residence), native of Shè-xiàn 歙縣 in Huī-zhōu 徽州. He passed the local *xiāng-gòng* in the Sòng Xián-chún era and was appointed by recommendation as *biān-jiào* (collator) of writings in the Imperial Historiographical Office and the Veritable Records Office. After the Sòng surrender in 1276, the Huī-zhōu prefectural school invited him to serve as Instructor; he soon resigned and lived out his life as a private literatus. A *jiāng-hú* poet attached to the circle of Liú Kè-zhuāng [[劉克莊]], Fāng Yuè 方岳 (*hào* Qiū-yá 秋崖) and Fāng Huí [[方回]], with marked interest in inner-and-outer alchemy (compared by the *Sìkù* editors to Yú Yǎn 俞琰). He is conventionally credited as the first to scale the Tiān-dū peak of Huáng-shān (1242, with Bào Yún-lóng 鮑雲龍 and Sòng Fù 宋復) — recorded in his preface to the *Huáng-shān jì-yóu*. His surviving works are collected in the *Gǔ-méi yí-gǎo* [[KR4d0397]]. CBDB person 35283.
