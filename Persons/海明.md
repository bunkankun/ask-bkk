Pòshān Hǎimíng 破山海明 (Wànlì 25 *dīngyǒu* 1/21 wǔ-hour = 8 March 1597 – Kāngxī 5 *bǐngwǔ* 3/16 hài-hour = 19 April 1666) was the thirty-fifth-generation Línjì (Yángqí-branch) patriarch and the sole Sichuan-based dharma-heir of **Mìyún Yuánwù** [[圓悟]] 密雲圓悟 (1566/7–1642). Lay surname **Jiǎn** 蹇 (branch of the Yǒnglè chief grand secretary Jiǎn Yì 蹇義 1364–1435, the 忠定公), lay name **Jiǎn Dòngyǔ** 蹇棟宇, self-styled **Lǎnyú** 懶愚. Native of **Dàzhúxiàn** 大竹縣 in **Shùnqìngfǔ** 順慶府 (modern Nánchōng area, Sìchuān). Tonsured at 19 *sùi* (1615) at Fóēnsì 佛恩寺 in his home district under the elder **Dàchí** 大持; the Shǒuléngyán crisis on *chángzhù zhēnxīn* drove him out of Sichuan; trained three years at **Pòtóushān** 破頭山 at Huángméi (whence the *biéhào* **Pòshān**), where a cliff-side self-oath and literal fall from the precipice resolved his doubt. Full ordination under **Zhànrán Yuánchéng** 湛然圓澄 in *guǐhài* (1623) at age 27 *sùi*. Joined **Mìyún Yuánwù** at Jīnsù 金粟 in *jiǎzǐ* (1624); received the *Cáoqī zhèngmài láiyuán* 曹谿正脈來源 transmission-slip in *dīngmǎo* (1627).

Fourteen abbacies across a 37-year preaching career, beginning with **Jiāhé Dōngtǎ** 嘉禾東塔 in *jǐsì* autumn (1629) and **Liángshān Wànfēng** 梁山萬峰 from *guǐyǒu* (1633) when he returned to Sichuan. After 1644 sheltered as village-level *hùfǎ* chaplain in Sichuan-interior monasteries through the Zhāng Xiànzhōng devastations, and in Shùnzhì *rénchén/guǐsì* (1652–53) founded **Shuāngguìsì** 雙桂寺 at Jīnchéngzhài (Liángpíng, modern Chóngqìng) on the remains of a Míng-elite villa with a pair of surviving ancient *guì* 桂 trees. Died in residence at Shuāngguì in spring 1666, *shìshòu* 70 / *sēnglà* 43, eighty-seven dharma-heirs enumerated including **Zhàngxuě Tōngzuì** [[通醉]] 丈雪通醉 (Zhāojué line), **Liányuè Yìnzhèng** [[印正]] 蓮月印正 (Yùquán line), **Yànjū Héshēn** 燕居合申, **Shèngkě Déyù** 聖可德玉 (Huáyán line), and **Kōnggǔ Dàochéng** 空谷道澄. Full-body stūpa at **Gènlóngshān** 艮龍山; shadow-hall at Chéngdū Zhāojuésì.

Notable Kanripo works by Pòshān: [[KR6q0402]] 《破山禪師語錄》 20 juan (J26 B177, the complete Yìnzhèng recension of 1680). Other non-Kanripo works include the twelve-juan Zhāojué edition of 1657 (compiled by Zhàngxuě), 《山居詩》, 《雙桂草》, and scattered inscriptions in 《錦江禪燈》 (also by Zhàngxuě). The sole Mìyún-line patriarch whose recorded sayings anchor a full Sichuanese "Shǔzōng" 蜀宗 lineage, and the subject of Jiang Wu, *Enlightenment in Dispute* (Oxford 2008) chs. 4 and 7; also central to the early-Qīng military-chaplaincy material (the "*jiǔròu sēng*" apologetic, the 1644+ Lǐ Yào-zi / Lǐ Yīyáng episode).
