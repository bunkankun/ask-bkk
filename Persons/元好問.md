Yuán Hǎo-wèn 元好問 (1190–1257), *zì* Yù-zhī 裕之, *hào* Yí-shān 遺山 ("Remnant of the Mountains"), native of Xiù-róng-xiāng 秀容鄉 in Xīn-zhōu 忻州 (modern Shān-xī), descended from the Xianbei Tuō-bá 拓拔 clan converted to the surname Yuán by the Northern Wèi. The foremost Jīn-dynasty literary figure — poet, historian, anthologist — and one of the canonical Chinese poets of the second millennium. Adopted by his uncle Yuán Gé 元格 (a long-time Jīn local administrator); studied under Hǎo Tiān-tǐng 郝天挺. A *Xián-tóng-jí* (re-classified as *jìnshì*) of Jīn Xīng-dìng 5 (1221) at age 32, on Zhào Bǐng-wén's [[趙秉文]] recommendation. Held office under Āi-zōng's reign as Magistrate of Chén-liú, Nèi-xiāng, and Nán-yáng; rose to *Shàng-shū-shěng yuán-wài-láng* and *Hàn-lín zhī-zhì-gào*. Held in Kāi-fēng through the Mongol siege of 1232; on the Jīn dynasty's fall in 1234 he was briefly held captive at Liáo-chéng in the Yán Shí 嚴實 family compound, then permitted to retire to his native Xiù-róng-xiāng and to his uncle's grave-site, where at the Yě-shǐ-tíng 野史亭 ("Wild-History Pavilion") he composed the *Rén-jīn lèi* and the *Nán-guān lù* manuscript histories of the Jīn dynasty (both lost as discrete works but absorbed by the Yuán-period Tuō-tuō *Jīn-shǐ* compilers). For the next twenty-three years he refused all Yuán-state appointment and supported himself by composing *bēi-zhì-míng* and *xíng-zhuàng* for late-Jīn-and-post-conquest figures on commission. Major works: the *Yí-shān jí* [[KR4d0420]] in 40 *juàn*; the *Zhōng-zhōu jí* 中州集 (anthology of 109 Jīn poets); the *Zhōng-zhōu yuè-fǔ* 中州樂府 (*cí*-anthology); the *Xù Yí-jiān zhì* 續夷堅志 (continuation of Hóng Mài's *Yí-jiān zhì*) [[KR3l0026]]; the *Lùn-shī jué-jù* 論詩絕句 in 30 pieces (the foundational Jīn-period poetics document).
