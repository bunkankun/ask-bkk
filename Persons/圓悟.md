# Yuánwù 圓悟 (Mìyún Yuánwù 密雲圓悟)

Late-Míng LínjìYángqí Chán master. *Zì* Juéchū 覺初; *hào* Mìyún 密雲 ("Dense Clouds"); also Tiāntóng 天童 (for Tiāntóngsì 天童寺 in Míngzhōu 明州, where he was abbot), Jīnsù 金粟 (for Jīnsùsì 金粟寺 in Hǎiyán 海鹽). Imperial title *Huìdìng chánshī* 慧定禪師. Lay surname Jiǎng 蔣. Lifedates 1567/1/5 (Jiājìng 45 / 11/16) – 1642/8/2 (Chóngzhēn 15 / 7/7), age 76 (per the *Mìyún chánshī yǔlù* 密雲禪師語錄 juan 12 annalistic chart). Native of Yíxīng 宜興 (Jiāngsū).

The dominant mid-Míng LínjìYángqí Chán master, whose lineage-line came to constitute the most prolific branch of late-Míng / early-Qīng Chán. Dharma-heir of Huànyǒu Zhèngchuán 幻有正傳 of Lóngchí 龍池. Received *fù fǎ* 付法 in 1611; abbacies at Lóngchí 龍池, Tiāntóng 天童, Yùwáng 育王, Jīnsù 金粟, and Tōngxuánsì 通玄寺 (also in Jiāngsū). His dharma-heirs formed one of the most important teaching networks of seventeenth-century Chinese Buddhism, including [[法藏|Hànyuè Fǎzàng]] 漢月法藏 (of the controversial Sānfēng sub-lineage), Fèiyǐn Tōngróng 費隱通容, Mùchén Dàomín 木陳道忞 (spiritual advisor to the Shùnzhì emperor), Pòshān [[海明|Hǎimíng]] 破山海明 (the Sichuan Chán reviver), and many others.

Major works:
- *Mìyún chánshī yǔlù* 密雲禪師語錄 (12 juan, J10 B150) — his collected Chán sermons and annals.
- *Pì wàng jiù lüè shuō* 闢妄救略說 [[KR6q0168]] — his 1638 polemical rebuttal of Fǎzàng's *Wǔ zōng yuán*, central to the major seventeenth-century Chán controversy.
- Several shorter treatises and sermon collections.

Historical significance: the master-lineage of nearly every prominent early-Qīng Chán master traces back to Yuánwù; his polemic against Fǎzàng's *Wǔ zōng yuán* constituted the "official" pro-direct-transmission position that the Yōngzhèng emperor's 1733 *Jiǎn mó biàn yì lù* would codify as imperial orthodoxy.

# Yuánwù 圓悟 (Kūyá Yuánwù 枯崖圓悟)

Late-Southern-Sòng Línjì Chán master; *hào* Kūyá 枯崖 ("Dry Cliff"). Native of **Fúqīng** 福清 in Fúzhōu 福州 (modern Fujian). According to the prefatory matter of his work, he returned to **Jìngshān Méngtáng** 徑山蒙堂 in **Jǐngdìng** 景定 4 (1263), where he assembled the materials he had collected over years of monastic travel — accounts of awakening-experiences (*rùdào jīyuán* 入道機緣) of senior monks, sermon-transcripts (*shìzhòng fǎyǔ* 示眾法語), and fragmentary inscriptions of figures whose names had not yet been registered in the *dēnglù* 燈錄 lineage-records — into a 2-juan compendium titled **《枯崖漫錄》** *Kūyá mànlù* ([[KR6r0096]], X87 no. 1613). In **Xiánchún** 咸淳 8 (1272) he was installed as abbot of **Quánnán Xīngfúsì** 泉南興福寺 in his home prefecture.

Per DILA Buddhist Person Authority A008261. Distinct from the late-Míng Línjì master Mìyún Yuánwù 密雲圓悟 (1567–1642, DILA A003688) above; also distinct from **Yuánwù Kèqín** 圓悟克勤 (1063–1135, DILA A003687), the great Northern-Sòng Línjì master and author of the *Bìyán lù* 碧巖錄.

Works in the Kanripo corpus: [[KR6r0096]] *Kūyá mànlù*.
