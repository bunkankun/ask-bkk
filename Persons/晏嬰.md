Statesman of the Spring-and-Autumn state of Qí, posthumous title Píngzhòng 平仲 ("the Steady"). His native place is conventionally given as Lái 萊 (the eastern Shandong area, then a recently absorbed territory of Qí). He served three successive Qí rulers — Dukes Líng 靈, Zhuāng 莊, and Jǐng 景 — rising to prime minister under Duke Jǐng (r. 547–490 BCE) and is remembered in classical sources for his austerity, frugality, sharp-tongued remonstrance, and shrewd diplomacy. His death is conventionally dated to 500 BCE during the reign of Duke Jǐng. The *Yànzǐ chūnqiū* 晏子春秋 ([[KR2g0003]]) is traditionally attributed to him, but already in the Hàn it was recognized that the work is a posthumous compilation by later hands; modern scholarship treats it as a Warring-States anthology of stories and remonstrances ascribed to him. The *Shǐjì*'s *GuǎnYàn lièzhuàn* 管晏列傳 (juàn 62) pairs him with Guǎn Zhòng 管仲 as the two paradigmatic Qí ministers.
