# Dúgū Jí 獨孤及 (725–777)

*Zì* Zhì-zhī 至之; posthumous *shì* Xiàn 憲 (whence Xiàn-gōng 憲公). Native of *Hé-nán* 河南 *Luò-yáng* 洛陽 by family origin (the *Dúgū* clan, of *Xiānbēi* descent and prominent at the Northern Wèi and Sui-Táng courts). One of the four canonical *Tiān-bǎo* / *Dà-lì*-period *fù-gǔ* prose stylists, alongside Xiāo Yǐng-shì [[蕭穎士]], Lǐ Huá [[李華]], and Yuán Jié [[元結]] — collectively the bridge from the High-Tang court style to the *Yuán-Hé* 元和 *gǔ-wén* movement of Hán Yù [[韓愈]] and Liǔ Zōng-yuán [[柳宗元]].

*Jìn-shì* of *Tiān-bǎo* 13 (754). Successively *Hùa-yīn* 華陰 magistrate, *zuǒ shí-yí* 左拾遺 in the *Sù-zōng* and early *Dài-zōng* courts, and (after the An Lùshān rebellion) *Pò-zhōu cì-shǐ* 鄱州刺史, *Shū-zhōu cì-shǐ* 舒州刺史, and finally *Cháng-zhōu cì-shǐ* 常州刺史 (775–777) — the *Pí-líng* 毗陵 of his collection's title. Died in office at *Cháng-zhōu* in *Dà-lì* 12 (777).

His extant collection — the *Pí-líng jí* [[KR4c0034]] in 20 *juǎn* — was compiled at his death by his disciple Liáng Sù 梁肅 [[梁肅]] and is unusually well-preserved for a *Tiān-bǎo* / *Dà-lì* prose collection. His *Lùn shǐ* 論史 essays and *xù* prefaces (particularly to Huáng-fǔ Rǎn's collection at [[KR4c0016]]) are the principal contemporary critical writings on early-medieval historiography and literary biography.
