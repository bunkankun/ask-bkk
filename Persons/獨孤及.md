# Dúgū Jí 獨孤及 (725–777)

*Zì* Zhìzhī 至之; posthumous *shì* Xiàn 憲 (whence Xiàngōng 憲公). Native of *Hénán* 河南 *Luòyáng* 洛陽 by family origin (the *Dúgū* clan, of *Xiānbēi* descent and prominent at the Northern Wèi and Sui-Táng courts). One of the four canonical *Tiānbǎo* / *Dàlì*-period *fùgǔ* prose stylists, alongside Xiāo Yǐngshì [[蕭穎士]], Lǐ Huá [[李華]], and Yuán Jié [[元結]] — collectively the bridge from the High-Tang court style to the *Yuán Hé* 元和 *gǔwén* movement of Hán Yù [[韓愈]] and Liǔ Zōngyuán [[柳宗元]].

*Jìnshì* of *Tiānbǎo* 13 (754). Successively *Hùayīn* 華陰 magistrate, *zuǒ shíyí* 左拾遺 in the *Sùzōng* and early *Dàizōng* courts, and (after the An Lùshān rebellion) *Pòzhōu cìshǐ* 鄱州刺史, *Shūzhōu cìshǐ* 舒州刺史, and finally *Chángzhōu cìshǐ* 常州刺史 (775–777) — the *Pílíng* 毗陵 of his collection's title. Died in office at *Chángzhōu* in *Dàlì* 12 (777).

His extant collection — the *Pílíng jí* [[KR4c0034]] in 20 *juǎn* — was compiled at his death by his disciple Liáng Sù 梁肅 [[梁肅]] and is unusually well-preserved for a *Tiānbǎo* / *Dàlì* prose collection. His *Lùn shǐ* 論史 essays and *xù* prefaces (particularly to Huángfǔ Rǎn's collection at [[KR4c0016]]) are the principal contemporary critical writings on early-medieval historiography and literary biography.
