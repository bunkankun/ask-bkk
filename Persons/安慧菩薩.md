Ānhuì púsà 安慧菩薩 (Sthiramati, c. 510–570 CE), Indian Yogācāra master of the post-Vasubandhu generation, traditionally associated with Valabhī (modern Vala in Gujarat) and Nālandā. He is one of the "ten great commentators" of the Yogācāra-Vijñānavāda lineage canonised in Xuánzàng's *Chéng wéi-shì lùn* 成唯識論 ([[KR6n0005]]) and the principal commentator on Vasubandhu's *Triṃśikā*; his *Triṃśikā-bhāṣya* survives in Sanskrit as well as in Chinese (T1613) and Tibetan. He also wrote commentaries on the *Madhyāntavibhāga* (Skt. extant), the *Abhidharmakośa* (Tibetan only, *Tattvārthā*), and on most of the principal Mahāyāna *sūtra* texts.

His attribution as author of the Mādhyamaka commentary [[KR6m0005]] *Dàshèng zhōngguān shìlùn* 大乘中觀釋論 (T1567) is doctrinally unusual but accepted by both the Indic colophon-tradition (preserved in the Northern-Song translation) and modern scholarship; the work is read as a witness to the late-Indic *yogācāra-madhyamaka* synthesis that flourished at Nālandā in the 6th–7th centuries. The dates 510–570 are a scholarly reconstruction; primary sources do not give precise lifedates.

Works in the Kanripo corpus (attributed): [[KR6m0005]] *Dàshèng zhōng-guān shì-lùn* (T1567); commentaries on the Yogācāra-Vijñānavāda corpus in KR6n.
