Dà-yù-lóng púsà 大域龍菩薩 (\*Dignāga, "Direction-Elephant"; the more usual Chinese name is 陳那 Chénnà; conventionally c. 480–540 CE), Indian Buddhist Yogācāra master, founder of the Buddhist *pramāṇa* (epistemology) tradition. Per the standard Indic-Buddhist hagiography (the *Dìngāo púsà zhuàn* 提婆菩薩傳 / Tibetan biographies), Dignāga was a *brāhmaṇa*-born native of southern India (Kāñcī), Vasubandhu's disciple, who founded the Buddhist epistemological tradition with the *Pramāṇasamuccaya* and related works. He is also recognized as a major *prajñāpāramitā* commentator: his *Prajñāpāramitā-piṇḍārtha-saṃgraha* ("Compendium of Prajñāpāramitā Topics" = [[KR6c0018|T1518]]) is the foundational verse-summary of the *Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā* in the late-Indic Mahāyāna tradition.

The unusual Chinese name 大域龍 is a calque-rendering of *Dignāga* (*dik* = "direction, region" → 大域; *nāga* = "elephant, dragon" → 龍), distinct from the more usual transliteration 陳那 (Chen-na ≈ Skt *cīna* / *cinta*). The 大域龍 form is preserved in the Northern-Sòng Yìjīngyuàn signatures. Per DILA Buddhist Person Authority A001255.

Works in the Kanripo corpus: [[KR6c0018]] *Fó-mǔ bōrě bōluómìduō yuán-jí yào-yì lùn* (T1518); and the *Pramāṇasamuccaya*-related materials in KR6h.
