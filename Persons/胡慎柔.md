**Hú Shènróu** 胡慎柔 (d. 1635), late-Míng Buddhist-monk physician, pupil of Zhōu Shènzhāi 周慎齋. No CBDB record.

His principal medical legacy is the *Shènróu wǔ shū* 慎柔五書 ([[KR3eh037]]) — a five-part treatise on consumption (*xū-láo* 虛勞) compiled posthumously by his disciple Shí Zhèn 石震 from Hú's notebooks. The 1786 (*Qián-lóng bǐng-wǔ*) postface by Wáng Chénliáng 王陳梁 records the work's transmission and printing history.

The Hú-Zhōu medical doctrine — drawing the doctrinal line from Lǐ Dōngyuán 李東垣 (脾胃中心) through Xuē Jǐ 薛己 (溫補) and emphasising preservation of the spleen-and-stomach through gentle, food-based therapy as the foundation of treating consumption — is one of the most-cited Ming sources in the later Jiāngnán *xū-láo* literature.
