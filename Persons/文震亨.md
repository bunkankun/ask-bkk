Wén Zhèn-hēng 文震亨 (1585–1645), *zì* Qǐ-měi 啟美. Native of Cháng-zhōu 長洲 (Sūzhōu), Jiāngsū. Great-grandson of Wén Zhēng-míng 文徵明 (1470–1559), the dominant Wú-school painter and calligrapher of the mid-Míng; grandson of Wén Péng 文彭 (1498–1573); son of Wén Cóng-jiǎn 文從簡 (1574–1648). Younger brother of Wén Zhèn-mèng 文震孟 (1574–1636, Grand Secretary under *Chóng-zhēn*). *Gòng-shēng* of the early *Tiān-qǐ* period; held office as *Wǔ-yīng diàn zhōng-shū shè-rén* (Secretariat Drafter at the Wǔ-yīng Hall) under *Chóng-zhēn*, recruited for his skill in *qín*-playing.

A painter, calligrapher, *qín* musician, garden designer, and the definitive theorist of the Sūzhōu literati lifestyle through his *Cháng wù zhì* 長物志 ([[KR3j0174]]). On the Manchu entry into Sūzhōu in 1645, he attempted suicide by drowning; rescued, he refused food for six days and died. His martyr's death — together with his work as the codifier of the Sūzhōu literati aesthetic — has made him an emblematic figure of the late-Míng / Sūzhōu cultural world.

Extensively treated in Craig Clunas, *Superfluous Things* (Polity, 1991/2004), and in the *Dictionary of Ming Biography* under Wén Zhēng-míng (lineage entry).
