The **ninth patriarch of the Tiāntái 天台 school** and the principal architect of the mid-Táng Tiāntái revival; one of the central scholastic figures of medieval Chinese Buddhism. DILA Authority A001307. Lay surname **Qī** 戚; native of **Cháng-zhōu** 常州 (modern Jiāngsū). Honorifics: **Jīngxī zūn-zhě** 荊溪尊者 (after his Jīngxī residence in his teacher's lifetime), **Yuán-tōng zūn-zhě** 圓通尊者, **Miàolè dàshī** 妙樂大師, **Jì-zhǔ fǎshī** 記主法師 ("Master of the [Sub-]Commentaries"). Born 711, died at the Fó-lǒng 佛隴 retreat at Tiāntái-shān on the 5th day of the 2nd month of Jiànzhōng 建中 3 (= 27 March 782), aged 72, with 43 years of monastic seniority.

At seventeen he received Tiāntái *zhǐ-guān* 止觀 instruction from **Fāng-yán** 方巖 of **Jīnhuá** 金華; at twenty he entered the school of **Xuán-lǎng** 玄朗 (673–754) of **Zuǒxī** 左溪, the eighth Tiāntái patriarch, under whom he completed his Tiāntái formation. He took monastic ordination only at age 38 at the Jìng-lè-sì 淨樂寺 in Yíxīng 宜興, and subsequently studied *vinaya* with **Tán-yī** 曇一 at **Yuèzhōu** 越州. After Xuán-lǎng's death in 754 he was acknowledged as Tiāntái patriarch and spent his mature years at the **Guóqīng-sì** 國清寺 on Tiāntái-shān.

Zhànrán's career falls in the immediate aftermath of the An Lushan rebellion (755–763), during which Tiāntái-shān was largely cut off from imperial patronage. His central project was the systematic *commentarial recovery* of the Zhìyǐ corpus: in particular he produced the three great Tiāntái subcommentaries — the **Fǎ-huá xuán-yì shì-qiān** 法華玄義釋籤 ([[KR6d0007]], T1717) on Zhìyǐ's *Xuán-yì* ([[KR6d0006]]), the **Fǎ-huá wén-jù jì** 法華文句記 (T1719) on Zhìyǐ's *Wén-jù*, and the **Zhǐ-guān fǔ-xíng zhuàn-hóng-jué** 止觀輔行傳弘決 (T1912) on Zhìyǐ's *Móhē zhǐguān* — which became the standard interpretive apparatus for the Tiāntái scholastic tradition.

His independent works include the **Jīn-gāng-bēi** 金剛錍 (T1932), the **Shí-bù-èr-mén** 十不二門 (T1927), and the **Zhǐ-guān dà-yì** 止觀大意 (T1914), which together developed the doctrine of the **buddha-nature of insentient beings** (*wú-qíng yǒu xìng* 無情有性) that became one of Tiāntái's most distinctive theoretical commitments — and the principal point of contention in subsequent Sòng-period *shān-jiā / shān-wài* 山家山外 polemics.

Through his disciple **Xíng-mǎn** 行滿 (whose successor **Dào-suì** 道邃 was the Chinese teacher of the Japanese Tendai founder **Saichō** 最澄, 767–822), Zhànrán's mature Tiāntái synthesis was directly transmitted to Japan and became the foundational doctrinal apparatus of the Hieizan establishment.
