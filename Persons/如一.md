# Rú-yī 如一 (Jí-fēi Rú-yī 即非如一)

Major Míng-Qīng transitional Línjì-Yángqí Chán master and **co-founder of the Ōbaku-shū** 黃檗宗 (the third school of Japanese Zen). *Hào* Jí-fēi 即非 ("Instantly-Not"); also Guǎng-shòu Jí-fēi 廣壽即非, Xuě-fēng Jí-fēi 雪峰即非. Lay surname Lín 林 (descended from the Sòng scholar-official Lín Xī-yì 林希逸). Native of Fú-qīng 福清 (Fú-jiàn). Lifedates 1616/6/27 – 1671/6/26 (Wànlì 44/5/14 – Kāngxī 10/5/20, age 56, *sēnglà* 40, per the *xíng yè jì* in *Jí-fēi quán lù* juan 25).

Tonsured at 17 under Xī-lái Hào 西來灝 at Lóng-shān-sì 龍山寺; received full precepts at 20. Studied under [[明方|Shí-yǔ Míng-fāng]] 石雨明方 and other masters before finally receiving dharma-transmission from **Yǐn-yuán Lóng-qí** 隱元隆琦 (1592–1673) at Huáng-bò-shān 黃檗山. Abbot at **Xuě-fēng Chóng-shèng-sì** 雪峰崇聖寺 in Fú-jiàn.

**Japan emigration (1657)**: Crossed to Japan at age 42 as one of Yǐn-yuán's senior lieutenants in the Ōbaku emigration-project. Abbot at Nagasaki Shèng-shòu-shān 聖壽山, Chóng-fú-sì 崇福寺; later Guǎng-shòu-sì 廣壽寺 in Bungo domain. Died at Nagasaki in 1671.

Principal surviving work: the *Jí-fēi chánshī quán lù* 即非禪師全錄 [[KR6q0231]] in 25 juan. Dharma-heirs include Xìng-tōng 性統 and other Japan-based Ōbaku masters.

Standard study: Baroni, Helen J. 2000. *Obaku Zen: The Emergence of the Third Sect of Zen in Tokugawa Japan*. University of Hawai'i Press.
