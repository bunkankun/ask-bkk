# Rúyī 如一 (Jífēi Rúyī 即非如一)

Major Míng-Qīng transitional Línjì-Yángqí Chán master and **co-founder of the Ōbakushū** 黃檗宗 (the third school of Japanese Zen). *Hào* Jífēi 即非 ("Instantly-Not"); also Guǎngshòu Jífēi 廣壽即非, Xuěfēng Jífēi 雪峰即非. Lay surname Lín 林 (descended from the Sòng scholar-official Lín Xīyì 林希逸). Native of Fúqīng 福清 (Fújiàn). Lifedates 1616/6/27 – 1671/6/26 (Wànlì 44/5/14 – Kāngxī 10/5/20, age 56, *sēnglà* 40, per the *xíng yè jì* in *Jífēi quán lù* juan 25).

Tonsured at 17 under Xīlái Hào 西來灝 at Lóngshānsì 龍山寺; received full precepts at 20. Studied under [[明方|Shíyǔ Míngfāng]] 石雨明方 and other masters before finally receiving dharma-transmission from **Yǐnyuán Lóngqí** 隱元隆琦 (1592–1673) at Huángbòshān 黃檗山. Abbot at **Xuěfēng Chóngshèngsì** 雪峰崇聖寺 in Fújiàn.

**Japan emigration (1657)**: Crossed to Japan at age 42 as one of Yǐnyuán's senior lieutenants in the Ōbaku emigration-project. Abbot at Nagasaki Shèngshòushān 聖壽山, Chóngfúsì 崇福寺; later Guǎngshòusì 廣壽寺 in Bungo domain. Died at Nagasaki in 1671.

Principal surviving work: the *Jífēi chánshī quán lù* 即非禪師全錄 [[KR6q0231]] in 25 juan. Dharma-heirs include Xìngtōng 性統 and other Japan-based Ōbaku masters.

Standard study: Baroni, Helen J. 2000. *Obaku Zen: The Emergence of the Third Sect of Zen in Tokugawa Japan*. University of Hawai'i Press.
