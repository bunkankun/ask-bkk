Hú Yīn 胡愔 (*hào* Jiàn-sù zǐ 見素子, "Master of Seeing the Simple") was a mid-ninth-century female Daoist active at Tàibái shān 太白山 in Shǎnxī. Her sole surviving work is the short illustrated treatise *Huángtíng nèijǐng wǔzàng liùfǔ bǔxiè tú* 黃庭內景五臟六腑補瀉圖 [[KR5b0116]] (DZ 432), which carries an authorial preface dated to the 848 *wùchén* year. The treatise — one of the most important Táng documents in Daoist medical-physiological theory — combines the visceral pantheon of the *Huángtíng nèijǐng* [[KR5b0015]] with specific methods for restoring imbalance through *qì*-regulation and diet.

Hú Yīn is one of the small number of named women Daoist authors of the Táng. No CBDB record; beyond her signed work nothing biographical is preserved. Despeux, *Immortelles de la Chine ancienne* (1990), discusses her place within the broader Táng female Daoist *fāngshù* tradition.
