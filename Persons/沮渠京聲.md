Jūqú Jīngshēng 沮渠京聲 (also written 且渠京聲; with the inherited title 安陽侯 / 沮渠安陽矦, "Marquis of Ānyáng"; DILA Authority A000751; d. 464) was a layman-translator of distinctive aristocratic background. He was the son of Jūqú Méngxùn 沮渠蒙遜 (r. 401–433), the Xiōngnú-descended ruler of the Northern Liáng 北涼 in modern Gānsù, and brother of the last Northern-Liáng emperor Jūqú Mùjiàn 沮渠牧犍 (r. 433–439). When the Northern Wèi 北魏 conquered the Northern Liáng in 439, Jūqú Jīngshēng fled south to the Liú-Sòng 劉宋, where he was patronised by Sòng Wén-dì 文帝 (r. 424–453) and his successors. He worked at Jiànkāng as a lay translator and editor, drawing on the Sanskrit manuscripts that Northern-Liáng scholarship had accumulated under Dharmakṣema's patronage.

His translations include the [[KR6a0089|*Bāguānzhāi jīng* 八關齋經]] (T89), the *Chánmì yào jīng* 禪秘要經 (T613), the *Zhìchánbìng mìyào jīng* 治禪病祕要經 (T620), the *Wùfó shénzhòu jīng* 五佛神咒經 (cf. T1043) and several other shorter works, mostly translated or revised from material brought from Northern Liáng. He died at Jiànkāng on 26 January – 12 July 464 CE (Dàmíng 大明 8) during the reign of Sòng Xiào-Wǔdì.
