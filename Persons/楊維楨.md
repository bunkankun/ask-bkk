The dominant literary figure of the late Yuán southeast. Surname **Yáng**; given name written variously as **維楨** or **維禎** (the *Sìkù* preferred 維禎; the SBCK preserves 維楨); style-name **Liánfū** 廉夫; sobriquets **Tiěyá** 鐵崖 ("Iron Cliff", from his early teacher's "iron cliff" tower in his hometown), **Tiědí dàorén** 鐵笛道人 ("Iron-Flute Daoist"), **Dōngwéi zǐ** 東維子 ("Master of the Eastern Hinge"). Born in *Guǐsì* 癸巳 of the Yuán *Dà-dé* era (1296) at Zhū-jì 諸暨 (Kuàijī, modern Zhèjiāng). He passed the Yuán *Tài-dìng* 4 (1327) *jìnshì*; held various posts including *Hángzhōu lù sī-zhèng*, *Tiāntái yǐn*, *Jiànchá yùshǐ*, *Jiāng-nán xíng-shū-mì-yuàn jīng-lì*. He resigned in the late Zhì-zhèng era and spent the last decades of his life in retirement at Sōngjiāng (Huá-tíng), where he led the **Tiěyá yuèfǔ** 鐵崖樂府 movement — the dominant poetic school of the late Yuán. In *Hóngwǔ* 2 (1369) the Míng Tài-zǔ summoned him to assist with the *Yuán shǐ* and ritual-book compilations; he came to court, served briefly, and refused permanent office, returning home and dying in *Hóngwǔ* 3 (1370). His participation in the Míng *Yuán shǐ* project is the basis for his complicated *yílǎo* status — the *Sìkù* compilers place him in the intermediate moral rank one below **Dīng Hènián** 丁鶴年 ([[KR4d0557]]) and one above those who took Míng office for advancement.

Major collections in the SKQS: *Dōng-wéi zǐ jí* 東維子集 ([[KR4d0585]]); *Tiěyá gǔ yuèfǔ* 鐵崖古樂府 ([[KR4d0586]]); *Fù-gǔ shī jí* 復古詩集 ([[KR4d0587]]); *Lìzé yíyīn* 麗則遺音 ([[KR4d0589]]). The CBDB record (id 12087) writes the name 楊維禎 and gives 1296–1370.

His literary circle is the principal node of late-Yuán Wú-zhōng literary culture: he is named or addressed in most of the late-Yuán Wú-zhōng *bié-jí* in this batch — including [[KR4d0548]] (Guō Yì), [[KR4d0561]] (Xiè Yìngfāng), [[KR4d0573]] (Yáng Hé), [[KR4d0578]] (Lǚ Chéng), [[KR4d0574]] (Gù Yīng's *Yùshān*), [[KR4d0575]] [[KR4d0576]] (Ní Zàn), and [[KR4d0569]] (Dài Liáng).
