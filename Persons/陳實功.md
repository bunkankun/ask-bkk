**Chén Shígōng** 陳實功 (*zì* Yùrén 毓仁; *hào* Ruòxū 若虛, c. 1555 – c. 1636, 明), late-Míng surgical physician of Dōnghǎi 東海 / Nántōng 南通 (Jiāngsū). Author of [[KR3ek014]] ***Wàikē zhèngzōng*** 外科正宗 (4 *juǎn*, 1617), the most influential Míng-period surgical text and the founding work of the so-called **正宗派** — the late-Míng surgical school that embraced active operative intervention with knife and cautery, in deliberate contrast to the later Qīng *quánshēng pài* of Wáng Hóngxù [[王洪緒]] [[KR3ek017]] which would reject such methods. Chén's clinical descriptions of nasal-polyp excision, cleft-lip repair, anal-fistula seton ligation, and breast carcinoma (*rǔyán* 乳岩) are among the most detailed pre-modern Chinese surgical operative records. The work's *wǔ jiè shí yào* 五戒十要 (five prohibitions and ten essentials) integrates ethical reflection with Buddhist karma-and-illness doctrine. Lifedates 1555–1636 are an inference from his 1617 statement that he had practised for over forty years with white hair; no firm documentary evidence. Not in CBDB.
