Huìguāng 慧光 / 惠光 (active 8th century), *hào* Dà-zhào jūshì 大照居士 ("Great Illumination Lay Practitioner"), Táng-dynasty lay Buddhist practitioner in the Chán tradition. Native of Yōngzhōu Chāng'ān 雍州長安 (the Táng capital, modern Xī'ān), lay surname Lǐ 李. Studied under two major early-Chán teachers whose identities are somewhat tentative: "Ān 安" (most plausibly identified as Lǎo'ān / Sōngshān Huìān 嵩山慧安, c. 582–709, a dharma-heir of Hóngrěn in the Dōngshān line) and "Huì 會" (most plausibly Hézé Shénhuì 荷澤神會, 684–758, the major Southern-School polemicist).

The dual-teacher pattern — with both a senior Northern-School-affiliated master and the leading Southern-School polemicist in the same practitioner's career — places Huìguāng uniquely at the Northern-Southern school transition of mid-8th century Chāng'ān and Luòyáng.

Only one work securely attributed: [[KR6q0107]] *Dàshèng kāi xīn xiǎn xìng dùn wù zhēn zōng lùn* 大乘開心顯性頓悟真宗論 (1 juan, T85 n2835, Dūnhuáng manuscript recension). No DILA authority entry under this specific identity; distinct from several other Chinese Buddhist figures of the names 慧光 / 惠光.

No biographical material survives beyond the third-person self-description in the *Zhēn zōng lùn*'s own preface.

Huìguāng 慧光 (469–538), styled *Guāngtǒng lǜshī* 光統律師 ("Vinaya Master Guāngtǒng"), *Shèng shāmí* 聖沙彌 ("Saintly Novice"), and *Dàjué Huìguāng* 大覺慧光 ("Huìguāng of Dàjué [Monastery]"). Lay surname Yáng 楊, native of Chánglú 長蘆 in the lower Hé-běi region. The most consequential Buddhist scholar-monk of the early sixth century in the northern dynasties: regarded by tradition as the "founder of the southern lineage" of the *Dìlùn* 地論宗 school (Dìlùn nán-dào pài 地論宗南道派) and a principal early systematizer of the *Sìfēn lǜ* 四分律 (Dharmaguptaka *Vinaya*) tradition in China.

Per the *Xù gāosēng zhuàn* 續高僧傳 (T2060, juan 21) and DILA A006354: at the age of thirteen Huìguāng accompanied his father to Luòyáng, where he was ordained under [[佛陀扇多]] Buddhaśānta — the famous *dhyāna* master and translator who, with Bodhiruci 菩提流支, would shortly translate Vasubandhu's *Daśabhūmika-vyākhyāna*. So precocious in scriptural learning was the young Huìguāng that he was popularly nicknamed *Shèng shāmí* "Saintly Novice." When in *Yǒngpíng* 永平 1 (508 CE) Lènà Mótí 勒那摩提 and Bodhiruci each produced separate translations of Vasubandhu's *Daśabhūmika*-commentary, Huìguāng — having access to the Sanskrit (or BHS) original — collated the two versions, identified the points of agreement and divergence, and produced a unified Chinese recension; this collation effectively founded the *Dìlùn* 地論 school as a distinct tradition. He subsequently composed the *Sìfēn lǜ shū* 四分律疏, the *Sìfēn lǜ jiè běn shū* 四分戒本疏, and other Vinaya commentaries that decisively established the *Sìfēn lǜ* as the dominant Vinaya tradition in northern China — the lineage from which Daoxuan 道宣 and the Tang 律宗 (*Lǜ-zōng*) would later descend.

Huìguāng's exegetical activity also included the very early *Huāyán jīng yì jì* 華嚴經義記 ([[KR6e0008|T2756]], one fascicle preserved at Dūnhuáng), which is one of the earliest known Chinese commentaries on the *Avataṃsaka* and a direct doctrinal antecedent to the later Huáyán school of Zhìyǎn and Fǎzàng. He died on the 14th of the 3rd month of *Yuán-xiàng* 元象 1 (538/4/30) at the Dàjué-sì 大覺寺 in Yè 鄴 (the Eastern Wèi capital, modern Lín-zhāng, Héběi), age 70. His students Fǎshàng 法上, Sēngfàn 僧範, and Dàopíng 道憑 carried his legacy forward into the late Northern Qí and early Suí; through Sēngfàn's lineage the *Dìlùn* tradition descended ultimately to the Suí-Tang Huáyán synthesis.
