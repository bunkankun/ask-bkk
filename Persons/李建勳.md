**Lǐ Jiàn-xūn** 李建勳 (873?–952), of *Lǒng-xī* 隴西 (the *jùn-wàng* / ancestral-clan designation; his actual residence was *Jīn-líng* / Nán-jīng). Foundational poet of the early **Southern-Táng** 南唐 court and the dynasty's first-generation chancellor. CBDB id 92981 confirms 873?–952.

His career began in the *Wú* state (the late-Táng successor regime in the *Jiāng-Huái* region under Yáng Hàng-mì 楊行密 and successors). With the Wú-Southern-Táng transition under Lǐ Biàn 李昪 (Liè-zǔ, r. 937–943), Lǐ Jiàn-xūn rose to *Tóng zhōng-shū mén-xià píng-zhāng-shì* (chancellor) in the early *Shēng-yuán* reign. He was retired with senior-honor titles in the early 940s and lived out his final years at *Jīn-líng* in poetic retirement, dying in 952 at the start of the *Bǎo-dà* reign of Lǐ Jǐng 李璟. The collection's title — *Lǐ chéng-xiàng* (Chancellor Lǐ) — refers to this terminal office.

His poetry is preserved in the two-*juǎn* fragmentary [[KR4c0114]] *Lǐ chéng-xiàng shī-jí* (SBCK reprint of the late-Sòng *Lín-ān Chén-zhái* cutting). Approximately 85 poems survive of an original 30-*juǎn* corpus (per *Sòng-shǐ yì-wén-zhì*). His addressees document the early-Southern-Táng poetic community — Sūn Fāng 孫魴, Pī Bīn 沈彬, *zhèng-shì xué-shì* and other Southern-Táng courtiers; his style — calmer and more meditative than Wéi Zhuāng [[韋莊]] and less imagistically dramatic than Guàn-xiū [[釋貫休]] or Qí-jǐ [[釋齊己]] — defines a transitional voice between late-Táng and Sòng *shī*. A small body of *cí* is also attributed to him (preserved in the *Quán Táng-Wǔ-dài cí*).
