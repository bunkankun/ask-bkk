Late-Míng / early-Qīng official, antiquarian, and connoisseur of calligraphy and painting; native of Yìdū 益都 (registered as Dàxīng in the capital region). *Zì* Ěrbó 耳伯; *hào* Běihǎi 北海 and Tuìgǔ 退谷. Took the *jìnshì* in Chóngzhēn 4 (1631); served the Míng as Censor (Yùshǐ); served the Shùn (Lǐ Zìchéng's regime) briefly in 1644, then transferred to the new Qīng with the Manchu entry, ultimately rising to Vice-Minister of Personnel under Shùnzhì. Withdrew from office and devoted the last decades of his long life to scholarship at his Beijing studio Tuìgǔ 退谷. His enormous corpus—the *Gēngzǐ xiāoxià jì* 庚子銷夏記 (a foundational work of Chinese painting connoisseurship), the *Yuáncháo diǎngù biānnián kǎo* 元朝典故編年考 ([[KR2m0019]]), the *Tiānfǔ guǎngjì* 天府廣記, *Chūnmíng mèngyú lù* 春明夢餘錄, and many shorter works—covers institutional history, art history, classical commentary, and capital-region topography. His reputation suffered for his service under both the Shùn and the Qīng but his scholarship is unanimously praised. CBDB confirms 1592–1676.
