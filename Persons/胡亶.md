# Hú Dǎn 胡亶

Sobriquet *Lìzhāi* 勵齋. Native of Rénhé 仁和 (Hángzhōu prefecture, Zhèjiāng). Birth and death years not securely recorded; flourished in the early Kāngxī period (1660s-1670s).

A private scholar with a particular specialty in observational astronomy. Wáng Zhuó's 王晫 *Jīnshì shuō* 今世説 (a Kāngxī-period biographical compendium modeled on the *Shìshuō xīnyǔ*) describes him as "*broadly mastering the various books, especially refined in the *Tiānguānjiā* (Celestial-Officers School) discourse — for solar-and-lunar eclipses, stellar positions, computation-and-prediction, [he was] without the slightest omission. In Chángān [Beijing] he debated repeatedly with the Western specialists at the [*Qīntiānjiān*] Bureau; the multitude all sighed in admiration*". His direct astronomical confrontations with the Beijing Jesuit Bureau staff (presumably Verbiest 南懷仁 and his assistants in the late 1660s) were apparently substantive and impressive enough to circulate in early-Kāngxī biographical literature.

Of his works only the *Zhōngxīng pǔ* 中星譜 ([[KR3f0022]], 1 *juàn*, completed Kāngxī 8 / 1669) survives in the *Sìkù*; the *Sìkù* 提要 records his lost works as the *Zhōutiān xiànjiè tú* 周天現界圖 (Diagram of the Celestial Boundaries) and the *Bùtiān gē* 步天歌 (Step-the-Heavens Song — presumably his own version of the long-standing *Bùtiān gē* mnemonic-poem tradition).
