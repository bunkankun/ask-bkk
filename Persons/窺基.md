The **founding systematizer of the Chinese Fǎ-xiàng** 法相 (= **Cí'ēn-zōng** 慈恩宗) Yogācāra-Vijñaptimātra school. Lay surname **Yù-chí** 尉遲 (alternate persona-name **Yù-chí Jī** 尉遲基); native of **Cháng'ān**. Born **632 CE**; died **682 CE**, age 51, at **Dà-cí'ēn-sì** 大慈恩寺.

He was the **nephew** of the famous Tang general **Yùchí Jìngdé** 尉遲敬德 (585–658) — making him one of the few Chinese-Buddhist patriarchs of high-aristocratic background. He took ordination at age 17 (in **Zhēnguān** 22 = **648**) at the imperial command of **Tàizōng** as a member of the imperial-translation-bureau ordinand cohort prepared for **Xuánzàng's** 玄奘 (596–664) just-returned translation team.

He became Xuánzàng's **principal Chinese disciple** and the central figure of the post-Xuán-zàng Yogācāra-school institutional consolidation. After Xuánzàng's death in 664, Kuījī continued the Yogācāra translation-and-commentarial work at **Dàcí'ēnsì** 大慈恩寺 (the imperial monastery established by Tàizōng for Xuánzàng's translation bureau), establishing the Chinese **Fǎxiàng** school's distinctive doctrinal-systematic framework.

His scholarly output is enormous — he is credited with **over 100 works** in the standard hagiographies, ranging across the Yogācāra-school canonical commentaries, the *Vajracchedikā* commentary [[KR6c0039]], the *Saddharmapuṇḍarīka* commentary, and many further works. His major doctrinal-systematic works include:

- **Chéng wéishí lùn shùjì** 成唯識論述記 (T1830, 20 juan) — the principal commentary on Xuánzàng's *Cheng wei-shi lun* (the foundational Chinese Yogācāra synthesis-treatise).
- **Chéng wéishí lùn shūyào** 成唯識論掌中樞要 (T1831, 4 juan) — abbreviated commentary on the same.
- **Yīnmíng rù zhènglǐ lùn shū** 因明入正理論疏 (T1840, 6 juan) — the principal Chinese commentary on Buddhist logic.
- **Dàshèng fǎyuàn yìlín zhāng** 大乘法苑義林章 (T1861, 7 juan) — comprehensive Mahāyāna doctrinal-systematic treatise.

The Chinese Fǎxiàng / Cí'ēnzōng school takes its second name (*Cí'ēnzōng*) from his Dàcí'ēnsì residence, and **Kuījī's** 窺基 doctrinal works are foundational for the school's transmission into Tang and beyond. Through the Yogācāra transmission to **Korea** (via **Wǒn-cheuk** 圓測, his elder fellow disciple) and **Japan** (via the Tang-pilgrim importations and the establishment of the **Hossō** 法相 school at Kōfuku-ji and Hōryū-ji), Kuījī's doctrinal legacy spread across all three of the East Asian Buddhist traditions.
