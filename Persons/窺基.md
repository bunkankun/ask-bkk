The **founding systematizer of the Chinese Fǎ-xiàng** 法相 (= **Cí'ēn-zōng** 慈恩宗) Yogācāra-Vijñaptimātra school. Lay surname **Yù-chí** 尉遲 (alternate persona-name **Yù-chí Jī** 尉遲基); native of **Cháng'ān**. Born **632 CE**; died **682 CE**, age 51, at **Dà-cí'ēn-sì** 大慈恩寺.

He was the **nephew** of the famous Tang general **Yù-chí Jìng-dé** 尉遲敬德 (585–658) — making him one of the few Chinese-Buddhist patriarchs of high-aristocratic background. He took ordination at age 17 (in **Zhēn-guān** 22 = **648**) at the imperial command of **Tài-zōng** as a member of the imperial-translation-bureau ordinand cohort prepared for **Xuán-zàng's** 玄奘 (596–664) just-returned translation team.

He became Xuán-zàng's **principal Chinese disciple** and the central figure of the post-Xuán-zàng Yogācāra-school institutional consolidation. After Xuán-zàng's death in 664, Kuī-jī continued the Yogācāra translation-and-commentarial work at **Dà-cí'ēn-sì** 大慈恩寺 (the imperial monastery established by Tài-zōng for Xuán-zàng's translation bureau), establishing the Chinese **Fǎ-xiàng** school's distinctive doctrinal-systematic framework.

His scholarly output is enormous — he is credited with **over 100 works** in the standard hagiographies, ranging across the Yogācāra-school canonical commentaries, the *Vajracchedikā* commentary [[KR6c0039]], the *Saddharmapuṇḍarīka* commentary, and many further works. His major doctrinal-systematic works include:

- **Chéng wéi-shí lùn shù-jì** 成唯識論述記 (T1830, 20 juan) — the principal commentary on Xuán-zàng's *Cheng wei-shi lun* (the foundational Chinese Yogācāra synthesis-treatise).
- **Chéng wéi-shí lùn shū-yào** 成唯識論掌中樞要 (T1831, 4 juan) — abbreviated commentary on the same.
- **Yīn-míng rù zhèng-lǐ lùn shū** 因明入正理論疏 (T1840, 6 juan) — the principal Chinese commentary on Buddhist logic.
- **Dà-shèng fǎ-yuàn yì-lín zhāng** 大乘法苑義林章 (T1861, 7 juan) — comprehensive Mahāyāna doctrinal-systematic treatise.

The Chinese Fǎ-xiàng / Cí'ēn-zōng school takes its second name (*Cí'ēn-zōng*) from his Dà-cí'ēn-sì residence, and **Kuī-jī's** 窺基 doctrinal works are foundational for the school's transmission into Tang and beyond. Through the Yogācāra transmission to **Korea** (via **Wǒn-cheuk** 圓測, his elder fellow disciple) and **Japan** (via the Tang-pilgrim importations and the establishment of the **Hossō** 法相 school at Kōfuku-ji and Hōryū-ji), Kuī-jī's doctrinal legacy spread across all three of the East Asian Buddhist traditions.
