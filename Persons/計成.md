Late-Míng professional garden designer, painter and rock-stacker, the most documented figure in his profession; *zì* Wú-pǐ 無否 ("Not-No"), *hào* Pǐ-dào rén 否道人 ("Master Pǐ"), of Wú-jiāng 吳江 in Sūzhōu prefecture. Born 1582 (CBDB); death-date uncertain — alive in *Chóng-zhēn jiǎ-xū* (1634, postface signed at age 53). Originally famed as a painter in the manner of the Five-Dynasties masters Guān Tóng 關仝 and Jīng Hào 荊浩; travelled in Yān 燕 (Hé-běi) and Chǔ 楚 (Hú-běi/Hú-nán) lands in youth; returned to Wú in middle age and settled at Rùn-zhōu 潤州 (Zhèn-jiāng). Designed the 15-*mǔ* garden of the provincial intendant Wú Yòu-yú 吳又予 in Jīn-líng (Nán-jīng) on the old site of the Yuán-dynasty 溫相 *gù-yuán* east of the city, and the *Luán-jiāng xī-zhù* of the *zhōng-hàn* (court-secretary) Wāng Shì-héng 汪士衡. Father of *Cháng-shēng* 長生 and *Cháng-jí* 長吉*, named in the postface. Author of [[KR3h0090]] *Yuán-yě* 園冶 (drafted 1631, published 1634), the single most important Chinese theoretical treatise on garden design — the foundational text of the Chinese tradition of garden criticism, with three canonical concepts: *yīn* 因 (working-with-site), *jiè* 借 (borrowing-views), and *tǐ-yí* 體宜 (appropriate-scale).
