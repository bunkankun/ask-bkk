**Zhào Jí** 趙佶 (1082–1135), posthumously **Sòng Huīzōng** 宋徽宗, eighth emperor of the Sòng (r. 1100–1126). Born the eleventh son of Shénzōng, ascended on the death of his elder half-brother Zhézōng. The most artistically and intellectually accomplished of all Sòng emperors: painter (founder of the *Xuānhé* 宣和 court painting style and codifier of court painting standards), calligrapher (inventor of the *Shòu-jīn* 瘦金 "slender gold" script), connoisseur (compiler of the *Xuānhé huà-pǔ* 宣和畫譜 and *Xuānhé shū-pǔ* 宣和書譜), poet, and Daoist religious patron (self-titled *Dàojūn huángdì* 道君皇帝 "Daoist-Lord Emperor"). His political reign coincided with the rise of the *Xīn-fǎ* 新法 faction under Cài Jīng 蔡京, leading to massive court patronage and corresponding fiscal crisis, and ended catastrophically with the 1125–1126 Jurchen invasion. Captured at the *Jìngkāng* fall of Kāifēng (1126–1127) and exiled to the far north, he died in captivity in 1135.

Medically, Huīzōng was the most personally engaged of Chinese imperial patrons. He authored the *Shèngjì jīng* 聖濟經 (42 chapters of theoretical medicine, attached as preface to the *Shèngjì zǒnglù*) and personally commissioned the *Shèngjì zǒnglù* 聖濟總錄 in 200 *juǎn* — the largest single Chinese medical compilation ([[KR3ed012]]). He established formal *Yī-xué* 醫學 (medical academies) at the prefectural level and patronised the Daoist physiological-medical synthesis. He was also a connoisseur of *bencao*, materia medica, and *cù-jì* 蹴鞠 (a traditional ball-game). CBDB id 6313 (1082–1135). *Sòngshǐ* j. 19–22. The principal modern study in English is Patricia Ebrey, *Emperor Huizong* (Harvard, 2014).
