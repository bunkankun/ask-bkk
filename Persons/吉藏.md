Jí-zàng 吉藏 (549–623 CE), Suí-Tang Chinese Buddhist master, the consolidator of the Sān-lùn 三論 (Three-Treatise / Mādhyamaka) school in China. According to the *Xù gāosēng zhuàn* 續高僧傳 (T2060, juan 11), Jí-zàng was of Parthian descent (his great-grandfather had migrated from An-xi 安息) but native to the lower-Yangzi region; he became a monk under Fǎ-lǎng 法朗 at the Xìng-huáng-sì 興皇寺 in Jiànkāng (the Sān-lùn lineage descending from Sēng-rúi 僧叡 → Tán-qìng 曇慶 → Fǎ-lǎng → Jí-zàng) and rose to become the principal Sān-lùn teacher of his generation, abbot of the Jiā-xiáng-sì 嘉祥寺 in Kuàijī (whence his common epithet 嘉祥大師 "Great Master of Jiā-xiáng"); under Suí Yáng-dì he was honoured as one of the Four Teachers of the Imperial Lectures (四道場主).

His commentarial output is enormous and includes commentaries on virtually every major Mahāyāna sūtra: the *Lotus*, the *Vimalakīrti*, the *Mahāparinirvāṇa*, the *Sukhāvatīvyūha*, the three Mādhyamika treatises, and most of the *Prajñāpāramitā* corpus. He is the principal Sān-lùn doctrinal author and the figure through whom Mādhyamika became a fully-developed Chinese-language doctrinal system. Per DILA Buddhist Person Authority A001067.

Works in the Kanripo corpus: numerous including [[KR6c0007]] *Dà-pǐn jīng yóu-yì* (T1696); [[KR6c0009]] *Dà-pǐn jīng yìshū* (X451); commentaries on the Lotus, Vimalakīrti, and Mādhyamika treatises in KR6h; and many others.
