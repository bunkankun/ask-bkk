# Qīng-liǎo 清了

Southern-Sòng Cáo-dòng 曹洞 Chán master, *hào* Zhēn-xiē 真歇 (also Jì-ān 寂庵); posthumous title Wù-kōng chánshī 悟空禪師. Dàjiàn-下第 14 世 dharma-heir of [[子淳]] Dān-xiá Zǐ-chún (1066–1119) — and thus, alongside his dharma-brother **Hóng-zhì Zhèng-jué** 宏智正覺 (1091–1157), the twin apex of Southern-Sòng Cáo-dòng and one of the principal objects of Dà-huì Zōng-gǎo 大慧宗杲's polemic against "silent-illumination Chán" 默照邪禪. Native of Zuǒ-mián Ān-chāng 左綿安昌 (Jiàn-zhōu, in Shǔ), lay surname Yōng 雍. 1088 – 17 November 1151 (lunar 紹興 21.10.1), *shì-shòu* 64, *sēng-là* 45.

Entered religion at eleven at Shèng-guǒ-sì 聖果寺 under Qīng-jùn 清俊; ordained after a *shì-jīng* 試經 on the *Lotus* at eighteen. Studied Buddhist scholasticism at Chéng-dū Dà-cí-sì 大慈寺. Attained awakening at Dān-xiá-shān under Zǐ-chún on the "self before the empty *kalpa*" 空劫以前自己 *huà-tóu* when Zǐ-chún struck him once as he tried to answer. At Cháng-lú 長蘆 (Zhēn-zhōu), where Zǔ-zhào 祖照 禪師 had made him *shì-zhě* 侍者 and then *fēn-zuò* 分座, was formally installed as abbot in the autumn of Xuān-hé 4 (1122), where 1700 *nà-zǐ* 衲子 gathered. After the Jìng-kāng catastrophe travelled to the Sì-míng coast and Mount Pǔ-tuó 補陀洛迦山; held successive abbacies at Tái-zhōu Tiān-fēng 天封, Mǐn Xuě-fēng 雪峰, Míng-zhōu Āyù-wáng 育王 (as 14th abbot, from early Shào-xīng), Wēn-zhōu Lóng-xiáng 龍翔, Hángzhōu Jìng-shān 徑山. In Shào-xīng 21 (1151) commanded by the court to inaugurate Chóng-xiān Xiǎn-xiào chán-yuàn 崇先顯孝禪院, built for the Cí-níng Tài-hòu 慈寧太后; died there four months after installation.

Works surviving: his two-juan *yǔlù* / *Jié-wài lù* 劫外錄 ([[KR6q0359]]; X71 n1426), edited by his attendants [[德初]] and [[義初]] (the latter also a named dharma-heir); the *Niàn-fó jìng-tǔ lún* 念佛淨土論 (X61 n1178), a standard Southern-Sòng witness to the Chán / Pure-Land synthesis on the Cáo-dòng side. Named dharma-heirs (eleven per the *tǎ-míng*): [[義初]] Yì-chū, Zōng-jué 宗玨, Huì-wù 慧悟, Xìng-yù 興譽, Fǎ-tōng 法通, and others.

Qīng-liǎo is the principal Cáo-dòng counterweight to Dà-huì's *kàn-huà* 看話 polemic; the *niān-gǔ* of his *Jié-wài lù* *juan* 2 preserves his own argumentative response to Dà-huì. Through his Mount Pǔ-tuó and Āyù-wáng abbacies he was also a foundational figure for the Southern-Sòng development of the *guān-yīn* 觀音 pilgrimage. The later post-Gāo-zōng Cáo-dòng establishment at Tiān-tóng, climaxing in Tiān-tóng Rú-jìng 天童如淨 and hence in Dōgen's Sōtō, runs in part through Qīng-liǎo's line.
