Chǔ Rénhuò 褚人獲 (courtesy names 學稼 Xuéjià and 石農 Shínóng; pseudonym 稻田漁叟 Dàotián Yúsǒu; b. Chóngzhēn 8 = 1635, Chángshu 常熟, Jiāngsū; death date unknown) was a Qīng prose writer, miscellaneist, and novelist. CBDB (id 90922) gives his birth year as 1635 and notes that the death year is not recorded; Wilkinson places his floruit ca. 1675–1695 (*Chinese History: A New Manual*, §32.8.2, which cites his *Jiānhú Jí* 堅瓠集 for market-place slang). He does not appear to have held official office; his career was that of a private literatus and writer in the Jiāngnán cultural milieu of the early-to-mid Kāngxī reign.

His most significant works are: (1) *Jiānhú Jí* 堅瓠集, a large collection of miscellaneous notes in multiple series, valued by later scholars for its documentation of vernacular vocabulary, popular customs, and anecdotal material of the early Qīng; and (2) *Suí Táng Yǎnyì* 隋唐演義 ([[KR4k0233]]), a 100-chapter historical novel about the transition from the Suí to the Táng dynasty, composed in the 1670s and first printed in 1695. The novel draws on earlier sources including the *Suíyáng Dì Yànshǐ* 隋煬帝豔史 and *chuánqí* tale traditions and became one of the standard entries in the roster of Chinese historical romance fiction (Wilkinson, Table 82).
