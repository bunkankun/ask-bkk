**Xú Líng** 徐陵 (507–583), *zì* Xiàomù 孝穆, posthumously titled *Zhāng* 章, native of Dōnghǎi Tán 東海郯 (modern eastern Shāndōng), was the great Liáng-Chén transition stylist and literary partner of [[庾信]] in the *Xú-Yǔ tǐ* 徐庾體 of *gōngtǐ* parallel-prose tradition. Son of the Liáng-Wǔ-dì-era *Tàizǐ Zuǒwèi shuài* Xú Chī 徐摛 (himself a master of the *gōngtǐ* style); a child prodigy — composing prose at eight, mastering *Zhuāngzǐ* and *Lǎozǐ* at twelve. He served the Liáng under *Jiǎnwéndì* 簡文帝 (then Crown Prince) at the Eastern Palace and compiled the *Yùtái xīnyǒng* 玉臺新詠 anthology at his command. Sent on diplomatic mission to Eastern Wèi in *Tàiqīng* 2 (548), he was retained in the Northern Qí during the *Hóu Jǐng* catastrophe (548–552). On his return, he played a leading role in the founding of the Chén under Chén Wǔdì 陳武帝 (Chén Bàxiān 陳霸先), and rose under Chén Wǔdì and Chén Wéndì 陳文帝 to *shìzhōng*, *Ānyòu jiāngjūn*, *Zuǒ guānglù dàifū*, *Nán Xúzhōu dà zhōngzhèng*, and *Jiànchāngxiàn kāiguóhóu* 建昌縣開國侯.

He was the principal stylist of all major Chén-state documents — *xí* (manifestos), *jūn shū* (military letters), and *chán shòu zhào cè* (dynastic-transfer proclamations). His *Yùtái xīnyǒng xù* 玉臺新詠序 is one of the great prefaces of medieval Chinese literature. Standard biographies in *Chén shū* 26 and *Nán shǐ* 62. CBDB confirms 507–583. His writings survive in [[KR4b0019]] *Xú Xiàomù jí jiānzhù* 徐孝穆集箋注.
