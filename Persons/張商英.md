Northern-Sòng statesman, calligrapher, ritualist and lay-Buddhist scholar; *zì* Tiānjué 天覺, *hào* Wújìn jūshì 無盡居士 ("the layman of the Inexhaustible"). Native of Xīnjīn 新津 (modern Sìchuān). *Jìnshì* of *Zhìpíng* 治平 2 (1065). Held a series of senior court offices including *Tōngfèng dàfū shǒu Shàngshū yòu púshè jiān Zhōngshū shìláng* 通奉大夫守尚書右僕射兼中書侍郎, *Shàngzhùguó* 上柱國, and was enfeoffed *Qīnghéjùn kāiguógōng* 清河郡開國公 with *shíyì* 食邑 of 2,900 households and *shíshí fēng* 食實封 of 900 — the very titles by which the colophon to *[[KR5a0156|DZ 155 Sāncái dìngwèi tú]]* introduces him. Briefly grand councillor (*Zǎixiàng* 宰相) under Huīzōng in 1110–1111. A close associate of the Buddhist *chán* master Yuánwù Kèqín 圜悟克勤 and the Daoist Línlíng sù 林靈素 milieu, he is celebrated for his synthesising religious interests. His Buddhist-canonical compositions in the Kanripo corpus include:
- **《護法論》** *Hùfǎ lùn* ([[KR6r0147]], T2114, 1 juan) — a Buddhist apologetic against Confucian critics of Buddhism, particularly **Hán Yù** 韓愈 and **Ōuyáng Xiū** 歐陽修.
- **《續清涼傳》** *Xù qīngliáng zhuàn* ([[KR6r0135]], T2100, 2 juan) — a personal-pilgrimage-cum-vision narrative of the author's pilgrimages to Mount Wǔtái 五臺 (the third of the canonical *Gǔ-Guǎng-Xù qīngliáng* trilogy of Wǔtái gazetteers). Lifedates 1043–1121 are confirmed by the local CBDB record (c_personid 272). Biographical sources: *Sòng shǐ* 351; Patricia Ebrey, *Emperor Huizong* (Cambridge, Mass.: Harvard, 2014), index s.v.
