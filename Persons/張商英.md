Northern-Sòng statesman, calligrapher, ritualist and lay-Buddhist scholar; *zì* Tiānjué 天覺, *hào* Wújìn jūshì 無盡居士 ("the layman of the Inexhaustible"). Native of Xīnjīn 新津 (modern Sìchuān). *Jìnshì* of *Zhìpíng* 治平 2 (1065). Held a series of senior court offices including *Tōngfèng dàfū shǒu Shàngshū yòu púshè jiān Zhōngshū shìláng* 通奉大夫守尚書右僕射兼中書侍郎, *Shàngzhùguó* 上柱國, and was enfeoffed *Qīnghéjùn kāiguógōng* 清河郡開國公 with *shíyì* 食邑 of 2,900 households and *shíshí fēng* 食實封 of 900 — the very titles by which the colophon to [[KR5a0156|DZ 155 *Sāncái dìngwèi tú*]] introduces him. Briefly grand councillor (*Zǎixiàng* 宰相) under Huīzōng in 1110–1111. A close associate of the Buddhist *chán* master Yuánwù Kèqín 圜悟克勤 and the Daoist Línlíng sù 林靈素 milieu, he is celebrated for his synthesising religious interests: in addition to the Sancai dingwei tu, he composed the *Hùfǎ lùn* 護法論 (a Buddhist apologetic) and prefaces and ritual-cosmographic works in the Daoist canon. Lifedates 1043–1121 are confirmed by the local CBDB record (c_personid 272). Biographical sources: *Sòng shǐ* 351; Patricia Ebrey, *Emperor Huizong* (Cambridge, Mass.: Harvard, 2014), index s.v.
