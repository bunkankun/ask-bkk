Sī-kōng Tú 司空圖 (837–908, *zì* Biǎo-shèng 表聖, late-life *hào* Zhī-fēi-zǐ 知非子 and Nài-rǔ jū-shì 耐辱居士), of *Hé-zhōng Yú-xiāng* 河中虞鄉 (modern Shānxī *Yǒng-jǐ*; ancestral *Hé-nèi*). *Jìn-shì* of late *Xián-tōng* (873). Served in the *Xuān-Shè mù* (under Wáng Níng), then as *Lǐ-bù láng-zhōng*; under **Xī-zōng** (873–88) he was *Zhī zhì-gào* and *Zhōng-shū shè-rén* — at the imperial residence-in-exile at *Fèng-xiáng* (after the imperial flight from Huáng Cháo).

Foreseeing the imperial collapse, he resigned and withdrew to the family villa at **Wáng-guān gǔ** 王官谷 in the *Zhōng-tiáo* 中條 mountains. *Lóng-jì* and *Qián-níng* periods, summoned with old offices and *Hù-* / *Bīng-* 2 *shì-láng* posts — refused; transferred to *Luò* and decreed to court — feigned senility, begged to return. When **Zhū Quán-zhōng** 朱全忠 (Zhū Wēn) summoned him, he firmly refused; when Zhū usurped the throne (founding the *Hòu-Liáng*, 907), Sī-kōng (on hearing of *Āi-zōng*'s murder) starved himself to death. *Xīn Táng-shū* placed him in the *Zhuó-xíng* (extraordinary-conduct) biography category.

Sī-kōng's enduring fame comes principally from the *Èr-shí-sì shī-pǐn* 二十四詩品 (Twenty-Four Categories of Poetry) — 24 prose-poem characterizations of poetic moods. The work circulated separately and became the canonical statement of late-Táng poetic aesthetics. **Modern philology** (Chén Shàng-jūn 陳尚君, 1995, building on earlier doubts) has argued for an Yuán-Míng forgery attribution to Yú Zhāo 虞兆 — the now-prevailing scholarly view.

Principal works in the corpus: *Sī-kōng Biǎo-shèng wén-jí* [[KR4c0095]] in 10 *juǎn* (the *Yī míng jí* prose); *Sī-kōng Biǎo-shèng shī jí* [[KR4c0096]] in 5 *juǎn* (SBCK verse). CBDB id 92445 confirms 837–908.
