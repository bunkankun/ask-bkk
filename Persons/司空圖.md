Sīkōng Tú 司空圖 (837–908, *zì* Biǎoshèng 表聖, late-life *hào* Zhīfēizǐ 知非子 and Nàirǔ jūshì 耐辱居士), of *Hézhōng Yúxiāng* 河中虞鄉 (modern Shānxī *Yǒngjǐ*; ancestral *Hénèi*). *Jìnshì* of late *Xiántōng* (873). Served in the *XuānShè mù* (under Wáng Níng), then as *Lǐbù lángzhōng*; under **Xīzōng** (873–88) he was *Zhī zhìgào* and *Zhōngshū shèrén* — at the imperial residence-in-exile at *Fèngxiáng* (after the imperial flight from Huáng Cháo).

Foreseeing the imperial collapse, he resigned and withdrew to the family villa at **Wángguān gǔ** 王官谷 in the *Zhōngtiáo* 中條 mountains. *Lóngjì* and *Qiánníng* periods, summoned with old offices and *Hù-* / *Bīng-* 2 *shìláng* posts — refused; transferred to *Luò* and decreed to court — feigned senility, begged to return. When **Zhū Quánzhōng** 朱全忠 (Zhū Wēn) summoned him, he firmly refused; when Zhū usurped the throne (founding the *HòuLiáng*, 907), Sīkōng (on hearing of *Āizōng*'s murder) starved himself to death. *Xīn Tángshū* placed him in the *Zhuóxíng* (extraordinary-conduct) biography category.

Sīkōng's enduring fame comes principally from the *Èrshísì shīpǐn* 二十四詩品 (Twenty-Four Categories of Poetry) — 24 prose-poem characterizations of poetic moods. The work circulated separately and became the canonical statement of late-Táng poetic aesthetics. **Modern philology** (Chén Shàngjūn 陳尚君, 1995, building on earlier doubts) has argued for an YuánMíng forgery attribution to Yú Zhāo 虞兆 — the now-prevailing scholarly view.

Principal works in the corpus: *Sīkōng Biǎoshèng wénjí* [[KR4c0095]] in 10 *juǎn* (the *Yī míng jí* prose); *Sīkōng Biǎoshèng shī jí* [[KR4c0096]] in 5 *juǎn* (SBCK verse). CBDB id 92445 confirms 837–908.
