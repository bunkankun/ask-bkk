# Shèng-zǔ Xuán-yè 聖祖玄燁 / The Kāngxī Emperor

The fourth emperor of the Qīng dynasty (reigned 1661–1722, age 7 to 68 — one of the longest reigns in Chinese history). Personal name *Xuán-yè* 玄燁 (Aisin Gioro Xuanye); reign-period name *Kāngxī* 康熙; posthumous temple-name *Shèng-zǔ Rén Huáng-dì* 聖祖仁皇帝 (Sage Ancestor Benevolent Imperial Ancestor); the *Sìkù* tíyào commonly refers to him by the temple-name. Born Shùnzhì 11 (1654); died Kāngxī 61 (1722).

The Kāngxī emperor was the most intellectually engaged of the Qīng emperors, with particular and well-documented personal interest in mathematics, astronomy, music theory, and the natural sciences. He received personal instruction in mathematics and astronomy from the resident Beijing Jesuits — primarily Antoine Thomas (Ān-duō 安多), Joachim Bouvet (Bái Jìn 白晉), Jean-François Gerbillon (Zhāng Chéng 張誠), and Tomás Pereira (Xú Rì-shēng 徐日昇) — across the 1680s-1700s. The Latin lecture-notes preserved in the Vatican Archives document the imperial student's level of mathematical engagement; Bouvet's published account *Histoire de l'Empereur de la Chine* (Paris, 1697) provides the European-side framing.

The Kāngxī mathematical-and-scientific project culminated in the *Lǜ-lì yuān-yuán* 律歷淵源 — the great 100-juan imperial synthesis chartered Kāngxī 52 (1713) and printed Yōngzhèng 2 (1724). Its three parts:

(1) **Lì-xiàng kǎo-chéng** 歷象考成 ([[KR3f0018]]) — the astronomical-and-calendrical synthesis (42 *juàn*).

(2) **Lǜ-lǚ zhèng-yì** 律呂正義 — the music-theoretical synthesis (5 *juàn*).

(3) **Shù-lǐ jīng-yùn** 數理精蘊 ([[KR3f0048]]) — the mathematical synthesis (53 *juàn*).

The principal editorial direction was carried by the Kāngxī emperor's son [[允祿]] Yǔnlù (the *Zhuāng qīn-wáng*, Kāngxī's 16th son) with [[何國宗]] Hé Guózōng and Méi Juéchéng 梅㲄成 ([[梅文鼎]] Méi Wéndǐng's grandson) as principal Chinese-mathematical-staff editors, and various Jesuit consultants in the consulting role. The Kāngxī emperor himself contributed substantial editorial direction; the *Lǜ-lì yuān-yuán* texts are formally attributed to imperial composition (*yù-zhì* 御製 / *qīn-dìng* 欽定 / *fèng-chì zhuàn* 奉敕撰).

Kāngxī's other intellectual projects of similar scope: the *Kāngxī zì-diǎn* 康熙字典 dictionary (1716, 47,035 character-headings); the *Pèi-wén yùn-fǔ* 佩文韻府 phonological-and-rhyme dictionary (1711); the *Quán Táng shī* 全唐詩 anthology of Tang poetry (1707); the *Zǐ-shǐ jīng-huá* 子史精華 anthology of philosophical-and-historical writings; the *Gǔ-jīn tú-shū jí-chéng* 古今圖書集成 grand encyclopedia (substantially completed under Kāngxī, finalized under Yōngzhèng).

The Kāngxī encounter with [[梅文鼎]] Méi Wéndǐng in 1702 — when Méi Wéndǐng's *Lì-xué yí-wèn* was presented by Lǐ Guāngdì 李光地 to the imperial chariot at Dé-zhōu, with the imperial bestowal of the four-character calligraphy *Jī-xué cān-wēi* 積學參微 and the subsequent summoning of Méi Juéchéng to court — is the foundational moment of imperial-scholarly recognition that established the Méi family's centrality in the *Lǜ-lì yuān-yuán* project.

The Kāngxī emperor's death in December 1722 occurred just before the *Lǜ-lì yuān-yuán* could be printed; his son the Yōngzhèng emperor completed the project and wrote the imperial preface (October 1723). The *Sìkù* tíyào of [[KR3f0018]] and [[KR3f0048]] both frame the *Lǜ-lì yuān-yuán* as Kāngxī's posthumous monument.
