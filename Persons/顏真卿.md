**Yán Zhēnqīng** 顏真卿 (709–784), *zì* **Qīngchén** 清臣, posthumous title **Wénzhōng** 文忠 — high-Tang loyal-minister, calligrapher, and master of the *kǎishū* and *xíngshū* canonical scripts. *Jìnshì* 734, served the An-Shǐ-rebellion period as a *Píngyuán tàishǒu* who held out against the rebels; later as *Tàizǐ tàishī* and ennobled as *Lǔ jùn kāiguó gōng* 魯郡開國公 (Duke of Lǔ); died a martyr's death at the hands of the rebel general Lǐ Xīliè 李希烈 in 784. As calligrapher, his *Yán shì* 顏氏 stylistic legacy is one of the foundational corpora of Chinese calligraphy. He is the celebrated author of the **Qiáo xiān guàn bēi jì** 橋仙觀碑記 — the foundational stele-text for the **Wáng Guō èr zhēnjūn** 王郭二真君 cult of *Huágàishān* (cf. [[KR5i0102]]) — composed during his term as Inspector of Línchuān (撫州), c. 770s. CBDB ID 95025. Standard reference: McNair, *The Upright Brush: Yan Zhenqing's Calligraphy and Song Literati Politics* (Hawai'i 1998).
