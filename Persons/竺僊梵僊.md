**Zhúxiān Fànxiān** 竺僊梵僊 (Jp. Jikusen Bonsen, 1292–1348) — Late-Yuán Chinese **Línjì-school** 臨濟宗 Chán master of the **Yángqípài** 楊岐派 (the dominant Línjì sub-school) who emigrated to Japan in 1329 and became one of the foundational figures of medieval Japanese Rinzai-Zen and Gozan literature. Style-name **Zhúxiān** 竺仙; self-sobriquets **Láilái Chánzǐ** 來來禪子 ("Come-Again Zen Child"), **Jíshèng Chuáng** 寂勝幢 ("Quiet-Conquering Banner"), and **Sīguī Sǒu** 思歸叟 ("Thinking-of-Return Old Man" — referring to his Chinese homeland).

Born **Zhìyuán 29 (1292)** in **Xiàngshān** 象山 (modern Zhèjiāng), of the **Xú** 徐 clan. At age eight entered the **Wúxīng Zīfúyuàn** 吳興資福院 (Zhèjiāng). At 18 received the full precepts under **Língshān Ruìyún Yǐn** 靈山瑞雲隱. Travelled widely as an itinerant scholar-monk; ultimately at **Bǎoníngsì** 保寧寺 (Nánjīng) became dharma-heir to **Gǔlín Qīngmào** 古林清茂 (1262–1329) of the Yángqípài. Hence his lineage is called the **Gǔlínpài** 古林派 (or **Méilín** 梅林派, "Plum-Grove sub-school") in Japan.

In **Tiānlì 2 (1329)** he travelled to Japan with the senior master **Míngjí Chǔjùn** 明極楚俊 (Mei-zū Soshun) at the invitation of the Japanese Zen establishment. Held abbacies at **Jōmyō-ji** 淨妙寺, **Jōchi-ji** 淨智寺, and **Kenchō-ji** 建長寺 in Kamakura, and at **Nanzen-ji** 南禪寺 and **Shinnyo-ji** 真如寺 in Kyoto. In **Kenmu 2 (1335)** he became the founding patriarch of **Muryō-ji** 無量寺.

Died at the **Ryōga-in** 楞伽院 of Jōmyō-ji on **Shōhei 3 (1348), Yuán Zhìzhèng 8** at age 57.

His Recorded Sayings — *Jikusen Oshō goroku* 竺僊和尚語錄 ([[KR6t0260]], T80n2554) — four fascicles, compiled by his disciple **Eigyō** 裔堯 and others. He also produced a *Shūmon senjimon* 宗門千字文 ("Thousand-Character Essay on the School Tradition"). His dharma-heirs in Japan included **Dainen Hōen** 大年法延, **Chintei Kaiju** 椿庭海壽, and others; the line became one of the **24 transmission-lines** of medieval Japanese Zen.
