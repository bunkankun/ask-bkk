**Wú Ruìfǔ** 吳瑞甫 (1872–1952), original name **Wú Xīhuáng** 吳錫璜, *zì* Ruìfǔ 瑞甫, *hào* Fǔtáng 黼堂. Native of **Tóngān** 同安 county in Xiàmén 廈門 (Fújiàn). Major Republican-era physician and **Sino-Western integrationist** (*zhōngxī huìtōng pài* 中西匯通派) practitioner of southern Fújiàn and South-East Asia.

He came from a hereditary medical family — his grandfather Wú Huījí 吳撝吉 founded the family practice — and read the Chinese medical canon (from *Língshū*, *Sùwèn* down through Hàn, Jìn, Táng, Sòng, Yuán, Míng witnesses) systematically from his youth. By his own report he had annotated more than 1,000 *juǎn* of medical texts by the time he wrote the *Zhōngxī wēnrè chuànjiě*. Doctrinally he held [[王士雄]] Wáng Mèngyīng's *Wēnrè jīngwěi* to be the supreme treatise on warm-disease, and Wèi Niàntíng 魏念庭, Yè Tiānshì, Xú Língtāi 徐靈胎, Kē Yùnbó 柯韻伯, and Chén Xiūyuán 陳修園 to be the most distinguished classical authorities.

His **Sino-Western integrationism** is distinctive in its breadth: over more than ten years he systematically acquired and studied Chinese translations of Western medical texts, the goal being a working clinical translation between the two systems — "applying Chinese methods where Chinese is appropriate, Western where Western is appropriate, as a mirror reflects whatever stands before it" (宜於中則中，宜於西則西，如鑑照物，隨物以應). His major works:

- *Zhōngxī wēnrè chuànjiě* 中西溫熱串解 ([[KR3eg021]]), Mínguó 9 (1920), composed at the *Huíchūnlú yīyuàn* 回春廬醫院, Xiàmén.
- Annotated edition of the *Sānyīn fāng* 三因方.
- *Nǎosuǐ bìng lùn* 腦髓病論 (Treatise on Brain-Marrow Diseases), 2 *juǎn*.
- *Zhōngxī màixué* 中西脈學 (Chinese-Western Pulse Diagnosis), 1 *juǎn*.
- *Zhōngxī nèikēxué* 中西內科學 (Chinese-Western Internal Medicine), 10 *juǎn*.

His clinical reputation extended throughout South-East Asia (the southern-Fújiàn diaspora communities). Source: book prefaces; modern reference works on Republican-period medicine.
