**Wú Ruì-fǔ** 吳瑞甫 (1872–1952), original name **Wú Xī-huáng** 吳錫璜, *zì* Ruì-fǔ 瑞甫, *hào* Fǔ-táng 黼堂. Native of **Tóng-ān** 同安 county in Xià-mén 廈門 (Fú-jiàn). Major Republican-era physician and **Sino-Western integrationist** (*zhōng-xī huì-tōng pài* 中西匯通派) practitioner of southern Fú-jiàn and South-East Asia.

He came from a hereditary medical family — his grandfather Wú Huī-jí 吳撝吉 founded the family practice — and read the Chinese medical canon (from *Líng-shū*, *Sù-wèn* down through Hàn, Jìn, Táng, Sòng, Yuán, Míng witnesses) systematically from his youth. By his own report he had annotated more than 1,000 *juǎn* of medical texts by the time he wrote the *Zhōng-xī wēn-rè chuàn-jiě*. Doctrinally he held [[王士雄]] Wáng Mèng-yīng's *Wēn-rè jīng-wěi* to be the supreme treatise on warm-disease, and Wèi Niàn-tíng 魏念庭, Yè Tiān-shì, Xú Líng-tāi 徐靈胎, Kē Yùn-bó 柯韻伯, and Chén Xiū-yuán 陳修園 to be the most distinguished classical authorities.

His **Sino-Western integrationism** is distinctive in its breadth: over more than ten years he systematically acquired and studied Chinese translations of Western medical texts, the goal being a working clinical translation between the two systems — "applying Chinese methods where Chinese is appropriate, Western where Western is appropriate, as a mirror reflects whatever stands before it" (宜於中則中，宜於西則西，如鑑照物，隨物以應). His major works:

- *Zhōng-xī wēn-rè chuàn-jiě* 中西溫熱串解 ([[KR3eg021]]), Mín-guó 9 (1920), composed at the *Huí-chūn-lú yī-yuàn* 回春廬醫院, Xià-mén.
- Annotated edition of the *Sān-yīn fāng* 三因方.
- *Nǎo-suǐ bìng lùn* 腦髓病論 (Treatise on Brain-Marrow Diseases), 2 *juǎn*.
- *Zhōng-xī mài-xué* 中西脈學 (Chinese-Western Pulse Diagnosis), 1 *juǎn*.
- *Zhōng-xī nèi-kē-xué* 中西內科學 (Chinese-Western Internal Medicine), 10 *juǎn*.

His clinical reputation extended throughout South-East Asia (the southern-Fújiàn diaspora communities). Source: book prefaces; modern reference works on Republican-period medicine.
