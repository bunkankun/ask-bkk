Huáng Shū-jǐng 黃叔璥 (1666–1742), *zì* Yù-zhāi 玉齋, *hào* Yù-pǔ 玉圃, also Dǔ-zhāi 篤齋; native of Dà-xīng 大興 in Zhí-lì (now Beijing). Mid-Qīng official and frontier-affairs author. *Kāng-xī jǐ-chǒu jìnshì* (1709). Held office as a Censor; in *Kāng-xī rén-yín* (1722) he was appointed (alongside Wú Dá-lǐ 吳達禮) as the first *xún-shì Tái-wān yù-shǐ* — an Imperial Censor stationed in Taiwan, the office freshly created by the Yōng-zhèng emperor in the wake of the *Zhū Yī-guì* uprising of 1721 to provide closer Qīng administrative supervision of the recently incorporated frontier prefecture. He served in Taiwan 1722–1724. Subsequently rose to *Cháng-Zhèn-Yáng tōng-dào* (Circuit Intendant for Cháng-zhōu, Zhèn-jiāng, and Yáng-zhōu). Best known for the *Tái-hǎi shǐ-chá lù* 臺海使槎錄 ([[KR2k0131]]) — the foundational early-Qīng systematic monograph on Taiwan, with its *Fān-sú liù-kǎo* the principal source for early-eighteenth-century Pingpu indigenous-peoples ethnography and language.
