Huáng Shūjǐng 黃叔璥 (1666–1742), *zì* Yùzhāi 玉齋, *hào* Yùpǔ 玉圃, also Dǔzhāi 篤齋; native of Dàxīng 大興 in Zhílì (now Beijing). Mid-Qīng official and frontier-affairs author. *Kāngxī jǐchǒu jìnshì* (1709). Held office as a Censor; in *Kāngxī rényín* (1722) he was appointed (alongside Wú Dálǐ 吳達禮) as the first *xúnshì Táiwān yùshǐ* — an Imperial Censor stationed in Taiwan, the office freshly created by the Yōngzhèng emperor in the wake of the *Zhū Yīguì* uprising of 1721 to provide closer Qīng administrative supervision of the recently incorporated frontier prefecture. He served in Taiwan 1722–1724. Subsequently rose to *Cháng-Zhèn-Yáng tōngdào* (Circuit Intendant for Chángzhōu, Zhènjiāng, and Yángzhōu). Best known for the *Táihǎi shǐchá lù* 臺海使槎錄 ([[KR2k0131]]) — the foundational early-Qīng systematic monograph on Taiwan, with its *Fānsú liùkǎo* the principal source for early-eighteenth-century Pingpu indigenous-peoples ethnography and language.
