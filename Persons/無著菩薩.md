Wú-zhuó púsà 無著菩薩 (\*Asaṅga, "Unattached"; conventionally c. 290–360 CE), Indian Buddhist Yogācāra master, the founder (with his younger brother [[世親菩薩|Vasubandhu]]) of the Yogācāra-Vijñānavāda philosophical tradition. Per traditional Indic-Buddhist hagiography (the *Pó-sǒu pán-dòu zhuàn* 婆藪槃豆傳, T2049, by Paramārtha), Asaṅga was a *brāhmaṇa* of Gandhāra origin, who at first was ordained in the Sarvāstivāda but, dissatisfied with the doctrinal completeness of *abhidharma*, withdrew to a forest retreat and through deep meditation received the Yogācāra teachings directly from the future Buddha Maitreya in Tuṣita heaven; from these revelations he composed the foundational Yogācāra works (*Mahāyāna-saṃgraha*, *Abhidharmasamuccaya*, *Yogācārabhūmi*) and converted his brother Vasubandhu to Mahāyāna.

His surviving Sanskrit and Chinese-translation works include: the *Mahāyāna-saṃgraha* (*Shè-dà-shèng lùn* 攝大乘論, T1592–94); the *Abhidharmasamuccaya* (*Dà-shèng āpídámó jí lùn* 大乘阿毘達磨集論, T1605); the *Vajracchedikā-vyākhyā* commentaries [[KR6c0030|T1510a]], [[KR6c0031|T1510b]], and [[KR6c0034|T1513]]; and several other Yogācāra treatises. The *Yogācārabhūmi* (T1579) is also traditionally credited to him though modern scholarship (Schmithausen) treats it as the work of his school rather than of Asaṅga personally.
