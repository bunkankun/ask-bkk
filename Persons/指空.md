**Dhyānabhadra** (Skt. *Dhyāna-bhadra*; Chinese 指空 *Zhǐkōng*, also transliterated 提納薄陀 *Tínàbótuó*, semantic-rendered 禪賢 *Chánxián*; Korean *Chigong* 지공; ca. 1289–1363) was a Magadhan-Indian Buddhist master active in late-Yuán China and late-Goryeo Korea. He claimed descent from the Pāla royal lineage and trained at the late phase of Nālandā and Vikramaśīla before their destruction in the Muslim invasions, after which he travelled across Tibet, Yuán China (Dadu = Beijing), and Goryeo Korea.

He resided in Goryeo 1326–1328 and exerted decisive influence on the late-Goryeo Buddhist establishment, with his disciples including Naong Hyegeun 懶翁慧勤 (1320–1376) and the chief preceptor of Goryeo king Gongmin. He returned to Yuán Dadu and remained there until his death in 1363.

Translations attributed to him in the East Asian Buddhist canon include the *Yú-sè-nǐ-shā pí-zuǒ-yě tuóluóní* 于瑟抳沙毘左野陀囉尼 ([[KR6j0164]] T979 — the *Uṣṇīṣa-vijaya-dhāraṇī*) and a recension of the *Avalokiteśvara-mahā-karuṇā-dhāraṇī* (T1113A). His translations preserve a distinctively Sanskritised transliteration register reflecting his late-Pāla-Sena Indian training.
