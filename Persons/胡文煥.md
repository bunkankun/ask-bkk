Hú Wénhuàn 胡文煥 (lifedates unrecorded; active late-Míng Wànlì period, c. 1573–1620), *zì* Défù 德甫 (or Déwén 德文), *hào* Quán'ān 全庵 and Bàoqín jūshì 抱琴居士 ("Harp-Embracing Lay-Practitioner"); Buddhist name Juéyīn 覺因 ("Awakening-Cause"). Late-Ming lay-literatus and major book publisher. Native of Qiántáng 錢塘 (Hángzhōu).

Hú's principal contribution was as a publisher rather than as an original author. His *Géjí cóngshū* 格致叢書 assembled several hundred rare titles — many previously unpublished or poorly-circulated — in a single collection, a major contribution to Ming-period book culture. Also authored operatic-literary works (*Qí huò jì* 奇貨記, *Xī pèi jì* 犀佩記, *Sān Jìn jì* 三晉記, *Yú qìng jì* 餘慶記), compiled the massive song-aria anthology *Qún yīn lèi xuǎn* 群音類選 (26 juan; the largest Ming song anthology), and produced numerous reference works in musicology (*Wénhuì táng qín pǔ* 文會堂琴譜), literary criticism (*Shīxué huìxuǎn* 詩學匯選), and material culture (*Gǔ qì jù míng* 古器具名).

Worked also on Buddhist texts as part of his broader publishing programme. His *Shí niú tú sòng* 十牛圖頌 ([[KR6q0160]], X64 n1270) — a late-Ming re-engraving of the ox-herding pictorial-allegorical cycle with supplementary *Kǔ-lè yīnyuán tú* — reflects the late-Ming *sānjiāo héyī* 三教合一 ("unity of the three teachings") integrative movement, in which classical Chán material was adapted for lay-Confucian-Daoist audiences.
