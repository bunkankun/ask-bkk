Northern-Sòng *cí*-master and the supreme musical theorist of the *cí* form (1057–1121); *zì* Měi-chéng 美成, *hào* Qīng-zhēn jūshì 清真居士 ("Recluse of Pure Reality"); a man of Qián-táng 錢塘 (modern Háng-zhōu). In the *Yuán-fēng* period (early 1080s) he presented the *Biàn-dū fù* 汴都賦 — an extended *fù* on the Northern Sòng capital — and on its strength was summoned to be *Tài-xué zhèng* 太學正 and ultimately *Dà-yuè zhèng* 大樂正, head of the imperial Music Bureau, where he played a central role in Huīzōng's *Dà-shèng yuè* 大晟樂 reform: reorganizing the *yàn-yuè* 燕樂 tune-system and standardizing the metric patterns into which *cí* were composed. Under Huīzōng he rose to *Huī-yóu-gé dài-zhì* 徽猷閣待制; held governorships at Shùn-chāng 順昌府 and Chǔ-zhōu 處州, where he died in *Xuān-hé* 3 / 1121. The *Sòng shǐ Wén-yuàn-zhuàn* (vol. 444) notes him as personally somewhat dissolute, deeply musical, and the most refined of his generation in *cháng-duǎn-jù*. His *Piàn-yù cí* / *Qīng-zhēn jí* [[KR4j0015]] preserves around 186 *cí*; the precision of his tonal-prosodic patterning (not merely *píng-zè* but the four-way distinction *píng-shàng-qù-rù*) made him the paradigm for the Southern-Sòng *gé-lǜ* 格律 school, and the model that Fāng Qiān-lǐ [[方千里]] [[KR4j0019]], Yáng Zé-mín, and Chén Yǔn-pìng each "matched" character-by-character through entire collections. The principal critical biographies are by Lóng Yú-shēng 龍榆生 and (in English) Stephen Owen, "Meaning the Words" (1994), and Kang-i Sun Chang's *Evolution of Chinese Tz'u Poetry* (1980).
