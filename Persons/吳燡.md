Mid-Qīng (late-Qián-lóng / Jiā-qìng / early-Dào-guāng) physician of Hǎi-líng 海陵 (Tài-zhōu 泰州, Jiāng-sū). Known principally through the preface of his maternal grandson Chén Zhào 陳照 (dated *Xián-fēng* 4 / 1854) to the recut edition of his sole documented work, [[KR3er101]] *Yī-xué jí-yào* 醫學輯要 (in four *juǎn*, first printed at Hǎi-líng in Wú's own lifetime, recut by Chén Zhào in 1854). The work is a systematic clinical handbook anchored in the classical four-fold *wàng-wén-wèn-qiē* diagnostic method, with particular detail on visual diagnosis and pulse-taking. Best known by his *zì* Xiǎo-shān 小珊. Not in CBDB. Note: not to be confused with the more famous Wáng Shì-xióng 王士雄 *Wú Xiǎo-shān* 吳小山, *fl.* mid-19th-c.
