Zhū Chángwén (1039–1098; *zì* Bóyuán 伯原, *hào* Lèpǔ 樂圃), a native of Sūzhōu 蘇州, passed the *jìnshì* of the *yǐ* (second) class while still very young (Zhìpíng 4, 1067) but, suffering from a chronic foot ailment (*zú-jí* 足疾), declined the appointment to take office and instead retired to manage the family-owned Lèpǔ Academy 樂圃書院 in Sūzhōu, where he taught and wrote for some twenty years. On the recommendation of Sū Shì 蘇軾, he was at length appointed *Sūzhōu jiàoshòu* (prefectural educational officer); subsequently summoned to court as *Tàicháng bóshì* (Erudite of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices), promoted to *Mìshū-shěng zhèng-zì* (Editor of the Imperial Library) and *Shūmì-yuàn biānxiū* (Compiler of the Bureau of Military Affairs), and died in office. He is the author of the *Wújùn tújīng xùjì* [[KR2k0010]], the *Lèpǔ yú-gǎo* 樂圃餘藁 (a poetry collection), the *Qín shǐ* 琴史 (the earliest extant systematic history of the *qín* lute), and the *Mò-chí biān* 墨池編 (a collection of treatises on calligraphy).

Catalog meta gives lifedates 1041–1100; CBDB id 11316 gives 1039–1098, which is the date pair followed in standard *Sòngshǐ* and Sūzhōu local-gazetteer evidence and is preferred here. Note: another *Zhū Chángwén* (CBDB 92525, Táng) is a homonym; the present entry is the Northern-Sòng Sūzhōu polymath.
