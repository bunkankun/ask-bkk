Zhōu Dáguān 周達觀 (*fl.* 1295–1312, with later traces as a Yuán-loyalist after the dynasty's transition); native of Wēnzhōu 溫州 in Zhèjiāng. *Hào* Cǎotíng yìmín 草庭逸民 (the "Idle Old Man of the Grass-Pavilion"). Otherwise biographically undocumented. Member of the Yuán imperial mission to Cambodia (*Zhēnlà* 真臘) under Chéngzōng Tiěmùěr in *Yuánzhēn* 1 *yǐwèi* (1295), under the leadership of an unnamed *qiǎnshǐ*; the mission departed from Wēnzhōu in early 1295, sailing past MǐnGuǎng, the Seven Stars sea, the Gulf of Tonkin, *Zhānchéng* (Champa), and the Mekong delta to *Zhēnpǔ*; reached the Khmer royal seat at Yāngshù (*Yāngshù chéng* — Angkor Thom) in 1296; Zhōu spent over a year in residence at the court of *Indravarman III / Śrīndravarman*; returned to China in *Dàdé* 1 *dīngyǒu* (1297). On the basis of his year-long residence he composed the *Zhēnlà fēngtǔ jì* 眞臘風土記 ([[KR2k0141]]) — the only first-hand pre-modern Chinese-language documentary monograph on Angkorian Cambodia, completed before 1311 (when Wú Yǎn 吾衍 contributed a colophon-poem to the manuscript). His later appearance is recorded as a Yuán-loyalist *yìmín* under his *hào* — suggesting he survived into the early 14th century. CBDB id 33210.
