Zhōu Dá-guān 周達觀 (*fl.* 1295–1312, with later traces as a Yuán-loyalist after the dynasty's transition); native of Wēn-zhōu 溫州 in Zhèjiāng. *Hào* Cǎo-tíng yì-mín 草庭逸民 (the "Idle Old Man of the Grass-Pavilion"). Otherwise biographically undocumented. Member of the Yuán imperial mission to Cambodia (*Zhēn-là* 真臘) under Chéng-zōng Tiě-mù-ěr in *Yuán-zhēn* 1 *yǐ-wèi* (1295), under the leadership of an unnamed *qiǎn-shǐ*; the mission departed from Wēn-zhōu in early 1295, sailing past Mǐn-Guǎng, the Seven Stars sea, the Gulf of Tonkin, *Zhān-chéng* (Champa), and the Mekong delta to *Zhēn-pǔ*; reached the Khmer royal seat at Yāng-shù (*Yāng-shù chéng* — Angkor Thom) in 1296; Zhōu spent over a year in residence at the court of *Indravarman III / Śrīndravarman*; returned to China in *Dà-dé* 1 *dīng-yǒu* (1297). On the basis of his year-long residence he composed the *Zhēn-là fēng-tǔ jì* 眞臘風土記 ([[KR2k0141]]) — the only first-hand pre-modern Chinese-language documentary monograph on Angkorian Cambodia, completed before 1311 (when Wú Yǎn 吾衍 contributed a colophon-poem to the manuscript). His later appearance is recorded as a Yuán-loyalist *yì-mín* under his *hào* — suggesting he survived into the early 14th century. CBDB id 33210.
