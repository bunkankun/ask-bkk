Zhāng Qiān-dé 張謙德 (1577–1643), better known as **Zhāng Chǒu** 張丑 (the name he adopted in adulthood); *zì* Shū-yì 叔益 and Qīng-fǔ 青父, *hào* Mǐ-ān 米庵. Native of Kūn-shān 崑山, Jiāngsū. Eldest son of [[張應文]] (Zhāng Yìng-wén). One of the most important late-Míng connoisseurs of calligraphy and painting. His principal works include the *Qīng hé shū huà fǎng* 清河書畫舫 (a treatise organized as a calligraphy-and-painting boat-cruise) in 12 *juàn*, the *Zhēn-jì rì-lù* 真迹日錄 (Day-by-Day Record of Authentic Traces) in 6 *juàn*, the *Mǐ-ān jiàn-shǎng tán* 米庵鑒賞談, the *Nán-yáng míng-huà biǎo* 南陽名畫表, and the *Qīng hé shū huà biǎo* 清河書畫表 (a tabular catalog of family holdings). Father and son together produced the *Qīng mì cáng* 清祕藏 ([[KR3j0173]]) — the father composed, the son polished. Zhāng Chǒu's works are foundational for late-Míng / early-Qīng connoisseurship: the *Qīng hé shū huà fǎng* established the genre of the literati-connoisseurship narrative.
