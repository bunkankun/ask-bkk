Mid-Táng polymath, scholar-official, lay Buddhist, and one of the principal mid-Táng literati associated with the Tiāntái 天台 school. *Zì* **Jìngzhī** 敬之, also referenced as **Liáng Kuānzhōng** 梁寬中 and **Liáng Hànlín** 梁翰林 (after his Hànlín Academy appointment). DILA Authority A001081. Native of **Lùhún** 陸渾 (modern Hénán). Born 753; died Zhēnyuán 貞元 9 (= 793–794 CE), aged 41.

A *jǐnshì* 進士 (presented-scholar) of Dàlì 大曆 9 (774); held senior appointments including Hànlín *xuéshì* 翰林學士 and other court positions under Dézōng 德宗. As a leading mid-Táng literary figure he was associated with the *Gǔwén* 古文 (ancient-prose) movement and was a friend and correspondent of Hán Yù 韓愈 (768–824).

His Buddhist activity centred on the Tiāntái school: he was a close associate of Zhànrán 湛然 ([[湛然]], 711–782), the ninth Tiāntái patriarch, and produced several important Tiāntái-tradition works, most notably:
- The **Shāndìng zhǐguān** 刪定止觀 ([[KR6d0134]], X55n0915, 3 *juan*) — the abridged-and-corrected version of Zhìyǐ's *Móhē zhǐguān*, providing the standard mid-Táng accessible recension of the Tiāntái meditation manual.
- The *Tiāntái jiàomén lùn* 天台教門論 (lost) — a Tiāntái doctrinal exposition.
- Various *zàn* 贊 (encomia), inscriptions, and stele texts for Tiāntái figures and institutions.

Liáng Sù's role as a Hàn-lín-Academy lay-Buddhist promoter of Tiāntái doctrine within the late-Táng court establishment was institutionally significant: he was one of the principal vehicles by which the Tiāntái scholastic tradition gained access to high-level imperial patronage in the late-Táng period.
