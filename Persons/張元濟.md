Major **late-Qīng / Republican-era** 清-民國 scholar, textual editor, and publisher — one of the foundational figures of 20th-century Chinese textual-editorial scholarship. *Zì* **Jú shēng** 菊生; *hào* **Xiǎo zhāi** 筱齋. **1867–1959**. Jìnshì 1892 (the last generation of Qīng civil-service examinees). Co-founder and longtime director of the **商務印書館** (Commercial Press) in Shanghai — under his leadership, the foremost Chinese publishing house of the 20th century.

**Career.** *Jìnshì* 1892 under the Guāng xù 光緒 emperor. Served briefly in various late-Qīng government positions but quickly gravitated toward scholarly-editorial work. In **1901**, he joined the newly-founded **Commercial Press** 商務印書館 in Shanghai — the first major modern Chinese publishing enterprise — as director of the editing-translation department; he remained there for the rest of his long career.

**Editorial legacy.** Zhāng Yuánjì's editorial work constitutes one of the great achievements of 20th-century Chinese philology:

1. **Sìbù cóngkān** 四部叢刊 (1919–1936, 3 series) — monumental facsimile-reproduction series of pre-modern Chinese texts in high-quality photographic reproduction. Over 500 titles published. Chief editor: Zhāng Yuánjì.

2. **Bǎi nà běn Èr shí sì shǐ** 百衲本二十四史 (1930–1937) — the "Patchwork" critical-edition of the Twenty-Four Standard Histories. Chief editor: Zhāng Yuánjì.

3. **Sìbù bèi yào** 四部備要 — companion series to *Sìbù cóngkān*.

4. Various **critical-edition monographs** on individual texts, including the *jiào kān jì* apparatus to the *Kàngcāng zǐ* (see [[KR5c0142]]).

5. **Commercial Press general editor** — oversaw the publication of dictionaries, encyclopaedias, and textbooks that shaped modern Chinese education.

**Engagement with the Kanripo corpus.** Zhāng Yuánjì's *Sìbù cóngkān* editorial work includes several Daoist classics — including the Sòng printed edition of the *Dòng líng zhēn jīng* with Hé Càn's commentary ([[KR5c0142]]). His **critical-edition apparatus** (*jiào kān jì* 校勘記) on these texts provides systematic variant-notes comparing multiple witnesses.

**Significance.** Zhāng Yuánjì is one of the **foundational modern Chinese textual-editorial scholars**, along with **Wáng Yúnwǔ** 王雲五 (his Commercial Press colleague), **Zhāng Shǒulíng** 張壽林, and others. His editorial work made rare classical texts widely accessible to 20th-century scholarship and provided the text-critical foundation for modern Chinese philological studies.

**Dating.** 1867–1959. Spanned the late Qīng, the Republican era (1912–1949), and the early People's Republic. Died Beijing 1959, age 92.
