# Jīn-shì 今釋

Early-Qīng Cáo-dòng 曹洞 Chán master, *zì* Xìng-yīn 性因 / Tián-yīn 恬因, *hào* Dàn-guī 澹歸 (also Gān-zhè-shēng 甘蔗生, Máo-píng nà-sēng 茅坪衲僧, Jiè-shān yě-nà 借山野衲). *Fǎ-sūn* 法孫 of [[道獨]] Zōng-bǎo Dào-dú (1600–1661) in the Shòu-chāng / Bó-shān Cáo-dòng line — transmitted through [[圅昰]] Tiān-rán Hán-shì 天然圅昰 (1608–1685). 28 January 1615 – 1 September 1680 (lunar 萬曆 42.12.29 – 康熙 19.8.9), *shì-shòu* 67. Native of Rén-hé 仁和 (Hángzhōu).

Best known as one of the most prominent **Ming loyalist-official-turned-monks** of the 17th century. As the layman **Jīn Bǎo** 金堡 he held successive Ming-dynasty offices (culminating as a Censor in the Yǒng-lì 永曆 Southern-Ming court, 1647–1650) and was imprisoned, tortured, and exiled for his impeachment of corrupt court-favourites. After the Yǒng-lì court collapsed he tonsured (c. 1651) at Guǎng-zhōu Hǎi-yún-sì under Tiān-rán Hán-shì; received full dharma-transmission from Hán-shì; took the name Jīn-shì 今釋 and the self-style Dàn-guī 澹歸 ("Floating Return").

Late in life he resided at Dān-xiá-shān 丹霞山 (Rén-huà, Guǎng-dōng), founded the Bié-chuán-sì 別傳寺 there, and produced a substantial literary corpus: his own *Biàn-xíng táng-jí* 遍行堂集 (prose, c. 30 juan), the *Dān-xiá Dàn-guī chánshī yǔlù* 丹霞澹歸禪師語錄, and — most consequentially for his role in Shòu-chāng transmission — the *chóng-biān* 重編 of his grand-teacher [[道獨]] Dào-dú's *yǔlù* ([[KR6q0373]]; X72 n1443).

His writings, because of their frank Ming-loyalist content and his persisting connection to the *yí-mín* 遺民 literary network in Guǎng-dōng, were placed on the *Qián-lóng forbidden-books index* 乾隆禁書目錄 in the 1770s, and portions of his prose collection were destroyed; the monastic *yǔlù* survived. He is the subject of a substantial modern Chinese-language secondary literature in the history of 17th-century literati-monks (Chén Yuán 陳垣, 《清初僧諍記》 and 《明季滇黔佛教考》; Wáng Kāi 王凱, et al.).
