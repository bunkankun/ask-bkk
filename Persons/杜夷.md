Dù Yí 杜夷 (258–323), *zì* Xíngqí 行齊, was a Western and Eastern Jìn 晉 classicist and recluse-scholar, a native of Lú-jiāng 廬江 Qián-xiàn 灊縣. His biography is in *Jìn shū* 晉書 j. 91 (*Rú-lín liè-zhuàn*); the dates follow the *Jìn shū* notice. He was famous in his own day for repeatedly declining offers of high office — from Sī-mǎ Lún 司馬倫, Sī-mǎ Yǐng 司馬穎, and (after the southward retreat of 317) the Eastern Jìn court itself — and for sustaining an austere life of scholarship and meditation. Eventually persuaded to take low-ranking office in the late Eastern Jìn court, he held the post of *zhōng-shū lìng* 中書令 only briefly.

His writings included classical commentaries (recorded in the *Suí shū jīngjí zhì*) and the *zǐ*-house treatise *Yōuqiú zǐ* 幽求子 (also given as *Yōuqiú xīnshū* 幽求新書). In the Kanripo corpus he is the attributed author of [[KR3a0136]] *Dù Shǐ yōuqiú xīnshū*. CBDB has no entry.
