# Wéi Yìngwù 韋應物 (737–*ca.* 791)

The principal post-Wáng-Wéi [[王維]] inheritor of the High-Tang *shānshuǐ tiányuán* contemplative-landscape tradition; conventionally paired with Táo Qián 陶潛 in Sū Shì 蘇軾's *Táo-Wéi* 陶韋 couplet. Native of *Chángān* 京兆 (modern Xīān, Shǎnxī), of the powerful *Wéi* clan.

A dissolute young aristocrat in his teens (he served in Xuánzōng's *Sānwèi* 三衛 imperial guard, a sinecure for the high nobility), Wéi self-described in the long autobiographical *Yǔ Lú Fǔyī èrshí èr yùn* 與盧弗一二十二韻 as having gambled, drunk, hunted, and beaten servants in his youth. The An Lùshān rebellion (755–763) forced him out of court service; in its aftermath he turned to study and entered the examinations. Successive provincial *cìshǐ* posts: *Chúzhōu cìshǐ* 滁州刺史 (*ca.* 781), *Jiāngzhōu cìshǐ* 江州刺史 (783), and finally *Sūzhōu cìshǐ* 蘇州刺史 (788–791) — whence *Wéi Sūzhōu*. Died in office at *Sūzhōu* or shortly after retirement *ca.* 791, aged 55.

His extant collection is the *Wéi Sūzhōu jí* [[KR4c0033]] in 10 *juǎn*. His best-known poems — the *Chúzhōu xī jiàn* 滁州西澗 and the *Qiū yè jì Qiū yuánwài* 秋夜寄丘員外 — embody the post-rebellion contemplative-landscape mode. His friendship with the *Wúxìng* monk-poet Jiǎorán [[釋皎然]] during his *Sūzhōu* governorship is the principal documented mid-Tang lay-monastic literary connection.
