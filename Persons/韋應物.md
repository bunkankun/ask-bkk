# Wéi Yìngwù 韋應物 (737–*ca.* 791)

The principal post-Wáng-Wéi [[王維]] inheritor of the High-Tang *shān-shuǐ tián-yuán* contemplative-landscape tradition; conventionally paired with Táo Qián 陶潛 in Sū Shì 蘇軾's *Táo-Wéi* 陶韋 couplet. Native of *Cháng-ān* 京兆 (modern Xī-ān, Shǎn-xī), of the powerful *Wéi* clan.

A dissolute young aristocrat in his teens (he served in Xuán-zōng's *Sān-wèi* 三衛 imperial guard, a sinecure for the high nobility), Wéi self-described in the long autobiographical *Yǔ Lú Fǔ-yī èr-shí èr yùn* 與盧弗一二十二韻 as having gambled, drunk, hunted, and beaten servants in his youth. The An Lùshān rebellion (755–763) forced him out of court service; in its aftermath he turned to study and entered the examinations. Successive provincial *cì-shǐ* posts: *Chú-zhōu cì-shǐ* 滁州刺史 (*ca.* 781), *Jiāng-zhōu cì-shǐ* 江州刺史 (783), and finally *Sū-zhōu cì-shǐ* 蘇州刺史 (788–791) — whence *Wéi Sū-zhōu*. Died in office at *Sū-zhōu* or shortly after retirement *ca.* 791, aged 55.

His extant collection is the *Wéi Sū-zhōu jí* [[KR4c0033]] in 10 *juǎn*. His best-known poems — the *Chú-zhōu xī jiàn* 滁州西澗 and the *Qiū yè jì Qiū yuán-wài* 秋夜寄丘員外 — embody the post-rebellion contemplative-landscape mode. His friendship with the *Wú-xìng* monk-poet Jiǎo-rán [[釋皎然]] during his *Sū-zhōu* governorship is the principal documented mid-Tang lay-monastic literary connection.
