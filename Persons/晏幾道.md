Northern-Sòng *cí*-poet, *zì* Shū-yuán 叔原, *hào* Xiǎo-shān 小山. Youngest son of the councillor and *cí*-master Yàn Shū [[晏殊]] (991–1055); native of Lín-chuān 臨川 (Jiāng-xī). Despite his father's high office, Jǐ-dào never reached substantive office and lived in straitened circumstances, holding only minor posts such as the *Yǐng-chāng-fǔ* prefectural sub-magistrate. His circle included Sū Shì [[蘇軾]], Huáng Tíng-jiān [[黃庭堅]], and the merchant-musician Shěn Lián 沈廉 (and his daughters Lián 蓮, Hóng 鴻, Pín 蘋, Yún 雲, the famous "four women" of the *Xiǎo-shān cí* prefaces). Author of the *Xiǎo-shān cí* [[KR4j0010]] in 1 *juǎn*, the most distinguished collection of Northern-Sòng *xiǎo-lìng* 小令 after his father's *Zhū-yù cí* [[KR4j0001]] and a touchstone of the personal, recollective lyric. Famously withdrew an early collection on Huáng Tíng-jiān's advice; the 1106 (Chóng-níng 5) preface by Huáng for *Xiǎo-shān cí* is one of the most-quoted Sòng *cí*-prefaces. Lifedates not given exactly in any surviving record; the conventional bracket *ca.* 1030 – *ca.* 1106 is reconstructed from his father's lifespan and the Huáng Tíng-jiān preface.
