Late-Míng polymath, scholar-official, bibliographer, and lay Buddhist; one of the central figures of the Wàn-lì 萬曆 era's literati Buddhism. DILA Authority A001685. *Zì* **Ruò-hóu** 弱侯, sobriquets **Dàn-yuán** 澹園 / **Yī-yuán** 漪園, also referenced as **Jiāo tài-shǐ** 焦太史 ("Senior Historian Jiāo"). Native of **Jiāng-níng** 江寧 (Nán-jīng / Jīn-líng). Born 1540; died 1620, aged 80.

A *jǐnshì* 進士 (presented-scholar) of Wàn-lì 17 (1589), placed first in the imperial examinations (*zhuàng-yuán* 狀元) — an extremely rare distinction. Appointed *Hàn-lín bīanxiū* 翰林編修 (Hanlin Academy junior compiler), then *Dōng-gōng jiǎng-dú-guān* 東宮講讀官 (lecturer to the heir apparent). His political career was complicated by factional struggles in the late Wàn-lì court and he was eventually forced into retirement.

His scholarly output was vast and multi-disciplinary: the bibliographical work *Guó-shǐ jīng-jí zhì* 國史經籍志, the historical *Xiàn-zhēng lù* 獻徵錄, the literary *Dàn-yuán xù-jí* 澹園續集, and a substantial Buddhist corpus including the present **Fǎhuá jīng jīng-jiě píng-lín** 法華經精解評林 ([[KR6d0078]], X31n0612, 2 *juan*) — a Lotus Sūtra critical-evaluation compendium.

His Buddhist activity placed him at the centre of the late-Wàn-lì literati Buddhist circle, alongside contemporaries such as **Lǐ Zhì** 李贄 (1527–1602), **Yúan Hóng-dào** 袁宏道 (1568–1610), and the *Míng-mò sì-dà gāo-sēng* 明末四大高僧 (Yún-qī Zhū-hóng 雲棲祩宏, Hān-shān Dé-qīng 憨山德清, Zǐ-bǎi Dá-guān 紫柏達觀, Ǒu-yì Zhì-xù 蕅益智旭). Jiāo Hóng's substantial role as a literati patron of the *Jiā-xīng-zàng* 嘉興藏 publishing project documents the institutional importance of late-Wàn-lì literati Buddhism in the production of the late-Míng monastic publishing renaissance.
