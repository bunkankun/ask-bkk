Founding-Míng statesman, strategist, and man of letters; *zì* Bówēn 伯溫; native of **Qīngtián** 青田 (Chǔzhōu 處州, Zhèjiāng). Yuán *jìnshì* of Yuántǒng 1 (1333). Served the Yuán in minor provincial posts but resigned repeatedly over policy disputes; in 1360 was recruited by **Zhū Yuánzhāng** [[朱元璋]] at Yīngtiān 應天 (Nánjīng) and became one of the principal architects of the strategy that defeated **Chén Yǒuliàng** 陳友諒 (1363, Lake Poyang) and **Zhāng Shìchéng** 張士誠 (1367) and brought down the Yuán (1368). After the Hóngwǔ accession served as *Yùshǐ zhōngchéng* 御史中丞 and *Tàishǐ lìng* 太史令 (1368); enfeoffed as **Chéngyìbó** 誠意伯 ("Earl of Sincere Resolve") in Hóngwǔ 3 (1370). Retired in 1371 and died at home in Qīngtián in Hóngwǔ 8 (1375), age 65 *suì*; the contemporary suspicion that he had been poisoned by **Hú Wéiyōng** 胡惟庸 is reflected in Liú Zhì's [[劉廌]] post-mortem *Yìyùn lù* and is cautiously accepted by most modern scholars. Posthumous title *Wénchéng* 文成.

Conventionally bracketed with **Sòng Lián** [[宋濂]] as the two greatest literary figures of the founding Hóngwǔ generation. The Sìkù tíyào to his collected works [[KR4e0005]] reads his place as analogous to that of the great Jīn-Yuán statesmen **Yēlǜ Chǔcái** 耶律楚材 and **Liú Bǐngzhōng** 劉秉忠, but his prose as superior to theirs. His most influential single composition is the *Yùlí zǐ* 郁離子, a Yuán-period (c. 1356–1358) political-allegorical fable-collection composed during his retreat from Yuán office, regarded as the immediate forerunner of Míng vernacular prose. The late-Míng / Qīng folklore attribution of the divinatory *Shāobǐng gē* 燒餅歌 to Liú is spurious. His SBCK collection is at [[KR4e0006]] (*Tàishī Chéngyìbó Liú Wénchéng gōng jí*).

Standard biographies: *Míng shǐ* j. 128 (with Sòng Lián); Goodrich & Fang, *Dictionary of Ming Biography* 1:932–938 (entry by Hok-lam Chan). Universally well-documented; no CBDB id required.
