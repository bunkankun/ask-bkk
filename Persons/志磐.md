Late Southern-Sòng Tiāntái historian-monk and ritualist; lifedates not preserved (DILA A000502). Style **Dà-shí** 大石 ("Great Stone"), with the further sobriquets *Yuè-bō* 月波 ("Moon-on-Waves") and *Dōng-hú* 東湖 (after his residence). Dharma-heir of *Wú-zhù Zōng-jìng fǎ-shī* 無住宗淨法師. Tonsured at *Sì-míng Fú-quán* 四明福泉, he then settled at *Dōng-hú-sì* 東湖寺 in Níng-bō (Sì-míng 四明), where he produced two major works: the *Fó-zǔ tǒng-jì* 佛祖統紀, the standard 54-fascicle Tiāntái-perspective universal Buddhist history (its self-preface dated *Xián-chún* 5 = 1269; T49n2035 / [[KR6r0012]]), and the present *Fǎ-jiè shèng-fán shuǐ-lù shèng-huì xiū-zhāi yí-guǐ* ([[KR6k0204]], X1497), the standard liturgical handbook for the *Shuǐ-lù* mass. A third work attributed to him, the *Zōng-mén zūn-zǔ yí* 宗門尊祖儀, does not survive. He is described in later gazetteers as widely-read and elegantly-written, with a polemical eye for distinguishing legitimate Tiāntái transmission from rival lineages.
