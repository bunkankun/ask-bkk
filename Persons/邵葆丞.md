Late-Guāng-xù-era Qīng minor official and physician of Jiāngxià 江夏 (= Wǔhàn region, Húběi). Originally a *cìshǐ* 剌史 (prefect / district-magistrate), Shào abandoned his official career to practise medicine, holding daily half-day free clinics for the poor — his *Xiǎoān yàowō* 小安藥窩 ("Little Peaceful Medicine-Nest") studio at Zhèngchéng 鄭城 (place uncertain — possibly Zhèngzhōu, Hénán) was the locus of his late-career writing. Claimed descent from [[邵雍]] Shào Yōng (Kāngjié 康節, 1011–1077). His son **Shào Zhīgào** 邵芝誥 held a *xūcì* 需次 appointment in Hubei. Composed [[KR3er102]] *Yīyì yīlǐ* 醫易一理 in *Guāngxù* 23 / 1897 at over seventy *suì*, with prefaces by his in-law Wáng Jǐngyí (1898) and the Wǔ-hàn-based official Chén Qióng (1901). The work is one of the most philosophically ambitious late-Qīng *zhōngxī huìtōng* 中西匯通 (Sino-Western integrative medicine) projects, integrating Western anatomical knowledge with the *Yìjīng* hexagrammic apparatus. Best known by his *hào* Sìjiǔ jūshì 四九居士.
