Late-Guāng-xù-era Qīng minor official and physician of Jiāng-xià 江夏 (= Wǔ-hàn region, Hú-běi). Originally a *cì-shǐ* 剌史 (prefect / district-magistrate), Shào abandoned his official career to practise medicine, holding daily half-day free clinics for the poor — his *Xiǎo-ān yào-wō* 小安藥窩 ("Little Peaceful Medicine-Nest") studio at Zhèng-chéng 鄭城 (place uncertain — possibly Zhèng-zhōu, Hé-nán) was the locus of his late-career writing. Claimed descent from [[邵雍]] Shào Yōng (Kāng-jié 康節, 1011–1077). His son **Shào Zhī-gào** 邵芝誥 held a *xū-cì* 需次 appointment in Hubei. Composed [[KR3er102]] *Yī-yì yī-lǐ* 醫易一理 in *Guāng-xù* 23 / 1897 at over seventy *suì*, with prefaces by his in-law Wáng Jǐng-yí (1898) and the Wǔ-hàn-based official Chén Qióng (1901). The work is one of the most philosophically ambitious late-Qīng *zhōng-xī huì-tōng* 中西匯通 (Sino-Western integrative medicine) projects, integrating Western anatomical knowledge with the *Yì-jīng* hexagrammic apparatus. Best known by his *hào* Sì-jiǔ jū-shì 四九居士.
