Liú Yǔxī 劉禹錫 (772–842), *zì* Mèng-dé 夢得, was a *Zhōng-shān Wú-jí* 中山無極 native (the *Péng-chéng* designation in his *Xīn Táng-shū* biography is the ancestral seat). *Jìn-shì* of *Zhēn-yuán* 9 (793) and *bó-xué hóng-cí* of the same year. He served at the Censorate and rose under the *Yǒng-zhēn* reform faction of Wáng Shū-wén 王叔文 in 805; the faction's collapse the same year produced the *Bā sī-mǎ* (Eight Marshals) episode, in which Liú was banished for ten years to *Lǎng-zhōu* 朗州. Recalled to *Cháng-ān* in 815, he was again exiled (to *Lián-zhōu* 連州) after the *Yuán-dū guān* peach-blossom poem incident; subsequent postings included *Kuí-zhōu* and *Hé-zhōu*. Late in life he served as *Tài-zǐ bīn-kè* (Lord-Guest of the Heir Apparent) — hence the title *Liú Bīn-kè*. Throughout his career he maintained a lifelong literary friendship with **Liǔ Zōng-yuán** [[柳宗元]] (the *Liú-Liǔ* pairing) and a sustained *chàng-hé* exchange with **Bái Jū-yì** [[白居易]].

Liú's principal works in the corpus: *Liú Bīn-kè wén-jí* [[KR4c0051]] (verse + prose, 30 + 10 *juǎn*; edited in part by Sòng Mǐn-qiú [[宋敏求]]). His best-known prose includes the *Lòu-shì míng* 陋室銘, the *Tiān lùn* 天論 (a substantial *yuán-qì*-cosmology essay countering Hán Yù's heaven-arguments), and the *Zǐ Liú-zǐ zì-zhuàn* 子劉子自傳 (autobiographical apologia for Wáng Shū-wén). His verse, particularly the *zhú-zhī cí* 竹枝詞 ("bamboo-branch songs," based on Sì-chuān folk forms) and the *Yáng-liǔ zhī cí* 楊柳枝詞, transformed Táng *yuè-fǔ* practice. CBDB id 33606.
