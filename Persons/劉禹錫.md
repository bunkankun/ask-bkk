Liú Yǔxī 劉禹錫 (772–842), *zì* Mèngdé 夢得, was a *Zhōngshān Wújí* 中山無極 native (the *Péngchéng* designation in his *Xīn Tángshū* biography is the ancestral seat). *Jìnshì* of *Zhēnyuán* 9 (793) and *bóxué hóngcí* of the same year. He served at the Censorate and rose under the *Yǒngzhēn* reform faction of Wáng Shūwén 王叔文 in 805; the faction's collapse the same year produced the *Bā sīmǎ* (Eight Marshals) episode, in which Liú was banished for ten years to *Lǎngzhōu* 朗州. Recalled to *Chángān* in 815, he was again exiled (to *Liánzhōu* 連州) after the *Yuándū guān* peach-blossom poem incident; subsequent postings included *Kuízhōu* and *Hézhōu*. Late in life he served as *Tàizǐ bīnkè* (Lord-Guest of the Heir Apparent) — hence the title *Liú Bīnkè*. Throughout his career he maintained a lifelong literary friendship with **Liǔ Zōngyuán** [[柳宗元]] (the *Liú-Liǔ* pairing) and a sustained *chànghé* exchange with **Bái Jūyì** [[白居易]].

Liú's principal works in the corpus: *Liú Bīnkè wénjí* [[KR4c0051]] (verse + prose, 30 + 10 *juǎn*; edited in part by Sòng Mǐnqiú [[宋敏求]]). His best-known prose includes the *Lòushì míng* 陋室銘, the *Tiān lùn* 天論 (a substantial *yuánqì*-cosmology essay countering Hán Yù's heaven-arguments), and the *Zǐ Liúzǐ zìzhuàn* 子劉子自傳 (autobiographical apologia for Wáng Shūwén). His verse, particularly the *zhúzhī cí* 竹枝詞 ("bamboo-branch songs," based on Sìchuān folk forms) and the *Yángliǔ zhī cí* 楊柳枝詞, transformed Táng *yuèfǔ* practice. CBDB id 33606.
