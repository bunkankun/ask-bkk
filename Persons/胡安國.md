Hú Ān-guó 胡安國 (1074–1138), *zì* Kānghóu 康侯, posthumously *Wén-dìng-gōng* 文定公, also called *Wǔyí xiānsheng* 武夷先生, was a major Northern–Southern Sòng transition classicist and *Chūnqiū* specialist. Native of Chóng-ān 崇安 (Fú-jiàn). *Jìnshì* of Shào-shèng 4 (1097); rose under Huī-zōng to Tài-cháng bóshì; out of office during the Cài Jīng dominance, returning under Gāozōng. His magnum opus is the *Chūnqiū chuán* 春秋傳 — the standard Southern-Sòng *Chūnqiū* commentary, designated authoritative by the imperial *Sì-shū wǔ-jīng dàquán* under Yǒng-lè (1415) and the principal Chinese *Chūnqiū* commentary in the Korean and Japanese reception. He is the founder of the *Hú-shì* 胡氏 *Chūnqiū* school continued by his son Hú Hóng 胡宏, his nephew Hú Yìn 胡寅, and others. He is also responsible (with Zēng Tián) for one of the two principal early *yǔ-lù*-records of Xiè Liángzuǒ's teaching, preserved within the *Shàngcài yǔ-lù* (KR3a0039). Biography: *Sòng shǐ* j. 435.
