Hú Ānguó 胡安國 (1074–1138), *zì* Kānghóu 康侯, posthumously *Wéndìnggōng* 文定公, also called *Wǔyí xiānsheng* 武夷先生, was a major Northern–Southern Sòng transition classicist and *Chūnqiū* specialist. Native of Chóngān 崇安 (Fújiàn). *Jìnshì* of Shàoshèng 4 (1097); rose under Huīzōng to Tàicháng bóshì; out of office during the Cài Jīng dominance, returning under Gāozōng. His magnum opus is the *Chūnqiū chuán* 春秋傳 — the standard Southern-Sòng *Chūnqiū* commentary, designated authoritative by the imperial *Sìshū wǔjīng dàquán* under Yǒnglè (1415) and the principal Chinese *Chūnqiū* commentary in the Korean and Japanese reception. He is the founder of the *Húshì* 胡氏 *Chūnqiū* school continued by his son Hú Hóng 胡宏, his nephew Hú Yìn 胡寅, and others. He is also responsible (with Zēng Tián) for one of the two principal early *yǔlù*-records of Xiè Liángzuǒ's teaching, preserved within the *Shàngcài yǔlù* ([[KR3a0039]]). Biography: *Sòng shǐ* j. 435. CBDB id 3394, dates 1074–1138.
