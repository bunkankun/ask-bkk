Ninkū 仁空 (1309–1388) was a late-Kamakura / Nanboku-chō Tendai master and a major figure of the **Hiei-zan precept-revival movement**. He is associated with the **Kurodani 黑谷 lineage** of Mt. Hiei and with the **Daijō-ji** 大乘寺 / **Saikyō-ji** 西教寺 institutional complex at Sakamoto, on the slopes of Mt. Hiei. The Kurodani lineage was the principal medieval Tendai sub-school dedicated to the disciplined observance of the **Brahmajāla bodhisattva precepts** in the Tendai *yuánjiè* tradition, and Ninkū was one of its leading 14th-century systematizers.

His [[KR6t0080]] *Xīn-xué pú-sà xíng-yào chāo* is the **canonical procedural handbook of the Kurodani-Daijō-ji precept tradition**, providing day-to-day liturgical regulations for the bodhisattva-bhikṣu under the *Brahmajāla* precept platform (six recollections, robe-and-bowl care, alms, time-meal, *poṣadha*, proxy-consent, rains-retreat, *pravāraṇā*, ordination procedure, lay-transformation regulations). The work was printed posthumously in 1400 by his disciple Myōkū 明空 of Rōzan-ji 廬山寺.

Ninkū should be situated as a forerunner of the **Tendai Shingi-shū** 天台真盛宗 tradition founded by his lineage-successor **Shinsei** 真盛 (1443–1495), and as a contemporary of the more famous **Yōkaku** 圓覺 / **Yōkaku Shōnin** of the *kuden* Tendai-precept-revival movement.

Within the Kanripo corpus his preserved work is [[KR6t0080]] *Xīnxué púsà xíngyào chāo*.
