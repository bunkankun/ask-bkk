Mid- to late-Táng official and *xiǎoshuō* writer, *zì* Zhīqián 之乾 (some sources Zhīgān), of *Càizhōu* 蔡州 *Zhūyáng* 朱陽 (modern Hénán). Son of Yuán Zī 袁滋 (749–818), the Dé-zōng-Xiàn-zōng-era grand-councillor and ambassador-extraordinary to the *Nánzhào* kingdom in *Zhēnyuán* 10 (794). *Jìnshì* of the mid-Táng. By *Xiántōng* 9 (868) he was *Hànlín xuéshì* and *Zhōngshū shěrén*; by *Qiánfú* 1 (874) *Lǐbù lángzhōng*; eventually rose to *Húbù shìláng*. His [[KR3l0112]] *Gānzé yáo* 甘澤謠, composed during the rainy season of 868, is one of the most influential single late-Táng *chuánqí* collections — the source of the *Niè Yǐnniáng*, *Hóngxiàn*, and *Yuánguān* / *Three Lives Stone* tales. CBDB id 94193; death-year given as 844 in some CBDB entries (probably a slip; he was still in office in 874).
