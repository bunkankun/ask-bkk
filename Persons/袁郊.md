Mid- to late-Táng official and *xiǎo-shuō* writer, *zì* Zhī-qián 之乾 (some sources Zhī-gān), of *Cài-zhōu* 蔡州 *Zhū-yáng* 朱陽 (modern Hé-nán). Son of Yuán Zī 袁滋 (749–818), the Dé-zōng-Xiàn-zōng-era grand-councillor and ambassador-extraordinary to the *Nán-zhào* kingdom in *Zhēn-yuán* 10 (794). *Jìn-shì* of the mid-Táng. By *Xián-tōng* 9 (868) he was *Hàn-lín xué-shì* and *Zhōng-shū shě-rén*; by *Qián-fú* 1 (874) *Lǐ-bù láng-zhōng*; eventually rose to *Hú-bù shì-láng*. His [[KR3l0112]] *Gān-zé yáo* 甘澤謠, composed during the rainy season of 868, is one of the most influential single late-Táng *chuán-qí* collections — the source of the *Niè Yǐn-niáng*, *Hóng-xiàn*, and *Yuán-guān* / *Three Lives Stone* tales. CBDB id 94193; death-year given as 844 in some CBDB entries (probably a slip; he was still in office in 874).
