**Kālayaśas** (畺良耶舍, c. 383–442) — Central-Asian Buddhist monk-translator (西域三藏) of the Liú-Sòng (劉宋) period, one of the principal early-fifth-century translators of *visualisation-sūtras* (觀經) into Chinese. Liu-Song-period sources gloss his name as 時稱 ("Time-Renown"). His traditional Chinese-Buddhist biography is preserved in the *Liáng Gāo-sēng zhuàn* 梁高僧傳 (T2059, fasc. 3, p. 343c11ff).

Sources establish that he was widely versed in the **Tripiṭaka** with a particular specialisation in *dhyāna* (禪為專業). In **Yuán-jiā 1 / 424 CE**, he reached *Jiànyè* 建業 (= Jiànkāng 建康, modern Nanjing). Emperor **Wén of Sòng** 宋文帝 (Liú Yìlóng 劉義隆, r. 424–453) received him with the highest honours and installed him at the **Zhōng-shān Dào-lín jīng-shè** 鍾山道林精舍 — the major imperial monastic establishment of the Liú-Sòng court.

His principal extant translations include:

- **Guān Wúliàngshòu jīng** 觀無量壽經 (T0365) — the *Sūtra on the Contemplation of the Buddha of Immeasurable Life*, one of the *Three Pure-Land Sūtras* (淨土三部經) and the foundational visualisation-text for the East Asian Pure-Land tradition.
- **Guān Yào-wáng Yào-shàng èr pú-sà jīng** 觀藥王藥上二菩薩經 ([[KR6j0383]], T1161) — the *Sūtra on the Contemplation of the Two Bodhisattvas Bhaiṣajyarāja and Bhaiṣajyasamudgata*, the canonical visualisation-source for the East Asian Buddhist healing-cult.

His introduction of the *guānjīng* (visualisation-sūtra) genre into Chinese had vast doctrinal consequences for the Pure-Land tradition (Shàndǎo's seventh-century synthesis is unimaginable without his *Guān Wúliàngshòu jīng*) and for the iconographic-and-soteriological framing of the Buddhist healing-cult.
