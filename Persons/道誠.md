Northern Sòng monk-scholar of **Qián-táng** 錢塘 (modern Hángzhōu), styled **Huì-wù dà-shī** 慧悟大師 ("Great Master of Compassionate Awakening") and resident successively at **Lóng-huá Chán-fǔ** 龍華禪府 and the **Yuè-lún Mountain hermitage** 月輪山 in his home prefecture. Birth and death dates are not preserved.

Active in **Xián-píng** 咸平 / **Jǐng-dé** 景德 (998–1007) as a **cì-zǐ shā-mén** 賜紫沙門 ("purple-robed *śramaṇa*") on the lecture circuit at imperial monasteries in **Biàn-jīng** 汴京 (the Northern Sòng capital, modern Kāifēng), then withdrew east to Qián-táng for a ten-year canon-reading retreat (ca. 1009–1019). The retreat was the immediate occasion for his compilation of the **Shì-shì yào-lǎn** 釋氏要覽 ([[KR6s0005]], T2127), prompted in its final form by **Zhēn-zōng's** Tiānxǐ 3 (1019) empire-wide ordination amnesty for monastic novices. The work was finalized with a gentry preface in Tiānxǐ 4 (1020).

He also produced the standard annotated edition of **Wáng Bó's** 王勃 *Shì-jiā rú-lái chéng-dào jì* — the **Shì-jiā rú-lái chéng-dào jì zhù** 釋迦如來成道記註 (X1509, two juan) — and is credited with two now-lost works: the *Ní méng-qiú* 尼蒙求 (one juan, an ABC primer for nuns) and *Shì-shì xū-zhī* 釋氏須知 (three juan).

The Sòng *Fó-zǔ tǒng-jì* 佛祖統紀, j. 44, lists him among the noted Tiāntái-aligned Sòng monastic lecturers of Zhēn-zōng's reign.
