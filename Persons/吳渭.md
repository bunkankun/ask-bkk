**Wú Wèi** 吳渭 (fl. c. 1270–1290), *zì* **Qīng-wēng** 清翁, sobriquet **Qián-zhāi** 潛齋 ("Hidden Studio"). Native of **Pú-jiāng** 浦江 in Wū-zhōu 婺州 (Jīn-huá 金華, Zhèjiāng). Held the office of **Magistrate (xiàn-lìng) of Yì-wū** 義烏 under the late Southern Sòng. After the Sòng surrender (1276) he refused all Yuán recruitment and retired to **Wú-xī** 吳溪 in Pú-jiāng. There in **Zhì-yuán 23–24 (1286–1287)** he founded the **Yuè-quán yín-shè** 月泉吟社 — the largest Sòng-loyalist poetry contest of the early Yuán — calling for submissions on the topic *Chūn-rì tián-yuán zá-xīng* 春日田園雜興 ("Mixed Inspirations on Spring-Day Farm-Garden", after Fàn Chéng-dà's well-known series) in *wǔ-* and *qī-yán lǜ-shī* form, distributed in the tenth month and harvested by the Lantern Festival of the following year. 2,735 *juǎn* (rolls) were submitted; **Fāng Fèng** ([[方鳳]]), **Xiè Áo** ([[謝翱]]), and **Wú Sī-qí** ([[吳思齊]]) were brought in as judges; 280 entries were selected and posted on the third day of the third month. The resulting anthology [[KR4h0060]] preserves 60 of these entries and 74 poems together with the *shè-yuē* (Society Pact), the *shì-wén* (Oath), and the critical apparatus — making it a unique documentary witness to **Sòng-yí-mín literary sociology**.
