**Zhū Xī** 朱熹 (1130–1200), *zì* Yuán huì 元晦 / Zhòng huì 仲晦, *hào* Huì'ān 晦庵 / Kǎo tíng 考亭 / Zǐ yáng 紫陽, posthumously Wén gōng 文公. The supreme synthesizer of **Dào xué** 道學 Neo-Confucianism, whose fusion of Zhōu Dūn yí, the Chéng brothers, Zhāng Zài, and the *Sì shū* 四書 (Four Books, selected and elevated by Zhū as the core Confucian curriculum) shaped East Asian philosophy, education, and orthodoxy for the next seven centuries. Chief works include the *Sì shū jí zhù* 四書集注, the *Yì xué qǐ méng* 易學啟蒙, and a massive corpus of letters, recorded sayings (*yǔ lù* 語錄), and edited anthologies. Under the pseudonym **Zōu Xīn** 鄒訢, he wrote the [[KR5d0018]] *Zhōu yì cān tóng qì kǎo yì* 周易參同契考異 — a philological-philosophical study of the foundational Daoist alchemical classic — demonstrating the breadth of his scholarly interests into Daoist metaphysics.
