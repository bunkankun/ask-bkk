Mid-Míng polymath, *zì* Yòngxiū 用修, *hào* Shēngān 升庵, of Xīndū 新都 (Sichuan). Son of the grand councillor Yáng Tínghé 楊廷和. Granted *jìnshì zhuàngyuán* (first place) in Zhèngdé 6 (1511) at age 24; held *Hànlín xiūzhuàn*, *Jīngyán jiǎngguān*. In Jiājìng 3 (1524), as a leader of the protest against the *Dà lǐ yì* (the *Great Rites Controversy*), he was beaten with court rods and exiled to Yúnnán; remained there for the rest of his life (1524–1559), refused permission to return even after the legal expiry of his sentence. The most prolific Míng *xiǎoxué* scholar; some 400+ works attributed to him. Two of his philological works are preserved in the Sìkù under KR1j: *Qízì yùn* [[KR1j0044]] and *Gǔyīn piánzì* [[KR1j0045]], both products of his exile-period scholarship. The Sìkù's overall judgment is that Yáng Shèn's lexicography is enormous in scope but uneven in critical method; his contemporary Jiāo Hóng [[焦竑]] called him "the most precise scholar of *zì*-learning of the [Míng] dynasty." Catalog meta gives lifedates 1488–1559, in agreement with the standard biographical record. No CBDB entry can be confirmed.
