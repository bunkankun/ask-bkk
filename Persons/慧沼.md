The **second patriarch of the Chinese Yogācāra (Fǎxiàng / Cí'ēn) school** 法相宗 / 慈恩宗, the principal Chinese institutional successor to **Xuánzàng** 玄奘 (602–664) and **Kuījī** 窺基 (632–682). DILA Authority A001708. Lay surname **Liú** 劉; native of **Zī-zhōu** 淄州 (modern Shāndōng); honorifics include **Sān-dōng yī-biàn-zhào** 山東一遍照 ("the One Universal Illuminator of Shāndōng") and **Zī-zhōu bàn-biàn Zhāo** 淄州半徧沼 ("Half-Universal Zhāo of Zī-zhōu"), both reflecting his stature as the principal northern transmitter of Cí'ēn doctrine in the late seventh and early eighth centuries.

Born Zhèngguān 貞觀 22 (648 CE); died in the Kāiyuán 開元 era between Kāiyuán 14 and 29 (726–741); age at death conventionally given as 94 (alternatively 79). The biographical data are not entirely consistent across sources; the most thorough modern reconstruction (Yáng Jiànxiāo 2018) settles on a defensible bracket of c. 648 – c. 741.

He took monastic ordination in his youth and entered Xuánzàng's translation bureau as a translator-disciple while still relatively junior; after Xuánzàng's death (664) he became a principal disciple of Kuījī and inherited the systematic exposition of Yogācāra doctrine in the Cí'ēn-sì 慈恩寺 lineage. After Kuījī's death (682) he became the recognised institutional head of the school, teaching primarily at the Dà-yún-sì 大雲寺 in Zī-zhōu and at the Dà-jiàn-fú-sì 大薦福寺 in Cháng'ān.

Major surviving works include the **Chéng-wéishí lùn liǎo-yì dēng** 成唯識論了義燈 (T1832, the standard Yogācāra subcommentary on Xuán-zàng's *Chéng-wéishí lùn*), the **Yīn-míng rù zhèng-lǐ lùn yì-zuǎn-yào** 因明入正理論義纂要 (T1840, on Buddhist epistemology / *hetuvidyā*), the **Yīn-míng zhèng-lǐ-mén lùn shū** 因明正理門論疏 (T1839), the **Néng-xiǎn zhōng-biān huì-rì lùn** 能顯中邊慧日論 (T1863), and the present **Fǎhuá xuán-zàn yì-jué** 法華玄贊義決 ([[KR6d0027]], T1724) — Huìzhāo's defense of Kuījī's Yogācāra reading of the Lotus Sūtra.
