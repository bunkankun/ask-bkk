# Chéng Dàwèi 程大位

Style name *Rǔsī* 汝思, sobriquet *Bīnqú* 賓渠. Native of Xiūníng 休寧 (Huīzhōu 徽州 prefecture, southern Ānhuī — the major Huī-merchant region). Born Jiājìng 12 (1533); died Wànlì 34 (1606). CBDB id 65808 gives the dates 1520–1600; the more widely accepted lifedates from the *Chéngshì jiāpǔ* 程氏家譜 (Chéng Family Genealogy) and the work's own dated prefaces are 1533–1606, followed here. The CBDB record likely reflects an earlier mistranscription; the genealogy is the authoritative source.

A Huīzhōu merchant-mathematician of the late Míng. According to his own preface to the *Suànfǎ tǒngzōng*, Chéng Dàwèi worked as a commercial agent (his family's profession) traveling on business throughout the lower-Yangzi region and the southern provinces, gathering mathematical books and instructional materials as he went; in his later years he settled at the family seat at Bīnqú 賓渠 (his sobriquet) and devoted himself to the compilation of the *Suànfǎ tǒngzōng*.

His principal works:

(1) **The *Xīnbiān zhízhǐ suànfǎ tǒngzōng* 新編直指算法統宗** ([[KR3fc027]]), more commonly *Suànfǎ tǒngzōng* 算法統宗, in 17 *juàn* — the great Wàn-lì-period mathematical compendium of 1592, the most influential mathematical work of the late Míng and the foundational textbook of Míng-Qīng abacus arithmetic.

(2) **The *Suànfǎ zuǎnyào* 算法纂要** in 4 *juàn* (1598) — an abridgement of the *Tǒngzōng* for everyday commercial use, also widely circulated.

Chéng Dàwèi's *Suànfǎ tǒngzōng* is the principal pre-modern Chinese abacus textbook and the central document of late-imperial Chinese practical-mathematical literature. Its influence — through repeated reprinting (over thirty editions by the early Qīng), through its transmission to Korea and Japan, and through its incorporation into the standard merchant-apprentice curriculum across the Qīng dynasty — is unmatched among Míng-Qīng mathematical works. The cliché *suànpán* 算盤 (abacus) form of mid-Míng commercial arithmetic is essentially Chéng Dàwèi's; the standardized abacus-mnemonics (*kǒujué*) that he codifies in the *Tǒngzōng* were the universal training material for commercial computation through the late-imperial period and into the twentieth century.
