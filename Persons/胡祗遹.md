Hú Zhī-yù 胡祗遹 (1227–1295), *zì* Shào-kāi 紹開 (per *Yuán-shǐ*; the *Sìkù* editors suspect this is a transcription error for Shào-wén 紹聞, since Hú's family-letter quotes the *Zhōu-shū Kāng-gào* "*shào-wén yī-dé yán*"), *hào* Zǐ-shān 紫山, posthumous *shì* Wén-jìng 文靖, native of Wǔ-ān 武安 in Cí-zhōu 磁州 (Hé-běi). In Zhōng-tǒng beginning (1260) recruited by Zhāng Wén-qiān 張文謙 (Vice-Director-of-the-Left at Dà-míng) as *yuán-wài-láng*; entered the central *Zhōng-shū-shěng* as *xiáng-dìng-guān*; in Zhì-yuán 1 (1264) by imperial edict joined the newly-founded Yuán Hàn-lín-yuàn as *Yìng-fèng Hàn-lín wén-zì* concurrent *Tài-cháng-bó-shì* — playing a major role in restoring the Yuán ritual-and-music system. Successively *Hù-bù yuán-wài-láng*, *Zuǒ-yòu-sī*; ran afoul of the senior Mongol financial-administrator Ahmad Fanākatī (Āhāmǎ 阿哈瑪) and was nominally lenient-promoted out of the central government to Vice-Director of Tài-yuán with concurrent *tí-jǔ tiě-yě* (iron-foundry inspection); replaced as *Hé-dōng tí-xíng fù-shǐ*; rose to *Jīng-Hú-běi-dào xuān-wèi fù-shǐ*, *Jì-níng-lù zǒng-guǎn*, ultimately *Jiāng-nán Zhè-xī-dào tí-xíng àn-chá-shǐ*. Posthumously promoted *Lǐ-bù shàng-shū* in Yán-yòu 5 (1318), *shì* Wén-jìng. His original 67-*juàn* collection was lost; the present 26-*juàn* *Zǐ-shān dà-quán-jí* [[KR4d0443]] was reassembled by his son Hú Chí 胡持 and his student Liú Gēng 劉賡. *Yuán-shǐ* j. 170.
