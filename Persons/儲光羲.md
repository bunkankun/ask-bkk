# Chǔ Guāngxī 儲光羲 (706–762)

Native of *Yǎn-zhōu* 兗州 *Lǔ-guó* (modern *Yǎn-zhōu*, Shān-dōng) per the *Tángshū yì-wén zhì*; alternative tradition (Yīn Fán 殷璠's *Dān-yáng jí* 丹陽集) makes him a *Yán-líng* 延陵 (modern *Cháng-zhōu* 常州, Jiāng-sū) man — the discrepancy is unresolved in the standard sources. *Jìn-shì* of *Kāi-yuán* 14 (726), the same year as Cuī Guó-fǔ 崔國輔 and Qí-mǔ Qián 綦母潛; further summoned for special promotion through a *Zhōng-shū-shěng* drafting test. Successively *Tài-zhù* 太祝, *Yǒng-jiā xiàn lìng* 永嘉縣令, and *jiān-chá yù-shǐ* 監察御史.

Captured at *Cháng-ān* during the An Lù-shān rebellion (756) and forced to take office under the rebel court at Luò-yáng. After the recovery of the capital he was demoted and exiled to *Líng-nán* 嶺南; he died in obscurity *ca.* 762.

His original 70-*juǎn* literary corpus — including a 15-*juǎn Zhèng-lùn* 政論 (treatise on government) and a 20-*juǎn Jiǔ-jīng fēn-shū yì* 九經分疏義 (sub-commentary on the Nine Classics) — is largely lost; only the 5-*juǎn Chǔ Guāng-xī shī jí* [[KR4c0026]] survives. Yīn Fán's contemporary evaluation in *Hé-yuè yīng-líng jí*: "*xuē jìn cháng-yán, dé Hào-rán zhī qì* 削盡常言得浩然之氣" — "cutting away all conventional speech, having Mèng Hào-rán's spirit." A leading figure of the High-Táng *shān-shuǐ tián-yuán* 山水田園 school after Wáng Wéi [[王維]] and Mèng Hào-rán [[孟浩然]], with a particularly pronounced Táo Qián 陶潛 inheritance.
