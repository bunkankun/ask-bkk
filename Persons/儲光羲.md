# Chǔ Guāngxī 儲光羲 (706–762)

Native of *Yǎnzhōu* 兗州 *Lǔguó* (modern *Yǎnzhōu*, Shāndōng) per the *Tángshū yìwén zhì*; alternative tradition (Yīn Fán 殷璠's *Dānyáng jí* 丹陽集) makes him a *Yánlíng* 延陵 (modern *Chángzhōu* 常州, Jiāngsū) man — the discrepancy is unresolved in the standard sources. *Jìnshì* of *Kāiyuán* 14 (726), the same year as Cuī Guófǔ 崔國輔 and Qímǔ Qián 綦母潛; further summoned for special promotion through a *Zhōngshūshěng* drafting test. Successively *Tàizhù* 太祝, *Yǒngjiā xiàn lìng* 永嘉縣令, and *jiānchá yùshǐ* 監察御史.

Captured at *Chángān* during the An Lùshān rebellion (756) and forced to take office under the rebel court at Luòyáng. After the recovery of the capital he was demoted and exiled to *Língnán* 嶺南; he died in obscurity *ca.* 762.

His original 70-*juǎn* literary corpus — including a 15-*juǎn Zhènglùn* 政論 (treatise on government) and a 20-*juǎn Jiǔjīng fēnshū yì* 九經分疏義 (sub-commentary on the Nine Classics) — is largely lost; only the 5-*juǎn Chǔ Guāngxī shī jí* [[KR4c0026]] survives. Yīn Fán's contemporary evaluation in *Héyuè yīnglíng jí*: "*xuē jìn chángyán, dé Hàorán zhī qì* 削盡常言得浩然之氣" — "cutting away all conventional speech, having Mèng Hàorán's spirit." A leading figure of the High-Táng *shānshuǐ tiányuán* 山水田園 school after Wáng Wéi [[王維]] and Mèng Hàorán [[孟浩然]], with a particularly pronounced Táo Qián 陶潛 inheritance.
