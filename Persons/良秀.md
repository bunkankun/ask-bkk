**Jippō Ryōshū** 實峰良秀 (Bunpō 2 → 1318; Ōei 12 / 1405-08-24), Late-Nanbokuchō / early-Muromachi Japanese **Sōtō-Zen** master in the Sōji-ji-line, dharma-heir of [[峨山]] **Gasan Jōseki** 峨山韶碩 (1276–1366). Style-name (字) Jippō 實峰 ("True-Peak"); dharma-name Ryōshū 良秀. Native of Echizen 越前 province.

One of [[峨山]] Gasan's **Five Disciples** (峨山五哲 *Gasan no goteki*) and the founder of the **Jippō-ha** 實峰派 sub-lineage of Sōji-ji. Successively abbot of multiple Sōji-ji-line foundations in the Hokuriku and Ōmi-Mino region, principal of which is recorded in his recorded sayings ([[KR6t0299]]) as the *Yōshō* abbacy. The preface to his recorded sayings by **Geppa Dōin** 月坡道印 (1675) identifies him as *"a sixth-generation grandson-in-dharma of the *Eihei old-Buddha* (Dōgen) and the heir of the *Gasan great* (Gasan Jōseki); he turned upside-down the *Soji-ji No-Character Seal*, with form like an iron-ox machine in full play, and freely played the *Shintō Boxwood Stringless Lute*, with notes that taught wood-people to sing and dance."*

His recorded sayings, the *Jippō Zenji goroku* 實峰禪師語録 ([[KR6t0299]]), survived in manuscript half-rotted by rain in a *kasaya-bag* before being recovered, collated, and printed by Geppa Dōin alongside the *Tsugen Reizenji manroku* ([[KR6t0298]]) as part of his late-Edo Sōtō-Zen *fukko* recovery campaign. The Jippō-ha sub-line is one of the four principal Sōji-ji sub-lineages active through the Edo period.
