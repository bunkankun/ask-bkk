**Shōkū** 證空 (Jishō 1 / 11 / 9 = 1177-12-30 → Kangen 5 / 11 / 26 = 1247-12-24), early-Kamakura Jōdo-shū master, the **founder of the Seizan branch** (西山派) of Pure-Land — one of the major post-Hōnen branches of Japanese Pure-Land Buddhism, parallel to the Chinzei branch of Benchō and Ryōchū. Common appellation **Zen'e-bō Shōkū** 善慧房證空; sectarian designation **Seizan Shōnin** 西山上人 ("Saint of the Western Mountains" — after his Ōhara-mountain residences). Lay-name a son of the Kuga clan (久我家) — i.e. of high aristocratic Fujiwara-Murakami-Genji descent; his birth-father was **Minamoto no Chikamune** 源親宗 (the *Sai-no-Daiben*). Adopted by **Kujō Kanezane** 九条兼実 (Hōnen's principal lay patron). Posthumously titled **Kanchi Kokushi** 鑑智國師 by Emperor Go-Komatsu (1407).

Entered [[源空]] **Hōnen's** assembly at Yoshimizu 吉水 at age 14 (1190); studied under Hōnen for some twenty-three years, until Hōnen's exile in 1207. Among Hōnen's principal direct disciples, Shōkū is the one with the longest continuous study-period (along with Shinran), and he was entrusted by Hōnen with the editorial preservation of the **Senchakushū** during the period of its restricted manuscript circulation; the *Senchakushū* preface tradition makes Shōkū one of the *named seal-witnesses* (証判) of the text. After Hōnen's death (1212), Shōkū's doctrinal position diverged from that of Benchō and the Chinzei line on the question of the **selection** (*senchaku*): Shōkū interpreted *senchaku* as more radically *exclusive* — denying the salvific efficacy of the *jōzen* (*disciplined-good*) and *sanzen* (*dispersed-good*) practices independently of nenbutsu — and developing a doctrinal scheme of **single nenbutsu equals nenbutsu-of-vow** (一念義 *ichinen-gi*, though properly distinguished from Kōsai's homonymous doctrine).

Shōkū established his teaching centre at the **Sai-no-yama** 西山 ("Western Mountains") west of Kyoto — Yoshimine-dera 善峯寺, Sankaku-ji 三鈷寺, Shōrin-in 勝林院, and the **Ōhara** 大原 cluster of temples — whence the school-name **Seizan-ha** 西山派 ("Western Mountains branch"). His principal disciples included [[淨音]] **Jōon** (founder of the Seizan **Senjō** 禪林寺 sub-line, at Eikan-dō), **Shōkū-bō Shōkū** 證入, **Renjaku-bō** 蓮寂房, and **Shōō** 證入. The Seizan branch produced major doctrinal compendia ([[KR6t0326]]–[[KR6t0332]] of Shōkū's own; [[KR6t0333]]–[[KR6t0334]] of Jōon's; [[KR6t0321]]–[[KR6t0323]] of Kōa Shōken's, in the Senjō sub-line) and was institutionally consolidated through the **Sai-no-yama jūshichi-ji** seventeen-temple network in medieval Kyoto.

Principal works: **Senchaku mitsuyō-ketsu** 選擇密要決 ([[KR6t0326]], 5 fasc.), the Seizan-line commentary on Hōnen's *Senchakushū*; **Shugyō yōketsu** 修業要決 ([[KR6t0327]]); **Tōma mandara kuyō shiki** 當麻曼荼羅供式 ([[KR6t0328]]) — liturgical service-text for the famous Tōma mandala; and others ([[KR6t0329]]–[[KR6t0332]]).
