Shinjaku Shinnō 真寂親王 (886–927), born **Saneyo Shinnō** 齊世親王, was the seventh son of **Emperor Uda** 宇多天皇 (867–931) and an early imperial-prince-monk (*hosshin'ō* 法親王) of the Heian Shingon school. He took ordination at **Ninnaji** 仁和寺 — the great imperial monastery founded by his father — under his father's preceptor **Yakushin** 益信 (827–906), the founder of the *Hirosawa-ryū* 廣澤流 lineage of Shingon. He rose to the rank of *hosshin'ō* and held senior monastic appointments throughout his life.

Shinjaku's principal scholarly contribution to medieval Shingon was the systematic *transcription into writing* of the oral teachings that had descended from **Jitsue** 實慧 (Kūkai's senior disciple) through Yakushin. His preserved *Bùguàn líng děng jì* [[KR6t0175]] (dated 922) is one of the earliest extant documents of early-Heian imperial-house esoteric practice — including notes on the *non-pour anointing bell* procedure, the wrathful-king mudrās, and the *Acala* summoning-rite. He stands as the documentary lynchpin between Kūkai's first-generation oral teachings and the systematic ritual literature of the Hirosawa branch.

Surviving work in the Kanripo corpus: [[KR6t0175]] *Bùguàn líng děng jì* (1 fasc., 922 CE).
