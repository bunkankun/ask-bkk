**Wú Róng** 吳融 (*zì* Zǐ-huá 子華, *ca.* 850 – 903), of *Shān-yīn* 山陰 in *Yuè-zhōu* 越州 (Zhè-jiāng). Late-Táng poet, prose-stylist, and *Hàn-lín xué-shì*. *Jìn-shì* of *Lóng-jì* 1 (889) under Zhāo-zōng, in the same cohort as Hán Wò 韓偓, with whom he served as fellow Hàn-lín academician. Rose under Zhāo-zōng to *Hàn-lín xué-shì chéng-zhǐ* 翰林學士承旨, *Hù-bù shì-láng zhī zhì-gào* 戶部侍郎知制誥, and was the principal late-Táng court drafter of imperial edicts; the *Táng-shū* records that on Zhāo-zōng's restoration in 901 Wú produced more than ten edicts in a single audience.

Wú Róng's poetic style — euphonious, polished, with mid-Táng residual elegance — was influential among the *Tiān-yòu* (904) generation. He is the author of [[KR4c0102]] *Táng-yīng gē-shī* 唐英歌詩 (the WYG three-*juǎn* poetry collection) and the famous critical preface to [[KR4c0110]] *Chán-yuè jí* 禪月集 — the principal contemporary appraisal of the monk-poet Guàn-xiū 貫休 [[釋貫休]]. The preface, dated *Jǐ-wèi* (899) at *Lóng-xìng-sì* in Jīng-mén during Wú's brief southern banishment, sets out Wú's view that since Lǐ Bái and Bái Jū-yì only Guàn-xiū could carry forward Táng poetic *qì-gǔ* in the new manner.
