**Wú Róng** 吳融 (*zì* Zǐhuá 子華, *ca.* 850 – 903), of *Shānyīn* 山陰 in *Yuèzhōu* 越州 (Zhèjiāng). Late-Táng poet, prose-stylist, and *Hànlín xuéshì*. *Jìnshì* of *Lóngjì* 1 (889) under Zhāozōng, in the same cohort as Hán Wò 韓偓, with whom he served as fellow Hànlín academician. Rose under Zhāozōng to *Hànlín xuéshì chéngzhǐ* 翰林學士承旨, *Hùbù shìláng zhī zhìgào* 戶部侍郎知制誥, and was the principal late-Táng court drafter of imperial edicts; the *Tángshū* records that on Zhāozōng's restoration in 901 Wú produced more than ten edicts in a single audience.

Wú Róng's poetic style — euphonious, polished, with mid-Táng residual elegance — was influential among the *Tiānyòu* (904) generation. He is the author of [[KR4c0102]] *Tángyīng gēshī* 唐英歌詩 (the WYG three-*juǎn* poetry collection) and the famous critical preface to [[KR4c0110]] *Chányuè jí* 禪月集 — the principal contemporary appraisal of the monk-poet Guànxiū 貫休 [[釋貫休]]. The preface, dated *Jǐwèi* (899) at *Lóngxìngsì* in Jīngmén during Wú's brief southern banishment, sets out Wú's view that since Lǐ Bái and Bái Jūyì only Guànxiū could carry forward Táng poetic *qìgǔ* in the new manner. CBDB id 93246 confirms d. 903; the dataset records "?–903"; the conventional birth year *ca.* 850 is the inferred figure based on his 889 *jìnshì* and the customary age-band for that examination cohort.
