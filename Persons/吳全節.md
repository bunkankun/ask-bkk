Wú Quánjié 吳全節 (*zì* Chéngjì 成季, *hào* Xiánxián lǎorén 閑閑老人; 1269–1346), Xuánjiào dà zōngshī 玄教大宗師 ("Grand Patriarch of the Mystic Teaching") under the Yuán, born at Ráozhōu 饒州 (modern Jiāngxī). He was the second leader of the Xuánjiào 玄教 — the imperial-court Daoist administrative office — succeeding [[Persons/張留孫|Zhāng Liúsūn]] 張留孫 (1248–1321) on the latter's death. Wú served at the Yuán court for many decades, exercising broad oversight of Daoist affairs in the South China region. He is celebrated in early-fourteenth-century literati prose as one of the most cultivated Daoist masters of the dynasty, friend of the painter-calligraphers Zhào Mèngfǔ 趙孟頫 (1254–1322) and Yú Jí 虞集 (1271–1348). It was Wú who first approached the Máoshān 茅山 forty-fourth patriarch Wáng Dàomèng 王道孟 — and, on his death, his successor [[劉大彬|Liú Dàbīn]] 劉大彬 — to compile what would become the [[KR5a0316|*Máoshān zhì* 茅山志]] (DZ 304); his preface to the work, signed *Tèjìn shàngqīng Xuánjiào dà zōngshī* 特進上卿玄教大宗師, is dated *Tàidìng* 4, first month, the *dīngmǎo* spring of 1327. The Yuán-period institutional history of Daoism is unintelligible without his and Zhāng Liúsūn's central role; for Daoist works in the Daozang, see also [[KR5a0247|DZ 246 *Xīshān qúnxiān huìzhēn jì*]] and the Xuánjiào school more generally.
