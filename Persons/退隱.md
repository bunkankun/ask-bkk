Cheongheo Hyujeong 清虛休靜 (Korean reading; Chinese *Qīngxū Xiūjìng*; 1520–1604), known universally in Korean tradition as Sŏsan Taesa 西山大師 ("Great Master of the West Mountain"); the single most influential Korean Buddhist figure of the mid-Chosŏn dynasty. *Zì* Rúxìn 汝信; multiple *hào* including Qīngxū 清虛 ("Clear-Empty"), Xīshān 西山, Xuányīng 玄應, and Tuìyǐn 退隱 ("Retreated-and-Concealed" — the *hào* used in the author-line of the *Chán jiā guī jiàn* [[KR6q0146]]). Imperial honours: Guóyī dù dà chánshī 國一都大禪師 ("Great Chán Master of National Uniqueness") and Fúzōng fǔjiào pǔjì dēngjiē zūnzhě 扶宗拊教普濟登階尊者. Lay surname Cuī 崔, native of Wánshān 完山.

Dharma-heir of Yǐnān Línguān 隱庵靈觀 (DILA A031593) in the Korean Sŏn 朝鮮禪 lineage, which derives historically from the Chinese LínjìYángqí tradition via Koryŏ-period transmissions. Passed the *sŭnggwa* 僧科 state Sŏn examination and held the supreme ecclesiastical offices of the Chosŏn Buddhist institution.

Hyujeong is the principal Korean Buddhist figure of the Japanese Imjin War period (1592–1598): despite the Chosŏn state's official anti-Buddhist Neo-Confucian ideology, Hyujeong rallied Buddhist monastic militia ("monk-soldiers" *sŭngpyŏng* 僧兵) to defend Korea against the Japanese invasion under Toyotomi Hideyoshi. The monastic militia's military contribution under Hyujeong's leadership contributed to the eventual Japanese defeat and restored Buddhism to a respected institutional position in late-Chosŏn Korean society.

Hyujeong's doctrinal project was the synthetic integration of the full classical-Chinese Chán doctrinal inheritance — DàhuìZōnggǎo *kànhuà chán*, Hóngzhì Zhèngjué / Cáodòng silent-illumination, Yǒngmíng Yánshòu's Chán-Pure-Land syncretism, Pojo Chinul's *dùnwù jiànxiū* — into a unified Korean synthesis that became normative for subsequent Chosŏn Korean Buddhism and continues to shape the living Korean Chogye-order tradition.

Major works:

- *Chán jiā guī jiàn* 禪家龜鑑 (Korean *Sŏn-ga kwi-gam*) ([[KR6q0146]], X63 n1255, 1 juan) — the foundational Chosŏn Sŏn doctrinal text; single most influential Korean-authored Buddhist text after Chinul's [[KR6q0097]] *Xiū xīn jué*;
- *Sŏn-gyo kyŏl* 禪教訣 ("Resolution of Sŏn and *Jiào* Doctrinal Teaching") — companion text advocating Sŏn-Gyo integration;
- *Sŏn-ga kwi-gam yŏk-chu* 禪家龜鑑諺註 — vernacular-Korean annotated edition for general readers;
- Numerous *yǔlù* and occasional writings preserved in his *Sŏsan sōn-jip* 西山先集.

Principal dharma-heirs include Samyeong Yujeong 四溟惟政 (1544–1610) — himself a celebrated monastic-military leader of the Imjin War — and Pyŏnyang Eongi 鞭羊彥機 (1581–1644), Soyŏ Taenung 逍遙太能 (1562–1649), and Jingmu Ilok 靜默一玉 (1562–1633), through whom Hyujeong's synthesis defined the four main Sŏn *gok* 谷 (lineage-streams) of the late Chosŏn.

Died on Wànlì 32 (1604/5, precise date between 31 January 1604 and 17 February 1605), aged 85, *chánlà* (禪臘) 65.
