Mid-to-late Táng monk-philologist, the most consequential Chinese Buddhist lexicographer of the pre-modern period. Lay surname **Péi** 裴, native of **Shūlèguó** 疏勒國 (Kashgar in the Western Regions). Born **737** CE; died at **Xīmíngsì** 西明寺 in Cháng'ān in **Yuánhé** 元和 15 = **820** CE, at age 84.

Originally trained in the Confucian arts, he turned to Buddhism in his "weak-cap year" (around twenty) and took **Bù-kōng** 不空 (Amoghavajra, 705–774) — the great Esoteric (*mì* 密) tripiṭaka of the mid-Táng court — as his teacher. He combined inner mastery of the Esoteric *mì-zàng* with outer mastery of Confucian *jīng-xué* and *zì-xué* (lexicography), and developed deep expertise in Sanskrit *śabda-vidyā* (*shēng-míng* 聲明) and Chinese *xùn-gǔ*.

His monumental work is the **Yīqiè jīng yīnyì** 一切經音義 ([[KR6s0013]], T2128) — the **Huìlín yīnyì** — a one-hundred-juan canon-wide phonological-semantic glossary of about 600,000 characters, composed over the period **Jiànzhōng** 4 (783) – **Yuánhé** 2 (807). The work substantially incorporates earlier *yīnyì* compilations (Xuányìng's [[KR6s0010]], Huìyuàn's [[KR6s0011]]/[[KR6s0012]], the lost works of Yúngōng and Guō Tàiyuán). It is universally regarded as the single most important Chinese-Buddhist philological work and is one of the principal canonical sources for Middle Chinese phonology, drawn on by every modern reconstruction of Tang Mandarin from Karlgren onward. The work was presented for inclusion in the imperial canon in **Dàzhōng** 5 = **851**, three decades after his death, by the senior Xīmíngsì officials Míngxiù, Qìyuán, and Xuáncè.

He also produced two short Esoteric ritual works: the one-juan **Xīnjí yùxiàng yíguǐ** 新集浴像儀軌 (a ritual on bathing the buddha-image) and the one-juan **Jiànlì màntúluó jí jiǎnzé dìfǎ** 建立曼荼羅及揀擇地法 (mandala-construction and site-selection).
