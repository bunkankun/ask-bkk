Mid-to-late Táng monk-philologist, the most consequential Chinese Buddhist lexicographer of the pre-modern period. Lay surname **Péi** 裴, native of **Shū-lè-guó** 疏勒國 (Kashgar in the Western Regions). Born **737** CE; died at **Xī-míng-sì** 西明寺 in Cháng'ān in **Yuán-hé** 元和 15 = **820** CE, at age 84.

Originally trained in the Confucian arts, he turned to Buddhism in his "weak-cap year" (around twenty) and took **Bù-kōng** 不空 (Amoghavajra, 705–774) — the great Esoteric (*mì* 密) tripiṭaka of the mid-Táng court — as his teacher. He combined inner mastery of the Esoteric *mì-zàng* with outer mastery of Confucian *jīng-xué* and *zì-xué* (lexicography), and developed deep expertise in Sanskrit *śabda-vidyā* (*shēng-míng* 聲明) and Chinese *xùn-gǔ*.

His monumental work is the **Yī-qiè jīng yīn-yì** 一切經音義 ([[KR6s0013]], T2128) — the **Huì-lín yīn-yì** — a one-hundred-juan canon-wide phonological-semantic glossary of about 600,000 characters, composed over the period **Jiàn-zhōng** 4 (783) – **Yuán-hé** 2 (807). The work substantially incorporates earlier *yīn-yì* compilations (Xuán-yìng's [[KR6s0010]], Huì-yuàn's [[KR6s0011]]/[[KR6s0012]], the lost works of Yún-gōng and Guō Tài-yuán). It is universally regarded as the single most important Chinese-Buddhist philological work and is one of the principal canonical sources for Middle Chinese phonology, drawn on by every modern reconstruction of Tang Mandarin from Karlgren onward. The work was presented for inclusion in the imperial canon in **Dà-zhōng** 5 = **851**, three decades after his death, by the senior Xī-míng-sì officials Míng-xiù, Qì-yuán, and Xuán-cè.

He also produced two short Esoteric ritual works: the one-juan **Xīn-jí yù-xiàng yí-guǐ** 新集浴像儀軌 (a ritual on bathing the buddha-image) and the one-juan **Jiàn-lì màn-tú-luó jí jiǎn-zé dì-fǎ** 建立曼荼羅及揀擇地法 (mandala-construction and site-selection).
