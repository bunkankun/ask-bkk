Late-Yuán / early-Míng recluse-poet of Jí-shuǐ 吉水 (Lú-líng region, Jiāngxī). Style-name **Yànzhāng** 彥章; self-styled **Jìngsī** 靜思 ("Quiet Thought"). Of the family seat at Gāocūn-Guìlín 髙村桂林. The family Confucian lineage was grounded in **Liú Zǐchéng** 劉子澄 (the *Jìng-chūn* 靜春 master) → and inherited the *Zǐ-yáng* (i.e. Zhū Xī) learning; descendants Guō Lín-jiàn 林磵, Xī-chuāng 西牕, Dī-xī 諟溪 in succession refined it. Guō Yù lived through the late-Yuán warfare in Jiāngxī, did not serve, and in *xīnhài* (1371, Hóngwǔ 4) was summoned by the Míng as *xiùcái* — he refused, citing deafness and lameness. He died in poverty after age 60 (his sixtieth-birthday poem dates to *yǐmǎo* = Hóngwǔ 8 = 1375; he survived several years more, dying probably in the 1380s). His *Jìngsī jí* 靜思集 ([[KR4d0568]]) is the surviving collection, transmitted privately within the Guō family until his eighth-generation grand-nephew **Guō Tíng-zhāo** 郭廷昭 had it cut in print in the Jiājìng era (1561) under the editorship of the prominent statesman-scholar **Luó Hóng-xiān** 羅洪先 of Niàn-ān (Jí-shuǐ kinsman, *Jiājìng zhuàngyuán*).
