# Sūn Guòtíng 孫過庭

Style name *Qiánlǐ* 虔禮 (or perhaps personal-name; the convention is unsettled). Native of Wú-jùn 吳郡 / Chén-liú 陳留; the secondary sources are inconsistent. Born ca. 646; died ca. 691. Active in the Wǔ Zé-tiān period.

The author of the *Shū-pǔ* 書譜 ([[KR3h0005]]) — completed Chuí-gǒng 3 (687) — universally regarded as the foundational systematic calligraphy treatise in the Chinese calligraphic tradition. The work is preserved both as written-text and as the original autograph manuscript (the calligraphic original survives in the National Palace Museum, Tài-běi, and is one of the most celebrated examples of Tang *cǎo-shū* (cursive script) calligraphy). The work's status as both *content-text* (calligraphy theory) and *form-exemplar* (master-example of cursive script) is unique in Chinese cultural history.

Sūn Guòtíng's *Shū-pǔ* articulates the foundational Chinese calligraphic-aesthetic theory: the *zhì-yán cuò-cǎo* (regular-and-cursive flow), the *bǐ-zhèn tǐ-shì* (brush-camp body-configuration), the *xīn-shǒu xiāng-yìng* (heart-and-hand mutually-correspondent), the *gǔ-jīn liè-bài* (ancient-and-modern arranged-and-displayed) — concepts that would shape every subsequent Chinese calligraphic-theoretical discussion through the late imperial period.

His other documented work — *Liù-shū lùn* — is lost.
