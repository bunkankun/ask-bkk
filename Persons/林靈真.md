Lín Língzhēn 林靈真 (本名 Wěifū 偉夫, zì Jūnzhāo 君昭, hào Shuǐnán 水南; *fǎmíng* 法諱 Língzhēn; b. 28 ix 1239 = 嘉熙己亥, d. 1302) was the eleventh-generation patriarch (*jiàozhǔ*) of the *Dōnghuá* 東華 transmission of the *Língbǎo dàfǎ* and the principal redactor of the *Língbǎo lǐngjiào jìdù jīnshū* ([[KR5b0150]]). He was a native of Línniè 林坳, Píngyáng 平陽, Wēnzhōu, of a gentry family — grandfather Lín Jié 林楶 (Wǔjīngláng 武經郎), father Lín Sìsūn 林嗣孫 (Bǎoyìláng 保義郎), mother née Xú 徐 of Táozhǔ 桃渚.

Lín was raised in classical and Daoist learning; he passed neither the *jǔrén* nor the *jìnshì* examinations, receiving only the modest rank of *Dēngshìláng* 登仕郎. After the loss of his mother in the SòngYuán transition he abandoned secular career, converted his ancestral residence into a Daoist *guàn* (*Dānyuánguàn* 丹元觀, gates inscribed 水南福地), and was ordained by Dài Wěi 戴煟, *Fùān xiānshēng* 復庵先生. He took *Shuǐnán* 水南 as *hào* in memory of his mother's pre-natal dream of southern flood-waters.

Lín studied successively under Lín Xūyī 林虛一 and Xuē Dōnghuá 薛東華, inheriting the *Dōnghuá* lineage descended from Níng Quánzhēn 寧全真 (q.v.). On returning to Wēnzhōu he was appointed *Xuánxué jiǎngshī* (玄學講師) and then *Dàolù* 道録 of Wēnzhōu prefecture by the reigning Daoist Patriarch (= the 38th Celestial Master, Zhāng Yǔchū 張與材, fl. ca. 1290–1316). He thereafter assembled the great compendium of *Língbǎo* liturgy (a *jìdù zhī shū* in ten juàn, plus *fúzhāng* in two juàn, growing eventually to the 321-juàn *Lǐngjiào jìdù jīnshū* of [[KR5b0150]]); the text was approved by the Patriarch, printed by the Wēnzhōu *Xuánsī* under his commission, and Lín was titled *Língbǎo tōngxuán hóngjiào fǎshī jiàomén gāoshì* 靈寶通玄弘教法師教門髙士.

He served until his death as abbot of the Tiānqīngguàn 天清觀 in Wēnzhōu; over a hundred disciples are said to have studied under him, including the *Xiánxián* Wúgōng 閑閑吳公, *Jīnhuá* Xiègōng 金華謝公, and many others named in the *Sìjiào lù*. He died in 1302 (大德六年). CBDB has no entry. Cf. Schipper & Verellen, *Taoist Canon* 2: 1027–1029; Boltz, *Survey* (1987), pp. 47–50; Wáng Chéngwén 王承文 (2002).
