Hé Jǐngmíng 何景明 (1483–1521), *zì* **Zhòngmò** 仲默, *hào* **Dàfù** 大復, of **Xìnyáng** 信陽 (Hénán) — therefore commonly called **Xìnyáng** in mid-Míng critical tradition. Hóngzhì 15 / *rénxū* (1502) *jìnshì*, at age 19; appointed *Zhōngshū shèrén*. Later *Shǎnxī tíxué*. *Míngshǐ Wényuàn zhuàn*. The second principal of the *QiánQīzǐ* (Former Seven Masters) — with **Lǐ Mèngyáng** ([[李夢陽]]) the *LǐHé* (Lǐ-and-Hé) pairing that defined mid-Míng literature. Hé's verse-style — *liàngjié jùnyǔ chū yú tiānxìng* (clear-clean conduct, elegant language out of natural disposition) — was the *Wùzhōng* (Wú-region) counter-pole to Lǐ Mèngyáng's *Guānzhōng* (Shǎnxī) *juéjiāng* (firm-resolute) line; the two famously exchanged poetry-discussion letters preserved in both their collections (cf. [[KR4e0150]]). Died young at 38. His writings are gathered in the *Dàfù jí* in 38 *juǎn* ([[KR4e0162]]) — compiled by **Kāng Hǎi** 康海 ([[康海]], [[KR4e0159]]) in Jiājìng 3 (1524), with second preface by **Wáng Shìzhēn** 王世貞.
