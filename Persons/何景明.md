Hé Jǐng-míng 何景明 (1483–1521), *zì* **Zhòng-mò** 仲默, *hào* **Dà-fù** 大復, of **Xìn-yáng** 信陽 (Hé-nán) — therefore commonly called **Xìn-yáng** in mid-Míng critical tradition. Hóng-zhì 15 / *rén-xū* (1502) *jìn-shì*, at age 19; appointed *Zhōng-shū shè-rén*. Later *Shǎn-xī tí-xué*. *Míng-shǐ Wén-yuàn zhuàn*. The second principal of the *Qián-Qī-zǐ* (Former Seven Masters) — with **Lǐ Mèng-yáng** ([[李夢陽]]) the *Lǐ-Hé* (Lǐ-and-Hé) pairing that defined mid-Míng literature. Hé's verse-style — *liàng-jié jùn-yǔ chū yú tiān-xìng* (clear-clean conduct, elegant language out of natural disposition) — was the *Wù-zhōng* (Wú-region) counter-pole to Lǐ Mèng-yáng's *Guān-zhōng* (Shǎn-xī) *jué-jiāng* (firm-resolute) line; the two famously exchanged poetry-discussion letters preserved in both their collections (cf. [[KR4e0150]]). Died young at 38. His writings are gathered in the *Dà-fù jí* in 38 *juǎn* ([[KR4e0162]]) — compiled by **Kāng Hǎi** 康海 ([[康海]], [[KR4e0159]]) in Jiā-jìng 3 (1524), with second preface by **Wáng Shì-zhēn** 王世貞.
