Wáng Xīnjìng 王心敬 (1656–1738), zì Ěrjī 爾緝, hào Fēngchuān 豐川 (after the Fēngchuān 豐川 river in his native region), was a Kāngxī-Yōngzhèng-period Confucian scholar from Hùxiàn 鄠縣 (Xī'ān 西安, Shǎnxī 陝西), and one of the principal Lǐ Yóng 李顒-school scholars of early-eighteenth-century Shǎnxī. He held no major office.

His scholarly output covers the classics broadly (with works on the *Yì*, *Shū*, *Shī*, *Lǐ*, *Chūnqiū*, the Four Books). The *Sìkù* editors give a sharply mixed assessment: of his *jīngxué* corpus, the works on the *Shū* and *Chūnqiū* are described as "fragmentary, piercing, daring to make heterodox arguments," while only the *Yì* commentary — the *Fēngchuān Yì shuō* 豐川易說 ([[KR1a0154]]) — is praised as "elucidating *Yì*-principle most substantively and clearly." The contrast within a single author's output is one of the more striking *Sìkù* internal discriminations.

The *Fēngchuān Yì shuō* in ten juàn argues that the *Yì* is the "book of human-affairs-discourse" (道人事之書), with yīnyáng waxing-and-waning serving as image (*xiàng*); rejects the chart-tradition entirely (*Hétú-Luòshū*, *guà biàn*, *cuò zōng*, *hùtǐ* all set aside); and rejects even the *Zuǒ zhuàn*-attested ancient divinatory method as too speculative. The *Sìkù* editors note this is "somewhat over-pressed," but the work is still vastly superior to chart-and-numerology speculative *Yìxué*.
