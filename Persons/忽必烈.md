Qubilai Khan 忽必烈 (1215–1294), grandson of Činggis Khan, was the fifth Great Khan of the Mongol Empire and founding emperor (廟號 *miàohào* Shìzǔ 世祖, r. 1260–1294) of the Yuán 元 dynasty in China. His reign marked the consolidation of Mongol rule over China, the relocation of the capital to Dàdū 大都 (modern Beijing), and the integration of Tibetan Vajrayāna Buddhism — patronised through the **imperial preceptors** (帝師 *dìshī*) of the Sa-skya school, including 'Phags-pa — into the imperial cult. The *Yùzhì jiùdùfómǔ zàn* 御製救度佛母讚 attributed to him survives as the prefatory imperial praise of the Tārā *aṣṭottara-śata-stotra* Chinese rendering by [[安藏]] Ān Zàng ([[KR6j0317]], T20n1108A), an unprecedented imperial-Chinese endorsement of the Tibetan-Vajrayāna Tārā cult in canonical Buddhist literature.
