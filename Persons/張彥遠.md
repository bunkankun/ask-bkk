Mid- and late-Táng official and art historian; *zì* Àibīn 愛賓, from a Hédōng 河東 family of prime-ministerial rank. He was great-great-grandson of Zhāng Jiāzhēn 張嘉貞 (*zǎixiàng* under Mínghuáng), great-grandson of the *Wèiguógōng* Yánshǎng 延賞, grandson of the *Gāopínggōng* Hóngjìng 弘靖 (both *Tóng pínɡzhānɡshì*), and son of Wénguī 文規, *Guāncháshǐ* of Guìguǎn. The Zhāng family had assembled a calligraphy and painting collection over four generations on a par with the imperial library — a fact Yànyuǎn emphasises throughout his prefaces. In the *Qiánfú* 乾符 era (874–879) he rose to *Dàlǐ qīng* 大理卿. He compiled in succession the [[KR3h0008]] *Fǎshū yàolù* (the indispensable Táng compendium of calligraphic literature, c. 847) and the [[KR3h0009]] *Lìdài mínghuà jì* (847, the first systematic Chinese art history). Both works survived because of his inherited library and preserved much of the *Liùcháo* and Táng art-historical literature that would otherwise be lost. Standard biographies in *Xīn Tángshū* 200 and *Jiù Tángshū* 129.
