Liú Yōng 劉墉 (1720–1804), *zì* Chóng-rú 崇如, *hào* Shí-ān 石庵 (and many other *hào*), posthumously *Wén-qīng* 文清. Native of Zhū-chéng 諸城, Shān-dōng. *Jìnshì* of Qiánlóng 16 (1751); son of the high official Liú Tǒng-xūn 劉統勳 (1700–1773). He held a long and distinguished career under Qiánlóng and Jiā-qìng — provincial education commissioner, *zhī-fǔ*, *bù-zhèng-shǐ*, *àn-chá-shǐ*, governor of Shān-xī, governor-general of multiple provinces, *Lǐ-bù shàng-shū*, *Tǐ-rén-gé dà-xué-shì*, and *Tài-zǐ tài-bǎo* — and is one of the most famous incorruptible-official figures of the Qiánlóng era, often paired in folk tradition with Jì Yún 紀昀 as the upright counterweight to Hé-shēn 和珅. He was named *zǒng-cái* of the *Qīndìng Huáng-yú Xī-yù tú-zhì* ([[KR2k0039]]) supplementation phase together with Fú Kāng-ān 福康安 in Qiánlóng 42 (1777). Liú Yōng was also a major calligrapher of the Qīng — one of the four great Qiánlóng-era calligraphers (with Wēng Fāng-gāng 翁方綱, Tiē Bǎ 鐵保, and Yǒng Xìng 永瑆 / Chéng Qīn-wáng) — and his cursive and standard scripts are widely collected.
