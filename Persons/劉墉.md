Liú Yōng 劉墉 (1720–1804), *zì* Chóngrú 崇如, *hào* Shíān 石庵 (and many other *hào*), posthumously *Wénqīng* 文清. Native of Zhūchéng 諸城, Shāndōng. *Jìnshì* of Qiánlóng 16 (1751); son of the high official Liú Tǒngxūn 劉統勳 (1700–1773). He held a long and distinguished career under Qiánlóng and Jiāqìng — provincial education commissioner, *zhīfǔ*, *bùzhèngshǐ*, *àncháshǐ*, governor of Shānxī, governor-general of multiple provinces, *Lǐbù shàngshū*, *Tǐréngé dàxuéshì*, and *Tàizǐ tàibǎo* — and is one of the most famous incorruptible-official figures of the Qiánlóng era, often paired in folk tradition with Jì Yún 紀昀 as the upright counterweight to Héshēn 和珅. He was named *zǒngcái* of the *Qīndìng Huángyú Xīyù túzhì* ([[KR2k0039]]) supplementation phase together with Fú Kāngān 福康安 in Qiánlóng 42 (1777). Liú Yōng was also a major calligrapher of the Qīng — one of the four great Qiánlóng-era calligraphers (with Wēng Fānggāng 翁方綱, Tiē Bǎ 鐵保, and Yǒng Xìng 永瑆 / Chéng Qīnwáng) — and his cursive and standard scripts are widely collected.
