Yǔ Chǎn 庾闡 (c. 286–339), *zì* Zhòngchū 仲初, was an Eastern Jìn 東晉 poet, *fù* writer, and literary essayist, a native of Yǐngchuān 潁川 Yānlíng 鄢陵 — the same Yǔ lineage that produced Yǔ Liàng 庾亮 (the powerful court minister) and Yǔ Bīng 庾冰. His biography is preserved in *Jìn shū* 晉書 j. 92 (*Wényuàn lièzhuàn*). He served in middle-ranking posts under Yuándì 元帝, Míngdì 明帝 and Chéngdì 成帝 — including *zhùzuò láng* 著作郎 (Director of the Imperial Archives) — and was a member of the *Lántíng* 蘭亭 generation of literary scholars.

He is principally remembered for his *Yángdū fù* 揚都賦 — the great early Eastern-Jìn celebration of the southern capital Jiànkāng, modeled on the metropolitan *fù* of Bān Gù 班固 and Zuǒ Sī 左思 — and for a substantial corpus of pentasyllabic verse, much of it preserved in *Wén xuǎn* and *Yìwén lèijù*. His shorter *zǐ*-house treatises survive only as Qing-era *jíyì* compilations. In the Kanripo corpus he is the attributed author of [[KR3a0133]] *Lì xué* 厲學 and [[KR3a0156]] *Wén shì* 文釋. CBDB has no entry; the dates 286–339 follow the *Jìn shū* notice.
