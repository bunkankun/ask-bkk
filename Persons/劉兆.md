# Liú Zhào 劉兆

*Zì* Yánshì 延世. Western-Jìn 西晉 Confucian classicist, native of Dōngpínglíng 東平陵 in Jǐnán 濟南 (modern Zhāngqiū 章丘 district, Shāndōng). Descended from the Western-Hàn marquis Liú Yuè 劉越 of Guǎngchuān. The *Jìn shū* biography (*juàn* 91, *Rúlín liè zhuàn*) reports that he attracted some thousands of disciples and devoted himself for decades to textual study while declining repeated summons to office — including offers by Jìn Wǔ-dì 晉武帝 (r. 265–290) of *bóshì* 博士 and other court positions. He died aged 66; precise birth-death years are not given in the standard sources but a late-third-century floruit is securely established (he was an adult under Wǔ-dì).

Liú's signature contribution was a three-way harmonisation of the rival *Chūnqiū* commentary traditions. According to the *Jìn shū*, he reasoned that the three *zhuàn* — *Zuǒ*, *Gōngyáng*, *Gǔliáng* — were like three brothers from a single household whose claims should be reconciled rather than weighed one against another, and so produced *Chūnqiū gōng-gǔ jiěgǔ* 春秋公羊穀梁解詁 (also cited as *Chūnqiū sānzhuàn jiěgǔ* 春秋三傳解詁 / *Chūnqiū gōng-gǔ jíjiě* 春秋公羊穀梁傳集解), using red ink to distinguish the contributions of each tradition. He also produced a substantial *Chūnqiū diào-rén* 春秋調人 (some seventy thousand characters, modelled on the *Zhōulǐ Diào-rén* office of conciliation), and a *Zhōuyì zhù* 周易注. All these works are lost as integral books; fragments are preserved chiefly in [[陸德明]] Lù Démíng's *Jīngdiǎn shìwén* 經典釋文, in the *Wénxuǎn* 文選 commentary tradition (notably the Lǐ Shàn 李善 annotations), and in Táng *zhèngyì* 正義 citations — the basis of the [[KR1e0127]] CHANT reconstruction and of [[馬國翰]] Mǎ Guóhàn's *Yùhán shānfáng jíyì shū* parallel restoration.
