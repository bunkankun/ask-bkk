**Liú Yì-qìng** 劉義慶 (403–444), Prince Kāng of Línchuān 臨川康王, was the second son of Liú Dào-lián 劉道憐 and a nephew of the Liú Sòng founder Wǔ-dì 武帝 (Liú Yù 劉裕). Adopted as heir to his uncle Liú Dào-guī 劉道規, Prince Jǐng of Línchuān, in 415, he inherited the principality and the rank of *Sī-tú* 司徒 in the southern court. His official career took him to senior provincial appointments — successive Governorships of Jīngzhōu 荊州 (from 432) and Jiāngzhōu 江州 — where his princely seat became one of the most active literary centres of the Yuán-jiā 元嘉 reign (424–453). Pieced together from the *Sòng shū* 宋書 51 and *Nán shǐ* 南史 13 biographies and the *Shìshuō xīnyǔ* preface tradition, the picture is of a contemplative prince less inclined to politics than to letters and the patronage of letters: he is credited with gathering a circle of literati that included Yuán Shū 袁淑, Lù Zhǎn 陸展, Hé Cháng-yú 何長瑜 and Bào Zhào 鮑照, and under his name (and presumably with their hands) the *Shìshuō xīnyǔ* 世說新語 ([[KR3l0002]]), the *Yōu-míng lù* 幽明錄 (志怪) and the *Xuān-yàn jì* 宣驗記 (Buddhist miracle tales) were assembled. CBDB id 468156 records the name with no lifedates entered; the dates 403–444 are the standard reading from the *Sòng shū* biography. A devout Buddhist in later life, he died of illness in 444.
