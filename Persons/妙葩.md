**Shun'oku Myōha** 春屋妙葩 (Ōchō 1 → 1311; Kakei 2 / 1388-09-13), Late-Kamakura → Nanbokuchō Japanese **Rinzai-Zen** master and the most powerful single figure in the **Gozan** 五山 ("Five-Mountain") establishment of the second half of the fourteenth century. Posthumous title **Chikaku Fumyō Kokushi** 智覺普明國師, bestowed by Emperor Go-En'yū 後圓融院 in Kōryaku 1 (1379-12-28); style-name (字) Shun'oku 春屋. Native of Awa 阿波 province; nephew (sister's son) of [[夢窓疎石]] **Musō Soseki** (1275–1351), entered the Sangha as a youth under Musō and received transmission from him at Tenryū-ji 天龍寺. Successively abbot (住山) of **Tenryū-ji** 天龍寺, **Nanzen-ji** 南禪寺, **Rinsen-ji** 臨川寺, and re-installed at Tenryū-ji a second time. In 1379 he was appointed the first **Sōroku** 僧錄 — head of the newly-established Bakufu Office of Monastic Registry (Sōroku-shi 僧錄司) which oversaw all Gozan and Jissetsu temples — making him the de-facto administrator of the entire metropolitan Zen establishment. A leading patron of Gozan-bungaku (Five-Mountain literature) and of the printing of Sōng / Yuán Zen texts. Disciples included [[周信]] Gidō Shūshin and [[中津]] Zekkai Chūshin, the two principal voices of mature Gozan Zen letters.
