Shì Dào-qián 釋道潛 (fl. *Yuán-fēng*–*Yuán-fú*, c. 1043–c. 1106; later renamed Tán-qián 曇潛 by *zhì-yǒu* Sū Shì 蘇軾 [[蘇軾]]), *zì* Cān-liáo-zǐ 參寥子, posthumously *Miào-zǒng dà-shī* 妙總大師. Of *Yú-qián* 於潛 (Hángzhōu prefecture) — *yīn* his *xiāng* (district) for the name. *Chán*-Buddhist monk and the most famous *shī-sēng* (poet-monk) of the *Sū-mén* circle. Lifelong *zhì-yǒu* of Sū Shì from the *Xī-níng*-period *Hángzhōu* phase onward; followed Sū to *Hángzhōu* (1071), to *Mì-zhōu* (1075), to *Xú-zhōu* (1078) — accompanying him through every major Sū-Shì posting except the *Hǎi-nán* exile. After Sū Shì's *Wū-tái shī àn* (1079) and *Huáng-zhōu* exile, Dào-qián remained associated; revisited Sū at *Hángzhōu* during the second magistracy (1090–1091); in 1097 with Sū's *Hǎi-nán* exile he was forcibly *huì-fú* (returned to lay status) on charges of complicity. *Cān-liáo-zǐ shī-jí* 參寥子詩集 [[KR4d0093]] in 12 *juǎn*. Famed for *Yán Líng-tǎn* and *Cū-yī chī-yán* poems; Sū Shì's prefaces to him (e.g. *Sòng Cān-liáo shī-xù*) are central Sòng *shī-xù* documents. Lù Yóu 陸游's *Lǎo-xué-ān bǐ-jì* ranks him *dāng-shí shī-sēng dì-yī* (the foremost *shī-sēng* of the day).
