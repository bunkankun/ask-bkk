Shì Dàoqián 釋道潛 (fl. *Yuánfēng*–*Yuánfú*, c. 1043–c. 1106; later renamed Tánqián 曇潛 by *zhìyǒu* Sū Shì 蘇軾 [[蘇軾]]), *zì* Cānliáozǐ 參寥子, posthumously *Miàozǒng dàshī* 妙總大師. Of *Yúqián* 於潛 (Hángzhōu prefecture) — *yīn* his *xiāng* (district) for the name. *Chán*-Buddhist monk and the most famous *shīsēng* (poet-monk) of the *Sūmén* circle. Lifelong *zhìyǒu* of Sū Shì from the *Xīníng*-period *Hángzhōu* phase onward; followed Sū to *Hángzhōu* (1071), to *Mìzhōu* (1075), to *Xúzhōu* (1078) — accompanying him through every major SūShì posting except the *Hǎinán* exile. After Sū Shì's *Wūtái shī àn* (1079) and *Huángzhōu* exile, Dàoqián remained associated; revisited Sū at *Hángzhōu* during the second magistracy (1090–1091); in 1097 with Sū's *Hǎinán* exile he was forcibly *huìfú* (returned to lay status) on charges of complicity. *Cānliáozǐ shījí* 參寥子詩集 [[KR4d0093]] in 12 *juǎn*. Famed for *Yán Língtǎn* and *Cūyī chīyán* poems; Sū Shì's prefaces to him (e.g. *Sòng Cānliáo shīxù*) are central Sòng *shīxù* documents. Lù Yóu 陸游's *Lǎoxuéān bǐjì* ranks him *dāngshí shīsēng dìyī* (the foremost *shīsēng* of the day).
