# Qīng-gǒng 清珙

Yuán-dynasty Yángqí-branch Línjì Chán master and hermit-poet, *hào* Shí-wū 石屋 ("Stone-House"); posthumously styled Fó-cí Huì-zhào chánshī 佛慈慧照禪師. Surname Wēn 溫, native of Cháng-shú 常熟 in Sū-zhōu. Per DILA: 1272 – Zhì-zhèng 12.7.21 (late summer 1352), *shì* 81, *là* 54. (Note: the Kanripo catalog for his *yǔlù* spells his personal name 清**洪** — a variant attested in some traditional bibliographies; the conventional form is 清**珙**.)

Tonsured at twenty under Yǒng-wéi 永惟 of Cháng-shú Xīng-jiào Chóng-fú-sì. Studied three years with Gāo-fēng Yuán-miào 高峰原妙 at Tiān-mù-shān, then with Jí-ān Zōng-xìn 及菴宗信, who confirmed his awakening. Spent most of his mature life at his self-built Tiān-hú 天湖 hermitage on Xiá-wù-shān 霞霧山, famed for its ascetic hermit life. Reluctantly accepted the Fú-yuán 福源 abbacy at Dāng-hú (Jiā-hé) from 1331 to 1338, then retired. Declined late-life imperial summons from the Yuán court.

Principal dharma-heir: the Korean monk **Táigǔ Bou** 太古普愚 (1301–1382), who received Qīng-gǒng's transmission poem with the "gold-scaled fish on a straight hook" (金鱗上直鉤) line and returned to Koryŏ to found the **Taego-jong** 太古宗, one of the two principal modern Korean Sŏn lineages. King Kongmin venerated Qīng-gǒng as *Kuksa* 國師 and collected his relics for a Korean reliquary.

Recorded sayings: *Shí-wū chánshī yǔlù* ([[KR6q0332]]). His celebrated *Shān-jū shī* 山居詩 sequence (in the Hán-shān tradition) has been translated into English by Red Pine (Bill Porter), *The Zen Works of Stonehouse* (1999, rev. 2009).
