Late-tenth-century **Liáo** dynasty monk-philologist of **Chóng-rén-sì** 崇仁寺 in **Yān-jīng** 燕京 (the Liáo southern capital, modern Beijing). Lay surname, native place, and lifedates are not preserved.

His one work — and the one for which he is universally remembered — is the ten-juan **Xù yī-qiè jīng yīn-yì** 續一切經音義 ([[KR6s0014]], T2129), a direct continuation of **Huì-lín's** 慧琳 great hundred-juan **Yī-qiè jīng yīn-yì** ([[KR6s0013]], 783–807). The continuation supplies *yīn-yì* glosses for ca. 110 newly canon-included texts (266 juan / 25 *zhì*) added to the canon after the *Kāi-yuán shì-jiào lù* (730), beginning with the *Dà-shèng lǐ-qù liù bō-luó-mì-duō jīng* of 788. The work was composed at the request of a court-Buddhist figure styled **Wú-ài dà-shī** 無礙大師, conventionally placed at **ca. 987** CE (Liáo Tǒng-hé 5). It is universally cited as the **Xī-lín yīn-yì** 希麟音義.

The work is a primary witness to the **Liáo canon** project of the late tenth and eleventh centuries and to the late-Táng to Liáo state of *Qiè-yùn* / *Guǎng-yùn*-tradition phonology in the Beijing region. It entered the standard East Asian canon through the Liáo–Goryeo transmission; via the Korean canon it entered the **Taishō** at T54 no. 2129.

The Kanripo catalog meta classifies the work as Sòng — likely reflecting its Sòng-canonical inclusion date — but DILA more accurately classifies Xī-lín as a Liáo monk, since Yān-jīng was Liáo territory throughout his lifetime.
