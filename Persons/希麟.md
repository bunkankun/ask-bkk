Late-tenth-century **Liáo** dynasty monk-philologist of **Chóngrénsì** 崇仁寺 in **Yānjīng** 燕京 (the Liáo southern capital, modern Beijing). Lay surname, native place, and lifedates are not preserved.

His one work — and the one for which he is universally remembered — is the ten-juan **Xù yīqiè jīng yīnyì** 續一切經音義 ([[KR6s0014]], T2129), a direct continuation of **Huìlín's** 慧琳 great hundred-juan **Yīqiè jīng yīnyì** ([[KR6s0013]], 783–807). The continuation supplies *yīnyì* glosses for ca. 110 newly canon-included texts (266 juan / 25 *zhì*) added to the canon after the *Kāiyuán shìjiào lù* (730), beginning with the *Dàshèng lǐqù liù bōluómìduō jīng* of 788. The work was composed at the request of a court-Buddhist figure styled **Wúài dàshī** 無礙大師, conventionally placed at **ca. 987** CE (Liáo Tǒnghé 5). It is universally cited as the **Xīlín yīnyì** 希麟音義.

The work is a primary witness to the **Liáo canon** project of the late tenth and eleventh centuries and to the late-Táng to Liáo state of *Qièyùn* / *Guǎngyùn*-tradition phonology in the Beijing region. It entered the standard East Asian canon through the Liáo–Goryeo transmission; via the Korean canon it entered the **Taishō** at T54 no. 2129.

The Kanripo catalog meta classifies the work as Sòng — likely reflecting its Sòng-canonical inclusion date — but DILA more accurately classifies Xīlín as a Liáo monk, since Yānjīng was Liáo territory throughout his lifetime.
