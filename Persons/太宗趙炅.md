The **second emperor of the Northern Sòng dynasty**: temple-name **Tài-zōng** 太宗, personal name **Zhào Jiǒng** 趙炅 (originally **Zhào Guāng-yì** 趙光義 before his accession). Born **939 CE**; died **997 CE**, having ruled from **976**. Younger brother and successor of the Sòng founding emperor **Tài-zǔ** 太祖 (**Zhào Kuāng-yìn** 趙匡胤, r. 960–976).

His reign is foundational for the institutional consolidation of the Northern Sòng dynasty, in particular through:

- The **completion of dynastic unification** with the conquest of the **Northern Hàn** 北漢 in 979 and (unsuccessfully) the **Liáo** in the campaigns of 979 and 986.
- The **expansion of the civil-service examination system** to its full Sòng-bureaucracy form.
- The **systematic patronage of Buddhist canonical-printing**: he was the principal sponsor of the **Kāibǎo canon-printing project** (the 開寶藏 / *Kāibǎo-zàng*, the first xylographic printing of the Chinese Buddhist canon), begun in **Kāi-bǎo** 4 (971) and completed in **Tài-píng-xìng-guó** 8 (983) at **Yì-zhōu** 益州 (modern Chéng-dū). The **5,048-juan** Kāibǎo canon became the prototype for all subsequent East Asian xylographic Buddhist canon-printings.
- His patronage of the **Indian translator-monk** **Tiān-xī-zāi** 天息災 (Devaśāntika), **Fǎ-tiān** 法天 (Dharmadeva), and **Shī-hù** 施護 (Dānapāla) — establishing the **Yì-jīng-yuàn** 譯經院 (Sutra Translation Bureau) at **Tài-píng-xìng-guó-sì** 太平興國寺 in Kāifēng in 980.

His personal Buddhist literary output is the **single most substantial body of imperially-composed Buddhist literature in pre-modern Chinese history**, comprising at least four substantial works preserved in the Goryeo Tripiṭaka:

- **Yù-zhì lián-huá xīn-lún huí-wén jì-sòng** 御製蓮華心輪迴文偈頌 ([[KR6s0057]], K1258, 25 juan) — *huí-wén* (palindrome) Buddhist verse-encomia.
- **Yù-zhì mì-zàng quán** 御製秘藏詮 ([[KR6s0058]], K1259, 30 juan) — discourse on the Buddhist secret canon.
- **Yù-zhì xiāo-yáo yǒng** 御製逍遙詠 ([[KR6s0059]], K1260, 11 juan) — Buddhist-Daoist syncretic *xiāo-yáo* (free-roaming) verse-encomia.
- **Yù-zhì yuán-shí** 御製緣識 ([[KR6s0060]], K1261, 5 juan) — Buddhist-doctrinal *yuán* (cause) and *shí* (consciousness) verse-encomia.

In addition, [[KR6s0061]] preserves further imperially-composed prefatory matter.

These works were composed during the mid-to-late portion of his reign (ca. **980–997**), most likely with court-monastic editorial assistance even where the imperial byline is unmodified. They demonstrate Tài-zōng's deep personal engagement with Buddhist doctrine and his programmatic effort to position the Sòng imperial state as a major Buddhist-canonical patron — an engagement that contrasts with the more reserved Buddhist policies of subsequent Northern Sòng emperors.

Source: standard Sòng dynastic sources (*Sòng-shǐ* 宋史, j. 4–5 *Tài-zōng běn-jì*); imperially-composed works at [[KR6s0057]]–[[KR6s0061]]; **Sòng huì-yào jí-gǎo** 宋會要輯稿 *Dào-shì* 道釋 sections.
