Wáng Ānshí 王安石 (1021–1086), *zì* Jièfǔ 介甫, *hào* Bànshān 半山, posthumously Wén 文 (subsequently rescinded under the Yuányòu 元祐 anti-reform government). Native of Línchuān 臨川 in Fǔzhōu 撫州 (modern Línchuān, Jiāngxī), thus the conventional sobriquet *Línchuān xiānshēng* 臨川先生. *Jìnshì* of Qìnglì 慶曆 2 (1042) at age 22, ranked fourth on the palace list. Provincial career chiefly in Yīn 鄞 (Zhèjiāng), Shūzhōu 舒州, and Cháng-zhōu 常州 before entering central government under Shénzōng 神宗 (r. 1067–1085) as Senior Grand Counsellor (cān-zhī zhèngshì 參知政事, 1069), then full Counsellor (1070).

The architect of the *Xīnfǎ* 新法 ("New Policies"): the Green Sprouts (qīngmiáo 青苗), Hired Service (mùyì 募役), Equitable Transport (jūnshū 均輸), Market Exchange (shìyì 市易), Baojia 保甲 militia, Baomǎ 保馬 horse-rearing, and Square-Field Land-Tax (fāng-tián jūn-shuì 方田均稅) reforms — collectively the most ambitious pre-modern Chinese state-economic restructuring. Removed from office in 1074 and 1076; retired to Jiāngníng 江寧 (Nanjing). Rehabilitated and re-honoured under Huīzōng 徽宗.

In the classical-textual sphere he is the principal architect of the *Sānjīng xīnyì* 三經新義 — the official Reform-period commentaries on the *Shī* 詩, *Shū* 書, and *Zhōulǐ* 周禮, of which only [[KR1d0004]] *Zhōuguān xīnyì* 周官新義 survives in nearly complete form (reconstructed from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* under the Sìkù project). The *Zhōuguān xīnyì* was, by Cài Tāo's 蔡絛 testimony in *Tiěwéi shān cóngtán* 鐵圍山叢談, the only one of the three commentaries actually written by Wáng's own hand — the *Shī* and *Shū* commentaries were drafted by his son [[王雱]] Wáng Pāng and his disciples. Wáng's *Zì shuō* 字說 (a graphological lexicon) is the central methodological document of the Reform-school exegesis and the principal target of Northern-Sòng anti-Reform philological polemics. He also produced a body of poetry and prose that secured his place among the Eight Masters of the Táng-Sòng (Táng-Sòng bā dàjiā 唐宋八大家); his collected works survive as *Línchuān xiānshēng wénjí* 臨川先生文集 (KR4d collection).

His biography is in *Sòngshǐ* j. 327. CBDB id 1762, dates 1021–1086 — verified against the *Sòngshǐ* lifedates and standard Sōng-shǐ scholarship; no discrepancy with the catalog meta.
