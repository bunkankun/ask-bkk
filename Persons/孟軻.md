# Mèng Kē 孟軻

*Zì* Zǐyú 子輿 (or Zǐchē 子車 in some sources). Native of Zōu 鄒 (modern Zōuxiàn 鄒縣, Shāndōng), once a small Zhōu vassal absorbed by Lǔ. According to Zhào Qí's 趙岐 *Tící* 題辭 (preserved at the head of [[KR1h0002]]), Mencius descended from the Lǔ ducal cadet line of Mèngsūn 孟孫; he was raised by his widowed mother — the famous "three movings" 三遷 of his upbringing — and studied under disciples of Zǐsī 子思 (Kǒng Jí 孔伋), grandson of Confucius. Hence his school is traditionally placed in the **SīMèng** 思孟 line of orthodox transmission.

Conventional dates **372–289 BCE**, derived from late traditions; modern scholarship treats both endpoints as approximate. Active during the late Warring States as an itinerant counsellor, visiting Liáng (Wèi), Qí, Sòng, Téng and Lǔ, and engaging the rulers Liáng Huì wáng 梁惠王, Qí Xuān wáng 齊宣王, and Téng Wén gōng 滕文公 — all immortalised as chapter-titles in his book.

His seven *piān* (later subdivided into 14 *juàn*) preserve dialogues with rulers, disciples (Wàn Zhāng 萬章, Gōngsūn Chǒu 公孫丑, Yuè Zhèngzǐ 樂正子, etc.), rivals (Gàozǐ 告子 on human nature; Xǔ Xíng 許行 on agronomism), and contemporaries. The *Mèngzǐ* 孟子 was elevated from "Masters" (*zǐ* 子) to "Classic" (*jīng* 經) status in the Northern Sòng and incorporated into Zhū Xī's 朱熹 *Sì shū* 四書 ([[KR1h0015]]–[[KR1h0017]]); from 1313 it was a core text of the imperial examinations.

Surviving works in Kanripo: the Mèngzǐ canonical text [[KR1h0001]]; Hàn-commentary edition [[KR1h0002]] with Zhào Qí's 趙岐 注; Sòng *zhùshū* edition [[KR1h0003]]; the *Mèngzǐ yīnyì* [[KR1h0009]] of Sūn Shì 孫奭; Sū Zhé's [[KR1h0011]] *Mèngzǐ jiě* 孟子解; Yú Yǔnwén's [[KR1h0014]] *Zūn Mèng biàn* 尊孟辨; Zhāng Jiǔchéng's [[KR1h0013]] *Mèngzǐ zhuàn* 孟子傳; Zhāng Shì's [[KR1h0022]] *Guǐsì Mèngzǐ shuō* 癸巳孟子說; among many others in this division.
