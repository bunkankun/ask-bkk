Early-Qīng poet, critic, and the most influential single voice of the Kāng-xī-era literary establishment; native of Xīn-chéng 新城 (Shāndōng). *Zì* Zǐ-zhēn 子真 and Yí-shàng 貽上; *hào* Ruǎn-tíng 阮亭, later and chiefly Yú-yáng-shān-rén 漁洋山人; posthumous name Wén-jiǎn 文簡. His personal name 禎 was variously written as 禛, 正, and 禎 — the *Sìkù* and many post-Yōngzhèng editions render it as 王士正 to avoid the Yōngzhèng emperor's personal name 胤禛 (the 禛 form being the original); the 王士禎 form is the modern standard.

*Jìnshì* of Shùn-zhì 15 (1658) at age 24. Served continuously through the Shùn-zhì, Kāng-xī courts, rising to Vice Minister of Punishments (刑部尚書 Xíng-bù shàng-shū) in 1704. The dominant figure of Kāng-xī-era poetics: his *shén-yùn* 神韻 ("spirit-resonance") theory, articulated in the *Tángxián sān-mèi jí* 唐賢三昧集 ([[KR4h0257]]) and across his many *shī-huà* works (*Yú-yáng shī-huà* 漁洋詩話 [[KR4i0058]], *Chí-běi ǒu-tán* 池北偶談, *Xiāng-zǔ bǐ-jì* 香祖筆記), set the agenda for Qīng poetic criticism for two generations. He was the senior of the Shāndōng poetic circle that also included his nephew Zhāng Dǔ-qìng [[張篤慶]] and his brother-in-law Zhāng Shí-jū [[張實居]]; Láng Tíng-huái [[郎廷槐]]'s *Shī-yǒu shī-zhuàn lù* ([[KR4i0059]]) records his exchanges with both of them. Lifedates 1634–1711 are confirmed by CBDB (personid 35063). Note: not to be confused with the late-Míng *Hòu-qī-zǐ* leader Wáng Shì-zhēn 王世貞 (1526–1590), whose name is written with different characters.
