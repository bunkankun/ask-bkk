**Gasan Jōseki** 峨山韶碩 (Kenji 2 → 1276; Jōji 5 / Shōhei 21 / 1366-11-08), Late-Kamakura → Nanbokuchō Japanese **Sōtō-Zen** master, principal dharma-heir of [[瑩山]] **Keizan Jōkin** (1268–1325) and **second-generation abbot of Sōji-ji** 總持寺 in Noto. Style-name (字) Gasan 峨山; dharma-name Jōseki 韶碩. Posthumous title **Myōkaku Tennō Kokushi** 妙覺天皇國師, with later additional titles. Native of Noto 能登 province (modern Ishikawa).

Tonsured young at Yōkō-ji 永光寺 under Keizan; received Keizan's transmission as the principal heir of the Sōji-ji-line. Succeeded as second abbot of Sōji-ji on Keizan's death (1325) and led the temple for the next four decades — the formative era in which Sōji-ji became the dominant Sōtō-Zen institution outside Eihei-ji. Trained an extraordinary cohort of senior disciples — the **Five Disciples** (峨山五哲 *Gasan no goteki*) — through whom every later Sōtō sub-lineage descends:
- **Tsūgen Jakurei** [[寂靈]] (1322–1391) — Tsūgen-ha
- **Mutan Sokan** 無端祖環 (?–1397) — Mutan-ha
- **Daitetsu Sōrei** 大徹宗令 (1333–1408) — Daitetsu-ha
- **Jippō Ryōshū** [[良秀]] (1318–1405) — Jippō-ha
- **Genō Hon'eki** 源翁本益 (1329–1400) — Genō-ha

These five lineages became the institutional structure of medieval and early-Edo Japanese Sōtō, and through them Sōji-ji-line Sōtō spread throughout the Japanese countryside. Gasan is conventionally credited as the principal **institution-building** patriarch of the Sōji-ji branch — Keizan provided the doctrinal and liturgical framework, Gasan and his five disciples translated this into a working temple-network. His own recorded sayings, the *Gasan Wajō goroku* 峨山和尚語録, survive separately (not in the present batch).
