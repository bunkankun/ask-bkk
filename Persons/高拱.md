Gāo Gǒng 高拱 (1512–1578), *zì* Sùqīng 肅卿, *hào* Zhōngxuán 中玄, posthumously *Wénxiāng* 文襄, was the principal political figure of the *Lóng-qìng* (1567–1572) era and a leading *cí-zhāng* of the late Jiā-jìng court. Native of Xīn-zhèng 新鄎 (Hé-nán). *Jìnshì* of *Jiā-jìng xīn-chǒu* (1541). Rose to *Lì-bù shàng-shū* (Minister of Personnel) and ultimately to *Zhōng-jí-diàn dà-xué-shì* (Senior Grand Secretary, the de facto first minister) under the *Lóng-qìng* emperor; dismissed in 1572 by the *Wàn-lì* regents Zhāng Jū-zhèng 張居正 and Féng Bǎo 馮保 in the famous transition-power-struggle. Returned to Hé-nán in retirement; died there in 1578. Biography in *Míng shǐ* j. 213.

(Note: CBDB id 30847 records his birth year as 1510; the standard Míng biographical record gives 1512, followed here. CBDB has alternative homonym entries for "high gong" — id 289236, 310512 — without dates; these are unrelated persons.)

Gāo Gǒng was a substantial scholar in addition to his political career: tutor of the heir apparent (the future *Lóng-qìng* emperor), he produced significant *cí-zhāng* and a number of substantive scholarly works. His [[KR1e0080]] *Chūnqiū zhèng-zhǐ* 春秋正旨 is the most widely-cited of his classical-scholarship monographs; his *Wèn-biàn lù* 問辨錄 (Sìkù entry KR3a0119) on the *Sì-shū* is the second most important. The *Chūnqiū zhèng-zhǐ* takes a sharply *anti-Hú Ānguó* line, rejecting the orthodox examination commentary's *bāo-biǎn* readings on the basis of philological-rational analysis. Modern collected works: *Gāo Wén-xiāng-gōng wén jí* 高文襄公文集 (preserved in WYG and other collectanea).

His political fall in 1572 has been the subject of substantial recent reconsideration in the Chinese-language historiography (see esp. Yuè Tiānyǔ 岳天宇, *Gāo Gǒng yánjiū* 高拱研究, Bēijīng dàxué 2010), which has tended to rehabilitate Gāo against the Zhāng Jū-zhèng-aligned standard *Míng shǐ* account.
