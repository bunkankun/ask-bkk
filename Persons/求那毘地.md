Guṇavṛddhi 求那毘地 (Skt. *Guṇavṛddhi* "Increase-of-Virtue"; Chinese gloss 安進 *Ān-jìn* / 德進 *Dé-jìn*; DILA Authority A000526; d. November 502 / February 503) was an Indian monk and translator of the late fifth century. The biographical sources are the *Gāosēng zhuàn* 高僧傳 (T2059, 345a–345c) and the *Chū sānzàng jì jí* 出三藏記集 (T2145). According to those sources he was a native of central India (中天竺) and a disciple of the great fifth-century Indian master Saṅghasena 僧伽斯; he travelled east to Jiànkāng and there worked under the patronage of the Xiāo-Qí 蕭齊 dynasty (479–502).

His most influential translation is the *Bǎiyù jīng* 百喻經 (T209, the *Hundred Parables Sūtra*, attributed to his teacher Saṅghasena), completed in Jiànyuán 建元 4 (482). He also translated the *[[KR6a0073|Xūdá jīng 須達經]]* (T73, the *Velāma-sūtra*), the *Sēngjiā lūn-dào jīng* 僧伽羅刹所集經 (lost), and several short *Āgama* extractions. He died at Jiànkāng during the winter of Zhōngxīng 中興 2 (= late 502 / early 503 CE). The text *Bǎiyù jīng* in particular has remained, in subsequent centuries, one of the most accessible introductions to Buddhist parable-literature in East Asia.
