Mid- to late-Táng Tiāntái 天台 master; the **tenth patriarch** of the Tiāntái school, in succession to Zhànrán 湛然 ([[湛然]], 711–782, the ninth patriarch). DILA Authority A001563. Honorifics: **Xīng-dào zūn-zhě** 興道尊者 ("Venerable Way-Promoter"), **Zhǐguān héshàng** 止觀和尚 ("Master of Cessation-and-Contemplation"). Lifedates unrecorded; productive period in the late eighth and early ninth centuries.

Dharma-disciple of Zhànrán; the principal post-Zhànrán transmitter of the Tiāntái scholastic tradition at Tiāntái-shān itself, in succession to Zhànrán's primary disciple **Xíng-mǎn** 行滿. Co-author with **Qián-shù** 乾淑 ([[乾淑]]) of the **Zhǐguān jì-zhōng yì-yì** 止觀記中異義 ([[KR6d0136]], X55n0918) — a study of variant readings in Zhànrán's *Zhǐguān fǔ-xíng zhuàn-hóng-jué* ([[KR6d0131]], T1912).

Of central historiographical importance: Dào-suì was the Chinese teacher of the Japanese Tendai 天台 founder **Saichō** 最澄 (767–822), who travelled to China in Yánlì 延曆 23 (804) and there received the Tiāntái transmission from Dào-suì at Tiāntái-shān before returning to Japan in 805 to establish the Tendai school at the Hieizan 比叡山 establishment. Through this transmission, Dào-suì is the institutional originator of the Japanese Tendai tradition.
