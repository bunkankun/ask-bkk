Mùjiānlián 目犍連 (full form: 大目乾連 *Dà-mù-gàn-lián*, *Mahāmaudgalyāyana*; Pāli: *Mahāmoggallāna*; also 拘律陀 *Jūlǜtuó* < Skt. *Kolita*; DILA Authority A000053; traditional dates c. 5th century BCE) was one of the two foremost disciples of Śākyamuni Buddha, ranked supreme in supernatural powers (*ṛddhi*). He was born in the village of Kolita near Rājagṛha and was the childhood friend and fellow-seeker of Śāriputra ([[舍利弗]]), with whom he jointly converted to Buddhism after hearing the verse on dependent origination from the monk Aśvajit. The two are consistently paired as the Buddha's right-hand disciples in both canonical and scholastic literature.

In the Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma tradition, Maudgalyāyana is credited with the composition of the *Dharma-skandha-pāda* ([[KR6l0002]]), which systematically enumerates the 37 factors of awakening and related dharmic categories. The DILA *Jùshě lùn shū* (cited in the *Mahāvibhāṣā*) also attributes the *Prajñapti-śāstra* ([[KR6l0003]]) partly to him; the *Mahāvibhāṣā* credits Maudgalyāyana with authorship of the *Prajñapti-śāstra* in a separate tradition. Modern scholarship treats all such attributions as literary conventions.

The story of Maudgalyāyana rescuing his mother from the realm of hungry ghosts is a central narrative of the *Yúlánpén jīng* 盂蘭盆經, which generated an important liturgical tradition in East Asia, distinct from his role in the Sarvāstivāda scholastic corpus.
