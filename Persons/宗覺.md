Shūkaku-Jiki 宗覺直 (full name; the catalog meta abbreviates to 宗覺 Shūkaku) was a late 17th-century Japanese **Shingon-Vinaya** (*Shingon-Risshū* 真言律宗) monk at the **Kushū-on Vinaya Cloister** 久修園律院 in Kyoto, the temple founded by **Jōgon** 淨嚴 (1639–1702) in 1678 as the institutional centre of the Edo-period Shingon-Risshū revival. The preface colophon of his work [[KR6t0058]] *Dà-shèng yuán-jiè xiǎn-zhèng lùn* identifies him as "*Kushū-on Ritsu-in bhikṣu* 宗覺直" and dates the work to Jōkyō 1 = 1684 CE; the cover-line identifies him as a "*Bhikṣu of the Capital* (京師)".

He is presumably a pupil or contemporary of Jōgon, working within the institutional structure of the Edo-Shingon-Risshū. His one surviving canonical work — [[KR6t0058]] *Dà-shèng yuán-jiè xiǎn-zhèng lùn* — is a five-chapter polemical defence of the joint observance of the Brahmajāla bodhisattva-precepts and the *Sì-fēn-lǜ prātimokṣa*, directed (without naming) against the contemporary **Anraku-in Tendai** *yuán-jiè* school of **Reikū Kōken** 靈空光謙 (1652–1739) which had begun to argue, around the 1680s, that the *yuán-jiè* alone sufficed and that the *Sì-fēn-lǜ* could be dispensed with in the *mò-fǎ* age.
