**Xiāoshān Zhúlínsì sēng** 蕭山竹林寺僧 ("The Monks of Bamboo-Grove Monastery at Xiāoshān"), the **collective attribution-lineage** of the gynecological works transmitted through the Bamboo-Grove Monastery 竹林寺 at Xiāoshān 蕭山 (Zhèjiāng) from (according to internal tradition) the Táng dynasty through the late Qīng — one of the most famous monastic medical lineages in late-imperial Chinese medicine.

The lineage is not a single individual: a substantial corpus of gynecological writings is transmitted collectively, including the *Zhú-lín-sì nǚkē mì-fāng* 竹林寺女科秘方 ([[KR3ei046]]), *Zhú-lín nǚkē zhèng-zhì* 竹林女科證治 ([[KR3ei026]] by Fāng Chānghán 方昌翰), *Níng-kūn mì-jí* 寧坤秘笈 ([[KR3ei013]]), and others. The texts are mutually intertextual and editorially layered, each Qīng recension preserving older lineage material while incorporating contemporary popular formulas.

Scholarly research (Yi-Li Wu, "The Bamboo Grove Monastery and Popular Gynecology in Qing China," *Late Imperial China* 21.1 [2000]: 41–76, and her *Reproducing Women*, 2010) situates the surviving textual corpus as a 17th- and 18th-century crystallisation of regional Zhèjiāng popular gynecological knowledge, with the Táng-origin attribution functioning as a marketing brand for the lineage. The monastery itself is historically attested as a Buddhist establishment of Xiāoshān, but its medical activity intensified in the late Míng and Qīng.
