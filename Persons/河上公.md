The legendary Daoist immortal known in tradition as the **Master of the River Bank** (*Héshàng gōng* 河上公) or the **Old Man of the River Bank** (*Héshàng zhàngrén* 河上丈人), attributed author of the foundational [[KR5c0065|*Dàodé zhēn jīng zhù* 道德真經註]] (DZ 682) — the single most influential commentary on the *Dàodé jīng* in the Chinese religious-Daoist tradition. Modern scholarship (Schipper 2004, Chan 1991, Robinet 1977) treats the Héshàng gōng as a legendary figure and the attributed commentary as an Eastern-Hàn (25–220 CE) composition.

**Traditional biography.** The legend is fullest in **Gé Hóng's** 葛洪 *Shén xiān zhuàn* 神仙傳 (4th cent. CE) and partially in Liú Xiàng's 劉向 earlier *Lìè xiān zhuàn* 列仙傳 (Western Hàn). Both versions tell the same basic story: a certain recluse, old and unnamed, was seen dwelling in a thatched hut (*máo ān* 茅庵) on the bank of a river in Shǎnzhōu 陝州 (near the Yellow River in present-day northwestern Henan). Hàn Wéndì 漢文帝 (r. 180–157 BCE) — in a version that places the encounter in the early Western Hàn — heard of the recluse and went to inquire about the *Dàodé jīng*. When the emperor, unable to part easily with his royal dignity, asked the recluse from his carriage rather than dismounting, the Héshàng gōng ascended into the clouds and rebuked him; repentant, Wéndì approached on foot and was granted the transmission of the commentary. The Héshàng gōng's final words to the emperor — "The Tao is honoured, its virtue is venerated" (*dào zūn, dé guì* 道尊德貴) — became the governing slogan of the Hàn Daoist imperial-cultivation tradition.

**Identification.** In the mature Daoist hagiography (from the Táng onwards), the Héshàng gōng is identified as an early incarnation or self-manifestation of [[老子]] (Lǎozǐ / Tài shàng Lǎo jūn 太上老君). He is also associated with the *Hé tú* 河圖 ("River Chart") revelatory tradition — hence one possible etymology of his name — and with the *Bā shǐ* 八史 ("Eight Archivists") of the early Língbǎo 靈寶 corpus.

**Attributed work.** The commentary ascribed to him — *Lǎozǐ Dàodé jīng Héshàng gōng zhāng jù* 老子道德經河上公章句 (also transmitted as simply *Dàodé zhēn jīng zhù* 道德真經註) — establishes the 81-chapter division of the received *Dàodé jīng*, gives each chapter its three-character title (*Tǐ dào* 體道, *Yǎng shēn* 養身, *Ān mín* 安民, etc.), and reads the scripture through a distinctive *zhì shēn–yǎng shēng* 治身養生 framework. Modern consensus places the composition in the Eastern Hàn period, probably in the 1st–2nd centuries CE. See the main work note at [[KR5c0065]].

**Cult and continuing influence.** The Héshàng gōng figure is venerated in the mature Daoist pantheon, especially within the *Zhèngyī* 正一 tradition. A shrine at the legendary encounter-site in Shǎnzhōu was maintained through the Hàn, Táng, and Sòng; the *Jiù Táng shū* 舊唐書 records Tàng-era imperial visits. In the *Shàngqīng* 上清 tradition, the Héshàng gōng is regularly listed among the revelations transmitted through the lineage from Lǎozǐ.
