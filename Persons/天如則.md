# Tiānrú Wéizé 天如惟則 (1286–1354)

A leading **Línjì-school** 臨濟宗 Chán master of the late Yuán, dharma heir of **Zhōngfēng Míngběn** 中峯明本 (1263–1323) and one of the most influential propagators of the integrated Chán-Pure Land synthesis (*chán-jìng shuāng-xiū* 禪淨雙修) that defined late-imperial Chinese Buddhism. Born in **Yǒng-xīn** 永新 (modern Jiāngxī), surname Tan 譚; he received the dharma-name 惟則 *Wéizé* (with sobriquet **Tiānrú** 天如) during his training under Zhōngfēng at **Tiānmù-shān** 天目山. After his master's death he settled at **Shīzǐ-lín** 師子林 (Lion Grove) in Sūzhōu 蘇州, the famous monastic compound and garden complex that he established with literati patrons; the *Shīzǐ-lín* would later pass into the lay-elite garden tradition and survives today as the well-known UNESCO-listed Sūzhōu garden.

His major surviving works are the **《淨土或問》** *Jìng-tǔ huò-wèn* [[KR6p0053]] (T1972), a single-fascicle question-and-answer Pure Land doctrinal treatise that is one of the foundational late-medieval texts of the Chán-Pure Land integration; the **《天如則禪師語錄》** *Tiānrú Zé chánshī yǔlù* (his recorded sayings, multiple recensions); and miscellaneous prefaces and verse. The *Huò-wèn* in particular argues forcefully that there is no doctrinal opposition between Chán and Pure Land cultivation — a position later endorsed and amplified by [[袾宏]] Yúnqī Zhūhóng, [[行策]] Jiéliú Xíngcè, and the other major late-Míng / Qīng Pure Land masters, and one that decisively shaped the institutional shape of late-imperial Chinese Buddhism. He died in 至正 14 (1354) at age 69.
