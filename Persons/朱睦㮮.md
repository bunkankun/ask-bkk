# Zhū Mùzhì 朱睦㮮

Style name *Guànfǔ* 灌甫, sobriquet *Xī-tíng* 西亭. Sixth-generation descendant of Zhū Sù 朱橚, Prince Dìng of Zhōu 周定王 (a son of Míng Tài-zǔ Hóngwǔ); he inherited the hereditary title *Zhèn-guó zhōng-wèi* 鎮國中尉 (Brigadier of the Imperial Defence) and was based at the Zhōu princely residence in Kāi-fēng. In the early Wàn-lì era he was nominated *Zōng-zhèng* 宗正 (Master of the Imperial Clan).

He built the *Wàn juàn táng* 萬卷堂 ("Hall of Ten Thousand Volumes") at his Kāi-fēng residence, which was one of the great late-Míng private book-collections — comparable in importance to Fàn Qīn 范欽's Tiān-yī gé 天一閣 and Hú Yīng-lín 胡應麟's Èr-yǒu shānfáng 二酉山房. He devoted his life to the editing, collation, and transmission of the imperial-clan's rare-book holdings, producing a series of highly respected scholarly works.

His chief surviving works:

1. **Shòu jīng tú** 授經圖 (Genealogical Tables of the Transmission of the Classics) — a systematic prosopography of the Hàn–Sòng commentary lineages on the *Five Classics*, separately catalogued in the *Sìkù*.
2. **Wǔ jīng jī yí** 五經稽疑 ([[KR1g0017]]) in 8 *juàn* — covering classical doubts on the *Wǔ jīng*; partially completed (the *Chūnqiū jī yí* portion was finished earlier and circulated separately).
3. **Yì xué shí dú** 易學識讀, **Chūnqiū sù zhuàn** 春秋諸傳, and other smaller works on individual classical books.

His *Wǔ jīng jī yí* self-preface, dated the tenth month of Wàn-lì 11 (1583), explicitly invokes Qū Yuán's posture — "*Qū Píng was of the imperial clan of Chǔ*… *being slandered, he composed the Lí Sāo*" — to frame his own private scholarship: a scholar-noble of the imperial Zhū clan, denied an active role in court politics, taking refuge in classical learning. His scholarship is broad-ranging and citationally robust; the *Sìkù* tíyào notes some over-credulity (citing Guō Jīng's *Yì jǔ zhèng*, a known forgery) but on the whole rates the work as carefully done.
