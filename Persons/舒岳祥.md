**Shū Yuèxiáng** 舒岳祥 (1219–1298), *zì* Shùnhóu 舜侯 (the older form was *zì* Jǐngwéi 景薇 — "Light-of-Wéi"), *hào* Lángfēng 閬風 or Yuándài 圓帶, was a late-Sòng poet, prose-writer, and *yímín* 遺民 of Níng-hǎi 寧海 in Tāizhōu 台州 prefecture (modern Tāizhōu, Zhèjiāng — CBDB native place 台州寧海縣新寧鄉). *Jìnshì* of *Bǎoyòu* 4 (1256). He served only briefly as Magistrate-of-Recorder (*wèi* 尉) of Fènghuà 奉化, and ended his official career with the honorary mid-rank title *Chéng-zhí láng* 承直郎. He withdrew completely after the fall of the Sòng in 1276 and devoted his remaining twenty-two years to teaching young men of his home region and to scholarly-and-literary composition.

According to the *Liǎng-Zhè míng-xián lù* 兩浙名賢錄, Shū's writings totalled 220 *juàn* across more than a dozen separately-named drafts and sub-collections — historical works (*Shǐ-shù* 史述, *Hàn-biǎn bǔ* 漢砭補, *Shǐ-jiā lù* 史家錄, *Sān-shǐ zuǎn-yán* 三史纂言), literary drafts (*Sūn-shù gǎo* 蓀墅稿, *Bì-dì gǎo* 避地稿, *Zhuàn-qí gǎo* 篆畦稿, *Dié-xuān gǎo* 蝶軒稿, *Wú-zhú-lǐ gǎo* 梧竹里稿), a *Tán cóng-cóng* and several continuations (*Xù*, *Cán*, *Chuán*, *Yì cóng*), a *Xī-yóu lù* 昔遊錄, and a *Shēn-yī tú-shuō* 深衣圖説 — almost all now lost. What survives is the *Lángfēng jí* 閬風集 in twelve *juàn*, reconstructed by the *Sì-kù* editors from material in the *Yǒnglè dà-diǎn* where Shū's various sub-collections appear largely under the umbrella name *Lángfēng*. Compiled work in the Kanripo corpus: [[KR4d0384]] *Lángfēng jí*.

Shū maintained over twenty years of correspondence with [[王應麟]] Wáng Yīnglín (1223–1296), the great Sì-míng polymath, and the *Lángfēng jí* preserves Wáng's substantial original preface — one of the few first-person primary documents of the Wáng–Shū friendship. Their two-decade correspondence is the longest-running attested *yímín* literary friendship of the eastern-Zhèjiāng late-Sòng circle. A Hú Chángrú 胡長孺 (Yǒngkāng) preface of 1311 (*Zhì-dà* 4) provided the prefatory matter for the early-Yuán printed edition. The *Sòng-shǐ* contains no biography of Shū, making the *Lángfēng jí* itself, with its two prefaces, the principal primary source for his life. He was a teacher of [[戴表元]] Dài Biǎoyuán (the early-Yuán Tāizhōu poet) and of [[劉莊孫]] Liú Zhuāngsūn.
