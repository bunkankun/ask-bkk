Dharmamitra 曇摩蜜多 (Chinese gloss 法秀 *Fǎ-xiù*; DILA Authority A001805; 356–442) was a Central-Asian Buddhist translator active in fifth-century China. The biographical sources are the *Chū sānzàng jì jí* 出三藏記集 (T2145, 105a–b) and the *Gāosēng zhuàn* 高僧傳 (T2059, 342c–343b). Born in Kāpiśī 罽賓 (Kashmir region), he travelled east via Kucha and Dūnhuáng, arriving in southern China during the Yuán-jiā 元嘉 era of the Liú-Sòng 劉宋 (424–453). He worked at the *Zhī-yuán* 祇洹 monastery in Jiànkāng 建康 (modern Nánjīng) and at the *Lóng-guāng* 龍光 monastery, producing translations across an eighteen-year period (424–442).

His translations include three short Mahāyāna sūtras of the Ākāśagarbha cult: the *[[KR6h0011|Xū-kōng-zàng púsà shén-zhòu jīng]]* (T0407) and the *[[KR6h0013|Guān xū-kōng-zàng púsà jīng]]* (T0409), both visualization-and-spell texts, plus the *Guān pǔ-xián púsà xíng-fǎ jīng* 觀普賢菩薩行法經 (T0277, the standard companion-text to the *Saddharma-puṇḍarīka*), the *Sengiqie-luo-cha jīng* 禪秘要法經 (T0613), the *Wǔ-mén-chán-jīng yào-yòng fǎ* 五門禪經要用法 (T0619), and several minor texts of dhyāna and visualization practice. His translation idiom is one of the principal sources for the late-Liú-Sòng visualization-meditation literature that fed directly into the Tiāntái and Huáyán traditions of the Suí and Táng. He died at Jiànkāng in Yuán-jiā 19 (442), aged 87.
