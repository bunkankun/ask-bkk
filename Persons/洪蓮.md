Early- and mid-Míng imperial-court Buddhist scholar-monk; one of the most prominent monastic editors of the **Yǒnglè–Xuāndé–Zhèngtǒng** decades (c. 1410s–1450s). *Zì* **Dú-fāng** 獨芳; lay surname **Wú** 吳; native of **Tài-yuán** 太原 (Shān-xī). Ordained at the **Ráo-yì-sì** 饒益寺, formally received full precepts at twenty. Recognized by the master **Xiū-yún** 休雲 with the bestowal of the dharma-robe; meditated at **Tiān-lóng-dòng** 天龍洞 and at **Míng-xiān-dòng** 名仙洞, where he set up a *maṇḍala* seat and conducted *yàn-kǒu* (flaming-mouth) hungry-ghost feeding rites for three years; thereupon noticed by the **King of Jìn** 晉王, who installed him as abbot of **Hóng-yòu-sì** 鴻祐寺.

Brought into the **Yǒnglè-court editorial machine**, he was commissioned by imperial order to annotate the *Dà-míng sān-zàng fǎ-shù* 大明三藏法數 and to collate the Tripiṭaka. Summoned to the *xiāng-diàn* 香殿 to dispute *Śūraṅgama* doctrine with *Jìn-fǎ-zhǔ* 進法主; commissioned to oversee the collation and copying of the Tripiṭaka at **Hǎi-yìn-sì** 海印寺. Successively favored under the **Rén-zōng** (Hóng-xī) and **Xuān-zōng** (Xuān-dé) reigns; appointed **Yòu jiǎng-jīng** 右講經 of the **Sēng-lù-sī** 僧錄司 (Buddhist Affairs Bureau), promoted to **Zuǒ jiǎng-jīng** 左講經 in **Zhèng-tǒng 7 (1442)**. Permitted in old age to forgo court audience while retaining office. Died seated upright after composing a final verse on **Jǐng-tài 7 / 7 / 4 (1456-08-13)**, age **91**, monastic age 71. Posthumously honored by the **Dài-zōng emperor** with the title **Jìng-fàn Yì-jiào Chán-shī** 淨梵翊教禪師 ("Chán Master Pure-Brahma Augmenting-the-Teaching"); funeral by imperial Rites Ministry. Principal Tripiṭaka work: the imperially-commissioned [[KR6c0056]] *Jīn-gāng jīng zhù-jiě* (X24 no. 468), a four-juan recension of the **17-house Sòng-period collected commentary on the *Vajracchedikā***, finalized in **1423** (Yǒnglè 21).
