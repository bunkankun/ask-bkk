Xú Lùqīng 徐鹿卿 (zì Défū 德夫, hào Quángǔ 泉谷, posthumous name Qīngzhèng 清正) was a native of Fēngchéng 豐城 (modern Jiāngxī). He took his *jìnshì* in Jiādìng 嘉定 16 (1223) and rose through a series of posts — Erudite of the Directorate of Education (國子監主簿), Vice-Minister of the Court of the Imperial Treasury (太府少卿), Vice-Minister of Rites (禮部侍郎) — retiring as Auxiliary Academician of the Huáwén Hall (華文閣待制). His biography is in *Sòngshǐ*. He was a close associate of Zhēn Déxiù 真德秀 (Xīshān 西山), who had recommended him as a man of "deep learning and upright character"; Liú Kèzhuāng [[劉克莊]] praised him as combining "the purity of [Dǒng] Zhòngshū with the practicality of Jiǎ Yì" (董子之醇,賈生之通). He is principally remembered for his outspoken memorials denouncing factional politics, imperial favourites, the deteriorating paper currency (*chǔ* 楮), and the empire's military weakness — themes developed across his memorials on the capital fire of 1232 and the 1240s frontier crisis. His original *Quángǔ wénjí* and several specialized policy compilations were lost; the surviving *Qīng-zhèng cún-gǎo* [[KR4d0335]] is a Wànlì-era reconstitution by his twelfth-generation descendant Xú Jiàn 徐鑒.
