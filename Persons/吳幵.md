Wú Jiān 吳幵 (fl. 1097–1132), *zì* Zhèng-zhòng 正仲, native of Chú-zhōu 滁州 (modern Ān-huī). Passed the *hóng-cí* 宏詞 examination in *Shào-shèng dīng-chǒu* 紹聖丁丑 (1097). Rose under the late Northern Sòng to *Hàn-lín chéng-zhǐ* 翰林承旨 (Drafter-in-Charge of the Hanlin Academy) during the *Jìng-kāng* 靖康 crisis (1126–1127). With Gěng Nán-zhòng 耿南仲 he urged the policy of ceding territory to the Jīn — a position that the historiographical tradition has uniformly judged as catastrophic for the Sòng cause. After the fall of Kāifēng he was complicit in conveying Jīn instructions and in the elevation of the puppet emperor Zhāng Bāng-chāng 張邦昌 (the *Chǔ* 楚 regime, 1127). After the Sòng restoration under *Jiàn-yán* 建炎 (1127ff.), he was banished and died in exile.

The *Sìkù* editors of [[KR4i0011]] judge bluntly: "*qí rén běn bù zú dào* — his person is unworthy of being spoken of"; but they admit that his *Yōu-gǔ-táng shī-huà* 優古堂詩話 [[KR4i0011]] contains material of real value for the study of Northern-Sòng poetics. His critical attention is focused on the *duó-tāi huàn-gǔ* 奪胎換骨 (rebirth-and-bone-exchange) techniques of allusion-recasting, and the work is a primary source for tracing the literary genealogies of Sū Shì 蘇軾 [[蘇軾]], Huáng Tíng-jiān 黃庭堅 [[黃庭堅]], and the wider *Yuán-yòu* 元祐 literary circle.
