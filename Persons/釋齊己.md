**Shì Qí-jǐ** 釋齊己 (originally Hú Dé-shēng 胡得生, *ca.* 864 – *ca.* 937), of Cháng-shā in Hú-nán. Late-Táng / Five-Dynasties Buddhist monk-poet, the most prolific and stylistically classical of the late-Táng monastic poets and the contemporary peer of **Guàn-xiū** 貫休 [[釋貫休]]. Tonsured at Tóng-qìng-sì 同慶寺 of Wéi-yáng-shān 溈陽山 (a center of the *Wéi-Yǎng* Chán lineage in Hú-nán) in his early years; took the *zì* 衡岳沙門 ("monk of Héng-yuè"). Spent his middle years in the Hú-nán / Jiāng-xī region in correspondence with the secular poet **Zhèng Gǔ** 鄭谷 (his most important poetic-critical interlocutor — the *yī-zì shī* "one-character teacher" anecdote about Qí-jǐ's *Zǎo-méi* belongs to their exchange) and with the cluster of late-Táng poets that included Sī-kōng Tú 司空圖 [[司空圖]], Lù Guī-méng 陸龜蒙, Pí Rì-xiū 皮日休, and Pī Bīn 沈彬.

Late in life, after the disorder following the Táng's collapse (907), he moved to *Jīng-nán* (modern Jiāng-líng, Hú-běi), where he was received as honored guest at the court of **Gāo Jì-xīng** 高季興 (founder of the *Nán-píng* / *Jīng-nán* kingdom, r. 924–928), who built him a *jìng-shì* (pure cell) and supplied him with provisions. There he became a close friend of the *Hàn-lín* literary client **Sūn Guāng-xiàn** 孫光憲, who edited the surviving 810 poems into the ten-*juǎn* [[KR4c0109]] *Bái-lián jí* 白蓮集 after Qí-jǐ's death, with a preface dated *Tiān-fú* 3 / 3 / 1 (= 938.04.04). Qí-jǐ's poetic style — clear in form, free of vulgar diction, bitter in thought, plain yet meaning-distant — sets the late-Táng monk-poet aesthetic in its classical key, distinct from the more startlingly unconventional manner of Guàn-xiū. His most celebrated single poem is the *Zǎo-méi* 早梅 ("the village deep in snow / one branch opened last night") — the canonical Chinese poetic *zǎo-méi* and the locus of the *yī-zì shī* anecdote.
