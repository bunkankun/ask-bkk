**Shì Qíjǐ** 釋齊己 (originally Hú Déshēng 胡得生, *ca.* 864 – *ca.* 937), of Chángshā in Húnán. Late-Táng / Five-Dynasties Buddhist monk-poet, the most prolific and stylistically classical of the late-Táng monastic poets and the contemporary peer of **Guànxiū** 貫休 [[釋貫休]]. Tonsured at Tóngqìngsì 同慶寺 of Wéiyángshān 溈陽山 (a center of the *WéiYǎng* Chán lineage in Húnán) in his early years; took the *zì* 衡岳沙門 ("monk of Héngyuè"). Spent his middle years in the Húnán / Jiāngxī region in correspondence with the secular poet **Zhèng Gǔ** 鄭谷 (his most important poetic-critical interlocutor — the *yīzì shī* "one-character teacher" anecdote about Qíjǐ's *Zǎoméi* belongs to their exchange) and with the cluster of late-Táng poets that included Sīkōng Tú 司空圖 [[司空圖]], Lù Guīméng 陸龜蒙, Pí Rìxiū 皮日休, and Pī Bīn 沈彬.

Late in life, after the disorder following the Táng's collapse (907), he moved to *Jīngnán* (modern Jiānglíng, Húběi), where he was received as honored guest at the court of **Gāo Jìxīng** 高季興 (founder of the *Nánpíng* / *Jīngnán* kingdom, r. 924–928), who built him a *jìngshì* (pure cell) and supplied him with provisions. There he became a close friend of the *Hànlín* literary client **Sūn Guāngxiàn** 孫光憲, who edited the surviving 810 poems into the ten-*juǎn* [[KR4c0109]] *Báilián jí* 白蓮集 after Qíjǐ's death, with a preface dated *Tiānfú* 3 / 3 / 1 (= 938.04.04). Qíjǐ's poetic style — clear in form, free of vulgar diction, bitter in thought, plain yet meaning-distant — sets the late-Táng monk-poet aesthetic in its classical key, distinct from the more startlingly unconventional manner of Guànxiū. His most celebrated single poem is the *Zǎoméi* 早梅 ("the village deep in snow / one branch opened last night") — the canonical Chinese poetic *zǎoméi* and the locus of the *yīzì shī* anecdote.
