# Jiānhuì púsà 堅慧菩薩 (\*Sāramati)

Indian Buddhist master conventionally dated to the late fourth or early fifth century CE — placed by the Indian-Buddhist hagiographic tradition between Asaṅga and Vasubandhu, though Tibetan sources sometimes locate him earlier. Chinese tradition (preserved in the Cí'ēn / Hua-yan commentaries) attributes to him two short Tathāgatagarbha treatises preserved in Chinese only: the *Mahāyāna-dharmadhātu-aviśeṣa-śāstra* — translated by [[提雲般若]] (Devaprajñā) twice during the Wǔ Zé-tiān era — as [[KR6n0089]] *Dà-shèng fǎ-jiè wú chā-bié lùn* 大乘法界無差別論 (T31n1626) and [[KR6n0090]] (T31n1627). The two are essentially the same text in slightly different recensions; both attribute authorship to Sāramati.

In some Tibetan and East Asian traditions Sāramati is also identified with the redactor of the *Ratnagotravibhāga* commentary, a position now generally accepted in modern critical scholarship (Takasaki 1966) — i.e. that Sāramati is the human author who systematised the traditionally Maitreya-attributed *Uttaratantra-śāstra* into its received form. If this identification is correct, then Sāramati is one of the principal architects of the Indian Tathāgatagarbha tradition, alongside Asaṅga and the (anonymous) author(s) of the *Ratnagotra* verses themselves.

The Chinese translation of his short *Dharmadhātu-aviśeṣa* is the basis for [[KR6n0091]] *Dà-shèng fǎ-jiè wú chā-bié lùn shū* by [[法藏]] (T44n1838) and the subsequent Hua-yan school commentarial development of the doctrine.
