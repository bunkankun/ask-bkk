**Ní Wéi-dé** 倪維德 (*zì* Zhòng-xián 仲賢, *hào* Mín-zhāi 敏齋, 1303–1377), late-Yuán / early-Míng physician of Wú-xiàn 吳縣 (Sūzhōu, Jiāng-sū). A hereditary physician of three generations on his mother's side, he studied medicine first under his maternal kin and then with Wáng Lǚ 王履, the leading disciple of [[朱震亨]] Zhū Dān-xī; through this line he is reckoned the principal carrier of the Dān-xī-school ophthalmological tradition into the early Míng. He served as imperial-medical-academy attendant (太醫院醫士) under the Yuán in his youth and retired to private practice in Sūzhōu before the Yuán-Míng transition; his contemporaries dubbed him "Yī shèng" 醫聖 ("medical-sage") for his diagnostic reputation. His single major surviving work is the [[KR3em001]] *Yuán-jī qǐ-wēi* 原機啟微 (2 *juǎn* with a Jiā-jìng-era appendix), the earliest fully-extant Chinese ophthalmology monograph organised by aetiology rather than by symptomatic taxonomy; the work supplanted the older five-wheel / eight-quadrant *Lóng-mù lùn* tradition (cf. [[KR3em003]]) and became the conceptual prototype of all subsequent literate-physician ophthalmology, including [[KR3em007]] *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn · Yǎn-kē xīn-fǎ yào-jué*.
