# Yú Xiāokè 余蕭客

Style name *Zhònglín* 仲林. Native of Chángzhōu 長洲 (in Sūzhōu prefecture, Jiāngsū). Born 1729, died 1777 — note: the catalog meta gives 1729–1777; CBDB id 66038 gives 1728–1778 (a one-year discrepancy at each end). The catalog meta dates are followed here as more conservative, and align with the standard *Yú Zhònglín nián pǔ* and the *Sūzhōu fǔ zhì* biographical entries.

A direct disciple of Huì Dòng 惠棟 ([[惠棟]]), and the leading Wú-school *Hàn xué* scholar of the second generation. Like his teacher he never held office; spent his life in classical study at Chángzhōu, supported by his teaching circle. The *Sìkù tíyào* on his *Gǔ jīng jiě gōu chén* 古經解鉤沉 ([[KR1g0031]]) records that he began composing the work in *jǐ-mǎo* (Qiánlóng 24 = 1759), completed the draft in *rén-wǔ* (Qiánlóng 27 = 1762), and during the years of compilation worked day and night at copying — to the point of nearly going blind.

His *Gǔ jīng jiě gōu chén* in 30 *juàn* (with sub-divisions effectively 33) is the methodologically most thoroughgoing recovery-compilation of pre-Táng classical commentary fragments — *Yì*, *Shū*, *Shī*, *Sānlǐ*, *Chūnqiū sān zhuàn*, *Xiàojīng*, *Lúnyǔ*, *Mèngzǐ*, *Ěryǎ*. The work's principles: (i) preface giving the master's name, native place, office, and titles of his works; (ii) where the work is extant, omit the entry; (iii) where the entry is incomplete, retain it; (iv) where the master's name is unknown but the work-name preserved (or vice versa), include both; (v) every fragment cited must give its source-text and *juàn*-number for verification; (vi) collate canonical text against the Northern-Sòng best-cuts. The work is the high-Qing reference standard for pre-Táng classical fragments and remains in scholarly use today.
