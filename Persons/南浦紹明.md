**Nanpo Jōmyō** 南浦紹明 (1235–1308) — Kamakura-period Japanese **Rinzai-Zen** master, posthumous imperial titles **Entsū Daiō Kokushi** 圓通大應國師 ("National Master Universal-Penetrating Great-Response") and **Daiō Kokushi** 大應國師.

Born 1235 in Suruga Province. Ordained at Kenchō-ji in Kamakura under [[道隆]] **Lánxī Dàolóng** (Rankei Dōryū). Travelled to Song-dynasty China in 1259; received Línjì-school transmission from **Xūtáng Zhìyú** 虛堂智愚 (1185–1269) at Jìngshānsì 徑山寺 — the **Xūtáng line** of Línjì Zen, which through Nanpo's transmission became the principal Japanese Rinzai-Zen lineage of the medieval Gozan system. Returned to Japan in 1267. Founded **Kōtoku-ji** 興德寺 in Hakata; later resided at **Kenchō-ji** in Kamakura. Died 1308 at age 74.

His Recorded Sayings — *Entsū Daiō Kokushi goroku* 圓通大應國師語錄 ([[KR6t0254]], T80n2548) — two fascicles, were compiled by his disciple **Soshō** 祖照 (and others). The Xūtáng-Nanpo line through his disciple **Shūhō Myōchō** 宗峰妙超 (Daitō Kokushi, 1282–1338) — founder of Daitoku-ji — and Shūhō's disciple **Kanzan Egen** 關山慧玄 (1277–1360) — founder of Myōshin-ji — produced the **Ō-tō-kan** 應燈關 ("the Daiō, Daitō, Kanzan triple-lineage") which became the principal medieval and modern Japanese Rinzai-Zen lineage.
