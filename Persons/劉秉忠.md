Liú Bǐng-zhōng 劉秉忠 (1216–1274), originally named Liú Kǎn 劉侃, *zì* Zhòng-huì 仲晦, monastic name Zǐ-cōng 子聰, *hào* Cáng-chūn jū-shì 藏春居士 ("Lay Master of Storing Spring"), posthumous title Tài-shī Wén-zhèn-gōng 太師文貞公, native of Xíng-zhōu 邢州 (modern Xíng-tái, Hé-běi). Initially a young Jīn local official; on the Jīn dynasty's fall in 1234 he entered Buddhist monastic life and studied under the Línjǐ Chán master Hǎi-yún Yìn-jiǎn 海雲印簡, who in 1242 presented him to the future Kublai Khan (then *Shì-zǔ Tài-zǔ*) at his Mongol encampment. From that point until his death he was Kublai's principal Sinitic political and ritual advisor — the central architect of the Yuán dynastic institutionalization. He: designed the imperial city of Dà-dū 大都 (modern Běijīng); designed the summer capital Shàng-dū 上都; drafted the dynastic name "Yuán" 元 (from the *Yì-jīng* phrase 大哉乾元 *Dà zāi qián yuán* "Great is the primal heaven"); fixed the official ritual, hierarchy, and ceremonial codes; selected Sinitic officials for appointment; and authored the 1267 *Wàn-yán shū* 萬言書 memorial summarizing his program. Broadly learned in *Yì-xué*, astronomy, geography, calendrical science, and the *Sān-shì* (Tài-yī, Liù-rén, Dùn-jiǎ) numerological arts. His collected works survive in the reduced six-*juàn* *Cáng-chūn jí* [[KR4d0422]]; his major prose memorials including the *Wàn-yán shū* are lost. He is also credited with the *Yù-chǐ jīng* 玉尺經 (a treatise on geomancy / dynastic founding, of disputed authenticity). CBDB person 28934. *Yuán-shǐ* j. 157.
