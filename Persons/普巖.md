# Pǔ-yán 普巖

Southern-Sòng Yángqí-branch Línjì Chán master, styled Yùn'ān Pǔ-yán 運菴普巖 (also Dàochǎng Pǔ-yán 道場普巖, *zì* Shào-zhān 少瞻). Native of Qìngyuán fǔ 慶元府 (Sì-míng 四明), surname Dù 杜. Per DILA A009658 and the contemporary *Yùnān chánshī xíng-shí* 〈炎宋安吉州道場山護聖萬歲禪寺運菴禪師行實〉: Shàoxīng 26.bǐngzǐ (1156) – Bǎoqìng 2.8.4 (4 September 1226), *shì* 71; he sat cross-legged and passed away at Dàochǎng-shān in Húzhōu.

Studied first with Shígǔ Yí 石鼓夷 and Wúyòng Quán 無用全 of the senior generation, then received dharma-transmission from [[崇岳]] Sōngyuán Chóngyuè (1132–1202) — under whom he served as *cānxué* 參學 and is accordingly one of the six named recorders of Sōngyuán's *yǔlù* ([[KR6q0311]]). His own abbacies ran through Dàshèng 大聖 in Zhènjiāng, Tiānníng 天寧 in Zhēnzhōu, and finally Hùshèng Wànsuì chán-sì 護聖萬歲禪寺 on Dàochǎng-shān 道場山 in Húzhōu. His *yǔlù*, the *Yùn'ān Pǔ-yán chánshī yǔlù* 運菴普巖禪師語錄, survives.

Yùn'ān is the crucial link in the 松源派 Sōngyuán *pài* of the Yángqí Línjì line: his principal dharma-heirs include Shífān Yǎn 石帆衍 and — most consequentially — Xūtáng Zhìyú 虛堂智愚 (1185–1269), whose Japanese heir Nānpo Jōmyō 南浦紹明 (1235–1308) founded the Ōtōkan 応燈関 lineage that became the main current of late-medieval and modern Japanese Rinzai Zen.
