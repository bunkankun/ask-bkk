**Lóu Shú** 樓璹 (1090–1162), *zì* Shòuyù 壽玉, of Sìmíng 四明 (Níngbō 寧波, Zhèjiāng). Southern-Sòng civil official, best known to art-history and agricultural-history scholarship as the compiler of the *Gēngzhī tú* 耕織圖 (Pictures of Tilling and Weaving), the canonical illustrated handbook of Sòng-period rice agriculture and silk production, presented to Emperor Gāozōng 高宗 c. 1145. He served subsequently as *JīngHúnán lù zhuǎnyùn pànguān* 荊湖南路轉運判官 (Fiscal Vice-Commissioner of the JīngHúnán Circuit) with acting jurisdiction over Tánzhōu 潭州 (Chángshā). On the death of his predecessor Liú Fǎng 劉昉 in autumn 1150, Lóu completed Liú's unfinished forty-*juǎn* paediatric encyclopaedia *Yòuyòu xīnshū* 幼幼新書 ([[KR3ej010]]) within a month, combining the two unwritten final *juǎn* into one and prefixing a newly compiled *qiúzǐ* (conception) treatise as a new opening *juǎn*. He is one of the principal Southern-Sòng administrative-cultural patrons of the Chángshā region.
