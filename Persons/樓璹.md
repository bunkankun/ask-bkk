**Lóu Shú** 樓璹 (1090–1162), *zì* Shòu-yù 壽玉, of Sì-míng 四明 (Níng-bō 寧波, Zhèjiāng). Southern-Sòng civil official, best known to art-history and agricultural-history scholarship as the compiler of the *Gēng-zhī tú* 耕織圖 (Pictures of Tilling and Weaving), the canonical illustrated handbook of Sòng-period rice agriculture and silk production, presented to Emperor Gāo-zōng 高宗 c. 1145. He served subsequently as *Jīng-Hú-nán lù zhuǎn-yùn pàn-guān* 荊湖南路轉運判官 (Fiscal Vice-Commissioner of the Jīng-Hú-nán Circuit) with acting jurisdiction over Tán-zhōu 潭州 (Cháng-shā). On the death of his predecessor Liú Fǎng 劉昉 in autumn 1150, Lóu completed Liú's unfinished forty-*juǎn* paediatric encyclopaedia *Yòuyòu xīnshū* 幼幼新書 ([[KR3ej010]]) within a month, combining the two unwritten final *juǎn* into one and prefixing a newly compiled *qiú-zǐ* (conception) treatise as a new opening *juǎn*. He is one of the principal Southern-Sòng administrative-cultural patrons of the Cháng-shā region.
