Gang Hui-maeng / Jiāng Xī-mèng 姜希孟 (1424–1483), *zì* (Korean: *ja*) Jǐng-chún 景醇 (Gyeong-sun), *hào* Sī-shū-zhāi 私淑齋 (Sa-suk-jae), Yún-sōng jū-shì 雲松居士, Wàn-sōng-gāng 萬松岡. Mid-Joseon Korean scholar-official. *Jìnshì* of *Jǐng-tài* 4 / 1453 (Joseon Danjong year 1). Held a sequence of senior posts under Sejo, Yejong, and Seongjong, reaching *Zhī Zhōng-shū-shěng shì* 知中樞府事 (Chief of the Royal Secretariat) and *Yú-chéng* (Hong-mun-gwan provost). Younger brother of [[姜希顏]] (Gang Hui-an, 1417–1464), a renowned Joseon painter and poet.

One of the leading Joseon literati of the late 15th century, known for poetry, prose, painting (a tradition shared with his brother), agricultural and horticultural writing (his *Yǎng-huā xiǎo lù* 養花小錄 is an important early Korean horticulture text), and Confucian moral compositions. His collected works *Sa-suk-jae jip* 私淑齋集 (*Sī-shū-zhāi jí*) gather his prose and poetry; the *Xùn zǐ wǔ shuō* 訓子五說 ([[KR3j0195]]) is among the more famous compositions.

Posthumous title *Wén-liáng* 文良. Discussed in standard Korean reference works including the *Han'guk minjok munhwa daebaekgwa sajeon*.
