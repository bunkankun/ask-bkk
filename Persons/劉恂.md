Liú Xún 劉恂 (*fl.* 890s–early 10th century). Late-Táng / early Five-Dynasties official. The principal source for his career is the *Sǔn-pǔ* of the Sòng monk Zàn-níng 贊寧, which records that under Zhāo-zōng (r. 888–904) he was sent forth as *Sī-mǎ* of Guǎngzhōu 廣州, and that on completing his term — when Cháng'ān was disturbed by the late-Táng political collapse — he settled in Nán-hǎi 南海 (Guǎngzhōu) rather than return north. There he composed the *Lǐngbiǎo lùyì* 嶺表錄異 ([[KR2k0108]]). The Sìkù tíyào, noting the work's references to Zhāo-zōng by his posthumous title, infers that the text was completed only in the Five-Dynasties period (ca. 905–940). Otherwise undocumented.
