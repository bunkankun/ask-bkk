Péng Jì-qīng 彭際清 / Péng Shào-shēng 彭紹升 (1740–1796), styled *Èr-lín jū-shì* 二林居士 ("Two-Forests Lay Practitioner") and *Zhī-guī-zǐ* 知歸子 ("Master Knowing-Refuge"), the most important late-Qīng lay Buddhist intellectual. Native of *Suzhou*. Born to a literati family of the Péng 彭 lineage of *Cháng-zhōu* 長洲, he passed the *jìnshì* 進士 examination in 1765 but turned to Buddhist study and lay practice, eventually becoming the principal lay-Buddhist scholar of late-eighteenth-century China.

His major works include the *Jū shì zhuàn* 居士傳 (a 56-fascicle compilation of lay-Buddhist biographies), the *Niàn fó tǎn* 念佛壇 (a Pure Land devotional manual), the *Wú liáng shòu jīng qǐ xìn lùn* 無量壽經起信論 (a commentary on the *Sukhāvatī-vyūha*), the [[KR6e0148|*Huáyán niàn fó sānmèi lùn* 華嚴念佛三昧論]] (X1030, *Treatise on the Recitation-of-Buddhas Samādhi of the Huáyán*), and the [[KR6e0147|*Yī chéng jué yí lùn* 一乘決疑論]] (X1029, *Treatise on Resolving Doubts about the One-Vehicle*). His distinctive synthesis combined the Pure Land lay-devotional tradition with the Huáyán-school doctrinal framework, producing a late-Qīng Buddhist syncretism that became the foundation of the early Republican-period Chinese Buddhist revival.

He died in 1796 at age 57.
