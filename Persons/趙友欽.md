# Zhào Yǒuqīn 趙友欽

Style name *Zǐgōng* 子恭, sobriquet *Yuándūzǐ* 緣督子 — known to his contemporaries as Yuándū xiānshēng 緣督先生 (Master Yuándū). Sòng Lián's 宋濂 preface gives his personal name variously as *Jìng* 敬 or *Yǒuqīn* 友欽. Native of Pòyáng 鄱陽 (in modern Jiāngxī), tenth-generation descendant of Zhào Yuánzuǒ 趙元佐, Prince of Hàn of the Northern Sòng — and thus a member of the Sòng imperial clan in Yuán-period diaspora. Astronomer, mathematician, optical experimenter, and Daoist priest of the Quánzhēn 全真 school, in which he became a patriarch; in 1329 he ordained Chén Zhìxū 陳致虛 (the *Shàngyángzǐ* 上陽子) as his successor.

He withdrew from worldly life early, retired for ten years on the East Sea coast, and devoted himself to the *Zhōuyì* (compiling tens of thousands of words of commentary, since lost), to alchemy (*Jīndān zhènglǐ* 金丹正理, *Yuándūzǐ xiān-fó tóngyuán lùn* 緣督子仙佛同源論, *Sānjiào yīyuán* 三教一源 — all lost), to calendrical computation (*Tuībù lìchéng* 推步立成 — lost), and to the synthesis-and-experimental treatise *Géxiàng xīnshū* 革象新書 — the only work that survives. The *Géxiàng xīnshū* contains his celebrated camera-obscura ("pinhole image") experiment using two arrays of candles and a perforated board: he documented systematically that aperture size, source intensity, and screen distance govern image brightness independently of aperture shape — the earliest experimental optics in Chinese science. He also computed an improved value of *π* by the method of inscribed regular polygons (the same Liú-Huī-style method, but pushed to higher orders).

Two parallel transmissions of the *Géxiàng xīnshū* survive: the original 5-juan recension passed through his disciple Zhū Huī 朱暉 to Zhāng Rùn 章濬, who obtained a preface from Sòng Lián 宋濂 and printed it ([[KR3f0005]]); and Wáng Yī 王禕's 2-juan early-Míng abridgment ([[KR3f0004]]). Both were included in the Sìkù quánshū under the precedent of the *Xīn / Jiù Tángshū* (parallel admission of two recensions of one work).
