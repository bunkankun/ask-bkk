# Zōng-běn 宗本

Northern-Sòng Yún-mén-zōng 雲門宗 Chán master, *zì* Wú-zhé 無喆; imperial title Yuán-zhào chánshī 圓照禪師 (from Zhé-zōng in 1082); posthumous *shì-hào* Fǎ-kōng chánshī 法空禪師. 1020 – 15 February 1100 (lunar 元符 2.12.28), *shì-shòu* 80, *xià* 52. Native of Wú-xī 無錫 (Cháng-zhōu), lay surname Guǎn 管. Dharma-heir of **Tiān-yī Yì-huái** 天衣義懷 (993–1064). The first appointed abbot of the new imperial-court Chán centre **Huì-lín chán-yuàn** 慧林禪院 at the Dà-xiàng-guó-sì 大相國寺 in the Sòng capital, from Yuán-fēng 5 (1082) — installed at Shén-zōng's personal request, making him the most prominent Buddhist public figure of the Northern-Sòng capital in the late 11th century.

Entered religion at nineteen at Sū-zhōu Chéng-tiān Yǒng-ān-sì 承天永安寺 under Dào-shēng 道昇; served as *jīn-shì* 巾侍 for ten years before taking the precepts; a further three years of service, then wandering; attained awakening under Tiān-yī Yì-huái at Chí-zhōu. Opened public teaching at Ruì-guāng 瑞光; moved to Jìng-cí 淨慈; summoned in 1082 to Huì-lín as its inaugural abbot; retired to Sū-zhōu Líng-yán-shān 靈巖山 in his late years. Died seated without illness in 1100. "His dharma-transmitters and dharma-spreaders are uncountable" (DILA).

His main three-seat *yǔlù* (Ruì-guāng / Jìng-cí / Huì-lín) circulated widely in the Northern Sòng; a supplementary *bié-lù* 別錄 preserving previously-unprinted *shàng-táng* and Líng-yán *jì-sòng* material — including the extraordinary 1085 *shàng-táng* preached before the spirit-throne of the late Emperor Shén-zōng at the imperial Fú-níng-diàn 福寧殿 — was compiled by his dharma-heir [[慧辯]] Wú-jì Huì-biàn 無際慧辯 of Líng-yán Xiù-fēng-sì: surviving as [[KR6q0380]] (X73 n1450). Principal named dharma-heirs: Shàn-běn Dà-tōng 善本大通, Jīn-shān Shàn-níng 金山善寧, Shǒu-yī Fǎ-zhēn 守一法真, Xiū-yóng Zhēn-wù 修顒真悟, and several others.
