Wāng Zhuó 汪晫 (1162–1237), *zì* Chǔwēi 處微, posthumously *Kāng-fàn xiānsheng* 康範先生 (a *sī yì* 私諡 awarded by his disciples), was a Southern Sòng *Lǐxué* recluse-classicist of Jī-xī 績溪 (Xīn-ān 新安, Huī-zhōu — the same prefecture as Zhū Xī's Zhū-jiāng-jī family seat). A near-contemporary of Zhū Xī (Zhū's birth 1130, Wāng's 1162), he chose retirement and never held office, devoting himself to *jīng-xué* in the *Yī-Luò* (Northern Sòng *Lǐxué*) and *Kǎo-tíng* (Zhū Xī) traditions. His major works are the *Zēng-zǐ quán-shū* 曾子全書 (KR3a0053) and the *Zǐ-sī-zǐ quán-shū* 子思子全書 (KR3a0054), composed in the Qìng-yuán to Jiā-tài era (1195–1204) — at the height of the *Qìng-yuán dǎng-jìn* against the *Lǐxué* faction — gathering the dispersed sayings of Zēngzǐ and Zǐsī from the *Lǐjì*, *Dà Dài lǐjì*, and other classical sources. The works were posthumously presented to court by his grandson Wāng Mèng-dòu 汪夢斗 in Xián-chún 10 (1274), with the imperial sanction granting Wāng Zhuó the posthumous title *Tōng-zhí láng* 通直郎 and depositing the books in the Imperial Library. (Note: the Kanripo catalog meta gives the surname as 王晫 — a typographical slip 王 for 汪. The correct surname 汪 is preserved in the WYG source itself, in the SKQS *tíyào*, and in the *Sòng shǐ* and other contemporary sources.)
