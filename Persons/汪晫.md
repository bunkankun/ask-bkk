Wāng Zhuó 汪晫 (1162–1237), *zì* Chǔwēi 處微, posthumously *Kāngfàn xiānsheng* 康範先生 (a *sī yì* 私諡 awarded by his disciples), was a Southern Sòng *Lǐxué* recluse-classicist of Jīxī 績溪 (Xīnān 新安, Huīzhōu — the same prefecture as Zhū Xī's Zhūjiāngjī family seat). A near-contemporary of Zhū Xī (Zhū's birth 1130, Wāng's 1162), he chose retirement and never held office, devoting himself to *jīngxué* in the *YīLuò* (Northern Sòng *Lǐxué*) and *Kǎotíng* (Zhū Xī) traditions. His major works are the *Zēngzǐ quánshū* 曾子全書 ([[KR3a0053]]) and the *Zǐsīzǐ quánshū* 子思子全書 ([[KR3a0054]]), composed in the Qìngyuán to Jiātài era (1195–1204) — at the height of the *Qìngyuán dǎngjìn* against the *Lǐxué* faction — gathering the dispersed sayings of Zēngzǐ and Zǐsī from the *Lǐjì*, *Dà Dài lǐjì*, and other classical sources. The works were posthumously presented to court by his grandson Wāng Mèngdòu 汪夢斗 in Xiánchún 10 (1274), with the imperial sanction granting Wāng Zhuó the posthumous title *Tōngzhí láng* 通直郎 and depositing the books in the Imperial Library. (Note: the Kanripo catalog meta gives the surname as 王晫 — a typographical slip 王 for 汪. The correct surname 汪 is preserved in the WYG source itself, in the SKQS *tíyào*, and in the *Sòng shǐ* and other contemporary sources.)
