Twelfth-century Southern Sòng connoisseur of ancient bronzes and seal-impressions; *zì* Zǐ-biàn 子弁 (variant transmission: name as 球, *zì* Kuí-yù 夔玉 or Kuí-shí 夔石). Son of one Cháng-rú 長孺, a friend and *tóng-nián jìnshì* of Lǐ Bǐng 李邴 of Jì-zhōu 濟州 *Rèn-chéng* 任城 (present-day Shāndōng) — making Wáng Qiú a Shāndōng man. No precise lifedates are recoverable; activity bracketed by Lǐ Bǐng's career (*jìn-shì* 1121, active to mid-1140s). Author of [[KR3h0085]] *Xiào-táng jí-gǔ lù* 嘯堂集古錄 (2 *juàn*), one of the four foundational Sòng *jīn-shí xué* works recording Shāng-Hàn ritual bronzes with inscription tracings and modern-script glosses. CBDB lists a Míng-period Wáng Qiú (id 126618, 1445–1507) — a different person, not to be confused with the Sòng author. Lǐ Bǐng's preface to the *Xiào-táng* notes that Wáng Qiú was already known in his lifetime for his calligraphic expertise in ancient scripts (*jīng yú gǔ-zì*); collectors from all directions sought his hand on silk.
