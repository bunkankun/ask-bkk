Lǐ Xián-yòng 李咸用 (fl. *Xián-tōng*–*Qián-fú*, ~860s–870s; of *Lǒng-xī* by ancestral seat). The header inscription of his collection identifies him as a *tuī-guān* (Examining Inspector) — a regional staff position. He failed the *jìn-shì* repeatedly and held subordinate posts in regional offices; his career details are scant in standard sources.

Lǐ's verse — preserved in the SBCK *Pī-shā jí* — is conservative, image-careful late-Táng *yuè-fǔ*-style and quatrain work, with strong influence from **Bào Róng** [[鮑溶]] and from **Zhāng Wèi**'s school typology. The title *Pī-shā* ("sand-sifting") is one of the more self-deprecating Tang collection-titles.

Principal work in the corpus: *Táng Lǐ tuī-guān Pī-shā jí* [[KR4c0087]] in 6 *juǎn* (SBCK). CBDB id 92988 (and homonyms 439043, 448144) has no dates.
