# Máo Jìn 毛晉

The major late-Míng book-publisher and bibliophile. *Zì* Zǐjìn 子晉 (whence the common reference Máo Zǐjìn); *hào* Jígǔgé zhǔrén 汲古閣主人 ("Master of the Drawing-from-Antiquity Pavilion"), Yǐnhú 隱湖 ("Hidden-Lake"). Native of Chángshú 常熟 (Jiāngsū). Lifedates 1599–1659.

Founder and operator of the **Jígǔ gé** 汲古閣 publishing-house in Chángshú — arguably the single most important non-governmental Chinese publisher of the late Míng and early Qīng. Over his career, Máo Jìn published or reprinted more than **six hundred major titles** across the Chinese literary, historical, philosophical, and religious traditions, including substantial Buddhist-canonical and Buddhist-literary materials. His editorial standards, typographical quality, and commitment to preservation of rare works established Jígǔ gé editions as among the most highly-regarded book-printings of the late-imperial period.

Buddhist-publishing contributions include Chán master [[通門|Mùyún Tōngmén]]'s 牧雲通門 *Lǎnzhāi bié jí* 嬾齋別集 [[KR6q0212]] (Máo and Mùyún were Chángshú fellow-natives and exact contemporaries); and collaborations on numerous other Buddhist-literary projects of the Wànlì / Chóngzhēn / Shùnzhì decades.

Máo Jìn's lay Buddhist commitments are less visible in his career than his secular literary-publishing work, but his contribution to Chinese Buddhist publishing through the Jígǔ gé has been significant for the modern canonical tradition: many classical Buddhist literary works survive today specifically in Jígǔgé printings.

Standard biographical sources: *Míng shǐ* 明史; extensive modern scholarship on the Jígǔ gé.
