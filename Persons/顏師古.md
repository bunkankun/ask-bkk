Early-Táng exegete and philologist, given name Zhòu 籀, *zì* Shīgǔ 師古 (which became his customary name), of Wàn-nián 萬年 in Yōng-zhōu (greater Cháng-ān). Grandson of Yán Zhītuī 顏之推, author of the *Yán-shì jiā-xùn* 顏氏家訓, and a key figure of the family's Sui–Tang philological line; his nephew was the calligrapher Yán Zhēnqīng 顏真卿. Held offices including *Tōng-zhí láng*, *Mì-shū jiàn*, *Hóng-wén-guǎn xuéshì*, and was on the editorial board of the *Wǔ-jīng zhèngyì*. He produced the canonical commentary on the *Hàn-shū* (presented 645), drawing on twenty-three earlier commentators; the work fixed the standard reading of the *Hàn-shū* for a millennium. He further annotated the *Jí-jiù piān* [[KR1j0017]] and authored *Kuāng-miù zhèng-sú* [[KR1j0009]] (presented posthumously 651 by his son Yán Yáng-tíng), a foundational Táng work of philological criticism. As the Sìkù *tíyào* on the *Kuāng-miù zhèng-sú* observes, Yán's lack of historical awareness about pronunciation — he treats medieval and Hàn rhyme-categories as continuous — paradoxically opened the door to the later doctrine of *xié-yīn* 叶音 that the Qing scholar Gù Yánwǔ had to combat. CBDB lifedates 581–645, in agreement with the *Jiù-Táng-shū* biography (juàn 73).
