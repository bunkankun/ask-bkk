Early-Táng exegete and philologist, given name Zhòu 籀, *zì* Shīgǔ 師古 (which became his customary name), of Wànnián 萬年 in Yōngzhōu (greater Chángān). Grandson of Yán Zhītuī 顏之推, author of the *Yánshì jiāxùn* 顏氏家訓, and a key figure of the family's Sui–Tang philological line; his nephew was the calligrapher Yán Zhēnqīng 顏真卿. Held offices including *Tōngzhí láng*, *Mìshū jiàn*, *Hóngwénguǎn xuéshì*, and was on the editorial board of the *Wǔjīng zhèngyì*. He produced the canonical commentary on the *Hànshū* (presented 645), drawing on twenty-three earlier commentators; the work fixed the standard reading of the *Hànshū* for a millennium. He further annotated the *Jíjiù piān* [[KR1j0017]] and authored *Kuāngmiù zhèngsú* [[KR1j0009]] (presented posthumously 651 by his son Yán Yángtíng), a foundational Táng work of philological criticism. As the Sìkù *tíyào* on the *Kuāngmiù zhèngsú* observes, Yán's lack of historical awareness about pronunciation — he treats medieval and Hàn rhyme-categories as continuous — paradoxically opened the door to the later doctrine of *xiéyīn* 叶音 that the Qing scholar Gù Yánwǔ had to combat. CBDB lifedates 581–645, in agreement with the *Jiù Tángshū* biography (juàn 73).
