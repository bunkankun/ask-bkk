Liú Xuéjī 劉學箕 (fl. ca. 1190–1230), *zì* Xízhī 習之, *hào* *Zhǒngchūnzǐ* 種春子 / *Fāngshìxián jūshì* 方是閒居士, was a Southern Sòng *yǐnshì* (recluse-scholar) of Chóngān 崇安 (Fújiàn). He was the great-grandson of Liú Gé 劉韐 (the *Jìngkāng*-era martyr-official), the grandson of Liú Zǐhuī 劉子翬 (Zhū Xī's teacher and a figure in the *Mínxué* lineage), and the son of Liú Píng 劉㻫. He never held office, settling in his late forties at *Nánshān* below his ancestral hill, building a *Fāngshìxián* hall by a lotus pond. His extant collection, *Fāngshìxián jūshì xiǎogǎo* 方是閒居士小稿 [[KR4d0317]] in 2 *juǎn*, is prefaced by Liú Huái 劉淮 (溪翁), Zhào Fān 趙蕃 (Dōnglǐ), and Zhào Bìyuàn 趙必愿 (Kāifēng) — all dated to *Jiādìng* 10 (1217). His *cí* — particularly the *Jīnlǚqū* setting his *shùhuái* to Xīn Qìjí's 辛棄疾 rhyme — is regarded by the *Sìkù* editors as nearly indistinguishable from Xīn Qìjí's own. CBDB index year 1168.
