Liú Xué-jī 劉學箕 (fl. ca. 1190–1230), *zì* Xí-zhī 習之, *hào* *Zhǒng-chūn-zǐ* 種春子 / *Fāngshìxián jūshì* 方是閒居士, was a Southern Sòng *yǐn-shì* (recluse-scholar) of Chóng-ān 崇安 (Fújiàn). He was the great-grandson of Liú Gé 劉韐 (the *Jìng-kāng*-era martyr-official), the grandson of Liú Zǐ-huī 劉子翬 (Zhū Xī's teacher and a figure in the *Mín-xué* lineage), and the son of Liú Píng 劉㻫. He never held office, settling in his late forties at *Nán-shān* below his ancestral hill, building a *Fāngshìxián* hall by a lotus pond. His extant collection, *Fāngshìxián jūshì xiǎogǎo* 方是閒居士小稿 [[KR4d0317]] in 2 *juǎn*, is prefaced by Liú Huái 劉淮 (溪翁), Zhào Fān 趙蕃 (Dōng-lǐ), and Zhào Bì-yuàn 趙必愿 (Kāi-fēng) — all dated to *Jiā-dìng* 10 (1217). His *cí* — particularly the *Jīn-lǚ-qū* setting his *shù-huái* to Xīn Qì-jí's 辛棄疾 rhyme — is regarded by the *Sì-kù* editors as nearly indistinguishable from Xīn Qì-jí's own. CBDB index year 1168.
