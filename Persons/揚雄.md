**Yáng Xióng** 揚雄 (53 BCE – 18 CE), *zì* Zǐ yún 子雲, was one of the great Western-Hàn literary and philosophical figures — *fù*-poet, lexicographer (*Fāng yán* 方言), and author of two major classical imitations: the ***Tài xuán jīng*** 太玄經 ([[KR5f0017]]) modeled on the *Yì jīng* and the ***Fǎ yán*** 法言 modeled on the *Lún yǔ*. Served at the court of Wáng Mǎng 王莽 — a connection that damaged his posthumous reputation but did not erase his stature as an intellectual forebear of the 2nd-century *xuán xué* 玄學 tradition.
