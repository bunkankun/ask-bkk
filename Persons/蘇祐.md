Sū Yòu 蘇祐 (1492–1571; CBDB id 133576), courtesy name Yǔnjí 允吉, was a native of Línqīng 臨清, Shāndōng. He passed the *jìnshì* 進士 examination and had a distinguished official career under the Jiājìng 嘉靖 emperor, serving in censorate and surveillance positions. He is recorded in the *Míng rén zhuànjì zīliào suǒyǐn* 明人傳記資料索引 (page 943). His most notable posting relevant to his literary legacy was as Surveillance Commissioner (*xúnàn yùshǐ* 巡按御史) for Xuān-Dà (Xuānhuà 宣化 and Dàtóng 大同), where he was directly involved in the suppression of the 1553 military mutiny at Dàtóng — the events documented in his [[KR4k0083]] *Yúnzhōng Shìjì* 雲中事記. He is also known for literary and poetic works. Sū Yòu's firsthand account of the Dàtóng crisis provides a rare official participant-observer perspective on the chronic instability of the northern frontier during the Jiājìng period.
