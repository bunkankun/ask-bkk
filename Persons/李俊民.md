Lǐ Jùn-mín 李俊民 (1176–1260), *zì* Yòng-zhāng 用章, *hào* Hè-míng dào-rén 鶴鳴道人 ("Daoist Master Crane-Call"), posthumous *shì* Zhuāng-jìng xiān-shēng 莊靖先生 ("Master Solemnly Tranquil"), native of Zé-zhōu 澤州 (modern Jìn-chéng 晉城 in Shān-xī). Said by family tradition to be a descendant of the Táng prince Lǐ Yuán-jiā 李元嘉 (Hán-wáng 韓王). In youth he passed through the Chéng-shì 程氏 *Lǐ-xué* school. Took the *jīng-yì* (Classics-specialist) *jìnshì* in first place (i.e. as *zhuàng-yuán*) in Jīn Chéng-ān 5 (1200); served briefly as *Yìng-fèng hàn-lín wén-zì* and soon withdrew. After the Jīn Southern Displacement (1213) he retreated to Sōng-shān as a Daoist householder under the name Hè-míng dào-rén. Yuán Shì-zǔ summoned him by ceremonial-carriage; he pleaded to return to the mountains. The post-1234 (the Jīn dynasty's actual fall to the Mongols) poetry in his collection dates conspicuously only by *gān-zhī* cycle — silently comparing himself to Táo Qián. He died in 1260 and received the posthumous *shì* Zhuāng-jìng. His collected works *Zhuāng-jìng jí* [[KR4d0419]] (10 *juàn*, 7 *juàn* of poetry + 3 of prose) were first cut by the Zé-zhōu prefect Duàn Zhèng-xíng in the 1240s, with prefaces by Lǐ Zhòng-shēn (1219), Wáng Tè-shēng, Liú Yíng, and his disciple Shǐ Bǐng-zhí (1243).
