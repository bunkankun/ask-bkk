Lǐ Jùnmín 李俊民 (1176–1260), *zì* Yòngzhāng 用章, *hào* Hèmíng dàorén 鶴鳴道人 ("Daoist Master Crane-Call"), posthumous *shì* Zhuāngjìng xiānshēng 莊靖先生 ("Master Solemnly Tranquil"), native of Zézhōu 澤州 (modern Jìnchéng 晉城 in Shānxī). Said by family tradition to be a descendant of the Táng prince Lǐ Yuánjiā 李元嘉 (Hánwáng 韓王). In youth he passed through the Chéngshì 程氏 *Lǐxué* school. Took the *jīngyì* (Classics-specialist) *jìnshì* in first place (i.e. as *zhuàngyuán*) in Jīn Chéngān 5 (1200); served briefly as *Yìngfèng hànlín wénzì* and soon withdrew. After the Jīn Southern Displacement (1213) he retreated to Sōngshān as a Daoist householder under the name Hèmíng dàorén. Yuán Shìzǔ summoned him by ceremonial-carriage; he pleaded to return to the mountains. The post-1234 (the Jīn dynasty's actual fall to the Mongols) poetry in his collection dates conspicuously only by *gānzhī* cycle — silently comparing himself to Táo Qián. He died in 1260 and received the posthumous *shì* Zhuāngjìng. His collected works *Zhuāngjìng jí* [[KR4d0419]] (10 *juàn*, 7 *juàn* of poetry + 3 of prose) were first cut by the Zézhōu prefect Duàn Zhèngxíng in the 1240s, with prefaces by Lǐ Zhòngshēn (1219), Wáng Tèshēng, Liú Yíng, and his disciple Shǐ Bǐngzhí (1243).
