Bǎo-bā 保巴 (lifedates not securely recorded; fl. early Yuán, c. 1290–1310), *zì* Pǔ-ān 普菴. The name 保巴 is the Sìkù-corrected form; the old base writes 保八.

A *Sè-mù* 色目 (Yuán-period non-Hàn-Chinese ethnic-class designation; principally Central Asian and West Asian peoples — Mongolian, Uighur, Tangut, Persian, Arab, etc.) scholar resident at Luò-yáng 洛陽 (modern Luòyáng, Hé-nán). Career: held *Tài-zhōng dà-fū* 太中大夫 (Grand Master of the Imperial Treasury, an honorific title) and prior to that *Huáng-zhōu-lù zǒng-guǎn* 黃州路總管 (Magistrate of Huáng-zhōu Circuit) concurrent with *Guǎn-nèi quàn-nóng-shì* 管內勸農事 (Within-Circuit Agriculture-Promotion Office). Final office unrecorded.

Bǎo-bā is methodologically significant as one of the relatively few Yuán-period *Sè-mù* scholars to compose substantive Confucian-canonical works. The Mongol Yuán dynasty (1271–1368) classified its subject populations into four ranks: Mongols, *Sè-mù*, Hàn-rén (Northern Chinese), Nán-rén (former Southern-Sòng populations); Bǎo-bā's *Sè-mù* status meant he was in the second-highest social-political class. His *Yì*-program — substantively grounded in Sòng *yìlǐ* (Chéng Yí + the Zhū-school) — represents the Yuán-period *Sè-mù*-Confucian-scholarship integration that helped the post-Mongol cultural integration.

CBDB has no entry for him under either 保巴 or 保八.

Within the Kanripo corpus he is the author of [[KR1a0073]] *Yì yuán ào yì* 易源奧義 (1 juan) + *Zhōuyì yuán zhǐ* 周易原旨 (8 juan) — combined under the title *Yì tǐ yòng* 易體用 ("*Yì*-Substance-and-Function") and prepared as a *jìn-tài-zǐ jiān* 進太子箋 (memorial-to-the-heir-apparent presentation manuscript).

The original work was three components: *Yì yuán ào yì*, *Zhōuyì yuán zhǐ*, and *Zhōuyì shàng zhān* 周易尚占 (the third was lost; a Míng-period coin-divination manual was forged under Bǎo-bā's name and inserted into Chén Jìrú's *Huì mì jí*; the Sìkù editors detected and separated the forgery into *shù-shù lèi* / mantic-arts category, while preserving the genuine Bǎo-bā work in the *Yì lèi*).

His work was originally prefaced by Fāng Huí 方囘 and Móu Yàn 牟巘 (both late-Sòng / early-Yuán figures), placing Bǎo-bā firmly in the early Yuán c. 1290–1310. The *Sān Yì* triple-classification (*tiān-shèng-xīn* — Heavenly / Sagely / Heart) and 8-step *Yì yuán xīn fǎ* pedagogical framework give the work methodological distinctiveness within the Yuán *Yì*-tradition.
