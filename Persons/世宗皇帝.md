The **fifth emperor of the Qīng dynasty**: temple-name **Shì-zōng** 世宗, era-name **Yōng-zhèng** 雍正. Personal name **Aisin-Gioro Yìnzhēn** 愛新覺羅·胤禛 (sometimes appearing in Buddhist canonical contexts as **胤禎** through scribal variation). Born **13 December 1678**; died **8 October 1735**, having ruled from **1722** (after succeeding his father **Kāng-xī** 康熙).

His reign is foundational for the Qīng dynasty's mature institutional consolidation:

- **Bureaucratic centralization** through the establishment of the **Jūn-jī-chù** 軍機處 (Council of State) in 1730, the principal organ of late-imperial Chinese decision-making until 1911.
- **Fiscal reforms**, including the famous **tān-dīng-rù-mǔ** 攤丁入畝 reform (head-tax integration into land-tax) and the expansion of the **huì-piào** 會票 administrative-currency system.
- **Tibetan Buddhist patronage** consolidating the Manchu-Mongol-Tibetan religious-political settlement.

He was an unusually serious **personal Chán Buddhist practitioner and student** — among the most doctrinally engaged Chinese sovereigns of any dynasty. His Buddhist literary output is correspondingly substantial:

- **Yù-xuǎn yǔ-lù** 御選語錄 ([[KR6s0069]], X1319, 19 juan, 1733) — an imperially-edited selection of Chán *yǔ-lù*.
- **Yù-zhì jiǎn-mó biàn-yì lù** 御製揀魔辨異錄 ([[KR6s0068]], X1281, 8 juan) — polemical refutation of **Hàn-yuè Fǎ-zàng** 漢月法藏 (1573–1635) in defense of the orthodox **Mì-yún Yuán-wù** 密雲圓悟 ([[KR6q0604]]) line of Línjì-Yángqí Chán.
- **Yù-lù Zōng-jìng dà-gāng** 御錄宗鏡大綱 ([[KR6s0070]], L1668, 20 juan) — imperially-compiled abridgment of **Yǒng-míng Yán-shòu's** 永明延壽 (904–975) hundred-juan **Zōng-jìng-lù** 宗鏡錄 (T2016).

He also commissioned the imperially-edited recension of the Míng *Jiào-shèng fǎ-shù* — the **Chóng-dìng jiào-shèng fǎ-shù** 重訂教乘法數 ([[KR6s0009]], L1666) — completed in 1735 and bearing his August 1735 imperial preface.

His Buddhist programmatic positioning was systematic: privileging the **Mì-yún Yuán-wù line of Línjì-Yángqí Chán** as the orthodox Chán transmission, suppressing the **Hàn-yuè Fǎ-zàng** deviation, integrating Chán practice with **Pure Land** and **Esoteric** lineages under the unified imperial canopy, and eventually inaugurating the major canonical-printing project that became the **Qiánlóng Lóng-zàng** 龍藏 canon (begun 1735, completed 1738 under his successor Qiánlóng).

He died on the 23rd day of the 8th month of Yōng-zhèng 13 = **8 October 1735**, succeeded by his son the **Qiánlóng emperor** 乾隆帝 (Hong-li 弘曆, r. 1735–1796).
