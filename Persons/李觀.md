Lǐ Guān 李觀 (766–794), *zì* Yuánbīn 元賓, was a *Zhàozhōu Zànhuáng* 趙州贊皇 native, nephew of the early-Táng *gǔwén* pioneer **Lǐ Huá** 李華. He took the *jìnshì* in *Zhēnyuán* 8 (792) — the *Lónghǔ bǎng* cohort of Hán Yù [[韓愈]] and Ōuyáng Zhān [[歐陽詹]] — and the *bóxué hóngcí* in *Zhēnyuán* 9 (793). Appointed *Tàizǐ jiàoshūláng* (Editor at the Heir-Apparent's Library), he died at 29 in 794, mourned in epitaph by Hán Yù (the *Lǐ Yuánbīn mùmíng* in *Chānglí jí*).

Lǐ Guān's prose is among the densest and most ornamentally archaic of the early-*Yuánhé* *gǔwén* practitioners — Lù Xīshēng's [[陸希聲]] preface to his collection (composed 890) classifies him as having favored *cí* (diction) over *zhì* (substance) in the *gǔwén* program, in deliberate contrast with Hán Yù's reverse priorities. The position became a critical commonplace in late-Táng and Sòng prose theory.

Principal work in the corpus: *Lǐ Yuánbīn wénbiān* [[KR4c0057]] in 3 *juǎn* + *wàibiān* 2 *juǎn*; main collection edited 890 by Lù Xīshēng [[陸希聲]], *wàibiān* compiled by Zhào Áng of Shǔ. Catalog meta and CBDB (id 92925) agree on dates 766–794.
