Southern-Sòng *cí*-writer (fl. *Shào-xīng* through *Qián-dào* / *Chún-xī*, c. 1141–1180); *zì* Yàn-zhōu 彥周, *hào* Lǎn-kū jūshì 孏窟居士 ("Recluse of the Lazy-Den"), of Dōng-wǔ 東武 (modern Zhū-chéng 諸城, Shān-dōng). Nephew of Cháo Bǔ-zhī [[晁補之]] (matrilineal line: his mother was a Cháo) — preserving the *Yuán-yòu* literary heritage that the Cháo family carried into the Southern Sòng. Per Chén Zhèn-sūn [[陳振孫]]'s *Shūlù jiětí*, served under *Shào-xīng* as *Zhí xué-shì* Prefect of Jiàn-kāng; the *Sìkù tíyào* documents from his *cí* that he lived on through the *Qián-dào* and *Chún-xī* periods, with *cháng-chóu* exchanges with Zhāng Shì [[張栻]] (*zì* Jìng-fū) and Liú Gōng-fù 劉恭甫. His sole extant work is the *Lǎn-kū cí* [[KR4j0031]], in one *juǎn*, preserved in the Máo Jìn [[毛晉]] cutting and the *Quán Sòng cí* (around 91 *cí*). The collection contains an early self-conscious imitation of Lǐ Qīng-zhào's *Yì-ān tǐ* 易安體 (*Yǎn ér méi · Xiào Yì-ān tǐ*), one of the earliest *cí* to invoke Lǐ as a named stylistic category.
