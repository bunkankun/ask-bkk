Southern-Sòng *cí*-writer (fl. *Shàoxīng* through *Qiándào* / *Chúnxī*, c. 1141–1180); *zì* Yànzhōu 彥周, *hào* Lǎnkū jūshì 孏窟居士 ("Recluse of the Lazy-Den"), of Dōngwǔ 東武 (modern Zhūchéng 諸城, Shāndōng). Nephew of Cháo Bǔzhī [[晁補之]] (matrilineal line: his mother was a Cháo) — preserving the *Yuányòu* literary heritage that the Cháo family carried into the Southern Sòng. Per Chén Zhènsūn [[陳振孫]]'s *Shūlù jiětí*, served under *Shàoxīng* as *Zhí xuéshì* Prefect of Jiànkāng; the *Sìkù tíyào* documents from his *cí* that he lived on through the *Qiándào* and *Chúnxī* periods, with *chángchóu* exchanges with Zhāng Shì [[張栻]] (*zì* Jìngfū) and Liú Gōngfù 劉恭甫. His sole extant work is the *Lǎnkū cí* [[KR4j0031]], in one *juǎn*, preserved in the Máo Jìn [[毛晉]] cutting and the *Quán Sòng cí* (around 91 *cí*). The collection contains an early self-conscious imitation of Lǐ Qīngzhào's *Yìān tǐ* 易安體 (*Yǎn ér méi · Xiào Yìān tǐ*), one of the earliest *cí* to invoke Lǐ as a named stylistic category.
