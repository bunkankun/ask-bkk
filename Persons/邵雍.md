**Shào Yōng** 邵雍 (1011–1077), *zì* Yáo fū 堯夫, posthumously Kāng jié 康節, self-styled Ān lè xiān sheng 安樂先生 ("Master Ease-and-Joy"). The great Northern-Sòng **cosmological-numerological philosopher**, reckoned with Zhōu Dūnyí 周敦頤, Zhāng Zài 張載, and the Chéng brothers as one of the **Five Masters of Northern Sòng** *dào xué*. Principal works: ***Huáng jí jīng shì*** 皇極經世 ([[KR5d0063]]) — a monumental cosmological-historical system articulating the world in terms of numerological cycles — and the poetry collection ***Yī chuān jī rǎng jí*** 伊川擊壤集 ([[KR5d0065]]). Declining official service, Shào lived as a recluse-scholar in Luò yáng. His number-diagrammatic readings of the *Yì jīng* were foundational for later Neo-Confucian and Daoist alchemical systems.
