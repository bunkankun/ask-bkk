# Dàomín 道忞 (Mù-chén Dàomín 木陳道忞)

Pivotal early-Qīng Línjì-lineage Chán master and spiritual advisor to the Shùnzhì emperor. *Hào* Shān-wēng 山翁 ("Mountain Old-Man"), Mù-chén 木陳 ("Wood-Chén"), Mèng-yǐn dàorén 夢隱道人 ("Dream-Hidden Daoist"); *shì* Hóng-jué 弘覺 / 宏覺 ("Vast Awakening"). Posthumous imperial title *Hóng-jué chán-shī* 弘覺禪師. Lay surname Lín 林. Native of Cháo-yáng 潮陽 (Guǎng-dōng). Lifedates 1596–1674.

Dharma-heir of [[圓悟|Mì-yún Yuán-wù]] 密雲圓悟 (1566–1642); successor to Yuán-wù as abbot of **Tiān-tóng-sì** 天童寺 in Míng-zhōu 明州 (Nìng-bō, Zhè-jiāng). One of the two or three most important Yuán-wù-lineage Chán masters of his generation.

**Imperial connection.** Invited by the Shùnzhì 順治 emperor in Shùnzhì 16 (1659) to travel to Beijing and teach at the Wàn-shàn-diàn 萬善殿 within the Forbidden City; his sermons and conversations with Shùnzhì are preserved in the *Tiāntóng Hóng-jué Mín chánshī běi yóu jí* 天童弘覺忞禪師北遊集 [[KR6q0199]]. The imperial relationship made Dào-mín one of the most politically-prominent Buddhist monks of the early Qīng, alongside the imperially-recognised Línjì master Yù-lín Tōngxiù 玉林通琇.

Dharma-heirs included [[真樸|Zhēn-pǔ]] 真樸 and a substantial lineage of early-to-mid-Qīng Línjì masters. Collected sermons preserved as the *Mù-chén héshàng yǔlù* 木陳和尚語錄 (J26 B182). Also compiled the **《禪燈世譜》** *Chán-dēng shì-pǔ* ([[KR6r0110]], X1601, 9 juan), a tabular Línjì-Cáo-dòng lineage-genealogy with sub-lines, prefaced 1632 by his lay-disciple **Huáng Duān-bó** 黃端伯.

Sources: *Tiāntóng Hóng-jué Mín chánshī běi yóu jí* juan 1; *Mù-chén héshàng yǔlù*; Jiang Wu (2008) ch. 7.
