# Zhèng Qiáo 鄭樵

Style name *Yúzhòng* 漁仲, sobriquet *Master Jiājì* 夾漈先生 (after Mt. Jiājì 夾漈山 above his Pútián 莆田 family residence in Fújiàn). Born 1104, died 1162. Sòng *jīngshǐ* (classics-and-history) polymath whose vocation lay outside the regular *jìnshì* track: he refused to take the metropolitan examinations on principle and lived as a scholar-recluse on Mt. Jiājì for over thirty years, supporting himself by teaching and by his family's modest means. *Sòngshǐ* j. 436 gives him a substantial biography in the *Rúlín liè zhuàn*.

His vast project — eventually presented to court in 1149 — was the *Tōngzhì* 通志 ([[KR2d0006]]), a 200-*juàn* universal history modeled formally on SīMǎ Qiān's *Shǐjì* and on Liú Zhījī's *Shǐtōng* but ambitiously expanded to embrace the entire *zhèngjīng* corpus, the philological treatises, the bibliographical record, and the institutional history of the Chinese empire. He was finally received at court in 1149 and granted the post of *Bìshūshěng zhèngzì* 秘書省正字 (Corrector at the Secret Pavilion) — but only briefly, and the rest of his life was spent in editorial revision. The *Tōngzhì* was officially deposited in the imperial archives in 1162, the year of his death.

His specifically classical works include the *Shī biàn wàng* 詩辨妄 (a sharp critique of the *Máo* preface tradition, of which only fragments survive), the *Liù jīng ào lùn* 六經奧論 traditionally attributed to him in [[KR1g0011]] (but which the *Sìkù* compilers convincingly demonstrate to be a Sòng-end pseudonymous compilation, *not* by him — see the *tíyào* there), and a number of smaller philological and bibliographical works embedded in the *Tōngzhì* monographs (*Liù shū lüè* 六書略, *Qī yīn lüè* 七音略, *Yìwén lüè* 藝文略, etc.).

He is in scholarly posterity as much an institutional historian and a philologist as a classicist; his *Liù shū lüè* is the founding statement of Sòng systematic *xiǎo xué*, and his *Qī yīn lüè* the first thoroughgoing Chinese phonological treatise to integrate Sanskrit-derived phonological method.
