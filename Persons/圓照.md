# Yuánzhào 圓照

Southern-Sòng Chán *cānxué bǐqiū* 參學比丘 (student-disciple) of [[祖先]] Pòān Zǔxiān (1136–1211); principal named editor ("and others") of his master's *yǔlù* ([[KR6q0314]]), compiled c. 1212. Lifedates unrecorded. Whether the same person as the Yuánzhào attendant of Wúmíng Huìxìng (see above) cannot be confirmed.

# Yuánzhào 圓照 (Tang canonical bibliographer)

Late-Tang Buddhist canonical bibliographer of **Xī-míng-sì** 西明寺 in Cháng-ān. Lay surname not preserved; lifedates approximately ca. **720s–800s** (active mid-late 8th c.). Held the senior monastic-administrative position of **lín-tán shā-mén** 臨壇沙門 ("ordination-platform-master *śramaṇa*") at Xī-míng-sì.

He was the **principal post-Zhì-shēng canonical bibliographer** of the Tang, responsible for two complementary canonical-bibliographic works that together constituted the standard late-Tang canonical bibliographic apparatus:

- **DàTáng Zhēnyuán xù Kāiyuán shìjiào lù** 大唐貞元續開元釋教錄 ([[KR6s0096]], T2156, 3 juan, **794 CE**) — focused continuation supplement to Zhìshēng's *Kāiyuánlù* covering the 343-juan post-730 translation output.
- **Zhēnyuán xīndìng shìjiào mùlù** 貞元新定釋教目錄 ([[KR6s0097]], T2157, 30 juan, **800 CE**) — comprehensive canonical-bibliographic restatement combining the *Kāiyuánlù* with the post-730 supplement into a unified standard late-Tang catalog.

His canonical-bibliographic project documented and codified the **post-Zhì-shēng late-Tang Buddhist canonical expansion** — particularly the substantial Esoteric translation output of the late-8th century under **Bù-kōng** 不空 (705–774), **Prajñā** 般若 (734–810?), and other late-Tang translation-figures. The expanded canonical roster (~5,390 juan vs Zhì-shēng's 5,048-juan original) became one of the principal canonical-bibliographic foundations of the early-Sòng **Kāi-bǎo canon-printing project** (971–983).

Source: DILA Buddhist Person Authority A001417; auto-prefaces of *Xù Kāiyuán shìjiào lù* (T2156) and *Zhēnyuán xīndìng shìjiào mùlù* (T2157); Hé Méi 何梅, *Lìdài hànwén dàzàngjīng mùlù xīnkǎo* (2014).
