Yóu Jiǔyán 游九言 (1142–1206), originally named Yóu Jiǔsī 游九思, *zì* Chéngzhī 誠之, *hào* Mòzhāi 默齋 ("Silence Studio"), native of Jiànyáng 建陽 (modern northern Fújiàn). Began his career as *wèi* 尉 (police-keeper) of Gǔtián 古田, then as magistrate of Guānghuà 光化 county, and ended as *cānmóu guān* (staff secretary) at the Jīng-Húè Xuānwǔ 荊鄂宣武 military headquarters under Hán Tuōzhòu's 韓侂胄 northern campaign. In the Duānpíng era (1234–6) he was posthumously honoured with *zhí Lóngtú gé* 直龍圖閣 and the posthumous title Wénjìng 文靖. CBDB places his lifedates 1142–1206; the *Sìkù tíyào* notes that the *Sòng shǐ yìwén zhì* 宋史藝文志 does not register his collected works. His surviving *Mòzhāi yígǎo* 默齋遺稿 [[KR4d0329]] (2 juan) was preserved by the Bào 鮑 family of Zhèjiāng (Zhībùzúzhāi 知不足齋) and rounded out by the *Sìkù* compilers from extracts in Liú Dàbīn 劉大彬's *Máoshān zhì* 茅山志 and various Míng-Qīng poetry anthologies. His *Yì Língmiào yíngxiǎng sòngshén qū xù* 義靈廟迎享送神曲序 records, in unusual detail, the story of Téng Yīng 滕膺, a Tāizhōu *sīhù* who resisted the rebel Fāng Là 方臘 — an important supplement to the standard histories.
