Yóu Jiǔ-yán 游九言 (1142–1206), originally named Yóu Jiǔ-sī 游九思, *zì* Chéng-zhī 誠之, *hào* Mò-zhāi 默齋 ("Silence Studio"), native of Jiàn-yáng 建陽 (modern northern Fújiàn). Began his career as *wèi* 尉 (police-keeper) of Gǔ-tián 古田, then as magistrate of Guāng-huà 光化 county, and ended as *cān-móu guān* (staff secretary) at the Jīng-Hú-è Xuānwǔ 荊鄂宣武 military headquarters under Hán Tuō-zhòu's 韓侂胄 northern campaign. In the Duānpíng era (1234–6) he was posthumously honoured with *zhí Lóng-tú gé* 直龍圖閣 and the posthumous title Wénjìng 文靖. CBDB places his lifedates 1142–1206; the *Sìkù tíyào* notes that the *Sòng shǐ yì-wén zhì* 宋史藝文志 does not register his collected works. His surviving *Mò-zhāi yí-gǎo* 默齋遺稿 [[KR4d0329]] (2 juan) was preserved by the Bào 鮑 family of Zhèjiāng (Zhī-bù-zú-zhāi 知不足齋) and rounded out by the *Sìkù* compilers from extracts in Liú Dà-bīn 劉大彬's *Máo-shān zhì* 茅山志 and various Míng-Qīng poetry anthologies. His *Yì Líng-miào yíng-xiǎng sòng-shén qū xù* 義靈廟迎享送神曲序 records, in unusual detail, the story of Téng Yīng 滕膺, a Tāi-zhōu *sī-hù* who resisted the rebel Fāng Là 方臘 — an important supplement to the standard histories.
