**Liú Juānzǐ** 劉涓子 (*fl.* late Jìn / early LiúSòng, late 4th – early 5th c.), the legendary author of the foundational early-medieval Chinese surgical formulary [[KR3ek040]] ***Liú Juānzǐ guǐyí fāng*** 劉涓子鬼遺方. Native of Jīngkǒu 京口 (modern Zhènjiāng, Jiāngsū); described as a military physician active under *Jìn Āndì* 晉安帝 (r. 397–418) and in the northern campaigns of *Sòng Wǔdì* Liú Yù 劉裕 (r. 420–422). The standard founding-narrative *xùlùn* of the *guǐyí fāng* has Liú, hunting at twilight in Dānyáng 丹陽, shoot a supernatural giant ("*Huángfù guǐ* 黃父鬼"); the next morning he tracks blood to the ghost's lair, finds three figures preparing medicine, scatters them, and seizes a scroll titled *Yōngjū fāng* 癰疽方 — the "ghost-bequeathed prescriptions" of the work's title. The formulae were then transmitted within Liú's family to Dàoqìng 道慶, and from him to the Southern-Qí editor [[龔慶宣]] who produced the surviving 499 CE recension. The legendary character of the founding story does not alter the documentary value of the formulae themselves, which represent an authentic Chinese surgical-medical apparatus of the late 4th / early 5th century.
