**Liú Juānzǐ** 劉涓子 (*fl.* late Jìn / early Liú-Sòng, late 4th – early 5th c.), the legendary author of the foundational early-medieval Chinese surgical formulary [[KR3ek040]] ***Liú Juān-zǐ guǐ-yí fāng*** 劉涓子鬼遺方. Native of Jīng-kǒu 京口 (modern Zhèn-jiāng, Jiāngsū); described as a military physician active under *Jìn Ān-dì* 晉安帝 (r. 397–418) and in the northern campaigns of *Sòng Wǔ-dì* Liú Yù 劉裕 (r. 420–422). The standard founding-narrative *xù-lùn* of the *guǐ-yí fāng* has Liú, hunting at twilight in Dān-yáng 丹陽, shoot a supernatural giant ("*Huáng-fù guǐ* 黃父鬼"); the next morning he tracks blood to the ghost's lair, finds three figures preparing medicine, scatters them, and seizes a scroll titled *Yōng-jū fāng* 癰疽方 — the "ghost-bequeathed prescriptions" of the work's title. The formulae were then transmitted within Liú's family to Dào-qìng 道慶, and from him to the Southern-Qí editor [[龔慶宣]] who produced the surviving 499 CE recension. The legendary character of the founding story does not alter the documentary value of the formulae themselves, which represent an authentic Chinese surgical-medical apparatus of the late 4th / early 5th century.
