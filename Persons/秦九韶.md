# Qín Jiǔsháo 秦九韶

Style name *Dàogǔ* 道古. Native of *Lǔ jùn* 魯郡 (the ancestral seat per his own preface; he himself was born and raised in Sì-chuān, his father having moved there). Born around 1208; died 1261. The Sìkù 提要 of [[KR3f0041]] *Shù-shū jiǔ-zhāng* (the work whose title is later corrupted to *Shù-xué jiǔ-zhāng*) notes the discrepancy that Qín claims to be of *Lǔ jùn* in a 1247 preface but *Lǔ jùn* had long fallen to the Yuán by then — concluding that this is an ancestral-genealogical claim rather than a current-residence claim.

The greatest mathematician of the Southern Sòng. Qín Jiǔsháo's life was tumultuous: his career encompassed military service against the Mongols, official posts in Sì-chuān and the lower-Yangzi region, repeated periods of disgrace-and-exile. He was twice impeached for corruption and once accused of murder; he died in disgrace at Méi-zhōu 梅州 (Guǎng-dōng) in 1261. Despite these biographical complications, his mathematical work is universally recognized as the supreme achievement of Sòng-Yuán mathematics — and one of the great mathematical works of any pre-modern tradition.

His sole surviving work is the *Shù-shū jiǔ-zhāng* 數書九章 ([[KR3f0041]] *Shù-xué jiǔ-zhāng* in the Sìkù-recorded title — apparently corrupted), in 18 *juàn* containing 81 problems organized into 9 chapters by topical-administrative concern (parallel to the *Jiǔ-zhāng*'s 9-chapter structure but with completely different topical content):

(1) **Dà-yǎn 大衍** — the Chinese Remainder Theorem in fully-systematic form (*Dà-yǎn qiú-yī shù* 大衍求一術), generalizing the [[KR3f0033]] *Sūn-zǐ suàn-jīng*'s problem-26 procedure into a complete algorithm for any system of linear congruences with pairwise-coprime moduli — and the more general case where the moduli have common factors. This is one of the great algorithmic achievements of pre-modern mathematics.

(2) **Tiān-shí 天時** — astronomical-calendrical computation, including planetary motion.

(3) **Tián-yù 田域** — area calculations, including the *Hǎi-dǎo*-style remote-sensing problems.

(4) **Cè-wàng 測望** — depth-and-distance measurement.

(5) **Fù-yì 賦役** — taxation and labor service.

(6) **Qián-gǔ 錢榖** — currency and grain.

(7) **Yíng-jiàn 營建** — civil engineering.

(8) **Jūn-lǚ 軍旅** — military formations and supply.

(9) **Shì-yì 市易** — commerce.

The work is the foundational source for several major mathematical innovations:

(a) **Lì tiān-yuán-yī shù** 立天元一術 — the systematic algebraic-equation methodology, expressing polynomial equations symbolically using counting-rod arrangements. This is the foundation of Sòng-Yuán algebra and the precursor of all subsequent Chinese algebraic work.

(b) **Hé-yīn shù** — what amounts to the *Horner method* for evaluating polynomials at specific values; later named the *Hé-yīn shù* in the Sòng-Yuán literature, fully systematized by Zhū Shìjié in the 14th century.

(c) **Indeterminate-equation systematic methodology** — generalizing the [[KR3f0039]] *Zhāng Qiūjiàn suàn-jīng*'s *Hundred Fowls* problem into a full algorithm for determining the solvability of systems of indeterminate linear equations.

The 提要 of [[KR3f0041]] judges Qín Jiǔsháo as the lone Southern-Sòng figure who successfully revived the technical mathematical tradition: "*During the Sòng dynasty the various Confucians esteemed empty-discussion and despised practical-use; mathematics, although one of the Sage-Gate Six Arts, was likewise scorned and not spoken of... The work has both flaws-and-merits visible alongside, but the refined-and-correct passages are the majority*". Through his work, the *Sòng-Yuán mathematical tradition* — which would also produce Lǐ Yě 李冶 ([[KR3f0042]] / [[KR3f0043]]), Yáng Huī 楊輝 ([[KR3f0046]]), and Zhū Shìjié 朱世傑 — was foundational for the late-imperial Chinese mathematical inheritance.
