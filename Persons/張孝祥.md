# Zhāng Xiàoxiáng 張孝祥 (1132–1170)

*Zì* Ān-guó 安國; *hào* Yú-hú jū-shì 于湖居士 ("Sojourner of Yú-hú [Lake]") — after his late-life sinecure-residence at Wú-hú 蕪湖 on the Yangtze (modern Ān-huī). Native of Hé-zhōu Wū-jiāng 和州烏江 (modern Mǎ-ān-shān, Ān-huī). Lifedates 1132–1170, CBDB id 3144.

*Zhuàng-yuán* (placed first) of the *jìnshì* of *Shàoxīng* 24 (1154) at age 22 — in the same examination Qín Xī 秦熺 (Qín Huì's son) also sat; the imperial commissioner overrode Qín Huì's 秦檜 pressure and placed Zhāng Xiào-xiáng first, making the contest one of the most politically charged of the era. Zhāng entered office as the immediate target of Qín Huì's hostility; Qín's death (1155) preempted the persecution. Under Xiào-zōng he served as *Zhī Jiàn-kāng-fǔ* during the Lóng-xīng (1163–1164) campaign as the protégé of the war chief Zhāng Jùn 張浚. The patron Zhāng Jùn's catastrophic defeat at *Fú-lí* (1163) brought Zhāng Xiào-xiáng down with the war camp; he ended his career as *Zhī Jīng-zhōu* and on a sinecure-temple appointment at Wú-hú, where he died at 38.

His enduring fame rests on his *cí* (lyric) poetry of the *háo-fàng* (heroic-romantic) tradition. The *Liù-zhōu gē-tóu* "*Cháng-huái wàng dào*" — lament on the loss of the North after *Fú-lí* — and the *Niàn-nú jiāo* "*Guò Dòng-tíng*" — composed on his 1166 retirement journey from Guǎng-xī across Dòng-tíng Lake on a moonlit autumn night — are the canonical anthology pieces of Southern-Sòng heroic-romantic *cí*. Xiè Yáo-rén's preface to his collection (1201) established the Zhāng–Sū Shì comparison as the principal axis of Sòng-Yuán reception, with Zhāng cast as a *tiān-cái* (innate-genius) successor to Sū Dōng-pō.
