# Lìnghú Chǔ 令狐楚 (766–837)

*Zì* Gǔshì 穀士 (var. 殼士). Native of Yízhōu Huáyuán 宜州華原 (modern Yáozhōu 耀州 in Shǎnxī). One of the leading mid- to late-Táng *zhìgào* 制誥 stylists, prose-letter master, and political patron.

*Jìnshì* of Zhēnyuán 7 (791); entered the staff of Wáng Gǒng 王拱, *Guìguǎn guānchá shǐ*, and later served as *Tàiyuán jiédùshǐ pànguān* before being summoned to court as *yòu shíyí* 右拾遺. He rose under Xiànzōng, becoming *Hànlín xuéshì* and *Zhōngshū shěrén*. Implicated in the dispute over Péi Dù 裴度's edict in *Yuánhé* 12 (817) he lost the *xuéshì* title but kept the *shěrén* post. Under Mùzōng and Wénzōng he reached *Lìbù shàngshū* and *Jiǎnjiào shàngshū zuǒ púyè*; he was finally appointed *Shānnán xīdào jiédùshǐ* and died in office in *Kāichéng* 2 (837), aged 72.

Lǐ Shāngyǐn [[李商隱]] received his apprenticeship in *zhāngzòu* 章奏 (memorial prose) from Línghú Chǔ, a debt acknowledged in both the *Jiù Tángshū* and *Xīn Tángshū* biographies. As a poet Lìnghú was less distinguished than as a prose stylist, but his anthology, the *Yùlǎn shī* 御覽詩 [[KR4h0011]], compiled at imperial command before 817, preserves 289 mid-Táng *jìntǐ* poems by 30 hands and is one of the principal early-ninth-century anthologies.

The CBDB lifedates 766–837 are followed here; the catalog meta gives 765–836 (a one-year shift reflecting a different reading of the same biographical evidence).
