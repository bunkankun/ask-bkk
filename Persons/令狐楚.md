# Lìnghú Chǔ 令狐楚 (766–837)

*Zì* Gǔshì 穀士 (var. 殼士). Native of Yízhōu Huáyuán 宜州華原 (modern Yáozhōu 耀州 in Shǎnxī). One of the leading mid- to late-Táng *zhì-gào* 制誥 stylists, prose-letter master, and political patron.

*Jìnshì* of Zhēn-yuán 7 (791); entered the staff of Wáng Gǒng 王拱, *Guì-guǎn guān-chá shǐ*, and later served as *Tàiyuán jié-dù-shǐ pàn-guān* before being summoned to court as *yòu shí-yí* 右拾遺. He rose under Xiànzōng, becoming *Hàn-lín xué-shì* and *Zhōng-shū shě-rén*. Implicated in the dispute over Péi Dù 裴度's edict in *Yuán-hé* 12 (817) he lost the *xué-shì* title but kept the *shě-rén* post. Under Mùzōng and Wénzōng he reached *Lì-bù shàng-shū* and *Jiǎn-jiào shàng-shū zuǒ pú-yè*; he was finally appointed *Shān-nán xī-dào jié-dù-shǐ* and died in office in *Kāi-chéng* 2 (837), aged 72.

Lǐ Shāngyǐn [[李商隱]] received his apprenticeship in *zhāng-zòu* 章奏 (memorial prose) from Líng-hú Chǔ, a debt acknowledged in both the *Jiù Táng-shū* and *Xīn Táng-shū* biographies. As a poet Lìng-hú was less distinguished than as a prose stylist, but his anthology, the *Yùlǎn shī* 御覽詩 [[KR4h0011]], compiled at imperial command before 817, preserves 289 mid-Táng *jìn-tǐ* poems by 30 hands and is one of the principal early-ninth-century anthologies.

The CBDB lifedates 766–837 are followed here; the catalog meta gives 765–836 (a one-year shift reflecting a different reading of the same biographical evidence).
