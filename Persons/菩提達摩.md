Semi-legendary South Indian monk traditionally counted as the twenty-eighth patriarch of Indian Chán and the first patriarch of Chinese Chán. Active in southern China and the Northern Wèi capital Luòyáng during the late fifth and early sixth centuries; biographical material in Yáng Xuànzhī's 楊衒之 *Luòyáng qiélán jì* 洛陽伽藍記 (mid-sixth-c.) places him at the Yǒngníng Sì 永寧寺. Later Chán hagiography (Daoxuan's 道宣 *Xù gāo sēng zhuàn* 續高僧傳, eighth-century *Lèngqié shī zī jì* 楞伽師資記, and the *Bǎolín zhuàn* 寶林傳) elaborated him into the originator of "wall-gazing" (*biguān* 壁觀) Chán and ascribed to him the *Èr rù sì xíng lùn* 二入四行論 (the only text most scholars accept as containing genuine teachings) plus a large body of Dūnhuáng apocrypha including the present *Tiānzhú-guó Pútídámó chánshī lùn* ([[KR6v0002]], [[KR6v0024]], [[KR6v0025]]) and *Xī zhēng lùn* ([[KR6v0004]]).
