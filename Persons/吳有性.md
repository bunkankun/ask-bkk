**Wú Yǒuxìng** 吳有性 (*zì* Yòukě 又可, 1582–1652, 明), late-Míng physician of Zhèn-zé 震澤 (modern Wú-jiāng 吳江, Jiāngsū). The founder of the Chinese ***Wēn-yì*** 瘟疫 (warm-epidemic) school of medicine. Author of the foundational ***Wēn-yì lùn*** 瘟疫論 ([[KR3e0087]], 2 juan + 1 juan supplement, completed Chóngzhēn rén-wǔ = 1642). Wú composed the work in response to the great Chóng-zhēn xīn-sì (1641) epidemic that swept through Northern and Southern Zhí-lì, Shān-dōng, and Zhè-jiāng simultaneously, killing many — when conventional *Shānghán* (cold-damage) treatment proved ineffective. His doctrinal innovation: that *wēn-yì* is fundamentally different from *Shānghán* — entering through the mouth and nose rather than through the surface of the body, lodging in the *Mó-yuán* 膜原 (membranous-source) intermediate space (neither external nor internal), and following 9 distinctive transmission patterns. The work is the foundation of the Qīng-period **Wēn-bìng school** (Yè Tiānshì 葉天士, Xuē Xuě 薛雪, Wú Jūtōng 吳鞠通, Wáng Mèngyīng 王孟英), which became the principal Qīng-period therapeutic framework for epidemic febrile diseases — a lineage extending to the modern TCM treatment of viral and bacterial infections including SARS (2003) and COVID-19 (2020).
