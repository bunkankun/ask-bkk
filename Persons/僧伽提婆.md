Sēngqié-típó 僧伽提婆 (\*Saṃghadeva, "Saṃgha-deity"; full name in Chinese 瞿曇僧伽提婆 = Gautama Saṃghadeva; lifedates unknown, fl. 380s–400 CE) was a Kashmirian (罽賓) Buddhist master active in the Eastern-Jìn translation effort at Cháng'ān, Lúshān 廬山, and the southern capital Jiànkāng 建康. According to the *Chū sānzàng jì jí* 出三藏記集 (T2145, juan 13) and the *Gāosēng zhuàn* 高僧傳 (T2059, 328c–329b), he arrived at Cháng'ān during the Fú-Qín 苻秦 period and was associated with the translation circle around Dàoān 道安 (312–385); after Dàoān's death and the political collapse of Fú-Qín, he travelled south to Lúshān at the invitation of Huìyuǎn 慧遠, where he produced his most important translations including [[KR6a0158|*Sānfǎ-dù lùn* 三法度論 (T1506)]] in 391 CE, retranslating the Tridharmaka-śāstra that had earlier been issued under Dàoān at Yè-sì as T1505. He subsequently moved to the Eastern-Jìn capital Jiànkāng, where he worked under court patronage in the late 390s and produced the standard Chinese version of the *Madhyama-āgama* — the [[KR6a0008|*Zhōng āhán jīng* 中阿含經 (T26)]] — and a partial translation of the *Ekottara-āgama*.

Saṃghadeva is among the most consequential of the second-generation Chinese Buddhist translators: where Ān Shìgāo 安世高 (fl. 148–170) had pioneered Hàn-period Buddhist translation in a stylistically archaic register, and Dàoān 道安 (312–385) had laid the editorial-philological groundwork through his "five losses, three difficulties" (五失本三不易), Saṃghadeva's Lúshān–Jiànkāng output, in close collaboration with Huìyuǎn, set the linguistic register of late-fourth-century Chinese Buddhist Abhidharma translation. He is also credited with the *Apítán bā jiàndù lùn* 阿毘曇八犍度論 (T1543), the *Apítán xīn lùn* 阿毘曇心論 (T1550), and other Abhidharma works.

Works in the Kanripo corpus: [[KR6a0008]] *Zhōng āhán jīng* (T26, *Madhyama-āgama*); [[KR6a0158]] *Sānfǎdù lùn* (T1506); and others. Per DILA Buddhist Person Authority A001237.
