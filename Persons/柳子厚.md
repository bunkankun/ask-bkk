# Liǔ Zǐ-hòu 柳子厚 / Liǔ Zōng-yuán 柳宗元 (773–819)

The major mid-Táng poet, essayist, and political reformer; one of the **Eight Masters of Táng and Sòng Prose** (唐宋八大家) and one of the principal figures of the *gǔ-wén* 古文 ("ancient prose") movement that revived classical literary Chinese against the high Táng *biàn-wén* 駢文 (parallel-prose) style. *Zì* **Zǐ-hòu** 子厚; native of Hé-dōng 河東 (modern Yùn-chéng 運城, Shānxī), whence his alternate epithet **Liǔ Hé-dōng** 柳河東; later exiled to and died at **Liǔ-zhōu** 柳州 (modern Guǎngxī), whence the further alternate epithet **Liǔ Liǔ-zhōu** 柳柳州.

He passed the *jìnshì* exam in 793 (Zhēn-yuán 9) at age 21 and rose rapidly in central government service, becoming closely associated with the reformist circle around Wáng Shū-wén 王叔文 during the brief Yǒngzhēn 永貞 reign (805). After the failure of the Yǒngzhēn reforms he was exiled to the southern provinces (first Yǒng-zhōu 永州 in modern Húnán, where he spent a decade; then Liǔ-zhōu in Guǎngxī) and died in exile at Liǔ-zhōu in 819, age 47. The Yǒng-zhōu years produced his most celebrated literary works — the *Yǒng-zhōu bā-jì* 永州八記 (Eight Records of Yǒng-zhōu) and the *Bǔ shé-zhě shuō* 捕蛇者說 (the famous essay on the snake-catcher) — and his deepest engagement with Buddhism.

The brief allegorical essay **《東海若》** *Dōng-hǎi ruò* — preserved with the Qīng-period commentary of [[實賢]] Xǐng'ān as the *Dōng-hǎi ruò jiě* [[KR6p0097]] — is one of his Buddhist-philosophical compositions, a parable on the nature of the dharma drawn from the *Zhuāng-zǐ* 莊子 *Qiū-shuǐ* 秋水 chapter (the dialogue between the river-god of the Yellow River and Ruò 若 of the Eastern Sea). His Buddhist commitments — particularly his interest in Chán and Pure Land — are unusually explicit for a major Confucian-trained literatus of the mid Táng and made him an attractive figure for the late-imperial Confucian-Buddhist syncretic tradition.
