Guī Yǒuguāng 歸有光 (1506–1571), *zì* Xīfǔ 熙甫, *hào* Zhènchuān 震川, of Kūnshān 崑山 (Sūzhōu, Jiāngsū), was one of the major prose stylists of the late Míng — leader of the *Táng-Sòng pài* 唐宋派 movement that revived Táng-Sòng *gǔwén* against the Archaist Front. He was a *jìnshì* of Jiājìng 44 (1565) — exceptionally late in his career, after eight prior failures in the metropolitan examination — and rose to *Tàipúsì chéng* (Vice-Chamberlain of the Imperial Stud). He resided most of his life at Āntíng 安亭, on the upper Sōngjiāng, where his direct topographical knowledge of the lower Tàihú-basin drainage informed the *Sān-Wú shuǐlì lù* ([[KR2k0067]]) — the principal late-Míng theoretical statement of the downstream-outlet school of Tàihú hydraulic policy. His prose collection *Zhènchuān xiānsheng jí* 震川先生集 (Sìkù: KR4) is the principal monument of his literary work; he was also a major commentator on the Confucian Classics, and on the *Shǐjì* and *Hànshū*.
