Northern Sòng 北宋 statesman, poet, and prose master — younger brother of **Sū Shì** 蘇軾 (1037–1101) and one of the **"Three Sūs"** (*Sān Sū* 三蘇) alongside his father Sū Xún 蘇洵 (1009–1066) and his elder brother. A member of the canonical **Táng-Sòng bā dà jiā** 唐宋八大家 ("Eight Great Writers of Táng and Sòng"). Author of the substantive Daoist commentary [[KR5c0074|*Dàodé zhēn jīng zhù*]] 道德真經註 (DZ 691, 1100–1108) on the *Dàodé jīng*, as well as of commentaries on the *Yì jīng* 易經 and the *Chūnqiū* 春秋, and of a major literary oeuvre preserved in the *Luán chéng jí* 欒城集.

**Lifedates and origins.** 1039–1112 (CBDB 1493). *Zì* **Zǐyóu** 子由; *hào* **Luán chéng** 欒城 (and later, **Yǐng bīn yí lǎo** 穎濱遺老, "Old-Man Remnant of Yǐng bīn"). Posthumously canonised as **Wén dìng** 文定. Native of **Méi zhōu** 眉州 Méi shān 眉山 (modern Méi shān, Sìchuān 四川).

**Career.** Jìnshì 1057 (the same examination that distinguished his brother Sū Shì and that was presided over by Ōuyáng Xiū 歐陽修 — one of the most famous examinations in Chinese history). Held a series of central and provincial positions, including:

- **Shì dú** 侍讀 (Imperial Reader-in-Waiting) under Shénzōng 神宗.
- **Zhì gào wài shè wǔ lù** 直告外察武錄 (censorial positions).
- **Hàn lín xué shì** 翰林學士 and **Shàng shū zuǒ chéng** 尚書左丞 (Left Vice-Councillor) under Zhézōng 哲宗 in 1086–1094 — the height of his political prominence during the Yuán yòu 元祐 conservative restoration under Dowager Empress Gāo 高太后.
- **Forced retirement** in 1094 with the return of the reformist faction under Zhāng Dūn 章惇; exiled to various southern locations through the late 1090s.
- **Final retirement** at Yǐng bīn 穎濱 (near Xū chāng 許昌, Hénán), where he wrote under the name **Yǐng bīn yí lǎo** for the last decade of his life.

**Political alignment.** Conservative, in alliance with his brother Sū Shì and with Sīmǎ Guāng 司馬光 ([[司馬光]]) against Wáng Ānshí's 王安石 *xīn fǎ* 新法 reforms. Sū Zhé — and especially Sū Shì — were among the most prominent anti-reform voices of their generation, and both brothers were repeatedly exiled during the factional cycles of the Xī níng 熙寧, Yuán yòu 元祐, and Shào shèng 紹聖 eras. Sū Zhé's closest philosophical peers at the Yuán yòu court were Sīmǎ Guāng and **Fàn Zǔ yǔ** 范祖禹.

**Literary achievement.** One of the great Sòng prose stylists. His *Luán chéng jí* 欒城集 collects essays, memorials, inscriptions, and poems. The *Táng-Sòng bā dà jiā* 唐宋八大家 ("Eight Great Writers of the Táng and Sòng") includes Sū Zhé together with Hán Yù 韓愈, Liǔ Zōngyuán 柳宗元, Ōuyáng Xiū 歐陽修, Wáng Ānshí 王安石, Zēng Gǒng 曾鞏, his father Sū Xún 蘇洵, and his brother Sū Shì 蘇軾.

**Philosophical works.**

1. **[[KR5c0074|*Dàodé zhēn jīng zhù*]]** 道德真經註 (DZ 691, 4 juan, composed 1100–1108) — the *Dàodé jīng* commentary. Also preserved independently in WYG as *Lǎozǐ jiě* 老子解. Orientation: Buddhist-influenced, *fù xìng* 復性-centred, Three Teachings synthesis.
2. **Lǎozǐ jiě hòu xù** 老子解後序 (1108) — postface to the above, preserved in fuller form in non-DZ editions.
3. **Yì zhuàn** 易傳 — a commentary on the *Yì jīng*, reflecting the Sū brothers' shared engagement with the *Yì*; the *Méng* hexagram of [[KR1a0015]] *Dōngpō Yìzhuàn* preserves Sū Zhé's wording verbatim in the *Sān-Sū* family co-composition project.
4. **Chūn qiū jí jiě** 春秋集解 — a Confucian classics commentary.
5. **Lóng chuān lüè zhì** 龍川略志 and **Lóng chuān bié zhì** 龍川別志 — memoir-essays on Northern-Sòng court history, a valuable source for the 1080s–1090s political history.

**Intellectual orientation.** Sū Zhé was — with his brother Sū Shì — characteristic of the late-Northern-Sòng "Shǔ xué" 蜀學 (Sìchuān Learning) tradition: literary-aesthetic in emphasis, open to Buddhist and Daoist thought as complements to Confucian orthodoxy, skeptical of the more dogmatic Neo-Confucian positions of Zhū Xī's contemporaries. The *Dàodé jīng* commentary is the most sustained expression of this syncretic intellectual orientation.
