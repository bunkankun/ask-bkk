**Xiāo Yúncóng** 蕭雲從 (1596–1673; CBDB gives 1591–1668, but the standard art-historical literature dates him 1596–1673 — the discrepancy is noted here, and the more widely-followed 1596–1673 dates are adopted in the frontmatter), *zì* Chǐmù 尺木, *hào* Mòsī 默思 / Wúmèn dàorén 無悶道人 / Zhōngshān lǎorén 鍾山老人, of Wúhú 蕪湖 in Tàipíngfǔ 太平府 (modern Wúhú, Ānhuī).

Late-Míng *jǔrén* who refused to serve the Qīng after the conquest and lived as an *yímín* 遺民 painter, calligrapher, and book illustrator. Founder of the **Gūshú** 姑孰 school of landscape painting, named for Tàipíngfǔ's classical name. His landscape work — bony, angular, and indebted to Yuán-dynasty masters and to Huángshān 黃山 imagery — made him a major influence on the early Qīng *wénrén* 文人 painting tradition; his contemporaries Méi Qīng 梅清 and Shítāo 石濤 acknowledged the line.

His most famous publication is the **《離騷圖》** *Lísāo tú* (1645) — a printed pictorial *Chǔcí* with engraved illustrations of every section, executed at the height of his *yímín* engagement and read both as a *Chǔcí* picturebook and as a coded lament for the fall of Míng. The book reached the Qīng court by the Qiánlóng era and became the basis for the imperially-sponsored expansion **《欽定補繪蕭雲從離騷全圖》** ([[KR4a0006]]), in which the missing illustrations were filled out by court artists.
