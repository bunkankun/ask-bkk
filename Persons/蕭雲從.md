**Xiāo Yúncóng** 蕭雲從 (1596–1673; CBDB gives 1591–1668, but the standard art-historical literature dates him 1596–1673 — the discrepancy is noted here, and the more widely-followed 1596–1673 dates are adopted in the frontmatter), *zì* Chǐ-mù 尺木, *hào* Mò-sī 默思 / Wú-mèn dào-rén 無悶道人 / Zhōng-shān lǎo-rén 鍾山老人, of Wúhú 蕪湖 in Tài-píng-fǔ 太平府 (modern Wúhú, Ānhuī).

Late-Míng *jǔ-rén* who refused to serve the Qīng after the conquest and lived as an *yí-mín* 遺民 painter, calligrapher, and book illustrator. Founder of the **Gū-shú** 姑孰 school of landscape painting, named for Tài-píng-fǔ's classical name. His landscape work — bony, angular, and indebted to Yuán-dynasty masters and to Huáng-shān 黃山 imagery — made him a major influence on the early Qīng *wén-rén* 文人 painting tradition; his contemporaries Méi Qīng 梅清 and Shí-tāo 石濤 acknowledged the line.

His most famous publication is the **《離騷圖》** *Lí-sāo tú* (1645) — a printed pictorial *Chǔ-cí* with engraved illustrations of every section, executed at the height of his *yí-mín* engagement and read both as a *Chǔ-cí* picturebook and as a coded lament for the fall of Míng. The book reached the Qīng court by the Qián-lóng era and became the basis for the imperially-sponsored expansion **《欽定補繪蕭雲從離騷全圖》** ([[KR4a0006]]), in which the missing illustrations were filled out by court artists.
