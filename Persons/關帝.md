**Guān dì** 關帝 — historical name **Guān Yǔ** 關羽 (160–220 CE), *zì* **Yún-cháng** 雲長, the Shǔ-Hàn general of Liú Bèi 劉備 famous from the *San guó zhì* 三國志 and the *San guó yǎn yì* 三國演義. From the late Sòng a popular cult-deity, progressively elevated through the Yuán, Míng, and Qīng to the rank of *Guān shèng dì-jūn* 關聖帝君 ("Holy Imperial Lord Guān"), and a tutelary divinity of warriors, merchants, oath-fellowships, and (most relevantly here) of late-imperial Daoist *fú-jī* 扶乩 cults. From the Míng-Qīng his cult is one of the principal "demon-subduing" (伏魔) divinities, with the Wàn-lì-era title 三界伏魔大帝神威遠震天尊關聖帝君, granted in 1614, fixing his Daoist-canonical name. He is the attributed planchette-revealer of an entire late-imperial corpus of moral-scripture, including [[KR5i0022]] *Yù-qīng zàn-huà jiǔ-tiān yǎn-zhèng xīn-yìn bǎo-chàn* (preface), [[KR5i0088]] *Guān dì zhōng-xiào zhōng-yì jīng*, and [[KR5i0089]] *Guān shèng dì-jūn běn zhuàn*. Standard reference: ter Haar, *Guan Yu: The Religious Afterlife of a Failed Hero* (Oxford UP 2017).
