Lù Shàn-jì 鹿善繼 (1575–1636), *zì* **Bó-shùn** 伯順, *hào* **Qián-yuè** 乾岳, posthumous title **Zhōng-jié** 忠節; native of **Dìng-xīng** 定興 (Bǎo-dìng prefecture, Hé-běi). *Jìnshì* of Wàn-lì 41 / *guǐ-chǒu* (1613); rose through the Ministry of Revenue to *Bīng-bù láng-zhōng* and *Tài-cháng-sì shào-qīng*. A *Wáng Yáng-míng-xué* exponent in the Northern Hé-běi tradition (the *Běi-fāng wáng-xué* of Sūn Qí-fēng 孫奇逢, with whom Lù was a close associate) and an outspoken member of the *Dōng-lín* circle in its post-1626 phase. During the *Tiān-qǐ* persecution of 1625, when the *Dōng-lín liù jūn-zǐ* including [[魏大中]] were arrested, [[魏學洢]] hid at Lù's Dìng-xīng residence under disguise while attempting to ransom his father — an episode preserved both in the *Máo-yán jí* ([[KR4e0243]]) and in Lù's own *Wú-zhī táng yí-shū* 無知堂遺書. He retired in *Tiān-qǐ* 5; recalled in *Chóng-zhēn* 9 / 1636 in the emergency of the Manchu invasion, he died defending Dìng-xīng against the Qīng cavalry on the eleventh month, twenty-eighth day of that year.
