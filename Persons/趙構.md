Founding emperor of the Southern Sòng (r. 1127–1162) and the dynasty's most accomplished imperial calligrapher. Ninth son of Huīzōng 徽宗; sole male of the imperial line to escape the Jin capture of Kāifēng in 1126–27; established the *Lín'ān* 臨安 court and reigned for thirty-six years before abdicating in favour of his adopted heir Xiàozōng 孝宗. After abdication he lived at the *Déshòu* 德壽 palace until 1187. Personally trained in calligraphy from boyhood, he first followed Huáng Tíngjiān, then Mǐ Fú, finally settling on the canonical Wèi-Jìn style of the two Wángs (Wáng Xīzhī, Wáng Xiànzhī). His [[KR3h0013]] *Sīlíng hànmò zhì* (one *juàn*, *Déshòu* period) is the principal imperial treatise on calligraphy in pre-Yuán China. His court calligraphic programme — copying of the Chunhua and other model-letter compendia, calligraphic supervision of Mǎ Hézhī's *Máoshī* illustrations, restoration of the Wèi-Jìn canon — is treated at length in McNair (1995) and Murray (1993). Posthumous designation 高宗; tomb-name *Sīlíng* 思陵.
