**Xiè Guān** 謝觀 (*zì* Lìhéng 利恆, late-life *hào* Chéngzhāilǎorén 澄齋老人, 1880–1950), Republican-era physician, medical historian, and lexicographer of the **Mènghé school** 孟河派 from Luóshùwān 羅墅灣, Wǔjìn 武進 (Jiāngsū). Grandson of the Mènghé physician Xiè Bǎochū 謝葆初 and son of the geographer-classicist Xiè Zhōngyīng 謝鍾英; trained from childhood in the *Nèijīng*, *Nànjīng*, *Shānghán*, *jīngfāng*, and *běncǎo* corpora. Graduate of Soochow University 東吳大學 (1901); briefly taught geography in Guǎngzhōu (from 1905) and edited geography and medical books at the Shanghai Commercial Press (商務印書館) from 1911. Founding principal of the **Shanghai College of Traditional Chinese Medicine** 上海中醫專門學校 (1917, invited by Dīng Gānrén 丁甘仁), and the principal Mènghé-school architect of Republican TCM institutional modernisation. In 1929, when the GMD government's Central Health Committee 中央衛生委員會 passed the resolution to abolish Chinese medicine (the *Fèizhǐ jiùyī àn* 廢止舊醫案), Xiè was elected chief representative of the Chinese medical profession to Nanjing and led the months-long campaign that forced the regime to rescind the ban. Editor of the *Zhōngguó yīxué dà cídiǎn* 中國醫學大辭典 (Commercial Press, 1921 — 37,000+ entries, 3.5 million characters, eight years' labour); author of the *Zhōngguó yīxué yuánliú lùn* 中國醫學源流論 ([[KR3es005]], c. 1924–1925; printed 1935), *Chéngzhāi yī'àn* 澄齋醫案, *Chéngzhāi zázhù* 澄齋雜著, *Zhōngguó yīxuéhuà* 中國醫學話, and *Zhōngguó yàoxuéhuà* 中國藥學話. His leading disciples were [[秦伯未]] Qín Bówèi (1901–1970) and [[張贊臣]] Zhāng Zànchén (1904–1993). Not to be confused with the late-Míng lay-Buddhist commentator [[謝觀光]] Xiè Guāngguāng.
