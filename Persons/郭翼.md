Yuán literatus of Kūnshān 崑山. Style-name **Xīzhòng** 熙仲; sobriquets **Dōngguō shēng** 東郭生 and **Yěwēng** 野翁. As a young man he studied under **Wèi Péi** 衛培 and was thoroughly grounded in *Yì* exegesis. He presented strategy memorials to **Zhāng Shì-chéng** 張士誠 (the late-Yuán warlord of the Sūzhōu region), which were not adopted; he then withdrew to Lóushàng to farm, eventually taking up a *xùndǎo* (assistant-instructor) post but quarreling with the times. His tomb-inscription was written by **Lú Xióng** 盧熊 and is preserved in Zhū Guī's 朱珪 *Míngjī lù* 名蹟録. Guō was bright and broadly learned but refused the examination curriculum, devoting himself instead to old-style prose and especially poetry. He moved in the orbit of **Yáng Wéi-zhēn** 楊維楨 and adopted Yáng's *Tiěyá yuèfǔ* 鐵崖樂府 manner. His *Lín-wài yěyán* 林外野言 ([[KR4d0548]]) preserves the bulk of his surviving poetry.
