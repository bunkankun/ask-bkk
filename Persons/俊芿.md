Shunjō 俊芿 (Jùn-réng, 1166–1227, dates *Wén-zhì* 文治 2 – *Ān-zhèn* 安貞 1; sometimes recorded 1167–1228 by alternate calendar conventions, the form preserved in DILA A000811), Japanese Vinaya monk and the principal early-Kamakura transmitter of Sòng Buddhist learning to Japan. Native of Higo 肥後 (modern Kumamoto, Kyūshū), childhood-name *Bù-kě-qì* 不可棄 ("Not-to-be-Discarded" — a *jūnshō* 童子 sobriquet recorded in his hagiographies). Posthumous titles: *Yuè-lún dà-shī* 月輪大師 (*Gachirin Daishi*) and *Dà-xìng zhèng-fǎ guó-shī* 大興正法國師 (*Daikō Shōbō Kokushi*).

In *Qìng-yuán* 慶元 5 (1199) Shunjō travelled to Sòng China, landing first at *Jìng-shān* 徑山 to study under its 30th-generation abbot *Méng-ān Yuán-cōng* 蒙庵元聰; he subsequently studied Tiāntái under *Zōng-yìn* 宗印 and Vinaya under the *Líng-zhī* 靈芝 school's heirs in the Hángzhōu region. He spent twelve years in Sòng China before returning to Japan in *Jiàn-lì* 建曆 1 (1211) carrying over two thousand fascicles of Buddhist texts, secular learning, and Sòng-period painting and calligraphy. On his return he was patronized by retired emperor Go-Toba 後鳥羽 and the *Kanjuji* 勧修寺 line of the Fujiwara, who in 1218 transferred to him the dilapidated *Sen-yū-ji* 仙遊寺 in north-eastern Kyōto; Shunjō rebuilt and renamed it **Sennyū-ji** 泉涌寺 ("Welling-Spring Temple," named for a spring that erupted on the precincts during construction).

Shunjō's *Sennyū-ji* became the center of *Hokkyō-ritsu* 北京律 ("Northern-Capital Vinaya"), the integrated *Tiāntái–Chán–Vinaya–Pure-Land* synthesis that he had brought back from Sòng China — a contrastive counterpart to *Eison*'s 叡尊 *Saidaiji* 西大寺 *Nankyō-ritsu* 南京律 lineage. The fourfold integration meant that Vinaya was practiced at *Sennyū-ji* under a Tiāntái doctrinal framework and a Chán meditative discipline, while Pure Land devotion served as the laity-orientated practice — the very model of the late-Sòng *jiè-zōng* 戒宗 reformist programme.

Shunjō's textual contributions to Sòng China include the **Lǜ-zōng wèn-dá** 律宗問答 ([[KR6k0256]], X1108) — a record of Vinaya-doctrinal questions he submitted to three Sòng Vinaya masters of the *Líng-zhī*–*Tiāntái* milieu in *Jiā-dìng* 嘉定 2 *jǐ-sì* (1209): [[了然]] *Liǎo-rán* of *Lín-ān-fǔ Bù-kōng jiào-yuàn* 臨安府不空教院, [[智瑞]] *Zhì-ruì* of *Huì-jī Jí-lè-yuàn* 會稽極樂院, and [[妙音]] *Miào-yīn* of *Zhī-yán Jìng-huái Jìng-fàn-sì* 芝嵓淨懷淨梵寺. The thirty questions probe the *Nán-shān* triple-contemplation programme, the relation of doctrinal *Vinaya* to ascetic practice, and the standing of various procedural rules. The work survives in two fascicles (上下).

Shunjō died at *Sennyū-ji* on the 8th day of the intercalary 3rd month of *Ān-zhèn* 安貞 1 *dīng-hài* (1227), aged 62. His Japanese disciples *Tankai* 湛海 and *Shinjō* 信如 carried the *Hokkyō-ritsu* lineage forward into the late Kamakura.
