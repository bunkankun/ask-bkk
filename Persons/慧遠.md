Huìyuǎn 慧遠 (334–416), conventionally styled *Lúshān Huìyuǎn* 廬山慧遠 ("Huìyuǎn of Lúshān" / *Lúshān Yuǎngōng* 廬山遠公), was the most consequential southern Chinese Buddhist of the late fourth century and the founder of the Lúshān 廬山 / Mt. Lú Buddhist establishment. Native of *Yànmén* 雁門 (modern Shānxī), of the Jiǎ 賈 family. According to the *Gāosēng zhuàn* 高僧傳 (T2059, juan 6), he became a disciple of [[道安]] Dàoān at age 21, accompanied Dàoān to Xiàngyáng during the southern Buddhist diaspora, and in 380 settled at the *Dōnglínsì* 東林寺 on Lúshān where he remained for the rest of his life.

Huìyuǎn established Lúshān as the principal southern Chinese Buddhist intellectual and devotional centre. He convened the famous *Liánshè* 蓮社 ("Lotus Society") of 123 lay and monastic devotees who took the *Báilián* 白蓮 ("White Lotus") oath in 402 to seek rebirth in *Sukhāvatī* — the foundational moment of Chinese Pure Land devotional practice. His correspondence with [[鳩摩羅什]] Kumārajīva (preserved as the *Dà chéng dà yì zhāng* 大乘大義章 / T1856) is one of the seminal documents of early Chinese Buddhist doctrinal scholarship: an extended dialogue between two of the great minds of fifth-century Chinese Buddhism on the questions of the *dharma-kāya*, *bodhisattva*-doctrine, and Mahāyāna soteriology.

Huì-yuǎn was the patron of [[佛馱跋陀羅]] Buddhabhadra in his Lúshān period (410 – 413), under whose direction Buddhabhadra translated the *Dámóduōluó chán jīng* 達摩多羅禪經 (T0618, the great early-Chinese meditation manual). He also patronised the early translation work of *Sēng-jiā-tí-pó* 僧伽提婆 / Saṃghadeva, who at Huì-yuǎn's request produced new translations of the *Abhidharma* and *Madhyamāgama*. He died at Lúshān in *Yì-xī* 義熙 12 (416), aged 83.

(Note: This person 慧遠 of Lúshān (334–416) is to be distinguished from the much later [[淨影慧遠]] *Jìngyǐng Huìyuǎn* / *Suí Yuǎn* (523–592, DILA A002000), the *Dìlùn*-school master and Suí imperial Buddhist scholar; in the secondary literature, Lúshān Huìyuǎn and Suí Huìyuǎn are sometimes confused. They are distinct persons. *Suí Huìyuǎn*'s person note follows below as a separate entry.)

[[淨影慧遠]] *Jìng-yǐng Huì-yuǎn* 淨影慧遠 / *Suí Yuǎn* 隋遠 / *Dà Yuǎn* 大遠 ("Big Yuǎn") / *Běi Yuǎn* 北遠 ("Northern Yuǎn") (523–592), the great *Dìlùn* 地論-school master and one of the most important Buddhist scholars of the Suí imperial period. He was the principal disciple of [[法上]] Fǎ-shàng (495–580), the head of the Northern Qí *Dìlùn* school, and after his master's retirement and death he carried the Dìlùn tradition into the early Suí. Resident at the imperial *Jìng-yǐng-sì* 淨影寺 ("Pure Reflection Monastery") in Cháng'ān (whence his epithet *Jìng-yǐng Huì-yuǎn*), he produced an immense corpus of doctrinal works including [[KR6e0063|*Shí dì jīng lùn yì jì* 十地經論義記]] (X753) — the great Suí commentary on the *Daśabhūmika-vyākhyāna* — the *Dà chéng yì zhāng* 大乘義章 (T1851), the *Dà niè-pán jīng yì jì* 大涅槃經義記 (T1764), and many others.

Huìyuǎn (Suí) is the immediate scholarly predecessor of the great Tang Buddhist synthesis: his *Dìlùn*-school articulation of *six characteristics*, *tathāgatagarbha*, and *dharmakāya* doctrine provided the conceptual scaffolding on which [[智儼]] Zhìyǎn and [[法藏]] Fǎzàng would build the mature Tang Huáyán scholasticism. His *Dà chéng yì zhāng* in 14 fascicles is the most important systematic exposition of mature Northern-Dynasties / Suí Mahāyāna doctrine.
