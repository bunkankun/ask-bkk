# Liú Huī 劉徽

Active in the late Cáo-Wèi 曹魏 / early Western-Jìn 西晉 period, third century CE. Birth and death years not securely recorded; dated by the *Wèi-shū* statement that he commenced his commentary on the *Jiǔ-zhāng suàn-shù* in *Jǐng-yuán* 4 (263 CE) of the Wèi, but his commentary makes reference to the *Jìn imperial-storehouse bronze* *hú* (a Jìn-era unit-vessel) — so he was active before 263 (the commentary's beginning) and continued after the Wèi-Jìn transition (265 CE), making him at least a contemporary of the early Jìn. The Sìkù catalog conventionally classifies him as 晉; the *Sìbù cóngkān* (SBCK) edition of the *Jiǔ-zhāng suàn-shù* prefers 魏.

The single most important Chinese mathematician of the third century. His magnum opus is the systematic commentary (*zhù* 注) on the *Jiǔ-zhāng suàn-shù* ([[KR3f0032]] *Jiǔ-zhāng suàn-shù*), which transformed that received compilation from a problem-collection into a coherent mathematical-theoretical work. Liu's commentary supplied: (a) systematic geometric proofs (*hé-mèi* 合冪 / *out-in compensating* methodology) for the procedures stated without proof in the original; (b) the foundational *gē-yuán* 割圓 (circle-cutting) method for computing π by inscribing successive regular polygons of doubling sides — Liú Huī computed π to 3.1416 via 192-gon, anticipating Zǔ Chōngzhī's 5th-century refinement to 3.1415926; (c) the geometric account of right-triangle (*gōu-gǔ*) area-relations that anticipates the formal Pythagorean theorem; (d) extensive elaboration of the *Fāng-chéng* (equation-systems) chapter, including what amounts to the systematic Gaussian-elimination algorithm in geometric form.

Liú Huī's also-extant *Hǎi-dǎo suàn-jīng* 海島算經 ([[KR3f0035]]) — a 9-problem treatise on right-triangle remote-sensing techniques (computing the height of mountains from shadow-and-sighting ratios) — was originally an appendix-chapter of his *Jiǔ-zhāng* commentary (the *Chóng-chā* 重差 chapter, mentioned in his preface to the *Jiǔ-zhāng* commentary), separately catalogued by the Tang as one of the *Suàn-jīng shí-shū* 算經十書 (Ten Mathematical Classics).

His mathematical method is rigorous and self-consciously methodological. The preface to the *Jiǔ-zhāng* commentary articulates an explicit theory of *fenced-and-derived* mathematical demonstration (one root-method gives birth to many specific procedures by *zhī-tiáo suī fēn ér tóng běn-gàn* "the branches although divided yet share a single trunk"); the geometric proofs by visualizing area-and-volume rearrangements (*chū-rù hùxiāng-bǔ* 出入互相補) anticipate the methodology of Western proof-by-area-rearrangement that would become standard in European Renaissance mathematics. Liú Huī is conventionally regarded as the founding figure of theoretically-rigorous Chinese mathematics, and his work was the indispensable foundation for the subsequent Chinese mathematical tradition through the Sòng-Yuán schools (Lǐ Yě, Zhū Shìjié, Yáng Huī, Qín Jiǔsháo) and into the Míng-Qīng synthesis.
