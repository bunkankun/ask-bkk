**Yáng Jìzhōu** 楊繼洲 (*zì* Jìshí 濟時, 1522–1620, 明), late-Míng imperial physician of Sānqú 三衢 (modern Qú-zhōu 衢州, Zhèjiāng). Heir to a hereditary medical family — his grandfather Yáng Yì 楊益 was an imperial physician at the Tài-yī-yuàn 太醫院 under the Jiā-jìng emperor — Yáng Jìzhōu served as a Tài-yī-yuàn imperial physician under the Wàn-lì emperor and was active in clinical practice for over forty years. His monumental ten-juan ***Zhēnjiǔ dàchéng*** 針灸大成 ([[KR3ee027]], 1601) is the single most influential acupuncture work in the East Asian medical tradition. Yáng built the *Dàchéng* on the foundation of his family's hereditary *Wèi-shēng zhēnjiǔ xuán-jī mì-yào* 衛生針灸玄機秘要, incorporating virtually the entire pre-existing Sòng-Yuán-Míng acupuncture corpus with his own editorial commentary and clinical case-records. No CBDB record could be matched with confidence; standard biographical references are in Yán Shìyún's *Zhōng-yī rénwù cídiǎn* and in the *Wàn-lì Qú-zhōu fǔ zhì*.
