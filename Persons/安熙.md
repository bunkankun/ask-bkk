Yuán-period 元 Neo-Confucian scholar, the principal second-generation transmitter of **Liú Yīn** [[劉因]] (1249–1293)'s tradition; the historical pivot between Liú Yīn and **Sū Tiān-jué** 蘇天爵 [[蘇天爵]] (1294–1352) in the Hé-běi *Lǐ-xué* lineage. *Zì* **Jìng-zhòng** 敬仲; *hào* **Mò-ān** 默庵 ("Silent-Hut"). Native of **Gǎo-chéng** 藁城 (modern Hé-běi).

**Discipleship.** Young, Ān admired Liú Yīn's reputation and wished to study with him; Liú Yīn was willing to transmit his learning to Ān — but Liú Yīn died (1293) before this could be carried out. Ān thereafter devoted his lifelong learning to following Liú Yīn's tradition (a *sī-shū* posthumous-discipleship rather than direct *shī-chuán*).

**Doctrinal defence.** Sū Tiān-jué's *xíng-zhuàng* records Ān's forceful defence of Zhū Xī's *Sì-shū jí-zhù* against its northern critics — first the Jīn-Yuán transition philosopher **Wáng Ruò-xū** 王若虛 (Hǔ-nán-school *Sì-shū* dissenter), then the later attacker **Chén Tiān-xiáng** 陳天祥. The *xíng-zhuàng* tradition relates that Ān forced Chén Tiān-xiáng to burn his anti-Zhū book — though the Sìkù editors note that Chén Tiān-xiáng's book in fact survives.

**Reception.** Ān's pupil **Sū Tiān-jué** 蘇天爵 [[KR4d0523]] composed the *xíng-zhuàng*, gathered his poetry-and-prose into a 10-*juàn* collection, and secured **Yú Jí** [[虞集]] (1272–1348) to compose the preface. The original 10 *juàn* survive only in 5 *juàn* in the Sìkù base.
