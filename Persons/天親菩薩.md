Tiān-qīn púsà 天親菩薩 (\*Vasubandhu, "Kinsman-of-the-Vasus"; conventionally c. 320–400 CE), Indian Buddhist master, the most consequential single Buddhist philosopher of the post-Aśoka period. Younger brother of [[無著菩薩|Asaṅga]], converted by him from Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma scholasticism to Mahāyāna Yogācāra. Per Paramārtha's *Pó-sǒu pán-dòu zhuàn* 婆藪槃豆傳 (T2049) and Tibetan biographies, Vasubandhu was born in Puruṣapura (Peshawar, Gandhāra) and was the principal Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma master of his generation, author of the *Abhidharmakośa* (the foundational Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma compendium); after his conversion to Mahāyāna by Asaṅga he composed the canonical Yogācāra treatises: the *Triṃśikā* (*Wéishí sānshí sòng*), the *Viṃśatikā*, the *Madhyānta-vibhāga-bhāṣya*, and many others.

The Chinese name 天親 ("Heaven-Kinsman") is a calque-translation of *Vasu-bandhu*; the alternative 世親 ("World-Kinsman") is the more usual modern form. The Indic name 婆藪槃豆 (*Pósǒu pándòu*) is the transliteration. The name is used variably across the Chinese canon.

His surviving Chinese-translation works are extensive: the *Abhidharmakośa* (T1558–60); the Yogācāra treatises *Triṃśikā* (T1586) and *Viṃśatikā* (T1590); the *Daśabhūmika-vibhāṣā* commentary (T1521); [[KR6c0032|T1511 *Vajracchedikā-prajñāpāramitā-sūtra-śāstra*]]; and many others. Per DILA Buddhist Person Authority A001280.
