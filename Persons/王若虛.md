Wáng Ruò-xū 王若虛 (1174–1243), *zì* Cóng-zhī 從之, self-styled Yōng-fū 慵夫 ("the Lazy Fellow") and Hū-nán yí-lǎo 滹南遺老 ("Old Remnant South of the Hū-tuó"), native of Gǎo-chéng 槁城 (modern Hé-běi). Jīn Chéng-ān 2 (1197) *jīng-yì* (Classics-specialist) *jìnshì*. Held office sequentially as Magistrate of Guǎn-chéng 管城 and Mén-shān 門山; was inducted by recommendation as *biān-xiū* of the Imperial Historiography Office; rose to *Yìng-fèng hàn-lín wén-zì*, *Zhuó-zuò zuǒ-láng*, Vice-Prefect of Píng-liáng; called as *Zuǒ-sī jiàn-yì*; transferred to Prefect of Yán-zhōu; ended as *Zhí-xué-shì*. When the Mongols took the Jīn northern lands he retired to his native Gǎo-chéng and refused to take Yuán office — the *yí-lǎo* self-designation in his collection's title marks him as one of the foundational Jīn-loyalist *yí-mín*. In old age he traveled east to Tài-shān, paused at the Cuì-měi Pavilion at Huáng-xiàn Peak, and died there. His major surviving work is the *Hū-nán yí-lǎo jí* [[KR4d0418]] (also catalogued as *Hū-nán jí* 滹南集) in 45 *juàn* — a comprehensive *kǎo-jù* corpus on the Five Classics, *Lúnyǔ*, *Mèng-zǐ*, *Shǐ-jì*, dynastic histories, and historical-literary criticism, with a substantial *Shī-huà* (juàn 38–40) that is one of the principal Jīn-period poetics treatises.
