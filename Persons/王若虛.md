Wáng Ruòxū 王若虛 (1174–1243), *zì* Cóngzhī 從之, self-styled Yōngfū 慵夫 ("the Lazy Fellow") and Hūnán yílǎo 滹南遺老 ("Old Remnant South of the Hūtuó"), native of Gǎochéng 槁城 (modern Héběi). Jīn Chéngān 2 (1197) *jīngyì* (Classics-specialist) *jìnshì*. Held office sequentially as Magistrate of Guǎnchéng 管城 and Ménshān 門山; was inducted by recommendation as *biānxiū* of the Imperial Historiography Office; rose to *Yìngfèng hànlín wénzì*, *Zhuózuò zuǒláng*, Vice-Prefect of Píngliáng; called as *Zuǒsī jiànyì*; transferred to Prefect of Yánzhōu; ended as *Zhíxuéshì*. When the Mongols took the Jīn northern lands he retired to his native Gǎochéng and refused to take Yuán office — the *yílǎo* self-designation in his collection's title marks him as one of the foundational Jīn-loyalist *yímín*. In old age he traveled east to Tàishān, paused at the Cuìměi Pavilion at Huángxiàn Peak, and died there. His major surviving work is the *Hūnán yílǎo jí* [[KR4d0418]] (also catalogued as *Hūnán jí* 滹南集) in 45 *juàn* — a comprehensive *kǎojù* corpus on the Five Classics, *Lúnyǔ*, *Mèngzǐ*, *Shǐjì*, dynastic histories, and historical-literary criticism, with a substantial *Shīhuà* (juàn 38–40) that is one of the principal Jīn-period poetics treatises.
