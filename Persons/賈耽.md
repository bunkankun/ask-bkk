Jiǎ Dān 賈耽 (730–805), courtesy name Dūnshī 敦詩, was a Tang statesman and geographer from Nánpí 南皮 (Cāngzhōu). He rose through provincial governorships (Liángzhōu, Shānnán, Yìchéng) and entered the central chancellery in 793, serving as 左僕射 / 同中書門下平章事 from 793 until his death in 805 under emperors Dézōng 德宗 and Shùnzōng 順宗. He is best known to historians of cartography and geography for his *Hǎinèi Huáyí tú* 海內華夷圖 (801), a large grid-based world map continuing the tradition of Péi Xiù 裴秀, and for the *Guǎngzhōu tōnghǎi yídào* 廣州通海夷道, the earliest surviving description of the maritime route from Guangzhou via the Strait of Malacca to the Persian Gulf, preserved in *Xīn Tángshū* 43. His official biographies are at *Jiù Tángshū* 138 and *Xīn Tángshū* 166. In Buddhist apocryphal literature his name was used as an authorizing imperial voice in late-Tang Dunhuang "warning sūtras" (e.g. [[KR6u0052]] *Quànshàn jīng* 勸善經 and [[KR6u0053]] *Xīn púsà jīng* 新菩薩經), where his title 左丞相 frames the prophecy as a chancellor's edict urging copying of the text to ward off epidemic disease.
