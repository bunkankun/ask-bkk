**Wáng Guóruì** 王國瑞 (alternate name 王國端 Wáng Guóduān, *fl.* Tiān-lì 2 = 1329, 元), Yuán-period acupuncturist of Wù-yuán 婺源 (modern Jiāngxī). The catalog meta gives his name as 王國瑞 but the SKQS *tíyào* uses 王國端 — the two are alternate orthographic forms of the same name. Author of the ***Biǎn-què shén-yīng zhēn-jiǔ Yù-lóng jīng*** 扁鵲神應鍼灸玉龍經 ([[KR3e0063]]) — a Yuán-period acupuncture treatise with the conceit of having received the Jade-Dragon (玉龍) verses from the legendary Biǎn Què 扁鵲. The pseudonymous attribution is — per the disciple Zhōu Zhòngliáng's preface — meant to lend authority to the work, not literally claimed. The work's distinctive feature is its complete reduction of acupuncture to mnemonic verse-form: the ***120-acupuncture-point Jade-Dragon Songs*** 一百二十穴玉龍歌 (85 verse-pieces), the ***Heavenly-Star 11-Point Verses*** 天星十一穴歌訣 (12 verse-pieces), the ***Six-Sixty-Sixth Acupoint Treatment Indications*** 六十六穴治證, and so on. Wáng's disciple Zhōu Zhòngliáng's preface dates to Tiān-lì 2 (1329).
