Early-Míng **Tiān-tāi** 天臺 exegetical master and senior monastic administrator. Native of **Shàng-yú** 上虞 in Zhèjiāng. Born **1352**; died at **Hǎi-yìn-sì** 海印寺 in Beijing in **Hóng-xī** 1 (3rd lunar month of 1425 = 29 March – 26 April 1425), age 74 (sources: *Bǔ xù gāo-sēng zhuàn* 補續高僧傳, j. 4).

He served as abbot of two of the most prestigious Tiāntái-lineage seats of the south — **Yǎn-fú-sì** 演福寺 and **Shàng-Tiānzhú-sì** 上天竺寺 in Hángzhōu — and was finally summoned to the early-Míng northern capital as rectifier-of-doctrine at **Hǎi-yìn-sì** 海印寺 in Beijing under the Yǒng-lè and Hóng-xī courts. He held the styles **Yī-ān** 一菴, **Tuì-wēng** 退翁, and (in later Tiāntái-lineage citation) **Tiān-zhú-rú** 天竺如.

His principal scholarly legacy is the **Dà Míng sān-zàng fǎ-shù** 大明三藏法數 ([[KR6s0007]], P1615), a fifty-juan imperial-commission Buddhist numerical-list lexicon completed in **Yǒng-lè** 17 (1419). The work — produced under the byline **上天竺前住持沙門一如等奉勑集註** ("former abbot of Shàng-Tiānzhú, Yī-rú and others, by imperial command compiled and annotated") — became the standard pre-modern Chinese-Buddhist reference for *fǎ-shù* (numerical-category Dharma sets) and continues to underlie the corresponding entries in nearly every Chinese, Japanese, and Western Buddhist dictionary.
