Yìng Huīqiān 應撝謙 (1615–1683), *zì* Sìyín 嗣寅, *hào* Qiánzhāi 潛齋. Native of Rénhé 仁和 (Hángzhōu, Zhèjiāng). Late-Míng / early-Qīng commoner-scholar in the *yímín* (Míng-loyalist) tradition; declined to take the Qīng *jìnshì* exam after the 1644 conquest and lived in retirement, devoted to classical and musical scholarship.

His chief surviving work is the *Gǔ yuè shū* 古樂書 ([[KR1i0014]]) — a two-juan music-theoretical treatise drawing on Cài Yuándìng's *Lǜlǚ xīnshū*, the *Shuōwén* annotations, Zhū Xī, Chén Xiángdào's *Lǐshū*, and Lǐ Zhīzǎo's *Pàngōng lǐyuè shū* 頖宮禮樂疏. The work is the most important Hángzhōu / Zhèjiāng-school music-theoretical contribution of the seventeenth century. He has a *Qīng shǐ gǎo* biography (juan 480, *Rúlín zhuàn*) which praises him as one of the great Hangzhou *yímín* and the moral exemplar of his generation. CBDB id 65930 confirms the lifedates.
