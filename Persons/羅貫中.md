**Luó Guànzhōng** 羅貫中 (born ca. 1315, died ca. 1400), *míng* Běn 本, *hào* Húhǎi Sǎnrén 湖海散人 ("Wanderer of Lakes and Seas"), is the conventional attributed author of several of the most important Chinese vernacular historical novels. His native place is disputed: traditional sources variously give Tàiyuán 太原 (Shanxi) or Qiantang 錢塘 (Hangzhou); the *Lùguǐ bù* 錄鬼簿 (a Yuan-era catalogue of dramatic writers) records him as a native of Tàiyuán and a disciple of the playwright Shī Huì 施惠. He is said to have lived through the turbulent final decades of the Yuan and the founding of the Ming, possibly having participated in one of the peasant rebel movements of the era.

Luó is most securely associated with the *Sānguó Yǎnyì* 三國演義 [[KR4k0063]] (earliest printed edition 1522), widely considered the first fully developed Chinese historical novel. He is also credited, with varying degrees of certainty, as author or co-author of the *Shuǐhǔ Quánzhuàn* 水滸全傳 [[KR4k0066]], the *Sānsuì Pínyāo Zhuàn* 三遂平妖傳 [[KR4k0061]], the *Suí Táng Yěshǐ* 隋唐野史 [[KR4k0067]], and the *Wǔdài Mìshǐ* 五代秘史 [[KR4k0069]]. Modern scholarship is cautious about accepting all of these attributions without reservation, particularly for the *Shuǐhǔ* (where the primary attribution is to Shī Nài'ān 施耐庵) and the minor historical novels.
