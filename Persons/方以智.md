Fāng Yǐzhì 方以智 (1611–1671), *zì* Mì-zhī 密之, *hào* Lù-qǐ 鹿起, dharma-name Wú-kě 無可 (later Yào-dì 藥地 / Hóng-zhì 弘智). Native of Tóng-chéng 桐城, Anhui. The leading polymath of seventeenth-century China, embedded from birth in one of the most prominent late-Míng scholar-official lineages: father Fāng Kǒng-zhào 方孔炤 (*Hú-guǎng xún-fǔ*, *Yìjīng* scholar); grandfather Fāng Dà-zhèn 方大鎮 (*Hàn-lín shì-jiǎng xué-shì*); great-grandfather Fāng Xué-jiàn 方學漸 (Anhui *Lǐ-xué* master). One of the *Sì gōng zǐ* 四公子 of Chóng-zhēn-period Nán-jīng with Chén Zhēn-huì 陳貞慧, Hóu Fāng-yù 侯方域, and Mào Xiāng 冒襄. Chóng-zhēn 13 (1640) *jìnshì*; appointed Hàn-lín *jiǎn-tǎo* 檢討. Fled south after the 1644 collapse, served briefly under the Southern Míng Lóng-wǔ and Yǒng-lì emperors, took Chán-Buddhist vows after 1650, and died at Wàn-ān 萬安 (Jiāng-xī) in 1671 — almost certainly by self-drowning to evade Qīng arrest. Major works include the *Tōng yǎ* 通雅 ([[KR3j0066]], an encyclopedic *xiǎo xué* compendium), the *Wù lǐ xiǎo zhì* 物理小識 (natural-philosophical encyclopedia), the *Dōng-xī jūn* 東西均 (philosophical synthesis), the *Yào dì pǎo zhuāng* 藥地炮莊 (a Chán-flavored Zhuāng-zǐ commentary), and a body of poetry.
