# Míng-yī 明一

Late-seventeenth-century Línjì Chán monk, dharma-heir of [[秀|Zhī-yán Xiù]] 芝巖秀 in the Mìyún → Lín-yě → Xíng-mì → Xiù sub-lineage. Head compiler (*děng biān* 等編) of [[KR6q0431]] 《芝巖秀禪師語錄》 (Jiā-xīng Canon J28 B213). The 明-generation character places him at the 35th-generation Línjì tier — one generation below Xiù's 超 generation.

Native place, lay surname, and lifedates unrecovered. No independent works survive under his name.

Sources: [[KR6q0431]] juan-head signature line "*sì-fǎ mén-rén Míng-yī děng biān* 嗣法門人明一等編".

Nara-to-early-Heian-era Japanese **Hossō** 法相 monk and one of the *Daianji no sanshū* 大安寺三衆 ("Three Masters of Daian-ji"). Compiler of [[KR6d0034]] *Fǎhuá lüè-chāo* 法華略抄 (T2188), a brief excerpting commentary on the *Lotus Sūtra*. Standard biographical sources place him at **Daian-ji** 大安寺 in Nara, where he is associated with the late-Nara consolidation of the Hossō (East Asian Yogācāra) tradition. His dates are conventionally given as **728–798** in Japanese reference works, though direct primary documentation is sparse and the dates carry some uncertainty.

The *Hokke ryakushō* is his principal surviving work in the East Asian canon; no other compositions are clearly attested under his name. Source: hobotei.xml entry TP595; Bussho kaisetsu daijiten 佛書解説大辭典 (Mochizuki).
