Late-Míng paleographer, *zhuàn*-script calligrapher, and antiquarian recluse; *zì* Fán-fū 凡夫, *hào* Hánshān-zǐ 寒山子; descended from the Sòng imperial clan, of Wúxiàn 吳縣 (Sūzhōu). Lifedates 1559–1625 per catalog meta. Lived as a recluse at Hánshān 寒山 (south of Sūzhōu) with his wife Lù Qīngzī 陸卿子, a major poet; the Hánshān residence became a centre of late-Míng antiquarian culture. Author of the [[KR3h0051]] *Hánshān zhǒu-tán* 寒山帚談 (extracted from his larger *Shuō-wén cháng-jiān* 說文長箋, a deeply unreliable but ambitious paleographic compendium); the systematic calligraphic doctrine of his *zhuàn*-script practice. Famed in his time as the principal innovator of *cǎo-zhuàn* 草篆 (cursive seal-script). His son Zhào Líntián 趙均 (1591–1641) continued the family seal-script tradition.
