Indian Mahāyāna Buddhist philosopher and poet, traditionally identified as the eighth-century *yú-jiā zhōng-guān pài* 瑜伽中觀派 / *Yogācāra-Mādhyamika* synthesist of Nālandā. Modern Western scholarly consensus (Pezzali, Crosby/Skilton, Williams) places his life-dates approximately 685–763, though earlier *Tibetan tradition* and some Chinese accounts place him in the sixth-seventh century. Born to King *Kuśalavarman* / *Dégǎi-wáng* 德鎧王 of *Surāṣṭra* / *Sūluó-shíchǐ-guó* 蘇羅室蹉國 in southern India, with the birth-name *Jì-kǎi* 寂鎧 (*Śāntavarman*); fled court life on the eve of his coronation, served twelve years at the court of the *Sīnharaja* of eastern India, then took ordination at *Nālandā* under *Shèngtiān* 勝天 (Jayadeva), receiving the new name *Jì-tiān* 寂天 (*Śāntideva*).

His principal works are the *Śikṣāsamuccaya* (*學處要集* / *Compendium of Trainings*) and the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* (*入菩薩行論* / *Introduction to the Bodhisattva's Conduct*), the latter the principal classical text on the cultivation of *bodhicitta* and bodhisattva conduct. The *Bodhicaryāvatāra* was first translated into Chinese in 985 CE by Tiānxīzāi as *Pútí xíng jīng* 菩提行經 (T32n1662, eight chapters, attributed to Nāgārjuna in error); the full ten-chapter Tibetan version was transmitted to Tibet by Atiśa and elevated by Tsongkhapa and the dGe-lugs-pa school as one of the *Bka'-gdams* six fundamental treatises (*Bka'-gdams gzhung-drug*).

Works in the Kanripo corpus: [[KR6v0044]] *Rù púsà xíng lùn guǎngjiě* — Lóng-lián's twentieth-century Tibetan-to-Chinese translation of the Bodhicaryāvatāra plus rGyal-tshab Dar-ma rin-chen's commentary.
