Táng-dynasty Buddhist *vinaya* exegete and encyclopedist. Native of **Yī-quē** 伊闕 (south of Luòyáng), lay surname **Hán** 韓, *zì* **Xuán-yùn** 玄惲 (also written 元惲 or 玄暉; in some Sòng witnesses **Xuán-huī** is also given). Ordained at twelve at **Qīng-lóng-sì** 青龍寺, where he undertook deep study of the *vinaya*. He took full ordination together with **Dào-xuān** 道宣 (596–667) under the **Nán-shān-律** master **Zhì-shǒu** 智首 (567–635) at **Hóng-fú-sì** 弘福寺 in **Suí Dà-yè** 11 = 615 CE — placing his birth ca. 600 by inference.

In **Xiǎn-qìng** 顯慶 (656–660), at the imperial summons of the Crown Prince (later Gāo-zōng), he was installed at the newly-completed **Xī-míng-sì** 西明寺 in Cháng'ān, where he resided alongside Dào-xuān and where the great compilations of his maturity were produced. The two masters jointly defined the *Nán-shān-律* lineage's apex generation.

His written output is enormous: the hundred-juan **Fǎ-yuàn zhū-lín** ([[KR6s0002]], T2122, completed 668) — a monumental Buddhist *lèi-shū* and the work for which he is most remembered — together with the twenty-juan **Zhū-jīng yào-jí** 諸經要集 ([[KR6s0003]], T2123, an abridgment of the canonical-citation portions of the *Fǎ-yuàn*), the **Sì-fēn-lǜ tǎo-yào** 四分律討要 (six juan), the **Sì-fēn-lǜ ní-chāo** 四分律尼鈔 (five juan), the **Dà-xiǎo-shèng chán-mén guān** 大小乘禪門觀 (ten juan), the **Xìn-fú lùn** 信福論, the **Jīn-gāng-jīng jí-zhù** 金剛經集註, and others.

Death: **Hóng-dào** 弘道 1 (683 CE / early 684 by Julian conversion); the death notice is preserved at *Sòng gāo-sēng zhuàn* j. 4 (T50n2061_p0726c07) and at the *Bǎi-zhàng qīng-guī zhèng-yì jì* 百丈清規證義記 (X76n1516_p0091c08).
