# Zhūhóng 袾宏

Yúnqī Zhūhóng 雲棲袾宏 (1535–1615), *zì* Fóhuì 佛慧, *hào* Liánchí 蓮池 ("Lotus Pond"); lay surname Shěn 沈. Native of Rénhé 仁和 (Hángzhōu). One of the **Four Eminent Monks of the late Míng** (*Wǎn Míng sì dà gāosēng* 晚明四大高僧) alongside Hānshān Déqīng 憨山德清, Zǐbǎi Zhēnkě 紫柏真可, and Ǒuyì Zhìxù 蕅益智旭.

Early Confucian education (successful at *shēngyuán* 生員 level) before ordination in 1566 after the deaths of his parents and wife. Studied under Biànróng Zhēnyuán 遍融真圓 and Xiàoyán Débǎo 笑巖德寶, then spent a long residence at Yúnqīsì 雲棲寺 on Wǔyúnshān 五雲山 near Hángzhōu — whence the name *Yúnqī*. Central figure in the late-Míng revival of Pure Land practice, and architect of the *sānjiāo héyī* 三教合一 ("unity of the three teachings") integrative programme that harmonised Chán, Pure Land, and Confucian moral cultivation.

Major writings include the *Āmítuó jīng shūchāo* 阿彌陀經疏鈔 (a commentary on the shorter *Sukhāvatīvyūhasūtra*), the *Zìzhī lù* 自知錄 (the *Morality Book of Self-Knowledge*, a systematic lay-Buddhist moral-accounting manual), the *Zhú chuāng suíbǐ* 竹窗隨筆 (three-part collection of essays on religious practice), numerous commentaries and Pure-Land instructional works, and numerous short prefaces and postfaces for reprinted classical texts. His 1609 preface to an earlier engraving of Pǔmíng's *Mùniú tú sòng* is preserved in the Kāngxī-1 compilation [[KR6q0161]].
