The **third emperor of the Míng dynasty**: temple-name originally **Tàizōng** 太宗 (changed in 1538 by Jiājìng to **Chéngzǔ** 成祖, by which name he is more commonly known in later sources), posthumous-title **Wénhuángdì** 文皇帝, era-name **Yǒnglè** 永樂. Personal name **Zhū Dì** 朱棣. Born **1360 CE**; died **1424 CE**, having ruled from **1402** (after seizing the throne from his nephew the **Jiànwén** emperor in the **Jìngnán** 靖難 war).

His reign is a foundational era of the Míng dynasty:

- **Relocation of the capital** from Nánjīng to **Beijing** (begun 1406, completed 1421) and the construction of the **Forbidden City**.
- The famous **Zhèng Hé** 鄭和 maritime expeditions (1405–1433, with Zhèng Hé's first six voyages under Yǒnglè).
- The compilation of the **Yǒnglè dàdiǎn** 永樂大典 (the great Yǒnglè encyclopedia, 1403–1408).
- The production of the **Sìshū / Wǔjīng dàquán** 四書五經大全 imperial Confucian-canonical compilations (1417).
- The northern campaigns against the **Mongols**.

His Buddhist program was equally substantial:

- **Sponsorship of the Northern Yǒnglè canon** (*Běizàng* 北藏) — begun 1410 in Beijing, completed 1440 under Zhèngtǒng — the standard Northern-Míng imperial canon.
- **Continuation of the Southern Yǒnglè canon** (begun 1372 under his father Tàizǔ, completed 1399).
- **Tibetan Buddhist** patronage, including imperial publication of Tantric texts (the *Hevajra-mūla-tantra*) and Tibetan-Buddhist preceptors at court.
- Construction of the famous **porcelain pagoda** at **Báoēnsì** 報恩寺 in Nánjīng (begun 1412).
- Commissioning of the **Dà Míng sānzàng fǎshù** ([[KR6s0007]], 1419) — the standard Buddhist *fǎshù* lexicon — under Tiāntái-master **Yīrú** 一如.

His personally composed Buddhist works are preserved in three substantial collections in the Kanripo:

- **Dà Míng Tàizōng Wénhuángdì yùzhì xùzàn wén** ([[KR6s0063]], P1610, 1 juan, ten prefaces and encomia).
- **Zhū fó shìzūn rúlái púsà zūnzhě shénsēng míng jīng** ([[KR6s0064]], P1611, 40 juan).
- **Zhū fó shìzūn rúlái púsà zūnzhě míngchēng gēqū** ([[KR6s0065]], P1612, 51 juan).

The two latter works, totaling 91 juan, are extended Buddhist devotional compilations that may have been edited by court-monastic teams under his imperial sponsorship rather than literally composed by him personally. Together with the preface-collection [[KR6s0063]], they constitute one of the most substantial bodies of Míng imperial Buddhist literature.

Source: standard Míng dynastic sources (*Míngshǐ* 明史, j. 5–7 *Chéngzǔ běnjì*); imperial Buddhist works at [[KR6s0063]]–[[KR6s0065]]; the Northern Yǒnglè canon prefatory matter.
