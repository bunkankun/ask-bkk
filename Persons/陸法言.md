Suí-period (late 6th century) phonologist, *míng* Cí 詞, *zì* Fǎyán 法言 (the *zì* by which he is universally known); native of Wéijùn 魏郡 (modern Línzhāng 臨漢, Héběi). Held minor office under the Northern-Qí, Northern-Zhōu, and Suí. The principal compiler of the *Qièyùn* 切韻 (5 *juàn*, Rénshòu 1, 601), the foundational rhyme-book of the Chinese phonological tradition: in 583 he had hosted the eight scholars Liú Zhēn, Yán Zhītuī, Wèi Yuān, Lú Sīdào, Lǐ Ruò, Xiāo Gāi, Xīn Déyuán, Xuē Dàohéng at his home for a discussion of how to reconcile the conflicting regional rhyme-books of Lǚ Jìng et al.; eighteen years later he completed the *Qièyùn* on the basis of those notes. The *Qièyùn* survives only in fragments (Tang manuscripts from Dunhuang and Turfan) but is the textual ancestor of every later canonical rhyme-book — Sūn Miǎn's [[孫愐]] *Tángyùn* (751), the *Yuánběn Guǎngyùn* [[KR1j0054]] (10th–early 11th c.), Chén Péngnián's [[陳彭年]] *Chóngxiū Guǎngyùn* [[KR1j0055]] (1011), and through these the entire reconstruction of Middle Chinese in modern phonology.
