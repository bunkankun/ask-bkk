Suí-period (late 6th century) phonologist, *míng* Cí 詞, *zì* Fǎ-yán 法言 (the *zì* by which he is universally known); native of Wéi-jùn 魏郡 (modern Lín-zhāng 臨漢, Hé-běi). Held minor office under the Northern-Qí, Northern-Zhōu, and Suí. The principal compiler of the *Qiè-yùn* 切韻 (5 *juàn*, Rén-shòu 1, 601), the foundational rhyme-book of the Chinese phonological tradition: in 583 he had hosted the eight scholars Liú Zhēn, Yán Zhī-tuī, Wèi Yuān, Lú Sī-dào, Lǐ Ruò, Xiāo Gāi, Xīn Dé-yuán, Xuē Dào-héng at his home for a discussion of how to reconcile the conflicting regional rhyme-books of Lǚ Jìng et al.; eighteen years later he completed the *Qiè-yùn* on the basis of those notes. The *Qiè-yùn* survives only in fragments (Tang manuscripts from Dunhuang and Turfan) but is the textual ancestor of every later canonical rhyme-book — Sūn Miǎn's [[孫愐]] *Táng-yùn* (751), the *Yuán-běn Guǎng-yùn* [[KR1j0054]] (10th–early 11th c.), Chén Péng-nián's [[陳彭年]] *Chóng-xiū Guǎng-yùn* [[KR1j0055]] (1011), and through these the entire reconstruction of Middle Chinese in modern phonology.
