Zēng Jǐ 曾幾 (1084–1166), *zì* Jí-fǔ 吉甫, *hào* Chá-shān jū-shì 茶山居士, native of Gàn-xiàn 贛縣 (Jiāng-xī), later moved to Hé-nán. Through his elder brother Zēng Bì 曾弼's *xù* (compassion-grace), conferred *Jiāng-shì-láng* 將仕郎. Tested in *Lì-bù* with high distinction; granted *Shàng-shè chū-shēn*; appointed *Xiào-shū-láng* 校書郎. Under Gāozōng cumulatively-officed to *Jiāng-xī* / *Zhè-xī tí-xíng* (Judicial Commissioner). For opposing Qín Guì 秦檜, dismissed; resided as guest at Shàng-ráo 上饒 at the *Chá-shān* 茶山 monastery, hence the *hào*. After Qín's death (1155), recalled as *Bì-shū shǎo-jiān* 秘書少監 and *Quán Lǐ-bù shì-láng*. Retired with *Tí-jǔ Yù-lóng-guān* honour. Posthumous canonisation *Wén-qīng* 文清.

The *Sì-kù* editors of [[KR4d0192]] place Zēng's poetry in the *Jiāng-xī* school, taking Dù Fǔ and Huáng Tíng-jiān as *zōng* (canonical patriarchs); Wèi Qìng-zhī's *Shī-rén yù-xiè* says Zēng's learning came-out from Hán Jū 韓駒 [[韓駒]] (also a *Jiāng-xī*-school figure). Most consequentially, Zēng was the principal teacher of Lù Yóu 陸游 — Zhào Gēng-fū's poem on the *Chá-shān jí* says: "*Duō-duō bī rén mén-dì-zǐ; Jiàn-nán yǐ jiàn yī-dēng chuán*" (the master-pressing pupils — Jiàn-nán [Lù Yóu] is already-seen as one-lamp's-transmission). Zēng → Lù = the principal late-Southern-Sòng poetic transmission line.

Lù Yóu's colophon to Zēng's memorial-drafts: at *Shào-xīng wèi* (= 1163), Zēng was at the *Yǔ-jì jīng-shè* (Huì-jī); Lù Yóu visited 3 days running; each visit Zēng spoke of *yōu-guó* (worry-of-state); Zēng was over 70, with a hundred-strong family-clan, but never *yōu* (worried) for himself — only for the state. The *Sì-kù* editors take Zēng's *yī fàn bù wàng jūn* (not-forgetting his lord even at one meal) as Dù-Fǔ-comparable.

CBDB id 1692 confirms 1084–1166.

His collection survives as *Chá-shān jí* 茶山集 [[KR4d0192]] in 8 *juǎn* (Sì-kù *Yǒng-lè dà-diǎn* reconstruction; original 30 *juǎn* per Lù Yóu's *mù-zhì*; 15 *juǎn* per *Shū-lù jiě-tí* and *Sòng shǐ Yì-wén-zhì*). Lost works include *Yì shì xiàng* 易釋象 in 5 *juǎn*. The recovered 558 poems are about 60% of the 910-poem total recorded by Liú Kè-zhuāng's *Hòu-cūn shī-huà*.
