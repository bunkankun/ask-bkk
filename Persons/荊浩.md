Late Táng / early Five Dynasties recluse-landscapist; *zì* Hàorán 浩然, a man of Qìnshuǐ 沁水 in Hénán. Withdrew to the *Hónggǔ* 洪谷 valley of the Tàiháng range during the late-Táng disturbances and styled himself *Hónggǔzǐ* 洪谷子. Founding master of the Northern monumental landscape tradition: Liú Dàochún's *Wǔdài mínghuà bǔyí* [[KR3h0014]] places him at the head of the landscape painters, and Tāng Hòu's *Huàjiàn* [[KR3h0037]] calls him "the highest of late Táng" and "ancestor of Fàn Kuān's school." Active c. 880–930. Two treatises on landscape painting circulate under his name — [[KR3h0012]] *Bǐfǎ jì* 筆法記 and the so-called *Huà shānshuǐ fù* 畫山水賦 — but the Sìkù editors regarded both as later pseudepigrapha attaching to his name. Modern scholarship (Munakata 1974) treats the *Bǐfǎ jì* as a tenth- or eleventh-century text preserving the landscape tradition's self-understanding.
