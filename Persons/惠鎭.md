Echin 惠鎭 (1281–1356), also known by his personal name **Enkan** 圓觀, was a Tendai master and political-religious figure of the late Kamakura and Nanboku-chō periods. He served as **Hosshō-ji zasu** 法勝寺座主 and as **abbot of Gennō-ji** 元應寺 — the Tendai temple in the imperial capital (Kyoto) established in 1319 by Emperor Hanazono 花園 and Empress Eifukumon-in 永福門院 as a state-protection institution. He was the personal **Tendai precept-master to Emperor Go-Daigo** 後醍醐天皇 (1288–1339) and is recorded in the *Taiheiki* 太平記 as one of the principal Buddhist participants in the **Genkō Incident** (元弘の變, 1331–1333) — the Go-Daigo–led plot to overthrow the Hōjō regency, in which Echin and his disciples performed *fudō* esoteric rites against the Bakufu.

Echin's doctrinal teaching is rooted in the **Tendai-Esoteric (Taimitsu 台密) synthesis** developed by Ennin and Annen ([[圓仁]], [[安然]]), and he is associated in the medieval Tendai lineage records with the *yuánmì* (round-and-esoteric) doctrinal framework. His [[KR6t0071]] *Yuánmìzōng èrjiào míngmù* is a single-fascicle catechetic nomenclature manual for the *yuánmì* tradition.

Beyond doctrinal works, Echin is also remembered for his role in the **medieval imperial Tendai patronage system** and for his patronage of major Hiei-zan reconstruction efforts during the politically turbulent Kenmu-Restoration and early Nanboku-chō decades. He survived the political collapse of the Go-Daigo regime and died in 1356 at 76.

Within the Kanripo corpus his preserved work is [[KR6t0071]] *Yuánmìzōng èrjiào míngmù*.
