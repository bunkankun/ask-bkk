Chén Qíqīng 陳耆卿 (1180–1236), *zì* Shòulǎo 壽老, *hào* Yúnchuāng 篔窻, native of Nínghǎi 寧海 in Tāizhōu 台州 (modern eastern Zhèjiāng). *Jìnshì* of Jiādìng 7 (1214) and the principal student-and-lineage-bearer of Yè Shì 葉適, founder of the *Yǒngjiā* (永嘉學派) intellectual tradition. His career: 1218 *zhǔbù* of Qīngtiánxiàn 青田縣; 1220 *fǔxué jiàoshòu* of Qìngyuánfǔ 慶元府 (Níngbō); subsequently *jìshì* (private secretary) at the Yíwáng / Jǐwáng establishment of Zhào Hóng 趙竑, the imperial heir designate displaced by Lǐzōng's 1224 succession; and finally *guózǐjiàn sīyè* 國子監司業 at court. His career is not registered in the *Sòng shǐ*; the principal documentation is in his collected works *Yúnchuāng jí* 篔窻集 (10 juan, surviving in the *Sìkù*) and in Xiè Duó's 謝鐸 *Chìchéng xīnzhì* 赤城新志 (Míng). His major historical contribution is the 40-juan *Chìchéng zhì* 赤城志 [[KR2k0017]] — the earliest extant gazetteer of Tāizhōu — which the *Sìkù* tíyào ranks as one of the model exemplars of the Southern Sòng prefectural-gazetteer corpus, attributing its prose quality to his apprenticeship under Yè Shì. Zhào Xībiàn's 趙希弁 *Dúshū fùzhì* 讀書附志 notes that his collected works contain a particular concentration of *jiānbiǎo* drafted for the Yífǔ. CBDB id 10881 confirms 1180–1236.
