**Lú Chén** 盧諶 (284–350), *zì* Zǐliàng 子諒, native of Fànyáng Zhuō-xiàn 范陽涿縣 (in modern Héběi). His standard biography is in *Jìn shū* 44 (列傳 14). He was the grandson of Lú Zhì 盧志 (the leading Western-Jìn *Yuán-kāng* 元康-era statesman and ritualist) and son of Lú Chěn 盧諶's elder brother Lú Yǒu 盧悠. He held office under both the Western Jìn and the Sixteen-Kingdoms northern regimes.

A Western-Jìn *shàng-shū láng* and *zhōng-shū shì-láng* under Sīmǎ Yuè, he was captured by Shí-Lè 石勒 (founder of Hòu-Zhào 後趙) in the Yǒngjiā disaster of 311 and forced into Hòu-Zhào service, where he rose to *zhōng-shū kǎn-jiàn* and *guāng-lù xūn* 光祿勳. His attempt to flee south to the Eastern Jìn court of Yuándì was thwarted, and he served the Hòu-Zhào, Hòu-Yān, and Rǎn Wèi successor states. He died with Rǎn Mǐn 冉閔 in 350, at the age of sixty-seven.

He is principally remembered as one of the finest poets of the Yǒngjiā disaster generation. His *Zèng Liú Kūn* 贈劉琨 (Presented to Liú Kūn) and Liú Kūn's reply *Zhòng zèng Lú Chén* 重贈盧諶 are in the *Wénxuǎn* (24, 25) and are read as the canonical late-Wèi-Jìn poetic exchange. He was also a calligrapher and ritualist. In this corpus he appears as the attributed author of [[KR1d0100]] *Zájìfǎ* — a practical handbook of sacrificial procedures preserved in *Běitáng shūchāo*, *Chūxué jì*, and *Tàipíng yùlǎn* citations.
