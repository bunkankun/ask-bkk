*rGyal-tshab Dar-ma rin-chen* 傑操達瑪仁欽 (1364–1432), commonly known by the honorific title *rGyal-tshab Je* 傑操傑 ("the Lord-Regent"), was the chief disciple and dharma-heir of Tsongkhapa Lobsang Drakpa (1357–1419), the founder of the *dGe-lugs-pa* (Yellow-Hat) school of Tibetan Buddhism. After Tsongkhapa's death in 1419 he succeeded him as the second throne-holder (*dga'-ldan khri-pa*) of *Ganden* monastery — hence "*rGyal-tshab*" "regent." Together with *Khedrup-je* 克主傑 (1385–1438), the third throne-holder, he is known as one of the *rJe-yab-sras gsum* "the Three Father-and-Son Lords" (Tsongkhapa = father, Gyaltsab and Khedrup = sons). His writings, collected in nine volumes of the *bKa'-'bum*, include sub-commentaries on Tsongkhapa's principal works plus extensive commentaries on Indian Mahāyāna treatises — including the *Bodhicaryāvatāra-pañjikā* on Śāntideva's *Bodhicaryāvatāra*, translated into Chinese by Lóng-lián as [[KR6v0044]] *Rù púsà xíng lùn guǎngjiě*.

Note: he should not be confused with the earlier *rGyal-sras Thogs-med bzang-po* (1295–1369), author of a different and earlier *Bodhicaryāvatāra* commentary (the *rGyal-sras lag-len so-bdun-ma*-related corpus).
