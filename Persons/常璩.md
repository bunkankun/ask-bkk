Cháng Qú 常璩 (*c.* 291–361), *zì* Dào-jiāng 道將, of Jiāngyuán 江原 (modern Chóngzhōu 崇州 in west Sìchuān). A senior figure under the short-lived Chéng-Hàn 成漢 dynasty (*Lǐ-shì Shǔ Hàn* 李氏蜀漢, 304–347), where he served as *Sǎn-qí cháng-shì* 散騎常侍 to its last ruler Lǐ Shì 李勢, he was one of the principal court officials who in *Yǒng-hé* 永和 3 (347) urged Lǐ Shì to surrender to the Eastern-Jìn general Huán Wēn 桓溫 (an act for which the Sìkù editors compare him to Qiáo Zhōu 譙周, who likewise advised Liú Shàn to capitulate to Wèi). After the fall of Chéng-Hàn he was relocated to Jiànkāng (modern Nánjīng), where he completed both the *Hàn-zhī shū* 漢之書 (also titled *Shǔ-Lǐ shū* 蜀李書), an internal court history of the Chéng-Hàn dynasty in ten juàn (lost), and his masterpiece, the *Huá-yáng Guó-zhì* 華陽國志 [[KR2i0003]] in twelve juàn — the earliest surviving regional history of southwest China and the first complete *dìfāng-zhì* in Chinese literature. The *Suí-zhì* placed both works in its *Bà-shǐ* 霸史 category. Cháng Qú came from the powerful Jiāng-yuán Cháng 江原常 lineage of the early-Jìn Shǔ scholar-gentry, and the partial Cháng-shì *shì-nǚ-zhì* later reconstructed by Zhāng Jiā-yǔn for the *Huá-yáng Guó-zhì* covers his own kinswomen. The *floruit* in the catalog meta is given as 265–316; the more precise dates 291–361 follow Wilkinson and the standard scholarly consensus and are adopted here.
