Lǐ Hánguāng 李含光 (*hào* Xuánjìngxiānshēng 玄静先生 / 玄靜先生; 683–769), the principal Shàngqīng 上清 patriarch of the mid-Tang and the personal Daoist preceptor of emperor Xuánzōng 玄宗 (r. 712–756). Lǐ was a native of Guǎnglíng 廣陵 (in modern Jiāngsū); he is the thirteenth-generation patriarch of the Shàngqīng / Máoshān 茅山 tradition, following directly from Sīmǎ Chéngzhēn 司馬承禎 (also a Xuánzōng preceptor), in whose lineage he had received initiation in 723. From the *Tiānbǎo* era onwards he resided primarily at Máoshān, where he reorganised the abbey's library and ritual practice; Xuánzōng visited him there in 731 and at the Dàojūngōng 道君宮 audience hall at Cháng'ān. His personal seal at court was sufficient that, on Lǐ's death in 769, the emperor Dàizōng 代宗 personally composed his epitaph.

Lǐ is named in the canonical record as the editor and *xù*-writer of several major Tang Daoist works: the *Tàishàng cíbēi dàochǎng xiāozāi jiǔyōu chàn* [[KR5b0246]] (DZ 543; preface dated to his Máoshān years); the *Sānyuán pǐnjiè jīng* (DZ 456); and the now-lost *Xuánjìng wénjí* 玄静文集 in 30 *juǎn*. His honorific Xuánjìngxiānshēng was granted by Xuánzōng. The source rubric of DZ 543 spells his name 李舎光 by graphical slip from 含光; the *Xuánjìng xiānshēng* title (uniquely Lǐ's) confirms the identification. Lǐ is the principal mid-Tang exemplar of the close imperial-Daoist preceptor relationship that characterised Xuánzōng's reign; the parallel Buddhist case is the Vajrayāna master Bùkōng 不空 (Amoghavajra).
