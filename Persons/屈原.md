**Qū Yuán** 屈原 (traditional dates ca. 343–278 BCE), personal name Qū Píng 屈平, *zì* Líng jūn 靈均 (in the *Lí sāo*: "the name they gave me was Zhèng zé 正則, the *zì* they styled me Líng jūn"). Aristocratic minister of the southern state of Chǔ 楚 under King Huái 楚懷王 (r. 328–299 BCE) and King Qǐngxiāng 楚頃襄王 (r. 298–263 BCE), traditionally credited as the founding voice of the *Chǔ cí* 楚辭 anthology and as the author of *Lí sāo* 離騷, *Tiān wèn* 天問, the *Jiǔ zhāng* 九章 group, most of the *Jiǔ gē* 九歌, and (variously) *Yuǎn yóu* 遠遊, *Bǔ jū* 卜居, *Yú fù* 漁父, and *Zhāo hún* 招魂.

The earliest connected biographical narrative is Sīmǎ Qiān's *Shǐ jì* 84 (*Qū Yuán Jiǎ Shēng liè zhuàn* 屈原賈生列傳), which casts him as a loyal Chǔ minister slandered by court rivals, exiled south of the Yangzi, and ultimately drowning himself in the Mìluó 汨羅 river — a death conventionally placed on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month (the origin myth of the *duānwǔ* 端午 festival). The historical Qū Yuán is, however, almost entirely a Hàn editorial construct: no pre-Hàn source names him, the Sīmǎ Qiān biography embeds two contradictory dating sequences, and modern philology (Schneider 1980; Du 2019) treats Qū Yuán as a figure assembled by Liú Xiàng and Wáng Yì to anchor the *Chǔ cí* anthology around a virtuous-minister archetype.

In the Kanripo corpus the body of poetry attributed to him forms the core of [[KR4a0001]] *Chǔ cí*, with the standard commentaries [[KR4a0002]] (Wáng Yì), [[KR4a0003]] (Hóng Xīngzǔ), [[KR4a0004]] (Zhū Xī), [[KR4a0005]] (Wú Rénjié), [[KR4a0007]] (Jiǎng Jì) all built on his persona.
