Yīn Jìngxián 陰勁弦 (fl. late 13th–early 14th c.), also recorded as Yīn Shíyù 陰時遇 (where Shíyù is given name and Jìngxián is the alternate); *zì* Shífū 時夫. Native of Fèngxīn 奉新 in Jiāngxī. He came from a family that had lived together as one *jiā* for several generations and held the *Bǎoyòu* (1253–1258) *jiǔjīng kē* 九經科 of the late Southern Sòng. After the Yuán conquest he refused service and lived in eremitic retreat. His chief work is the *Yùnfǔ qúnyù* 韻府群玉 ([[KR3k0039]]), the foundational *yùnfǔ* (rhyme-treasury) reference work, compiled with the editorial collaboration of his elder brother Yīn Fùchūn 陰復春 (*zì* Zhōngfū 中夫). The work was widely used through the Míng and Qīng and was a principal source for the Kāngxī imperial *Pèiwén yùnfǔ*.
