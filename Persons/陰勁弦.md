Yīn Jìng-xián 陰勁弦 (fl. late 13th–early 14th c.), also recorded as Yīn Shí-yù 陰時遇 (where Shí-yù is given name and Jìng-xián is the alternate); *zì* Shí-fū 時夫. Native of Fèng-xīn 奉新 in Jiāng-xī. He came from a family that had lived together as one *jiā* for several generations and held the *Bǎo-yòu* (1253–1258) *jiǔ-jīng kē* 九經科 of the late Southern Sòng. After the Yuán conquest he refused service and lived in eremitic retreat. His chief work is the *Yùn-fǔ qún-yù* 韻府群玉 ([[KR3k0039]]), the foundational *yùn-fǔ* (rhyme-treasury) reference work, compiled with the editorial collaboration of his elder brother Yīn Fù-chūn 陰復春 (*zì* Zhōng-fū 中夫). The work was widely used through the Míng and Qīng and was a principal source for the Kāng-xī imperial *Pèi-wén yùn-fǔ*.
