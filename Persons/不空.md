The most influential Indian Tantric master in Chinese history; **Amoghavajra** (Bù-kōng 不空 = "Indestructible"; also 不空金剛 Bù-kōng Vajra = Amoghavajra). One of the **Three Great Tantric Masters** (三大士) of Kāi-yuán Tángmì alongside **Śubhakarasiṃha** 善無畏 and his master **Vajrabodhi** [[金剛智]]. Lifedates **705 – 774** (born **神龍元年 = 705**, died Dà-lì 9 = 774). Native of **North India** (some sources Sogdiana) — accompanied his uncle to Java as a youth, then to China where he met Vajrabodhi at fifteen. Returned briefly to India and Sri Lanka (741–746) for further Tantric initiation. Returned to Chang-an in 746 with new texts and apparatus.

Served as imperial Tantric tutor under **Xuán-zōng**, **Sù-zōng**, and **Dài-zōng** — three successive emperors. Court titles included **Kāi-fǔ yí-tóng sān-sī, tè-jìn shì Hóng-lú-qīng, Sù-guó-gōng, shí-yì sān-qiān-hù, cì zǐ zèng Sī-kōng, shì Dà-zhèng-jiān, hào Dà-guǎng-zhì** 開府儀同三司特進試鴻臚卿肅國公食邑三千戶賜紫贈司空謚大正監號大廣智 — the most elevated court titles ever conferred on a foreign monk in Táng China. Resident at **Dà-xìng-shàn-sì** 大興善寺.

Translated 110+ texts including the **Vajraśekhara cycle**, the **Mahāvairocana-sūtra** apparatus, and the **Lǐ-qù-jīng** ([[KR6c0120]] *Dà-lè jīn-gāng bù-kōng zhēn-shí sān-mó-yē jīng* 大樂金剛不空真實三麼耶經, T8 no. 243) — the most influential Chinese rendering of the *Adhyardhaśatikā Prajñāpāramitā* and a foundational text of Japanese **Shingon** Buddhism via Kūkai's transmission. Established the formal Tángmì lineage continued by his disciples **Huì-guǒ** 惠果, **Hán-guāng** 含光, and others; the Japanese Shingon school traces its lineage through Kūkai (空海) → Huì-guǒ → Bù-kōng.
