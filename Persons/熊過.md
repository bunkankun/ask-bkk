Xióng Guò 熊過, zì Shūrén 叔仁, hào Nánshā 南沙, was a mid-Míng *Yìjīng* scholar and official from Fùshùn 富順 (Xùzhōu 敘州, modern Sìchuān 四川). He passed the *jìnshì* examination in *Jiājìng jǐchǒu* 嘉靖己丑 = 1529 and rose to Director of the Sacrifices and Offerings Bureau in the Ministry of Rites (Lǐbù cíjì sī lángzhōng 禮部祠祭司郎中).

His self-preface to the *Zhōuyì xiàngzhǐ juélù* 周易象旨決錄 ([[KR1a0098]]) records that he was exiled to Yúnnán 滇 in *xīnchǒu* 辛丑 = 1541 and there encountered Yáng Shèn 楊慎 (1488–1559, the great late-Míng polymath also exiled to Yúnnán); it was Yáng who first encouraged him to bring the work to completion. Xióng's *Yì*-method was a deliberate revolt against the Sòng *yìlǐ* tradition: having first read the Sòng commentaries and found them unsatisfying, he turned to Hàn *xiàngshù* and made the *xiàng* 象 (symbol) the central focus of his exegesis. The *Sìkù* editors regard him as the most prominent of the Míng-period Hàn-revivalist *Yì*-scholars, although they note that his philological work, while careful, occasionally relied on dubious sources (Guō Jīng 郭京 and Wú Chéng 吳澄 in particular).

CBDB has no dates for him; standard biographical sources also leave them open. Active dates can be inferred from his 1529 *jìnshì* and 1541 Yúnnán exile to circa 1500–1560.
