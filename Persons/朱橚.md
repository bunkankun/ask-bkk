Zhū Sù 朱橚 (1361–1425), 5th son of Míng-tài-zǔ Zhū Yuánzhāng. Enfeoffed Prince of Zhōu 周王; later renamed Prince of Dìng 定王. Famed early-Míng botanist and pharmacognosist. Major works: *Jiù huāng běn cǎo* 救荒本草 (KR3d0006, 1406, the famine-emergency edible-plant pharmacopoeia, illustrating 414 plants) and *Pǔ jì fāng* 普濟方 (the world's largest pre-modern medical formulary, 168 juan, 61,739 prescriptions). His agricultural-botanical work — undertaken during his exile to Yún-nán in 1389–1391 and his subsequent Kāi-fēng tenure — is methodologically pioneering in pre-modern Chinese applied botany.
