Liáng critic and poet, *zì* Zhòng-wěi 仲偉, of Yǐng-chuān 潁川 Cháng-shè 長社 (modern Cháng-gě 長葛, Hé-nán). With his elder brother Zhōng Yán 鍾岏 and younger brother Zhōng Yǔ 鍾嶼 he was renowned for learning. During the Southern Qí *Yǒng-míng* 永明 era (483–493) he entered the *Guó-zǐ jiān* (Imperial Academy); under Wáng Jiǎn's 王儉 sponsorship he was selected *xiù-cái* of his native province and rose to *Wáng-guó shì-láng* (attendant in a princely establishment). Crossing into the Liáng he served successively as *Wáng-guó shì-láng*, *Wǎn-líng-lìng*, and finally as *Jì-shì* (recorder) to the Prince of Jìn-ān 晉安王 (the later Liáng Jiǎn-wén-dì 簡文帝 — though held under the prince during his Jīng-zhōu commandery, not yet on the throne); he died in office. His brother Yán was author of *Liáng-shū* 49 records his biography; *Nán shǐ* 72 records both brothers.

Zhōng's *Shīpǐn* 詩品 [[KR4i0003]] (completed c. 513–517), the first systematic critical evaluation of Chinese poetry from the Hàn down to his own day, ranks 122 (variant 123) poets in five-character verse into upper, middle, and lower grades, prefacing each grade with a discursive introduction. It is — together with Liú Xié's [[劉勰]] *Wénxīn diāolóng* [[KR4i0001]] — one of the two foundational works of Chinese literary criticism. Wilkinson dates Zhōng to ca. 468–518; CBDB does not record him; his lifedates derive from his official career and from the *Liáng shū* notice. Western scholarship: Wixted in *Early Medieval Chinese Texts: A Bibliographical Guide* (IEAS, Berkeley, 2015); Brigitta Lee, *Imitation, Remembrance and the Formation of the Poetic Past: Studies in the Early Medieval Chinese Reception of Tao Yuanming* (Stanford, 2007); and Yeh's translations in Wong Siu-kit and Lo (1996).
