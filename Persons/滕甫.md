Téng Fǔ 滕甫 / Téng Yuánfā 滕元發 (1020–1090), zì Dádào 達道, posthumous title Zhāngmǐn 章敏, of *Dōngyáng* 東陽 (Wúzhōu 婺州, modern Zhejiang). His original courtesy name was *Yuánfā* 元發; he later took *Yuánfā* as his given name and adopted *Dádào* as courtesy name in order to avoid the personal-name taboo of the imperial relative *Gāo-Lǔwáng* 高魯王 (Zhào Yǔnbì 趙允弼, posthumously Gāo-Lǔwáng) — and then resumed his original name *Yuánfā* once the taboo lapsed. He passed the *jìnshì* and rose through Northern Sòng officialdom: *Tōngpàn Húzhōu* (in which capacity he composed the *Zhēngnán lù* [[KR2g0053]], probably 1052–54), then *Zhī Hángzhōu*, *Hànlín Xuéshì*, and ultimately *Lóngtúgé Xuéshì*. His career spanned the Rénzōng-Yīngzōng-Shénzōng-Zhézōng reigns. Téng's reputation rests primarily on his political alignment with the *Bāo Zhěng* / Sīmǎ Guāng moderate-conservative faction during the Wáng Ānshí reform years, his prefectural administration in Lǐngnán during the Nóng Zhìgāo crisis, and his vindication of his patron Sūn Miǎn 孫沔 in the *Zhēngnán lù*. His full biography is in *Sòng shǐ* j.332.
