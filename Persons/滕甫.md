Téng Fǔ 滕甫 / Téng Yuánfā 滕元發 (1020–1090), zì Dádào 達道, posthumous title Zhāngmǐn 章敏, of *Dōng-yáng* 東陽 (Wúzhōu 婺州, modern Zhejiang). His original courtesy name was *Yuán-fā* 元發; he later took *Yuán-fā* as his given name and adopted *Dá-dào* as courtesy name in order to avoid the personal-name taboo of the imperial relative *Gāo-Lǔ-wáng* 高魯王 (Zhào Yǔn-bì 趙允弼, posthumously Gāo-Lǔ-wáng) — and then resumed his original name *Yuán-fā* once the taboo lapsed. He passed the *jìn-shì* and rose through Northern Sòng officialdom: *Tōng-pàn Húzhōu* (in which capacity he composed the *Zhēng-nán lù* [[KR2g0053]], probably 1052–54), then *Zhī Hángzhōu*, *Hàn-lín Xué-shì*, and ultimately *Lóng-tú-gé Xué-shì*. His career spanned the Rénzōng-Yīngzōng-Shénzōng-Zhézōng reigns. Téng's reputation rests primarily on his political alignment with the *Bāo Zhěng* / Sī-mǎ Guāng moderate-conservative faction during the Wáng Ān-shí reform years, his prefectural administration in Lǐng-nán during the Nóng Zhì-gāo crisis, and his vindication of his patron Sūn Miǎn 孫沔 in the *Zhēng-nán lù*. His full biography is in *Sòng shǐ* j.332.
