Dù Guāngtíng 杜光庭 (*zì* Bīnshèng 賓聖; *hào* Guǎngchéng xiānshēng 廣成先生), native of Chǔzhōu 處州 (modern Zhèjiāng), was the most prolific and institutionally central Daoist author of the late Táng and Former-Shǔ 前蜀 period. Originally a *jìnshì* candidate who failed the examinations, he entered Tiāntái shān 天台山 as a disciple of the Shàngqīng master Yīng Yíjié 應夷節 in his twenties. He rose to prominence at the Táng court under Xīzōng 僖宗, serving as ritual officiant and *yǐnjià* 引駕 preceptor; when Xīzōng fled Cháng'ān to Chéngdū during the Huáng Cháo 黃巢 rebellion (880), Dù accompanied him and subsequently remained in Shǔ under the Former-Shǔ founder Wáng Jiàn 王建 and his son Wáng Yǎn 王衍. Wáng Yǎn granted him the title *Chuánzhēn tiānshī* 傳真天師 and the honorific *Guǎngchéng xiānshēng* 廣成先生 at a reciprocal-investiture ceremony of 923.

Dù's œuvre reshapes virtually every genre of medieval Daoist writing: doctrinal commentary (*Dàodé zhēnjīng guǎngshèngyì* 道德真經廣聖義), hagiography (*Lìdài chóngdào jì* 歷代崇道記, *Yōngchéng jí xiānlù* 墉城集仙錄), Daoist topography (*Dòngtiān fúdì yuèdú míngshān jì* 洞天福地嶽瀆名山記), ritual (*Tàishàng huánglù zhāiyí* 太上黃籙齋儀 and numerous other liturgies), miracle-tale compilation (*Dàojiào língyànjì* 道教靈驗記), and editorial recensions of earlier scriptures — including the definitive twenty-*juàn* recension of the *Dòngyuān shénzhòu jīng* [[KR5b0019]] to which he supplied the preface narrating its purported revelation to Wáng Zuǎn on Mǎjī shān. The standard monograph is Franciscus Verellen, *Du Guangting (850–933): Taoïste de cour à la fin de la Chine médiévale* (Paris, 1989).
