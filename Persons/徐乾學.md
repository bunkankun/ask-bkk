Xú Qiánxué 徐乾學 (1631–1694). *Zì* Yuányī 原一, *hào* Jiàn'ān 健菴. Native of Kūnshān 崑山 (modern Kūnshān, Jiāngsū). *Jìnshì* of Kāngxī *gēngxū* (Kāngxī 9 = 1670), ranked second (*bǎngyǎn* 榜眼) in the palace examination. Career: rose through Hànlin posts, *Lǐbù shìláng* 禮部侍郎, *Dūcháyuàn zuǒ-dū-yùshǐ* 都察院左都御史, ultimately *Xíngbù shàngshū* 刑部尚書 (Minister of Justice). Removed from office in the Kāngxī 28 (1689) impeachment of his patronage faction; spent his last five years in private scholarship.

Owner of one of the most important private libraries of the early Qīng — the *Chuán-shì lóu* 傳是樓 — second to none in his era. Major patron of the leading early-Qīng evidential scholars, including Yán Ruòqú 閻若璩 (1636–1704), who was a member of his household scholarly circle and contributed substantially to his major projects.

Author of [[KR1d0051]] *Dú-lǐ tōng-kǎo* 讀禮通考 in 120 juan — the most comprehensive Qīng treatise on Chinese mourning ritual ever composed, organising material under eight major rubrics: *sāngqī* (mourning periods), *sāngfú* (mourning garments), *sāng-yíjié* (mourning procedures), *zàng-kǎo* (burial investigations), *sāng-jù* (mourning implements), *biàn-lǐ* (variant rituals), *sāng-zhì* (mourning regulations), and *miào-zhì* (temple regulations). The work was begun during Xú's mother's mourning period and refined over a decade-plus of three drafts; published posthumously. The Sìkù *tíyào* characterises the work as "this Heaven-and-Earth's indispensable book" and explicitly identifies Qín Huìtián's 秦蕙田 *Wǔ-lǐ tōng-kǎo* 五禮通考 as a direct continuation of Xú's project.
