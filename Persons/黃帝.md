**Huáng dì** 黃帝 / **Xuān-yuán Huáng-dì** 軒轅黃帝 — the Yellow Emperor, mythical sage-ruler of pre-historic China, conventionally placed in the 27th-century BCE, founding-ancestor of Daoism and traditional Chinese medicine. As a Daoist patriarch he is the receiver of the *Way* from Guǎng-chéng-zǐ at Mt. Kōng-tóng (cf. *Zhuāng zǐ* "Zài-yòu" 在宥); the founding revealer-author of the *Yīn fú jīng* ([[KR5i0031]]–[[KR5i0033]]); the recipient of the Five-Sprout-Three-One transmission from Tiān-zhēn Huáng-rén at Mt. Émēi; the ascending Daoist who bequeathed the *Huáng dì nèi jīng* 黃帝內經 medical canon and the *Huáng dì jiǔ dǐng shén dān jīng* 黃帝九鼎神丹經 outer-alchemy classic. Numerous works of late-imperial Daoism are conventionally pseudepigraphically attributed to him, including [[KR5i0029]] *Jīn-bì lóng-hǔ shàng-jīng* (whose Yellow Emperor attribution is denied by Péng Hào-gǔ in his preface). Standard reference: Lewis, *The Construction of Space in Early China* (SUNY 2006), ch. on the Yellow-Emperor mythography; LeBlanc, *Huai-nan Tzu: Philosophical Synthesis* (HKU 1985), on the Han-era cult.
