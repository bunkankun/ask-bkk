Empress Rén-xiào 仁孝皇后 (1362–1407, posthumous title Rén-xiào Wén Huáng-hòu 仁孝文皇后), née Xú 徐, principal consort of the Yǒng-lè 永樂 emperor (Míng Chéng-zǔ 明成祖, Zhū Dì 朱棣, r. 1402–1424). Eldest daughter of the founding-Míng general Xú Dá 徐達 (1332–1385), enfeoffed Duke of Wèi 魏國公; married Zhū Dì in 1376, when Zhū Dì was Prince of Yān 燕王; entitled Empress in 1402 on Zhū Dì's accession to the throne after the Jìng-nán 靖難 campaign.

She was a notably devout Buddhist throughout her life and patronised major Buddhist publishing and ritual projects. Her own writings include the *Quàn-shàn shū* 勸善書 (a moral admonition-text in five fascicles) and the [[KR6i0580]] *Dà-Míng Rén-xiào Huánghòu mèng-gǎn Fó-shuō dì-yī xī-yǒu dà-gōng-dé jīng* 大明仁孝皇后夢感佛說第一希有大功德經 (X01 no. 010), a Mahāyāna sūtra she described as having received in a dream-vision on the first day of Hóng-wǔ 31 (1398). She died Yǒng-lè 5 / 7 / 4 (= 6 August 1407), aged 46. Her tomb, the Cháng-líng 長陵, is the principal mausoleum of the Míng tombs (十三陵) at Tiānshòu-shān 天壽山 outside Beijing; the Yǒng-lè emperor was buried beside her on his death in 1424.

Sources: *Míng shǐ* 明史 j. 113 (Hòu-fēi zhuàn), the *Quàn-shàn shū*'s preface, and the prefatory matter of the *Mèng-gǎn jīng*.
