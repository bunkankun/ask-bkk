Late-Míng Buddhist monk, principal disciple of **Yúnqī Zhūhóng** 雲棲袾宏 ([[袾宏]], 1535–1615), the most influential master of the late-Míng Pure-Land/Chán synthesis. Sobriquet **Sìkōng** 似空 ("apparent emptiness"); commonly cited as **Yúnqī Sìkōng** 雲棲似空. Lifedates unrecorded; active in the years immediately before and after his master's death (the *Jīngāng jīng bēi* preface of [[KR6c0063]] is dated 1618, three years after Yúnqī's death). DILA characterizes him as 法門龍象，親承提命 ("a dragon-elephant of the dharma-gate, personally receiving the master's instructions"); commissioned by Yúnqī to undertake the *Jīngāng* commentary, *gōu xuán zuǎn yào, què yī shān fán, jí zhū jiā zhī dà chéng* 鈎玄纂要，搉一刪繁，集諸家之大成 ("hooking the profound and gathering the essential, weighing one against another and pruning the profuse, gathering a great compendium of the various schools"). Listed among Yúnqī's "topmost disciples" (*shàngshǒu dìzǐ*) in *Bāshíbā zǔ dàoyǐng zhuànzàn* j. 4 ([[KR6q0045]]) by Hānshān Déqīng [[德清]]. His sole surviving work is the two-juan *Jīngāng jīng bēi* 金剛經鎞 ([[KR6c0063]], X25 no. 475).
