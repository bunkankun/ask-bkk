Late-Míng Buddhist monk, principal disciple of **Yún-qī Zhū-hóng** 雲棲袾宏 ([[袾宏]], 1535–1615), the most influential master of the late-Míng Pure-Land/Chán synthesis. Sobriquet **Sì-kōng** 似空 ("apparent emptiness"); commonly cited as **Yún-qī Sì-kōng** 雲棲似空. Lifedates unrecorded; active in the years immediately before and after his master's death (the *Jīn-gāng jīng bēi* preface of [[KR6c0063]] is dated 1618, three years after Yún-qī's death). DILA characterizes him as 法門龍象，親承提命 ("a dragon-elephant of the dharma-gate, personally receiving the master's instructions"); commissioned by Yún-qī to undertake the *Jīn-gāng* commentary, *gōu xuán zuǎn yào, què yī shān fán, jí zhū jiā zhī dà chéng* 鈎玄纂要，搉一刪繁，集諸家之大成 ("hooking the profound and gathering the essential, weighing one against another and pruning the profuse, gathering a great compendium of the various schools"). Listed among Yún-qī's "topmost disciples" (*shàng-shǒu dì-zǐ*) in *Bā-shí-bā zǔ dào-yǐng zhuàn-zàn* j. 4 ([[KR6q0045]]) by Hān-shān Dé-qīng [[德清]]. His sole surviving work is the two-juan *Jīn-gāng jīng bēi* 金剛經鎞 ([[KR6c0063]], X25 no. 475).
