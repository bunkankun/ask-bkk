The **Sixth Patriarch (六祖) of Chinese Chán Buddhism** and the foundational figure of the **Southern Chán school**. Lay surname **Lú** 盧; native of **Xīn-zhōu** 新州 in **Lǐng-nán** 嶺南 (modern southern Guǎng-dōng). Born **638 CE**; died **713 CE**, age 76, at **Cáo-xī** 曹溪 in northern Guǎng-dōng.

He was the dharma-heir of the **Fifth Patriarch Hóng-rěn** 弘忍 (601–674) — receiving the patriarchal *yī-bāo* 衣鉢 (robe-and-bowl) transmission, per the foundational legend, in a poetry-contest with the senior disciple **Shén-xiù** 神秀 (606?–706) — and established the **Southern Chán** lineage at **Bǎo-lín-sì** 寶林寺 (later renamed **Nán-huá-sì** 南華寺) on **Cáo-xī** mountain. His enlightenment is famously narrated as occurring upon hearing the *Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā-sūtra*'s passage "*Where the heart abides nowhere, there it should abide*".

The **Liù-zǔ tán-jīng** 六祖壇經 (*Platform Sutra of the Sixth Patriarch*, Taishō 2007 / 2008) is the principal source for his life and teaching — itself a foundational Chán scripture. The text exists in multiple recensions including the early Dunhuang manuscripts and the standardized late-Tang / Sòng versions.

His doctrinal-meditative contribution is the **Southern Chán "sudden awakening"** (頓悟 *dùn-wù*) doctrine — distinguished from the **Northern Chán "gradual cultivation"** (漸修 *jiàn-xiū*) of the Shén-xiù lineage. Through his successors — particularly **Nán-yuè Huái-ràng** 南嶽懷讓 (677–744) and **Qīng-yuán Xíng-sī** 青原行思 (671–740) — the Southern Chán lineage gave rise to the **Five Houses** (五家) of mature Tang Chán: **Línjì** 臨濟, **Cáo-dòng** 曹洞, **Yún-mén** 雲門, **Fǎ-yǎn** 法眼, **Wěi-yǎng** 溈仰. All subsequent Chinese, Korean, Vietnamese, and Japanese Chán / Sŏn / Thiền / Zen lineages descend from his Southern Chán transmission.

He was posthumously titled **Dà-jiàn chán-shī** 大鑑禪師 ("Great Mirror Chán Master") by **Tang Xiàn-zōng** 憲宗 in **815 CE**, after the famous epitaph composed by **Liǔ Zōng-yuán** 柳宗元.

The two *Vajracchedikā* commentaries attributed to him in the Buddhist canon ([[KR6c0047]]–[[KR6c0048]]) are of **modern-scholarly disputed authenticity** — likely Sòng-period Chán-school compositions attached to his name to lend the foundational patriarchal authority.
