The signature-line attribution of the late-Táng / Five-Dynasties *Shí-wáng jīng* 十王經 ("Ten Kings of the Underworld") corpus, two of whose constituent texts are preserved in the canon: [[KR6i0583]] *Fó-shuō Dìzàng Púsà fāxīn yīnyuán Shí-wáng jīng* (X01 no. 020) and [[KR6i0584]] *Fó-shuō yùxiū Shí-wáng shēng-qī jīng* (X01 no. 021). The attributions identify him as *Chéngdū Mádà Shèng-cí'ēn-sì shā-mén* 成都麻大聖慈恩寺沙門 ("a *śramaṇa* of the Mádà Shèng-cí'ēn-sì in Chéngdū"), referring to the Shèng-cí-sì 大聖慈寺 (also commonly named *Dà Shèng-cí'ēn-sì* 大聖慈恩寺), one of the most prominent Táng-period Sichuan monasteries (founded under Xuanzong, expanded under late-Táng emperors). His historicity is uncertain: modern scholarship (esp. Stephen Teiser, *The Scripture on the Ten Kings*, 1994) treats *Cángchuān* as most plausibly a literary persona constructed within the *Shí-wáng* tradition rather than a historically attested individual monk. The Sichuan-monastic context of the *Shí-wáng* corpus is, however, well-attested through late-Táng iconographic and inscriptional sources. Lifedates not securely recorded; the active period within which the texts were composed is bracketed at *circa* 800–950.
