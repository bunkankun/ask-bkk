Kamalaśīla 蓮華戒 (Sanskrit *Kamalaśīla* "Lotus-Conduct"; honorifically *bodhisattva* / 菩薩 in Sòng Chinese transmissions; c. 740–795) was a major late-Indian Buddhist philosopher of the Madhyamaka school, pupil of Śāntarakṣita and his successor as the leading representative of the *Yogācāra-Svātantrika-Madhyamaka* synthesis. He is principally known to history for two contributions: (1) his **trilogy of *Bhāvanā-krama*** ("Stages of Cultivation"), the first of which survives in Sanskrit, all three in Tibetan translation, and a parallel/related work in Chinese as the *Guǎng-shì pútí-xīn lùn* 廣釋菩提心論 ([[KR6o0069]], translated by [[施護]] in the Sòng); and (2) his role as the principal Indian disputant at the **bSam-yas debate** (792–794) at the court of the Tibetan king Khri-srong-lde-btsan, where he defended the gradualist position of the Indian Mahāyāna against the Chinese Chan master Mo-ho-yen 摩訶衍's instantaneous-awakening doctrine. According to traditional Tibetan sources, Kamalaśīla was assassinated in Tibet in 795 by partisans of the defeated Chinese side. His other major works are commentaries on Śāntarakṣita's *Tattvasaṃgraha* (the *Tattvasaṃgraha-pañjikā*) and on the *Madhyamaka-ālaṃkāra*. The *Bhāvanā-krama* trilogy is the principal documentary witness to the Madhyamaka position on graduated practice; the Chinese *Guǎng-shì pútí-xīn lùn* is one of the very few works of Kamalaśīla preserved outside the Tibetan tradition.
