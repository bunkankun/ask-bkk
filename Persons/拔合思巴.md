ʼPhags-pa Lama (Tibetan *Chos-rgyal-ʼphags-pa*; Chinese 拔合思巴 / 八思巴 / 癹思巴), 1235-04-02 to 1280-12-22, was the **Fifth Patriarch of the Sa-skya** 薩迦派 sect of Tibetan Buddhism and the dominant Buddhist authority of the early Yuán empire. Born into the Sa-skya family of southern Tibet on the sixth day of the third month of *Duān-píng* 2 (= 2 April 1235); died on the twenty-second day of the eleventh month of *Zhì-yuán* 17 (= 22 December 1280).

In 1253 (aged 19) he was summoned to the court of *Khubilai* 忽必烈 (later Yuán Shì-zǔ) and conferred the *bodhisattva-precepts* on the future emperor. Upon Khubilai's enthronement (1260), he was created **Imperial Preceptor** (*Dì-shī* 帝師) and given charge of **Buddhist affairs throughout the empire** and of *Tibetan-region administration* (*Xuān-zhèng-yuàn* 宣政院 jurisdiction). His standing as the *de facto* head of Yuán Buddhism was unmatched. By imperial commission, he created the **ʼPhags-pa script** (*bā-sī-bā wén* 八思巴文) in 1269 — a writing system based on Tibetan letterforms, designed to write Mongolian, Chinese, Tibetan, Sanskrit, and Uyghur — for the imperial chancery.

His extant Chinese-canon works include the *Zhāng suǒ-zhī lùn* 彰所知論 (T1645), the *Gēn-běn shuō yī-qiè yǒu-bù chū-jiā shòu jìn-yuán jié-mó yí-fàn* ([[KR6k0191]], T1904), and the *Gēn-běn shuō yī-qiè yǒu-bù bì-chú xí-xué lüè-fǎ* ([[KR6k0192]], T1905). The Chinese versions of the latter two were prepared at the Yuán court for the introduction of *Mūlasarvāstivāda* ordination procedure into Mongol-imperial Buddhist practice. His chief Chinese disciple was **Shā-luó-bā** 沙囉巴 (DILA A000529).
