Mid-Míng religious thinker and founder of the **Sānyī jiào** 三一教 ("Three-in-One Teaching") religious movement; the most successful of the late-Míng *sānjiào héyī* 三教合一 syncretists. *Zì* **Màoxūn** 懋勳, sobriquets **Lóngjiāng** 龍江, **Sānjiào zhǔrén** 三教主人 / **Sānyī jiàozhǔ** 三一教主, **Zǐgǔzǐ** 子谷子. Born **Zhèngdé 12 = 1517** in **Pútián** 莆田 (Fújiàn) into a prosperous gentry family; renounced an examination career in 1551 to pursue a syncretic religious vocation. Taught a mature synthesis in which Confucian moral cultivation, Daoist inner-alchemical practice (*nèidān*), and Buddhist meditative insight were harmonized as three aspects of a single **Confucian-grounded** *xìng* 性 ("inborn nature"); developed a **nine-stage cultivation system** culminating in the *jiǔxù xīnfǎ* 九序心法.

Built a large devotional movement centered on the **Sānyī Cí** 三一祠 in Pútián, with extensive lay and gentry following across Fújiàn, Jiāngnán, and southeast China. The movement survived him and continues today in southeastern China, Taiwan, and overseas Fujianese diaspora communities. Composed extensive treatises on the three teachings and a series of commentaries on each tradition's key texts; the *Jīngāng jīng tǒnglùn* 金剛經統論 ([[KR6c0064]], X25 no. 476), in which he reads the *Vajracchedikā* through Sānyī cosmogony, is one of his Buddhist-side compositions. Died **1598** (Wànlì 26).

Standard study: Kenneth Dean, *Lord of the Three in One: The Spread of a Cult in Southeast China* (Princeton UP, 1998); also Judith Berling, *The Syncretic Religion of Lin Chao-en* (Columbia UP, 1980). DILA A000853.
