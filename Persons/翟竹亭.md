**Zhái Zhútíng** 翟竹亭 (b. *guāngxù* yǐmǎo, 1875; death date undocumented), *hào* Húyuè cūnsǒu 湖岳村叟 (Old Villager of Hú [Lǐ Bīnhú = [[李時珍]]] and Yuè [Zhāng Jǐngyuè = [[張介賓]]]), of **Qǐxiàn 杞縣, Hénán**. The lineage's ancestral home was Hóngdòng 洪洞 in Shānxī; the family had been relocated to Qǐxiàn in the early Hóngwǔ reign of the Míng, later moved to Dàzhài village in Chángyuán 長垣 (Zhílì), and returned to Qǐxiàn after the *xiánfēng* rénzǐ (1852) Yellow River breach at **Tóngwǎxiāng 銅瓦廂**.

Though he had no formal schooling, he was a disciplined autodidact. At 16 he studied medicine under **You Xiàngēng 尤憲庚**, a Tōngxǔ 通許 physician then practising at the Qǐxiàn City God Temple, who taught him pulse-theory and acupuncture. After five years he was practising independently. His scholarly orientation was the Jīn-Yuán four masters, the Míng-period epidemic-disease theorist **[[吳有性]] Wú Yǒuxìng** (Wú Yǒukě), the *Běncǎo gāngmù* tradition of **[[李時珍]] Lǐ Shízhēn** (Lǐ Bīnhú), and the *Lèijīng* / orthodox-internal-medicine tradition of **[[張介賓]] Zhāng Jièbīn** (Zhāng Jǐngyuè); the latter two physicians supplied his self-chosen *hào*.

After four decades of rural practice in eastern Hénán — a region that was the most epidemic-prone area of late-Qīng / Republican-period north China — he compiled his selected cases as the [[KR3ep104]] *Húyuè cūnsǒu yīàn* 湖岳村叟醫案 (2 *juǎn*, 17 categories, 237 cases), revised jointly by his friends Wèi Guìsēn 衛桂森, Jiāng Jiāmò 江家謨, and Mèng Jìhóng 孟繼鴻, proof-read by his disciples Chén Wényuán 陳文元 and Shī Hépǔ 史和璞, and finally collated and printed by Zhāng Wénfù 張文甫 of Kāifēng Medical College in 1963.
