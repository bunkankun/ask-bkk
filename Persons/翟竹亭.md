**Zhái Zhú-tíng** 翟竹亭 (b. *guāng-xù* yǐ-mǎo, 1875; death date undocumented), *hào* Hú-yuè cūn-sǒu 湖岳村叟 (Old Villager of Hú [Lǐ Bīn-hú = [[李時珍]]] and Yuè [Zhāng Jǐng-yuè = [[張介賓]]]), of **Qǐ-xiàn 杞縣, Hé-nán**. The lineage's ancestral home was Hóng-dòng 洪洞 in Shānxī; the family had been relocated to Qǐ-xiàn in the early Hóng-wǔ reign of the Míng, later moved to Dà-zhài village in Cháng-yuán 長垣 (Zhí-lì), and returned to Qǐ-xiàn after the *xián-fēng* rén-zǐ (1852) Yellow River breach at **Tóng-wǎ-xiāng 銅瓦廂**.

Though he had no formal schooling, he was a disciplined autodidact. At 16 he studied medicine under **You Xiàn-gēng 尤憲庚**, a Tōng-xǔ 通許 physician then practising at the Qǐ-xiàn City God Temple, who taught him pulse-theory and acupuncture. After five years he was practising independently. His scholarly orientation was the Jīn-Yuán four masters, the Míng-period epidemic-disease theorist **[[吳有性]] Wú Yǒu-xìng** (Wú Yǒu-kě), the *Běn-cǎo gāng-mù* tradition of **[[李時珍]] Lǐ Shí-zhēn** (Lǐ Bīn-hú), and the *Lèi-jīng* / orthodox-internal-medicine tradition of **[[張介賓]] Zhāng Jiè-bīn** (Zhāng Jǐng-yuè); the latter two physicians supplied his self-chosen *hào*.

After four decades of rural practice in eastern Hé-nán — a region that was the most epidemic-prone area of late-Qīng / Republican-period north China — he compiled his selected cases as the [[KR3ep104]] *Hú-yuè cūn-sǒu yī-àn* 湖岳村叟醫案 (2 *juǎn*, 17 categories, 237 cases), revised jointly by his friends Wèi Guì-sēn 衛桂森, Jiāng Jiā-mò 江家謨, and Mèng Jì-hóng 孟繼鴻, proof-read by his disciples Chén Wén-yuán 陳文元 and Shī Hé-pǔ 史和璞, and finally collated and printed by Zhāng Wén-fù 張文甫 of Kāi-fēng Medical College in 1963.
