**Wǔ Zhīwàng** 武之望 (*zì* Shūqīng 叔卿, *hào* Yángyū 陽紆, 1552–1629, CBDB 347912 — entry without dates), late-Míng official-physician of Sānyuán 三原 (Shǎnxī). Jìnshì 1589; held a series of magistrate, prefect, and circuit posts; ultimately governor-general of Shǎnxī (*Sān-biān zǒng-dū* 三邊總督), in which capacity he took his own life in 1629 after the loss of Yúlín 榆林 to rebels — a dramatic late-Míng official-suicide of the kind that became increasingly common in the dynasty's final two decades.

His medical reputation rests on two great encyclopedic works in the late-Míng *gāng-mù* 綱目 ("outline-and-detail") tradition: the *Jǐ-yīn gāng-mù* 濟陰綱目 (1620; on women's medicine, the male-counterpart pair to the present work) and the *Jǐ-yáng gāng-mù* 濟陽綱目 ([[KR3eh024]], 108 *juǎn*; on male / general internal medicine, completed but printed posthumously). Together with [[王肯堂]] Wáng Kěntáng's *Liù-kē zhèng-zhì zhǔn-shéng* (see [[KR3eh011]]), they represent the apex of the late-Míng *gāng-mù* compilation genre.

The *Jǐ-yīn / Jǐ-yáng* pair is doctrinally significant as one of the principal late-Míng systematic articulations of *yīn-yáng* therapeutic balance applied to gender-medicine: Wǔ argues that women's medicine and men's medicine are not different *kē* (specialty) but the same medicine differentiated by *yīn* and *yáng* substrate, and the two works are intentionally pendant.
