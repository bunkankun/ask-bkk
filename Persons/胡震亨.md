Late-Míng scholar-official, bibliophile, and the principal late-Míng compiler of the Táng poetic corpus; native of Hǎiyán 海鹽 (modern Jiāxìng 嘉興 prefecture, Zhèjiāng). *Zì* Xiàoyuán 孝轅; *hào* Dùnsǒu 遯叟, Chìchéngshānrén 赤城山人. *Jǔrén* of Wànlì 5 (1577, *dīngchǒu*); held office as Magistrate of Dìngzhōu 定州 (in modern Héběi), rising to Vice Director (員外郎) in the Ministry of War. Famed in late-Míng Jiāxìng literary circles for his library and antiquarian taste. His magnum opus is the *Tángyīn tǒngqiān* 唐音統籤, a ten-set monumental Táng-poetry compendium (its "*qiān*" labels are the ten Heavenly Stems 甲 to 癸); only the *jiǎ* and *wù* sets were printed in Hú's lifetime, the remaining sets surviving in manuscript and gradually issued from the Kāngxī era onward. The tenth set, the *Tángyīn guǐ qiān* (this *Sìkù* entry), was first printed in 1718 from a manuscript acquired by a Jiāngníng (Nanking) bookshop. Hú's *Tángyīn tǒngqiān* directly anticipated the imperial *Quán Táng shī* 全唐詩 ([[KR4h0246]]) of 1707, which incorporated much of his material. Lifedates 1569–1644 follow the catalog meta and the standard biographical record; CBDB (personid 30629) records only an index year of 1570, consistent with this bracket. He is the central figure of late-Míng Táng-poetry scholarship.
