Late-Míng scholar-official, bibliophile, and the principal late-Míng compiler of the Táng poetic corpus; native of Hǎi-yán 海鹽 (modern Jiā-xìng 嘉興 prefecture, Zhèjiāng). *Zì* Xiào-yuán 孝轅; *hào* Dùn-sǒu 遯叟, Chì-chéng-shān-rén 赤城山人. *Jǔrén* of Wàn-lì 5 (1577, *dīng-chǒu*); held office as Magistrate of Dìng-zhōu 定州 (in modern Héběi), rising to Vice Director (員外郎) in the Ministry of War. Famed in late-Míng Jiā-xìng literary circles for his library and antiquarian taste. His magnum opus is the *Táng-yīn tǒng-qiān* 唐音統籤, a ten-set monumental Táng-poetry compendium (its "*qiān*" labels are the ten Heavenly Stems 甲 to 癸); only the *jiǎ* and *wù* sets were printed in Hú's lifetime, the remaining sets surviving in manuscript and gradually issued from the Kāng-xī era onward. The tenth set, the *Táng-yīn guǐ qiān* (this *Sìkù* entry), was first printed in 1718 from a manuscript acquired by a Jiāng-níng (Nanking) bookshop. Hú's *Táng-yīn tǒng-qiān* directly anticipated the imperial *Quán Táng shī* 全唐詩 ([[KR4h0246]]) of 1707, which incorporated much of his material. Lifedates 1569–1644 follow the catalog meta and the standard biographical record; CBDB (personid 30629) records only an index year of 1570, consistent with this bracket. He is the central figure of late-Míng Táng-poetry scholarship.
