Dǒng Qíchāng 董其昌 (1555–1636), *zì* Yuánzǎi 元宰 (also written 玄宰), *hào* Sībái 思白, Xiāngguāng jūshì 香光居士; posthumous title Wénmǐn 文敏. Native of Huátíng 華亭 (Sōngjiāng prefecture, modern Shànghǎi). *Jìnshì* of *Wànlì* 17 / *jǐchǒu* (1589); appointed *shùjíshì* (Hanlin bachelor). Rose through the *Zhānshì fǔ* (Office of Imperial Tutors) to *Lǐbù shàngshū* (Minister of Rites), with the additional honorific *Tàizǐ tàibǎo*. Biography in *Míng shǐ · Wényuàn zhuàn*.

Dǒng is by far the most influential Chinese art theorist of the 17th century — the architect of the *nán běi èr zōng* (Southern–Northern Two Schools) periodization of literati painting and the dominant calligrapher of the late Míng. His chief theoretical writings are gathered in the *Huàchán shì suíbǐ* 畫禪室隨筆 ([[KR3j0158]]); his collected literary writings are the *Róngtái jí* 容臺集 (which the Sìkù editors recorded in their *cún mù*). He was also a major art collector and connoisseur. His career was politically and socially controversial — the 1616 *mín chāo Dǒng huàn* (Citizens' Sacking of Dǒng) riots against his household at Huátíng are documented in the *Míng-Qīng historical record* and discussed at length in modern scholarship (Wai-kam Ho, *The Century of Tung Ch'i-ch'ang*).
