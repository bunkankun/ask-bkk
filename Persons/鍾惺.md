**Zhōng Xīng** 鍾惺 (1574 – 1624), *zì* **Bó-jìng** 伯敬, *hào* **Tuì-gǔ** 退谷 and **Tuì-ān** 退庵, late-life Buddhist sobriquet **Wǎn-zhī jūshì** 晚知居士 ("Lay practitioner of late-life knowing"). Native of **Jìng-líng** 竟陵 (in modern Tiān-mén 天門, Hú-běi). One of the two principal founders of the **Jìng-líng school** (*Jìng-líng pài* 竟陵派) of late-Ming poetry and literary criticism, alongside **Tán Yuán-chūn** 譚元春 (1586 – 1637).

*Jìn-shì* of **Wàn-lì 38 = 1610**. Held offices including **Hàn-lín** *shù-jí-shì* 翰林庶吉士, **Lǐ-bù xíng-rén** 禮部行人, and **Fú-jiàn tí-xué fù-shǐ** 福建提學副使. Most influential as a literary editor and anthologist: with Tán Yuán-chūn co-edited the **Shī-guī** 詩歸 (*Return to Poetry*; 51 fascicles), an iconoclastic anthology of pre-Tang and Tang verse that defined the *Jìng-líng* aesthetic of *yōu-shēn gū-jiào* 幽深孤峭 ("recondite depth, isolated jaggedness") in opposition to both the *Qián-Hòu Qī-zǐ* 前後七子 archaism and the *Gōng-ān* 公安 school's spontaneity. Also co-edited the **Gǔ-shī guī** 古詩歸 and **Táng-shī guī** 唐詩歸 separately.

In late life Zhōng turned to Buddhist study and composed the **Lèngyán-jīng rú-shuō** 楞嚴經如說 in 10 fascicles ([[KR6j0694]], X13 no. 286), a literati's running gloss on the *Śūraṃgama-sūtra* notable for the application of his characteristic *Jìng-líng* close-reading technique to the sūtra's text.
