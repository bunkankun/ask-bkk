**Zhōng Xīng** 鍾惺 (1574 – 1624), *zì* **Bójìng** 伯敬, *hào* **Tuìgǔ** 退谷 and **Tuìān** 退庵, late-life Buddhist sobriquet **Wǎnzhī jūshì** 晚知居士 ("Lay practitioner of late-life knowing"). Native of **Jìnglíng** 竟陵 (in modern Tiānmén 天門, Húběi). One of the two principal founders of the **Jìnglíng school** (*Jìnglíng pài* 竟陵派) of late-Ming poetry and literary criticism, alongside **Tán Yuánchūn** 譚元春 (1586 – 1637).

*Jìnshì* of **Wànlì 38 = 1610**. Held offices including **Hànlín** *shùjíshì* 翰林庶吉士, **Lǐbù xíngrén** 禮部行人, and **Fújiàn tíxué fùshǐ** 福建提學副使. Most influential as a literary editor and anthologist: with Tán Yuánchūn co-edited the **Shīguī** 詩歸 (*Return to Poetry*; 51 fascicles), an iconoclastic anthology of pre-Tang and Tang verse that defined the *Jìnglíng* aesthetic of *yōushēn gūjiào* 幽深孤峭 ("recondite depth, isolated jaggedness") in opposition to both the *Qián-Hòu Qīzǐ* 前後七子 archaism and the *Gōngān* 公安 school's spontaneity. Also co-edited the **Gǔshī guī** 古詩歸 and **Tángshī guī** 唐詩歸 separately.

In late life Zhōng turned to Buddhist study and composed the **Lèngyán-jīng rú-shuō** 楞嚴經如說 in 10 fascicles ([[KR6j0694]], X13 no. 286), a literati's running gloss on the *Śūraṃgama-sūtra* notable for the application of his characteristic *Jìng-líng* close-reading technique to the sūtra's text.
