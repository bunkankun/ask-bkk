**Zhāng Sān-fēng** 張三丰 — semi-legendary Daoist immortal of the late-Yuán to early-Míng transition (c. 1340–1410, exact dates contested), traditionally identified with the figures **Zhāng Quán-yī** 張全一 and **Zhāng Jūn-bǎo** 張君寶. Conventionally credited as the founder of the **Wǔ-dāng Daoist tradition** centred at Wǔ-dāng-shān 武當山 (Hú-běi), and (post-Míng) as the originator of the *Tài-jí quán* 太極拳 martial-and-meditative tradition. Repeatedly summoned by Yǒng-lè (1402–1424) and not found, leading to the Ming-Qing legendary corpus of his immortality and extensive travels. Posthumously titled *Tōng-wēi xiǎn-huà zhēn-rén* 通微顯化真人 by Tiān-shùn 1459, and *Tài-yuán shèn-zhì rén-yīng zhēn jūn* by Yǒng-lè. His attributed corpus — almost wholly pseudepigraphic and assembled in the late Míng / Qīng — fills 8 juàn in the *Zhāng Sān-fēng quán jí* 張三丰全集 (cf. [[KR5i0071]]) and is cited in the planchette-revealed *Jīn huá zōng zhǐ* ([[KR5i0046]]) under his cult-title. CBDB ID 130313. Standard reference: Pierre-Henry de Bruyn, "Daoism in the Ming," in Lagerwey & Marsone (eds.), *Modern Chinese Religion I* (Brill 2014).
