**Xuē Jǐ** 薛己 (*zì* Lìzhāi 立齋, 1487–1559, 明), Míng-period imperial physician of Wú-xiàn 吳縣 (modern Sū-zhōu, Jiāngsū). One of the most prolific late-Míng medical writers, with 16 separate works gathered in the ***Xuē-shì yī-àn*** 薛氏醫案 ([[KR3e0070]], 77 juan total). Originally trained as an external-medicine specialist (*yáng-yī*), Xuē later became the leading Míng practitioner-and-theorist of internal medicine — particularly known for the systematic application of [[李杲]] Lǐ Gǎo's Spleen-and-Stomach school doctrine combined with the *Bā-wèi yuán* 八味丸 / *Liù-wèi wán* 六味丸 prescription pair (the Sòng *Jīn-guì* foundational kidney-yīn-and-yáng tonification prescriptions, originally Zhāng Zhòngjǐng's). Xuē died of a surgical condition (*yáng* — "*finally died of an external [surgical] condition*"); critics took this as evidence that his warming-tonifying approach had backfired. Father of Xuē Kái 薛鎧 (also a physician; the *Bǎo yīng cuō yào* 保嬰撮要 in Xuē Jǐ's *Yī-àn* is by him). Xuē's 16-work corpus shaped 16th-century Míng clinical practice and was a central target of Zhào Xiàn-kě's 趙獻可 *Yī guàn* 醫貫 expansion-and-formalization. Catalog meta dynasty 明 is correct.
