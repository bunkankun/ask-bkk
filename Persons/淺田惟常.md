**Asada Sōhaku** 浅田宗伯 (1815–1894; *imina* Asada Korehisa 惟常 / 淺田惟常, *zì* 識此, *hào* Ritsuen 慄園 — written 栗園 in some sources), the towering late-Edo and Meiji *kanpō* 漢方 physician of Shinano 信濃 origin. The most influential clinical *kanpō* author of the nineteenth century. Court physician to the Tokugawa shogunate from 1865 and to the Meiji emperor after the Restoration; he attended the future Emperor Taishō as an infant. As the foremost *kanpō* representative during the Meiji-era institutional displacement of *kanpō* by Western medicine, he founded and directed the Hakusaidō 博濟堂 beriberi hospital from 1878 and wrote the institutional postscript (*Hakusaidō ki* 博濟堂記) to [[KR3eh054]] *Kakke gairon* 腳氣概論 (1879). His more famous independent works include *Kōchū Shōkan ron* 攷注傷寒論, *Zatsubyō ron* 雑病論, *Shoyaku kunkō* 諸薬訓蒙, *Kakushikō shū* 橘窓書影 (case histories), *Senshetsu iwa* 先哲醫話, *Mochiyō saiyō* 勿誤藥室方函口訣, and the medico-biographical *Kōkoku meii den* 皇國名醫傳 ([[KR3es006]], 3 juan, Kaei 4 = 1851) with its companion appendix *Kyōrin zawa* 杏林雜話 (1865). He is the eponym of Asada-ryū 浅田流 *kanpō*, still the largest tradition of Japanese herbal medicine. The whole *Asada zenshū* runs to some 80 titles in 200+ volumes; the standard bibliographic survey is Mayanagi Makoto's online catalogue.
