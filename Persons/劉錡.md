Liú Qí 劉錡 (1098–1162), *zì* Xìnshū 信叔. Native of Dé-shùn 德順 commandery (mod. Gān-sù, Jìng-níng). Son of the Northern-Sòng general Liú Zhòng-wǔ 劉仲武. One of the four great Southern-Sòng recovery-era generals, alongside [[岳飛]] Yuè Fēi, [[韓世忠]] Hán Shì-zhōng, and [[張俊]] Zhāng Jùn. Famous above all for the victory at *Shùn-chāng* 順昌 in 1140, where his army of fewer than twenty thousand "Eight-Word" troops (*bā-zì jūn* 八字軍) decisively defeated the *Jīn* heavy cavalry of Wū-zhū 兀朮 (Wányán Zōngbì), one of the most celebrated military feats in Sòng history. Held high commands during the *Shào-xīng* recovery campaigns; was distrusted and partially marginalized by [[秦檜]] Qín Huì in the post-peace settlement. Rehabilitated in his last years and given posthumous title *Wǔ-mù* 武穆 (the same title later given to Yuè Fēi). His campaigns are recorded in [[KR3l0048]] *Sānzhāo běiméng huìbiān* and discussed warmly in [[KR3l0073]] *Dúxǐng zázhì* and other recovery-faction *bǐ-jì*.
