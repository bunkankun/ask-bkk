Late-Qīng lay Buddhist scholar **Péng Shàoshēng** 彭紹升 (1740 – *Jiāqìng* 1 1st month 20th day = 28 February 1796), *zì* **Yǔnchū** 允初, *hào* **Chǐmù** 尺木 / **Èrlín jūshì** 二林居士 / **Zhīguīzǐ** 知歸子, *fǎmíng* (Buddhist *upāsaka* name) **Jìqīng** 際清 (whence the alternate **Péng Jìqīng** 彭際清). Native of *Chángzhōu* 長洲 (modern Sūzhōu, Jiāngsū). The single most consequential lay-Buddhist intellectual of the High Qīng — a leading figure in the *jūshìfójiào* 居士佛教 (lay Buddhist) revival of the late-eighteenth century, and one of the principal exponents of *rúfó héyī* 儒佛合一 (Confucian-Buddhist unity).

Earned his *jìn-shì* 進士 in 1761 at the age of twenty-two but declined office. As a young man he was attracted first to the *Lù-Wáng xīn-xué* 陸王心學 (Lù-Xiàngshān–Wáng-Yángmíng "school of mind") and to Daoism; reading the works of *Hān-shān Dé-qīng* 憨山德清, *Zǐ-bó Zhēn-kě* 紫柏真可, and *Lián-chí Zhū-hóng* 蓮池祩宏 of the late-Míng *sì-dà-shī* 四大師 turned him to Buddhism. At twenty-nine he adopted vegetarianism; he then read systematically through the *Mahāyāna* and *Hīnayāna* sūtras and śāstras. After examining other schools he settled on Pure Land devotion as his core practice, taking the bodhisattva-precepts at thirty-four under the Chán master *Wén-xué Shí-dìng* 聞學實定 with the *fǎ-míng* *Jì-qīng* 際清.

Péng's lay-Buddhist program had three institutional dimensions: (a) he established a *niànfó dàochǎng* 念佛道場 in the family compound at Sūzhōu; (b) he organized regular *fàngshēng huì* 放生會 ("animal-releasing assemblies") at the family-owned *Wénxīnggé* 文星閣 ("Star-of-Literature Pavilion"); (c) he closed himself in *Wénxīnggé* monastic quarters as a recluse, naming his cell *Yīxíng* 一行 ("Single-Practice"). His writings amount to over twenty titles, of which the most consequential are:
- **Yīshèng juéyí lùn** 一乘決疑論 (X1550; *Resolution of Doubts about the Single Vehicle*) — Péng's chief Buddhist-doctrinal work, arguing for Tiāntái's *yīshèng* 一乘 (one vehicle) doctrine and against the *sānshèng* 三乘 (three vehicles) reading;
- **Jūshì zhuàn** 居士傳 (*Biographies of [Buddhist] Laymen*) — the foundational late-imperial reference for lay-Buddhist biography;
- **Shànnǚrén zhuàn** 善女人傳 (*Biographies of Good Women*) — the parallel volume for laywomen;
- **Tǐrén yàoshù** 體仁要術 ([[KR6k0263]], X1139) — a freestanding compilation of *fàngshēng* documents and devotional texts pertaining to the *Wénxīnggé fàngshēng huì*;
- **Wúliàngshòu jīng qǐxìn lùn** 無量壽經起信論 (Pure-Land doctrinal treatise on the *Sukhāvatīvyūha*).

Péng's elder son *Péng Xīsù* 彭希涑 carried on the lay-Buddhist tradition and edited the posthumous *Èrlín jūshì jí* 二林居士集. Péng's lay-Buddhist circle in Sūzhōu intersected with the major Qīng evidential-scholarship (*kǎozhèng* 考證) milieu through his marriage to a Yújiā 余家 cousin of *Yú Xiàokè* 余蕭客.
