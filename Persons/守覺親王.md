Shukaku Shinnō 守覺親王 (1150–1202) was the second son of **Emperor Go-Shirakawa** 後白河天皇 (1127–1192) and the great imperial-prince-monk (*hosshin'ō* 法親王) of late-12th-century Japan. He was tonsured into Shingon at **Ninnaji** 仁和寺 and became its sixth abbot — the **Sixth Omuro** 第六御室 — and the principal religious counselor of his father's cloistered-court Buddhism after Go-Shirakawa's own tonsure in 1169. Through the era of the Genpei War 源平合戰 (1180–1185) and the establishment of the Kamakura *bakufu*, Shukaku stood as the most senior religious-political figure of imperial Buddhism, and his many compilations preserve the imperial-house Shingon practice of that pivotal moment.

His scholarly output is exceptional in scope: he compiled and edited at least **five major ritual encyclopedias** preserved in the Taishō canon, drawing on the transmissions of his teachers and contemporaries: [[KR6t0194]] *Sawa-shō* (ten fasc., from Kakujō 覺成), [[KR6t0195]] *Hishō* (eighteen fasc., from Shōken 勝賢), [[KR6t0196]] *Ison-shō* (two fasc., independent compilation), [[KR6t0197]] *Uki* (one fasc.), [[KR6t0198]] *Saki* (one fasc.), [[KR6t0199]] *Gyoki* (one fasc.), and [[KR6t0200]] *Tsuiki* (one fasc.). Together these comprise the *Hosshōji-no-miya Goshō* 法性寺宮御書 — the great imperial-prince-monk's ritual collection.

In addition to his Shingon scholarship, Shukaku was an accomplished *waka* poet and patron of the arts; the *Senzaishū* 千載集 and *Shinkokinshū* 新古今集 both include his compositions.
