Suǒ Jìng 索靖 (239–303), *zì* Yòu-ān 幼安, of Dūn-huáng 燉煌 in Jiǔ-quán 酒泉 (Liáng-zhōu 涼州). One of the great Western-Jìn calligraphers, regarded together with Wèi Guàn 衛瓘 as the principal heir of Zhāng Zhī's 張芝 *zhāng-cǎo* 章草 (regulated cursive) tradition; the *Jìn shū* records the contemporary saying *Yī-tái èr miào* 一臺二妙 ("the One Bureau and the Two Marvels") for the pair as colleagues in the Imperial Secretariat. CBDB id 275110 records him under Western Jìn but supplies no lifedates; the standard *Jìn shū* 60 biography places his birth in 239 and his death in 303, fighting for the imperial party against Hé Jiàn's 河間王 forces during the *Bā-wáng zhī luàn* 八王之亂.

A maternal grand-nephew of Zhāng Zhī ("the Sage of Cursive" 草聖), Suǒ inherited the family-school *zhāng-cǎo*; his self-composed *Cǎo-shū zhuàng* 草書狀 ([[KR3h0091]]) — preserved entire in *Jìn shū* 60 and *Tài-píng yù-lǎn* 747 — is one of the earliest extant Chinese rhapsodies on cursive script and a foundational document for the East-Asian theory of *cǎo-shū*. He served as *Shàng-shū láng* 尚書郎 and *Yǎ-zhōu cì-shǐ* 雅州刺史; held the title *Pèi-guó gōng* 沛國公 from his role in suppressing the Hé Jiàn uprising. The *Suǒ-shi* of Dūn-huáng remained a calligraphic lineage into the Táng (Suǒ Jìng's autograph specimens, including the *Yuè-yí tiē* 月儀帖 and *Chū-shī sòng* 出師頌, are central to the Táng-Sòng *fǎ-tiē* 法帖 tradition, though the surviving rubbings are all later transcriptions).
