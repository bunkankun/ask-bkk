# Jìng-duān 淨端

Northern-Sòng Yún-mén-zōng 雲門宗 Chán master with Tiāntái scholastic training, *zì* Míng-biǎo 明表; self-style Ān-xián hé-shàng 安閑和尚; best known as **Duān shī-zi** 端師子 ("Lion-Duān"). 1031 – 11 January 1104 (lunar 崇寧 2.12.5), *shì-shòu* 74, *sēng-là* 49. Native of Guī-ān 歸安 (Wú-xīng), lay surname Qiū 丘.

Entered religion as a young child (age 6) at Wú-shān Jiě-kōng-yuàn 吳山解空院 under Bǎo-xiān 寶暹; took full precepts only at twenty-six. Studied the Tiāntái doctrinal system under **Jìng-jué Rén-yuè** 淨覺仁岳 (992–1064); later went to **Bǎo-jué Qí-yuè** 寶覺齊岳 (Yún-mén-zōng), where — on observing a street-festival lion-dance — he made a spontaneous leap and crouch into the lion-posture, earning Qí-yuè's confirmation and the *cóng-lín* nickname Duān shī-zi. Was invited by the chancellor Zhāng-gōng (probably Zhāng Dūn 章惇, 1035–1105) to teach at **Wú-shān**; later moved to **Hú-zhōu Xī-yú-sì** 湖州西余寺. Lived four times in "small *jiū*" poor-monastery settings, with performance-costume chán pedagogy using coloured silks imitating lion-fur.

His *yǔlù* ([[KR6q0379]]; X73 n1449), re-edited a century later by his *fǎ-sūn* [[師皎]] Shī-jiǎo, preserves his *jì-sòng* in the Hán-shān 寒山 / Shí-dé 拾得 manner — dharma-doctrine by street-comic performance. A distinctive late-Northern-Sòng figure whose Chán / Tiāntái crossover and performance-orientation stand somewhat outside the main lineage-narratives of the period.
