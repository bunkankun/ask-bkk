**Shì Wén-yíng** 釋文瑩 (fl. *Xī-níng* — *Yuán-fēng*, c. 1068–1085), *zì* Dào-wēn 道溫. Native of Qián-táng 錢塘 (modern Hángzhōu). The most prolific literatus-monk of the central-Northern-Sòng era and the only major Buddhist cleric whose secular anecdote-collections entered the *Sìkù* mainstream. Relocated from his native Wú to Jīng-zhōu (modern Hú-běi), where he resided at the Jīn-luán-sì 金鑾寺 monastery beside the Xiāng River — the setting evoked in the title of his [[KR3l0045]] *Xiāngshān yělù* 湘山野錄 (composed *Xī-níng*, c. 1068–1077). His later [[KR3l0046]] *Yùhú yěshǐ* 玉壺野史 (composed *Yuán-fēng*, c. 1078–1085) was written at his retirement-place *Yùhú* and is more systematic in its drawing on the documentary literature ("*I have collected several thousand juàn of literary collections from the founding to Xī-níng — the shén-dào-bēi, mù-zhì, xíng-zhuàng, shí-lù, zòu-yì — and assembled their content into one work*"). CBDB id 34797 records the name without firm lifedates.

His literary friendships are well-attested: he was acquainted with Sū Shùn-qīn 蘇舜欽 and Ōu-yáng Xiū 歐陽修 (delivering Sū's letter of introduction to the demoted Ōu-yáng at Chú-zhōu, per *Xiāngshān yělù*'s own entry — though Lì È's *Sòng shī jì-shì* reports the opposite); he was a close associate of *Xī-níng*-era poets. His Buddhist identity is conspicuous only in the *jì-yǐn* details of certain entries; the works are primarily secular *bǐ-jì*.

The Tài-zōng accession entry in the *Xiāngshān yělù* *xù-lù* (the so-called "*chì-shǔ tóng*" 燭斧聲 axe-tapping-candle anecdote suggesting fratricide) is the single most-debated passage in his corpus and one of the most consequential interpretive cruxes in Sòng historiography.
