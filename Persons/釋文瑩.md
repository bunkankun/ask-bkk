**Shì Wényíng** 釋文瑩 (fl. *Xīníng* — *Yuánfēng*, c. 1068–1085), *zì* Dàowēn 道溫. Native of Qiántáng 錢塘 (modern Hángzhōu). The most prolific literatus-monk of the central-Northern-Sòng era and the only major Buddhist cleric whose secular anecdote-collections entered the *Sìkù* mainstream. Relocated from his native Wú to Jīngzhōu (modern Húběi), where he resided at the Jīnluánsì 金鑾寺 monastery beside the Xiāng River — the setting evoked in the title of his [[KR3l0045]] *Xiāngshān yělù* 湘山野錄 (composed *Xīníng*, c. 1068–1077). His later [[KR3l0046]] *Yùhú yěshǐ* 玉壺野史 (composed *Yuánfēng*, c. 1078–1085) was written at his retirement-place *Yùhú* and is more systematic in its drawing on the documentary literature ("*I have collected several thousand juàn of literary collections from the founding to Xīníng — the shéndàobēi, mùzhì, xíngzhuàng, shílù, zòuyì — and assembled their content into one work*"). CBDB id 34797 records the name without firm lifedates.

His literary friendships are well-attested: he was acquainted with Sū Shùnqīn 蘇舜欽 and Ōuyáng Xiū 歐陽修 (delivering Sū's letter of introduction to the demoted Ōuyáng at Chúzhōu, per *Xiāngshān yělù*'s own entry — though Lì È's *Sòng shī jìshì* reports the opposite); he was a close associate of *Xīníng*-era poets. His Buddhist identity is conspicuous only in the *jìyǐn* details of certain entries; the works are primarily secular *bǐjì*.

The Tàizōng accession entry in the *Xiāngshān yělù* *xùlù* (the so-called "*chìshǔ tóng*" 燭斧聲 axe-tapping-candle anecdote suggesting fratricide) is the single most-debated passage in his corpus and one of the most consequential interpretive cruxes in Sòng historiography.
