**Hé Chéngtiān** 何承天 (370–447), native of Dōng-hǎi Tán-xiàn 東海郯縣 (in modern Shāndōng), Liú-Sòng astronomer, calendarist, ritualist, and historian. His standard biography is in *Sòng shū* 64 (列傳 24, *Hé Chéngtiān zhuàn*). A child prodigy, he was orphaned young and educated by his maternal uncle Xú Guǎng 徐廣 (the *Chē-qí jì-shǐ* and known classicist). He held office through the late Eastern-Jìn and Liú-Sòng courts, rising under Liú Yù to *Zhèn-jūn cān-jūn* 鎮軍參軍 and *Dōng-yáng tài-shǒu* 東陽太守, and eventually under Sòng Wéndì (r. 424–453) to *Tài-zǐ shuài-gēng-lìng* 太子率更令 and *Zhuó-zuò láng* 著作郎 with the formal commission to compile the dynastic history of the Liú-Sòng — which was eventually completed by Shěn Yuē 沈約.

His principal scientific achievement is the *Yuánjiā lì* 元嘉曆 of 443, his major refinement of the *Tài-shǐ lì* tropical-year and lunar-month constants and the first Chinese calendar to use a 391-year intercalation cycle (replacing the older 19-year *zhāng* cycle). The calendar was the standard for the Liú-Sòng and was inherited by the Qí and Liáng dynasties.

He was also an important polemicist in the early-medieval Buddhist-Confucian debate, author of the *Dá Zōng Jū-shì shū* 答宗居士書 (a critique of Buddhist *karma* doctrine, preserved in *Hóng míng jí* 弘明集), engaging directly with Zōng Bǐng 宗炳. In this corpus he appears as the attributed author of [[KR1d0101]] *Lǐlùn* (originally a 100-juàn compendium of *Sānlǐ* *biàn-lùn*, now reduced to a single-juàn extract of *Tōngdiǎn* citations).
