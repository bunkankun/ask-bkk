**Fujaku** 普寂 (1707–1781) was an Edo-period (江戶時代) Japanese scholar-monk based at the **Chōsen Ritsu-in** 長泉律院 in Edo (now Tokyo). One of the most prolific and doctrinally wide-ranging eighteenth-century Tokugawa scholastics, he is known for a deliberately cross-school programme that combined training across **Hossō** 法相, **Sanron** 三論, **Kegon** 華嚴, **Tendai** 天台, and **Vinaya** 律 traditions, all interpreted from a foundational **Pure Land** 浄土 doctrinal standpoint.

His principal contribution to Yogācāra studies is the pair of Indian-Yogācāra commentaries preserved in the Taishō supplements:
- [[KR6n0021]] *Jōyuishikiron ryakusho* 成唯識論略疏 (T2267, 6 fasc.) on the *Chéng wéishí lùn* ([[KR6n0016]], T1585);
- [[KR6n0062]] *Shōdaijōronshaku ryakusho* 攝大乘論釋略疏 (T2269, 5 fasc.) on Paramārtha's *Mahāyāna-saṃgraha* commentary ([[KR6n0061]], T1595).

He also authored B0184 *Ken'yō shōbō fukko-shū* 顯揚正法復古集 — a polemical work in the *daijō hibussetsu-ron* 大乗非仏説論 debate. The two Yogācāra *ryakusho* commentaries together established the canonical shape of Edo-period engagement with the Indian Yogācāra commentarial tradition, structured by characteristic ten-gate prolegomena (*xuán-tán* 懸譚) that incorporated Japanese transmission-history into the canonical apparatus.
