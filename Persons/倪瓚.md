Late-Yuán painter, poet, and *fēngliú* recluse of Wúxī 無錫 (Xīshān 錫山). Style-name **Yuánzhèn** 元鎮; sobriquet **Yúnlín** 雲林; alternative **Níyū** 倪迂 ("Stubborn Ní") and **Fēngyuè zhǔrén** 風月主人. One of the **Four Yuán Masters of Painting** (Yuán sìjiā: Huáng Gōngwàng, Wáng Méng, Wú Zhèn, Ní Zàn). Born to substantial wealth, in his mature years he handled none of the family wealth, devoting himself instead to building the **Qīngmì gé** 清閟閣 ("Pure-and-Private Pavilion") to house his ancient calligraphy and painting collection. His painting style — sparse landscape, river-side trees, an empty pavilion, no people — became the defining late-Yuán *yùn* (resonance) and was the principal influence on the early Wú-school painting (Shěn Zhōu 沈周 → Wén Zhēngmíng 文徵明). He was extraordinarily fastidious about cleanliness — a much-anthologized eccentricity, frequently compared to the Sòng-period **Mǐyuè** 海岳 (Mǐ Fú 米芾). In the late Zhìzhèng era, with warfare imminent, he distributed his estate, took to a small boat, and lived between the Wǔhú 五湖 (Tàihú) and the Sānmǎo 三泖 (the Sōngjiāng river-channels), spending his last decades as a "*tiānsuízǐ*"-style floating recluse. He survived to *Hóngwǔ* 7 (1374). His *Ní Yúnlín xiānsheng shī jí* 倪雲林先生詩集 ([[KR4d0575]]) and *Qīngmìgé quánjí* 清閟閣全集 ([[KR4d0576]]) are the two main literary collections; the first was compiled by **Jiǎn Xī** (Cháoyáng) 蹇曦 of Jīngxī, with a 1460 preface by Qián Pǔ 錢溥; the second is the *Sìkù* WYG recension (12 *juǎn*, edited by the Míng Cáo Péilián 曹培廉). His friendship with **Gù Yīng** 顧瑛 ([[KR4d0574]]), **Yáng Wéizhēn** 楊維楨, **Zhāng Yǔ** 張雨, and **Dài Liáng** 戴良 ([[KR4d0569]]) — who composed his *Yúnlín xiānshēng shī xù* — is documented in many late-Yuán Wúzhōng sources.
