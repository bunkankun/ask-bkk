Mǎmíng púsà 馬鳴菩薩 (\*Aśvaghoṣa, "Horse-Voice Bodhisattva"; conventionally c. 80–150 CE), Indian Buddhist poet, dramatist, and *bodhisattva*-class master, traditional figure at the Kuṣāṇa court of King Kaniṣka. Aśvaghoṣa is the foundational Sanskrit Buddhist literary author, credited with the *Buddhacarita* (the celebrated Sanskrit Buddha-biography epic, surviving in Sanskrit chapters 1–14, with the remainder preserved in Chinese as [[KR6b0049|*Fó suǒ-xíng zàn* 佛所行讚 (T192)]] and Tibetan), the *Saundarānanda*, the *Sūtrālaṃkāra* (large-scale *avadāna*-anthology preserved in Chinese as [[KR6b0058|*Dà zhuāng-yán lùn jīng* 大莊嚴論經 (T201)]]), and the *Dà-chéng qǐ-xìn lùn* 大乘起信論 (the *Awakening of Faith*, T1666 — the attribution to Aśvaghoṣa is, however, doubted in modern scholarship).

His Buddha-biography *Buddhacarita* established the model for Indic Buddhist hagiographic literature; his *bodhisattva*-poetic style — *kāvya* applied to Buddhist subject-matter — was foundational for later Sanskrit Buddhist *kāvya* writers including Āryaśūra.
