Late Southern-Sòng *cí*-master (fl. c. 1220–1260; conventional life-dates ca. 1200/12 – ca. 1260/72), *zì* Jūn-tè 君特, *hào* Mèng-chuāng 夢窗 ("Dream-Window"), of Qìng-yuán 慶元 (Níng-bō, Zhè-jiāng). One of the two supreme prosodic-school *cí*-writers of the late Southern Sòng (with Jiāng Kuí [[姜夔]]) and the chief inheritor of Zhōu Bāng-yàn [[周邦彥]]'s *gé-lǜ* 格律 line. Never held official rank; survived as a long-term retainer / *mù-bīn* 幕賓 in the households of the Wú-shì of Sū-zhōu and the Shǐ-shì of Sì-míng; in his late years patronized by Jiǎ Sì-dào 賈似道. His *Mèng-chuāng gǎo* [[KR4j0048]] preserves around 340 *cí* — originally divided into four *jiǎ-yǐ-bǐng-dīng* 甲乙丙丁 sub-collections (probably not his own arrangement; later compilers used the ten heavenly stems as labels without intending chronological order). The *jué-bǐ* (last brush-stroke) *Yīng tí xù* 鶯啼序 is at 240 characters one of the longest single *cí* in the corpus. Critical reception is split (the *Sìkù tíyào* preserves the canonical dichotomy): Shěn Yì-fù [[沈義父]]'s *Yuè-fǔ zhǐ-mí* praises him as "deeply attaining the marvellousness of *Qīng-zhēn*"; Zhāng Yán 張炎's *Cí yuán* charges his *cí* are "like a seven-jewelled tower dazzling the eye, but broken-down does not make a coherent fragment." Modern criticism (Lin Shuen-fu, 1978) places Wú as the Lǐ Shāng-yǐn 李商隱 of the *cí* form. Standard editions: Yáng Tiě-fū 楊鐵夫, *Wú Mèng-chuāng cí jiān shì* (Guǎng-dōng rén-mín, 1989); Xià Chéng-tāo 夏承燾, *Wú Mèng-chuāng xì-nián* (in *Táng Sòng cí-rén nián-pǔ*).
