Late Southern-Sòng *cí*-master (fl. c. 1220–1260; conventional life-dates ca. 1200/12 – ca. 1260/72), *zì* Jūntè 君特, *hào* Mèngchuāng 夢窗 ("Dream-Window"), of Qìngyuán 慶元 (Níngbō, Zhèjiāng). One of the two supreme prosodic-school *cí*-writers of the late Southern Sòng (with Jiāng Kuí [[姜夔]]) and the chief inheritor of Zhōu Bāngyàn [[周邦彥]]'s *gélǜ* 格律 line. Never held official rank; survived as a long-term retainer / *mùbīn* 幕賓 in the households of the Wúshì of Sūzhōu and the Shǐshì of Sìmíng; in his late years patronized by Jiǎ Sìdào 賈似道. His *Mèngchuāng gǎo* [[KR4j0048]] preserves around 340 *cí* — originally divided into four *jiǎyǐbǐngdīng* 甲乙丙丁 sub-collections (probably not his own arrangement; later compilers used the ten heavenly stems as labels without intending chronological order). The *juébǐ* (last brush-stroke) *Yīng tí xù* 鶯啼序 is at 240 characters one of the longest single *cí* in the corpus. Critical reception is split (the *Sìkù tíyào* preserves the canonical dichotomy): Shěn Yìfù [[沈義父]]'s *Yuèfǔ zhǐmí* praises him as "deeply attaining the marvellousness of *Qīngzhēn*"; Zhāng Yán 張炎's *Cí yuán* charges his *cí* are "like a seven-jewelled tower dazzling the eye, but broken-down does not make a coherent fragment." Modern criticism (Lin Shuen-fu, 1978) places Wú as the Lǐ Shāngyǐn 李商隱 of the *cí* form. Standard editions: Yáng Tiěfū 楊鐵夫, *Wú Mèngchuāng cí jiān shì* (Guǎngdōng rénmín, 1989); Xià Chéngtāo 夏承燾, *Wú Mèngchuāng xìnián* (in *Táng Sòng círén niánpǔ*).
