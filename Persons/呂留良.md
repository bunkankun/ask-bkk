**Lǚ Liú-liáng** 呂留良 (*zì* Yòng-huì 用晦, *hào* Wǎn-cūn 晚村, 1629–1683), Confucian scholar, *yí-mín* 遺民 (Míng loyalist), and medical-textual editor of Tóng-xiāng 桐鄉 (Jiā-xìng, Zhèjiāng). After failing to retain office under the Qīng, Lǚ shaved his head and entered Buddhist monastic life under the dharma name Nài-kě 耐可. He became one of the most influential early-Qīng Cheng-Zhū Confucian masters of Jiāng-Nán, and through his editorial labour was the principal mid-seventeenth-century redactor and circulator of [[趙獻可]] Zhào Xiànkě's *Yī-guàn* ([[KR3er001]]) and other late-Míng *wēn-bǔ* 溫補 medical classics — making Lǚ a foundational figure in the high-Qīng canonisation of the late-Míng warming-tonifying tradition. In 1733, fifty years after his death, Lǚ was posthumously executed by the *Yōng-zhèng* emperor for his earlier *yí-mín* activity in connection with the Zēng Jìng 曾靜 sedition case; his bones were exhumed and dismembered, his works were proscribed, and his immediate descendants were exiled or executed. This *Lǚ Liú-liáng case* 呂留良案 remained the most famous Qīng *wén-zì yù* 文字獄 (literary inquisition) and cast a political shadow over Lǚ's medical-editorial legacy that is repeatedly invoked in mid-Qīng *kǎo-jù yī-xué* literature (see [[KR3eq048]] *Yī-guàn biān* of [[徐大椿]]).
