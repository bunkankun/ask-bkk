Mid-Míng official and the leading Jiā-jìng-era pre-Táng poetry anthologist. **CBDB id 123662, dates 1513–1572**. *Zì* **Rǔyán** 汝言; *hào* **Shǎozhōu** 少洲. Native of **Línqú** 臨朐 (Shāndōng) — the same lineage as the later **Féng Qí** [[馮琦]] (1558–1603). *Jìnshì* of **Jiājìng *wùxū* (1538)**. Served in many regional posts including **Hézhōng *shǒu*** (prefect of Hézhōngfǔ — the position during which he began the *Gǔshī jì* compilation in 1544). Rose ultimately to **Guānglùsì qīng** (Minister of the Court of Imperial Entertainments).

His monumental work is the **[[KR4h0107]]** *Gǔshī jì* 古詩紀 (156 *juǎn*), the **definitive late-imperial repository of pre-Táng Chinese poetry**, compiled over 14 years (1544–1557) and published in stages. The work covers all pre-Táng verse from *gǔyì* (pre-canonical lost poetry) through ChénSuí; subsequent anthologies (Zāng Màoxún, Zhāng Zhīxiàng, [[梅鼎祚]] Méi Dǐngzuò) and modern editions (Lù Qīnlì 1983) take it as their starting-point. He also published separately the *Fēngyǎ guǎngyì* 風雅廣逸 (10 *juǎn*, the *gǔyì* portion separately) earlier in his career.

His textual blunders — flagged by **Féng Shū** ([[馮舒]], 1593–1649) in **[[KR4h0108]]** *Shījì kuāngmiù* — are real but do not affect the work's primary position as the principal source.
