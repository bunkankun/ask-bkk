Mid-Míng official and the leading Jiā-jìng-era pre-Táng poetry anthologist. **CBDB id 123662, dates 1513–1572**. *Zì* **Rǔ-yán** 汝言; *hào* **Shǎo-zhōu** 少洲. Native of **Lín-qú** 臨朐 (Shāndōng) — the same lineage as the later **Féng Qí** [[馮琦]] (1558–1603). *Jìnshì* of **Jiā-jìng *wù-xū* (1538)**. Served in many regional posts including **Hé-zhōng *shǒu*** (prefect of Hé-zhōng-fǔ — the position during which he began the *Gǔ-shī jì* compilation in 1544). Rose ultimately to **Guāng-lù-sì qīng** (Minister of the Court of Imperial Entertainments).

His monumental work is the **[[KR4h0107]]** *Gǔ-shī jì* 古詩紀 (156 *juǎn*), the **definitive late-imperial repository of pre-Táng Chinese poetry**, compiled over 14 years (1544–1557) and published in stages. The work covers all pre-Táng verse from *gǔ-yì* (pre-canonical lost poetry) through Chén-Suí; subsequent anthologies (Zāng Mào-xún, Zhāng Zhī-xiàng, [[梅鼎祚]] Méi Dǐng-zuò) and modern editions (Lù Qīn-lì 1983) take it as their starting-point. He also published separately the *Fēng-yǎ guǎng-yì* 風雅廣逸 (10 *juǎn*, the *gǔ-yì* portion separately) earlier in his career.

His textual blunders — flagged by **Féng Shū** ([[馮舒]], 1593–1649) in **[[KR4h0108]]** *Shī-jì kuāng-miù* — are real but do not affect the work's primary position as the principal source.
