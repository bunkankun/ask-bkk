Northern-Sòng *cí*-master, originally named Sānbiàn 三變, later Yǒng 永, *zì* Qíqīng 耆卿, of Chóngān 崇安 in Jiànzhōu 建州 (modern Fújiàn). Conventional birth date *ca.* 984 (modern reconstructions vary 984–987); died *ca.* 1053. Member of the literary "Three Liǔ" 三柳 of his generation with his brothers Sānfù 三復 and Sānjiē 三接. *Jìnshì* of *Jǐngyòu* 1 / 1034 — by tradition he failed several times under his original name 三變 and was finally placed only after changing his given name; the long unsuccessful years in the entertainment quarters of Biànjīng 汴京 (Kāifēng) and the southern lake-cities made him the canonical poet of *qīnglóu* 青樓 (courtesan-quarter) life. Reached the modest post of *Túntián yuánwài láng* 屯田員外郎 (Vice-director of Land-Reclamation Bureau), whence his sobriquet *Liǔ Túntián* 柳屯田. Often called *Liǔ Qī* 柳七 from his seventh-son ranking. His *Yuèzhāng jí* 樂章集 [[KR4j0003]] is the first great Northern-Sòng *cí*-collection in the *màncí* 慢詞 ("long-tune") form, decisively expanding the *cí* beyond the *xiǎolìng* of Yàn Shū [[晏殊]] and Zhāng Xiān [[張先]] in length, prosody, and topical range (urban-mercantile and entertainment-quarter scenes). Yè Mèngdé [[葉夢得]]'s *Bìshǔ lùhuà* 避暑錄話 records that the entertainment-bureau (*Jiàofāng* 教坊) musicians of Biànjīng would secure new tunes only after asking Liǔ for the *cí*-words; and a Western Xià defector told Yè in Dāntú that "wherever there is well-water for drinking, people sing Liǔ's *cí*" 凡有井水飲處即能歌柳詞. Zhāng Duānyì 張端義's *Guì'ěr jí* 貴耳集 similarly records Xiàng Píngzhāi 項平齋's dictum that "for *shī* one should study Dù, for *cí* one should study Liǔ — both speak plainly." Liǔ's reputation as a *cí*-master was always paired with social disapproval (mocked in the *Sòng shǐ* and by Yàn Shū) for his demotic register and his close association with courtesan culture. Biographical material in *Sòng shǐ* (only briefly); the principal modern study is Xuē Ruìshēng 薛瑞生, *Liǔ Yǒng cí jiàozhù* 柳永詞校注 (Zhōnghuá shūjú, 2002). No precise birth or death year is recorded in the standard histories; CBDB and the modern *Quán Sòng cí* place him conventionally 984–1053.
