Hú Kuí 胡奎 (1331–1405), *zì* **Xūbái** 虛白, *hào* **Dǒunán lǎorén** 斗南老人 ("Old Man of the South-of-the-Dipper" — a title with a legendary origin preserved in **Zhū Quán** 朱權's preface to his collection, involving a Daoist's *juéjù* riddle that the *Sìkù* editors regard as a *hàoshì zhě* fabrication), native of **Hǎiníng** 海寧 (Zhèjiāng). Born in Yuán Zhìshùn era. As a youth a pupil of **Gòng Shītài** 貢師泰. In the early Míng summoned as a *rúshì* and appointed *Níngwángfǔ jiàoshòu* 寧王府教授 — household tutor at the Princely Establishment of Níng under [[朱權]] **Zhū Quán** 朱權 (1378–1448), Hóngwǔ's seventeenth son, one of the principal early-Míng literary patrons. His verse collection [[KR4e0067]] *Dǒunán lǎorén jí* 斗南老人集 in six *juǎn* (over 1,900 poems — one of the largest early-Míng single-author *biéjí*) was cut by the **Níngwángfǔ Wényīngguǎn** 寧王府文英館 in the Hóngwǔ era and survives via the **Kūnshān Xúshì Chuánshìlóu** *yǐngchāo*; a separate four-*juǎn* manuscript by **Yáo Shòu** 姚綮 (Yúndōng yìshǐ 雲東逸史) passed through **Xiàng Yuánbiàn**'s 項元汴 *Tiānlàigé* and afterward through **Gāo Jīgǔtáng** 高氏稽古堂 and **Mǎ Sīzàn** 馬思贊's libraries.
