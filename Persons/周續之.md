# Zhōu Xùzhī 周續之

*Zì* Dàozǔ 道祖. Eastern-Jìn / Liú-Sòng classicist and Buddhist lay-scholar from Yànmén 鴈門 / Yángzhōu 雁門 (his family was originally Yǔzhōu but relocated south during the Yǒngjiā disturbance, settling in Yùzhāng 豫章 in modern Jiāngxī). Orphaned at eight; raised by relatives; studied the Five Classics from age twelve. After completing his classical training he withdrew to Mount Lú 廬山 to study under the Buddhist master [[慧遠]] Huì Yuǎn, and became one of the celebrated "Eighteen Worthies of the Eastern Grove" (*Dōnglín shí-bā xián* 東林十八賢), the group of lay-Confucian Lú-shān associates of Huì Yuǎn's vihāra. He maintained close intellectual contact with the principal Eastern-Jìn poet [[陶淵明]] Táo Yuānmíng and the Buddho-Confucian scholar Liú Yímín 劉遺民; the three are remembered as the "Three Recluses of Xúnyáng" (*Xúnyáng sān-yǐn* 潯陽三隱).

Repeatedly summoned to court — first by the Eastern-Jìn court, then more insistently by Sòng Wǔ-dì 宋武帝 (Liú Yù 劉裕) after the dynastic transition in 420 — and on each occasion lectured on the Five Classics for the emperor and his sons; his court-lectures on *Lǐ jì* and the *Máo Shī* were particularly admired. He repeatedly refused permanent office, returning to Mount Lú after each summons, and Liú Yù granted him the courtesy-title *guāng jūn* 光君 in recognition of his unwillingness to enter regular service.

His principal classical works — a *Máo Shī xù yì* 毛詩序義 [[KR1c0070]], a *Gōng-yáng zhuàn yì* 公羊傳義, a *Lǐ-jì yì* 禮記義, and an *Ér yǎ yì* 爾雅義 — were lost as integral texts by the Sòng period; substantial fragments of the *Máo Shī xù yì* survive in the *Běitáng shūchāo*, *Yán-shì jiāxùn*, *Kuāng miù zhèng sú*, and *Jīngdiǎn shìwén*, and are reconstructed in Mǎ Guóhàn's *Yùhán shānfáng jíyì shū*. His ritual correspondence — particularly on the three-year mourning question (when *zhǎn cuī* 斬縗 gives way to *qí cuī* 齊縗 after the *zú kū* 卒哭 ceremony) — is widely cited by later *lǐ xué* writers.
