**Xú Yín** 徐夤 (also commonly written 徐寅; *zì* Zhāomèng 昭夢, *fl. ca.* 880s–910s), of Pǔtián 莆田, Mǐn 閩 (Fújiàn). Late-Táng *fù*-writer and *jìnshì* of *Qiánníng* 初 (894) under examiner Lǐ Zé 李擇, who admired his "止戈為武" *fù*. Posted as *Mìshūshěng zhèngzì* 秘書省正字 (Rectifier of Texts at the imperial library) — the official designation that became his sobriquet "Xú zhèngzì" 徐正字. With the disorder following the *Huáng Cháo* rebellion he returned south to Mǐn, where Wáng Shěnzhī 王審知 received him into his entourage but treated him with insufficient ceremony; Xú dramatically broke off ("a foot or so of water with weirs in front and dams behind — how could it hold a ten-thousand-bushel ship?") and retired to *Yánshòu xī* 延壽溪. Earlier, he had reportedly presented *fù* to Zhū Wēn 朱溫 (Liáng Tàizǔ) at Biàn but, having violated a name-tabu in his cups, retreated south to recover his standing by composing a *Yóu Dàliáng fù* 遊大梁賦 with cleverly flattering allusions.

Xú's *fù* — especially *Rénshēng jǐhé* 人生幾何, *Zhǎnshé jiàn* 斬蛇劍, and *Yùgōu shuǐ* 御溝水 — were the work for which he was most famous in his own day. According to his own preface, copies of these *fù* in gold ink were displayed as folding-screens in every household of Bóhǎi (Parhae) when its envoy Gāo Yuángù 高元固 visited Mǐn. He was a literary correspondent of Hán Wò 韓偓 and Wú Róng 吳融 and a contemporary of Luó Yǐn 羅隱 [[羅隱]]. Both his works in the corpus — [[KR4c0101]] (the SBCK ten-*juǎn* Sòng-recension *Diàojī wénjí* compiled by his descendant Xú Shīrén in 1129) and [[KR4c0107]] (the WYG two-*juǎn* *Xú zhèngzì shī fù* compiled later from anthology-gleanings) — preserve overlapping but textually distinct portions of his oeuvre. CBDB id 447201 (Táng, no specific dates given). The orthography 徐夤 / 徐寅 reflects a long-standing graphic alternation in the textual tradition; both refer to the same person.
