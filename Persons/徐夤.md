**Xú Yín** 徐夤 (also commonly written 徐寅; *zì* Zhāo-mèng 昭夢, *fl. ca.* 880s–910s), of Pǔ-tián 莆田, Mǐn 閩 (Fú-jiàn). Late-Táng *fù*-writer and *jìn-shì* of *Qián-níng* 初 (894) under examiner Lǐ Zé 李擇, who admired his "止戈為武" *fù*. Posted as *Mì-shū-shěng zhèng-zì* 秘書省正字 (Rectifier of Texts at the imperial library) — the official designation that became his sobriquet "Xú zhèng-zì" 徐正字. With the disorder following the *Huáng Cháo* rebellion he returned south to Mǐn, where Wáng Shěn-zhī 王審知 received him into his entourage but treated him with insufficient ceremony; Xú dramatically broke off ("a foot or so of water with weirs in front and dams behind — how could it hold a ten-thousand-bushel ship?") and retired to *Yán-shòu xī* 延壽溪. Earlier, he had reportedly presented *fù* to Zhū Wēn 朱溫 (Liáng Tài-zǔ) at Biàn but, having violated a name-tabu in his cups, retreated south to recover his standing by composing a *Yóu Dà-liáng fù* 遊大梁賦 with cleverly flattering allusions.

Xú's *fù* — especially *Rén-shēng jǐ-hé* 人生幾何, *Zhǎn-shé jiàn* 斬蛇劍, and *Yù-gōu shuǐ* 御溝水 — were the work for which he was most famous in his own day. According to his own preface, copies of these *fù* in gold ink were displayed as folding-screens in every household of Bó-hǎi (Parhae) when its envoy Gāo Yuán-gù 高元固 visited Mǐn. He was a literary correspondent of Hán Wò 韓偓 and Wú Róng 吳融 and a contemporary of Luó Yǐn 羅隱 [[羅隱]]. Both his works in the corpus — [[KR4c0101]] (the SBCK ten-*juǎn* Sòng-recension *Diào-jī wén-jí* compiled by his descendant Xú Shī-rén in 1129) and [[KR4c0107]] (the WYG two-*juǎn* *Xú zhèng-zì shī fù* compiled later from anthology-gleanings) — preserve overlapping but textually distinct portions of his oeuvre. CBDB id 447201 (Táng, no specific dates given). The orthography 徐夤 / 徐寅 reflects a long-standing graphic alternation in the textual tradition; both refer to the same person.
