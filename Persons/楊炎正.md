Southern-Sòng *cí*-writer (fl. *Chún-xī*–*Jiā-tài*, c. 1180–1210); *zì* Jì-wēng 濟翁, *hào* Xī-qiáo 西樵, of Lú-líng 廬陵 (Jiāng-xī). Close literary friend of Xīn Qì-jí [[辛棄疾]]: the *Jià-xuān cí* preserves a *Shuǐ-diào gē-tóu* "matching Yáng Jì-wēng's rhyme" and two *Dié liàn huā* annotated *Hé Yáng Jì-wēng yùn*. His name has been a chronic source of textual confusion: Chén Zhèn-sūn [[陳振孫]]'s *Shūlù jiětí* gives the correct *Yáng Yán-zhèng, zì Jì-wēng*; Mǎ Duān-lín [[馬端臨]]'s *Wénxiàn tōngkǎo* mis-cuts as *Yáng Yán, zì Zhǐ Jì-wēng*; Máo Jìn [[毛晉]]'s *Liùshí jiā cí* perpetuated the error; Lì È [[厲鶚]]'s *Sòng shī jì-shì* settled the matter using parallel evidence from the *Wǔ-lín jiù-shì* and *Quán fāng bèi zǔ*. His sole surviving work is the *Xī-qiáo yǔ-yè* [[KR4j0041]] in one *juǎn*, preserving 37 *cí* in the Máo Jìn cutting, of which 6 are matching-Xīn-Qì-jí. The collection is conventionally read as the principal witness to the Xīn-Qì-jí literary circle of Shàng-ráo and Yáng-zhōu in the late twelfth century.
