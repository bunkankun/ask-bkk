**Hakuin Ekaku** 白隱慧鶴 (Jōkyō 2 / 1686-01-19 → Meiwa 5 / 1769-01-18 — 12 / 11 Meiwa 5 OS), the single most consequential **Rinzai-Zen** master of the Edo period and the architect of the modern Japanese Rinzai-Zen curriculum. Style-name (字) Hakuin 白隠 ("White Hidden"); dharma-name Ekaku 慧鶴 ("Wisdom Crane"); pagoda-name Kakurin 鵠林 ("Goose Grove"). Posthumous title **Shinki Dokumyō Zenji** 神機獨妙禪師, with the later additional title **Shōshū Kokushi** 正宗國師 conferred at the 100-year memorial in 1868. Native of Hara 原 village in Suruga 駿河 province (modern Numazu, Shizuoka).

Tonsured young at Shōin-ji 松蔭寺 in his native village; trained widely; received transmission from **Shōju Rōjin Etan** 正受老人慧端 (1642–1721) in the Inzan / Myōshin-ji-Reiun-ha line. Returning to Shōin-ji aged 31, Hakuin spent the remaining four decades of his life there, developing and disseminating the **kōan curriculum** that came to dominate Japanese Rinzai-Zen — beginning with his celebrated *Mu* and *one-hand-clapping* (隻手の音聲 / *Sekishu no onjō*) introductory kōan and progressing through *jakugo* (capping-phrase) practice on the *Bìyán-lù*, *Wúmén-guān*, *Xū-tang-roku*, and the recorded sayings of [[妙超]] Daitō Kokushi.

Hakuin's principal heirs **Tōrei Enji** 東嶺圓慈 [[圓慈]] (1721–1792) and **Gasan Jitō** 峩山慈棹 (1727–1797) — the *Two Pillars* (*Ryōchū* 兩柱) — institutionalised his curriculum at Ryūtaku-ji and Enpuku-ji respectively, and every present-day Japanese Rinzai-Zen training-line descends through one of them. Hakuin is also notable for his **paintings and calligraphy** (an extensive corpus of Zen brushwork survives), and for *Yasen kanwa* 夜船閑話 ("Idle Talk on a Night Boat"), the *naikan* meditation-therapy text. His major Zen-literary works are *Sokkō-roku kaien fusetsu* 息耕録開筵普説 (commentary on the Xū-tang yulu), *Itsumadegusa* 邊鄙以知吾 (autobiography), and the **Kaiankoku-go** 槐安國語 (the present text [[KR6t0280]]) — his great commentary on Daitō Kokushi's recorded sayings.
