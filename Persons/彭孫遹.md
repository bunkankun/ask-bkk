Péng Sūnyù 彭孫遹 (1631–1700), *zì* Jùnsūn 駿孫, *hào* Xiànmén 羨門 and *Jīnsùshānrén* 金粟山人, of Hǎiyán 海鹽 (Jiāxīng prefecture, Zhèjiāng). CBDB id 33922; lifedates firm.

*Jìnshì* of Shùnzhì 16 (1659, *jǐhài*), appointed *nèi-gé zhōngshū* (Grand-Secretariat secretary). In Kāngxī 18 (1679, *jǐwèi*) summoned to take the *Bóxué hóngcí* 博學鴻詞 special examination; placed *first* in the *zhào shì* (the imperial-presence final round, *yī děng dì-yī míng*) — Péng was therefore the top *Bóxué hóngcí* of his cohort, the most prestigious early-Qīng literary appointment available. Promoted to *biānxiū*, then *Lì-bù shì-láng* concurrently *Hànlín xué-shì*. He was the senior figure of the Hǎi-yán branch of the Péng lineage, which produced a number of major Qīng scholar-officials (his clansman [[彭紹升]] of Cháng-zhōu Wú-jùn being the most famous in the next generation).

Author of multiple separately-circulating sub-collections: the *Nán-yùn jí* 南陗集 (3 juan, his early lower-Yangzi miscellany), the *Xiāng-lián chàng-hé jí* 香奩唱和集 (the early-Qīng *xiāng-lián* / boudoir-poetry *chàng-hé* anthology), the *Jīn-sù cí* 金粟詞 (one of the most influential early-Qīng *cí*-lyric collections), and the *Yán-lù cí* 延露詞 (his other major *cí*-lyric collection). All four were printed independently in his lifetime. His son Péng Jǐng-zēng 彭景曾 compiled the unified 37-juan *Sōng-guì-táng jí* 松桂堂集 (later included in the 1743 *Sōng-guì-táng quánjí* 松桂堂全集 [[KR4f0024]] with Qián Chén-qún's preface), waiting fifty years after Péng Sūn-yù's death for the imperial-court literary climate to favor publication. The 1743 imprint is the canonical reception text.
