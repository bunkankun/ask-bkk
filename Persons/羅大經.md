Luó Dà-jīng 羅大經 (*fl.* 1224–1248; CBDB id 33647), *zì* Jǐng-lún 景綸, was a late-Southern-Sòng *bǐjì* writer of Lú-líng 廬陵 (modern Jí-zhōu, Jiāng-xī). His career is incompletely recorded. From the *Zhú gǔ lǎo-rén wèi shuō* entry of his book — referring to his *tóng-nián* (same-year *jìnshì*) Pó-yáng *Jǐng-yán* — we know he was a *jìnshì*; from the *Gāo Dēng wǔ Qín Guì* entry — saying he had served as Róng-zhōu *Fǎ-cáo yuàn* — we know he held office in *Lǐng-nán* (Guǎng-xī). His one surviving work is the *Hè-lín yù lù* 鶴林玉露 ([[KR3j0127]]) in 16 *juàn* — a wide-ranging late-Southern-Sòng *bǐjì* hovering between *shī huà* (poetry talk) and *yǔ lù* (recorded sayings). His position is that of a *wén-zhāng zhī shì* (literary stylist) with admiration for the *dào-xué* (Lǐxué) school — though, the *Sì-kù* editors observe, he holds two-sided views on key issues (cf. on *cí kē* he cites both Zhāng Shì against and Zhēn Dé-xiù for). He cites Zhū Xī, [[張栻]] (Zhāng Shì), Zhēn Dé-xiù 真德秀, Wèi Liǎo-wēng 魏了翁, Yáng Wàn-lǐ 楊萬里 — but also extols [[陸九淵]] (Lù Jiǔ-yuān), Ōu-yáng Xiū, and Sū Shì.
