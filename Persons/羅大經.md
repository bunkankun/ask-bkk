Luó Dàjīng 羅大經 (*fl.* 1224–1248; CBDB id 33647), *zì* Jǐnglún 景綸, was a late-Southern-Sòng *bǐjì* writer of Lúlíng 廬陵 (modern Jízhōu, Jiāngxī). His career is incompletely recorded. From the *Zhú gǔ lǎorén wèi shuō* entry of his book — referring to his *tóngnián* (same-year *jìnshì*) Póyáng *Jǐngyán* — we know he was a *jìnshì*; from the *Gāo Dēng wǔ Qín Guì* entry — saying he had served as Róngzhōu *Fǎcáo yuàn* — we know he held office in *Lǐngnán* (Guǎngxī). His one surviving work is the *Hèlín yù lù* 鶴林玉露 ([[KR3j0127]]) in 16 *juàn* — a wide-ranging late-Southern-Sòng *bǐjì* hovering between *shī huà* (poetry talk) and *yǔ lù* (recorded sayings). His position is that of a *wénzhāng zhī shì* (literary stylist) with admiration for the *dàoxué* (Lǐxué) school — though, the *Sìkù* editors observe, he holds two-sided views on key issues (cf. on *cí kē* he cites both Zhāng Shì against and Zhēn Déxiù for). He cites Zhū Xī, [[張栻]] (Zhāng Shì), Zhēn Déxiù 真德秀, Wèi Liǎowēng 魏了翁, Yáng Wànlǐ 楊萬里 — but also extols [[陸九淵]] (Lù Jiǔyuān), Ōuyáng Xiū, and Sū Shì.
