Late-Qīng physician of Lán-zhōu 蘭州 (Gān-sù), son of [[朱序東]] Zhū Xù-dōng and the second-generation head of the Lán-zhōu Zhū-shì hereditary medical lineage. Locally famous as the *Zhū sān-yé* 朱三爺 ("Mr. Zhū Three") because of his pre-eminence in the small Lán-zhōu medical community of his time. Documented by his grandson Zhū Tài-yán 朱太岩 in the 1991 preface to [[KR3er113]] *Yī-xué xīn-wù zá-zhèng yào-yì*: famed for the painstaking pulse-diagnosis (taking the pulse repeatedly to ensure accuracy), prudent prescription, attention to dosage even by fraction-cents, and willingness to attend emergency calls at any hour. Supplemented his father Zhū Xù-dōng's eyebrow-notes on Chéng Guó-péng's *Yī-xué xīn-wù* (1732). A *Liáng-zhōu jiào-yú* Sūn Shòu-shān 孫壽山 plaque inscribed *Dān-xī pǐn-wàng* 丹溪品望 ("Dān-xī-level reputation") hung in the family's southern hall in his honour. Not in CBDB.
