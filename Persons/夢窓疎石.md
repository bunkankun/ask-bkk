**Musō Soseki** 夢窓疎石 (1275–1351) — the supreme figure of medieval Japanese **Rinzai-Zen**, founder and dominant master of the **Musō-ha** 夢窓派 — by far the most influential lineage of the medieval Five-Mountain Zen system. Posthumous imperial titles **Musō Kokushi** 夢窓國師, **Shōgaku Shinshū Fusai** 正覺心宗普濟 ("True-Awakening Heart-School Universal-Saving"), and others bestowed by **three** successive emperors during his lifetime ("三朝加敬" — "the three reigns added respect to him") plus **four** further imperial titles after his death — making him the most heavily honored Japanese Buddhist master in history.

Born 1275 in Ise Province (modern Mie). Initially studied Tendai and esoteric Buddhism; then turned to Zen under [[顯日]] **Kōhō Kennichi** (1241–1316) — dharma-heir of [[祖元]] **Wúxué Zǔyuán** at Engaku-ji — by whom he was recognized as enlightened. Resided successively at numerous temples; the *Bukkō* / *Wúxué* line through Kōhō to Musō is the foundational genealogy.

Musō's principal achievements:
1. **Founded eight major Zen temples** as *opening-mountain founding-abbot* (八提鈯斧六處開山 — "eight times raising the blunt-axe, opening six mountains"), including the great **Tenryū-ji** 天龍寺 in Kyoto (1339, founded at the request of Emperor Go-Daigo and Ashikaga Takauji), **Erin-ji** 惠林寺, **Zuisen-ji** 瑞泉寺, and **Saihō-ji** 西芳寺 (the "Moss Temple").
2. **Garden design**: Musō is the legendary founder of the Japanese **Zen garden** tradition (*karesansui* 枯山水), credited with the designs of the Saihō-ji and Tenryū-ji gardens.
3. **Imperial-and-shōgunal influence**: Spiritual advisor to Emperors Go-Daigo and Hanazono, to Shōgun Ashikaga Takauji, and to the Ashikaga regent system. Effective architect of the *Ankoku-ji ritō* 安國寺利塔 ("Country-Pacifying Temples and Stūpas") system — the parish-network of provincial Zen temples established by the Ashikaga.
4. **Founder of Gozan literature**: numerous literary disciples carried his line into the dominant Muromachi-period Chinese-style literary culture.

Died 1351, age 77. His Recorded Sayings — *Musō Kokushi goroku* 夢窓國師語錄 ([[KR6t0261]], T80n2555) — three fascicles, were compiled by his disciple **Honkan** 本元 and others, with a preface dated **Bunna 3 (1354), 4th month, Buddha's birthday** by **Shimei Tōryōsō Eiyu** 四明東陵叟永璵 — a Chinese-emigrant master at the Japanese Gozan.
