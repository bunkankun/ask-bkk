**Dòu Jié** 竇傑 (*zì* Hànqīng 漢卿; universally known in the medical tradition as Dòu Hànqīng 竇漢卿; *hào* Mò'ān 默庵, 1196–1280, 元), Jīn–Yuán transitional acupuncturist and statesman of Féi-xiāng 肥鄉 (ancient Míng 古洺, in modern Hé-běi). Served first the Jīn court, then rose under the Mongols to the title Tài-shī 太師 (Grand Preceptor). Author of the foundational **Zhēn jīng zhǐ-nán** 針經指南 ([[KR3ee017]]), comprising the *Biāo-yōu fù* 標幽賦 — the canonical post-Sòng acupuncture didactic rhapsody — and the *Liú-zhù bā-xué* 流注八穴 doctrine of eight confluent acupoints connecting the twelve regular channels with the eight extraordinary vessels (公孫–衝, 列缺–任, 後谿–督, 臨泣–帶, 內關–陰維, 外關–陽維, 照海–陰蹺, 申脈–陽蹺). His work was printed posthumously in Yuán-zhēn 1 (1295) by his disciple Niú Liángyòu 牛良祐. Dòu's *bā-xué* doctrine is the conceptual basis of all post-Yuán *Líng-guī bā-fǎ* chronoacupuncture and remains foundational to modern East-Asian acupuncture practice.
