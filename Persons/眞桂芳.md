Zhēn Guì-fāng 眞桂芳 (also written 真桂芳; lifedates uncertain, *fl. c.* 1270–1300), better known by his *hào* Zhēn Shān-mín 真山民 ("True Mountain-Man"), traditionally identified as a late-Sòng *jìnshì* of Kuò-cāng 括蒼 (Lì-shuǐ 麗水, Zhè-jiāng). After the Sòng surrender he refused all Yuán recruitment and lived as a wandering *yí-mín*, leaving inscriptions and verse at the sites he passed. The *Sìkù* editors of the *Zhēn Shān-mín jí* [[KR4d0403]] explicitly note that the author's actual identity (including the very surname) was disputed: candidates included a clansman of Zhēn Dé-xiù [[真德秀]] (whose *shì* was Wén-zhōng), or simply someone who styled himself "Mountain-Man" as a pseudonym, or specifically Zhēn Guì-fāng of Kuò-cāng. Modern scholarship generally accepts the Kuò-cāng identification on the strength of Yuán-and-later local tradition. No CBDB record. The verse is in the late-Sòng *wǎn-Táng pài* manner.
