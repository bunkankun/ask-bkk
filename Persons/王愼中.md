Wáng Shènzhōng 王愼中 (1509–1559), *zì* Dàosī 道思, *hào* Zūnyán 遵巖, of Jìnjiāng 晉江 (Quánzhōu, Fújiàn). Took the *jìnshì* in Jiājìng 5 (1526); office reached *Hénán Bùzhèng sī cānzhèng*. He is one of the founding figures of the **Tang-Sòng-pài** (唐宋派, "Táng-Sòng School") in Míng prose theory, with **Táng Shùnzhī**, **Guī Yǒuguāng**, and **Máo Kūn**. His literary-historical conversion-narrative — first an adherent of the **Lǐ Mèngyáng / Hé Jǐngmíng** *Qín-Hàn* archaist program, then an awakening to **Ōuyáng Xiū** and **Zēng Gǒng**, the burning of juvenilia, and the singular imitation of Sòng-prose models — is one of the most often-cited episodes in Míng literary history. He is paired with Táng Shùnzhī as "**Wáng Táng**" 王唐. His collection is the *Zūnyán jí* [[KR4e0185]]. He died in 1559; the WYG recension is the posthumous 1571 cleaned-up edition prepared by his son **Wáng Tóngkāng** and son-in-law **Zhuāng Guózhēn**. CBDB 695301 has only zero markers; standard Míng reference works give 1509–1559.
