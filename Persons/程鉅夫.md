The singular Yuán-period 元 imperial Hàn-Confucian *Yù-táng* (Hàn-lín) official, bridge-figure of Khubilai-and-successor empire-cultural policy, founder of Yuán-imperial-Hàn-Confucian cultural-recovery of southern literati after 1276. Original given-name **Wén-hǎi** 文海; taboo-avoided for the Yuán Wǔ-zōng's *miào-huì* "Hǎi-shān" 海山 — Chéng adopted his *zì* **Méng-qīng** 孟卿 as substitute name in formal usage. *Hào* **Xuě-lóu** 雪樓 ("Snow-Tower"). Native of **Yǐng-zhōu Jīng-shān** 郢州京山 (modern Hú-běi).

**Foundational mission.** Imperial commissioner of the **Zhì-yuán 23 (1286) southern-recruitment expedition**: Chéng travelled south by imperial commission to recruit Sòng-remnant scholars into Yuán court service. The mission recovered into Yuán imperial service:
- **Zhào Mèng-fǔ** 趙孟頫 [[趙孟頫]] (the great calligrapher-painter-scholar)
- **Yú Jí** 虞集 [[虞集]] (the foundational Yuán-Hàn-lín scholar)
- And dozens of other major southern literati.

This is the single most-consequential Yuán-Confucian recruitment act.

**Career.** Successive *Hàn-lín* offices over three decades through the Khubilai → Chéng-zōng → Wǔ-zōng → Rén-zōng reigns. Composer of substantial portions of Yuán imperial *zhì-zhào* (edicts), *zhì-cí* (decree-words), *cè-wén* (investiture-texts), *jì-wén* (sacrificial-texts), *zhāi-yì* (vegetarian-feast intentions), *qīng-cí* (blue-words for Daoist rituals), *biǎo* (memorials), and *zhì-yǔ* (offerings-words). The body of his *Chì-cì bēi* (imperially-commissioned steles) for senior Mongol-Khangli aristocrats and Hàn-Confucian elders forms the largest single Yuán-imperial epigraphic-biography corpus preserved through manuscript transmission.
