Wáng Mào 王楙 (1151–1213), *zì* Miǎn-fū 勉夫, self-styled Yě-kè 野客 ("the rustic guest"); contemporaries called him Jiǎng-shū-jūn 講書君 ("Master of Book-Lecturing"). Of Cháng-zhōu 長洲 (modern Sūzhōu, Jiāngsū). CBDB id 10342. Native-place attestations include Wú-xiàn 吳縣 and Wújiāng 吳江, both within the Sūzhōu metropolitan area.

Wáng Mào never sat for the *jìnshì* examinations. Out of devotion to his mother he declined office and lived in scholarly retirement, spending his days reading and composing. His one surviving major work is the *Yěkè cóngshū* 野客叢書 (30 juan, with the appended *Yělǎo jì wén* 野老紀聞 in 1 juan, which preserves his father's notes), one of the principal antiquarian *bǐjì* of the Southern Sòng. The first preface is dated *Qìng-yuán* 1 (1195); a second author's note is dated *Jiā-tài* 2 (1202); both are written from his Cháng-zhōu studio, *Bù-qī táng* 不欺堂 ("Hall of Not-Self-Deception"). The work circulated in the *Bǎo-yán táng mì-jí* 寶顏堂秘笈 of Chén Jìrú 陳繼儒 in a heavily abridged 12-juan version; the *Sìkù* editors restored the full 30-juan recension and pointedly rejected Chén's text.

Within the Kanripo corpus he is the author of [[KR3j0045]] *Yěkè cóngshū* 野客叢書. He is referenced in the *Sìkù tíyào* as a critic of Hóng Mài's 洪邁 *Róng-zhāi suí-bǐ* 容齋隨筆.
