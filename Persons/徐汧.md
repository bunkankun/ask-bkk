Xú Qiān 徐汧 (1597–1645), *zì* **Jiǔ-yī** 九一, *hào* **Wù-zhāi** 勿齋, posthumous title **Wén-mù** 文穆 (Southern Míng); native of **Cháng-zhōu** 長洲 (Sū-zhōu). *Jìnshì* of *Chóng-zhēn* 1 / *wù-chén* (1628); rose through Hànlín posts to *Zhān-shì-fǔ shào-zhān-shì*. A close associate of the **Dōng-lín** circle in its post-1626 phase and a friend of [[凌義渠]]; the *Sìkù* tiyao to the *Líng Zhōng-jiè-gōng jí* ([[KR4e0241]]) names him jointly with his disciple **[[姜垓]]** as the editor of the surviving six *juǎn* (4 verse + 2 prose) of Líng's writings, which Líng himself had burned before his suicide in 1644. After the fall of Běi-jīng and Nán-jīng, Xú attempted to join the Lú-wáng resistance; on the fall of Sū-zhōu to Qīng troops in *Hóng-guāng* 1 / 1645 he drowned himself in a pond at his home.
