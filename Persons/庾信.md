**Yǔ Xìn** 庾信 (513–581), *zì* Zǐ-shān 子山, also styled Lán-chéng 蘭成, native of Xīn-yě 新野 in Nán-yáng (modern Hénán), was the great *bridge* poet of the Liáng-Northern-Zhōu transition and the canonical master of medieval Chinese parallel prose (*pián-tǐ wén* / *sì-liù wén* 四六文). Son of Yǔ Jiān-wú 庾肩吾 (Liáng *Sǎn-jì cháng-shì zhōng-shū-lìng*), he served the Liáng Tái-chéng 臺城 court at *Yòu-wèi jiāng-jūn* 右衛將軍, enfeoffed Wǔ-kāng-xiàn-hóu 武康縣侯. In 554 he was sent on a diplomatic mission to the Western Wèi and held there as a hostage when Hóu Jǐng's 侯景 rebellion devastated the Liáng south. He never returned. Under Western Wèi he served as *Chē-qí dà-jiāng-jūn yí tóng sān-sī* 車騎大將軍儀同三司; under Northern Zhōu, as *Sī-zōng zhōng-dài-fū* 司宗中大夫. He retired in early Dà-xiàng (579) and died in *Kāihuáng* 1 (581) just under the Suí.

His work divides into two phases: (a) the elegant *gōng-tǐ* 宮體 *fù* and *shī* of the Liáng Tái-chéng court (paired with Xú Líng 徐陵 as the *Xú-Yǔ tǐ* 徐庾體); (b) the post-554 Northern phase, the period of his late masterpieces — the *Āi Jiāng-nán fù* 哀江南賦, the *Xiǎo yuán fù* 小園賦, the *Kū shù fù* 枯樹賦. Dù Fǔ's iconic line *Yǔ Xìn wén zhāng lǎo gēng chéng* 庾信文章老更成 ("Yǔ Xìn's prose grew finer with age") canonized the late Yǔ as the formative model of Táng parallel prose. Standard biographies in *Zhōushū* 41 and *Běi shǐ* 83. CBDB confirms 513–581.

His writings survive in two annotated editions in the corpus: [[KR4b0017]] *Yǔ Kāifǔ jí jiān-zhù* 庾開府集箋註 (Wú Zhàoyí, Kāngxī era) and [[KR4b0018]] *Yǔ Zǐshān jí zhù* 庾子山集註 (Ní Fán, 1689; the standard modern reference).
