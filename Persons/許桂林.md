**Xǔ Guìlín** 許桂林 (*zì* Tóngshū 同叔, *hào* Yuènán 月南; 1779–1821; native of Hǎi-zhōu 海州, Jiāngsū) was a mid-Qīng Confucian scholar, mathematician and astronomer of the Yáng-zhōu / Hǎi-zhōu *kǎozhèng* circle. He is best known for the *Xuān-xī tōng* 宣西通 ([[KR3fa034]] and [[KR3fa035]]) — a four-*juǎn* treatise (with companion single-*juǎn* digest) which uses the *Hóng-fàn* 洪範 "*xuán*" 璿 and *Yáo diǎn* "*qī-zhèng*" canonical passages as the framework for an integrated presentation of the Jesuit-Tychonic-Keplerian astronomy. The work is one of the most influential late-Jiā-qìng / early-Dào-guāng synthesist texts: Xǔ argues, with great philological care, that the *Hóng-fàn*'s "*xuán-jī yù-héng*" 璿璣玉衡 contains in nuce the celestial-and-equatorial coordinate system, the precession-of-the-equinoxes phenomenon, and the *qī-zhèng* planetary apparatus.
