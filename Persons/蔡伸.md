Late Northern–early Southern Sòng *cí*-writer (1088–1156), *zì* Shēn-dào 伸道, *hào* Yǒu-gǔ jūshì 友古居士 ("Recluse who Befriends Antiquity"), of Pú-tián 莆田 (Fú-jiàn). Grandson of the great calligrapher and statesman Cài Xiāng 蔡襄 (1012–1067). In *Xuān-hé* he served as Vice-Magistrate (*tōng-pàn*) at Péng-chéng 彭城 (Xú-zhōu) — at the same time and bureau as Xiàng Zǐ-yīn [[向子諲]], with whom he maintained an extensive *cháng-chóu* exchange that is the primary biographical anchor for his *cí*. After the southern crossing reached the rank of *Zuǒ zhōng dài-fū* 左中大夫 and held magistracies including Hè-zhōu 和州. His sole extant work is the *Yǒu-gǔ cí* [[KR4j0018]], in one *juǎn*, preserved in the late-Míng Máo Jìn [[毛晉]] cutting and reproduced in WYG; modern editions reconstruct around 175 *cí*. The *Sìkù tíyào* places him "slightly below Xiàng Zǐ-yīn" but his peer in talent and brush-force; the relationship makes him a key witness to the late-1120s–1140s transition between the Northern-Sòng *gé-lǜ* tradition and the post-southern-crossing patriot-*cí*.
