**Hán Guāng** 含光 (fl. mid-8th c.) — one of the **six principal disciples** (六哲 / 六大弟子) of **Amoghavajra** [[不空]] in the Tang Esoteric (Tángmì 唐密) school. Together with **Huì-guǒ** 惠果, **Huì-lǎng** 慧朗, **Tán-zhēn** 曇貞, **Yuán-jiǎo** 元皎, and **Jué-chāo** 覺超, he received the full Vajraśekhara/Garbhadhātu transmission directly from Bù-kōng. He served as Bù-kōng's emissary to Mt. **Wǔtái** 五臺山, where he was instrumental in the establishment of the **Jīn-gé-sì** 金閣寺 (Golden Pavilion) and of the cult of **Mañjuśrī** as the imperial guardian of the Tang. The Esoteric chronicler **Zàn-níng** 贊寧 (in the *Sòng Gāosēng-zhuàn*) lists him among Bù-kōng's foremost transmitters and describes his oral teaching as the source for several of the Vināyaka-cycle ritual manuals — notably [[KR6j0504]] (T1273), recorded by him as a digest of the secret meaning, and [[KR6j0505]] (T1274), compiled by his disciple **Jǐng-sè** [[憬瑟]] from his oral transmission.

Source: DILA Buddhist Person Authority A001396; *Sòng Gāosēngzhuàn* 宋高僧傳 fasc. 27 (T50n2061); Chou Yi-liang, "Tantrism in China," *Harvard Journal of Asiatic Studies* 8 (1945): 241–332; Geoffrey Goble, *Chinese Esoteric Buddhism: Amoghavajra, the Ruling Elite, and the Emergence of a Tradition* (Columbia UP, 2019).
