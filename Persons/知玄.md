Zhīxuán 知玄 (811–883), with sobriquet **Hòujué** 後覺 and the imperial title **Wùdá guóshī** 悟達國師 ("National Preceptor Wùdá"), was a late-Tang Buddhist scholar-monk and one of the most celebrated court Buddhists of the *Wǔzōng* and *Yìzōng* reigns. Native of **Hóngyǎ** 洪雅 (Sìchuān). Lay surname **Chén** 陳; styled the *Chén púsà* 陳菩薩 ("Chén Bodhisattva") in his youth.

Tonsured at age seven under **Fǎ-tài** 法泰 of *Níng-yí-sì* 寧夷寺; took full ordination under the Vinaya master **Biàn-zhēn** 辯貞 at *Jìng-zhòng-sì* 淨眾寺. He lectured on the *Niè-pán jīng* (*Mahāparinirvāṇa-sūtra*) at fourteen and was already known as the *Chén pú-sà*. Travelling to the capital, he was summoned by **Wǔ-zōng** to debate with the Daoists; his *yǒu-yán fēng-lì* 出言鋒利 ("razor-sharp speech") in the imperial debate is recorded in his biography. He subsequently lectured in the imperial palace and was granted the *zǐ jiā-shā* 紫袈裟 (purple robe) and titled *Sān-jiào shǒu-zuò* 三教首座 ("Senior Officer of the Three Teachings"). The emperor founded *Fǎ-qián-sì* 法乾寺 on the site of an old residence and installed Zhī-xuán there. He later sought leave to return to Sì-chuān and resided at *Dān-jǐng-shān* 丹景山 in Péng-zhōu.

Died in *Zhōng-hé* 3 (883), aged seventy-three (54 years in the saṃgha). His extant works include the **Cí-bēi shuǐ-chàn fǎ** 慈悲水懺法 ([[KR6k0199]], T1910) — the *Repentance Liturgy of the Compassionate Water* — composed after his celebrated encounter with the arhat **Jiā-nuò-jiā** 迦諾迦 (Kanaka), which is preserved in the *Sòng gāo-sēng zhuàn* j. 6 hagiography; the **Shèng-mán jīng shū** 勝鬘經疏 (commentary on the *Śrīmālādevī*-sūtra); the **Bō-ruò xīn-jīng shū** 般若心經疏 (commentary on the *Heart Sūtra*); and the **Jīn-gāng-jīng shū** 金剛經疏 (commentary on the *Vajracchedikā*).
