Luó Hóngxiān 羅洪先 (1504–1564), *zì* Dáfū 達夫, *hào* Niàn'ān 念菴, posthumous *shì* Wéngōng 文恭, of Jíshuǐ 吉水 (Jiāng-xī). Took the *jìnshì* in Jiā-jìng 8 (1529) in the *dì-yī rén* (*zhuàngyuán*, top-graduate) rank; office reached *Zuǒ Chūnfāng zànshàn*. Early-Lóng-qìng posthumous elevation to *Guānglù sì shǎoqīng*. He is one of the principal figures of the **Jiāng-yòu** (Jiāng-xī) wing of the **Yáng-míng** (Wáng Shǒurén) school: he never personally met Wáng Shǒurén but studied with **Lǐ Zhōng** 李中 and indirectly inherited the *liáng-zhī* tradition. He afterwards composed the canonical Wáng Shǒurén *nián-pǔ* and styled himself a Wáng disciple. His own doctrine, *jìng-guān běn-tǐ* (still-observing the original-substance), is a meditative extension of *liáng-zhī* doctrine that did not avoid the criticism of *rù-Chán* (entering Chán). Beyond his *Niàn-ān wén-jí* [[KR4e0187]], he is the principal author of the *Guǎng-yú-tú* 廣輿圖 — the most influential atlas of Míng China. **Yán Sōng** wished to recommend him and could not — a fact praised in the *Sìkù* tíyào.
