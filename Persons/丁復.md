Dīng Fù 丁復 (Yuán), *zì* Zhòng-róng 仲容, native of Tiān-tái 天台 (Zhèjiāng). Recommended in early Yán-yòu (1314 onward) but declined office, instead drifting in poetic *fàng-qíng* through Jiāng and Huái before settling in Jīn-líng 金陵 (Nán-jīng), where his residence — surrounded by ancient *guì* (cypresses) — gave the name *Kuài-tíng* 檜亭 to his collected works [[KR4d0499]]. He composed prodigiously (reportedly several thousand pieces) but discarded the drafts on completion; his surviving corpus was assembled posthumously by his son-in-law Ráo Jiè-zhī [[饒介之]] (the *qián-jí* 前集) and his disciple Lǐ Qiān-zhī [[李謙之]] (the *xù-jí* 續集), and consolidated into a 9-*juǎn* recension by **Zhāng Wéi-yuǎn** 張惟遠 (Náng-tái jiān-chá yù-shǐ) at the *Jí-qìng xué-gōng* in Zhì-zhèng 10 (1350). His verse, in Lǐ Huán's 李桓 characterization, "began as Lǐ Bái, then evolved toward an autonomous style."
