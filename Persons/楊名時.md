Yáng Míngshí 楊名時 (1660–1736), zì Bīnshí 賓實, hào Níngzhāi 凝齋, posthumous title Wéndìng 文定, was a high KāngxīYōngzhèngQiánlóng court official, *Yìjīng* scholar, and provincial administrator from Jiāngyīn 江陰 (Chángzhōu 常州, Jiāngsū 江蘇). He passed the *jìnshì* in *Kāngxī xīnwèi* 康熙辛未 = 1691 — under Lǐ Guāngdì 李光地 ([[李光地]]) as chief examiner — and rose to Minister of Rites and Senior Tutor of the Heir Apparent. He served as Yúnnán 雲南 Governor and is associated with the early-Qīng administrative integration of the southwest borderlands.

Yáng was Lǐ Guāngdì's principal *Yìxué* pupil and the leading early-Qiānlóng-period continuation of the Lǐ Guāngdì school. His main *Yì* writing is the *Zhōuyì zhájì* 周易劄記 ([[KR1a0141]]) in two juàn, completed after the imperial *Zhōuyì zhé zhōng* ([[KR1a0117]]) of 1715. While Yáng's exposition of the *Shuōguà zhuàn* and the *Qǐ méng* extends the *xiāntiān* diagrams (in keeping with Lǐ Guāngdì's tradition), his canonical exegesis is purely *yìli*-oriented and does not lapse into chart-and-numerology. He is recognized as a faithful but moderate transmitter of his master's *Yìxué*. While he served as Yúnnán Governor, the local scholar Xià Zōnglán 夏宗瀾 came to study *Yì* with him; the *yǔlù*-style *Yì shuō* 易說 produced by Xià from these sessions preserves substantial material on Yáng's teaching method.
