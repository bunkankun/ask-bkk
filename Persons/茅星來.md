Máo Xīnglái 茅星來 (1678–1748), *zì* Qǐsù 豈宿, was an early-Qīng Confucian classicist of Guī-ān 歸安 (Hú-zhōu 湖州, Zhè-jiāng). Best known for the *Jìn-sī lù jí-zhù* 近思錄集註 (KR3a0043), the Qīng-period replacement for Yè Cǎi's Sòng-period *jí-jiě* on the *Jìn-sī lù*. The work was composed under his father's instruction, completed in Kāngxī *xīn-chǒu* (1721), and supplemented by a second preface in Qiánlóng *bǐng-chén* (1736) — fifteen years after the body of the work — making it the labour of his lifetime. He is methodologically distinctive among early-Qīng *Lǐxué* commentators for arguing (in his *hòu-xù*) that the practice of post-Sòng *Lǐxué* must include the Hàn-Táng glossographic-classical tradition (Mǎ Róng, Zhèng Xuán, Jiǎ Yì, Kǒng Yǐngdá) — a position that distinguishes him from purist *Lǐxué* loyalists.
