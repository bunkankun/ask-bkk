# Fàn-qí 梵琦

Late-Yuán / early-Míng Yángqí-branch Línjì Chán master and Chinese Pure-Land synthesist, *zì* Chǔ-shí 楚石 ("Chu-Stone"), self-style Xī-zhāi lǎo-rén 西齋老人. Imperial title Fó-rì Pǔ-zhào Huì-biàn chánshī 佛日普照慧辯禪師, conferred by Yuán Shùndì in Zhì-zhèng 7 (1347, the same year [[祖銘]] Gǔ-dǐng Zǔ-míng received his own imperial title). A posthumous title **Dà-jué Pǔ-jì chánshī** 大覺普濟禪師 circulates in later Chán literature (e.g., the comparative judgement by Mèng-ān Chāo-gé 夢菴超格 in the preface to [[KR6q0164]] ranks him "supreme" among Kuò-ān-tradition *mùniú-tú* harmonisers under this title). Native of Xiàng-shān 象山 (Míng-zhōu), lay surname Zhū 朱 (lay name Tán-yào 曇曜). Dharma-heir of [[行端]] Yuán-sǒu Xíng-duān (1255–1341), and the most nationally-known of Xíng-duān's students. 5th-generation dharma-descendant of Dà-huì Zōng-gǎo 大慧宗杲.

Born on the 20th of the 6th month of Yuán-zhēn 2 (28 July 1296); his mother is said to have dreamt of the sun falling into her bosom before his birth. Ordained at nine at Hǎi-yán Tiān-níng 天寧永祚 under Nè-wēng Mó 訥翁模; full precepts at sixteen at Hángzhōu Zhāo-qìng-sì 昭慶寺; at twenty-two became *shì-zhě-diǎn-zàng* 侍者典藏 at Dào-chǎng-sì 道場寺. His awakening came in stages — a first opening on the *Śūraṃgama* passage on seeing and darkness; a second, decisive *wù* 悟 when, at an inn in Yàn-dū 燕都 (he was travelling on imperial summons for a *xuǎn-shàng* 選上 assembly at the Yīng-zōng court), he was woken at night by drums on a colour-tower. Returned south, and Xíng-duān at Jìng-shān formally conferred dharma-transmission on him.

Held abbacies in succession at Tiān-níng Yǒng-zuò 天寧永祚, Hángzhōu Bào-guó-sì 報國寺, Jiā-xīng Běn-jué 本覺, and Jiā-xīng Guāng-xiào 光孝 — the Jiā-xīng / Hǎi-yán axis that made him the leading Chán authority of the lower Yangtze in the Zhì-zhèng years. In Zhì-zhèng 19 (1359) he retired to Yǒng-zuò-sì and built a hermit's *yuàn* to its west, which he named Xī-zhāi 西齋 — seat of his late-life project, the **Xī-zhāi jìng-tǔ shī** 西齋淨土詩, a large collection of Chán-inflected Pure-Land verse foundational for the later Míng synthesis of Chán and Pure-Land. In Hóng-wǔ 3 (1370), shortly after the founding of the new dynasty, he was summoned by imperial edict for a sermon at Nán-jīng. He died on the 26th of the 7th month of that year (25 August 1370), *shì-shòu* 75, *sēng-là* 63, after a final shout.

Co-editor of Xíng-duān's *yǔlù* ([[KR6q0352]]). His own twenty-juan *yǔlù* survives as **Chǔ-shí Fàn-qí chánshī yǔlù** 楚石梵琦禪師語錄 ([[KR6q0353]]; X71 n1420). Other works include the *Jìng-tǔ shī*, the *Cí-shì shàng-shēng jì* 慈氏上生偈, the *Běi-yóu jí* 北遊集, the *Fèng-shān jí* 鳳山集, harmonising *jì* on the Hán-shān / Shí-dé / Fēng-gān corpus (transmitted in [[KR6q0216]] *Tiān-tái sān shèng shī jí hé-yùn*), and harmonising verses on Kuò-ān Shī-yuǎn's 廓庵師遠 *mù-niú-tú* ([[KR6q0164]]). Named dharma-heir: Sū-zhōu Wàn-shòu Yíng-zhōng Jǐng-huán 瑩中景瓛 (DILA A001249). Principal biographical sources: the *xíng-zhuàng* and *tǎ-míng* in *juan* 20 of his *yǔlù*; *Bǔ-xù gāo-sēng zhuàn* *juan* 14 (X77); *Zēng-jí Xù-chuándēng-lù* *juan* 5.
