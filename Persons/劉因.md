# Liú Yīn 劉因 (1249–1293)

*Zì* Mèng-jí 夢吉, *hào* Jìng-xiū 靜脩 (the *Jìng-xiū xiān-shēng* 靜脩先生 of the *Yuán-shǐ Rú-lín zhuàn*). Native of Róng-chéng 容城 (modern Hé-běi Róng-chéng-xiàn, just southwest of Bao-ding 保定). Yuan-period Cheng-Zhu *Lǐ-xué* scholar — together with Xǔ Héng 許衡 (1209–81), one of the **two pillars of early-Yuán Neo-Confucianism**.

In Zhì-yuán 19 (1282) Khubilai Khan summoned him to court as *Chéng-dé láng* 承徳郎 兼 *Yòu-zàn-shàn dài-fū* 右贊善大夫; he served briefly, then resigned and returned home. He was again summoned as *Jí-xián xué-shì* 集賢學士 but did not respond. His career — the only Yuán Confucian of his stature to systematically refuse Mongol office — gave him an ambivalent place in the Yuán *Lǐ-xué* canon: respected for his learning, but somewhat off-stage in political-institutional terms.

Surviving works in Kanripo:
- [[KR1h0031]] *Sì-shū jí-yì jīng-yào* 四書集義精要 (28 *juàn*, WYG, incomplete) — a careful pruning of the post-Zhū-Xī *Sì-shū jí-yì* corpus, removing redundant material and selecting the substantive readings.

Liú Yīn's collected works are the *Jìng-xiū xiān-shēng wén-jí* 靜脩先生文集 (KR4d corpus). He was the elder of Sū Tiān-jué 蘇天爵 (the *Yuán wén-lèi* 元文類 compiler), who praised this *Sì-shū jí-yì jīng-yào* as "*jiǎn-yán jīng-dāng* 簡嚴精當" (concise-strict, precise-and-apposite) — the verdict echoed by the Sìkù tíyào at [[KR1h0031]].
