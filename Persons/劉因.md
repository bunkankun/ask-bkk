# Liú Yīn 劉因 (1249–1293)

*Zì* Mèngjí 夢吉, *hào* Jìngxiū 靜脩 (the *Jìngxiū xiānshēng* 靜脩先生 of the *Yuánshǐ Rúlín zhuàn*). Native of Róngchéng 容城 (modern Héběi Róngchéngxiàn, just southwest of Bao-ding 保定). Yuan-period Cheng-Zhu *Lǐxué* scholar — together with Xǔ Héng 許衡 (1209–81), one of the **two pillars of early-Yuán Neo-Confucianism**.

In Zhìyuán 19 (1282) Khubilai Khan summoned him to court as *Chéngdé láng* 承徳郎 兼 *Yòuzànshàn dàifū* 右贊善大夫; he served briefly, then resigned and returned home. He was again summoned as *Jíxián xuéshì* 集賢學士 but did not respond. His career — the only Yuán Confucian of his stature to systematically refuse Mongol office — gave him an ambivalent place in the Yuán *Lǐxué* canon: respected for his learning, but somewhat off-stage in political-institutional terms.

Surviving works in Kanripo:
- [[KR1h0031]] *Sìshū jíyì jīngyào* 四書集義精要 (28 *juàn*, WYG, incomplete) — a careful pruning of the post-Zhū-Xī *Sìshū jíyì* corpus, removing redundant material and selecting the substantive readings.

Liú Yīn's collected works are the *Jìngxiū xiānshēng wénjí* 靜脩先生文集 (KR4d corpus). He was the elder of Sū Tiānjué 蘇天爵 (the *Yuán wénlèi* 元文類 compiler), who praised this *Sìshū jíyì jīngyào* as "*jiǎnyán jīngdāng* 簡嚴精當" (concise-strict, precise-and-apposite) — the verdict echoed by the Sìkù tíyào at [[KR1h0031]].
