Zhào Xī-hú 趙希鵠 (*fl.* c. 1180–1240). A Sòng imperial clansman, listed in the *Sòng shǐ shì-xì biǎo* as a descendant of Tài-zǔ via the Yān-wáng Dé-zhāo 燕王德昭 branch. Domiciled at Yuán-zhōu 袁州 (Yí-chūn 宜春, Jiāng-xī); internal reference in his book records his return from Lǐng-yòu (Guǎng-dōng) to Yí-chūn in *Jiā-xī gēng-zǐ* (1240). His one surviving work, the *Dòng-tiān qīng-lù* 洞天清錄 ([[KR3j0167]]) in 1 *juàn*, is one of the foundational texts of Chinese antiquarian and connoisseurship literature, organized into eleven topical sections covering ancient *qín*, inkstones, bronzes, rare stones, water-droppers, brush-rests, calligraphy, stone-engravings, papers, and paintings. Beyond the date 1240 in the work itself, his career is undocumented. CBDB records no firm dates; Sìkù editors give *fl.* 1180–1240 as a conservative bracket.
