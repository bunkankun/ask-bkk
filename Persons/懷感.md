# Huáigǎn 懷感 (d. 699?)

A late seventh-century Tang Buddhist monk and one of the *Five Patriarchs* of the Chinese Pure Land tradition (in the standard *wǔ zǔ* enumeration: Lú-shān Huì-yuǎn → [[曇鸞]] Tán-luán → [[道綽]] Dàochuò → [[善導]] Shàn-dǎo → Huáigǎn → Shǎo-kāng → Yán-shòu, etc.). Native of *Cháng'ān* 長安. He was originally trained in the *Yúqié* 瑜伽 / Faxiang 法相 (Dharma-character) school of [[玄奘]] Xuánzàng (602–664) and his disciple [[窺基]] Kuíjī (632–682), and was a noted scholar of the *Yúqié shī-dì lùn*. According to the standard biography in *Sòng gāo-sēng zhuàn* 宋高僧傳 *juǎn* 6, Huáigǎn was sceptical of Pure Land devotion until he encountered Shàn-dǎo and engaged in an intensive niàn-fó retreat — after which he experienced a personal vision of Amitābha and was definitively converted to the Pure Land path.

His major work is the *Shì jìng-tǔ qún-yí lùn* 釋淨土群疑論 [[KR6p0039]] (T47N1960), a substantial seven-*juǎn* doctrinal treatise that collects and answers the principal Yogācāra-school objections to Pure Land devotion. The text is one of the longest and most doctrinally sophisticated Tang Pure Land works and represents the *Faxiang*-school engagement with Pure Land doctrine — the application of *vijñaptimātratā* (consciousness-only) ontology to the question of the Pure Land's metaphysical status. Modern scholarship (Mochizuki, Ueyama) treats Huáigǎn as the principal Tang Pure Land thinker engaging the Yogācāra tradition.

He died at *Qiān-fú-sì* 千福寺 in Cháng'ān, conventionally placed at 699. The *Qún-yí lùn* was completed by his disciple Huáiyùn 懷惲 — Huáigǎn died before finishing the manuscript, and the closing portions were edited and arranged by his pupil.
