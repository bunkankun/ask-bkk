Buddhabhadra (Fótuóbátuóluó 佛馱跋陀羅, "Awakened Worthy"; Chinese rendering *Juéxián* 覺賢; also *Fódùbátuóluó* 佛度跋陀羅 etc.) was an Indian translator-monk active in early-fifth-century China and one of the principal architects of the Chinese Buddhist textual heritage. Born in central India in 359 (per the *Gāosēng zhuàn* 高僧傳, T2059, 334b–335c) into a family of the Śākya clan, he studied meditation and Vinaya under Buddhasena (佛大先) in Kashmir before being summoned eastward at the request of the Chinese pilgrim Zhì Yán 智嚴 ([[智嚴]]). Travelling overland through the Pamirs and the Tarim oases, he reached Cháng'ān in or around 408 (variants 406/407 in the sources), where he initially collaborated with Kumārajīva (鳩摩羅什). A doctrinal-disciplinary clash with Kumārajīva's circle — Buddhabhadra was a strict *dhyāna*-practitioner of the northern meditational lineage, while the Cháng'ān assembly was developing its own scholastic style — led to his expulsion. He moved south, joining [[慧遠]] Huìyuǎn at Lúshān 廬山 (where he translated the *Dámóduōluó chán jīng* 達摩多羅禪經, T618, in 410–413), and finally settled at the *Dàochǎngsì* 道場寺 in Jiànkāng 建康 (modern Nánjīng) under the patronage of the Eastern Jìn court.

There, between 418 and 420, he led the translation of the *[[KR6e0001|Dà fāngguǎng fó huáyán jīng 大方廣佛華嚴經]]* / *Buddhāvataṃsaka* in 60 fascicles — the foundational text of the Chinese Huáyán tradition — working from a 36,000-verse Sanskrit (or Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit) original brought from Khotan by the monk Zhī Fǎlǐng 支法領, with [[法業]] Shì Fǎyè as scribe. He also translated the *Mahāsāṅghika Vinaya* (《摩訶僧祇律》, T1425) in collaboration with the returning pilgrim [[法顯]] Fǎxiǎn (416–418); the *Guān Fó sānmèi hǎi jīng* 觀佛三昧海經 (T643); the *Dà fāngděng rúláizàng jīng* 大方等如來藏經 (T666; the earliest Chinese rendering of the *Tathāgatagarbha-sūtra*); the *Wúliàngshòu jīng* 無量壽經 fragments; the *Fóshuō chūshēng wúliàngmén chí jīng* 佛說出生無量門持經 (T1012); and several short discourses and Vinaya-related works. He died at the Dàochǎng-sì in Yuánjiā 元嘉 6 (429) at the age of 71.

Buddhabhadra's importance is twofold. First, his translations introduced into Chinese several texts of fundamental importance for the development of distinctive East Asian Buddhist traditions — above all the *Huáyán jīng*, which became the scriptural basis of the Huáyán school under Dùshùn 杜順, [[智儼]] Zhìyǎn, and [[法藏]] Fǎzàng, and the *Tathāgatagarbhasūtra*, which seeded a long lineage of Chinese reflection on innate Buddha-nature. Second, he embodied the early-fifth-century encounter between the northern *dhyāna* tradition and southern Chinese court patronage: his Lúshān connection and his Dàochǎngsì translation bureau established a southern alternative to the Cháng'ān centre and shaped the institutional style of subsequent Liú-Sòng-period translation work.
