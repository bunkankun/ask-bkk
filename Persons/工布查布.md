**Gönpojab** / **Gōngbùchábù** 工布查布 (Qīng dynasty, mid-18th c.) — Mongol-Tibetan-Chinese translator and Buddhist iconographer of the Qīng court, native of the **Wūzhūmùqín** 烏朱穆秦 (Üjümüčin) Mongol tribe. Lifedates uncertain. He was distinguished for his command of Sanskrit, Tibetan, Mongolian, and Chinese, and was retained by the **Yongzheng Emperor** 世宗 (r. 1722–1735) at the imperial court as **Director-General of Western [Tibetan] Studies** (西番學總理). Under the **Qiánlóng Emperor** 高宗 (r. 1735–1796) he produced his three principal Chinese translations from Tibetan / Mongolian:

- **Fó shuō zàoxiàng liángdù jīng** 佛說造像量度經 (T0928 in some recensions; *Sūtra of Image-Construction Measurements*) — his most influential translation, the canonical reference for Buddhist iconography (proportional measurements of Buddha-images) in late-imperial China; substantially shaped Qing-period Buddhist sculpture and painting.
- **Fó shuō Mílè púsà fāyuànwáng jì** 佛說彌勒菩薩發願王偈 (T1144).
- **Yàoshī qī fó gòngyǎng yíguǐ rúyìwáng jīng** 藥師七佛供養儀軌如意王經 ([[KR6j0099]], T19n0927) — Esoteric ritual text for the Seven Medicine Buddhas (translated from Tibetan).

He also authored **Fó shuō zàoxiàng liángdù jīng jiě** 佛說造像量度經解 (T1419) and **Zàoxiàng liángdù jīng xùbǔ** 造像量度經續補 — auto-commentaries on the iconographic measurement scripture.

His translations are an important witness to the late-imperial Qīng-court Tibetan-Buddhist establishment and to the institutional integration of Tibetan Esoteric Buddhism into the late-imperial Chinese Buddhist canon.
