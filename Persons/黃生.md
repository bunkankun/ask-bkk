Late-Míng / early-Qīng philologist, *zì* Fú-mèng 扶孟, *hào* Bái-shān 白山, of Shè-xiàn 歙縣 (Huīzhōu, modern Anhui). Held the rank of *zhū-shēng* under the Míng but did not take office under the Qing, working privately as a scholar after the dynastic transition. His two surviving philological works are the *Zì-gǔ* [[KR1j0014]] (a one-*juàn* miscellany on individual graphs, modeled in title on Zhāng Yī's [[張揖]] lost *Gǔ-jīn zì-gǔ*) and the more substantial *Yì-fǔ* 義府 (two *juàn*, also in the Sìkù), both centered on *liù-shū* analysis and on testing received glosses against citations from across the *Classics* and Hàn-Wèi sources. Wáng Niànsūn and the Gāo-yóu Wáng-shì Qing philologists drew on Huáng's analyses regularly. CBDB lifedates 1622–1696. (Note: the catalog meta gives only "b. 1622," but CBDB and the *tíyào* together pin both birth and death dates.)
