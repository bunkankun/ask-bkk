Late-Míng / early-Qīng philologist, *zì* Fúmèng 扶孟, *hào* Báishān 白山, of Shèxiàn 歙縣 (Huīzhōu, modern Anhui). Held the rank of *zhūshēng* under the Míng but did not take office under the Qing, working privately as a scholar after the dynastic transition. His two surviving philological works are the *Zìgǔ* [[KR1j0014]] (a one-*juàn* miscellany on individual graphs, modeled in title on Zhāng Yī's [[張揖]] lost *Gǔjīn zìgǔ*) and the more substantial *Yìfǔ* 義府 (two *juàn*, also in the Sìkù), both centered on *liùshū* analysis and on testing received glosses against citations from across the *Classics* and HànWèi sources. Wáng Niànsūn and the Gāoyóu Wángshì Qing philologists drew on Huáng's analyses regularly. CBDB lifedates 1622–1696. (Note: the catalog meta gives only "b. 1622," but CBDB and the *tíyào* together pin both birth and death dates.)
