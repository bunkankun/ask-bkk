Shēn Hán-guāng 申涵光 (1619–1677), *zì* **Fú-mèng** 孚孟 (also written 鳧盟), *hào* **Cōng-shān** 聰山; native of **Yǒng-nián** 永年 (Hé-běi). Eldest son of the late-Míng martyr [[申佳胤]] (1603–1644). After his father's suicide at the *Chóng-zhēn* end-times of 1644 he refused the new dynasty's calls to office and lived out his life as a *yí-mín* in Yǒng-nián. Best known as the founder of the early-Qīng **Jī-fǔ shī-pài** 畿輔詩派 ('Northern Metropolitan poetry school' — i.e. the Hé-běi-based Northern alternative to the dominant Wú-Yuè and Jiāng-nán schools); with his younger brothers Hán-pàn 涵盼 and Hán-fēn 涵盻 (the *Yǒng-nián Shēn shì sān xiōng-dì* 永年申氏三兄弟) and his disciples Yǐn Yuè 殷岳, Liú Yīn 劉因 and others, he constituted the dominant Northern poetic circle of the *Shùn-zhì*–*Kāng-xī* transition. His own collected works are the *Cōng-shān-jí xuǎn* 聰山集選. He edited his father's surviving verse into the *Shēn Zhōng-mǐn shī-jí* 申忠愍詩集 in six *juǎn* ([[KR4e0242]]) and supplied a *jiā-zhuàn* (family biography) of his father, which the *Sìkù* tiyao quotes. The standard biography is in *Qīng-shǐ liè-zhuàn*. CBDB 65917.
