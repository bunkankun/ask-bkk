Lǐ Déyù 李德裕 (787–850, *zì* Wénráo 文饒), of *Zhàojùn* 趙郡 (in fact a *Chángān* native by long residence), was the dominant chief minister of the *Wǔzōng* / *Huìchāng* period (840–846) and the leading figure on the *Lǐ* side of the *NiúLǐ* factional struggle that defined late-Táng court politics. Son of the chief minister Lǐ Jífǔ 李吉甫 (758–814), he entered service through the *yīn* (hereditary) privilege rather than through *jìnshì* — a fact his political enemies (the *Niú* faction, identifying themselves with the examination-elite) used against him throughout his career.

His tenures as chief minister: under *Mùzōng* (821), under *Wénzōng* briefly, and the dominant tenure under *Wǔzōng* (840–846). The *Huìchāng* program included: the suppression of Buddhism (841–845, the *Huìchāng fǎnán* — closing some 4,600 monasteries and laicizing 260,000 monks and nuns); the campaign against the Uyghur (*Huíhú*) remnants on the northern frontier; and the resolution of the *Liú Zhěn* / *Zélù* military settlement (843–844). At the *Xuānzōng* succession in 846 he was demoted, then progressively exiled — finally to *Yázhōu* 崖州 (modern *Hǎinán*), where he died in 850. His last work, the *Qióngchóu zhì* 窮愁志 (4 *juǎn* of historical essays), was composed in *Yázhōu* exile.

Principal work in the corpus: *Huìchāng yīpǐn jí* [[KR4c0067]] (20 *juǎn zhèngjí* + 10 *juǎn biéjí* + 4 *juǎn wàijí*); also *Cì Liǔshì jiùwén* (separately catalogued elsewhere in KR).
