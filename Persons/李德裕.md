Lǐ Dé-yù 李德裕 (787–850, *zì* Wén-ráo 文饒), of *Zhào-jùn* 趙郡 (in fact a *Cháng-ān* native by long residence), was the dominant chief minister of the *Wǔ-zōng* / *Huì-chāng* period (840–846) and the leading figure on the *Lǐ* side of the *Niú-Lǐ* factional struggle that defined late-Táng court politics. Son of the chief minister Lǐ Jí-fǔ 李吉甫 (758–814), he entered service through the *yīn* (hereditary) privilege rather than through *jìn-shì* — a fact his political enemies (the *Niú* faction, identifying themselves with the examination-elite) used against him throughout his career.

His tenures as chief minister: under *Mù-zōng* (821), under *Wén-zōng* briefly, and the dominant tenure under *Wǔ-zōng* (840–846). The *Huì-chāng* program included: the suppression of Buddhism (841–845, the *Huì-chāng fǎ-nán* — closing some 4,600 monasteries and laicizing 260,000 monks and nuns); the campaign against the Uyghur (*Huí-hú*) remnants on the northern frontier; and the resolution of the *Liú Zhěn* / *Zé-lù* military settlement (843–844). At the *Xuān-zōng* succession in 846 he was demoted, then progressively exiled — finally to *Yá-zhōu* 崖州 (modern *Hǎi-nán*), where he died in 850. His last work, the *Qióng-chóu zhì* 窮愁志 (4 *juǎn* of historical essays), was composed in *Yá-zhōu* exile.

Principal work in the corpus: *Huì-chāng yī-pǐn jí* [[KR4c0067]] (20 *juǎn zhèng-jí* + 10 *juǎn bié-jí* + 4 *juǎn wài-jí*); also *Cì Liǔ-shì jiù-wén* (separately catalogued elsewhere in KR).
