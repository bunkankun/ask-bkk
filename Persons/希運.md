Huángbò Xīyùn 黃檗希運 (d. Dàzhōng 4 = 850; birth year unrecorded), Táng-dynasty Hóngzhōu-school 洪州宗 Chán master, dharma-heir of Bǎizhàng Huáihǎi 百丈懷海 (720–814, DILA A001897) and grand-disciple of Mǎzǔ Dàoyī 馬祖道一 (709–788). Posthumous title Duànjì chánshī 斷際禪師 ("Chán Master of Severing-the-Limit"). Native of Mǐn-xiàn 閩縣 (Fújiàn). Left home young at the Huángbò shān 黃檗山 monastery in Fújiàn; later travelled to Jiāngxī and received dharma-transmission from Bǎizhàng at the Hóngzhōu Bǎizhàng shān 百丈山; thereafter returned to occupy the abbacy of the Huángbò shān 黃檗山 in Gāo'ān xiàn 高安縣 of Hóngzhōu 洪州 (modern Jiāngxī), carrying his old mountain-name with him to the new seat — the convention that every subsequent mountain he resided on was renamed "Huángbò" after his original home. Died Dàzhōng 4 (850).

Xīyùn's doctrinal signature is *yī xīn* 一心 ("one mind") — "all Buddhas and all sentient beings are nothing but one mind, and there is no separate dharma"; the identification of mind with Buddha; the rejection of *xiū-zhèng* 修證 (cultivation-and-verification) as obscuring rather than producing awakening; and the insistence on direct recognition that *cǐ xīn jí shì fó* 此心即是佛 ("this mind is simply the Buddha"). His teaching is the doctrinal bridge between Mǎzǔ Dàoyī's Hóngzhōu-school formulations and [[義玄|Línjì Yìxuán]]'s 臨濟義玄 own, more polemically-direct *yǔlù* voice.

Principal recorded dharma-heirs: [[義玄|Línjì Yìxuán]] 臨濟義玄 (d. 867, DILA A003627) — founder of the Línjì 臨濟 school, one of the Five Houses of classical Chán; Chǔnán 楚南 (A003631); Língguān 靈觀 (A003632); and several others. The Línjì lineage that subsequently dominated East-Asian Chán / Sŏn / Zen descends entirely through Xīyùn.

Works in the Kanripo corpus: [[KR6q0087]] *Huángbò shān Duànjì chánshī chuánxīn fǎyào* 黃檗山斷際禪師傳心法要 (1 juan, T48 n2012A) and [[KR6q0088]] *Wǎnlíng lù* 宛陵錄 (T48 n2012B) — both compiled by the lay disciple [[裴休]] Péi Xiū from his 842–843 and 848–849 teaching-sessions with Xīyùn, consolidated and prefaced by Péi in Dàzhōng 11 (857).
