Niú Sēng-rú 牛僧孺 (780–848 or 849; *zì* Sī-àn 思黯; ennobled as Qí-zhāng-jùn-gōng 奇章郡公, hence often called Niú Qí-zhāng 牛奇章), of Ān-dìng 安定 (modern Gānsù), was one of the leading statesmen and *xiǎo-shuō* writers of the mid-Táng. His standard biography is *Jiù Táng shū* 172.4469-73; CBDB id 32054. He passed the *jìn-shì* in *Yuán-hé* 元和 3 (808) and rose through a series of provincial and ministerial offices to *Zǎi-xiàng* (chief minister) under both Jìng-zōng 敬宗 (824–826) and Wén-zōng 文宗 (826–840), and to *Tài-zǐ shào-shī* 太子少師 under Wǔ-zōng 武宗. He is the principal figure of the *Niú* faction in the protracted *Niú-Lǐ* 牛李 factional dispute (Lǐ being [[李德裕]] Lǐ Dé-yù), the dominant political quarrel of the *Mù-zōng* — *Wǔ-zōng* period. Demoted late under Wǔ-zōng, he was rehabilitated under Xuān-zōng 宣宗 and died in 848 (some sources 849).

As a *xiǎo-shuō* writer, Niú Sēng-rú is the author of the *Xuán-guài lù* 玄怪錄 ([[KR3l0095]]) — the great mid-Táng *zhì-guài* and *chuán-qí* anthology in 10 *juàn*, which inaugurated the late-Táng tradition of fully-developed *chuán-qí* fiction as a genre distinct from earlier *zhì-guài*. His *Xuán-guài lù* influenced his successor Lǐ Fù-yán's 李復言 *Xù xuán-guài lù* 續玄怪錄 ([[KR3l0096]]) and, through his maternal grandson [[張讀]] Zhāng Dú, the *Xuān-shì zhì* 宣室志 ([[KR3l0110]]) — making the Niú-line family-literary tradition the principal source of Táng anomaly-anthology writing across three generations. He is also noted in late-Táng sources as a major patron of Chán 禪 Buddhist monasteries (notably the *Tiān-tāi shān* 天台山 establishments) and of Mao-shan 茅山 Daoism. Wilkinson §62 cites him as a foundational figure of the Táng *chuán-qí* / *biji* line.
