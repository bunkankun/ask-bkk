Niú Sēngrú 牛僧孺 (780–848 or 849; *zì* Sīàn 思黯; ennobled as Qízhāngjùngōng 奇章郡公, hence often called Niú Qízhāng 牛奇章), of Āndìng 安定 (modern Gānsù), was one of the leading statesmen and *xiǎoshuō* writers of the mid-Táng. His standard biography is *Jiù Táng shū* 172.4469-73; CBDB id 32054. He passed the *jìnshì* in *Yuánhé* 元和 3 (808) and rose through a series of provincial and ministerial offices to *Zǎixiàng* (chief minister) under both Jìngzōng 敬宗 (824–826) and Wénzōng 文宗 (826–840), and to *Tàizǐ shàoshī* 太子少師 under Wǔzōng 武宗. He is the principal figure of the *Niú* faction in the protracted *NiúLǐ* 牛李 factional dispute (Lǐ being [[李德裕]] Lǐ Déyù), the dominant political quarrel of the *Mùzōng* — *Wǔzōng* period. Demoted late under Wǔzōng, he was rehabilitated under Xuānzōng 宣宗 and died in 848 (some sources 849).

As a *xiǎoshuō* writer, Niú Sēngrú is the author of the *Xuánguài lù* 玄怪錄 ([[KR3l0095]]) — the great mid-Táng *zhìguài* and *chuánqí* anthology in 10 *juàn*, which inaugurated the late-Táng tradition of fully-developed *chuánqí* fiction as a genre distinct from earlier *zhìguài*. His *Xuánguài lù* influenced his successor Lǐ Fùyán's 李復言 *Xù xuánguài lù* 續玄怪錄 ([[KR3l0096]]) and, through his maternal grandson [[張讀]] Zhāng Dú, the *Xuānshì zhì* 宣室志 ([[KR3l0110]]) — making the Niú-line family-literary tradition the principal source of Táng anomaly-anthology writing across three generations. He is also noted in late-Táng sources as a major patron of Chán 禪 Buddhist monasteries (notably the *Tiāntāi shān* 天台山 establishments) and of Mao-shan 茅山 Daoism. Wilkinson §62 cites him as a foundational figure of the Táng *chuánqí* / *biji* line.
