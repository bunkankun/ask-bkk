**Kǒng Yǎn** 孔衍 (268–320), *zì* Shūyuán 舒元, twenty-second-generation descendant of Confucius (the *Zhōng-gōng* 中宮 line), native of Lǔ-jùn Qūfù 魯郡曲阜. His standard biography stands in *Jìn shū* 78 (列傳 48). A precocious classicist, recommended young into the Western-Jìn court, he held the offices of *zhōng-shū láng* 中書郎 and *zhuǎn xiào-jūn cān-jūn* 校軍參軍 under Sīmǎ Yī and Sīmǎ Ruì. With the Yǒngjiā disaster of 311 he followed Sīmǎ Ruì south to Jiànkāng, where under Yuándì he was made *Píng-yuán jì-shǐ* and later *Zhōng-shū shì-láng*. He composed two principal classical-history compendia, the *Hàn Wèi chūnqiū* 漢魏春秋 in 9 *juàn* and the *Hòu-Hàn shàngshū* 後漢尚書 in 6 *juàn* (both lost, surviving as fragments), and was a leading court ritualist of the early Eastern-Jìn period.

In this corpus he appears as the attributed author of [[KR1d0097]] *Xiōnglǐ* — a freestanding treatise on the *xiōnglǐ* (mourning-rite) division of the *Wǔlǐ* system, preserved in *Tōngdiǎn* citations and including the sub-treatises *Shìmiào cáng zhǔ shì lùn*, *Jìn zhāo-hún zàng lùn*, and *Guāilí lùn*.
