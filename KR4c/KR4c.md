# KR4c 別集類·唐五代 — Individual Collected Works of the Tang and Five Dynasties

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR4c sits inside the Sìkù *jíbù* 集部 *biéjí lèi* 別集類 (Collections → individual collected works), which the *Sìkù quánshū zǒngmù* arranges chronologically. KR4c is the **Tang and Five Dynasties** segment — the *biéjí* of named individual writers active between the early seventh century (Wáng Jì 王績, d. 644) and the close of the *Wǔdài* tenth century (Shì Qíjǐ 釋齊己's *Báilián jí*, prefaced 938; Dù Guāngtíng 杜光庭's *Guǎngchéng jí*, *fl.* 901–933). It is the largest single dynastic block of *biéjí* in the entire Kanripo corpus, reflecting the Sìkù compilers' inheritance of just under a thousand recorded Tang *biéjí* titles (of which roughly a quarter survived to the eighteenth century) — see Wilkinson, *Chinese History: A New Manual*, §61.3.10 for the survival figures, and §30.4 for the *biéjí* form generally.

The division reads in two registers at once. Its primary axis is the conventional periodization of Tang poetry into 初唐 (early), 盛唐 (high), 中唐 (mid), and 晚唐 (late) — the four-stage canon codified in Yán Yǔ's 嚴羽 *Cānglàng shīhuà* and naturalized into every late-imperial anthology — together with the *Wǔdài* tail. Its secondary axis is the SòngYuánMíngQīng *commentary apparatus* on the canonical Tang masters: roughly thirty of the 113 KR4c entries are not Tang autographs at all but later philological / annotated editions, especially the Sòng *jízhù* and Qīng *xiángzhù* productions on Dù Fǔ, Lǐ Bái, Hán Yù, Liǔ Zōngyuán, Wáng Wéi, Lǐ Shāngyǐn, Wēn Tíngyún, and Lǐ Hè. The division is therefore as much a record of how the Tang was *read* by the SòngYuánMíngQīng tradition as it is a record of Tang writing itself. For the Wilkinson framing of the literary corpus see §54 (Tang literature), which identifies Wáng Wéi, Dù Fǔ, Lǐ Bái, and Bái Jūyì as the High-Tang core and frames Hán YùLiǔ Zōngyuán as the *gǔwén* anchor.

## Important texts and text clusters

- **The Four Outstanding Ones of the early Tang (初唐四傑)** — [[KR4c0003]] *Wáng Zǐān jí* (Wáng Bó), [[KR4c0004]] *Yíngchuān jí* (Yáng Jiǒng), [[KR4c0005]] *Lú Shēngzhī jí* (Lú Zhàolín), and [[KR4c0006]]–[[KR4c0007]] (Luò Bīnwáng). With [[KR4c0001]] *Dōnggāo zǐ jí* (Wáng Jì), [[KR4c0008]] *Chén Shíyí jí* (Chén Zǐáng), [[KR4c0009]] *Zhāng Yāngōng jí* (Zhāng Shuō), [[KR4c0010]] *Lǐ Běihǎi jí* (Lǐ Yōng), and [[KR4c0093]] *Sòng Zhīwèn jí*, these form the early-Tang cluster whose stylistic transition from *liùcháo* ornament to mature regulated verse is the conventional point of departure for the High Tang.
- **The High-Tang core (盛唐)** — [[KR4c0011]] *Qǔjiāng jí* (Zhāng Jiǔlíng), [[KR4c0012]] *Lǐ Tàibái wén jí* (Lǐ Bái), [[KR4c0015]] / [[KR4c0017]] / [[KR4c0018]] / [[KR4c0020]] (the principal annotated editions of Dù Fǔ), [[KR4c0021]] / [[KR4c0022]] (annotated Wáng Wéi), [[KR4c0023]] *Gāo Chángshì jí* (Gāo Shì), [[KR4c0024]] *Cháng Jiàn shī*, [[KR4c0025]] *Mèng Hàorán jí*, [[KR4c0026]] *Chǔ Guāngxī shī jí*, [[KR4c0029]] *Cén Jiāzhōu shī* (Cén Shēn). The four named pillars of High-Tang verse — Wáng Wéi, Mèng Hàorán, Lǐ Bái, Dù Fǔ — are the canonical anchor; Gāo Shì and Cén Shēn carry the *biānsài* 邊塞 (frontier) sub-style.
- **The Dù Fǔ commentary genealogy** — [[KR4c0015]] *Jiǔjiā jízhù Dù shī* (Guō Zhīdá, 1181), [[KR4c0017]] *Bǔzhù Dù shī* (the Huáng Xī / Huáng Hè *fùzǐ* annotation, 1216), [[KR4c0018]] *Jí qiānjiā zhù Dù gōngbù shī jí* (Gāo Chǔfāng, *ca.* 1300), [[KR4c0019]] *Dù shī jūn* (Táng Yuánhóng, Míng), and [[KR4c0020]] *Dù shī xiángzhù* (Qiú Zhàoáo, 1693). Together they form the densest single-poet commentary tradition in Chinese letters.
- **The Lǐ Bái commentary genealogy** — [[KR4c0012]] (the Sòng Mǐnqiú / Zēng Gǒng 30-*juǎn* recension), [[KR4c0013]] *Lǐ Tàibái jí fēnlèi bǔzhù* (Yáng Qíxián / Xiāo Shìyún, 1310–20), [[KR4c0014]] Wáng Qí's 1758 *jízhù*.
- **The Hán Yù commentary genealogy (the densest critical-apparatus cluster in the division)** — [[KR4c0042]] *Hán jí jǔzhèng* (Fāng Sōngqīng, 1189), [[KR4c0043]] *Yuánběn Hán jí kǎoyì* (Zhū Xī's *Kǎoyì*), [[KR4c0044]] *Biéběn Hán wén kǎoyì* (Wáng Bódà's editorial recasting), [[KR4c0045]] *Wǔbǎijiā zhù Chānglí wén jí* (Wèi Zhòngjǔ, 1228–33), [[KR4c0046]] *Dōngyǎtáng Chānglí jí zhù* (Liào Yíngzhōng), [[KR4c0047]] *Hán jí diǎnkān* (Chén Jǐngyún, Qīng).
- **The Liǔ Zōngyuán cluster** — [[KR4c0048]] *Liǔ Hédōng jí* (Hán Chún's *gǔxùn*, 1170s), [[KR4c0049]] *Liǔ Hédōng jí zhù* (Tóng Zōngshuō / Zhāng Dūnyí / Pān Wěi), [[KR4c0050]] *Wǔbǎijiā zhù Liǔ xiānshēng jí* (Wèi Zhòngjǔ's matching anthology to the Hán Yù 五百家). The HánLiǔ pair, edited in parallel at *Jiànān* and at *Jiànyáng*, is the consummation of the Sòng *gǔwén* canon-building project.
- **The YuánBái 元白 pairing and the 新樂府 movement** — [[KR4c0068]] *Yuánshì Chángqìng jí* and [[KR4c0069]] *Báishì Chángqìng jí*, the two collections that together preserve the *xīn yuèfǔ* manifesto-cycle and the canonical mid-Tang social-realist verse. [[KR4c0070]] Wāng Lìmíng's 1702 *Bái Xiāngshān shī jí* is the Qīng critical edition.
- **The WǎnTáng LǐDù pair** — [[KR4c0072]] *Fánchuān jí* (Dù Mù) and [[KR4c0074]] *Lǐ Yìshān shī jí* (Lǐ Shāngyǐn), with [[KR4c0075]] *Lǐ Yìshān shī jí zhù* (Zhū Hèlíng, Qīng) and [[KR4c0076]] *Lǐ Yìshān wénjí jiānzhù* (Xú Shùgǔ + Xú Jiǒng).
- **The 韓孟 difficult-bitter school** — [[KR4c0058]] *Mèng Dōngyě shī jí* (Mèng Jiāo), [[KR4c0059]] *Chángjiāng jí* (Jiǎ Dǎo), [[KR4c0060]] *Chānggǔ jí* + [[KR4c0061]] *Gēshī biān* + [[KR4c0062]] *Jiānzhù píngdiǎn Lǐ Chángjí gēshī* (the three Lǐ Hè editions), [[KR4c0063]] *Jiàngshǒu jūyuánchí jì* (Fán Zōngshī's notoriously cryptic prose piece), [[KR4c0092]] *Yùchuānzǐ shī jí* (Lú Tóng). These constitute the *qíjué* (strange-and-sharp) counter-current to the *YuánBái* popularizing program.
- **The Buddhist monk-poets** — [[KR4c0002]] *Hánshān shī jí* (the early-9th-century Cold Mountain corpus, with Fēnggān and Shídé), [[KR4c0031]] *Zhùshān jí* (Shì Jiǎorán, the principal *Dàlì* monk-poet and theorist of *jìng* 境), [[KR4c0109]] *Báilián jí* (Shì Qíjǐ), [[KR4c0110]] *Chányuè jí* (Shì Guànxiū). Together these are the textual foundation of the late-Tang and Sòng *shīsēng* 詩僧 tradition.
- **The political-document prose canon** — [[KR4c0039]] *Hànyuàn jí* (Lù Zhì's *Hànlínyuàn zhìgào*), [[KR4c0067]] *Huìchāng yīpǐn jí* (Lǐ Déyù, the *NiúLǐ* faction's *Lǐ* head as *Wǔzōng*'s chief minister), [[KR4c0040]]–[[KR4c0041]] (Quán Déyú, the YuánHé court drafter). These are the canonical Tang official-document *biéjí*, models for the Sòng *Hànlínyuàn* drafting tradition.
- **The Five-Dynasties / Late-Tang holdovers** — [[KR4c0086]] *Bìyún jí* (Lǐ Zhōng, 南唐), [[KR4c0111]] *Huànhuā jí* (Wéi Zhuāng), [[KR4c0112]] *Guǎngchéng jí* (Dù Guāngtíng, the foundational late-Tang Daoist literary corpus, under 後蜀 Wáng Jiàn patronage), [[KR4c0114]] *Lǐ chéngxiàng shījí* (Lǐ Jiànxūn, 南唐). These mark the dynastic boundary that the Sìkù compilers drew loosely: poets and prose-writers active well into the *Wǔdài* but trained inside Tang literary conventions remain in this category rather than passing into KR4d.
- **The Korean Tang-resident** — [[KR4c0100]] *Guìyuàn bǐgēng jí* (Chǒe Ch'iwǒn 崔致遠), the Silla aristocrat-turned-Tang *jìnshì* whose collected *Yángzhōu* staff correspondence is the most important Tang-period East Asian diplomatic literary corpus.

## Important persons

- [[李白]] — the supreme High-Tang lyricist; the *LǐDù* pairing with [[杜甫]] anchors the High-Tang canon.
- [[杜甫]] — the *shī shèng* 詩聖, the dense-textured High-Tang corpus that absorbed the entire later commentary tradition.
- [[王維]] — the *shānshuǐ tiányuán* master, the *WángMèng* pair with [[孟浩然]].
- [[白居易]] — the *YuánBái* social-realist core; the most widely circulated Tang poet during his own lifetime, and the East Asian (Japanese Heian, Koryŏ) canonical Tang voice.
- [[元稹]] — Bái's lifelong literary partner; author of the *Yīngyīng zhuàn* 鶯鶯傳 and the 1812 *xīn yuèfǔ* manifesto.
- [[韓愈]] — leader of the Tang *gǔwén* movement; the most heavily-commentated Tang prose corpus.
- [[柳宗元]] — Hán Yù's *gǔwén* partner and equally heavily-commentated.
- [[陳子昂]] — author of the *Gǎn yù* cycle, the conventional founder of the High-Tang *fùgǔ* program.
- [[張九齡]] — the *Kāiyuán* *zǎixiàng* whose *Gǎn yù* cycle continues Chén Zǐáng's lineage and feeds directly into Lǐ Bái's *Gǔ fēng*.
- [[孟浩然]] — the *Xiāngyáng* hermit-poet, the *WángMèng* counterpart to Wáng Wéi.
- [[韋應物]] — *Wéi Sūzhōu*, the *TáoWéi* pair with Táo Qián, principal post-rebellion contemplative-landscape voice.
- [[杜牧]] — the *WǎnTáng* prose-and-verse master; the *Zuì yán* on the *fānzhèn* crisis.
- [[李商隱]] — Dù Mù's *WǎnTáng* counterpart, the *wútí* lyricist, principal model of the Northern-Sòng *Xīkūn tǐ*.
- [[孟郊]] / [[賈島]] / [[李賀]] / [[盧仝]] — the *HánMèng* difficult-bitter school of late-mid-Tang verse.
- [[釋皎然]] — *Dàlì* poet-monk and theorist (*Shī shì* 詩式); the bridge between High-Tang practice and late-Tang critical theory.
- [[釋寒山]] / [[釋貫休]] / [[釋齊己]] — the three canonical Tang monk-poets, foundational for the Sòng *Chán* poetic tradition and (for Hánshān) for twentieth-century Anglophone reception.
- [[陸贄]] — the supreme *Hànlínyuàn* drafter, the model for all Sòng official-document prose.
- [[司空圖]] — late-Tang martyr-recluse and (traditionally) author of the *Èrshísì shīpǐn*.
- [[羅隱]] / [[皮日休]] / [[陸龜蒙]] / [[韋莊]] — the WǎnTáng to Wǔdài tail of socially-engaged verse.
- [[杜光庭]] — the foundational late-Tang / 後蜀 Daoist literary voice.
- [[崔致遠]] — Silla diplomat-literatus, the principal East Asian Tang-correspondence corpus.
- [[宋敏求]] — Sòng compiler whose recensional work on Lǐ Bái ([[KR4c0012]]), Liú Yǔxī ([[KR4c0051]]), Mèng Jiāo ([[KR4c0058]]), and Yán Zhēnqīng ([[KR4c0028]]) underlies the standard SòngYuán text of half the Tang canon.
- [[朱熹]] — author of the *Hán wén kǎoyì* ([[KR4c0043]]), the most influential Sòng critical apparatus on the Tang corpus.
- [[魏仲擧]] — *Jiànān* commercial publisher whose matching *Wǔbǎijiā* anthologies ([[KR4c0045]], [[KR4c0050]]) defined the YuánMíng *kējǔ*-market reception of Hán Yù and Liǔ Zōngyuán.
- [[仇兆鰲]] — Qīng author of the *Dù shī xiángzhù* ([[KR4c0020]]), still the standard variorum on Dù Fǔ.
- [[王琦]] / [[趙殿成]] / [[馮集梧]] / [[朱鶴齡]] — the Qīng critical-edition workshop on Lǐ Bái, Wáng Wéi, Dù Mù, and Lǐ Shāngyǐn respectively.

## Topics

- **The four-stage canon: 初/盛/中/晚 Tang.** The chronological periodization codified in Yán Yǔ's *Cānglàng shīhuà* and naturalized into every MíngQīng anthology runs as the unspoken organizational principle of the division. Each phase has its emblematic figures (the *Sì jié* and Chén Zǐáng in the early; WángMèngLǐDù in the high; HánLiǔYuánBái in the mid; LǐDùWēn in the late) and its emblematic stylistic register.
- **The 古文運動 (Hán YùLiǔ Zōngyuán *gǔwén* movement).** The mid-Tang programmatic return to "ancient prose" — *HànWèi*-style direct argument-and-narrative against the *liùcháo* parallel-prose tradition — anchored in [[韓愈]] and [[柳宗元]] and theorized in their letters. The downstream Sòng commentary apparatus (the Fāng Sōngqīng / Zhū Xī / Wáng Bódà / Wèi Zhòngjǔ / Liào Yíngzhōng / Chén Jǐngyún chain) is the densest critical-edition genealogy of any Tang prose corpus.
- **新樂府 vs. 苦吟 vs. 古體**. The mid-Tang split between [[白居易]] and [[元稹]]'s social-realist *xīn yuèfǔ* program (with its accessible diction and political-didactic aim), the *HánMèng* difficult-bitter school of [[孟郊]], [[賈島]], [[李賀]], and [[盧仝]] (recondite vocabulary and bizarre image), and the [[李白]]-Chén Zǐáng *fùgǔ* line of archaic *gǔfēng* — three competing visions of what mid-Tang verse should be doing.
- **山水田園 (landscape-and-farmstead) and the *TáoWéiWángMèng* lineage.** The post-Táo Qián 陶潛 contemplative landscape mode, anchored in [[王維]] and [[孟浩然]] in the High Tang and in [[韋應物]] and [[柳宗元]] in the mid Tang; Sū Shì's Sòng-period codification of the *TáoWéi* pair defined the SòngYuánMíng reading.
- **邊塞 (frontier) verse.** [[高適]] and [[岑參]] (with [[王昌齡]]) as the principal voices of the High-Tang frontier-campaign genre — a register that thins out almost completely after the *An Lùshān* rebellion.
- **The *WǎnTáng* dense-allusive style and its Northern Sòng inheritance.** [[李商隱]]'s *wútí* poems and [[杜牧]]'s allusive *juéjù* became the model for the early-Sòng *Xīkūn tǐ* 西崑體 of Yáng Yì, Liú Zǐyí, and Qián Wéiyǎn; the Sòng *Yuányòu* generation's reaction against this style is one of the principal storylines of the *Wényuán yīnghuá* / *Tángshī* anthology selection process.
- **Buddhist and Daoist literary participation.** The monks ([[釋皎然]], [[釋寒山]], [[釋貫休]], [[釋齊己]]) and the Daoist priest-writers ([[吳筠]], [[杜光庭]]) hold a distinct register: half *shī*, half *jì* (gāthā) / Daoist ritual hymn, with circulation through monastic-archive transmission rather than the official *biéjí* network.
- **The SòngYuánMíngQīng commentary enterprise.** A third of the KR4c entries are not Tang-period autographs but later annotated, collated, or anthologized editions. The Sòng commentary on Dù Fǔ alone runs to five major recensions in this division; Hán Yù has six; Liǔ Zōngyuán three. The KR4c material is therefore as much a record of Tang reception-history as of Tang composition.
- **Recension and textual problem.** The transmission of every major Tang collection is bibliographically distinctive. Bái Jūyì curated his own corpus by depositing manuscript copies at *Dōnglín sì*, *Shèngshàn sì*, *Sūzhōu Nánchán yuàn*, and *Xiāngshān sì* ([[KR4c0069]]); Lǐ Bái's collection was assembled by his cousin Lǐ Yángbīng at his deathbed and lay almost lost until the *Xiánpíng* (998–1003) recovery ([[KR4c0012]]); Wáng Bó's corpus is partly recovered from a *Shōsō-in* Tang MS ([[KR4c0003]]); the Chén Zǐáng *juǎn* 7 lacunae are filled from the *Wén yuàn yīng huá* ([[KR4c0008]]). The transmission stories are themselves a major chapter in Tang literary history.
- **The dynastic boundary.** The *Wǔdài* Five-Dynasties holdovers — [[釋齊己]] under *Jīngnán Nánpíng*, [[釋貫休]] under *Former Shǔ*, [[韋莊]] under *Former Shǔ*, [[李建勳]] under *NánTáng*, [[杜光庭]] under *後蜀* — are filed in KR4c rather than in the Sòng division, on the principle that Tang training rather than Tang reign-date governs categorization.
