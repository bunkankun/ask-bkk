# KR4c 別集類·唐五代 — Individual Collected Works of the Tang and Five Dynasties

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR4c sits inside the Sìkù *jí-bù* 集部 *bié-jí lèi* 別集類 (Collections → individual collected works), which the *Sìkù quánshū zǒngmù* arranges chronologically. KR4c is the **Tang and Five Dynasties** segment — the *bié-jí* of named individual writers active between the early seventh century (Wáng Jì 王績, d. 644) and the close of the *Wǔ-dài* tenth century (Shì Qí-jǐ 釋齊己's *Bái-lián jí*, prefaced 938; Dù Guāng-tíng 杜光庭's *Guǎng-chéng jí*, *fl.* 901–933). It is the largest single dynastic block of *bié-jí* in the entire Kanripo corpus, reflecting the Sìkù compilers' inheritance of just under a thousand recorded Tang *bié-jí* titles (of which roughly a quarter survived to the eighteenth century) — see Wilkinson, *Chinese History: A New Manual*, §61.3.10 for the survival figures, and §30.4 for the *bié-jí* form generally.

The division reads in two registers at once. Its primary axis is the conventional periodization of Tang poetry into 初唐 (early), 盛唐 (high), 中唐 (mid), and 晚唐 (late) — the four-stage canon codified in Yán Yǔ's 嚴羽 *Cāng-làng shī-huà* and naturalized into every late-imperial anthology — together with the *Wǔ-dài* tail. Its secondary axis is the Sòng-Yuán-Míng-Qīng *commentary apparatus* on the canonical Tang masters: roughly thirty of the 113 KR4c entries are not Tang autographs at all but later philological / annotated editions, especially the Sòng *jí-zhù* and Qīng *xiáng-zhù* productions on Dù Fǔ, Lǐ Bái, Hán Yù, Liǔ Zōng-yuán, Wáng Wéi, Lǐ Shāng-yǐn, Wēn Tíng-yún, and Lǐ Hè. The division is therefore as much a record of how the Tang was *read* by the Sòng-Yuán-Míng-Qīng tradition as it is a record of Tang writing itself. For the Wilkinson framing of the literary corpus see §54 (Tang literature), which identifies Wáng Wéi, Dù Fǔ, Lǐ Bái, and Bái Jū-yì as the High-Tang core and frames Hán Yù-Liǔ Zōng-yuán as the *gǔ-wén* anchor.

## Important texts and text clusters

- **The Four Outstanding Ones of the early Tang (初唐四傑)** — [[KR4c0003]] *Wáng Zǐ-ān jí* (Wáng Bó), [[KR4c0004]] *Yíng-chuān jí* (Yáng Jiǒng), [[KR4c0005]] *Lú Shēng-zhī jí* (Lú Zhào-lín), and [[KR4c0006]]–[[KR4c0007]] (Luò Bīn-wáng). With [[KR4c0001]] *Dōng-gāo zǐ jí* (Wáng Jì), [[KR4c0008]] *Chén Shí-yí jí* (Chén Zǐ-áng), [[KR4c0009]] *Zhāng Yān-gōng jí* (Zhāng Shuō), [[KR4c0010]] *Lǐ Běi-hǎi jí* (Lǐ Yōng), and [[KR4c0093]] *Sòng Zhī-wèn jí*, these form the early-Tang cluster whose stylistic transition from *liù-cháo* ornament to mature regulated verse is the conventional point of departure for the High Tang.
- **The High-Tang core (盛唐)** — [[KR4c0011]] *Qǔ-jiāng jí* (Zhāng Jiǔ-líng), [[KR4c0012]] *Lǐ Tài-bái wén jí* (Lǐ Bái), [[KR4c0015]] / [[KR4c0017]] / [[KR4c0018]] / [[KR4c0020]] (the principal annotated editions of Dù Fǔ), [[KR4c0021]] / [[KR4c0022]] (annotated Wáng Wéi), [[KR4c0023]] *Gāo Chángshì jí* (Gāo Shì), [[KR4c0024]] *Cháng Jiàn shī*, [[KR4c0025]] *Mèng Hào-rán jí*, [[KR4c0026]] *Chǔ Guāng-xī shī jí*, [[KR4c0029]] *Cén Jiā-zhōu shī* (Cén Shēn). The four named pillars of High-Tang verse — Wáng Wéi, Mèng Hào-rán, Lǐ Bái, Dù Fǔ — are the canonical anchor; Gāo Shì and Cén Shēn carry the *biān-sài* 邊塞 (frontier) sub-style.
- **The Dù Fǔ commentary genealogy** — [[KR4c0015]] *Jiǔ-jiā jí-zhù Dù shī* (Guō Zhī-dá, 1181), [[KR4c0017]] *Bǔ-zhù Dù shī* (the Huáng Xī / Huáng Hè *fù-zǐ* annotation, 1216), [[KR4c0018]] *Jí qiān-jiā zhù Dù gōng-bù shī jí* (Gāo Chǔ-fāng, *ca.* 1300), [[KR4c0019]] *Dù shī jūn* (Táng Yuán-hóng, Míng), and [[KR4c0020]] *Dù shī xiáng-zhù* (Qiú Zhào-áo, 1693). Together they form the densest single-poet commentary tradition in Chinese letters.
- **The Lǐ Bái commentary genealogy** — [[KR4c0012]] (the Sòng Mǐn-qiú / Zēng Gǒng 30-*juǎn* recension), [[KR4c0013]] *Lǐ Tài-bái jí fēn-lèi bǔ-zhù* (Yáng Qí-xián / Xiāo Shì-yún, 1310–20), [[KR4c0014]] Wáng Qí's 1758 *jí-zhù*.
- **The Hán Yù commentary genealogy (the densest critical-apparatus cluster in the division)** — [[KR4c0042]] *Hán jí jǔ-zhèng* (Fāng Sōng-qīng, 1189), [[KR4c0043]] *Yuán-běn Hán jí kǎo-yì* (Zhū Xī's *Kǎo-yì*), [[KR4c0044]] *Bié-běn Hán wén kǎo-yì* (Wáng Bó-dà's editorial recasting), [[KR4c0045]] *Wǔ-bǎi-jiā zhù Chāng-lí wén jí* (Wèi Zhòng-jǔ, 1228–33), [[KR4c0046]] *Dōng-yǎ-táng Chāng-lí jí zhù* (Liào Yíng-zhōng), [[KR4c0047]] *Hán jí diǎn-kān* (Chén Jǐng-yún, Qīng).
- **The Liǔ Zōng-yuán cluster** — [[KR4c0048]] *Liǔ Hé-dōng jí* (Hán Chún's *gǔ-xùn*, 1170s), [[KR4c0049]] *Liǔ Hé-dōng jí zhù* (Tóng Zōng-shuō / Zhāng Dūn-yí / Pān Wěi), [[KR4c0050]] *Wǔ-bǎi-jiā zhù Liǔ xiān-shēng jí* (Wèi Zhòng-jǔ's matching anthology to the Hán Yù 五百家). The Hán-Liǔ pair, edited in parallel at *Jiàn-ān* and at *Jiàn-yáng*, is the consummation of the Sòng *gǔ-wén* canon-building project.
- **The Yuán-Bái 元白 pairing and the 新樂府 movement** — [[KR4c0068]] *Yuán-shì Cháng-qìng jí* and [[KR4c0069]] *Bái-shì Cháng-qìng jí*, the two collections that together preserve the *xīn yuè-fǔ* manifesto-cycle and the canonical mid-Tang social-realist verse. [[KR4c0070]] Wāng Lì-míng's 1702 *Bái Xiāng-shān shī jí* is the Qīng critical edition.
- **The Wǎn-Táng Lǐ-Dù pair** — [[KR4c0072]] *Fán-chuān jí* (Dù Mù) and [[KR4c0074]] *Lǐ Yì-shān shī jí* (Lǐ Shāng-yǐn), with [[KR4c0075]] *Lǐ Yì-shān shī jí zhù* (Zhū Hè-líng, Qīng) and [[KR4c0076]] *Lǐ Yì-shān wén-jí jiān-zhù* (Xú Shù-gǔ + Xú Jiǒng).
- **The 韓孟 difficult-bitter school** — [[KR4c0058]] *Mèng Dōng-yě shī jí* (Mèng Jiāo), [[KR4c0059]] *Cháng-jiāng jí* (Jiǎ Dǎo), [[KR4c0060]] *Chāng-gǔ jí* + [[KR4c0061]] *Gē-shī biān* + [[KR4c0062]] *Jiān-zhù píng-diǎn Lǐ Cháng-jí gē-shī* (the three Lǐ Hè editions), [[KR4c0063]] *Jiàng-shǒu jū-yuán-chí jì* (Fán Zōng-shī's notoriously cryptic prose piece), [[KR4c0092]] *Yù-chuān-zǐ shī jí* (Lú Tóng). These constitute the *qí-jué* (strange-and-sharp) counter-current to the *Yuán-Bái* popularizing program.
- **The Buddhist monk-poets** — [[KR4c0002]] *Hán-shān shī jí* (the early-9th-century Cold Mountain corpus, with Fēng-gān and Shí-dé), [[KR4c0031]] *Zhù-shān jí* (Shì Jiǎo-rán, the principal *Dà-lì* monk-poet and theorist of *jìng* 境), [[KR4c0109]] *Bái-lián jí* (Shì Qí-jǐ), [[KR4c0110]] *Chán-yuè jí* (Shì Guàn-xiū). Together these are the textual foundation of the late-Tang and Sòng *shī-sēng* 詩僧 tradition.
- **The political-document prose canon** — [[KR4c0039]] *Hàn-yuàn jí* (Lù Zhì's *Hàn-lín-yuàn zhì-gào*), [[KR4c0067]] *Huì-chāng yī-pǐn jí* (Lǐ Dé-yù, the *Niú-Lǐ* faction's *Lǐ* head as *Wǔ-zōng*'s chief minister), [[KR4c0040]]–[[KR4c0041]] (Quán Dé-yú, the Yuán-Hé court drafter). These are the canonical Tang official-document *bié-jí*, models for the Sòng *Hàn-lín-yuàn* drafting tradition.
- **The Five-Dynasties / Late-Tang holdovers** — [[KR4c0086]] *Bì-yún jí* (Lǐ Zhōng, 南唐), [[KR4c0111]] *Huàn-huā jí* (Wéi Zhuāng), [[KR4c0112]] *Guǎng-chéng jí* (Dù Guāng-tíng, the foundational late-Tang Daoist literary corpus, under 後蜀 Wáng Jiàn patronage), [[KR4c0114]] *Lǐ chéng-xiàng shī-jí* (Lǐ Jiàn-xūn, 南唐). These mark the dynastic boundary that the Sìkù compilers drew loosely: poets and prose-writers active well into the *Wǔ-dài* but trained inside Tang literary conventions remain in this category rather than passing into KR4d.
- **The Korean Tang-resident** — [[KR4c0100]] *Guì-yuàn bǐ-gēng jí* (Chǒe Ch'iwǒn 崔致遠), the Silla aristocrat-turned-Tang *jìn-shì* whose collected *Yáng-zhōu* staff correspondence is the most important Tang-period East Asian diplomatic literary corpus.

## Important persons

- [[李白]] — the supreme High-Tang lyricist; the *Lǐ-Dù* pairing with [[杜甫]] anchors the High-Tang canon.
- [[杜甫]] — the *shī shèng* 詩聖, the dense-textured High-Tang corpus that absorbed the entire later commentary tradition.
- [[王維]] — the *shān-shuǐ tián-yuán* master, the *Wáng-Mèng* pair with [[孟浩然]].
- [[白居易]] — the *Yuán-Bái* social-realist core; the most widely circulated Tang poet during his own lifetime, and the East Asian (Japanese Heian, Koryŏ) canonical Tang voice.
- [[元稹]] — Bái's lifelong literary partner; author of the *Yīng-yīng zhuàn* 鶯鶯傳 and the 1812 *xīn yuè-fǔ* manifesto.
- [[韓愈]] — leader of the Tang *gǔ-wén* movement; the most heavily-commentated Tang prose corpus.
- [[柳宗元]] — Hán Yù's *gǔ-wén* partner and equally heavily-commentated.
- [[陳子昂]] — author of the *Gǎn yù* cycle, the conventional founder of the High-Tang *fù-gǔ* program.
- [[張九齡]] — the *Kāi-yuán* *zǎi-xiàng* whose *Gǎn yù* cycle continues Chén Zǐ-áng's lineage and feeds directly into Lǐ Bái's *Gǔ fēng*.
- [[孟浩然]] — the *Xiāng-yáng* hermit-poet, the *Wáng-Mèng* counterpart to Wáng Wéi.
- [[韋應物]] — *Wéi Sū-zhōu*, the *Táo-Wéi* pair with Táo Qián, principal post-rebellion contemplative-landscape voice.
- [[杜牧]] — the *Wǎn-Táng* prose-and-verse master; the *Zuì yán* on the *fān-zhèn* crisis.
- [[李商隱]] — Dù Mù's *Wǎn-Táng* counterpart, the *wú-tí* lyricist, principal model of the Northern-Sòng *Xī-kūn tǐ*.
- [[孟郊]] / [[賈島]] / [[李賀]] / [[盧仝]] — the *Hán-Mèng* difficult-bitter school of late-mid-Tang verse.
- [[釋皎然]] — *Dà-lì* poet-monk and theorist (*Shī shì* 詩式); the bridge between High-Tang practice and late-Tang critical theory.
- [[釋寒山]] / [[釋貫休]] / [[釋齊己]] — the three canonical Tang monk-poets, foundational for the Sòng *Chán* poetic tradition and (for Hán-shān) for twentieth-century Anglophone reception.
- [[陸贄]] — the supreme *Hàn-lín-yuàn* drafter, the model for all Sòng official-document prose.
- [[司空圖]] — late-Tang martyr-recluse and (traditionally) author of the *Èr-shí-sì shī-pǐn*.
- [[羅隱]] / [[皮日休]] / [[陸龜蒙]] / [[韋莊]] — the Wǎn-Táng to Wǔ-dài tail of socially-engaged verse.
- [[杜光庭]] — the foundational late-Tang / 後蜀 Daoist literary voice.
- [[崔致遠]] — Silla diplomat-literatus, the principal East Asian Tang-correspondence corpus.
- [[宋敏求]] — Sòng compiler whose recensional work on Lǐ Bái ([[KR4c0012]]), Liú Yǔ-xī ([[KR4c0051]]), Mèng Jiāo ([[KR4c0058]]), and Yán Zhēn-qīng ([[KR4c0028]]) underlies the standard Sòng-Yuán text of half the Tang canon.
- [[朱熹]] — author of the *Hán wén kǎo-yì* ([[KR4c0043]]), the most influential Sòng critical apparatus on the Tang corpus.
- [[魏仲擧]] — *Jiàn-ān* commercial publisher whose matching *Wǔ-bǎi-jiā* anthologies ([[KR4c0045]], [[KR4c0050]]) defined the Yuán-Míng *kē-jǔ*-market reception of Hán Yù and Liǔ Zōng-yuán.
- [[仇兆鰲]] — Qīng author of the *Dù shī xiáng-zhù* ([[KR4c0020]]), still the standard variorum on Dù Fǔ.
- [[王琦]] / [[趙殿成]] / [[馮集梧]] / [[朱鶴齡]] — the Qīng critical-edition workshop on Lǐ Bái, Wáng Wéi, Dù Mù, and Lǐ Shāng-yǐn respectively.

## Topics

- **The four-stage canon: 初/盛/中/晚 Tang.** The chronological periodization codified in Yán Yǔ's *Cāng-làng shī-huà* and naturalized into every Míng-Qīng anthology runs as the unspoken organizational principle of the division. Each phase has its emblematic figures (the *Sì jié* and Chén Zǐ-áng in the early; Wáng-Mèng-Lǐ-Dù in the high; Hán-Liǔ-Yuán-Bái in the mid; Lǐ-Dù-Wēn in the late) and its emblematic stylistic register.
- **The 古文運動 (Hán Yù-Liǔ Zōng-yuán *gǔ-wén* movement).** The mid-Tang programmatic return to "ancient prose" — *Hàn-Wèi*-style direct argument-and-narrative against the *liù-cháo* parallel-prose tradition — anchored in [[韓愈]] and [[柳宗元]] and theorized in their letters. The downstream Sòng commentary apparatus (the Fāng Sōng-qīng / Zhū Xī / Wáng Bó-dà / Wèi Zhòng-jǔ / Liào Yíng-zhōng / Chén Jǐng-yún chain) is the densest critical-edition genealogy of any Tang prose corpus.
- **新樂府 vs. 苦吟 vs. 古體**. The mid-Tang split between [[白居易]] and [[元稹]]'s social-realist *xīn yuè-fǔ* program (with its accessible diction and political-didactic aim), the *Hán-Mèng* difficult-bitter school of [[孟郊]], [[賈島]], [[李賀]], and [[盧仝]] (recondite vocabulary and bizarre image), and the [[李白]]-Chén Zǐ-áng *fù-gǔ* line of archaic *gǔ-fēng* — three competing visions of what mid-Tang verse should be doing.
- **山水田園 (landscape-and-farmstead) and the *Táo-Wéi-Wáng-Mèng* lineage.** The post-Táo Qián 陶潛 contemplative landscape mode, anchored in [[王維]] and [[孟浩然]] in the High Tang and in [[韋應物]] and [[柳宗元]] in the mid Tang; Sū Shì's Sòng-period codification of the *Táo-Wéi* pair defined the Sòng-Yuán-Míng reading.
- **邊塞 (frontier) verse.** [[高適]] and [[岑參]] (with [[王昌齡]]) as the principal voices of the High-Tang frontier-campaign genre — a register that thins out almost completely after the *An Lùshān* rebellion.
- **The *Wǎn-Táng* dense-allusive style and its Northern Sòng inheritance.** [[李商隱]]'s *wú-tí* poems and [[杜牧]]'s allusive *jué-jù* became the model for the early-Sòng *Xī-kūn tǐ* 西崑體 of Yáng Yì, Liú Zǐ-yí, and Qián Wéi-yǎn; the Sòng *Yuán-yòu* generation's reaction against this style is one of the principal storylines of the *Wén-yuán yīng-huá* / *Tángshī* anthology selection process.
- **Buddhist and Daoist literary participation.** The monks ([[釋皎然]], [[釋寒山]], [[釋貫休]], [[釋齊己]]) and the Daoist priest-writers ([[吳筠]], [[杜光庭]]) hold a distinct register: half *shī*, half *jì* (gāthā) / Daoist ritual hymn, with circulation through monastic-archive transmission rather than the official *bié-jí* network.
- **The Sòng-Yuán-Míng-Qīng commentary enterprise.** A third of the KR4c entries are not Tang-period autographs but later annotated, collated, or anthologized editions. The Sòng commentary on Dù Fǔ alone runs to five major recensions in this division; Hán Yù has six; Liǔ Zōng-yuán three. The KR4c material is therefore as much a record of Tang reception-history as of Tang composition.
- **Recension and textual problem.** The transmission of every major Tang collection is bibliographically distinctive. Bái Jū-yì curated his own corpus by depositing manuscript copies at *Dōng-lín sì*, *Shèng-shàn sì*, *Sū-zhōu Nán-chán yuàn*, and *Xiāng-shān sì* ([[KR4c0069]]); Lǐ Bái's collection was assembled by his cousin Lǐ Yáng-bīng at his deathbed and lay almost lost until the *Xián-píng* (998–1003) recovery ([[KR4c0012]]); Wáng Bó's corpus is partly recovered from a *Shōsō-in* Tang MS ([[KR4c0003]]); the Chén Zǐ-áng *juǎn* 7 lacunae are filled from the *Wén yuàn yīng huá* ([[KR4c0008]]). The transmission stories are themselves a major chapter in Tang literary history.
- **The dynastic boundary.** The *Wǔ-dài* Five-Dynasties holdovers — [[釋齊己]] under *Jīng-nán Nán-píng*, [[釋貫休]] under *Former Shǔ*, [[韋莊]] under *Former Shǔ*, [[李建勳]] under *Nán-Táng*, [[杜光庭]] under *後蜀* — are filed in KR4c rather than in the Sòng division, on the principle that Tang training rather than Tang reign-date governs categorization.

## Timeline

- 705–707 — *Luò Bīn-wáng wén jí* 駱賓王文集 [[KR4c0007]] — recension close after Luò's death/disappearance in the 684 anti-Wǔ-hòu rising.

- 727–760 — *Cháng Jiàn shī* 常建詩 [[KR4c0024]] — High-Tang *jìn-shì*-bracket landscape verse.
- 740 — *Qǔ-jiāng jí* 曲江集 [[KR4c0011]] — death-bracket close of Zhāng Jiǔ-líng's *Kāi-yuán* *zǎi-xiàng* corpus.
- 740–745 — *Mèng Hào-rán jí* 孟浩然集 [[KR4c0025]] — Wáng Shì-yuán's *Yí-chéng* preface compilation, just after Mèng's 740 death.

- 760–763 — *Táng Chǔ Guāng-xī shī jí* 唐儲光羲詩集 [[KR4c0026]] — High-Tang *shān-shuǐ tián-yuán* poet, post-rebellion recension.
- *c.* 760 — *Xiāo Mào-tǐng wén jí* 蕭茂挺文集 [[KR4c0035]] — Xiāo Yǐng-shì's *gǔ-wén* essays, precursor to Hán Yù.
- 775–830 — *Wáng Sī-mǎ jí* 王司馬集 [[KR4c0064]] — Wáng Jiàn's *yuè-fǔ* and palace-verse.

- 779–843 — *Cháng-jiāng jí* 長江集 [[KR4c0059]] — Jiǎ Dǎo's *kǔ-yín* (bitter-chanting) lifework.
- 783 — *Qián Zhòng-wén jí* 錢仲文集 [[KR4c0037]] — Qián Qǐ, one of the *Dà-lì* ten talents.
- 790 — *Liú Suí-zhōu jí* 劉隨州集 [[KR4c0032]] — Liú Cháng-qīng's pentasyllabic-master collection.
- 790–816 — *Chāng-gǔ jí* 昌谷集 / *Gē-shī biān* 歌詩編 [[KR4c0060]] / [[KR4c0061]] — Lǐ Hè's recondite *guǐ-cái* (ghost-talent) lifework.
- 791 — *Wéi Sū-zhōu jí* 韋蘇州集 [[KR4c0033]] — Wéi Yìng-wù's death-bracket close at *Sū-zhōu*.
- 793 — *Zhù-shān jí* 杼山集 [[KR4c0031]] — Shì Jiǎo-rán's imperial-archive deposit (the *Zhēn-yuán* 8 *dié*), Yú Dí's preface.
- 792–802 — *Ōuyáng Xíng-zhōu wén jí* 歐陽行周文集 [[KR4c0056]] — Ōuyáng Zhān, Hán Yù's intimate friend and Lǐng-nán *gǔ-wén* contemporary.
