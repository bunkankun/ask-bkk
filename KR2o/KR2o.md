# Shǐpíng lèi 史評類 — Historical Criticism

## Scope and scholarly tradition

The *Shǐpíng* 史評 (literally "appreciation/evaluation of historical writing") category sits at the end of the History branch (*Shǐbù* 史部) of the *Sìkù quánshū* classification, where it gathers two distinct but adjacent kinds of work: critical assessments of past events (lessons drawn from a particular dynasty or episode in the form of a "mirror," *jiàn* 鑑) and critical assessments of historical writing itself — what a modern reader would call historiography. As Wilkinson notes (Wilkinson, *Chinese History: A New Manual*, § 46.6.4), the category as a catalog rubric was introduced in the twelfth century by Cháo Gōngwǔ 晁公武 (ca. 1105–80) in his *Jùnzhāi dúshū zhì* 郡齋讀書志 and retained ever after in the *Sìkù* and in the *Shūmù dáwèn* 書目答問. The unifying thread is that none of these works is itself a *history* in the annalistic, annals-biography, or topical forms collected in `KR2a` 正史類 through `KR2n` 史鈔類; rather, every work here stands at one remove from the historical record and judges it.

Chronologically `KR2o` spans almost twelve hundred years, from the foundational *Shǐtōng* 史通 of [[劉知幾]] (Liú Zhījǐ, 661–721, completed 710 / extended posthumously 722) to the *Wénshǐ tōngyì* 文史通義 of [[章學誠]] (Zhāng Xuéchéng, 1738–1801). Between these two methodological landmarks the bulk of the division consists of Sòng and Yuán "lessons-from-the-past" works built around the *Zīzhì tōngjiàn* 資治通鑑 ([[KR2b0010]]) and Zhū Xī's *Gāngmù* 綱目 reduction of it. As Wilkinson observes (§ 48.5), it was the *Gāngmù* — not the *Zīzhì tōngjiàn* itself and certainly not the *Twenty-Four Histories* — that most educated people from the Sòng through the Qīng actually read; the works in `KR2o` are the upstream and downstream sediment of that fact. The Qīng additions to the category are dominated by imperial historiography: the Kāngxī emperor's *yùpī* 御批 (imperial top-of-page comments) appended to Zhū Xī's *Gāngmù* and its continuations, and the Qiánlóng emperor's own selected comments and didactic mirrors. Historical criticism, in other words, in late-imperial form became another vehicle for sovereign self-articulation.

## Important texts and text clusters

The division's twenty-five works fall into recognisable clusters.

**Foundational treatises on the writing of history.** [[KR2o0001]] *Shǐtōng* 史通 (*Comprehensive Treatise on Historiography*) of [[劉知幾]] is the first sustained Chinese theory of historical writing, the work in which the analytical pairs Six Schools (*Liù jiā* 六家) and Two Forms (*Èr tǐ* 二體, i.e. *biānnián* 編年 vs. *jìzhuàn* 紀傳) were articulated and in which the demand for *zhí bǐ* 直筆 (straight-brushed recording, free of moral or political interference) was first programmatically defended. [[KR2o0002]] *Shǐtōng tōngshì* 史通通釋 (1750) by [[浦起龍]] (Pǔ Qǐlóng, 1679–c. 1762) is the standard annotated edition that has framed every subsequent reading of the *Shǐtōng*. [[KR2o0025]] *Wénshǐ tōngyì* 文史通義 of [[章學誠]] (drafts 1771–96, posthumous editing to 1832) is the second great Chinese theoretical work on history, organised around the principle *liù jīng jiē shǐ yě* 六經皆史也 ("the Six Classics are all history"), and is conventionally paired with the *Shǐtōng* as the two poles of pre-modern Chinese historiography (Wilkinson § 46.6.4.1–2).

**Sòng "mirror" works on individual dynasties.** [[KR2o0003]] *Tángjiàn* 唐鑑 (1086) of [[范祖禹]] (Fàn Zǔyǔ, 1041–98) — a participant in the *Zīzhì tōngjiàn* project — is the model exemplar of the genre, drawing systematic lessons from the Táng for the present court; it was the work Sīmǎ Guāng singled out as his deputy's masterpiece. [[KR2o0004]] *Tángshǐ lùnduàn* 唐史論斷 of [[孫甫]] (Sūn Fǔ, 998–1057) is the earlier and more polemical equivalent; [[KR2o0010]] *Liùcháo tōngjiàn bóyì* 六朝通鑑博議 of [[李燾]] (Lǐ Tāo, 1115–84) extends the same exercise to the period of disunion; [[KR2o0007]] *Sānguó záshì* 三國雜事 of [[唐庚]] (Táng Gēng, 1071–1121) does the same for the Three Kingdoms; [[KR2o0012]] *Liǎng-Hàn bǐjì* 兩漢筆記 of [[錢時]] (Qián Shí, fl. 1230s) for the two Hàn. [[KR2o0011]] *Sòng dàshìjì jiǎngyì* 宋大事記講義 of [[呂中]] (Lǚ Zhōng, fl. 1247–52) is the rare contemporaneous self-mirror — a Southern Sòng critique of the Sòng's own history, in *jiǎngyì* 講義 (lecture) form for imperial instruction.

**Works centred on the *Zīzhì tōngjiàn*.** [[KR2o0005]] *Tángshū zhíbǐ* 唐書直筆 of [[呂夏卿]] (Lǚ Xiàqīng, 1015–70), although nominally a working notebook for the *Xīn Tángshū* drafting, became canonised as a critical-historical text. [[KR2o0006]] *Tōngjiàn wènyí* 通鑑問疑 of [[劉羲仲]] (Liú Xīzhòng, son of Liú Shù 劉恕) preserves the questions Liú put to Sīmǎ Guāng during the *Tōngjiàn*'s compilation and Sīmǎ's replies — a unique window into the workshop. [[KR2o0014]] *Tōngjiàn dáwèn* 通鑑答問 of [[王應麟]] (Wáng Yīnglín, 1223–96) is the late-Sòng pedagogical companion that codifies the questions a serious reader of the *Tōngjiàn* must be able to answer.

**Sòng evidential-corrective notes.** [[KR2o0013]] *Jiùwén zhèngwù* 舊聞證誤 of [[李心傳]] (Lǐ Xīnchuán, 1167–1244), the historian of the Southern Sòng restoration, applies evidential method to received Sòng anecdote and is singled out by Wilkinson (§ 46.6.4) alongside [[洪邁]]'s *Róngzhāi suíbǐ* 容齋隨筆 (in `KR3l`) as one of the great pre-Liú Zhījǐ Sòng contributions to historical criticism. [[KR2o0009]] *Shèshǐ suíbǐ* 涉史隨筆 of [[葛洪]] (Gě Hóng, jìnshì 1184, not to be confused with the Jìn-dynasty Daoist) is the looser, more belletristic *suíbǐ* cousin.

**The *jīngyán* 經筵 lecture genre as historical criticism.** [[KR2o0008]] *Jīngwò guǎnjiàn* 經幄管見 of [[曹彥約]] (Cáo Yànyuē, 1157–1228) preserves an imperial-lecturer's running commentary on history; alongside [[KR2o0011]] it documents how, by the late Southern Sòng, historical criticism had become a tool of *jīngyán* pedagogy at the court.

**Compilations of historical judgments.** [[KR2o0015]] *Lìdài míngxián quèlùn* 歷代名賢確論, an anonymous Sòng compilation of one hundred *juǎn*, gathers the considered judgments (*quèlùn* 確論) on past worthies from earlier writers and is the largest reference work of historical opinion in the division.

**Yuán synthetic-outline criticism.** [[KR2o0016]] *Lìdài tōnglüè* 歷代通略 of [[陳櫟]] (Chén Lì, 1252–1334) and [[KR2o0017]] *Shíqī shǐ zuǎn gǔjīn tōngyào* 十七史纂古今通要 of [[胡一桂]] (Hú Yīguì, fl. early 14th c.) abridge and judge the *Seventeen Histories* in a single critical synthesis — the Yuán face of the Sòng's *Gāngmù* impulse, oriented to private tutors and provincial examination candidates.

**Míng historical criticism.** [[KR2o0018]] *Xuéshǐ* 學史 of [[邵寶]] (Shào Bǎo, 1460–1527) is a methodical Míng student's handbook on how to read the *Histories*; [[KR2o0019]] *Shǐjiū* 史糾 of [[朱明鎬]] (Zhū Mínghào, 1607–52) is the late-Míng evidential-corrective work in the line of Lǐ Xīnchuán.

**The imperially-annotated *Gāngmù* cluster.** [[KR2o0020]] *Yùpī Zīzhì tōngjiàn gāngmù* 御批資治通鑑綱目 collects Zhū Xī's 1172 outline-history of pre-Sòng times with over one hundred *yùpī* added by the Kāngxī emperor (printed 1708); [[KR2o0021]] *Yùpī Zīzhì tōngjiàn gāngmù qián biān* 御批資治通鑑綱目前編 attaches the same imperial *yùpī* apparatus to [[金履祥]]'s 1273 pre-Yáo extension of the *Gāngmù*; [[KR2o0022]] *Yùpī Xù Zīzhì tōngjiàn gāngmù* 御批續資治通鑑綱目 does the same for [[商輅]] (Shāng Lù, 1414–86) and his collaborators' Sòng-Yuán continuation. Together these three works are the embodiment of the *Gāngmù* tradition as it stood at the high tide of Qīng imperial appropriation. Wilkinson (§ 48.5–8) treats them as the principal vehicle by which historical knowledge actually reached the late-imperial reading public.

**The Qiánlóng emperor's own historical criticism.** [[KR2o0023]] *Píngjiàn chǎnyào* 評鑑闡要 (1768–71) is the 798 *yùpī* the Qiánlóng emperor selected from his own commentary on the *Yùpī lìdài tōngjiàn jílǎn* 御批歷代通鑑輯覽 (1767) and reissued separately as a free-standing historical criticism. [[KR2o0024]] *Qīndìng gǔjīn chǔ'èr jīnjiàn* 欽定古今儲貳金鑑 (1783) is the same emperor's didactic mirror specifically on crown-prince succession — a topic of acute personal interest in the wake of his 1735–73 succession crises.

## Important persons

- [[劉知幾]] — author of the *Shǐtōng*; the Táng historian whose career inside the *Shǐguǎn* 史館 produced the first Chinese theoretical work on history-writing and the analytical vocabulary used in the division thereafter.
- [[浦起龍]] — eighteenth-century editor whose annotated *Shǐtōng tōngshì* (1750) made the *Shǐtōng* readable for late-imperial scholars and remains the basis of every modern edition.
- [[范祖禹]] — Sòng historian, deputy on the *Tōngjiàn* project, author of the model "mirror" treatise *Tángjiàn*; the figure who set the template for the dynasty-by-dynasty critical mirror.
- [[朱熹]] — through the *Zīzhì tōngjiàn gāngmù*, the historian who reshaped the popular reception of all Chinese history and supplied the doctrine of *zhèngtǒng* 正統 that organises every later Gāngmù-style work in the division.
- [[金履祥]] — Yuán historian-philosopher and Zhū-Xī school transmitter whose *Tōngjiàn qián biān* extends the *Gāngmù* back before 403 BCE.
- [[商輅]] — Míng grand-secretary, sole *sānyuán* 三元 (top in all three examination levels) of the Míng, chief compiler of the imperially commissioned Sòng-Yuán continuation of the *Gāngmù*.
- [[李心傳]] — Southern Sòng historian of the post-Jingkang restoration whose *Jiùwén zhèngwù* is the high point of pre-Qīng evidential historiography.
- [[王應麟]] — late-Sòng polymath who within `KR2o` produced the *Tōngjiàn*-reading curriculum *Tōngjiàn dáwèn*; his presence here is one wing of a career also visible in `KR1` (Classics) and `KR3` (Masters).
- [[聖祖]] (the Kāngxī emperor, 1654–1722) — author of the *yùpī* apparatus that frames the *Gāngmù* cluster in this division.
- [[高宗弘曆]] (the Qiánlóng emperor, 1711–99) — author of both [[KR2o0023]] and [[KR2o0024]]; the historian-sovereign whose interventions close the division.
- [[章學誠]] — Qīng historical theorist, author of *Wénshǐ tōngyì*, the latest-in-time work in the division and the work that re-opens the questions Liú Zhījǐ had posed a millennium earlier.

## Topics

- *Zhí bǐ* 直筆 — the historian's duty of straight recording, free of patron pressure or moral euphemism. Liú Zhījǐ's foundational demand; reaffirmed in modified form by Zhāng Xuéchéng under the heading *shǐdé* 史德 (the historian's virtue) and contested across the *Gāngmù* tradition's praise-and-blame practice.
- *Zhèngtǒng* 正統 — legitimate dynastic succession. Sīmǎ Guāng accepted Wèi as the Three Kingdoms successor; Zhū Xī overturned this in favour of Shǔ-Hàn, and the resulting *Gāngmù* doctrine governs every imperially-annotated work in the cluster. Wilkinson treats the topic at length (§ A.2.1–6).
- The Two Forms — *biānnián* 編年 (annalistic) versus *jìzhuàn* 紀傳 (composite annals-biography). Liú Zhījǐ's pair; Zhāng Xuéchéng's diagnosis of the *jìzhuàn* form's exhaustion is the late-imperial reopening of the same question.
- *Liù jīng jiē shǐ* 六經皆史 — the Classics-as-history thesis. The organising claim of the *Wénshǐ tōngyì*; antecedents in Wáng Yángmíng and Lǐ Zhì. In `KR2o` the thesis is what allows historical criticism to absorb classical hermeneutics and step out of its category.
- The *Gāngmù* 綱目 form — the *gāng* / *mù* (outline / detail) layout that Zhū Xī adapted from administrative documents and that became, by the late Míng, the default vehicle in which historical narrative was read. The whole imperial cluster [[KR2o0020]]–[[KR2o0022]] is `KR2o`'s record of the form.
- *Yùpī* 御批 — imperial top-of-page comments on a historical work. Practised at scale by the Kāngxī and Qiánlóng emperors, the *yùpī* turns historical criticism from a scholar's genre into a sovereign's.
- *Jīngyán* 經筵 historical lecture as a critical genre — the imperial-classroom lecture (*jiǎngyì* 講義) becoming a published work of historical criticism. Exemplified by [[KR2o0008]] and [[KR2o0011]].
- Evidential method (*kǎojù* 考據) on received historical anecdote — Lǐ Xīnchuán's *Jiùwén zhèngwù* model, extended in the Míng by Zhū Mínghào's *Shǐjiū*.
- *Fāngzhì* 方志 as serious historical writing — a programmatic claim original to Zhāng Xuéchéng and the doctrinal underpinning of all later Chinese local historiography.
