# Dà-shèng rù-dào cì-dì 大乘入道次第
*The Stages of Entering the Path in the Mahāyāna*
by [[智周]] (Zhì-zhōu, 撰)

## About the work

A single-fascicle systematic treatise by [[智周]] **Zhì-zhōu** (668–723), the third patriarch of the Cí'ēn-school, laying out the bodhisattva-path in stages (*cì-dì* 次第 = *krama*) within the Yogācāra-Cí'ēn doctrinal framework. Preserved in the *Taishō* at T45n1864. Functions as an introductory practice-manual to the bodhisattva-path complementing Huìzhǎo's [[KR6n0127]] *Quàn-fā pú-tí xīn jí*.

### Prefaces

Authorship line: "*Shā-mén Zhì-zhōu zhuàn* 沙門智周撰." Opens with a dedication verse: "I bow my head to the unequalled good charioteer / Who can pull beings out of the sinking suffering of birth-and-death; / The nature and characteristic of [his] empty-space [wisdom] are without coming-or-going — / Like a shadow following a form, responding to those to be transformed; / The single-flavour true characteristic, the wondrous sweet-dew…"

### Abstract

The *Rù-dào cì-dì* is a compact didactic treatment of the Mahāyāna path, designed to be intelligible to monks beginning Yogācāra study. It systematically presents the *path* (*dào* = *mārga*) in its standard Yogācāra five-stage form — *zī-liáng-dào* 資糧道 (path of accumulation), *jiā-xíng-dào* 加行道 (path of preparation), *jiàn-dào* 見道 (path of seeing), *xiū-dào* 修道 (path of cultivation), *jiū-jìng-dào* 究竟道 (path of completion) — together with the analyses of the *èr-zhàng* (twofold obscuration) cut at each, and the *èr-kōng* (twofold emptiness) realised at each. The work is characteristic of the third-generation Cí'ēn-school's effort to consolidate the doctrinal apparatus of [[玄奘]] and [[窺基]] into pedagogically accessible practice manuals.

The dating window 700–723 brackets Zhì-zhōu's productive period as third patriarch of the Cí'ēn-school. The work was sufficiently authoritative that the Hé-xī Cí'ēn-school monk [[曇曠]] composed an extended *kāi-jué* (decisional supplement) on it — [[KR6n0130]] *Dà-shèng rù-dào cì-dì kāi-jué* — at Dūnhuáng in the third quarter of the eighth century.
