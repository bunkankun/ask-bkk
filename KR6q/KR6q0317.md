# Jué-àn Kě-xiāng chánshī yǔlù 絕岸可湘禪師語錄

*Sayings-Record of Chán Master Jué-àn Kě-xiāng* — a one-juan late-Sòng / early-Yuán *yǔlù* of **Jué-àn Kě-xiāng** [[可湘]] 絕岸可湘 (1206 – Zhìyuán 27 / 1290, *shì* 85), principal dharma-heir of [[師範]] Wújùn Shīfàn in the Pò-ān line. Compiled by six *sì-fǎ mén-rén* 嗣法門人 led by the principal heir [[妙恩]] Duàn-yá Miào-ēn, then abbot of the Kāi-yuán chán-sì at Quánzhōu.

## About the work

One-juan *yǔlù* in *Xuzangjing* X70 n1384. Eight successive abbacy records: Jiāxīng-fǔ Liú-hóng Xīng-shèng 流虹興聖 (entered Bǎoyòu 1.10.28 = 20 November 1253), Wēnzhōu Yàn-shān Néng-rén 鴈山能仁, Yuèzhōu Jiǔ-yán Huì-yún 九巖慧雲, Tiāntái Hùguó Guǎng-ēn 護國廣恩, Lín'ān-fǔ Chóng-ēn Yǎn-fú 崇恩演福, Wēnzhōu Jiāng-xīn Lóngxiáng Xīngqìng 江心龍翔興慶, Fúzhōu Xuě-fēng Chóng-shèng 雪峯崇聖 (from Xiánchún 8 / 1272 for ten years), and a *zài-zhù Xuě-fēng* 再住雪峯 record. Closing with *niān-gǔ*, *sòng-gǔ*, *fǎ-yǔ*, *zàn*, *sòng*, *xiǎo fó-shì*, and a *bá*. The byline of the opening juan gives "住泉州開元禪寺嗣法門人 妙恩 等編" — principal editor being Miào-ēn while he was abbot of the Kāi-yuán-sì at Quánzhōu.

### Abstract

Per DILA A003948 and the *xù* tradition: Kě-xiāng (*hào* Jué-àn 絕岸, also Xuě-fēng Xiāng 雪峯湘), surname Gě 葛, native of Níng-hǎi 寧海 in Táizhōu. He received decisive dharma-transmission from Wújùn Shīfàn at Jìng-shān and subsequently held the eight abbacies listed above, his tenure at Xuě-fēng Chóng-shèng in Fúzhōu being the longest and the one that gave him the alternate style Xuě-fēng Xiāng. After a decade at Xuě-fēng he retired to Bǎo-shòu-sì at Hángzhōu, dying in Zhìyuán 27 (1290). His dharma-heirs included Yún-sǒu Cóng 雲叟從 and Duàn-yá Miào-ēn 斷崖妙恩 — the latter, abbot of Quánzhōu Kāi-yuán-sì and himself posthumously honored as "Guǎng-míng Tōng-huì Pǔ-jì chánshī" 廣明通慧普濟禪師, being the principal compiler of this *yǔlù*. The *yǔlù* was therefore compiled posthumously, c. 1290–1300, at Quánzhōu.

## Translations and research

*No substantial secondary literature located* in English. Kě-xiāng is treated in passing in Japanese surveys of Sòng-Yuán Línjì (Ishii Shūdō), and in Chinese studies of the Fújiàn Chán tradition around Xuě-fēng.
