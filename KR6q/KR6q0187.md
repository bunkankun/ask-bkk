# Hù fǎ lù 護法錄

*Record of Defending the Dharma* — a ten-juan late-Míng compilation of the Buddhist writings of the great early-Míng scholar-official-literatus [[宋濂|Sòng Lián]] 宋濂 (1310–1381, posthumous title *Wén-xiàn* 文憲), extracted from his extensive literary corpus (*Sòng xué-shì quán jí* 宋學士全集) by [[袾宏|Yúnqī Zhūhóng]] 雲棲袾宏 (1535–1615), the great late-Míng Pure-Land master, and then revised and authenticated (*dìng* 訂) by the leading Wànlì-era scholar-official and Buddhist sympathiser [[錢謙益|Qián Qiānyì]] 錢謙益 (1582–1664, *zì* Shòu-zhī 受之, *hào* Mù-zhāi 牧齋, Yú-shān 虞山). Qián's authenticating preface is dated *Wànlì bǐng-chén dōng shí-yī yuè shuò* 萬曆丙辰冬十一月朔 = first day of the eleventh month of Wànlì 44 = November 1616.

## About the work

A ten-juan Buddhist-literary anthology, J21 B110. Non-commentary; *commentedTextid* omitted. The work collects Sòng Lián's Buddhist-themed writings — prefaces to Buddhist sūtras and monastic records, tomb-inscriptions for monks, temple-and-monastery records, biographical sketches of Buddhist figures, and related literary genres — extracted from his larger literary corpus and reorganised as a free-standing Buddhist monument. The title *Hù fǎ lù* 護法錄 ("Record of Defending the Dharma") situates the collection within the broader Buddhist-apologetic tradition extending back through Sòng-era works like Qi-sōng's *Fǔ jiào biān* 輔教編 and Zhāng Shāng-yīng's *Hù fǎ lùn* 護法論.

Qián Qiānyì's 1616 preface makes the collection's doctrinal-political argument explicit: Sòng Lián, as the senior scholar-compiler-consultant of the founding Hóngwǔ 洪武 emperor, is — Qián argues — uniquely positioned to testify to the Míng state's doctrinal inheritance of Buddhist support. Qián cites the *Hóngwǔ* emperor's own anthology (*Yù zhì wén jí* 御製文集) to the effect that "Heaven's command has unified [us as] the heirs of the Three Teachings" (*tiān mìng wǒ zǔ tǒng hé sān jiào* 天命我祖統合三教), and reads Sòng Lián's Buddhist-literary production as the articulated consequence of this founding policy. The *Hù fǎ lù* thereby functions as a late-Wànlì Buddhist assertion of Buddhism's legitimate place within the founding ideological-political order of the Míng, opposed to the increasingly-dominant Neo-Confucian exclusivism.

### Abstract

[[宋濂|Sòng Lián]] 宋濂 (DILA A007271, 1310–1381). *Zì* Jǐng-lián 景濂; *hào* Qián-xī 潛溪, Wú-xiàng jūshì 無相居士 ("Formless Layman"), Xuán-zhēn-zǐ 玄真子 ("Master of Profound-Truth"). Posthumous title *Wén-xiàn* 文憲 ("Letters and Norms"). Native of Pǔ-jiāng 浦江 (Zhèjiāng). One of the Founding Four Masters of the Míng (*Míng chū sì dà jiā* 明初四大家, alongside Wáng Yáng 王褘, Liú Jī 劉基, and Zhāng Yǔ 張羽). Served Zhū Yuán-zhāng from the latter's pre-imperial period onward, becoming the senior literary-editorial authority of the early Míng — editor of the *Yuán shǐ* 元史 (Yuán dynastic history), chief imperial tutor, and *wén-xuān dǐng* 文學儒宗 (Head of Literary-Confucian Orthodoxy). As a lay Buddhist, he engaged seriously with Chán and with the Huáyán tradition; his Buddhist writings fill much of what the *Hù fǎ lù* preserves. Died in exile in 1381, a victim of the late-Hóngwǔ political purges.

[[袾宏|Yúnqī Zhūhóng]] 雲棲袾宏 (1535–1615): the great late-Míng Pure-Land master, one of the Four Eminent Monks of the late Míng. His extraction of Sòng Lián's Buddhist writings into a separate anthology was part of Yúnqī's broader mission of Buddhist publishing-revival at Yúnqī-sì 雲棲寺. Yúnqī died in 1615, the year before Qián's final authenticating preface; the collection must have been largely complete during Yúnqī's lifetime.

[[錢謙益|Qián Qiānyì]] 錢謙益 (1582–1664): the dominant scholar-official-poet of the Míng-Qīng transitional period. *Zì* Shòu-zhī 受之; *hào* Mù-zhāi 牧齋, Yú-shān 虞山, Dōng-jiàn lǎo-rén 東澗老人, *Hǎi-yìn dì-zǐ* 海印弟子 ("Disciple of Hǎi-yìn"), Fú-jīn dì-zǐ 幅巾弟子, Jù-shā jūshì 聚沙居士. Native of Cháng-shú 常熟 (Jiāngsū). Jìn-shì in 1610 (Wànlì 38). Leader of the *Dōng-lín-pài* 東林派 at the end of the Míng; grudging collaborator with the Qīng after 1644. A major Buddhist lay participant and sponsor, his *Hù fǎ lù* authenticating preface represents one of his early Buddhist literary interventions (at age 34 at the time of writing). Husband of the famous courtesan-poet Liǔ Rú-shì 柳如是.

Dating: *notBefore* c. 1600 (Yúnqī's mature compilation period; the extracting-and-anthologising must have been undertaken during his final years at Yúnqī-sì); *notAfter* 1616 (Qián's authenticating preface, *Wànlì bǐng-chén dōng shí-yī yuè shuò Hànlín-yuàn biān-xiū Yú-shān Qián Qiānyì jǐn xù* 萬曆丙辰冬十有一月朔翰林院編脩虞山錢謙益謹序). The received ten-juan recension is the 1616 Qián-certified form of Yúnqī's pre-1615 compilation.

## Other points of interest

The *Hù fǎ lù*'s triple authorship — early-Míng literatus (Sòng Lián) composition, late-Míng monk (Yúnqī) compilation, late-Míng / early-Qīng scholar-official (Qián) certification — makes the text an unusually rich document of the Buddhist cultural heritage-tradition spanning 200+ years of the Míng dynasty. The chain of transmission itself functions as an argumentative device: Buddhism's legitimate place within Chinese culture is testified by its presence in the founding-era's senior literary master, preserved through the late-Míng Buddhist revival, and authenticated by the most eminent scholar-official of the Wànlì-Chóngzhēn period.

Sòng Lián's Buddhist writings preserved here are a major primary source for early-Míng Buddhist intellectual history, including tomb-inscriptions for important early-Míng monks and records of monastery-restoration projects under the Hóngwǔ emperor's direct or indirect patronage. The text also preserves several of Sòng Lián's essays on the relationship of Buddhism to Confucian statecraft.
