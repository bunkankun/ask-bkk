# Liǎo-ān Qīng-yù chánshī yǔlù 了菴清欲禪師語錄

Nine-juan Yuán-into-early-Míng *yǔlù* of **Liǎo-ān Qīng-yù** [[清欲]] 了菴清欲 (1292 – 1367; *zì* Liǎo-ān 了菴, *hào* Nán-táng 南堂; imperial title Cí-yún Pǔ-jì chánshī 慈雲普濟禪師), principal dharma-heir of [[清茂]] Gǔ-lín Qīng-mào (1262–1329; [[KR6q0345]]) and — with his own dharma-heir [[文康]] Mù-ān Wén-kāng — the link that carries the Gǔ-lín line from Qīng-mào into the early Míng. *Xuzangjing* X71 no. 1414. Compiled collaboratively by twenty named *cān-xué bǐ-qiū* 參學比丘, *xiǎo-shī bǐ-qiū* 小師比丘 and *shī-zhí bǐ-qiū* 師姪比丘 (nephew-disciples), each pair taking responsibility for a juan: [[一志]] and [[元皓]] (*juan* 1 — Jí-qìng-lù Zhōng-shān Kāi-fú chán-sì 集慶路中山開福禪寺, 1329); [[可興]] and [[文康]] (*juan* 2 — Jiā-xīng-lù Běn-jué chán-sì 嘉興路本覺禪寺, 1333); [[祖運]] and [[志道]] (*juan* 3 — Píng-jiāng-lù Líng-yán chán-sì 平江路靈巖禪寺, 1345); [[祖杲]] and [[得度]] (*juan* 4 — *jǔ-gǔ* 舉古); [[文煥]], [[克明]] and [[宗成]] (*juan* 5 — *sòng-gǔ* 頌古 + *zàn-yǔ*); [[汝現]], [[元寶]] and [[慧朗]] (*juan* 6–7 — *jì-sòng* 偈頌); [[景毅]] and [[大圓]] (*juan* 8 — *fǎ-yǔ* 法語); [[海壽]] and [[祖灊]] (*juan* 9 — *tí-bá* 題䟦, plus the collection's three closing biographical pieces). The Kanripo catalog additionally lists [[宗胤]] and [[起宗]] in the collective editorial line.

### Abstract

The collection is framed by **Sòng Lián** 宋濂 (1310–1381), court historian-in-chief of the newly-founded Míng and compiler of the *Yuán-shǐ* 元史 — whose long preface (dated the 1st of the 2nd month of Hóngwǔ 3 / 1370) recalls his youth at Pǔ-yáng-jiāng 浦陽江 reading Qīng-yù's *jì-zàn* at the instance of Qiān-yán Yuán-cháng 千巖元長 and travelling widely, always asking visitors "have you seen Nán-táng?" The same Sòng Lián also supplies the *Xíng-dào-jì* 行道記 that closes *juan* 9 — written at the repeated urging of Qīng-yù's dharma-heir Wén-kāng (then about to install at Fú-zhōu Kāi-yuán-sì 福州開元寺 and requesting Sòng Lián's authorizing hand), and delayed by Sòng Lián's duties editing the *Yuán-shǐ*. *Juan* 9 further carries a *tí-bá* by [[正印]] **Yuè-jiāng Zhèng-yìn** 月江正印 (who styles himself "the former Āyù-wáng elder and older brother in the dharma" 前育王老師弟) dated Zhìzhèng 6 / Bǐng-xū 丙戌 (1346), confirming direct intergenerational contact between the Hǔ-yán / Gǔ-lín and Liǎo-ān lines; and a *Xù-jí xù* 續集序 by Zhì-rén 至仁 (Dōng-zhōu Zhì-rén 東洲至仁, his later-life contemporary and dharma-associate), followed by the *Xù-jí* 續集 and a *xíng-zhuàng* 行狀.

Qīng-yù was a native of Líng-hǎi 臨海 (Tái-zhōu), lay surname Zhū 朱; born on the 1st of the 5th month of Zhìyuán 29 (1292). Orphaned at nine, he was taken to Shuāng-jìng-shān 雙徑山 (Jìng-shān) by his uncle Mào Shàng-rén 茂上人 as a *tóng-zǐ*; took the precepts at sixteen under [[淨伏]] Hǔ-yán Jìng-fú. After study with Xī-bái Míng-zàng-zhǔ 希白明藏主, who had come from Āyù-wáng and recognised his potential, Qīng-yù went to Sū-zhōu Kāi-yuán-sì to meet Qīng-mào ("Fó-xìng-gōng" 佛性公), whose classic Chán exchange with him — on "the ship route or the land route" / the carp on the mountain and the dust in the well — broke him through and established the dharma-transmission. He served subsequently at the Kāi-yuán and Bǎo-níng seats of Qīng-mào, and then held three successive abbacies: Lì-shuǐ Kāi-fú 溧水開福 (from Tiān-lì / Jǐ-sì 1329, the year of Qīng-mào's death), Jiā-xīng Běn-jué 本覺 (from Yuán-tǒng / Guǐ-yǒu 1333, ten years), and Sū-zhōu Líng-yán 靈巖 (from Zhìzhèng / Yǐ-yǒu 1345, three years); his late years, after Líng-yán burned in the mid-Zhìzhèng wars, were spent at the Cí-yún stupa-hermitage 慈雲塔院 built for him by his nephew-disciple [[祖灊]] Zǔ-qián north of the Zuì-lǐ 欈李 pavilion. He died on the 25th of the 8th month of Zhìzhèng 27 (27 September 1367), aged 76, *sēng-là* 60 — the *xíng-dào-jì*'s reading "至正癸卯" (= Zhìzhèng 23 / 1363) is a well-known textual crux, disagreeing with the standard biographical lifedates carried forward in *Wǔ-dēng quán-shū*, *Zēng-jí Xù-chuándēng-lù* and DILA; followed here, with a note of the divergence.

The text has an unusually complex printing history. The colophon at the head of *juan* 9 — "緣入梓。日東比丘海壽書。天台周東山刊。時至正已亥春起手。明年庚子春畢工。應安戊申重刊京臨川禪寺" — makes clear that an initial Japan-side cutting was executed by the Japanese monk [[海壽]] Hǎi-shòu as calligrapher and one Zhōu Dōng-shān 周東山 as cutter, started in the spring of Zhìzhèng 19 / 1359 and completed in the spring of Zhìzhèng 20 / 1360; a second cutting at Kyōto's Rinsen-ji 臨川禪寺 followed in the first year of the Japanese era Ōan 應安 (戊申 / 1368). The Xuzangjing base text includes material (notably Sòng Lián's preface of 1370 and *xíng-dào-jì*) later than either of these printings, which means the Xuzangjing form is a post-1370 consolidated recension — the dated date-bracket of the content is thus 1329 (first installation at Kāi-fú) to 1370 (Sòng Lián's preface).

## Translations and research

*No substantial Western-language secondary literature located.* Qīng-yù's *mò-jì* / *bokuseki* in Japanese temple collections are a substantial secondary subject; see Japanese *bokuseki* studies, as well as the biographical apparatus transmitted in *Zēng-jí Xù-chuándēng-lù* *juan* 6, *Wǔ-dēng quán-shū* *juan* 52, and the *Xíng-dào-jì* by Sòng Lián (whose preface-writing for a Chán master also figures in *Sòng Xué-shì wén-jí* 宋學士文集).

## Other points of interest

Qīng-yù figures prominently in the biography of his teacher [[清茂]] and of his own dharma-heir [[文康]] (Mù-ān Wén-kāng, [[KR6q0348]]); the present collection is read by Japanese specialists for its prefaces, which together trace an arc from the late-Yuán Jiāng-nán literary elite (via [[正印]] Yuè-jiāng Zhèng-yìn and Zhì-rén) into the newly-founded Míng court (via Sòng Lián, 1370), and in fact constitute one of the earliest Hóng-wǔ-era testimonies to how the old Yuán Chán-hierarchy was being re-positioned vis-à-vis the new state.
