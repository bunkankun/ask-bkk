# Tán-fāng Shǒu-zhōng chánshī yǔlù 曇芳守忠禪師語錄

Two-juan Yuán *yǔlù* of **Tán-fāng Shǒu-zhōng** [[守忠]] 曇芳守忠 (1275 – 1348), Yángqí-branch Línjì master; compiled by his principal dharma-heir [[繼祖]] Jì-zǔ, abbot of Chángzhōu-lù Shàn-quán chán-sì 常州路善權禪寺. Shǒu-zhōng held five major abbacies — Jiàn-kāng-lù Chóng-yīn chán-sì 崇因禪寺 (from Dàdé 9 / 1305, fifteen years), Jiàn-kāng-lù Jiǎng-shān Tài-píng Xīng-guó chán-sì 蔣山太平興國禪寺 (from Zhìzhì 1 / 1321), Jí-qìng-lù Jiǎng-shān Dà-chóng-xǐ Wàn-shòu-sì 蔣山大崇禧萬壽寺 (a new foundation bestowed by the future Wén-zōng 文宗 in Tàidìng 3 / 1326 and imperially retitled in Tiānlì 1 / 1328), Hángzhōu-lù Jìng-shān Xīng-shèng Wàn-shòu chán-sì 徑山興聖萬壽禪寺 (as its 49th abbot, from Zhìzhèng 2 / 1342), and Dà-lóng-xiáng Jí-qìng-sì 大龍翔集慶寺 (from Zhìzhèng 5 / 1345) — which gave the collection its frequent alternate title *Wǔ-huì lù* 五會錄 ("Records of Five Assemblies"). *Xuzangjing* X71 no. 1410; *juan* 1 gathers the first four abbacy-records (Chóng-yīn, Jiǎng-shān Tài-píng Xīng-guó, Dà-chóng-xǐ Wàn-shòu, Jìng-shān); *juan* 2 contains the Dà-lóng-xiáng record plus *jì-sòng*, *fó-zǔ zhēn-zàn*, *zì-zàn*, *fù-lù*, *tí-bá*, and — valuably for biography — the *xíng-yè-jì* 行業記 by the disciple Kè-xīn 克新 (Zhìzhèng 9 / 1349) and the *tǎ-míng* 塔銘 by the eminent Yuán statesman-historian Ōuyáng Xuán 歐陽玄 (also Zhìzhèng 9 / 1349), calligraphed by Zhào Yōng 趙雍 with a seal-script title by Zhào Yǎn 趙儼.

### Abstract

The compilation is anchored by the preface of Yú Jí 虞集 (1272–1348) — Yuán *hàn-lín* academician and leading cultural figure — styling himself "Wéi-xiào jū-shì" 微笑居士 of Shǔ-jùn, dated Zhìzhèng 6 / 1346 at Línchuān, supplying the doctrinal framework in which the book has long been read: a genealogy from Yuán-wù Kè-qín 圜悟克勤 through Sōng-yuán Chóng-yuè 松源崇嶽, 無得通 Wú-dé Tōng, [[普度]] Xū-zhōu Pǔ-dù, and [[德珍]] Yù-shān Dé-zhēn (*běn-shī* 本師 to Shǒu-zhōng, carrying the imperial title Fó-guāng Hǎi-yìn 佛光海印) down to Shǒu-zhōng himself. Shǒu-zhōng was a native of Dū-chāng 都昌 in Nán-kāng (Jiāngxī), lay surname Huáng 黃; he was tonsured at eleven under Yù-shān Dé-zhēn at Yún-jū-shān 雲居山, trained widely at Jiǎng-shān, Tiān-xǐ, Tàipíng-sì, Xiū-xiū-ān 休休菴 (under Měng-shān Yì 蒙山異), and Jìng-shān (under [[淨伏]] Hǔ-yán Jìng-fú, as 綱維 for a year), and attained awakening under Dé-zhēn at Líng-yǐn on the "cypress tree in the courtyard" 庭前柏樹子 *huàtóu*. His career was shaped decisively by relations with Tuō-huān 脫歡 (江浙行省丞相 from 1321), with the future Wén-zōng 文宗 (then 太子梁王, during the imperial progress to Jīn-líng in 1325, which led to the foundation of Chóng-xǐ Wàn-shòu on Bǎo-zhū-fēng), and with the Shùndì court (reception at the 奎章閣 in 1330, extensive imperial gifts, multiple title-investitures). Imperial titles he received include Fó-hǎi Pǔ-yìn dà-chánshī 佛海普印大禪師 (1328) and Dà-zhōng dà-fū Guǎng-cí Yuán-wù dà-chánshī 大中大夫廣慈圓悟大禪師 (1329). He died at Dà-lóng-xiáng on the 28th of the 10th month of Zhìzhèng 8 (27 November 1348), *shì-shòu* 74, *sēng-là* 56; his ashes were divided, with the principal stupa at Lóng-pán-ān 龍蟠菴 (the "Immovable Stupa" 不動塔) and a second on Líng-xiāo-fēng 凌霄峯 at Jìng-shān. Named dharma-heirs in the *Zēng-jí Xù-chuándēng-lù* include [[繼祖]] of Shàn-quán, Lán-jiāng Qīng-chǔ 蘭江清濋, Shòu-yán Chūn 壽巖椿, Xī-yuán Dá 西源達, Yuán-zōng Xìng 圓宗性, and Xī-yǐn Zǔ 西隱祖.

Two features make this *yǔlù* exceptional among Yuán Chán collections. First, its dense documentary record: the *tǎ-míng* by Ōuyáng Xuán and the *xíng-yè-jì* by Kè-xīn — both completed within a year of the master's death — together furnish an unusually complete paper trail for imperial-era Chán patronage, including specific titles, dates, gift-lists, and the diplomatic background to Shǒu-zhōng's 1330 audience at the Kuí-zhāng-gé 奎章閣 and his refusal of the Dà-lóng-xiáng foundership (which he redirected to Xiào-yǐn Dà-xīn 笑隱大訢, then of Zhōng Tiān-zhú). Second, its position in late-Yuán Jiāng-nán cultural networks: the preface by Yú Jí and the calligraphy by Zhào Yōng (grandson of Zhào Mèng-fǔ 趙孟頫) tie Shǒu-zhōng directly to the highest register of Yuán literary-elite patronage, and explain the collection's wide circulation under the *Wǔ-huì lù* title.

## Translations and research

*No substantial secondary literature located* in Western-language scholarship. Chinese and Japanese coverage is confined to biographical compendia (*Zēng-jí Xù-chuándēng-lù* *juan* 6; *Xù-dēng zhèng-tǒng* *juan* 24; *Nán-Sòng Yuán-Míng chán-lín sēng-bǎo zhuàn*; *Shì-shì jī-gǔ lüè xù-jí* 釋氏稽古略續集) and to studies of the Jiǎng-shān Chóng-xǐ and Dà-lóng-xiáng foundations — which, because of Shǒu-zhōng's close connection with Wén-zōng as crown prince and then emperor, occupy a particular place in the historiography of Yuán imperial Buddhism.

## Other points of interest

Ōuyáng Xuán's *tǎ-míng* preserves the unusually specific detail that when Shǒu-zhōng's cremation-yielded *shè-lì* lodged in leaves in the surrounding grove, villagers gathered the branches and sold them in the marketplace (樵牧連枝。取鬻諸市). The inscription, a primary document of Yuán religious patronage, reports by name the court donation-lists transmitted through the Xuānzhèng-yuàn 宣政院 and the Dà-xǐ zōng-yīn-yuàn 大禧宗禋院, with specific metallic weights, bolts of *nà-shī-shī* 納失失 (*nasij*) brocade, and named artisan-donors (the court-painter Zhào-yuàn-pàn 趙院判 and the sculptor-officials Shàng-Liú èr-zǒng-guǎn 尚劉二總管 executed the new Buddha-hall icons by imperial order).
