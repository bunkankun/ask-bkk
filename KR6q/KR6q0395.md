# Wú-huàn chán-shī yǔlù 無幻禪師語錄
*Recorded Sayings of Chán Master Wú-huàn*
by [[性沖]] (說), [[慧廣]] (編集)

## About the work

Two-juan recorded-sayings of the late-Míng **Línjì Yáng-qí 臨濟楊岐** Chán master **Wú-huàn Gǔ-zhàn Xìng-chōng** [[性沖]] (1540–1612), direct dharma-heir of [[如空|Wú-qù Rú-kōng]] 無趣如空 (1491–1580) and principal teacher at **Chē-xī-ān** 車溪庵 in the Sū-zhōu region (*jì-jì* 寄跡 for over twenty years) and at **Jìng-shān** 徑山 near Hángzhōu. Compiled (*biān-jí* 編集) by his direct disciple **Nán-míng Huì-guǎng** [[慧廣]] (1576–1620) and printed through the patronage of **Chéng Shàng-fǔ** 程尚甫. Preserved in the *Jiāxīng zàng* as J25 no. B156. The text is framed by three prefaces — by **Zhàn-rán Yuán-chéng** 湛然圓澄 (1561–1626, dated Wàn-lì 42 = 1614), by **Càí Shàn-jì** 蔡善繼 (*Xià-xiū-shēng* 夏休生) of Wú-xìng, and by **Yún-qī Zhū-hóng** 雲棲袾宏 (1535–1615) — and closed by Xìng-chōng's *xíng-zhuàng* 行狀 signed by the editor Huì-guǎng and fellow disciples (*ménrén Huì-guǎng děng qǐ-shǒu shù* 門人慧廣等稽首述). This is the companion volume to the *yǔlù* of Xìng-chōng's master in [[KR6q0394]]. Non-commentary; *commentedTextid* omitted.

### Abstract

**Author.** Xìng-chōng, styled *Gǔ-zhàn* 古湛 ("Ancient Deep Stillness") and epithet *Wú-huàn* 無幻 ("Without Illusion"), was a native of **Hé-jùn Xiù-shuǐ** 禾郡秀水 (= Jiā-xìng Xiù-shuǐ, Zhèjiāng), lay surname **Zhāng** 張, lay name **Qiáo** 橋 (given from his mother's dream of a rooster flying up from a bridge). His early Buddhist formation came under the layman **Fāng Yàn-shān** 方硯山, who directed him to read the scriptures; when Xìng-chōng concluded that "the Tripiṭaka and twelve-fold corpus are but fish-traps and rabbit-snares" (*sān-zàng shí-èr-bù dà-yuē zhǐ-shì quán-tí ěr* 三藏十二部大約秪是筌蹄耳), Fāng sent him to [[如空|Wú-qù Rú-kōng]] with the endorsement that this was "a Línjì child in truth, an authentic figure of the Way." On first meeting Rú-kōng rebuked him — "remembering this tangle fouls a single pure-white inch of you" (*jì cǐ yī luò-suǒ wū rǔ yī piàn qīng-bái dì* 記此一絡索汙汝一片清白地) — and set him to investigation. Xìng-chōng could not take the tonsure immediately because of his mother; he served her while practising at Yuán-míng-sì 圓明寺 and was *yìnkě*'d only after she died. On his announced intention to retire to Jìng-shān, Rú-kōng pronounced the cryptic: "your karmic connection lies at the water's edge" (*zǐ yuán zài shuǐ biān* 子緣在水邊). The transmission verse was delivered in connection with a striking incident: Rú-kōng was about to burn his accumulated verses and *jì-sòng* 偈頌; Xìng-chōng begged to rescue and anthologise them, and brought the completed anthology back as a gift — presenting it as "some farmland and its original title-deed, requesting the Master's signature." Rú-kōng opened it: "This is mine — where is yours?" Xìng-chōng replied, "Master, do not grasp at the market." Rú-kōng threw down the manuscript; Xìng-chōng turned to leave, then submitted four verses of his own. Rú-kōng conferred robe-dharma with the verse *Shī chuán niān-huā zōng, shì wǒ wēi-xiào fǎ, qīn shǒu zhǎn fù rǔ, chí-fèng biàn chén-shā* 師傳拈花宗／示我微笑法／親手展付汝／持奉遍塵剎. After Rú-kōng's 1580 death Xìng-chōng stayed at Jìng-shān three years, then sealed himself into door-closed *bì-guān* at Yuán-míng-sì for another three. On opening the gate he accepted the invitation of the monk **Zhèng-chuān** 證川 of **Chē-xī-ān** and made his principal residence there for more than twenty years (ca. 1589–1611), fulfilling the "water's edge" prediction (Chē-xī = "stream-cart" referring to the adjacent stream). In spring Wàn-lì 38 *gēng-xū* (1610) Jìng-shān established a chán-period and requested him as leader; he refused; the following spring (1611) he accepted reluctantly, served half a year, fell ill with *pí-jí* 脾疾 (spleen disorder), and returned to Chē-xī; on Wàn-lì 39.12.4 (= 6 January **1612**) he arranged final matters, sat upright, and died, aged 72 with 30 *là* (ordained years). His relic-bones were enshrined in a stūpa at Jìng-shān. Dharma-heir and compiler: Huì-guǎng.

**Contents.** Juan 1 (*shàng*): three prefaces → **住車溪庵語** (*Zhù Chē-xī-ān yǔ* — record of his Chē-xī abbacy, *xiǎo-cān* 小參, *niān-xiāng* 拈香 dedications including the anniversary-*jì-chén* 忌辰 of Rú-kōng, *jī-yuán* 機緣 dialogues with *Xiù-zuò-zhǔ* 秀座主 and other interlocutors) → **徑山語** (*Jìng-shān yǔ* — the brief but dense half-year teaching at Jìng-shān in 1611, opening with his pronouncement that he had come because "Wú-qù lǎo-rén left an unfinished *gōng-àn* that this old man came out to conclude") → **示禪人** (*shì chán-rén* — dharma-instructions to named and unnamed practitioners) → **機緣** (*jī-yuán* — extended encounter-dialogues) → **拈古** (*niān-gǔ* — comments on classical cases) → **頌古** (*sòng-gǔ* — twenty-five verses on set cases, from Huáng-lóng's abbacy-selection to Lǔ-zǔ 魯祖's wall-facing, Mù-zhōu's 喪考妣, the *Qīng-zhōu bù-shān* 青州布衫, Zhào-zhōu's *chī-chá qù* 喫茶去, Yún-mén's *rì-lǐ kàn-shān* 日裏看山, the Sixth Patriarch's *bú-huì fó-fǎ* 不會佛法, Dé-shān's alms-bowl, Yǎng-shān's *yī-èr-sān* verse, and others) → **讚** (*zàn* — encomia on Guān-yīn images of several types: 水月, 魚籃, etc.). Juan 2 (*xià*): **偈頌** (*jì-sòng* — extensive verse corpus), opening with the remarkable *Jí Wú-qù héshàng yǔ jì ǒu-chéng sān shǒu* 集無趣和尚語偈偶成三首 (three verses spontaneously produced while anthologising Rú-kōng's sayings — the very anthology that triggered Rú-kōng's robe-transmission); then *Cān-chán jì shí-yī shǒu* 參禪偈 (11 pieces), *Shì chán-rén liù shǒu* (6), occasional verses of place and travel (*Shān-lóu jì Huàn* 山樓寄幻, *Dēng Wū-zhān-shān* 登烏瞻山, *Jìng-shān huí zhì Yuán-míng chán-yuàn guān-jū èr shǒu* 徑山回至圓明禪院關居二首, *Zhá-xī dào-zhōng* 霅溪道中), exchanges with lay interlocutors (including the jū-shì **Shěn-yuán** 靜源, **Chōng-xuán Jì** 沖玄計 jū-shì, **Méi-cūn** 梅村, **Xī-guī-zǐ** 西歸子), seasonal meditations (*Shān-jū shí shǒu* 山居十首, *Xuě-zhōng jí-shì* 雪中即事), and extended *niàn-fó sòng* 念佛頌, *zhǐ-xīn sòng* 指心頌, and the *Mù-niú tú sòng* 牧牛圖頌 (verses on the 10-frame Ox-Herding picture sequence). The juan closes with the *xíng-zhuàng*.

**Lay dedications in the prefaces.** Càí Shàn-jì 蔡善繼 (1565–1625) of Wú-xìng 吳興, a prominent Míng jū-shì known by his *hào* *Xià-xiū-shēng* 夏休生, writes the middle preface and emphasises the extraordinary coincidence that father-and-son *yǔlù* — Rú-kōng's [[KR6q0394]] and Xìng-chōng's present [[KR6q0395]] — appeared simultaneously, a pairing he compares to the rare **Udumbara-flower re-blooming** and to the Yuán-dynasty **Gāo-fēng / Zhōng-fēng 高峰 / 中峰 父子** double-transmission. Càí invokes **Yún-qī Zhū-hóng** 雲棲袾宏 as the figure who alone could adjudicate the rarity; and in the third preface, Yún-qī himself (*lǎo-bìng sēng Zhū-hóng hé-nán jǐn zhì* 老病僧袾宏和南謹識) modestly declines to expound, saying only that without having met Xìng-chōng in person he nonetheless reads the *yǔlù* "as though seeing the master's face."

**Dating.** notBefore 1580 (Rú-kōng's death; Xìng-chōng's independent teaching career begins). notAfter 1615 (Yún-qī Zhū-hóng's death on Wàn-lì 43.7.4 = 27 August 1615, terminus for the third preface and hence for the received text as framed). The Zhàn-rán Yuán-chéng preface is explicitly dated to Wàn-lì 42 *jiǎ-yín* (1614) 夾鍾月 (second lunar month) and gives the tightest verifiable pre-publication terminus. The Chē-xī-ān teaching occupies ca. 1589–1611; the Jìng-shān half-year is mid-1611; compilation by Huì-guǎng falls between 1612 and the 1614 preface.

## Translations and research

*No substantial Western-language secondary literature located on Xìng-chōng specifically.* He appears in Jiang Wu, *Enlightenment in Dispute: The Reinvention of Chan Buddhism in Seventeenth-Century China* (Oxford, 2008) only as background to the Míng-Qīng transition Línjì revival. The trilateral lay-clerical frame of the present *yǔlù* (Càí Shàn-jì, Yún-qī Zhū-hóng, Zhàn-rán Yuán-chéng) makes it an excellent primary text for studies of the late-Wàn-lì Jiā-xìng / Sū-zhōu Buddhist network; see Chün-fang Yü, *The Renewal of Buddhism in China: Zhuhong and the Late Ming Synthesis* (Columbia, 1981) for Yún-qī's circle, and the collected studies in Timothy Brook, *Praying for Power* (Harvard, 1993) for the lay-donor pattern. For the Tiān-mù-shān Línjì sub-lineage that Xìng-chōng preserved, see Natasha Heller, *Illusory Abiding* (Harvard, 2014).

## Other points of interest

- The anecdote of Xìng-chōng's **rescue and anthologisation of Rú-kōng's verses** — preserved in both the 行狀 at the end of juan 2 and the compressed *Jí Wú-qù héshàng yǔ jì ǒu-chéng sān shǒu* 集無趣和尚語偈偶成三首 at the head of juan 2 — is the internal explanation for why Rú-kōng's own *yǔlù* ([[KR6q0394]]) survives at all: its edited form is likely traceable back to this same editorial effort by Xìng-chōng. The two *yǔlù* are thus not just parallel father-and-son records but textually co-dependent.
- Xìng-chōng's half-year **Jìng-shān abbacy** (1611) is dated in the *xíng-zhuàng* and provides a rare dated glimpse into the state of Jìng-shān teaching on the eve of the **Mì-yún Yuán-wù** 密雲圓悟 / **Hàn-yuè Fǎ-zàng** 漢月法藏 generation that would reanimate Línjì Chán in the Tiān-qǐ and Chóng-zhēn eras.
- The *yǔlù* closes with a full **Mù-niú tú sòng** 牧牛圖頌 — Xìng-chōng's rendering of the ten-stage Ox-Herding sequence — a late-Míng revival of the classic Pǔ-míng 普明 / Kuò-ān 廓庵 meditative programme.
- The three-preface opening by a **Cáo-dòng** figure (Yuán-chéng, self-identifying here as 27th-generation Cáo-dòng, not as his more usual Yún-mén persona), a **Pure Land / syncretist** (Yún-qī), and a **lay literatus** (Càí) gives an unusually rich map of Xìng-chōng's cross-sectarian reception.
