# Zhēn-xiē Qīng-liǎo chánshī yǔlù 真歇清了禪師語錄

Two-juan Southern-Sòng Cáo-dòng 曹洞 *yǔlù* of **Zhēn-xiē Qīng-liǎo** [[清了]] 真歇清了 (*hào* Zhēn-xiē 真歇, also 寂菴 Jì-ān; posthumous title Wù-kōng chánshī 悟空禪師; 1088 – 17 November 1151), dharma-heir of [[子淳]] Dān-xiá Zǐ-chún (1066–1119) and thus — alongside his dharma-brother Hóng-zhì Zhèng-jué 宏智正覺 (1091–1157) — the twin apex of Southern-Sòng Cáo-dòng and a principal voice in the Cáo-dòng response to Dà-huì Zōng-gǎo 大慧宗杲's *kàn-huà* 看話 polemic. *Xuzangjing* X71 no. 1426. The collection is more commonly known in Sòng and later sources under its alternate title **Jié-wài lù** 劫外錄 ("Record Beyond the Aeons"). Compiled by Qīng-liǎo's attendants [[德初]] Dé-chū and [[義初]] Yì-chū (the latter also a named dharma-heir, DILA A001465).

### Abstract

The collection is framed by a Southern-Sòng preface by **Wú Mǐn** 吳敏 (Zhōng-qiáo jū-shì 中橋居士, the Song dynasty official known for his stand at the Jìng-kāng catastrophe), dated the 1st of the 1st month of Shào-xīng 28 (1 February 1158) — seven years after Qīng-liǎo's death — which places his career in a characteristic Cáo-dòng frame: "Fú-róng's grandson, Dān-xiá's son, who obtained the dharma on Bō-yú-fēng 鉢盂峯 by having nothing to obtain, and preached the dharma at Yī-wěi 一葦 [Cháng-lú 長蘆] by having nothing to preach." Wú Mǐn claims a thousand-seven-hundred-monk seat at Cháng-lú and — in a striking phrase — "the sound of wooden cockerels crowing at frost, stone tigers roaring at clouds."

The collection has an exceptionally complex transmission history. Both juan were already organised around Qīng-liǎo's teaching in his own lifetime; *juan* 2, structured as *niān-gǔ* 拈古 with a closing *bá* by Yì-yuǎn 義遠, is the part that most directly engages the Dà-huì polemic, contesting in particular Dà-huì's *dōng-shān* 東山 critique of "*jì-ān mò-zhào* 寂菴默照." (The closing anonymous colophon to the *niān-gǔ* says baldly: "In the Shào-xīng era, when the Miào-xǐ 妙喜 [= Dà-huì] lineage was denouncing *Mò-zhào Jì-ān* 默照寂菴, this [work] may be called 'entering the hall and seizing the spear, taking the lance and striking the shield,'" 入其室操其戈取其矛擊其盾.)

The Xuzangjing text is a late-Edo Japanese re-cut. The opening preface ***Jiào-zhèng chóng-kè Jié-wài lù yǐn*** 較正重刻劫外錄引 is by **Hō-men-zan Kantō** 方面山盥董, Japanese Sōtō monk and self-identified 33rd-generation descendant (*yuǎn-sūn* 遠孫) of Qīng-liǎo; the text is dated the 5th of the 1st month of Meiwa *Dīng-hài* 丁亥 (3 February 1767), written at the Kinryūken 金龍軒 of Suiryū-san Zuiryūzan 瑞龍山 in east Kyōto. Hō-men-zan complains that the previous Japanese Kan-ei 寬永-era printing had been riddled with errors and missing a preface to the *tǎ-míng*, and records that while residing at Hi-no-hi Konchū-in 惠日艮嶽院 in the autumn of 1766 he found an old manuscript in a pile of worm-eaten books and corrected the text against it. He also notes with regret that the *tǎ-míng* references "two collections" of Qīng-liǎo's language (*yǔlù liǎng-jí xíng yú shì* 語錄兩集行於世) — implying the existence of a lost *guǎng-lù* 廣錄 comparable to Hóng-zhì's — which he was unable to recover and has replaced, in part, with scattered fragments ("a piece of jade from Kūn-shān that cannot be discarded").

Qīng-liǎo was a native of Zuǒ-mián Ān-chāng 左綿安昌 (Jiàn-zhōu), lay surname Yōng 雍; ordained at eleven under Qīng-jùn 清俊 of Shèng-guǒ-sì 聖果寺; took the precepts at eighteen after a *shì-jīng* 試經 on the *Lotus*; studied Buddhist scholasticism at Chéng-dū Dà-cí-sì 大慈寺. Exited Shǔ to Biàn-hàn 沔漢 and was sent by Zǔ-zhào 祖照 禪師 (at Cháng-lú) to Dān-xiá-shān; attained awakening under Zǐ-chún when Zǐ-chún asked him "What is the self before the empty *kalpa*?" 如何是空劫以前自己 and struck him once as he tried to answer. Installed at Cháng-lú 長蘆 in the autumn of Xuān-hé 4 (1122) on the recommendation of the *jīng-zhì* 經制 official Chén Gōng-zhāng 陳公璋; at the end of the Jìng-kāng era travelled to the Sì-míng coast and established himself at Bǔ-tuó Luò-jiā 補陀洛迦山, then at Tái-zhōu Tiān-fēng 天封; then Fújiàn Xuě-fēng 雪峰; then (from early Shào-xīng) at Āyù-wáng 育王 (as 14th abbot), then Wēn-zhōu Lóng-xiáng 龍翔, then Jìng-shān 徑山. In Shào-xīng 21 (1151) the imperial court commanded the founding of Chóng-xiān Xiǎn-xiào chán-yuàn 崇先顯孝禪院 for the Cí-níng Tài-hòu 慈寧太后 (the mother of Emperor Gāo-zōng) and summoned Qīng-liǎo as its inaugural abbot, where he died four months after installation, on the 1st of the 10th month (17 November 1151), *shì-shòu* 64, *sēng-là* 45. Named dharma-heirs (eleven): Huì-wù 慧悟, Xìng-yù 興譽, [[義初]] Yì-chū, Zōng-jué 宗玨, Fǎ-tōng 法通, and others.

Date bracket: first abbacy at Cháng-lú in 1122 through the Wú Mǐn preface of 1158 (the Meiwa re-cut of 1767 is outside the recensional window of the Sòng text).

## Translations and research

*No book-length Western-language study of the *yǔlù* or the *Jié-wài lù* located*; partial coverage in Morten Schlütter, *How Zen Became Zen* (2008) and in studies of the Dà-huì / *mò-zhào* polemic. Biographical apparatus: the *tǎ-míng* preserved in *juan* 1, titled *Chóng-xiān Zhēn-xiē Liǎo chánshī tǎ-míng* 崇先真歇了禪師塔銘; *Jiā-tài pǔ-dēng-lù* *juan* 9 (X79); *Bǔ-xù gāo-sēng zhuàn* (X77). Qīng-liǎo's independent Pure-Land text *Niàn-fó jìng-tǔ lún* 念佛淨土論 (X61 n1178) is a standard witness for the Cáo-dòng contribution to the Chán / Pure-Land synthesis.

## Other points of interest

Qīng-liǎo is, together with Hóng-zhì, the principal Cáo-dòng target of Dà-huì Zōng-gǎo's polemic against "*mò-zhào xié-chán* 默照邪禪" ("silent-illumination heresy-Chán"); the *Jié-wài lù*'s *niān-gǔ* section preserves Qīng-liǎo's own counter-polemic, which — in combination with Hóng-zhì's *Mò-zhào míng* 默照銘 and Hóng-zhì's and Qīng-liǎo's mutual *bǐ-mù* 筆目 — is a foundational Cáo-dòng doctrinal document for the "silent-illumination" tradition that eventually becomes one of the chief rubrics of Japanese Sōtō *shikantaza* 只管打坐 practice.
