# Dānxiá Dànguī chánshī yǔlù 丹霞澹歸禪師語錄
*Recorded Sayings of Chán Master Dàn-guī of Dān-xiá*
by [[今釋]] (說), [[今辯]] (重編)

Three-juan *yǔlù* of **Dànguī Jīnshì** [[今釋]] 澹歸今釋 (**1615-01-28 – 1680-09-01**, age 67), Cáodòng dharma-heir of **Tiānrán Hánshì** [[函昰]]. As the layman **Jīn Bǎo** 金堡 (*zì* **Dàoyǐn** 道隱), Jīnshì held the **Chóngzhēn 庚辰** = **1640** *jìnshì* and rose to *bīngkē jǐshìzhōng* in the **Yǒnglì** 永曆 court of the Southern Míng — one of the so-called **"Five Tigers"** 五虎 of that court — before tonsure at **Léifēng** in **壬辰** = **1652** under Tiānrán. He is among the most studied of the late-Míng / early-Qīng *yímín* 遺民 monk-scholars. Compiled (*zhòngbiān* 重編) by his co-disciple **Lèshuō Jīnbiàn** [[今辯]] — the third in 今辯's series of major Hǎiyún 海雲-lineage *yǔlù* re-editions, alongside [[KR6q0526]] (his teacher), [[KR6q0527]] (his teacher's *fǎxiōng* 函可), and [[KR6q0528]] (his co-disciple 今無).

The *yǔlù* records his teaching at **Dānxiá Biéchuánsì** 丹霞別傳寺, the monastery he himself constructed at **Sháozhōu Rénhuà** 仁化 (Guǎngdōng) over five years (1662–1667) amid wartime danger; he formally succeeded as abbot in **甲寅** = **1674** when Tiānrán returned to Lúshān. *notBefore* = **1662** (Dānxiá entry); *notAfter* ≈ **1690** (the closing *tǎmíng* in juan 3 was composed by 今辯 nine years after Jīnshì's death = ca. 1689). Printed as Jiāxīng Canon J38 B409. The catalog registers the dynasty as 明 — appropriate to Jīnshì's continuing *yímín* self-understanding even after tonsure.

**Contents.** Juan 1: *shàngtáng*. Juan 2: *shìzhòng*, *fóshì*, *yímìng* (last instructions), *wèndá*. Juan 3: *niāngǔsòng*, *sònggǔniān*, **行狀** (biographical inscription), and **塔銘** (stupa-inscription) — both authored by his co-disciple [[今辯]].

## Other points of interest

The 行狀 in juan 3 contains the famous account of Dànguī's burial of **Qú Shìsì** 瞿式耜 and **Zhāng Tóngchǎng** 張同敞, the two senior Yǒng-lì-court martyrs killed at Guìlín, and his open letter to the Qīng feudatory princes **Dìngnán** 定南 and **Píngnán** 平南 invoking historical precedents (Táng Gāozǔ honoring Yáo Jūnsù; Zhōu Shìzōng honoring Liú Rénshàn; Míng Tàizǔ honoring the Yuán Yùshǐdàfū Fúshòu) for honoring fallen loyalists of the previous dynasty — a remarkable document of *yímín* ethics by a tonsured former official. Dànguī's larger literary collection 《徧行堂集》 was placed on the **Qiánlóng forbidden-books index** in the 1770s; the *yǔlù*, embedded in the Buddhist canon, escaped destruction.
