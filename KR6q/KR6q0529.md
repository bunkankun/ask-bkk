# Dān-xiá Dàn-guī chán-shī yǔ-lù 丹霞澹歸禪師語錄
*Recorded Sayings of Chán Master Dàn-guī of Dān-xiá*
by [[今釋]] (說), [[今辯]] (重編)

Three-juan *yǔ-lù* of **Dàn-guī Jīn-shì** [[今釋]] 澹歸今釋 (**1615-01-28 – 1680-09-01**, age 67), Cáodòng dharma-heir of **Tiān-rán Hán-shì** [[函昰]]. As the layman **Jīn Bǎo** 金堡 (*zì* **Dào-yǐn** 道隱), Jīn-shì held the **Chóngzhēn 庚辰** = **1640** *jìn-shì* and rose to *bīng-kē jǐ-shì-zhōng* in the **Yǒng-lì** 永曆 court of the Southern Míng — one of the so-called **"Five Tigers"** 五虎 of that court — before tonsure at **Léi-fēng** in **壬辰** = **1652** under Tiān-rán. He is among the most studied of the late-Míng / early-Qīng *yí-mín* 遺民 monk-scholars. Compiled (*zhòng-biān* 重編) by his co-disciple **Lè-shuō Jīn-biàn** [[今辯]] — the third in 今辯's series of major Hǎi-yún 海雲-lineage *yǔ-lù* re-editions, alongside [[KR6q0526]] (his teacher), [[KR6q0527]] (his teacher's *fǎ-xiōng* 函可), and [[KR6q0528]] (his co-disciple 今無).

The *yǔ-lù* records his teaching at **Dān-xiá Bié-chuán-sì** 丹霞別傳寺, the monastery he himself constructed at **Sháo-zhōu Rén-huà** 仁化 (Guǎng-dōng) over five years (1662–1667) amid wartime danger; he formally succeeded as abbot in **甲寅** = **1674** when Tiān-rán returned to Lú-shān. *notBefore* = **1662** (Dān-xiá entry); *notAfter* ≈ **1690** (the closing *tǎ-míng* in juan 3 was composed by 今辯 nine years after Jīn-shì's death = ca. 1689). Printed as Jiā-xīng Canon J38 B409. The catalog registers the dynasty as 明 — appropriate to Jīn-shì's continuing *yí-mín* self-understanding even after tonsure.

**Contents.** Juan 1: *shàng-táng*. Juan 2: *shì-zhòng*, *fó-shì*, *yí-mìng* (last instructions), *wèn-dá*. Juan 3: *niān-gǔ-sòng*, *sòng-gǔ-niān*, **行狀** (biographical inscription), and **塔銘** (stupa-inscription) — both authored by his co-disciple [[今辯]].

## Other points of interest

The 行狀 in juan 3 contains the famous account of Dàn-guī's burial of **Qú Shì-sì** 瞿式耜 and **Zhāng Tóng-chǎng** 張同敞, the two senior Yǒng-lì-court martyrs killed at Guì-lín, and his open letter to the Qīng feudatory princes **Dìng-nán** 定南 and **Píng-nán** 平南 invoking historical precedents (Táng Gāo-zǔ honoring Yáo Jūn-sù; Zhōu Shì-zōng honoring Liú Rén-shàn; Míng Tài-zǔ honoring the Yuán Yù-shǐ-dàfū Fú-shòu) for honoring fallen loyalists of the previous dynasty — a remarkable document of *yí-mín* ethics by a tonsured former official. Dàn-guī's larger literary collection 《徧行堂集》 was placed on the **Qián-lóng forbidden-books index** in the 1770s; the *yǔ-lù*, embedded in the Buddhist canon, escaped destruction.
