# Yú-ān Zhì-jí chánshī yǔlù 愚菴智及禪師語錄

Ten-juan Yuán-to-early-Míng *yǔlù* of **Yú-ān Zhì-jí** [[智及]] 愚菴智及 (*zì* Yǐ-zhōng 以中, 別號 Xī-lù 西麓; imperial title from the Yuán Dì-shī Míng-biàn zhèng-zōng Guǎng-huì chánshī 明辨正宗廣慧禪師; 1311 – 3 October 1378), Yángqí-branch Línjì dharma-heir of [[行端]] Yuán-sǒu Xíng-duān ([[KR6q0352]]) — and thus *shī-xiōng* 師兄 to [[梵琦]] Chǔ-shí Fàn-qí, [[曇噩]] Mèng-táng Tán-è, [[祖銘]] Gǔ-dǐng Zǔ-míng, and [[法林]] Zhú-quán Fǎ-lín within the Yuán-sǒu circle — and 53rd *liè-zǔ* of Jìng-shān. *Xuzangjing* X71 no. 1421. Compiled by ten named *mén-rén* 門人: [[觀通]] Ruì-guāng Xī-yuǎn Guān-tōng 瑞光希遠觀通 (dharma-heir), [[溥震]], [[正除]], [[希顏]] Tiān-tóng Yòng-yú Xī-yán 天童用愚希顏 (dharma-heir), [[忻悟]] Líng-yǐn Kōng-sǒu Xīn-wù 靈隱空叟忻悟 (dharma-heir), [[寶盈]] Xùn-zhōng Bǎo-yíng 巽中寶盈 (dharma-heir), [[至寶]], [[慧鏡]], [[護位]], and [[道彝]].

The preserved structure: *juan* 1 Chāng-guó Lóng-jiào chán-sì 昌國隆教禪寺 (from Zhì-zhèng 2 / Rén-wǔ 壬午 1342, 30 *tiáo* 條); *juan* 2 Pǔ-cí chán-sì 普慈禪寺 (39 *tiáo*); *juan* 3 Hángzhōu Jìng-cí Bào-ēn chán-sì 淨慈報恩禪寺 (from 戊戌 1358, 34 *tiáo*); *juan* 4 Jìng-shān Xīng-shèng Wàn-shòu chán-sì (first term, 11 *tiáo*); *juan* 5 second term at Jìng-shān (59 *tiáo*); *juan* 6 *xiǎo-cān* (13 *tiáo*); *juan* 7 *sòng-gǔ* (55 首) and *zàn-yǔ* (35 首); *juan* 8 onwards close with miscellaneous verse and — in *juan* 10 — the closing *tǎ-míng* 塔銘.

### Abstract

The preface is by **Sòng Lián** 宋濂 (same *zhí-fèn* 職分 as in his preface to [[KR6q0352]]; dated 洪武8 / Yǐ-mǎo 乙卯.2.21 = 22 March 1375); he writes at the request of Qīng-liáng Zǐ-pián 清涼子楩 (用堂楩公), the same intermediary through whom the [[KR6q0352]] re-cutting had been arranged, carrying a manuscript prepared by [[觀通]] Guān-tōng. Sòng Lián's preface presents Zhì-jí as a "master of a generation" whose teaching "resolves the bindings of entanglement like a falcon slipping its jesses and rising into the clouds."

Zhì-jí was a native of Wú-xiàn 吳縣 (Sū-zhōu), lay surname Gù 顧. He entered religion as a child at Qióng-lóng Hǎi-yún-yuàn 穹窿海雲院 and later heard the Huáyán master Xián-shǒu 賢首 on the *Dharma-dhātu Contemplation* 法界觀. First visited Xiào-yǐn Dà-xīn 笑隱大訢 at Jiàn-yè; a crucial *wù* 悟 came on seeing autumn leaves fall onto the courtyard. Went to Shuāng-jìng (Jìng-shān), met Xíng-duān, submitted his attainment, and received *yìn-kě*.

Abbatial sequence: Chāng-guó Lóng-jiào 昌國隆教 (from 1342), Pǔ-cí 普慈, Jìng-cí 淨慈 (from 1358 by invitation of Chéng-xiàng Dá-shī 達失 [Dā-shì Tiě-mù-ěr]), two terms at Jìng-shān. Imperial title from the Yuán Dì-shī: Míng-biàn Zhèng-zōng Guǎng-huì chánshī 明辨正宗廣慧禪師. In Zhì-zhèng 35 = Yǐ-mǎo 乙卯 (1375) he returned to his home temple Hǎi-yún 海雲, where he died on the 4th of the 9th month of Hóng-wǔ 11 (3 October 1378), *shì-shòu* 68.

Named dharma-heirs — in addition to the four among the editors — include **Dú-ān Dào-yǎn** 獨庵道衍 of Běi-jīng Qìng-shòu 慶壽 (better known in Míng history as Yáo Guǎng-xiào 姚廣孝, the key strategist of the Yǒng-lè usurpation), Hǔ-qiū Nán-chēn Zhì-bǎo 虎丘南琛智寶, and Xùn-zhōng Bǎo-yíng. Zhì-jí's line is thus, in the Míng, a critical node between the late-Yuán Jiāng-nán Chán establishment and the political-cultural restructuring under Chéng-zǔ.

## Translations and research

*No substantial Western-language secondary literature on the yǔlù proper located.* Yao Guangxiao / Dào-yǎn studies (e.g., the Sino-Japanese monograph tradition on the Yongle usurpation) touch on Zhì-jí as his teacher. Chinese biographical apparatus: *Zēng-jí Xù-chuándēng-lù* *juan* 1 and 5; *Xù-dēng zhèng-tǒng* *juan* 13; *Wǔ-dēng quán-shū* *juan* 55; *Jìng-shān zhì* (53rd *liè-zǔ*).
