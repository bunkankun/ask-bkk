# Gǔ yīn wáng zhuàn 古音王傳

*Biography of the Ancient-Sound King* — a one-juan late-Míng allegorical-philosophical narrative by [[廣真|Chuīwàn Guǎngzhēn]] 吹萬廣真 (1582–1639), with extensive commentarial annotations by three contemporaries: [[古心|Gǔxīn]] 古心 (who provides *fán zé* 凡則 "editorial principles"), the layman [[黃毓英|Huáng Yùyīng]] 黃毠英, and [[元善|Yuánshàn]] 元善. The title evokes the classical Buddhist legend-figure *Gǔyīnwángfó* 古音王佛 ("Ancient-Sound King Buddha") — a trans-cosmic Buddha of the primordial era preceding the present world-age — which Guǎngzhēn uses as an allegorical frame for presenting his Chán-doctrinal position.

## About the work

A one-juan Chán allegorical-narrative text, J40 B481. Non-commentary (Guǎngzhēn's original text is a creative allegorical-biographical composition); *commentedTextid* omitted.

The text uses a legendary-biographical narrative as a framework for Chán-doctrinal exposition. The multiple commentators' annotations represent an unusual collaborative-commentarial publication: three contemporaries provide systematic running commentary on Guǎngzhēn's original allegory.

Note: the catalog classifies the dynasty as 清 (Qīng) but Guǎngzhēn's death in 1639 places him in the Míng; the text's composition thus falls within the late-Míng period.

### Abstract

See [[廣真]]'s person note. The *Gǔ yīn wáng zhuàn* complements Guǎngzhēn's *Yī guàn bié chuán* [[KR6q0235]] by providing his allegorical-narrative teaching-voice alongside the systematic-treatise voice of the former.

**Commentators**:
- **[[古心|Gǔxīn]]** 古心: senior Buddhist colleague or disciple of Guǎngzhēn.
- **[[黃毓英|Huáng Yùyīng]]** 黃毓英: lay Buddhist associate.
- **[[元善|Yuánshàn]]** 元善: Buddhist colleague.

All three commentators' lifedates and full biographical details are unrecorded.

Dating: same as [[KR6q0235]] — Guǎngzhēn's mature productive period (*notBefore* 1620, *notAfter* 1639).
