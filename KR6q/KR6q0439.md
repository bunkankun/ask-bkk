# Fǎ-lán Chéng chán-shī yǔ-lù 法瀾澂禪師語錄
*Recorded Sayings of Chán Master Fǎ-lán Chéng*
by [[原澂]] (說), [[清杲]] (編次)

## About the work

Two-juan *yǔlù* of **Fǎ-lán Yuán-chéng** [[原澂]] 法瀾原澂 — dharma-heir of **Shā-wēng Hǎi** 沙翁海 (also **敬太海老和尚** 敬太海老和尚) in the Hú-běi Jīng-zhōu regional Chán tradition, and the mid-Kāng-xī abbot of multiple **Jīng-zhōu-fǔ** seats including the classical **Tiān-wáng Chán-sì** 荊州天王禪寺 (ancestral seat of the Táng-era master **Tiān-wáng Dào-wù** 天王道悟, 727–808). *Fǎ-huì* 法諱 **Yuán-chéng** 原澂 (catalog-form; short-form **Chéng** 澂 in the work-title), *hào* **Fǎ-lán** 法瀾 ("Dharma-Wave"). Compiled by dharma-heir **Qīng-gǎo** [[清杲]] 清杲. Non-commentary; *commentedTextid* omitted. Printed as Jiā-xīng Canon J29 B229.

### Abstract

**Author.** Fǎ-lán Yuán-chéng was dharma-heir of **Shā-wēng Hǎi lǎo-héshàng** 沙翁海老和尚 — described in the opening *shàng-táng* as "*zhōng-xìng běn-sì sì zuò dào-chǎng běn-shī* 中興本寺四坐道場本師" ("restorer-master of the four abbacies of this temple") — a Jīng-zhōu regional master who revived the Tiān-wáng Chán-sì seat and three others. The *běn-shī*'s alternative-name is **Jìng-tài Hǎi** 敬太海 per the opening incense-line.

**Four Jīng-zhōu abbacies (1670–1673+).** (1) **Jīng-zhōu Tiān-wáng Chán-sì** 荊州天王禪寺 — first abbacy, entered **康熙 9 庚戌 2.1** = **21 March 1670**. (2) **Dāng-yáng Dù-mén Chán-sì** 當陽度門禪寺 — the Dù-mén-sì is the ancestral seat of **Shén-xiù** 神秀 (early 8th-century Northern Chán founder) in Dāng-yáng. (3) **Jīng-zhōu Pú-tí-chǎng** 荊州菩提場. (4) **Jīng-zhōu Bào-ēn Chán-sì** 荊州報恩禪寺. The seat-sequence represents a substantial mid-Kāng-xī Chán recovery in the Jīng-zhōu region — including the restoration of two classical ancestral seats (Tiān-wáng-sì of the Táng-era Tiān-wáng Dào-wù, and Dù-mén-sì of Shén-xiù).

**Compositional history.** Single front-preface by **Wáng Cái-dǐng** 王才鼎 (**鍾山** Zhōng-shān lay-patron), dated **康熙十二年 歲枉癸丑 七月既望** = **Kāng-xī 12 / 1673 lunar 7.16** = ~**26 August 1673** Western. The preface's notable feature is its strong **Confucian-Chán syncretic argument**: Wáng explicitly compares "*chéng-yì zhèng-xīn* 誠意正心" of Confucianism to "*míng-xīn jiàn-xìng* 明心見性" of Chán, arguing that the two paths converge in unity — a Qīng-era lay-Confucian Chán-justification register typical of the Kāng-xī period. *notBefore* = **1670** (Kāng-xī 9.2.1 Tiān-wáng entry); *notAfter* = **1673** (Wáng Cái-dǐng preface).

**Contents by juan.** (j.1) front-preface + **Tiān-wáng Chán-sì** *shàng-táng* + **Dù-mén Chán-sì** *shàng-táng*; (j.2) **Pú-tí-chǎng** + **Bào-ēn Chán-sì** *shàng-táng* + **jī-yuán** 機緣 + **rù-shì** 入室 + **jì-sòng** 偈頌 + **fó-shì** 佛事 + **塔記(附)** 塔記(附).

## Other points of interest

- **Tiān-wáng-sì restoration.** The **Jīng-zhōu Tiān-wáng Chán-sì** is the ancestral seat of the Táng-era master **Tiān-wáng Dào-wù** 天王道悟 (727–808), one of the famous **Mǎ-zǔ Dào-yī** 馬祖道一 successors and a founder-figure of the pre-Línjì Chán tradition. Yuán-chéng's 1670 restoration-abbacy is a specific mid-Kāng-xī reset for this ancestral seat, and his inherited master **Shā-wēng Hǎi**'s earlier four-abbacy restoration-program forms the immediate pre-history. For Jīng-zhōu Chán institutional history, this *yǔlù* is a focused primary source.
- **Dù-mén-sì and Shén-xiù connection.** The **Dāng-yáng Dù-mén-sì** 當陽度門禪寺 is the seat of **Shén-xiù** 神秀 (606?–706), the early-Táng founder of the **Northern Chán** tradition (displaced in orthodox historiography by Huì-néng's Southern Chán). Yuán-chéng's Dù-mén abbacy is a small but specific Qīng-era restoration of this canonically-Northern-Chán seat within a Southern-Chán Línjì-institutional framework — a syncretic-historical gesture worth noting for anyone tracing the reception of Northern Chán by post-Míng Línjì.
- **Wáng Cái-dǐng's Confucian-Chán syncretism.** The 1673 preface's explicit **诚意正心 / 明心見性** dual-path synthesis is a prime example of **Kāng-xī-era lay-Confucian Chán-patronage rhetoric** — reflecting the Qīng-court-approved Confucian-Buddhist accommodation policy that Kāng-xī would formalize later with his patronage of both traditions. For historians of Qīng religious-political discourse, the preface is a specific datum.
