# Wèi-lín Dào-pèi chánshī huán-shān lù 為霖道霈禪師還山錄

Four-juan late-career *yǔlù* of **Wèi-lín Dào-pèi** [[道霈]] 為霖道霈 (1615–1702), titled *Huán-shān lù* 還山錄 ("Record of Returning to the Mountain") — marking Dào-pèi's late-life return to Gǔ-shān after itinerant teaching in Fú-jiàn. *Xuzangjing* X72 no. 1440. Recorded (*lù* 錄) by four of his attendants: [[興燈]] Xīng-dēng, [[心亮]] Xīn-liàng, [[法雲]] Fǎ-yún, and [[性朗]] Xìng-lǎng — each marked as *shì-zhě* 侍者 in the editorial byline.

### Abstract

The text is bracketed by two prefaces and closed with a postface, which between them record its dual Chinese-Japanese publication history. The body carries a self-preface (*fēi-jiā-sǒu* 非家叟 = Dào-pèi) dated the *dēng-jié* 燈節 of Kāng-xī 27 / 1688; the four-juan core was thus finalised in early 1688 at Gǔ-shān. The Xuzangjing printing, however, preserves Japanese prefatorial material: an opening *xù* 序 by **Dú-ān Xuán-guāng** 獨菴玄光 (Dokuan Genkō, 1630–1698; a Japanese Rinzai monk of Edo with Ōbaku connections), dated 元祿癸酉十月二日 (31 October 1693); and a closing *hòu-xù* 後序 by **Jū-zān** 壽山祝 (a Chinese-born Sōtō monk then resident at Sa-shū Gyokuryū-san Fuku-shō-sen-ji 薩州玉龍山福昌禪寺 in Kyūshū, formerly a personal disciple of Dào-pèi's in Fú-jiàn), dated 元祿癸酉九月廿八日 (27 October 1693). Dokuan's preface narrates how Jū-zān, en route through Kyoto on business to the Edo *dà-fǔ*, brought the manuscript of the *Huán-shān lù* to Kyoto for cutting, and how he himself (Dokuan) met Jū-zān at Settsu-ga 攝津賀 *lǚ-guǎn* 旅館 and, in conversation, recognised the Chinese Sōtō monk as a Fú-jiàn acquaintance from forty years earlier — "his hair had gone white and his face wrinkled" — and was asked to write the preface. Jū-zān's closing postscript, dated three days earlier, is a substantial statement of Shòu-chāng-lineage orthodoxy against contemporary Japanese Chán rivals, positioning Dào-pèi's Gǔ-shān as the continuation of authentic *zǔ-yìn* 祖印.

The content, organised across four juan, includes a long retrospective self-memoir ***Lǚ-bó huàn-jī*** 旅泊幻蹟 (in *juan* 4) — the source for DILA's biographical dates — tracing Dào-pèi's life from his 1615 birth at Jiàn-ān Dōng-báiyún 東白雲 through his long Gǔ-shān residency. Further material ranges through *shàng-táng*, *xiǎo-cān*, stupa-management records, registry-book prescriptions ("*cháng-zhù zhī yǒu jiāo-tóu bù* 常住之有交頭簿" — the established custom of a hand-over register for monastic property succession, which Dào-pèi reformed in Kāng-xī *Yǐ-chǒu* 乙丑 / 1685 by launching a new register), and memorial prose. The sections dated to Shùn-zhì *Dīng-yǒu* 丁酉 (1657) preserve Dào-pèi's first-person record of his master [[元賢]]'s death and the immediate administrative transition at Gǔ-shān.

Date bracket: 1657 (first-person testimony to Yuán-xián's death and the succession) through the 1693 Japanese reprinting. The Chinese *nèi-shū* 內書 is dated 1688 but covers material accumulated across Dào-pèi's thirty-year abbacy from 1660.

## Translations and research

*No book-length Western-language study located.* Dokuan Genkō's opening preface is of independent interest for Japanese Zen historiography (Dokuan was a leading voice in late-17th-century Japanese disputes over Ōbaku and Chinese Chán authenticity). The Jū-zān postscript gives rare documentation of Fújiàn-Kyūshū Sōtō transmission networks in the late 17th century.

## Other points of interest

The *Lǚ-bó huàn-jī* 旅泊幻蹟 in *juan* 4 — Dào-pèi's first-person "dream-travel-vestiges" memoir — is one of the most detailed surviving late-Míng / early-Qīng Chán-abbatial autobiographies and the principal source for DILA's biographical record. The Dokuan / Jū-zān Japanese-publication frame documents the specific Fú-jiàn → Kyūshū Sōtō transmission corridor of the 1660s–1690s, parallel to and competing with the simultaneous Ōbaku-ji 黃檗寺 transmission from the same Fú-jiàn region.
