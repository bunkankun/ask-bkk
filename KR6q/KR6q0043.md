# HuángMíng míngsēng jí lüè 皇明名僧輯略

compiled by [[袾宏]] (Zhūhóng, sobriquet Yúnqī 雲棲, 1535–1615), late Míng

## About the work

A one-juan late-Míng compendium of brief biographies of eminent Míng-dynasty monks, compiled by Yúnqī Zhūhóng — one of the Four Great Masters of the Late Míng (*wǎn Míng sì dàshī* 晚明四大師, alongside Hānshān Déqīng, Zǐbǎi Zhēnkě, and Ǒuyì Zhìxù). Title — "Brief Compilation of Famous Monks of the Imperial Míng" — frames it as an internal Míng-Buddhist memorial work.

### Tiyao


### Abstract

Zhūhóng selects and briefly summarises the biographies of approximately two dozen eminent Míng monks across multiple traditions (Chán, Pure Land, Vinaya, doctrinal). The compilation reflects Zhūhóng's broadly inclusive late-Míng Buddhist programme, in which Chán practice, Pure Land devotion, and scholastic learning were treated as complementary rather than alternative options. The brevity and selectivity make this a teaching work rather than a comprehensive biographical record.

## Translations and research

No complete English translation. Substantial Western scholarship on Zhūhóng: Chün-fang Yü, *The Renewal of Buddhism in China: Chu-hung and the Late Ming Synthesis* (Columbia UP, 1981) — the standard English study of Zhūhóng and his Buddhist programme; this work is treated within Yü's broader study.

## Other points of interest

The compilation testifies to the late-Míng Buddhist self-consciousness of being a coherent — if doctrinally various — tradition, and to Zhūhóng's role in articulating that consciousness for monastic and lay readers alike.
