# Gāo-fēng Yuán-miào chánshī yǔlù 高峰原妙禪師語錄

Two-juan Yuán-dynasty *yǔlù* of **Gāo-fēng Yuán-miào** [[原妙]] 高峰原妙 (Jiāxī 2.3.23 / 16 April 1238 – Yuán-zhēn 1.12.1 / 13 January 1296), Yángqí-branch Línjì master, dharma-heir of [[祖欽]] Xuě-yán Zǔ-qīn and teacher of Zhōng-fēng Míng-běn 中峰明本. *Xuzangjing* X70 n1400. Compiled "by the participating disciples" (*cān-xué mén-rén biān*) — names not given in the extant text — and re-cut at Líng-yǐn by Hóng-lǐ 弘禮 in the Wàn-lì period. Preface by [[袾宏|Yún-qī Zhū-hóng]] 雲棲袾宏 (1535–1615) dated Wàn-lì 27.foshi-huanxi-ri (Wàn-lì 27 = 1599, monks' "Buddha-rejoicing day", the close of summer retreat) — Zhū-hóng's preface records that he had treasured Yuán-miào's text for thirty years, wished the *dà-zàng* had absorbed it earlier, and encountered criticism from Pure Land devotees in his *Lián-shè* community ("How is it that having turned all streams to the direction of the Pure Land you are now so eagerly praising this collection?"). Juan 1 contains *fǎ-yǔ* from the hermitage at Shuāng-jì-ān 雙髻庵 in Húzhōu (from Xiánchún *jiǎ-xū* spring / 1274), *shēng-zuò* records, *shì-zhòng* 示眾 talks. Juan 2 contains *xiǎo-cān*, *jì-sòng*, and the *xíng-zhuàng* with Yuán-miào's precise birth and death dates.

### Abstract

Per DILA A-entry for 原妙 and the *xíng-zhuàng*: Yuán-miào (*hào* Gāo-fēng 高峰 "High-Peak"; posthumous title Pǔ-míng Guǎng-jì chánshī 普明廣濟禪師) was a native of Wú-jiāng 吳江 (Sū-zhōu). He received dharma-transmission from Xuě-yán Zǔ-qīn after an extended crisis-and-breakthrough training pattern that became celebrated in later Línjì pedagogy (the famous "great-doubt" instruction to focus on *wàn-fǎ guī yī, yī guī hé chù* "the ten-thousand dharmas return to one — to where does the one return?"). His most famous feature is the self-imposed seclusion at Shī-guān 死關 ("Death-Barrier") on Xī-fēng 西峰 of Tiān-mù-shān from 1279, where he refused all callers and lived for the last eighteen years of his life; his teaching was conducted through letters and occasional hermitage *shì-zhòng*. His dharma-heir Zhōng-fēng Míng-běn 中峰明本 (1263–1323) transmitted the line into the Yuán state-approved Chán of the early fourteenth century. The *yǔlù* was deeply prized in the Míng Chán revival (particularly by Zhū-hóng) and in later Japanese Rinzai; the Líng-yǐn re-cutting sponsored by Hóng-lǐ was the basis of its wide Qīng-era circulation.

The companion text *Gāo-fēng Yuán-miào chánshī chán-yào* ([[KR6q0334]]) distils Yuán-miào's teachings into a single-juan "essentials" manual, explicitly modelled by its compiler Qiáo-zǔ 喬祖 on the "gāng 綱 : mù 目 :: lǐng 領 : lǚ 縷" schema — "if the rope is thrown out but the net-cords are not gathered, the net will not spread; if the collar is lifted but the threads are not pulled, the robe will not shake clean." The two texts together form the extant standard corpus.

## Translations and research

Yuán-miào's *yǔlù* and *chán-yào* are staples of Japanese Rinzai and Korean Sŏn training. English-language scholarship: Jeffrey Broughton, *The Chan Whip Anthology: A Companion to Zen Practice* (Oxford University Press, 2015) — a translation of Yún-qī Zhū-hóng's *Chán-guān cè-jìn* 禪關策進 (compiled 1600), which cites Yuán-miào extensively. Morten Schlütter, *How Zen Became Zen* (Kuroda, 2008), discusses Yuán-miào in the context of the Yángqí-branch continuation of Dàhuì's *kàn-huà* program. Korean work on Taego Bou (whose master Shí-wū Qīng-gǒng was a dharma-brother of Zhōng-fēng in the Xuě-yán line) regularly references Gāo-fēng.
