# Dé-fēng chán-shī Bō-rě yǔ-lù 德風禪師般若語錄
*Recorded Sayings of Chán Master Dé-fēng of Bō-rě [-sì]*
by [[如惺]] (等編)

Six-juan *yǔ-lù* of **Dé-fēng** [[德風]] (b. **1622-10-08**, death unknown), Línjì-Yángqí dharma-heir of **Bǎi-chī Xíng-yuán** 百癡行元 (1611–1662), thus a co-disciple of **Lián-fēng Sù** [[素]] (cf. [[KR6q0530]]). Native of **Shàng-yáng / Chuān-shā** 上洋川沙 (Sōngjiāng / Shànghǎi area), lay surname **Gù** 顧. The text bears Dé-fēng's name only in the title position; the catalog persons-list registers only [[如惺]] as compiler. The author's name in the inner title is given as 德風禪師, with the editorial note "侍者如惺等編" — Rú-xīng was Dé-fēng's personal attendant.

**Abbacies.** Principally **Wǔ-hú Bō-rě-sì** 鵡湖般若寺 (in Wú-jiāng / Sū-zhōu region — *Wǔ-hú* 鵡湖 is the local name for an inlet of Tài-hú), entered **順治戊戌 12/12** = **1659-01-04** (the formal *rù-yuàn*), with the abbacy invitation accepted on **丁酉正月初三** = **1657-02-15**; later **Hù-guó chán-yuàn** 護國禪院. The *yǔ-lù*'s subtitle 般若語錄 reflects Bō-rě-sì as the principal preaching site.

The preface is by lay disciple **Shī-fēng jū-shì Shěn Zōng-jìng** 獅峰居士沈宗敬 dated **康熙二十八年歲次己巳八月朔** = **1689-09-14**. *notBefore* = **1657** (acceptance of Wǔ-hú abbacy); *notAfter* = **1689** (preface). Printed as Jiā-xīng Canon J38 B411.

**Contents.** Juan 1: prefaces, *shàng-táng* records from his Bō-rě and Hù-guó tenures. Juan 2: *sòng-gǔ*, *Lè-dào gē* 樂道歌. Juan 3–4: *wèn-dá jī-yuán*, mountain-dwelling poems (*shān-jū jì*), boat-dwelling poems (*chuán-jū*), and a long sequence of *shì*-verses to disciples and lay friends. Juan 5–6: continuation, including the autobiographical *xíng-yóu* 行繇 — the principal source for his life — and a closing *jǐng-shì* poem on the brevity of worldly things, signed by lay donors who funded the printing.

## Other points of interest

The first-person *xíng-yóu* in juan 6 is unusual for a *yǔ-lù* — most parallel collections have a third-person *xíng-zhuàng* by a disciple. Dé-fēng's account narrates his sequential testing under five separate masters before final transmission from Bǎi-chī, giving an unusually detailed picture of mid-17th-century mendicant *cān-fǎng* circuits in the lower Yangtze (Lóng-huá → Lù-zhí → Nán-xiáng → Qī-zhēn → Yún-jiān).
