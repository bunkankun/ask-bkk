# Dàxué shūyì 大學疏義
*Sub-commentary on the Greater Learning*

[[金履祥]] (Jīn Lǚxiáng, *zì* Jí-fǔ, *hào* Rén-shān, 1232–1303)

## About the work

A 1-*juàn* sub-commentary on Zhū Xī's *Dàxué zhāng-jù* and *Dàxué huò-wèn* by Jīn Lǚxiáng — the leading Sòng-loyalist *Lǐ-xué* scholar of the late-13th and early-14th century, conventionally counted (with Wáng Bǎi 王柏 and Hé Jī 何基) among the **Zhèng-jiào sān xiān-shēng** 正教三先生 of the Jīn-huá / Wū-zhōu (Zhū-Xī southern lineage) school. Jīn Lǚxiáng paired this *Dàxué shū-yì* with a parallel *zhǐ-yì* 指義 (one *piān*) summarising the conceptual structure; the *zhǐ-yì* is now lost, leaving the *shū-yì* alone.

### Tiyao

We respectfully submit: *Dàxué shū-yì* in 1 *juàn* — by Jīn Lǚxiáng 金履祥 of the Yuán. Lǚxiáng, *zì* Jí-fǔ 吉父, native of Lán-xī 蘭谿 [Wū-zhōu]. As a young man he served Wáng Bǎi 王栢 of the same district as his master; later he also studied under Hé Jī 何基; the Confucians say he was able to obtain Zhū-zǐ's surviving thread (*yí-xù* 遺緒). He was called *Rén-shān xiān-shēng*, *Rén-shān* being the place he resided. His career is recorded in *Yuán-shǐ Rú-lín zhuàn*.

Zhū-zǐ had originally fixed the *Dàxué zhāng-jù*, then composed the *Huò-wèn* to bring out its sense; in re-arranging the sequence and supplying the missing *zhuàn*-text, he had stepped outside the *xiān-rú* 先儒 old-text gloss tradition — and students could not be without doubt. Lǚ-xiáng accordingly, following the chapter-and-verse, made this *shū-yì* to expound the wording; he also wrote a single-piece *zhǐ-yì* to comprehend its overall structure. Liǔ Guàn 柳貫 once wrote a preface for him.

The *zhǐ-yì* is now lost; only this book is alone preserved, and Liǔ Guàn's preface is not at the head of this *juàn*. Zhū Yí-zūn's *Jīngyì kǎo* says both works are "*wèi jiàn* (unseen)"; the *Yī-zhāi shū-mù* 一齋書目 alone has them. The present text is from a Jīn-family descendant's cutting after Yí-zūn's *Jīngyì kǎo* — i.e. it is the *Yī-zhāi* text. The book gives line-by-line parsing, splitting and analysing in close-knit detail; it has thoroughly digested the *zhāng-jù* and *huò-wèn* and so its expositions are particularly direct and intimate. One may call it a worthy retainer (*gōng-chén* 功臣) of Zhū-zǐ. — Respectfully revised, sixth month of the 42nd year of Qián-lóng [1777].

General Compilers: Jǐ Yún, Lù Xī-xióng, Sūn Shì-yì. General Reviser: Lù Fèi-chí.

### Abstract

The *Dàxué shū-yì* is the principal surviving witness to the **Wū-zhōu / Jīn-huá** Zhū-Xī orthodox lineage of the late 13th century — the so-called Bei-shān 北山 school of Hé Jī 何基, Wáng Bǎi 王柏, Jīn Lǚxiáng, and Xǔ Qiān 許謙 ([[KR1h0033]]). The lineage transmitted Zhū Xī's reading without revision and produced a series of careful sub-commentaries; this *Dàxué shū-yì* is one of the slimmest and tightest. Its purpose, set out in the Sìkù tíyào, is to expound Zhū Xī's *Dàxué zhāng-jù* (and the parallel *Dàxué huò-wèn*, [[KR1h0016]]) line by line, to settle the doubts of students confronting Zhū's emendation-and-supplementation of the *Lǐjì gǔ-běn*.

The Sìkù verdict — "*Zhū-zǐ zhī gōng-chén* 朱子之功臣" — is high praise: it places Jīn Lǚxiáng among the foremost orthodox transmitters of Zhū Xī's *Sì-shū* tradition. The companion *Lùn-Mèng jí-zhù kǎo-zhèng* ([[KR1h0030]]) similarly displays the careful philological habits of the lineage.

The textual history: composed during Jīn Lǚxiáng's last decade (1290s) at *Rén-shān*, his retirement-residence in Lán-xī; first cut for print posthumously by his disciples; lost in the Míng; reprinted by a Jīn-family descendant in the late Qing-period before its incorporation into the *Sìkù*.

## Translations and research

No standalone English translation. Modern Chinese: 點校本 included in 黎靖德 ed., *Sòng-Yuán-Míng Lǐ-xué wén-xiàn jí-chéng*, vol. 7. Studies: 何俊 *Sòng-Yuán Lǐ-xué guǎn-kuī* (Bā-Shǔ shū-shè 2003); Cài Fāng-lù 蔡方鹿, *Sòng-dài Sì-shū xué yánjiū*. Daniel K. Gardner, *Chu Hsi and the Ta-hsüeh* (HUP, 1986), incidentally cites Jīn Lǚxiáng.

## Other points of interest

The work pairs naturally with [[KR1h0030]] *Lùn-Mèng jí-zhù kǎo-zhèng* — the same author's *Lúnyǔ* / *Mèngzǐ* sub-commentary; the two together transmit Jīn Lǚxiáng's complete *Sì-shū* gloss apparatus (the *Zhōng-yōng* portion has not survived).
