# KR2l 職官類 — Histories: Official Posts

## Scope and scholarly tradition
KR2l 職官類 (*Zhíguān lèi*, "Official Posts") is the twelfth sub-class of the Sìkù *Histories* division (KR2, 史部). It collects two distinct but adjacent textual traditions that the Sìkù editors treated together: (a) institutional monographs and gazetteers of particular central-government bureaus — most prominently the Hànlín Academy 翰林院, the Imperial Library *Mìshū shěng* 秘書省 / *Mìshū jiàn* 秘書監, the Ministry of Rites 禮部, the Court of Imperial Sacrifices 太常寺, and the Directorate of Education 國子監 — and (b) the *guānzhēn* 官箴 corpus, manuals of magistracy addressed to county-level officials. The first cluster is the source-base for the institutional history of Chinese imperial central government; the second is the principal premodern documentation of local-level administrative practice. Wilkinson treats the Sìkù *zhíguān* category and its successors at §19.5 (local government), §19.6 (ranks and prestige titles), and §19.7 (official posts), and the *guānzhēn* genre specifically at §19.5.3.2 — where he identifies *Guānzhēnshū jíchéng* 官箴書集成 (Huángshān, 1997, 101 titles) and Pierre-Étienne Will's *Handbooks and anthologies for officials in imperial China* (Brill, 2020, 1,165 titles) as the standard modern references.

Chronologically, the division begins with the Táng 唐 (the *Táng liùdiǎn* of 738 and Lǐ Zhào's *Hànlín zhì* of 819 — Wilkinson notes the *Táng liùdiǎn* at §25.4.1 as "the earliest extant collection of administrative law" to survive intact) and runs through to mid-Qīng (the *Cílín diǎngù* of 1747 and the *Qīndìng lìdài zhíguān biǎo* of 1780, the closing imperial syntheses). The bulk of the corpus is Sòng and Míng, reflecting the maturation of the central bureaucracy and the rise of professionalized magistracy after the Yuánfēng reorganization of 1078–1080 and the Southern Sòng intensification of local administration. The transmission picture is also distinctive: a number of the major Sòng monographs (*Líntái gùshì*, *Nán Sòng guǎngé lù*, *Zhōuxiàn tígāng*) were lost in standalone form by the late Míng and recovered by the Sìkù editors from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* 永樂大典 — one of the more visible cases of *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn*-mediated restoration in the entire Sìkù enterprise.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The Hànlín cluster** — successive monographs on the Hànlín Academy, the imperial drafting bureau, in a continuous Tang–Sòng–Míng–Qīng lineage:
- *Hànlín zhì* 翰林志 [[KR2l0002]] by Lǐ Zhào (819) — the founding monograph and the model for all that followed.
- *Hànyuàn qúnshū* 翰苑羣書 [[KR2l0004]] edited by Hóng Zūn (1173) — a Southern Sòng collectanea that preserves nine Tang and Northern Sòng treatises on the Academy that survive nowhere else in standalone form.
- *Yùtáng zájì* 玉堂雜記 [[KR2l0006]] by Zhōu Bìdà (1182) — the principal Southern Sòng documentary source, in a strongly first-person texture.
- *Hànlín jì* 翰林記 [[KR2l0009]] by Huáng Zuǒ (c. 1530s) — the comprehensive Míng-period monograph, with prosopographical name-lists by reign.
- *Cílín diǎngù* 詞林典故 [[KR2l0013]] by Èěrtài and Zhāng Tíngyù (1747) — the imperial Qīng counterpart, framed by Qiánlóng's own preface as the closure of a millennium-long genre.

**The Imperial Library cluster** — institutional self-portraits of the *Mìshū shěng* / *Mìshū jiàn*, in chronological succession:
- *Líntái gùshì* 麟臺故事 [[KR2l0003]] by Chéng Jù (1131) — composed at the moment of the Library's reconstitution in the south after the Jìn invasion.
- *Nán Sòng guǎngé lù* 南宋館閣錄 [[KR2l0005]] by Chén Kuí (1177, with anonymous continuation to 1269) — the principal Southern Sòng record.
- *Mìshū jiàn zhì* 祕書監志 [[KR2l0008]] by Wáng Shìdiǎn and Shāng Qǐwēng (c. 1340–1352) — the Yuán-period monograph, distinctive for incorporating the Astronomical Directorate and preserving Yuán chancery *zhíyì* 直譯 (literal-translation) prose.

**Comparative tables and prosopography**:
- *Táng liùdiǎn* 唐六典 [[KR2l0001]] (738) — the foundational comprehensive treatment of Tang central government, modelled on the *Zhōulǐ*.
- *Sòng zǎifǔ biānnián lù* 宋宰輔編年錄 [[KR2l0007]] by Xú Zìmíng (1215) — chronological prosopography of every Sòng Grand Councillor and Vice Grand Councillor, embedding full *zhì* 制 texts of investiture and dismissal.
- *Qīndìng lìdài zhíguān biǎo* 欽定歷代職官表 [[KR2l0015]] (1780) — the great Qiánlóng comparative tabulation of every imperial office from antiquity to the Qīng, organized retrospectively from the Qīng establishment. Wilkinson treats it at §19.6 as the foundation of all modern reference work on Chinese historical nomenclature.

**Specialized bureau and frontier monographs**:
- *Lǐbù zhìgǎo* 禮部志稿 [[KR2l0010]] by Lín Yáoyú and Yú Rǔjí (1620) — the 100-*juǎn* gazetteer of the late-Míng Ministry of Rites, supplementing the *Míng huìdiǎn* on court ritual and ceremonial.
- *Tàicháng xùkǎo* 太常續考 [[KR2l0011]] (anon., c. 1643) — anonymous compilation of the Chóngzhēn-era Court of Imperial Sacrifices, with an office-name list extending to 1643.
- *Tǔguān dǐbù* 土官底簿 [[KR2l0012]] (anon., c. 1500–1521) — the principal early-Míng roster of 363 *tǔsī* (native chieftain) lineages in Yúnnán, Guǎngxī, Sìchuān, Guìzhōu, Húguǎng, and Guǎngdōng; a major source for the institutional history of southwest China.
- *Qīndìng Guózǐjiàn zhì* 欽定國子監志 [[KR2l0014]] by Liáng Guózhì (1778–1785) — the Qiánlóng-era gazetteer of the Directorate of Education and Confucius Temple.

**The *guānzhēn* cluster** — manuals of magistracy, the principal premodern documentation of local-level administrative practice:
- *Zuòyì zìzhēn* 作邑自箴 [[KR2l0019]] by Lǐ Yuánbì (1117) — the earliest dated *guānzhēn*, anchoring the genre in Northern Sòng oral magistracy tradition.
- *Guānzhēn* 官箴 [[KR2l0017]] by Lǚ Běnzhōng (early twelfth century) — the source of the formula *qīng-shèn-qín* 清慎勤 ("pure, prudent, diligent"), later canonized by the Kāngxī court.
- *Zhōuxiàn tígāng* 州縣提綱 [[KR2l0016]] (anon., late twelfth or thirteenth century) — Southern Sòng manual whose false attribution to Chén Xiāng the Sìkù editors definitively debunked.
- *Bǎiguān zhēn* 百官箴 [[KR2l0018]] by Xǔ Yuèqīng (1260–1285) — rhymed admonitions for every Sòng central-government office, in the style of Yáng Xióng's lost *Guān zhēn*; a Sòng-loyalist 遺民 composition.
- *Zhòulián xùlùn* 晝簾緒論 [[KR2l0020]] by Hú Tàichū (1235) — a tightly organized fifteen-chapter manual that became influential on Yuán-Míng successors.
- *Sān shì zhōnggào* 三事忠告 [[KR2l0021]] by Zhāng Yǎnghào (c. 1308–1329) — the most important Yuán contribution to the genre, addressed in turn to the county magistrate, the Censor, and the Grand Councillor.
- *Yùdìng rénchén jǐngxīn lù* 御定人臣儆心錄 [[KR2l0022]] by the Shùnzhì emperor and Wáng Yǒngjí (1655) — early Qīng imperial admonition, framed as a coded indictment of the recently executed grandees Tántài, Shí Hàn, and Chén Míngxià.

## Important persons

- [[張九齡]] (Zhāng Jiǔlíng, 678–740) — Tang chief councillor and editor-in-chief of *Táng liùdiǎn* [[KR2l0001]]; the Sìkù editors' restoration of his authorship over Lǐ Línfǔ's is a model of evidential textual criticism.
- [[李林甫]] (Lǐ Línfǔ, d. 752) — Tang chief councillor; conventionally credited with the commentary to *Táng liùdiǎn*, though produced by his editorial team.
- [[李肇]] (Lǐ Zhào, fl. 819) — Tang Hànlín Academician, author of the *Hànlín zhì* and the founder of Hànlín-treatise literature.
- [[程俱]] (Chéng Jù, 1078–1144) — Junior Director of the Imperial Library and author of the first Sòng institutional monograph on that office, [[KR2l0003]].
- [[洪遵]] (Hóng Zūn, 1120–1174) — Hànlín Academician Recipient of Edicts, editor of *Hànyuàn qúnshū* [[KR2l0004]]; younger brother of the polymath Hóng Mài 洪邁.
- [[陳騤]] (Chén Kuí, 1128–1203) — Imperial Librarian and compiler of *Nán Sòng guǎngé lù* [[KR2l0005]]; also a major rhetorician (*Wén zé* 文則).
- [[周必大]] (Zhōu Bìdà, 1126–1206) — three-time Grand Councillor and author of *Yùtáng zájì* [[KR2l0006]]; one of the dominant cultural figures of the Chúnxī court.
- [[徐自明]] (Xú Zìmíng, fl. early thirteenth century) — *Tài cháng bóshì* of Yǒngjiā, compiler of the *Sòng zǎifǔ biānnián lù* [[KR2l0007]], a major prosopographical source for Sòng political history.
- [[王士點]] (Wáng Shìdiǎn, d. 1359) and [[商企翁]] (Shāng Qǐwēng, fl. mid-fourteenth century) — co-compilers of the Yuán *Mìshū jiàn zhì* [[KR2l0008]].
- [[黃佐]] (Huáng Zuǒ, 1490–1566) — mid-Míng Hànlín Compiler, author of the *Hànlín jì* [[KR2l0009]]; also editor of the *Guǎngdōng tōngzhì*.
- [[林堯俞]] (Lín Yáoyú) and [[俞汝楫]] (Yú Rǔjí) — Minister of Rites and *shēngyuán* compiler, respectively, of the late-Míng *Lǐbù zhìgǎo* [[KR2l0010]].
- [[鄂爾泰]] (Èěrtài, 1677–1745) and [[張廷玉]] (Zhāng Tíngyù, 1672–1755) — senior Manchu and Han Grand Secretaries of the Yōngzhèng/Qiánlóng court, joint compilers of the imperial *Cílín diǎngù* [[KR2l0013]].
- [[梁國治]] (Liáng Guózhì, 1723–1786) — Qiánlóng-era chief editor of the *Qīndìng Guózǐjiàn zhì* [[KR2l0014]].
- [[永瑢]] (Yǒng Róng, 1744–1790) and [[紀昀]] (Jǐ Yún, 1724–1805) — nominal and actual chief editors of the *Qīndìng lìdài zhíguān biǎo* [[KR2l0015]]; Jǐ Yún also chief editor of the Sìkù bureau itself.
- [[李元弼]] (Lǐ Yuánbì, fl. 1117) — Northern Sòng magistrate, author of the earliest dated *guānzhēn* [[KR2l0019]].
- [[呂本中]] (Lǚ Běnzhōng, 1084–1145) — Northern–Southern Sòng poet and moralist, originator of the *qīng-shèn-qín* formula in *Guānzhēn* [[KR2l0017]].
- [[許月卿]] (Xǔ Yuèqīng, 1216–1285) — Sòng loyalist (*yímín*) and author of *Bǎiguān zhēn* [[KR2l0018]]; changed his *zì* from 太空 to 宋士 after the dynasty's fall.
- [[胡太初]] (Hú Tàichū, fl. 1235–1253) — Prefect of Chùzhōu and author of the tightly organized fifteen-chapter *Zhòulián xùlùn* [[KR2l0020]].
- [[張養浩]] (Zhāng Yǎnghào, 1270–1329) — Yuán statesman, *sǎnqǔ* poet, and author of *Sān shì zhōnggào* [[KR2l0021]] — the only *guānzhēn* to span all three levels of imperial administration.
- [[世祖]] (Fúlín 福臨, the Shùnzhì emperor, 1638–1661) and [[王永吉]] (Wáng Yǒngjí, 1599–1659) — imperial author and Grand-Secretary compiler of *Yùdìng rénchén jǐngxīn lù* [[KR2l0022]].

## Topics

- **The Tang–Qīng Hànlín lineage.** The five Hànlín monographs ([[KR2l0002]], [[KR2l0004]], [[KR2l0006]], [[KR2l0009]], [[KR2l0013]]) form one of the most continuous single-institution documentary chains in the Chinese historical literature, from the office's mature consolidation under Xuánzōng (early eighth century) to its closing Qīng codification (mid-eighteenth century). Each work is consciously written as a continuation of its predecessors; Hóng Zūn's *Hànyuàn qúnshū* is the principal vehicle by which the Tang and Northern Sòng strata are preserved at all.

- **The Imperial Library as a literary self-subject.** The three central Library monographs ([[KR2l0003]], [[KR2l0005]], [[KR2l0008]]) document successive stages of the *Mìshū shěng* / *Mìshū jiàn*: Northern-Sòng-into-Southern-Sòng reconstitution after 1127, mature Southern Sòng practice down to 1269, and the Yuán reorganization under Mongol rule (with the Astronomical Directorate folded in). Together they are the only continuous source on imperial book-collection, copying, and editorial practice across the Sòng–Yuán divide.

- **Administrative restoration and the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn*.** Several major Sòng monographs in this division (*Líntái gùshì*, *Nán Sòng guǎngé lù*, *Zhōuxiàn tígāng*) had been lost in standalone form by the late Míng and were recovered from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* by the Sìkù editors — a clearly visible Sìkù-era restoration enterprise.

- **The *guānzhēn* genre and the ethics of office.** The seven-text *guānzhēn* cluster ([[KR2l0016]]–[[KR2l0022]]) traces the evolution of a distinctive Chinese genre: practical-moral handbooks addressed to magistrates and other officials, fusing personal-cultivation maxims (*qīng-shèn-qín*) with practical-administrative advice on lawsuits, taxation, clerk-management, and corvée. The genre takes shape in the Northern Sòng (Lǐ Yuánbì, 1117), develops the Confucian moralizing register in the Sòng-Yuán transition (Lǚ Běnzhōng, Xǔ Yuèqīng, Hú Tàichū, Zhāng Yǎnghào), and is appropriated as an instrument of imperial discipline in the early Qīng (Shùnzhì's *Jǐngxīn lù*).

- **Comparative cross-dynastic nomenclature.** The trajectory from Sūn Féngjí's Sòng *Zhíguān fēn jì* through to Qiánlóng's *Qīndìng lìdài zhíguān biǎo* (1780, [[KR2l0015]]) exemplifies the late-imperial impulse to map every office onto every predecessor system, with the Qīng establishment as the organizing baseline — a retrospective and Qīng-centric reading of all imperial institutional history.

- **Prosopography as institutional history.** Three works in this division ([[KR2l0007]], [[KR2l0008]], [[KR2l0010]]) anchor their institutional accounts in name-lists of office-holders down to surprisingly low levels (junior directors, clerical sub-officials), making the division an unexpectedly rich biographical resource. Xú Zìmíng's chronicle, in particular, sometimes corrects the *Sòngshǐ* "Year-table of Grand Councillors."

- **Frontier administration and *gǎitǔ guīliú*.** The anomalous *Tǔguān dǐbù* ([[KR2l0012]]) is the principal pre-conquest source for the Míng *tǔsī* system in southwestern China and is therefore the single most important documentary basis for modern ethnohistorical study of Yi, Bai, Zhuang, Miao, and Yao political organization in the Yuán-Míng-Qīng transition.

## Timeline

- **738** — *Táng liùdiǎn* 唐六典 [[KR2l0001]] — the foundational compendium of Tang central government, modelled on the *Zhōulǐ*; commentary completed by Lǐ Línfǔ shortly after Zhāng Jiǔlíng's draft.
- **819** — *Hànlín zhì* 翰林志 [[KR2l0002]] — Lǐ Zhào's foundational monograph on the Tang Hànlín Academy.
- **1117** — *Zuòyì zìzhēn* 作邑自箴 [[KR2l0019]] — Lǐ Yuánbì's earliest dated *guānzhēn*; the Northern Sòng founding statement of the genre.
- **c. 1100–1145** — *Guānzhēn* 官箴 [[KR2l0017]] — Lǚ Běnzhōng's pithy admonition manual, source of the *qīng-shèn-qín* formula.
- **1131** — *Líntái gùshì* 麟臺故事 [[KR2l0003]] — Chéng Jù's Sòng Imperial Library monograph, composed at the moment of post-Jìn reconstitution.
- **1173** — *Hànyuàn qúnshū* 翰苑羣書 [[KR2l0004]] — Hóng Zūn's Sòng collectanea of Tang and Northern Sòng Hànlín literature.
- **1177–1269** — *Nán Sòng guǎngé lù* 南宋館閣錄 [[KR2l0005]] — Chén Kuí's Southern Sòng Imperial Library record with anonymous continuation.
- **c. 1170–1300** — *Zhōuxiàn tígāng* 州縣提綱 [[KR2l0016]] — anonymous late-Southern-Sòng *guānzhēn*; the false Chén Xiāng attribution debunked by the Sìkù editors.
- **1182** — *Yùtáng zájì* 玉堂雜記 [[KR2l0006]] — Zhōu Bìdà's first-person memoir of his Hànlín service under Xiàozōng.
- **1215** — *Sòng zǎifǔ biānnián lù* 宋宰輔編年錄 [[KR2l0007]] — Xú Zìmíng's chronological prosopography of every Sòng Grand Councillor, completed in 1215, first printed 1257.
- **1235** — *Zhòulián xùlùn* 晝簾緒論 [[KR2l0020]] — Hú Tàichū's fifteen-chapter manual, written for his father-in-law's magistracy.
- **c. 1260–1285** — *Bǎiguān zhēn* 百官箴 [[KR2l0018]] — Xǔ Yuèqīng's rhymed admonitions for every Sòng office; a Sòng-loyalist composition.
- **c. 1308–1329** — *Sān shì zhōnggào* 三事忠告 [[KR2l0021]] — Zhāng Yǎnghào's three-part Yuán *guānzhēn* covering magistrate, Censor, and Grand Councillor.
- **c. 1340–1352** — *Mìshū jiàn zhì* 祕書監志 [[KR2l0008]] — Wáng Shìdiǎn and Shāng Qǐwēng's Yuán monograph on the Imperial Library Directorate.
- **c. 1500–1521** — *Tǔguān dǐbù* 土官底簿 [[KR2l0012]] — anonymous early-Míng roster of 363 *tǔsī* lineages.
- **c. 1530–1566** — *Hànlín jì* 翰林記 [[KR2l0009]] — Huáng Zuǒ's comprehensive Míng Hànlín monograph, completed during the Jiājìng reign.
- **1620** — *Lǐbù zhìgǎo* 禮部志稿 [[KR2l0010]] — Lín Yáoyú and Yú Rǔjí's 100-*juǎn* gazetteer of the late-Míng Ministry of Rites.
- **c. 1628–1644** — *Tàicháng xùkǎo* 太常續考 [[KR2l0011]] — anonymous Chóngzhēn-era continuation; office-name list to 1643, the eve of the dynasty's collapse.
