# Kǒng-shì zǔ-tíng guǎng-jì 孔氏祖庭廣記
*Expanded Record of the Ancestral Hall of the Kǒng Lineage*
by [[孔元措]] (撰)

## About the work
A twelve-*juàn* expanded gazetteer-cum-genealogy of the Kǒng descent line and the Qūfù 曲阜 ancestral cult-site, compiled by Kǒng Yuán-cuò 孔元措 (51st-generation descendant of Confucius and *Yǎnshèng-gōng* 衍聖公 under the Jīn) and completed in *Zhèng-dà* 4 (*dīng-hài* 丁亥, 1227), tenth month, the day of the full moon. The work belongs to the same family as the *Dōngjiā zájì* 東家雜記 ([[KR2g0002]]) compiled by Kǒng Chuán 孔傳 of the southern Kǒng branch a century earlier; in his preface, Yuán-cuò explicitly invokes the two earlier private *Kǒng-shì zǔ-tíng* compilations (one by his 46th-generation ancestor Kǒng Zhī-hóng 知洪州軍州事, and a fragmentary continuation by his 49th-generation kinsman, the *Xiángfú-xiàn-bù* 主祥符縣簿) and notes that both books circulated together (兩書並行) until the wars of the Sòng-Jīn collapse devastated the Qūfù compound and burned much of the Kǒng family's textual archive. Drawing on these two earlier works and on materials gathered in consultation with the *Tài-cháng* 太常 ritualists, he expanded the body of material by 302 episodes, prefixed images of the Sage's portrait and of the temple buildings and hand-planted cypresses, and gave the consolidated work the title *Kǒng-shì zǔ-tíng guǎng-jì*. The text also reproduces the texts (not merely the names) of inscribed steles in the temple compound and the Kǒng family cemetery, both Hàn and post-Hàn, "lest length of time efface them and they cannot be recovered." This is the principal Northern (Jīn) counterpart to Kǒng Chuán's southern *Dōngjiā zájì*.

### Prefaces
The work is prefixed by Kǒng Yuán-cuò's own *yǐn* 引 dated *Zhèng-dà* 4 (1227), tenth month, full-moon day, signed "Zī-zhèng dàfū, hereditarily enfeoffed *Yǎnshèng-gōng*, Director of the Academy of Worthies (*Jí-xián yuàn* 集賢院) and concurrent *Tài-cháng chéng* 太常丞, fifty-first-generation descendant Yuán-cuò." It records an ancillary editorial team: the *Tài-xué* student Mǎ Tiān-zhāng 馬天章 of Jiè-shān 介山 prepared the portraits; ritual officers Wáng Róu-lì 王柔立 of Xùn-yí 浚儀 and Jīn Táng 靳唐 of Zhōng-shān 中山 collated; the *Tài-cháng-sì Tài-qí-rì zhào* 太常寺太祈日照 Zhāng Lù 張簵, the *Jí-xián yuàn sī-yì* and concurrent *Tài-cháng-sì fèng-lǐ-láng* 奉禮郎 *quán bó-shì* 權博士 Mǎ Suì-liáng 馬遂良 of Gǔyàn 古燕, and the *Huì-mín sī lìng* 惠民司令 with concurrent *Tài-cháng bó-shì* 太常博士 Mǐ Zhāng 米章 of Fù-píng 富平, also collated. The preface remarks that the dates given in the work are reckoned from the conventional Confucius birthyear (Zhōu Líng-wáng 21 = *gēng-xū* 庚戌, i.e. 551 BCE) to the year of compilation, "1,778 years in all." There is no Sì-kù-tíyào: the work is preserved here in the SBCK reproduction of the Jīn-period printed edition, which had been deposited in the Kōnoike-bunko 鴻池文庫 collection.

### Abstract
The *Kǒng-shì zǔ-tíng guǎng-jì* is the principal Jīn-dynasty gazetteer-and-genealogy of the Kǒng family at Qūfù and one of the most important records of the Northern *Yǎnshèng-gōng* line that remained in the ancestral seat after the Sòng court withdrew south in 1127. The author Kǒng Yuán-cuò (CBDB record uncertain; standard prosopographical sources give him as 51st-generation descendant of Confucius, holder of the Jīn enfeoffment of *Yǎnshèng-gōng*, with floruit centred on the *Zhēn-yòu* and *Zhèng-dà* reigns of the late Jīn) was the senior representative of the Northern Kǒng branch under Jīn rule. The work supersedes both of the earlier private *Kǒng-shì zǔ-tíng* compilations (now lost) and supplements the southern *Dōngjiā zájì*. Its 302 added episodes, drawing on the *Tài-cháng* ritualists' working files for materials on imperial honours and ritual privileges, reflect the institutional perspective of the Jīn imperial ritual office; the prefatory portraits and architectural illustrations make the work a key visual source as well. The 1227 date precedes by only six years the Mongol sack of Kāifēng (1233) — Kǒng Yuán-cuò was subsequently brought to the Mongol court and, under Ögödei, helped re-establish the *Yǎnshèng-gōng* enfeoffment under early Yuán rule. The compilation is therefore among the last major textual products of late-Jīn imperial scholarship.

## Translations and research
*No substantial Western-language translation located.* For the Northern *Yǎnshèng-gōng* line and the place of the *Guǎng-jì* in the Kǒng-family literature, see Thomas A. Wilson, *On Sacred Grounds: Culture, Society, Politics, and the Formation of the Cult of Confucius* (HUP, 2003), and Christopher Agnew, "Migrants and Mutineers: The Rebellion of Kǒng Yǒu-dé and Seventeenth-Century Northeast Asia" (2009). Modern critical editions include the Zhōnghuá shū-jú reprint and the *Sì-bù cóng-kān* 四部叢刊 photographic reproduction of the Jīn imprint, which is what is preserved in the present source file.

## Other points of interest
The text incorporates the texts of pre-Sòng steles standing in the *Kǒng-lín* 孔林 cemetery and the temple compound — many of which were subsequently lost to fire, war, or weather — and is therefore a primary witness for early epigraphic materials at Qūfù. The 1233 Mongol assault and the subsequent transfer of Kǒng Yuán-cuò to the Mongol court make this 1227 imprint a kind of *terminus* for in-house Jīn Kǒng-family scholarship.
