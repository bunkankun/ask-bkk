# Zhù jiě Shānghán lùn 註解傷寒論
*Annotated Commentary on the Treatise on Cold Damage Disorders*
by [[張機]] (Zhāng Jī, 東漢) — original; [[王叔和]] (Wáng Shūhé, 晉) — Western-Jìn editor; [[成無己]] (Chéng Wúyǐ, b. ca. 1063, *fl.* into 1156, 金) — commentator

## About the work

The *first sustained commentary* on the *Shānghán lùn*, in ten juan, by the Jīn-dynasty physician Chéng Wúyǐ 成無己. Chéng's annotation, completed in 1144 and printed shortly thereafter, glosses the canonical text section-by-section with the standard Jīn-period Neoclassical apparatus of *Sùwèn*-derived doctrine. This commentary became the standard reading of the *Shānghán lùn* through the Yuán and the Míng, and was supplanted only by Fāng Yǒuzhí's 方有執 *Tiáobiàn* ([[KR3ef015]]) and Yú Chāng's 喻昌 *Shànglùn piān* ([[KR3ef026]]) in the late 16th century.

### Abstract

The composition date is fixed at 1144, the date of Chéng Wúyǐ's preface (in some witnesses signed 紹興十四年甲子). Yán Qìzhī 嚴器之, Chéng's friend and disciple, provided the principal supporting preface, dated Shàoxīng 紹興 16 (1146), which is the source of most biographical detail on Chéng. Zhāng Xiàozhōng 張孝忠's 拔 of Kāixǐ 開禧 1 (1205) provides the second early biographical witness. Chéng's commentary explicitly grounds the *Shānghán lùn*'s clinical prescriptions in the *Sùwèn* 素問 and *Língshū* 靈樞 doctrinal apparatus — a synthesis that was the great Jīn-period contribution to clinical theory. The work pairs with Chéng's *Shānghán mínglǐ lùn* 傷寒明理論 ([[KR3ef040]]) and the *Lùn fāng* 論方, which together form a unified commentary-and-treatise system on the *Shānghán* canon.

The *Sòngběn* base text of the *Shānghán lùn* underlies Chéng's annotation, transmitted through the校正醫書局 collation of 1065. Chéng's recension was reprinted multiple times under the Yuán and Míng, and is preserved both in the *Zhōngjǐng quánshū* 仲景全書 of Zhào Kāiměi (1599) and in the *Sìkù quánshū*.

## Other points of interest

The JīnYuán "Four Masters" — [[劉完素]], [[張從正]], [[李杲]], [[朱震亨]] — all argued against Chéng Wúyǐ's strict *Shānghán*-doctrine line, advocating various Sūnwèn-derived alternative therapeutic strategies. Nonetheless Chéng's annotation remained the standard pedagogical commentary into the early Qīng.
