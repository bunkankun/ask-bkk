# Jīnguì yào lüè guǎng zhù 金匱要略廣註
*Extensive Annotation of the Jīnguì yào lüè*
by [[李彣]] (Lǐ Wén, *zì* Zhèn-shēng 振聲, *hào* Xī-yuán 西垣, *fl.* late seventeenth century, 清)

## About the work

An eight-juan early-Kāngxī commentary on the *Jīnguì yào lüè* by **Lǐ Wén** 李彣 (字 振聲 Zhèn-shēng, *hào* 西垣 Xī-yuán), a student of Pān Dēnglín 潘鄧林. The work — dated Kāngxī 21 = 1682 in the editorial preface preserved in modern reprints — is one of the earliest sustained Qīng *Jīnguì* commentaries, slightly later than [[徐彬]] Xú Bīn's *Jīnguì yào lüè lùn zhù* and Chéng Lín 程林's *Jīnguì yào lüè zhí jiě*. The "*guǎng zhù*" 廣註 (extensive annotation) title refers to Lǐ's distinctively broad citation practice.

### Abstract

Composition date 1682 (Kāngxī 21). The modern editorial preface preserved in the Kanripo source — written by **Dù Xiǎolíng** 杜曉玲 for the modern critical edition — outlines Lǐ's distinctive citational range. The annotation covers not only the standard Sòng-Yuán-Míng medical authorities (徐之才, 朱肱, 許叔微, 張子和, 朱丹溪, 王履, 張兼善, 趙養葵, 樓全善, 王三陽, 喻嘉言, with the gynaecological 郭稽中 and 武之望, and pharmacological 陳藏器 and 李時珍) but also non-medical sources: the *Yì jīng* 周易, *Shàng shū* 尚書, and the military classic *Hǔ qián jīng* 虎鈐經. This citational breadth is characteristic of late-seventeenth-century Confucian-medical synthesis and a distinguishing feature of Lǐ's *guǎng zhù*.

The work survives only in the Kāngxī 21 (1682) blockprint and in several manuscript copies, making it relatively rare even in the Qīng. Tamba no Motokata's bibliographical survey in [[KR3ef085]] records Lǐ as a major *Jīnguì* commentator known to him through citations in the *Yī zōng jīn jiàn* 醫宗金鑑 but personally unconsulted ("惜未得見之"). The modern Chinese critical edition (Rénmín wèishēng, with collation by Dù Xiǎolíng combining the 1682 blockprint and the surviving manuscripts) makes the text broadly available for the first time.

## Other points of interest

Lǐ's citation of the *Hǔ qián jīng* 虎鈐經 — a Sòng-period military-strategy classic — in a *Jīnguì* commentary is one of the more striking instances of late-seventeenth-century cross-genre intellectual practice: Lǐ uses the *Hǔ qián jīng*'s logic of opportunity-and-timing as an analogue for the clinical logic of timing-and-prescription in *Jīnguì* therapeutics. The work is also notable for its careful attribution of Lǐ's teacher Pān Dēnglín 潘鄧林 — preserving one of the few documented teacher-disciple lines in Kāngxī-era *Jīnguì* clinical study.
