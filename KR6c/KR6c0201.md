# Bānruò xīnjīng mìjiàn kāimén jué 般若心經祕鍵開門訣
*Gate-Opening Resolutions on the Secret Key to the Heart Sūtra*
by [[濟暹]] (撰)

## About the work

A three-juan **late-Heian Esoteric sub-commentary** on Kūkai's **Bānruò xīnjīng mìjiàn** ([[KR6c0199]], the *Hannya shingyō hiken*). Composed by the prolific Daigo-ji 醍醐寺 Ono-ryū 小野流 Shingon scholar **Saisen** 濟暹 ([[濟暹]], 1025–1115). The text is precisely dated by its colophon: *yúshí Chéngdé yuánnián shíèryuè xuángè bǐ yě* 于時承徳元年十二月玄各比也 — **the twelfth month of Chōtoku 1 = late 1097 / early 1098 CE**, when Saisen was 72 (or 73 by traditional reckoning). Considerably more extensive than Kakuban's later *Hiken ryakuchū* ([[KR6c0200]]), the *Kaimon-ketsu* is a **three-fold-structured systematic commentary** that takes the *Hiken* not only as the object of annotation but as the **anchor of a comprehensive doctrinal exposition of the Esoteric reception of the *Heart Sūtra***. Preserved in Taishō Vol. 57, No. 2204.

### Abstract

**Author's preface and self-positioning.** Saisen opens with a striking metaphor: "a single color, a single fragrance — none does not fully fuse the principle of the universal Dharma-realm; the hundred grasses and hundred grains — all serve as medicine to expel the manifold diseases." If even the most ordinary things contain the Dharma-realm, how much more does an abbreviated sūtra contain inexhaustible doctrine, and how much more does the Esoteric dhāraṇī contain unimpeded therapeutic power? The work is positioned as the labor of a *mò-yè zhī pín-dào* ("a poor monk of the leaf-tip generation"): "I have barely tasted the residual flavor of the *amṛta*, yet I have not removed the famine of dharma-thirst; I have somewhat smelled the lingering fragrance of *ghee*, but my heart's stupid sickness is not yet cured." On the basis of this self-deprecating opening Saisen presents his sub-commentary as **gate-opening resolutions** — *kāi-mén jué* 開門訣 — that "open up the treasure-store" of Kūkai's compressed *Hiken*.

**Tripartite structure.** The work is organized under three principal divisions:

1. *Dàwén dìyī: míng jiàoqǐ yīnyuán fēn* 大文第一明教起因縁分 — "the great-text first part: clarifying the causes-and-conditions of the teaching's arising"
2. *Dàwén dìèr: shì tímù fēn* 大文第二釋題目分 — "the great-text second part: glossing the title"
3. *Dàwén dìsān: shì běnwén fēn* 大文第三釋本文分 — "the great-text third part: glossing the body of the text"

The first division is itself sub-divided into the **causes-and-conditions of the parent sūtra** (the *Heart Sūtra* itself) and the **causes-and-conditions of the present sub-commentary**.

**Parent-sūtra cause-and-conditions.** Following the standard Esoteric formula, Saisen explains that all beings transmigrate in the two species of *saṃsāric* existence (*fēn-duàn* 分段 and *biàn-yì* 變易) because of the two graspings of self and dharmas; "therefore the unsurpassed World-Honored One, the great compassionate father of the world, arises with unconditioned great compassion and the unimpeded power of expedient means, and to rescue such beings ... preaches this profound *Prajñāpāramitā* sūtra of the formless-ultimate-essence." The argument is then anchored in *Lotus Sūtra* passages on the buddhas' single great purpose (*kāi shì wù rù fó-zhī-jiàn* 開示悟入佛知見) — Saisen integrates the *Heart Sūtra*'s Esoteric reception with the *Lotus*-school *one-vehicle* doctrine, an interesting synthetic move.

**Sub-commentary cause-and-conditions.** Saisen divides into *zǒng* (general) and *bié* (particular) causes. The general cause is the *Vajra-summit*'s exhortation that all Esoteric bodhisattvas should each expound the *three-mystery* gate (三密門) to extend the Esoteric teaching across the worlds. The particular cause is twofold: (a) Saisen's **personal vow** (依自己本願) — quoting Kūkai's own *Sānjiào lùn* 三教論 on the latter's vow to spread the Esoteric Dharma — and (b) Saisen's **acceptance of his master's command** (依本師教命), which sub-divides into the cosmic dimension (Kūkai-as-Buddha's-envoy) and the institutional dimension (Saisen's specific Daigo-ji lineage-transmission obligation).

The remaining two divisions of the work — the **title-exposition** and the **body-commentary** — proceed phrase-by-phrase through Kūkai's text in the *kaimon-ketsu* form, with each lemma followed by an extended doctrinal exposition that integrates *Mahāvairocana-sūtra*, *Vajra-summit*, *Prajñāpāramitā-śāstra*, and the broader Esoteric corpus. The result is the **most extensive late-Heian sub-commentary on the *Hiken***, occupying three juan to Kakuban's one-juan *Ryakuchū*.

**Authorship and dating** are secure: the colophon is unambiguous and the author signature is Saisen's standard form. The *Chōtoku* era spans 1097–1099 CE; with the twelfth-month notation, the work is dated to late 1097 or early 1098 (depending on intercalary calculation).

## Other points of interest

The work is a key witness to the **late-11th-century institutionalization** of the Kūkai commentarial tradition in the **Ono-ryū** lineage of Daigo-ji. Its precise dating to Chōtoku 1 (1097/98) gives it a unique status as one of the **best-dated Esoteric scholastic compositions of late-Heian Japan**: most contemporary Shingon writings have to be dated only by author-lifespan. Saisen's work is one of the **principal pre-Kakuban** sub-commentaries on the *Hiken* and predates Kakuban's *Ryakuchū* ([[KR6c0200]]) by approximately a generation. The two sub-commentaries together — Saisen's monumental three-juan *Kaimon-ketsu* and Kakuban's compact one-juan *Ryakuchū* — represent the two principal scholastic models of late-Heian / early-medieval Esoteric sub-commentary: the *kaimon-ketsu* form (systematic re-structuring and expansion) versus the *lüèzhù* form (close phrase-by-phrase annotation).

The synthetic move of integrating *Lotus*-school *one-vehicle* doctrine into the Esoteric *Heart-Sūtra* exegesis is characteristic of the late-Heian period's growing interest in **Tendai-Shingon syntheses**: Saisen here points toward the doctrinal-comparative interests that would flower in the next generation with Kakuban himself.
