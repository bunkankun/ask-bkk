# Jīngāng jīng bēi 金剛經鎞
*The Diamond Sūtra Comb*
by [[廣伸]] (述)

## About the work

A two-juan **late-Wàn-lì *Vajracchedikā* commentary** by **Guǎng-shēn** 廣伸 (sobriquet **Sì-kōng** 似空, also 雲棲似空), principal disciple of **Yún-qī Zhū-hóng** 雲棲袾宏 ([[袾宏]], 1535–1615). The title-character *bēi* 鎞 ("comb" / "ear-pin"; an instrument for parting hair, by extension for unraveling something tangled) figures the work's task: to comb through the dense Indian śāstra apparatus on the *Vajracchedikā* and present a unified late-Míng reading. Preserved in *Xù-zàng-jīng* X25 no. 475. *notBefore* set conservatively to **1610** (Yún-qī's late period during which the project was undertaken under his direction); *notAfter* = **1618**, the date of the prefatory matter (萬曆戊午秋日 by 虎林洪瞻祖廣揞 of Hú-lín = Hángzhōu). Catalog dynasty 明.

### Abstract

The opening preface (No. 475-A) by **Hóng Zhān-zǔ** 洪瞻祖 (sobriquet **Guǎng-ǎn** 廣揞, of Hú-lín) places the work in a programmatic Buddhist-Confucian parallel: as Yáo could not do without Shùn, and Wén-wáng without Confucius, so **Buddha Śākyamuni cannot do without Maitreya** (迦文不能無彌勒) — meaning that the *Vajracchedikā* must be read through Maitreya's verses (the Vasubandhu / Asaṅga *vyākhyā* tradition) rather than through later Chinese essayists' free interpretations. The preface positions Yún-qī as the **lóng-xiàng** 龍象 (dragon-elephant) of the dharma-gate who alone among late-Míng masters had grasped Maitreya's secret transmission, and identifies Guǎng-shēn as the disciple whom Yún-qī commissioned to write up the commentary on the basis of his master's own oral notes (the *Mō-xiàng yī-jì* 摸象一記, "the elephant-feeling note" — Yún-qī's modest title for his draft). Guǎng-shēn's task was to *gōu xuán zuǎn yào, què yī shān fán, jí zhū jiā zhī dà chéng, bǔ Mō-xiàng zhī wèi-bèi* 鈎玄纂要，搉一刪繁，集諸家之大成，補摸象之未備 ("hook the profound, gather the essential, weigh and prune the profuse, compendize the schools, supplement what the *Mō-xiàng* had not completed").

The body of the commentary, attributed *Gǔ-háng Yún-qī dì-zǐ Guǎng-shēn shù* 古杭雲棲弟子廣伸述 ("expounded by Guǎng-shēn, disciple of Yún-qī of ancient Hángzhōu"), divides the work into a *tí-mù* (title-gloss), *yì-rén* (translator notice), and *běn-wén* (sūtra-text-with-commentary) — the standard three-part scholastic frame. The opening title-gloss runs to several pages and incorporates the **eighteen abodes** schema of Asaṅga and the **twenty-seven doubts** schema of Vasubandhu, harmonized through Yún-qī's signature Pure-Land-Chán synthesis.

## Translations and research

- For Yún-qī Zhū-hóng's broader project see Chün-fang Yü, *The Renewal of Buddhism in China: Chu-hung and the Late Ming Synthesis* (Columbia UP, 1981).
- The disciple-commentary genre — by which Yún-qī's pedagogy reached print after his death through the labors of Guǎng-shēn and others — is treated in Jiang Wu, *Enlightenment in Dispute* (Oxford UP, 2008).

## Other points of interest

The text is the principal extant work of Guǎng-shēn and one of the few late-Míng *Vajracchedikā* commentaries to take the **Maitreya-Vasubandhu śāstra tradition** as its working frame, in deliberate contrast to the more Chán-centric readings of Zǐ-bǎi ([[KR6c0061]]) and Hān-shān ([[KR6c0062]]). The 1618 publication date — three years after Yún-qī's death — situates it among the immediate posthumous Yún-qī editorial productions of the Sì-fēn-lǜ Bǐ-qiū-jiè biographical apparatus.
