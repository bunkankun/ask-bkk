# Liú-shì jú-pǔ 劉氏菊譜
*Mr Liú's Treatise on Chrysanthemums*
by [[劉蒙]] (Liú Méng, 撰)

## About the work

The earliest surviving Chinese monograph on the chrysanthemum (*jú* 菊, *Chrysanthemum × morifolium* / *Chrysanthemum indicum*). A one-*juàn* late-Northern-Sòng work by Liú Méng 劉蒙 of Péng-chéng (Xú-zhōu), Huīzōng-period person; dated by internal evidence to Chóng-níng *jiǎ-shēn* (1104), composed after Liú's visit to his fellow-connoisseur Liú Yuán-sūn 劉元孫 at Lóng-mén near Luòyáng. The work is the prototype of the Chinese *jú-pǔ* (chrysanthemum-treatise) genre, predating Shǐ Zhèngzhì's [[KR3i0032]] and Fàn Chéng-dà's [[KR3i0034]] by several decades.

The work's structure: (1) *Pǔ-xù* 譜叙 (Preface); (2) *Shuō* 說 (General Discussion); (3) *Shuō-yí* 說疑 (Discussion of Doubtful Cases); (4) *Dìng-pǐn* 定品 (Establishing Varieties); (5) thirty-five varietal entries describing form, colour, and origin-locality; (6) three *zá-jì* (miscellaneous notes) concluding entries. The work ranks the *Lóng-nǎo jú* 龍腦菊 (Borneol-Camphor chrysanthemum) as the supreme variety.

### Tiyao

The combined *tíyào* covering this work, [[KR3i0032]], [[KR3i0033]], and [[KR3i0034]] is in this file. The portion concerning *Liú-shì jú-pǔ* (translated): *We submit that the* Liú-shì jú-pǔ *is by Liú Méng of the Sòng. Méng, a man of Péng-chéng, his career is not detailed. The preface records that in Chóng-níng* jiǎ-shēn *(1104) he travelled to Lóng-mén and visited Liú Yuán-sūn's residence, where the two together examined and discussed [chrysanthemum varieties] and made this treatise. So he was a Huī-zōng-period person; therefore Wáng Détóng's* Zhū-shǐ *already cites his theories. Jiāo Hóng's* Guó-shǐ jīng-jí zhì *lists him after Fàn Chéng-dà — this is mistaken. The book first has the preface, next the general discussion, next the discussion of doubtful cases, next the establishment of varieties, next the list of chrysanthemum-names in thirty-five entries, each narrating the variety's form-and-colour and ranking it; the* Lóng-nǎo *is given as first. It ends with three miscellaneous-records. The various chrysanthemum names-and-ranks discussed in the book each detail their place of origin: from Biàn-liáng (Kāifēng) up to Xī-jīng (Luòyáng), Chén-zhōu, Dèng-zhōu, Yōng-zhōu, Xiāng-zhōu, Huá-zhōu, Fū-zhōu, Yáng-zhái — generally all are products of the Central Plain region, gathered in the Luòyáng gardens. This is different from later Shǐ Zhèngzhì and Fàn Chéng-dà who specialised in Wú (Sū-zhōu) plantings. However, varieties like* Jīn-qián *(Gold-Coin) and* Tú-mí *(Rose) are also recorded in the Shǐ and Fàn treatises — perhaps these originated in the north and were later transmitted to the south.*

### Abstract

The work is the foundational document of the Chinese chrysanthemum-treatise tradition and a major source for late-Northern-Sòng floricultural geography. Liú Méng's thirty-five varieties cover the Luòyáng region and adjacent Central Plain prefectures (Kāifēng, Chén-zhōu, Dèng-zhōu, Yōng-zhōu, Xiāng-zhōu, Huá-zhōu, Fū-zhōu, Yáng-zhái) — the heart of late-Northern-Sòng horticulture. The work's ranking of *Lóng-nǎo* (Borneol-Camphor) chrysanthemum as supreme reflects the Northern-Sòng preference for fragrant white varieties; the later Southern-Sòng treatises preferred yellow and pink varieties.

The composition date is set precisely by the Chóng-níng *jiǎ-shēn* (1104) preface; the surviving work probably went through revisions before being finalized c. 1110. The work was preserved in the Sòng *Bǎichuān xuéhǎi* and reaches the *Sìkù* through that channel.

The principal contribution of the work to subsequent Chinese floricultural history is the systematic varietal-grading framework — color (white, yellow, purple, red), petal-form (single, double, ball-form, anemone-form), fragrance, growth-habit — that became the standard for all subsequent chrysanthemum-monographs. The work also documents the late-Northern-Sòng *jú* trade: the named gardens around Luòyáng (the principal Sòng chrysanthemum centre); the seasonal sale at the Kāi-fēng *huā-shì* (flower-market); the gift-and-tribute economy of chrysanthemum-pots at the late Huīzōng court.

## Other points of interest

The Liú Méng / Liú Yuán-sūn collaborative observation method documented in the preface — two scholars meeting at a known garden-site and jointly cataloguing varietal differences — became the standard model for *pǔ-lù* fieldwork in subsequent Sòng treatises. The work is the proximate ancestor of the entire Chinese chrysanthemum-monograph tradition.
