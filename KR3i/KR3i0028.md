# Táng-shuāng pǔ 糖霜譜
*Treatise on Sugar-Frost (Crystallized Sugar)*
by [[王灼]] (Wáng Zhuó, 撰)

## About the work

The world's first technical monograph on crystallized sugar (the *táng-shuāng* 糖霜 — literally "sugar-frost" — pure sucrose crystallized from sugarcane juice, the great Chinese contribution to global confectionery technology). A one-*juàn* mid-Southern-Sòng work in seven *piān* by Wáng Zhuó 王灼 *zì* Huì-shū 晦叔, *hào* Yí-táng 頤堂, of Suì-níng 遂寧 in Shǔ-zhōu — the Sòng-period centre of sugar-crystallization technology. Composed during Wáng's *Shào-xīng*-era *mù-liáo* career, c. 1154–1175.

The seven chapters: (1) *Yuán-wěi dì-yī* 原委第一 (Origin) — narrates the legendary founding of the *táng-shuāng* industry by the Buddhist monk Zōu Hé-shàng 鄒和尚 in Tang Dà-lì (766+); (2) (untitled) on the historical development of cane-sugar from antiquity (the earliest mention being in the *Chǔ-cí Zhāo-hún* 楚辭招魂 — "cugar-juice" — and the *Sān-guó-zhì*); (3) (untitled) on sugarcane cultivation; (4) (untitled) on the apparatus of sugar-making; (5) (untitled) on the crystallization technique; (6) (untitled) on the unpredictability of crystallization (with reference to Xuān-hé tribute episodes); (7) (untitled) on the medicinal nature, flavour, and culinary uses of sugar-frost.

### Tiyao

We submit that the *Táng-shuāng pǔ* is in one *juàn* by Wáng Zhuó of the Sòng. Zhuó, *zì* Huì-shū, *hào* Yí-táng, a man of Suì-níng, was a *mù-guān* (private-secretary) during *Shào-xīng*. This compilation divides into seven *piān*; only the opening *piān* is titled "Origin: First" — narrating that in Tang Dà-lì, Zōu Hé-shàng first created sugar-frost. From the second *piān* onward there are no titles.

To examine the text: the second *piān* speaks of making sugar from cane from beginning to end — saying that *zhè-jiāng* 蔗漿 (sugarcane-juice) first appears in the *Chǔ-cí*, and *zhè-yí* 蔗餳 (cane-syrup) first in the *Sān-guó-zhì*. The third *piān* speaks of growing the cane; the fourth speaks of sugar-making apparatus; the fifth speaks of the method of crystallization; the sixth speaks of how the sugar-frost either crystallizes or fails to, seemingly subject to fate-and-fortune — extending to Xuān-hé-period imperial-tribute matters. The seventh speaks of the nature-and-flavour and the various edible-preparations of sugar-frost.

Now in the Sòng, the producing-localities of sugar-frost were five: Fú-táng, Sì-míng, Pān-yú, Guǎng-hàn, Suì-níng — and Suì-níng was the foremost. Wáng Zhuó was born in Suì-níng, hence he wrote this *pǔ*. The literary-quotations he gathers begin with Sū Shì and Huáng Tíngjiān. Investigating: the ancients used *táng* 糖 for what we call *zāo* 糟 (lees); the *Jìn-shū Hé Zēng zhuàn* says "*the crab approaching the糟 stirs all the more*" — that is the usage. The *Shuō-wén* has the character *yí* 飴 but not *táng* 糖. Xú Xuàn's new-supplement-characters do include *táng*, but he glosses it as *yí*, not mentioning cane-manufacture. Xuàn was a Five-Dynasties / early-Sòng person and still did not know of cane-sugar — so Wáng's tracing-of-anecdotes starting from Yuán-yòu is not negligence-and-omission.

However, Wáng calls the *zǐ-sè* (purple) sugar-frost top quality and the white-coloured inferior; whereas today's preference is to value white and despise purple. Wáng says sugar-frost requires one-and-a-half years to crystallize, with the crystallization spontaneous; today the process is very easy and not the same. Such are also points where ancient and modern differ; we should not press later judgement upon earlier persons. Submitted Qiánlóng 46 month 12 (1781).

### Abstract

The *Táng-shuāng pǔ* is one of the most important works in the entire Chinese technical literature: it is *the* foundational document of crystallized-sugar technology, the technology that — diffused via the Yuán-period overland trade to the Islamic world, and onward to medieval Europe — would become the foundation of the modern global sugar industry.

The work documents the principal facts of Sòng-period Chinese sugar-history:

1. **Origin myth.** *Táng-shuāng* (crystallized sugar) was invented by the Buddhist monk Zōu Hé-shàng 鄒和尚 (the "Zōu Monk") who came from outside to settle on Mt Sān 傘山 in Suì-níng during the Tang Dà-lì period (766–779). The monk's pet, an old cow, accidentally inscribed Buddhist-style sand-mandalas on the surface of stored cane-juice; the cane-juice, exposed in the sun, gradually crystallized along the patterns — Zōu Hé-shàng systematized the procedure into a craft. This origin-myth dates the Chinese invention of crystallized sugar to the mid-Tang, plausibly correct: pre-Tang Chinese sugar-products were liquid syrups (*zhè-jiāng*) or thick *yí* (*zhè-yí*).

2. **Geography of production.** Sòng-period crystallized-sugar production was centered in five regions: Fú-táng 福唐 (Fú-jiàn coast), Sì-míng 四明 (Níng-bō), Pān-yú 番禺 (Guǎngzhōu), Guǎng-hàn 廣漢 (modern Sì-chuān), and Suì-níng 遂寧 (Sì-chuān). Suì-níng was the foremost producer — a striking fact, as it places the apex of pre-modern Chinese sugar-crystallization in an inland Sì-chuān location rather than the more obvious coastal southern regions. Wáng Zhuó, native of Suì-níng, was therefore the ideal author.

3. **Process.** The work describes in detail the *káng-bǎn* 槓板 (lever-press) for extracting cane-juice; the *róng-róng* 熔濃 (concentration-by-evaporation) stage; and the long *jié-shuāng* 結霜 (crystallization) stage requiring one-and-a-half years of static crystallization — a period strikingly long, perhaps explained as the time required for spontaneous nucleation in the absence of seed-crystals.

4. **Aesthetic of color.** The Sòng-period ideal of crystallized sugar was *zǐ-sè* (purple) — apparently from molasses-retention in the crystal mass. The modern preference for pure-white sugar (achieved by repeated re-crystallization and bleaching) was already noticed by the *Sìkù* editors and reflects an aesthetic change associated with the rise of refined sugar in the Yuán-Míng-Qing period.

5. **Sugar at the Sòng court.** The Xuān-hé-period tribute references in *piān* 6 establish that crystallized sugar was an imperial-tribute product to the Northern Sòng court.

The composition date is uncertain. Wáng Zhuó's known career spans the *Shào-xīng* period (1131–1162); the work is conservatively dated c. 1154–1175. His other principal work, the *Bì-jī màn-zhì* 碧鷄漫志 ([[KR4j0078]]), is the founding theoretical work of *cí* (song-lyric) studies and is roughly contemporary.

## Other points of interest

The *Táng-shuāng pǔ* is also a major document for Sòng-period Sì-chuān economic and cultural history — Suì-níng's sugar-industry was one of the principal sources of pre-modern Sì-chuān commercial wealth and remained so into the Qing. The remarkable fact that crystallized sugar-technology spread *outward* from China to the Islamic world (and ultimately Europe) — rather than the more common Indian-to-China-to-elsewhere pattern — places this work at a critical node of pre-modern technological transfer.
