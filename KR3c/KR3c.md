## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3c 法家類 (*Fǎ-jiā lèi*) sits within KR3 子部 (*Zǐ-bù*, Masters) and corresponds to the Sìkù quánshū *Zǐ-bù — Fǎ-jiā lèi*. The label is older and broader than its modern translation "Legalists" suggests: the Sìkù editors used 法家 as a bibliographic basket holding both the classical Warring States theorists of *fǎ* 法 (codified law), *shù* 術 (administrative technique), and *shì* 勢 (positional power), and the later judicial-casebook tradition concerned with the practical application of law in case adjudication. The division is therefore split internally into two distinct textual clusters connected only by the SKQS classifiers' decision to treat 法 as a single rubric.

The first cluster is the canonical pre-Qín statist literature — the *Guǎn-zǐ*, *Shāng-jūn shū*, *Hán Fēi-zǐ*, and the *Míng-jiā*-adjacent *Dèng Xī-zǐ* — composed or compiled in the Warring States and surviving in received recensions whose dating runs from the late Zhōu into the early Hàn. Wilkinson §58.6.3.2 catalogues these as the principal Warring States *zǐ*-texts on government, with the *Shāng-jūn shū* singled out as the only extant pre-Hàn work describing the economic and social reforms of a pre-imperial polity. The *Hàn shū yìwén zhì* assigns the *Guǎn-zǐ* to the Daoists and the *Shāng-jūn shū* and *Hán Fēi-zǐ* to the *Fǎ-jiā* proper, of which it lists only ten titles; the SKQS reclassification pulls the *Guǎn-zǐ* into 法家 on the strength of its *qīng-zhòng* 輕重 (light-and-heavy) economic chapters and statist orientation. The second cluster is the Wǔ-dài-to-Sòng *yí yù* 疑獄 (difficult cases) genre — the *Yí yù jí*, *Zhé yù guī jiàn*, and *Táng yīn bǐ shì*. Wilkinson treats these at §25.3.2 (Sòng penal-law sources) and §137 (Five Legal Casebooks); they are forensic-pedagogical anthologies of judicial decisions, not works of political philosophy, but in the SKQS taxonomy they share the rubric of 法.

## Important texts and text clusters

**Classical Legalist *zǐ*-texts.** Four canonical Warring-States compilations form the core of the division:

- *Guǎn-zǐ* 管子 [[KR3c0001]] — 24-juan, 76-篇 Warring States compilation attributed to the Spring-and-Autumn chancellor of Qí, foundational for Chinese economic, administrative, and political thought, with the *qīng-zhòng* chapters among the earliest pre-modern treatments of price-currency theory and state monopoly.
- *Hán Fēi-zǐ* 韓非子 [[KR3c0005]] — the synthesising classical Legalist text by Hán Fēi, integrating Shēn Bùhài's *shù*, Shèn Dào's *shì*, and Shāng Yāng's *fǎ* into a coherent statist programme; the principal theoretical source for the Legalist tradition.
- *Shāng-zǐ* 商子 [[KR3c0004]] (= *Shāng-jūn shū* 商君書) — the only extant pre-Hàn work documenting a concrete legal-administrative reform programme, namely Shāng Yāng's reforms in Qín; doctrinally the most radical of the cluster, with its agriculture-and-warfare (*nóng-zhàn* 農戰) doctrine and suppression of the *liù shī* 六蝨.
- *Dèng Xī-zǐ* 鄧析子 [[KR3c0003]] — a brief 2-篇 pseudepigraphic text preserving *Míng-jiā* (School of Names) technical terminology under a Spring-and-Autumn attribution, of late Warring States or early Hàn actual composition.

**Míng-period commentary.** A single commentarial work belongs to the classical cluster:

- *Guǎn-zǐ bǔ zhù* 管子補注 [[KR3c0002]] — Liú Jì's 1490 supplementary commentary to the *Guǎn-zǐ*, complementing Fáng Xuánlíng's Táng commentary; the standard Mid-Míng layer.

**The *yí yù* casebook tradition.** Three works form a tight Wǔ-dài-to-Sòng triad of judicial-decision anthologies:

- *Yí yù jí* 疑獄集 [[KR3c0006]] — Hé Níng and his son Hé Mèng's foundational casebook of difficult judicial cases, late Wǔ-dài / early Sòng; the seed text of the genre.
- *Zhé yù guī jiàn* 折獄龜鑑 [[KR3c0007]] — Zhèng Kè's mid-Southern-Sòng expansion of the *Yí yù jí*, the standard pre-Yuán reference work for case adjudication.
- *Táng yīn bǐ shì* 棠陰比事 [[KR3c0008]] — Guì Wànróng's 1196 casebook, distinguished by its mnemonic four-character couplet pairings of cases; the most popularly circulated of the three and the only Chinese pre-modern legal work with a major early Western Sinological translation (van Gulik 1956).

## Important persons

- [[管仲]] — Guǎn Zhòng (d. 645 BCE), Spring-and-Autumn chancellor of Qí; named author of the *Guǎn-zǐ*, though the received text is a Warring States compilation.
- [[房玄齡]] — Fáng Xuánlíng (578–648), the early-Táng statesman; provided the standard premodern commentary to the *Guǎn-zǐ* preserved in the SKQS edition.
- [[鄧析]] — Dèng Xī (ca. 545–501 BCE), legendary *Míng-jiā* thinker; nominal author of the *Dèng Xī-zǐ*.
- [[商鞅]] — Shāng Yāng (ca. 390–338 BCE), the architect of the Qín legal reforms; nominal author of the *Shāng-jūn shū* / *Shāng-zǐ*, though the received text is a posthumous compilation by his successors.
- [[韓非]] — Hán Fēi (ca. 280–233 BCE), Hán prince-scholar executed in Qín; the synthesising philosopher of the pre-Qín Legalist tradition and author of most of the *Hán Fēi-zǐ*.
- [[何犿]] — Hé Fán (Yuán), commentator on the *Hán Fēi-zǐ* in the SKQS edition.
- [[劉績]] — Liú Jì (b. 1468), Mid-Míng scholar; author of the *Guǎn-zǐ bǔ zhù*, the principal Míng-period commentary on the *Guǎn-zǐ*.
- [[和凝]] — Hé Níng (898–955), Wǔ-dài statesman who served under successive northern dynasties; founder of the *yí yù* casebook genre with the *Yí yù jí*.
- [[和㠓]] — Hé Mèng (956–1000), son of Hé Níng; supplemented his father's casebook.
- [[鄭克]] — Zhèng Kè (12th c.), Southern Sòng compiler whose *Zhé yù guī jiàn* became the standard Sòng-period casebook.
- [[桂萬榮]] — Guì Wànróng (Sòng), author of the *Táng yīn bǐ shì* in 1196.
- [[吳訥]] — Wú Nè (1372–1457), early Míng official who produced the cut-and-supplemented Míng recension of the *Táng yīn bǐ shì* transmitted in the SKQS.

## Topics

- **The triad *fǎ — shù — shì*.** The classical-Legalist conceptual core, drawn from Shāng Yāng, Shēn Bùhài, and Shèn Dào respectively, and integrated by Hán Fēi. *Fǎ* (law as the universal, impersonal mechanism of governance) is the doctrine most closely associated with the *Shāng-jūn shū* (KR3c0004); *shù* (the ruler's technique for managing officials) and *shì* (the positional advantage of the throne) are theorised most explicitly in the *Hán Fēi-zǐ* (KR3c0005).
- **State power vs. Rú-Mò moral persuasion.** All four classical texts engage, polemically, with Confucian and Mohist programmes of virtue-based government; the *Wǔ dù* 五蠹 of the *Hán Fēi-zǐ* and the *liù shī* 六蝨 doctrine of the *Shāng-jūn shū* are the canonical loci. The division is the principal home of the anti-Rú polemic in the Sìkù.
- **Agriculture-and-warfare (*nóng-zhàn* 農戰).** The *Shāng-jūn shū*'s programme of harnessing the peasantry as the dual fiscal and military base of the state; the most influential single doctrine in actual Warring-States and early-imperial statecraft.
- **Pseudepigraphy and recompilation.** Three of the four classical texts (*Guǎn-zǐ*, *Dèng Xī-zǐ*, *Shāng-jūn shū*) carry attributions to figures who could not have written the received works. Dating policy for the division consequently brackets the received recensions, not the lifedates of the attributed authors — a recurrent issue across the entries.
- **The *Míng-jiā* trace.** The *Dèng Xī-zǐ* (KR3c0003) is the only text in the division attached to the School of Names rather than to *Fǎ-jiā* proper; its classification here reflects the SKQS editors' decision to absorb the small, fragmentary *Míng-jiā* corpus into the adjoining bibliographic categories.
- **The judicial casebook as a genre.** The Wǔ-dài-to-Sòng *yí yù* tradition (KR3c0006 → KR3c0007 → KR3c0008) is forensic-pedagogical: cases are presented as exemplars in which the judicial determination required exceptional reasoning, with the *Táng yīn bǐ shì* refining the form to mnemonic four-character couplets for memorisation by serving officials. The genre is distinct from the legal codes proper (which are catalogued elsewhere) and from forensic medicine (Sòng Cí's *Xǐ yuān jí lù*).
- **Inner-court Legalism, outer-court Confucianism.** Wilkinson §66 captures the political-cultural commonplace that the bifurcation of practical (Legalist) and ideological (Confucian) modes of governance is structural to the Chinese imperial system from the Hàn onward. The KR3c texts are the canonical pre-imperial sources for the inner-court half of that bifurcation.

## Timeline

- ca. 400–240 BCE — *Guǎn-zǐ* 管子 [[KR3c0001]] — Warring-States compilation under the Guǎn Zhòng attribution; the broadest of the cluster's source-bases.
- ca. 350–200 BCE — *Shāng-zǐ* 商子 [[KR3c0004]] — post-Shāng-Yāng compilation by his school in Qín; the canonical *fǎ*-text.
- ca. 300–100 BCE — *Dèng Xī-zǐ* 鄧析子 [[KR3c0003]] — late-Warring-States or early-Hàn pseudepigraphon preserving *Míng-jiā* terminology.
- ca. 260–233 BCE — *Hán Fēi-zǐ* 韓非子 [[KR3c0005]] — Hán Fēi's lifetime composition; the synthesising work of pre-Qín Legalism.
- ca. 940–1000 — *Yí yù jí* 疑獄集 [[KR3c0006]] — Hé Níng's seed casebook, supplemented by Hé Mèng; foundation of the *yí yù* genre.
- ca. 1131–1180 — *Zhé yù guī jiàn* 折獄龜鑑 [[KR3c0007]] — Zhèng Kè's mid-Southern-Sòng expansion of the *Yí yù jí*.
- 1196 — *Táng yīn bǐ shì* 棠陰比事 [[KR3c0008]] — Guì Wànróng's couplet-form casebook, the popular Sòng-period summation of the genre.
- 1490 — *Guǎn-zǐ bǔ zhù* 管子補注 [[KR3c0002]] — Liú Jì's Mid-Míng supplementary commentary; the latest work in the division.
