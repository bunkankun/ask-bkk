## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3ei is the **gynecology and obstetrics** (*fù-kē* 婦科, also *nǚ-kē* 女科, with the obstetrical sub-field *chǎn-kē* 產科) subdivision of the Kanripo medicine class (KR3e 醫家類, *yī-jiā lèi*, "Masters → Medicine"). The catalog lists 78 texts, of which 76 are catalogued here (the slot KR3ei002, a duplicate-title placeholder for *Fù Qīngzhǔ nǚkē*, has no per-text note). All 76 entries come from the same early-modern Chinese-medical *cóngshū* (catalog source `hxwd`) — the *Hǎi-wài huí-guī Zhōng-yī shàn-běn gǔjí cóngshū* 海外回歸中醫善本古籍叢書 and its companion compilations — that complements the warm-disease and internal-medicine subdivisions [[KR3eg]] and [[KR3eh]] by gathering the literature on women's bodies, menstrual regulation, pregnancy, childbirth, post-partum care, and the production of offspring. The genres represented include foundational Táng and Sòng obstetrical compendia, late-Míng systematic gynecological *summae*, Qīng popular childbirth handbooks, family-school and monastic-lineage transmissions, *zhǒng-zǐ* / *guǎng-sì* (fertility / heir-producing) treatises that fuse Confucian ethics with medical pharmacology, Edo-period Japanese obstetric monographs, and Republican-era TCM-college textbooks. The doctrinal canon under which the corpus organises itself — *jīng-dài-tāi-chǎn* 經帶胎產 (menstrual cycle, vaginal discharge, ante-natal, post-partum), supplemented by *zhǒng-zǐ* 種子 (fertility), *zhēng-jiǎ* 癥瘕 (uterine masses), *rǔ-bìng* 乳病 (breast conditions), and *qián-yīn* 前陰 (genital conditions) — is a stable late-imperial typology that runs unchanged from the Sòng synthesis through the imperial Qīng standard texts to the modern PRC TCM gynecology curriculum.

The chronological span runs from the mid-Táng — [[昝殷]] Zàn Yīn's *Jīngxiào chǎnbǎo* ([[KR3ei048]], c. 847), the earliest extant Chinese specialised obstetrical monograph — through the Northern and Southern Sòng foundational obstetrical compilations ([[KR3ei056]], [[KR3ei068]], [[KR3ei076]], [[KR3ei077]]) and [[宋仲甫]] Sòng Zhòngfǔ's catechetical *Nǚkē bǎiwèn* ([[KR3ei031]]), through the great Míng synthesis (the Xuē Jǐ corpus of 1547–1548, the late-Wàn-lì systematic encyclopaedias of Wáng Kěntáng and Wǔ Zhīwàng, and the *wēn-bǔ* gynecology of Zhāng Jièbīn and Zhào Xiànkě), to the Qīng popular childbirth manual ([[KR3ei055]], 1715) and the imperial Qiánlóng standard textbook ([[KR3ei007]], 1742), and into the early-Republican TCM-college re-edition tradition (Zhāng Shānléi's 1915–1933 *Shěn-shì nǚkē jíyào jiānshū* [[KR3ei041]]; Chéng Ménxuě's posthumous case-records [[KR3ei047]]). Three Edo-period Japanese obstetrical works of the Kagawa school ([[KR3ei062]], [[KR3ei065]], [[KR3ei066]]) ground the division in East-Asian medical exchange; one Daoist female-alchemy anthology ([[KR3ei036]]) records the late-imperial taxonomic convention that grouped all female-body-focused texts together regardless of religious-medical distinction. Wilkinson §41.7.7 (Gynecology and Pediatrics: Furth 1987, Leung 2006, Lǐ Zhēndé 1996–1997, Yao 2005, Dikötter 1998) frames the secondary literature; the standard one-volume English overview is Charlotte Furth's *A Flourishing Yin: Gender in China's Medical History, 960–1665* (1999), and the principal Qīng-period monograph is Yi-Li Wu's *Reproducing Women: Medicine, Metaphor, and Childbirth in Late Imperial China* (2010). Hinrichs and Barnes, *Chinese Medicine and Healing* (2013) supplies the one-volume general reference.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The Táng and Sòng foundational obstetrical corpus.** Four works form the earliest stratum of specialised obstetrical literature in the division:
- *Jīngxiào chǎnbǎo* 經效產寶 ([[KR3ei048]], c. 847) of [[昝殷]] Zàn Yīn of Chéngdū — the earliest extant Chinese specialised obstetrical monograph, 378 prescriptions, transmitted via Bái Mǐnzhōng's mid-Táng presentation to the court and rediscovered in Sòng-period reconstruction. Foundational source for all subsequent *Chǎn-bǎo* lineage compilations.
- *Wèi-shēng jiā-bǎo chǎn-kē bèi-yào* 衛生家寶產科備要 ([[KR3ei056]], c. 1180–1190) of [[朱端章]] Zhū Duānzhāng — the single most important surviving witness to Southern-Sòng obstetrical literature, preserving in its *juǎn* 4 and 6 the otherwise-lost *Chǎn-yù bǎo-qìng jí* of [[李師聖]] Lǐ Shī-shèng and *Bèi-chǎn jì-yòng fāng* of [[虞流]] Yú Liú, recut by Lù Xīnyuán in 1887.
- *Chǎn-yù bǎo-qìng fāng* 產育寶慶方 ([[KR3ei076]], 1075–1108) — Lǐ Shī-shèng's 11th-century obstetrical formulary, preserved bibliographically here but substantively transmitted only through [[KR3ei056]].
- *Chǎn-bǎo zhū-fāng* 產寶諸方 ([[KR3ei077]], Sòng) and *Jì-shēng chǎn-bǎo* 濟生產寶 ([[KR3ei068]], 1180–1535) — further anonymous Sòng-Yuán *Chǎn-bǎo*-lineage compilations.

**The Sòng catechetical gynecology.** *Nǚkē bǎiwèn* 女科百問 ([[KR3ei031]], c. 1220–1230) of [[宋仲甫]] Sòng Zhòngfǔ (= Qí Zhòngfǔ 齊仲甫) — the principal Sòng gynecological work in the division, structured as one hundred numbered *wèn-dá* (question-and-answer) entries opening with the foundational doctrinal premise that *jīng-xuè* (essence-blood) is the source of male-female differentiation; the *bǎi-wèn* format would be replicated in numerous Qīng popular gynecologies (cf. [[KR3ei004]], [[KR3ei034]]).

**The Xuē-shì 薛氏 mid-Míng cluster.** Three works by or associated with [[薛己]] Xuē Jǐ (1487–1559), Imperial Medical Academy president under the Jiājìng emperor, who established the *Xuē-pài* 薛派 gynecology lineage as the principal mid-Míng synthesis:
- *Jiàozhù Fùrén liángfāng* 校註婦人良方 ([[KR3ei039]], 1547) — Xuē's critical annotation of [[陳自明]] Chén Zìmíng's 1237 Sòng *Fù-rén dà-quán liáng-fāng* (the principal Sòng gynecological synthesis, parent text catalogued at [[KR3e0038]]), the channel through which the Sòng *fù-kē* canon entered Míng-Qīng circulation.
- *Nǚkē cuōyào* 女科撮要 ([[KR3ei009]], 1548) — Xuē's own case-record gynecology in two *juǎn*, applying his Lǐ Dōng-yuán *Bǔ-zhōng yì-qì tāng* spleen-stomach plus *Liù-wèi / Bā-wèi dì-huáng wán* kidney-tonification synthesis to women's medicine.
- *Xuē-shì jì-yīn wàn-jīn shū* 薛氏濟陰萬金書 ([[KR3ei033]], 1569) of [[薛古愚]] Xuē Gǔyú, edited by [[鄭敷政]] Zhèng Fūzhèng — a Wàn-lì-era family-lineage compilation transmitting Xuē-school clinical doctrine into the late Míng.

**The late-Míng systematic gynecology trilogy.** Three monumental late-Míng *summae* together form the textual foundation of imperial-Qīng *fù-kē* practice and teaching:
- *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng·Nǚkē* 證治準繩·女科 ([[KR3ei030]], 1607) of [[王肯堂]] Wáng Kěntáng — the gynecology division of his great six-section *Liù-kē Zhèng-zhì zhǔn-shéng* (cf. [[KR3e0078]], [[KR3eh011]], [[KR3eh052]]); the doctrinally even-handed standard reference of the late Míng.
- *Jì-yīn gāng-mù* 濟陰綱目 ([[KR3ei006]], 1620) of [[武之望]] Wǔ Zhīwàng — a critical re-organisation and condensation of Wáng Kěntáng's *Nǚkē* into a tighter *gāng-mù* hierarchical structure, paired with Wǔ's male-medicine companion *Jǐ-yáng gāng-mù* [[KR3eh024]] as the late-Míng systematic articulation of *yīn-yáng* gender-medicine. With the appended *Bǎo-shēng suì-shì* 保生碎事 (*Jì-yīn cí-yòu wài-biān* 濟陰慈幼外編) of Wáng Qí 汪淇 on neonatal care.
- *Nǚkē jīnglún* 女科經綸 ([[KR3ei029]], 1684–1700) of [[蕭塤]] Xiāo Xūn — the late-Míng / early-Qīng citation-and-commentary expansion that completes the trilogy.

**The late-Míng *Mìng-mén* / *wēn-bǔ* gynecology cluster.** Two works extend the *Mìng-mén* doctrinal programme (kidney water-and-fire, *Bā-wèi dì-huáng wán* therapeutics) into women's medicine, in deliberate opposition to both the Lǐ Dōng-yuán spleen-stomach and Zhū Dānxī yīn-nourishing doctrines:
- *Fùrén guī* 婦人規 ([[KR3ei028]], 1624–1640) of [[張介賓]] Zhāng Jièbīn — the gynecological section of his comprehensive *Jǐng-yuè quán-shū* [[KR3e0086]], organised under nine female-specific categories; the canonical late-Míng *wēn-bǔ* gynecology.
- *Hándān yígǎo* 邯鄲遺稿 ([[KR3ei025]], 1610–1640) of [[趙獻可]] Zhào Xiànkě — the gynecological application of his *Yī-guàn* [[KR3ed015]] *Mìng-mén* doctrine, in two *juǎn* on *tāi-qián* and *chǎn-hòu*; first printed only in 1796.

**The Qīng imperial standard.** *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn·Fù-kē xīn-fǎ yào-jué* 醫宗金鑑·婦科心法要訣 ([[KR3ei007]], 1742) of [[吳謙]] Wú Qiān et al. — the gynecology section (*juǎn* 44–49) of the Qiánlóng imperial *Yùzuǎn Yīzōng jīn-jiàn* [[KR3e0090]], in seven-character rhymed mnemonic verses with prose commentary; the standard textbook for the *Tài-yī yuàn* 太醫院 examinations and the *de facto* curriculum of Qīng-period gynecology pedagogy, indebted in arrangement to [[KR3ei006]].

**The Fù-Qīng-zhǔ 傅青主 / *Fù-pài* pseudepigraphic cluster.** Four texts ascribed to the Míng loyalist [[傅山]] Fù Shān (1607–1684), all in fact early- to late-Qīng compilations whose attribution functions as legitimating fiction:
- *Fù Qīngzhǔ nǚkē* 傅青主女科 ([[KR3ei001]], first printed 1827) — the single most influential gynecological formulary in Chinese-medicine practice since the late 19th century; its *Wán-dài tāng* 完帶湯, *Yì-huáng tāng* 易黃湯, and *Tiáo-gān tāng* 調肝湯 remain core curriculum-formulas in modern PRC TCM gynecology. Demonstrated by Mǎ Jìxìng to draw substantially on [[陳士鐸]] Chén Shìduó's *Shí-shì mì-lù* and *Biàn-zhèng lù* (1687).
- *Fù Qīngzhǔ nǚkē gēkuò* 傅青主女科歌括 ([[KR3ei021]], 1827–1900) — versified mnemonic paratext to the above.
- *Fù-shì nǚkē* 傅氏女科 ([[KR3ei042]]) and *Nǚkē xiānfāng* 女科仙方 ([[KR3ei043]]) — variant late-Qīng recensions in the same pseudepigraphic stream.

**The Bamboo-Grove Monastery 竹林寺 cluster.** The famous Xiāoshān 蕭山 (Zhèjiāng) **monastic gynecological lineage** — the only institutionally identified Chinese-Buddhist monastic medical tradition of the late-imperial period, comparable to *Shàolín* 少林 in martial arts. Yi-Li Wu (2000, 2010) has shown the corpus to be a Qīng-period crystallisation of regional Zhèjiāng popular gynecological knowledge with monastic-lineage attribution functioning as a marketing brand:
- *Níngkūn mìjí* 寧坤秘笈 ([[KR3ei013]], 1786) — the 1786 *Qiánlóng* recension by an editor signing as "Lì-táng of Yuè-tián at Yé-xī".
- *Nǚkē mìyào* 女科秘要 ([[KR3ei020]], 1793) — a parallel recension.
- *Zhúlín nǚkē zhèngzhì* 竹林女科證治 ([[KR3ei026]], 1850–1881) — edited by [[方昌翰]] Fāng Chānghàn.
- *Zhúlínsì nǚkē mìfāng* 竹林寺女科秘方 ([[KR3ei046]]) — the eponymous recension.

**The popular Qīng childbirth manual.** *Dáshēng biān* 達生編 ([[KR3ei055]], 1715) of [[亟齋居士]] Jí-zhāi Jūshì — by far the most widely-circulated and most-reprinted Qīng obstetrical text, addressed in plain language to lay readers and disseminated through Buddhist charity-printing (*gōng-dé* merit-accumulation). Its core doctrines — *shuì-zhe shēng* ("birthing in one's sleep"), *Shēng-huà tāng* 生化湯 as universal post-partum master-formula, the *yùn-fù wǔ-jì* (Five Avoidances for the Pregnant Woman) — became standard furniture of Chinese popular medical culture. Hinrichs and Barnes (2013, p. 161) call it "one of the most famous and ubiquitous medical works of the Qing"; the explicit target of [[KR3ei062]] (Kagawa Gen'etsu) in 1765.

**The Qīng obstetric *zhuān-shū* cluster.** A substantial mid- to late-Qīng specialised obstetrical literature distinct from the broader *fù-kē* compendia:
- *Chǎn-bǎo* 產寶 ([[KR3ei057]], 1728) of [[倪枝維]] Ní Zhīwéi — Qīng resumption of the *Chǎn-bǎo* lineage name.
- *Tāi-chǎn xīn-fǎ* 胎產心法 ([[KR3ei058]], 1730) of [[閻純璽]] Yán Chúnxǐ — three-juan systematic obstetric monograph.
- *Pánzhū jí tāichǎn zhèngzhì* 盤珠集胎產症治 ([[KR3ei049]], 1780–1820) and *Tāi-chǎn zhǐ-nán* 胎產指南 ([[KR3ei053]], 1750–1856), both of [[單南山]] Dān Nánshān.
- *Tāi-chǎn mì-shū* 胎產秘書 ([[KR3ei054]], 1750–1809) of [[陳笏庵]] Chén Hù-ān.
- *Chǎn-yùn jí* 產孕集 ([[KR3ei059]], 1830–1850) and its revision *Chóng-dìng chǎn-yùn jí* 重訂產孕集 ([[KR3ei060]], 1850–1863) of [[張曜孫]] Zhāng Yàosūn — a late-Qīng pair from the prominent Yánghú 陽湖 literary-medical Zhāng-Wáng lineage.
- *Chǎnhòu shíbā lùn* 產後十八論 ([[KR3ei051]], 1729) — anonymous focused post-partum monograph.
- *Píng-zhù chǎn-kē xīn-fǎ* 評註產科心法 ([[KR3ei069]]) of [[汪樸齋]] Wāng Pǔzhāi.
- *Chǎnjiàn* 產鑑 ([[KR3ei050]], 1618) of [[王化貞]] Wáng Huàzhēn and *Yìn-chǎn quán-shū* 胤產全書 ([[KR3ei063]], 1600–1613) of [[王肯堂]] — the principal late-Míng predecessors of the genre.

**The Kagawa-school Edo Japanese obstetric cluster.** Three Edo-period Japanese works form a coherent late-18th-century reformist current openly critical of Chinese obstetrical orthodoxy:
- *Sǎn lùn* 產論 ([[KR3ei062]], 1765) of [[賀川玄悅]] Kagawa Gen'etsu (1700–1777) — the foundational treatise of Edo-period Japanese obstetrics, rejecting *zhèn-dài* prenatal abdominal-binding and the *chǎn-yǐ* labour-chair, introducing manual obstetrical intervention (*huí-shēng-shù* 回生術, *gōu-bāo-shù* 鉤胞術), and openly attacking [[KR3ei055]] *Dáshēng biān*'s passive-quietism. With Yamawaki Tōmon's 48-case appendix.
- *Sǎn lùn yì* 產論翼 ([[KR3ei066]], 1775) of [[賀川玄迪]] Kagawa Genteki — adoptive son and successor; expansion of the *Sǎn lùn*.
- *Sǎn-kē fā-méng* 產科發蒙 ([[KR3ei065]], 1799) of [[片倉元周]] Katakura Kakuryō — Kagawa-school systematic textbook of obstetrics.

**The *zhǒng-zǐ* 種子 / *guǎng-sì* 廣嗣 (fertility / heir-producing) cluster.** A characteristic genre of late-imperial *fù-kē*, fusing Confucian-ethical prerequisites for fertility with medical pharmacology:
- *Guǎng-sì jì-yào* 廣嗣紀要 ([[KR3ei074]], 1540–1580) of [[萬全]] Wàn Quán (1499–1582) — the most-completely-developed Confucian-ethical version of the *zhǒng-zǐ* doctrine; 16 *juǎn*; the principal mid-Míng work in the genre.
- *Guǎng-sì yào-yǔ* 廣嗣要語 ([[KR3ei071]], 1540–1570) of [[俞橋]] Yú Qiáo — earlier and more concise mid-Míng treatment.
- *Yí-lín cè* 宜麟策 ([[KR3ei072]], 1860–1900) — Qīng compilation of Zhāng Jièbīn's *zhǒng-zǐ* writings by [[汪和鼎]] Wāng Hédǐng, extracted from the *Jǐng-yuè quán-shū*.
- *Yùn-yù xuán-jī* 孕育玄機 ([[KR3ei073]], 1621) of [[陶本學]] Táo Běnxué.
- *Xù-zēng dà-shēng yào-zhǐ* 續增大生要旨 ([[KR3ei075]], 1762–1900) of [[唐千頃]] Táng Qiānqǐng — popular *zhǒng-zǐ*-and-childbirth handbook in the lineage of [[KR3ei055]].
- *Wàn-shì nǚkē* 萬氏女科 ([[KR3ei040]], 1549–1582) — Wàn Quán's gynecological companion to [[KR3ei074]].

**The Wàn-family lineage.** [[萬全]] Wàn Quán of Luótián 羅田 (Húběi) is the principal mid-Míng paediatrician-gynaecologist; his *Guǎng-sì jì-yào* ([[KR3ei074]]) and *Wàn-shì nǚkē* ([[KR3ei040]]) anchor the family-school in this division, paired with his paediatrics catalogued elsewhere (cf. Hinrichs and Barnes 2013, p. 153).

**The Shěn-Wáng-Zhāng late-Qīng / Republican lineage.** A three-generation editorial-textual lineage:
- *Shěn-shì nǚkē jíyào* 沈氏女科輯要 ([[KR3ei010]], 1770–1780) of [[沈又彭]] Shěn Yòupéng — mid-*Qián-lóng* clinical-pragmatic essentials handbook in the Pínghú-Sūzhōu medical milieu; printed in 1850 by [[王士雄]] Wáng Shìxióng (Mèng-yīng) with his own *Wēn-bìng*-school annotations.
- *Shěn-shì nǚkē jíyào jiānshū* 沈氏女科輯要箋疏 ([[KR3ei041]], 1915–1933) of [[張壽頤]] Zhāng Shòuyí (Shānléi 山雷) — the early-Republican critical annotation, prepared as a teaching text for the Lánxī Zhōngyī Zhuānménxuéxiào 蘭谿中醫專門學校 (one of the first formal TCM colleges); the standard modern TCM gynecology textbook in this lineage, preserving all three editorial strata.

**Other major Qīng gynecological monographs.** *Nǚkē yàozhǐ* 女科要旨 ([[KR3ei008]], 1801–1820) of [[陳念祖]] Chén Niànzǔ (Xiūyuán) — the great Qīng populariser's gynecology; *Nǚkē zhǐyào* 女科指要 ([[KR3ei016]], 1740–1771) of [[徐大椿]] Xú Dàchūn — the central mid-Qīng *Shāng-hán*-school critic's gynecology; *Nǚkē qièyào* 女科切要 ([[KR3ei017]], 1750–1800) of [[吳道源]] Wú Dàoyuán; *Nǚkē jīngyào* 女科精要 ([[KR3ei018]], 1702) of [[馮兆張]] Féng Zhàozhāng; *Fùkē yùchǐ* 婦科玉尺 ([[KR3ei023]], 1773) of [[沈金鰲]] Shěn Jīn-áo; *Nǚkē zhézhōng zuǎnyào* 女科折衷纂要 ([[KR3ei024]], 1850–1900) of [[凌德]] Líng Dé; *Nǚkē zhǐzhǎng* 女科指掌 ([[KR3ei027]], 1724) of [[葉其蓁]] Yè Qíshēn; *Tóngyuán yīshū·Fùrénkē* 彤園醫書·婦人科 ([[KR3ei037]], 1801–1850) of [[鄭玉壇]] Zhèng Yùtán; *Jīnguì qǐyuè (Fùkē)* 金匱啟鑰（婦科） ([[KR3ei038]], 1850–1900) of [[黃朝坊]] Huáng Cháofāng; *Fùkē bīngjiàn* 婦科冰鑑 ([[KR3ei044]], 1750–1850) of [[柴得華]] Chái Déhuá; and *Nèifǔ mìchuán jīngyàn nǚkē* 內府秘傳經驗女科 ([[KR3ei015]], 1580–1619) of [[龔廷賢]] Gōng Tíngxián.

**Late-Qīng and Republican closure.** *Mìzhēn jì-yīn* 秘珍濟陰 ([[KR3ei035]], 1830) of [[周詒觀]] Zhōu Yíguān; *Nǚkē mìzhǐ* 女科秘旨 ([[KR3ei022]], 1885–1886) of [[輪印禪師]] Lúnyìn Chánshī; *Jìshēng jí* 濟生集 ([[KR3ei014]], 1896) of [[王春亭]] Wáng Chūntíng; the Buddhist-monastic *Nǚkē zhǐyào* 女科旨要 ([[KR3ei032]]) of [[雪岩禪師]] Xuěyán Chánshī and *Tāi-chǎn xīn-shū* 胎產新書 ([[KR3ei078]], 1800–1900) of [[雪巖禪師]] / [[靜光輪應禪師]]; the late-Qīng Sì-chuān fertility manual *Shēng-shēng bǎo-lù* 生生寶錄 ([[KR3ei061]], 1800–1825) of [[袁于江]] Yuán Yújiāng; and the Republican-era *Zhúquánshēng nǚkē jíyào* 竹泉生女科集要 ([[KR3ei019]], 1925–1931); *Tāi-chǎn zhèng-zhì* 胎產證治 ([[KR3ei064]], 1929–1930, an early-Republican edition under Wáng Kěntáng's name); *Chéng Ménxuě yígǎo* 程門雪遺稿 ([[KR3ei047]], 1930–1972) of [[程門雪]] Chéng Ménxuě, principal of the post-1949 Shanghai TCM College; and *Gāo Shū-lián tāichǎn fāng-àn* 高淑濂胎產方案 ([[KR3ei067]], 1934) of [[高淑濂]] Gāo Shūlián.

**The Daoist female-alchemy outlier.** *Nǚdān hébiān xuǎnzhù* 女丹合編選註 ([[KR3ei036]], 1880–1906) of [[賀龍驤]] Hè Lóngxiāng — a late-Qīng Sìchuān anthology of *nǚ-dān* 女丹 (female internal alchemy) Daoist scriptures, including the *zhǎn chì-lóng* 斬赤龍 technique (alchemical cessation of menstruation). Not a medical text in the conventional sense; its presence documents the late-imperial taxonomic convention of grouping all female-body-focused texts together regardless of religious-medical genre.

**Anonymous and minor texts.** A number of slimmer works of less-defined provenance round out the division: *Zhāng-shì fùkē* 張氏婦科 ([[KR3ei003]]); *Fùkē wèndá* 婦科問答 ([[KR3ei004]]); *Fùkē mìfāng* 婦科秘方 ([[KR3ei005]]); *Jiāchuán nǚkē jīngyàn zhāiqí* 家傳女科經驗摘奇 ([[KR3ei011]]); *Fùkē mìshū* 婦科秘書 ([[KR3ei012]], of [[陳起]] Chén Qǐ); *Fùkē bǎibiàn* 婦科百辨 ([[KR3ei034]], of [[莊履嚴]] Zhuāng Lǚyán); *Zhèng-shì jiāchuán nǚkē wànjīn fāng* 鄭氏家傳女科萬金方 ([[KR3ei045]]); *Qián-shì mì-chuán chǎn-kē fāng-shū* 錢氏秘傳產科方書 ([[KR3ei052]]); and *Yù-lín yàn-fāng* 毓麟驗方 ([[KR3ei070]]).

## Important persons

- [[俞橋]] Yú Qiáo (mid-Míng, *fl.* 1540s) — author of [[KR3ei071]] *Guǎng-sì yào-yǔ*, an early systematic *zhǒng-zǐ* treatise.

- [[傅山]] Fù Shān (Qīngzhǔ, 1607–1684) — Míng loyalist polymath, cultural attributee of the four-text *Fù-pài* pseudepigraphic cluster ([[KR3ei001]], [[KR3ei021]], [[KR3ei042]], [[KR3ei043]]). The works are early-19th-century compilations drawing on [[陳士鐸]] Chén Shìduó.
- [[蕭塤]] Xiāo Xūn (early Qīng, *fl.* 1684) — author of [[KR3ei029]] *Nǚkē jīnglún*, completing the late-Míng / early-Qīng systematic gynecology trilogy.

## Topics

- **Woman as predominantly *yīn* substrate (*nǚ-zǐ yǐ xuè wéi běn*).** The doctrinal premise that women's bodies are *yīn*-and-blood substrate, articulated in the Sòng *Fù-rén dà-quán liáng-fāng* of Chén Zì-míng and codified in the late-Míng "*jì-yīn*" 濟陰 ("aiding the yīn") title-formula adopted by [[KR3ei006]] *Jì-yīn gāng-mù*. This *yīn*-grounded gender-medical anthropology is paired with Wǔ Zhīwàng's male-medicine *Jǐ-yáng gāng-mù* ([[KR3eh024]]) as a single *yīn-yáng* gender-medical system.
- **The canonical *jīng-dài-tāi-chǎn* 經帶胎產 typology.** The stable late-imperial topical organisation of *fù-kē* — menstrual cycle, vaginal discharge, ante-natal, post-partum — runs unchanged from [[KR3ei031]] *Nǚkē bǎiwèn* through [[KR3ei030]], [[KR3ei006]], [[KR3ei007]] to the Republican-era TCM textbooks. Supplemented by *zhǒng-zǐ* (fertility), *zhēng-jiǎ* (uterine masses), *rǔ-bìng* (breast conditions), and *qián-yīn* (genital conditions).
- **The Sòng-foundational obstetrical *Chǎn-bǎo* 產寶 lineage.** From [[昝殷]]'s [[KR3ei048]] (c. 847) through the four Sòng compilations [[KR3ei056]], [[KR3ei068]], [[KR3ei076]], [[KR3ei077]] and into the Qīng obstetric *zhuān-shū* genre. *Chǎn-bǎo* as a recurring title-name signals participation in the lineage; the substantive transmission runs through Zhū Duānzhāng's [[KR3ei056]] as the principal Sòng-edition witness.
- **The Xuē-pài / Lǐ Dōng-yuán synthesis as the mid-Míng gynecological mainstream.** Xuē Jǐ's combination of *Bǔ-zhōng yì-qì tāng* spleen-stomach therapeutics with *Liù-wèi / Bā-wèi dì-huáng wán* kidney-tonification ([[KR3ei009]], [[KR3ei039]]) becomes the canonical late-Míng formulary, subsequently systematised by Wǔ Zhīwàng ([[KR3ei006]]) and codified by the imperial *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn* ([[KR3ei007]]).
- **The *wēn-bǔ* / *Mìng-mén* doctrinal counter-position.** [[KR3ei028]] Zhāng Jièbīn and [[KR3ei025]] Zhào Xiànkě extend their *wēn-bǔ* programme into gynecology in deliberate opposition to both the Lǐ Dōng-yuán and Zhū Dānxī doctrinal lines; the late-Míng / early-Qīng systematic doctrinal tension structures the entire division. (Cf. [[KR3eh]] for the parallel *záběng* expression of the same controversy.)
- **The *gāng-mù* 綱目 systematic genre.** [[KR3ei006]] *Jì-yīn gāng-mù* (paired with [[KR3eh024]] *Jǐ-yáng gāng-mù*) realises in the gynecological domain the late-Wàn-lì *gāng-mù* hierarchical-systematic project that finds its most famous realisation in Lǐ Shízhēn's [[KR3e0079]] *Běncǎo gāngmù*.
- **The pseudepigraphic *Fù-pài* and the modern PRC TCM canon.** The Fù-Qīng-zhǔ-attributed [[KR3ei001]] is the single most-influential gynecological formulary in modern Chinese-medicine clinical practice; its *Wán-dài tāng*, *Yì-huáng tāng*, *Píng-gān kāi-yù-zhǐ-bēng tāng*, and *Jiā-jiǎn bǔ-zhōng-yì-qì tāng* are core curriculum-formulas. The work's late-Qīng pseudepigraphic origin — drawing substantively on [[陳士鐸]] Chén Shìduó — is well-established philologically but does not affect its present clinical status. An exemplary case in which legitimating attribution outweighs philological authorship.
- **Monastic medicine: the Bamboo-Grove Monastery.** The Xiāoshān 竹林寺 monastic gynecological lineage ([[KR3ei013]], [[KR3ei020]], [[KR3ei026]], [[KR3ei046]]) is the only institutionally identified Chinese-Buddhist monastic medical tradition of the late-imperial period. Yi-Li Wu (2000) treats it as the principal case study in late-imperial popular gynecology; the corpus is a Qīng-period crystallisation of regional Zhèjiāng knowledge with monastic-lineage attribution functioning as a marketing brand.
- **Popular vs technical obstetrical literature.** [[KR3ei055]] *Dáshēng biān* is the principal Qīng popular-register obstetrical text — addressed to lay readers (both men and women), printed through Buddhist charity merit-accumulation networks — distinct from the technical *fù-kē* literature for practitioners ([[KR3ei006]], [[KR3ei007]], [[KR3ei028]] etc.). The popular-technical distinction is structurally important for understanding the division's diffusion patterns.
- **The active-vs-quietist obstetrical controversy.** [[KR3ei062]] Kagawa Gen'etsu's *Sǎn lùn* (1765) is the foundational Japanese reformist treatise; it openly attacks [[KR3ei055]] *Dáshēng biān*'s quietist programme of *shuì-zhe shēng* and the Chinese practice of *zhèn-dài* prenatal abdominal-binding, advocating active obstetrical manipulation (*huí-shēng-shù* 回生術, *gōu-bāo-shù* 鉤胞術). One of the few clear cases of early-modern East-Asian medical exchange in the reverse direction — Chinese practices rejected by a Japanese practitioner developing an alternative empirical system.
- **The *zhǒng-zǐ* / *guǎng-sì* genre and Confucian-medical synthesis.** [[KR3ei074]] *Guǎng-sì jì-yào*, [[KR3ei071]] *Guǎng-sì yào-yǔ*, [[KR3ei072]] *Yí-lín cè*, and [[KR3ei075]] *Xù-zēng dà-shēng yào-zhǐ* form a distinctive late-imperial sub-genre that fuses Confucian-ethical prerequisites for fertility (*xiū-dé*, *guǎ-yù*, *yǎng-xīn*) with medical pharmacology. Fertility is theorised as not purely a medical-pharmacological problem but a *xiū-dé* problem; the parental generation's moral cultivation is the principal cause of fertile, healthy offspring.
- **Female internal alchemy as a taxonomic question.** [[KR3ei036]] *Nǚdān hébiān xuǎnzhù* is a Daoist alchemical anthology, not a medical text in the conventional sense; its presence in a *fù-kē* cataloging division documents the late-imperial Chinese taxonomic convention that all female-body-focused texts — medical, alchemical, didactic — belonged together. The *zhǎn chì-lóng* technique (alchemical cessation of menstruation) is the female counterpart of the male *zhǎn bái-hǔ* (cessation of seminal emission).
- **Family-school transmission and the late-Qīng / Republican TCM-college reform.** The Shěn-Wáng-Zhāng editorial line ([[KR3ei010]] → [[KR3ei041]]) traces a three-generation textual lineage that culminates in the early-Republican TCM-college standard textbook tradition (Zhāng Shānléi at Lánxī Zhōngyī Zhuānménxuéxiào). The lineage exemplifies the longue-durée editorial process by which late-imperial *fù-kē* knowledge was transformed into the modernised TCM-college curriculum (cf. Scheid 2007 in Wilkinson §41.7.5; Andrews 2014).
- **Sòng-Yuán-Míng-Qīng cross-divisional connections.** Several core gynecological works are explicitly excerpts or re-organisations of works held elsewhere in the KR catalog: [[KR3ei028]] is *juǎn* 38ff of [[KR3e0086]] *Jǐng-yuè quán-shū*; [[KR3ei007]] is *juǎn* 44–49 of [[KR3e0090]] *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn*; [[KR3ei030]] is one of six sections of [[KR3e0078]] *Liù-kē Zhèng-zhì zhǔn-shéng*; [[KR3ei039]] is Xuē Jǐ's edition of Chén Zì-míng's [[KR3e0038]]. The *fù-kē* sub-discipline is constituted both through specialised monographs and through the systematic disaggregation of comprehensive medical encyclopaedias for specialised-practitioner audiences.
