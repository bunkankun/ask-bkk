# Tāi-chǎn zhǐ-nán 胎產指南
*A Compass for Pregnancy and Childbirth*
by [[單南山]] (Shàn Nánshān, *fl.* mid–late 18th c.)

## About the work

An eight-*juǎn* obstetrical manual of the late Qiánlóng / Xián-fēng eras, organised as a practical handbook covering all the standard categories of pre-partum (*tāi-qián*), parturient (*lín-chǎn*), and post-partum (*chǎn-hòu*) gynecology and obstetrics. The work's therapeutic axis is explicitly stated in its prefatory matter — "for pre-partum disorders one takes the Dān-xī *Ān-tāi yǐn* 安胎飲 as the principal formula; for post-partum disorders one takes the *Shēng-huà tāng* 生化湯 as the principal formula" — placing it squarely in the late-Míng / Qīng *Shēng-huà tāng* obstetrical orthodoxy descending from Fù-Qīng-zhǔ. Its appeal in popular practice rested on its brevity and the directness of its case-and-prescription format, allowing even a non-pulsologist to "look up the symptom, check the formula, follow the procedure" (對症檢方，依法調治). Its title alludes to the legendary "south-pointing chariot" (*zhǐ-nán chē* 指南車) presented by the Duke of Zhōu to the Yuè-cháng emissaries — a compass for the lost in a region of obstetrical danger.

### Abstract

Per the 1856 (Xián-fēng *bǐng-chén*) preface of Zhōu Dān-chén 周丹忱 of Sì-míng 四明 (Níng-bō), the work originated as a manuscript circulated under the name of one Shàn Nánshān 單南山, "whose native place and personal name can no longer be ascertained." It first came to wide use through Qián Shēng-suì 錢升穟 of Yuè-jùn 越郡 (Shàoxīng), who, having obtained the secret manuscript, gained province-wide fame as an obstetrician. In *Qián-lóng guǐ-sì* (1773) the manuscript was acquired in Níng-bō by Yī Xué-zēng 伊學曾 (originally from Sì-míng), who in turn became famous for obstetrics in his native district. After Yī Xué-zēng's death the manuscript passed to his nephew Cài Yì-tíng 蔡益亭 (*míng-jīng* graduate), who circulated copies; his protégé Ōu Kǒng-zhāng 歐孔章 of Hù-xī 滬西 ultimately commissioned the wood-block edition, with Chén Cǎi-zhōng 陳彩盅 of Tiān-tái 天台 as its editor. The blocks were cut at the Ōu family Zhōng-xiàn dì 中憲第 mansion in 1856. The edition added Yī Xué-zēng's clinical case-notes *Nǚ-kē yī-àn* 女科醫案 (7 *juǎn*) as appended companion text.

The composition window for the underlying *Zhǐ-nán* manuscript itself is therefore best taken as the mid-18th century at latest — before Qián Shēng-suì's adoption (Shàn Nánshān must precede Qián, who is documented active in Yuè-jùn before 1773). The 1856 print is its first surviving recension, and `notBefore`/`notAfter` are bracketed to cover the period from c. 1750 through to first publication. Shàn Nánshān is also credited with the companion work *Pán-zhū jí tāi-chǎn zhèng-zhì* 盤珠集胎產症治 ([[KR3ei049]]) — the two works circulate as a paired set in the Hǎi-wài huí-guī series.

The catalog meta gives no person beyond Shàn Nánshān; the editorial figures Zhōu Dān-chén, Chén Cǎi-zhōng, Yī Xué-zēng, Cài Yì-tíng, and Ōu Kǒng-zhāng are mentioned in the prose body but not in the work-note frontmatter, in conformity with the project rule.
