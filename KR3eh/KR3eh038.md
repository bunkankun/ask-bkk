# Fēngláogǔgé sìdà zhèngzhì 風勞臌膈四大證治
*Treatment of the Four Great Syndromes — Wind-Stroke, Consumption, Drum-Distension, and Diaphragmatic Blockage*
by [[姜禮]] (Jiāng Lǐ, *zì* Tiānxù 天敘, b. 1654)

## About the work

A systematic monograph on the four "great syndromes" of late-imperial Jiāngnán internal medicine — *zhōngfēng* 中風 (wind-stroke), *xūláo* 虛勞 (consumption), *gǔzhàng* 鼓脹 (drum-distension), and *yēgé* 噎膈 (esophageal blockage) — popularly grouped as *fēngláogǔgé*, *shíbìng nányī* 風勞臌膈，實病難醫 ("the hard diseases to cure"). Combines Zhāng Zhòngjǐng *Shānghán* doctrine, *Nánjīng* pulse method, and JīnYuán Dānxī / Cóngzhèng commentary into focused chapters with critical case comments and appended *zápíng* sections on related complaints.

### Abstract

Jiāng Lǐ was the second-generation patriarch of the Lóngshā 龍砂 (Jiāngyīn 江陰) Jiāngshī 姜氏 medical lineage, born in *Shùnzhì* 11 = 1654 according to the family preface by his descendant Jiāng Wénjùn 姜文駿. The composition window of 1700–1735 here brackets his mature working life from age 46 to age 81.

A particularly noteworthy feature: Jiāng explicitly rejects an external-wind etiology for *zhōngfēng*, anticipating by nearly a century the slightly later treatises of [[熊笏]] Xióng Hù (1821, [[KR3eh035]]) and [[蔣寶素]] Jiǎng Bǎosù (1840, [[KR3eh018]]). The *fēngláogǔgé* grouping itself is one of the great late-imperial Jiāngnán medical heuristics for organising the most clinically intractable internal-medicine syndromes.

**Severe textual loss**: the original manuscript and family copies were lost in the Tài-píng-era wars, both in the *Xiánfēng*-era *bīngxiè* (1860) and again in *Gēngshēn* = 1860. The received text is a **1956 reconstruction** by Chéng Dàn'ān 承淡安 and Wáng Yīrén 王一仁 from a *Zhōngyī xuéhuì* 中醫學會 journal serialisation of a stray 19th-century manuscript, which itself had its lower *juǎn* missing. Explicit caveats are recorded in the prefaces by Cáo Jiādá 曹家達, Qú Jiǎnzhuāng 瞿簡莊, Chéng Dàn'ān, and Jiāng Wénjùn. The KR3eh038 text is therefore best treated as a 20th-century reconstituted edition, not as a pre-1900 textual witness.
