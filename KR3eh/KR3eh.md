## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3eh is the **miscellaneous-diseases / internal-medicine** (*záběng* 雜病, also termed *nèikē* 內科) subdivision within the Kanripo medicine class (KR3e 醫家類, *yījiā lèi*, "Masters → Medicine"). All 57 texts here come from a single early-modern Chinese-medical *cóngshū* (catalog source `hxwd`) that, in complementary distribution with the warm-disease corpus collected in [[KR3eg]] 溫病, gathers the internal-medicine *záběng* literature — those internally-engendered disorders (*nèishāng* 內傷) that the late-imperial tradition recognised as a doctrinal field categorically distinct from externally-contracted cold-damage (*shānghán* 傷寒) and warm-disease (*wēnbìng* 溫病). The field as constituted in this division ranges across the entire spectrum of pre-modern Chinese internal medicine: spleen-stomach disorders (*píwèi* 脾胃), consumption and depletion (*xūláo* 虛勞 / *xūsǔn* 虛損), wind-stroke (*zhōngfēng* 中風), blood disorders (*xuèzhèng* 血證), phlegm-fire (*tánhuǒ* 痰火), dryness-heat wasting / diabetes (*sānxiāo* 三消), beriberi (*jiǎoqì* 腳氣), malaria (*nüè* 瘧), dysentery (*lì* 痢), madness (*diānkuáng* 癲狂), insomnia, leprosy (*máfēng* 麻瘋), and male medicine (*nánkē* 男科). The genres represented include foundational doctrinal monographs, single-disease specialist treatises (*zhuānshū* 專書), versified mnemonics (*gējué* 歌訣), encyclopaedic compendia, family-school transmissions, and Republican-era Sino-Western integrationist primers.

The chronological span runs from the Northern Sòng — [[董汲]] Dǒng Jí's foot-qi monograph *Jiǎoqì zhìfǎ zǒngyào* ([[KR3eh046]], 1085–1100), the only Sòng text and the earliest specialised treatise on beriberi in Chinese — through the JīnYuán foundational corpus ([[KR3eh009]], [[KR3eh015]] of 李杲 Lǐ Gǎo; [[KR3eh047]] of 劉完素 Liú Wánsù; [[KR3eh045]] of 王好古 Wáng Hǎogǔ; [[KR3eh041]] of 葛乾孫 Gě Qiánsūn) and the MíngQīng mainstream (the bulk of the division), to the late Republican / pre-war period: the latest text is [[大塚敬節]] Ōtsuka Keisetsu's modern *kanpō* primer *Zhōngguó nèikē yījiàn* ([[KR3eh027]], Shōwa 1933). Five Edo and Meiji *kanpō* 漢方 contributions ([[KR3eh008]], [[KR3eh026]], [[KR3eh027]], [[KR3eh054]], [[KR3eh055]]) and three late-Qīng / Republican Chinese texts ([[KR3eh012]], [[KR3eh019]], [[KR3eh049]], [[KR3eh056]]) ground the division in the late East-Asian and Sino-Western medical exchange. Wilkinson §41.7.3 (middle-period medicine — Hymes 1987; Grant 2003 on Wāng Jī 汪機 of Huīzhōu) and §41.7.4 (late-imperial medicine — Chao 2009 on Sūzhōu physicians 1600–1850; Ng 1990 on madness, directly relevant to [[KR3eh048]]) frame the secondary literature; §41.7.5 (twentieth-century medicine — Andrews 2013, Croizier 1968, Scheid 2002, Lei 2014) and §41.7.6 (Leung 2009 on leprosy = [[KR3eh043]]; MacPherson 1998 on cholera; Benedict 1996 on plague; Hymes 2021 on the 1232 Kāifēng siege foundational to [[KR3eh009]]) cover the rest. T. J. Hinrichs and Linda L. Barnes (eds.), *Chinese Medicine and Healing* (Harvard Belknap, 2013) is the standard one-volume English overview.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The JīnYuán foundational corpus.** Four doctrinal monographs by three of the JīnYuán Four Masters (the fourth, 朱震亨 Zhū Dānxī, appears in the division only via pseudepigrapha and downstream synthesis):
- *Sānxiāo lùn* 三消論 ([[KR3eh047]]) of [[劉完素]] Liú Wánsù — the foundational systematic treatise on *sānxiāo* (*xiāokě* 消渴 / *xiāozhōng* 消中 / *shènxiāo* 腎消, roughly diabetes), arguing dryness-heat 燥熱 etiology against the Sòng "lower-burner cold" view, with cooling-moistening prescriptions (*Shénbái sǎn*, *Zhūdù wán*, *Gěgēn wán*, *Sānhuáng wán*, *Rénshēn báizhú sǎn*).
- *Nèiwài shāng biànhuò lùn* 內外傷辨惑論 ([[KR3eh009]]) of [[李杲]] Lǐ Gǎo — the *PíWèi* (補土 / spleen-stomach) school manifesto, born of the 1232 Mongol siege of Kāifēng (Hymes 2021); locus classicus of the 內傷 / 外感 distinction and of the *Bǔzhōng yìqì tāng* 補中益氣湯 prescription.
- *Píwèi lùn* 脾胃論 ([[KR3eh015]]) of [[李杲]] — the most influential single Dōngyuán treatise, advancing the three central doctrines that *pí* is *hòutiān zhī běn*, that *píwèi nèishāng bǎibìng yóushēng*, and that *gānwēn chú dàrè* (sweet-warm tonification clears severe heat); preface by 元好問, postface by 羅天益.
- *Yīnzhèng lüèlì* 陰證略例 ([[KR3eh045]]) of [[王好古]] Wáng Hǎogǔ — focused monograph on the *yīn*-syndromes of *Shānghán*, distinguishing "*wàishāng nèiyīn*" from true *yáng*-syndromes; the JīnYuán transitional bridge between Lǐ Dōngyuán and the Míng *wēnbǔ* tradition.

**The Yuán–Míng Dānxī line.** Mediating between the JīnYuán founders and the Míng *wēnbǔ* school: *Yīxué zhèngchuán* ([[KR3eh006]], 1515) of [[虞摶]] Yú Tuán is the principal mid-Míng systematisation of the Zhū Dānxī tradition (Yú's great-grandfather a direct Dānxī disciple), preserving the unique *Dānxī huótào* 丹溪活套 of Zhū's clinical heuristics; *Záběng zhìlì* ([[KR3eh021]]) of [[劉純]] Liú Chún and *Chóngdìng Línglán yàolǎn* ([[KR3eh014]]) of [[王綸]] Wáng Lún / 王肯堂 Wáng Kěntáng are compact 4th-generation Dānxī handbooks. The pseudo-Dānxī *Huófǎ jīyào* ([[KR3eh002]]) is doctrinally closer to the 易水 / 東垣 line. *Tánhuǒ diǎnxuě* ([[KR3eh040]]) of [[龔居中]] Gōng Jūzhōng is the systematic late-Míng treatment of the Dānxī *xiànghuǒ* / phlegm-fire complex.

**The late-Míng / early-Qīng *wēnbǔ* (warming-tonifying) line.** [[薛己]] Xuē Jǐ's *Nèikē zhāiyào* ([[KR3eh010]], 1529–1545) is the seminal Míng *Mìngmén* 命門 case-record collection and the first systematic clinical application of *Mìngmén* doctrine; it inaugurates the lineage 孫一奎 → 趙獻可 → 張介賓 → 李中梓 → 喻昌. [[胡慎柔]] Hú Shènróu's *Shènróu wǔ shū* ([[KR3eh037]]) and [[李中梓]] Lǐ Zhōngzǐ's *Bìngjī shāzhuàn* ([[KR3eh023]], printed 1666 in the *Shìcái sān shū*) extend the line; [[蔡貽績]] Cài Yíjī's *Nèishāng jí yào* ([[KR3eh051]], 1823) carries it into the late Qīng.

**The MíngQīng encyclopaedic compendia.** Three of the great pre-modern synthetic compendia of internal medicine:
- *Yīxué zhèngchuán* 醫學正傳 ([[KR3eh006]]) of [[虞摶]] — mid-Míng, Dānxī-based.
- *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng · Zábìng* 證治準繩·雜病 ([[KR3eh011]], 1602) and *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng · Shānghán* ([[KR3eh052]], 1604) of [[王肯堂]] Wáng Kěntáng — the principal late-Míng synthetic encyclopaedia of pre-1600 Chinese medicine, parts of the great six-section *Liùkē zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng* (1602–1608).
- *Jǐyáng gāngmù* 濟陽綱目 ([[KR3eh024]], 1620–1626) of [[武之望]] Wǔ Zhīwàng — 108-juan male-and-general internal-medicine encyclopaedia, paired with his *Jǐyīn gāngmù* (gynaecology, 1620; cf. Wilkinson §41.7.7 on the late-Míng gender-medicine genre).

**The Qīng imperial and synthetic mainstream.** *Yīzōng jīnjiàn · Záběng xīnfǎ yàojué* ([[KR3eh004]], 1739–1742), the internal-medicine division (*juan* 39–43) of the Qiánlóng imperial *Yùzuǎn Yīzōng jīnjiàn* compiled by [[吳謙]] Wú Qiān et al., is the standard imperial textbook of the Tàiyī yuàn 太醫院 examinations. *Jīnguì yì* ([[KR3eh003]]) of [[尤怡]] Yóu Yí (?–1749) — wings to his own *Jīnguì xīndiǎn* — organises 48 *mén* of *záběng* around Zhāng Zhòngjǐng's four axes. *Yīxué cóngzhòng lù* ([[KR3eh025]], 1810–1820) of [[陳念祖]] Chén Niànzǔ (Xiūyuán) is the mainstream-consensus handbook of the great Qīng medical populariser. *Dàfāngmài* ([[KR3eh020]], 1820–1855) of [[何彤園]] Hé Tóngyuán is the family-transmitted comprehensive 12-juan internal-medicine handbook (the technical term *Dàfāngmài* designates the adult-internal-medicine specialty as distinct from 小方脈 paediatric and 外科 external medicine).

**The Lǐngnán polemical school.** A distinctive late-imperial southern-Chinese current critical of Zhāng Jièbīn's *wēnbǔ* policy as catastrophic in the subtropical climate, aligning instead with Liú Héjiān cooling-fire and Zhū Dānxī yīn-nourishing therapeutics: [[何夢瑤]] Hé Mèngyáo's *Yī biàn* ([[KR3eh005]], 1748–1751, the founding text); [[潘名熊]] Pān Míngxióng's *Píngqín shūwū yīlüè* ([[KR3eh033]], 1865); [[梁玉瑜]] Liáng Yùyú and 陶保廉 Tāo BǎoLín's *Yīxué dáwèn* ([[KR3eh030]], 1880–1897). Pān and Liáng explicitly continue Hé's century-earlier polemic.

**The Xīn'ān (Huīzhōu) school.** [[汪文綺]] Wāng Wénqǐ's *Zázhèng huìxīn lù* ([[KR3eh007]], 1750–1754, with its much-cited *Fèiyōng lùn* three-stage protocol for lung-abscess) and [[吳澄]] Wú Chéng's *Bùjū jí* ([[KR3eh028]], 1727–1739) are the principal Qīng Xīn'ān-school monographs. Wú Chéng's *Bùjū jí* establishes the distinctive *wàisǔn* 外損 / *nèisǔn* 內損 nosology, opposing simple DōngyuánDānxī tonification and arguing that externally-induced depletion (residual evil-qi, post-febrile sequelae) requires combined purgation and supplementation. (On Huīzhōu medical culture see Grant 2003 in Wilkinson §41.7.3.)

**The 虛勞 / *xūláo* (consumption) cluster** — the largest specialised cluster in the division, eight texts:
- *Bùjū jí* ([[KR3eh028]]) — Xīn'ān-school systematic monograph, 50 juan.
- *Héshì xūláo xīnchuán* ([[KR3eh036]]) of [[何炫]] Hé Xuàn — Qīngpǔ Héshì family-school polemic against *wēnbǔ*, famous *qīwù* 七誤 chapter.
- *Shènróu wǔ shū* ([[KR3eh037]]) of the Buddhist monk-physician [[胡慎柔]] Hú Shènróu, pupil of 周慎齋 Zhōu Shènzhāi — the Dōngyuán → Xuē Jǐ → Zhōu line of spleen-stomach preservation by gentle food-based therapy.
- *Xūsǔn qǐwēi* ([[KR3eh039]]) of [[洪煒]] Hóng Wěi — Qián-lóng-era synthesis of Zhòngjǐng, Dānxī, Xuē Jǐ, and Jǐngyuè into clear *qì / xuè / yīn / yáng* depletion differentiations.
- *Tánhuǒ diǎnxuě* ([[KR3eh040]]) of [[龔居中]] Gōng Jūzhōng — the systematic late-Míng treatment of *tánhuǒ* phlegm-fire as the Dānxī-tradition explanation of *láozhài* 癆瘵 consumption; widely reprinted in Edo Japan.
- *Zēngdìng shíyào shénshū* ([[KR3eh041]]) of [[葛乾孫]] Gě Qiánsūn (Yuán) with Qīng commentary by [[陳念祖]] Chén Niànzǔ — the famously concise ten-prescription consumption formulary (*shíhuī sǎn*, *dúshēn tāng*, *bǎozhēn tāng*, *bǎifèng gāo*, *bǔsuǐ dān* etc.), arranged in 天干 sequence.
- *Gě Kějiǔ shíyào shénshū gējué* ([[KR3eh044]]) — anonymous late-Qīng versified mnemonic paratext to the above.
- *Lǐxū yuánjiàn* ([[KR3eh042]]) of [[汪綺石]] Wāng Qǐshí — the foundational late-Míng systematic treatise on *xūsǔn*, establishing the **three-organ root** doctrine (*zhìfèi, zhìpí, zhìshèn*) and the **six etiologies** (*jiǔsè, yōusī, ráoyán, fánláo, jīngqiáng, kèbìng*) that became the standard nosology in modern TCM teaching on consumption; manuscript only until 1896.

**The 中風 / wind-stroke cluster.** Three texts engage the principal 18th–19th-century internal-medicine controversy on stroke etiology: [[姜禮]] Jiāng Lǐ's *Fēngláogǔgé sìdà zhèngzhì* ([[KR3eh038]], c. 1700–1735) treats 中風 as one of the four "great syndromes" of Jiāngnán internal medicine (alongside 虛勞, 鼓脹, 噎膈), explicitly rejecting external-wind etiology; [[熊笏]] Xióng Hù's *Zhōngfēng lùn* ([[KR3eh035]], 1821, printed 1884) is the focused monograph defending the categorical distinction of *piānkū* hemiplegic wind-stroke as a 衛氣 condition from external 八方賊風, against the JīnYuán internalist consensus (左血右氣, 內風 from 血虛, 相火); [[蔣寶素]] Jiǎng Bǎosù's *Yī lüè shísān piān* ([[KR3eh018]], 1840) defends 真中風 (external wind-stroke) more generally and provides the division's earliest systematisation of *fúxié* 伏邪 doctrine.

**The 血證 / blood-disorder treatise.** [[唐宗海]] Táng Zōnghǎi's *Xuèzhèng lùn* ([[KR3eh012]], 1884) is the foundational systematic monograph on blood-disorders, organised around Táng's signature four-stage schema 止血 → 消瘀 → 寧血 → 補血. It is also the principal late-Qīng exhibit for the *ZhōngXī huìtōng* 中西匯通 movement, integrating Western cardiovascular anatomy with classical 氣血 doctrine. Táng's late versified *Lìzhèng sānzì jué* ([[KR3eh056]], 1884–1897) extends the same integrationist register to dysentery.

**Single-disease specialist treatises (*zhuānshū* 專書).** A characteristic genre of the division, beyond the consumption and wind-stroke clusters already noted:
- 三消 / diabetes: *Sānxiāo lùn* ([[KR3eh047]]) — foundational, see above.
- 腳氣 / beriberi: *Jiǎoqì zhìfǎ zǒngyào* ([[KR3eh046]]) of [[董汲]] Dǒng Jí — the founding Sòng treatise; with the two Japanese monographs [[KR3eh054]] (Meiji Hakusaidō hospital manual) and [[KR3eh055]] (Edo *suǐdoku* theory of Imamura Ryōan), the cluster spans 800 years.
- 麻瘋 / leprosy: *Mìchuán dàmáfēng fāng* ([[KR3eh043]]) — anonymous late-Qīng formulary (cf. Leung 2009 in Wilkinson §41.7.6).
- 癲狂 / madness: *Diānkuáng tiáobiàn* ([[KR3eh048]], 1863) of [[郭楚賢]] Guō Chǔxián — focused diagnostic monograph distinguishing 癲 (catatonic = *yīn*) from 狂 (manic = *yáng*), modelled on Yáng Lìshān's *Hánwēn tiáobiàn* (cf. Ng 1990).
- 瘧 / malaria: *Jiēnüè lùn shū* ([[KR3eh057]]) of [[盧之頤]] Lú Zhīyí — Míng commentary on the *Sùwèn* "Nüè lùn" chapters (HXWD transmission of the work catalogued at [[KR3e0088]]); *Nüèlì chéngfǎ* ([[KR3eh050]], 1882) of 王祉庭 Wáng Zhǐtíng — pragmatic three-formula-per-disease handbook for the 1882 Nánjīng outbreak; *Zhìnüè jīyào* ([[KR3eh049]], 1912) of 劉裁吾 Liú Cáiwú — Republican Sino-Western treatise engaging quinine and iron tonics.
- 痢 / dysentery: *Lìjí míngbiàn* ([[KR3eh053]], 1857) of 吳士瑛 Wú Shìyīng — focused monograph extending Yú Jiāyán's *nìliú wǎnzhōu* 逆流挽舟 protocol; *Lìzhèng sānzì jué* ([[KR3eh056]]) of Táng Zōnghǎi — *ZhōngXī huìtōng* versified mnemonic.
- 男科 / men's disorders: *Fùshì nánkē* ([[KR3eh034]]) — pseudepigraphic late-Qīng compilation under 傅山 Fù Shān, companion to the famous *FùQīngzhǔ nǚkē*.

**The pedagogical / mnemonic cluster.** Six texts in versified or enumerative pedagogical formats: *Yīzōng jīnjiàn · Záběng xīnfǎ yàojué* ([[KR3eh004]], imperial 七言歌訣); *Yīxué miàodì* ([[KR3eh017]], Hé Qíwěi's 76-chapter 七言歌括); *Yīxué cóngzhòng lù* ([[KR3eh025]], Chén Niànzǔ's symptom-formula format); *Yīxué suìjīn* ([[KR3eh029]], Zhōu Lǐ's mid-Míng 歌訣 primer preserved chiefly in Japanese reprints); *Yīxué yàoshù* ([[KR3eh031]], Hú Wénhuàn's late-Wàn-lì enumerative digest); *Gě Kějiǔ shíyào shénshū gējué* ([[KR3eh044]]).

**The Edo and Meiji *kanpō* cluster (five texts).** *Zábìng guǎngyào* ([[KR3eh008]], 1848–1856) of [[丹波元堅]] Tamba no Motokata is the apex of Edo *kōshōgaku* 考證學 medical philology, a planned 9-rubric 73-*mén* compendium of 300+ pre-Qīng Chinese sources (5 of 9 rubrics completed). *Zhèngzhì zhāiyào* ([[KR3eh026]]) of 中川成章 Nakagawa Shigeaki is a late-Edo *koihō* 古醫方 digest. *Jiǎoqì gōuyào* ([[KR3eh055]], 1861) of [[今邨亮]] Imamura Ryōan develops the influential **water-poison** (水毒 *suǐdoku*) theory of beriberi, with postscript by 淺田惟常 Asada Sōhaku. *Jiǎoqì gàilùn* ([[KR3eh054]], 1879) is the imperially-chartered Tokyo Hakusaidō 博濟堂 beriberi-hospital manual of 岡田昌春 Okada Masaharu and 清川玄道 Kiyokawa Gendō, operating under Asada — a programmatic *kanpō* response to the early-Meiji beriberi crisis just before the thiamine-deficiency explanation. *Zhōngguó nèikē yījiàn* ([[KR3eh027]], 1933) of [[大塚敬節]] Ōtsuka Keisetsu is the foundational 20th-century Japanese *kanpō* revival textbook, originally a collaboration with teacher 湯本求真 Yumoto Kyūshin, mapping biomedical internal-medicine categories onto Han-formula therapy.

**The Republican Chinese cluster.** Two early-Republican vernacular textbooks engaging Western biomedicine: *Tōngsú nèikē xué* ([[KR3eh019]], 1916–1936) of 張拯滋 Zhāng Zhěngzī, in the *ZhōngXī huìtōng* mode (tuberculosis as 肺病, tonsillitis as 扁桃腺炎, malaria distinguished from classical 瘧), with Chinese herbal formulae anchored on Zhòngjǐng and the JīnYuán Four; and *Zhìnüè jīyào* ([[KR3eh049]], 1912) of 劉裁吾 Liú Cáiwú, which argues *nüè* is fundamentally a 榮衛 disorder against the Yú Chāng–Yè Tiānshì–Wáng Mèngyīng consensus while engaging quinine therapy.

**The MèngHé 孟河 polemic.** [[陸懋修]] Lù Màoxiū's *Shìbǔzhāi yīshū* ([[KR3eh032]], 1860–1886) is the MèngHé school's *Shānghán*-revival manifesto, written in polemical *yīhuà* style against the late-Qīng *wēnbǔ* tendency; it includes a *Bǔ Hòu Hàn shū Zhāng Jī zhuàn* — a biographical supplement for Zhāng Zhòngjǐng. The polemic implicitly reactivates [[KR3eh042]] (Wāng Qǐshí's 虛損 doctrine) and continues [[KR3eh036]] (the Qīngpǔ Héshì critique of *wēnbǔ*).

**The Daoist visceral-pharmacology pseudepigraphon.** *Fǔxíng jué zàngfǔ yòngyào fǎyào* ([[KR3eh001]]) — a short treatise attributed to [[陶弘景]] Táo Hóngjǐng (456–536) but in fact a late-Táng / Five-Dynasties recension, famous for preserving the *Tāngyè jīngfǎ tú* 湯液經法圖 (a circular diagram mapping the *zàngfǔ* viscera to the 五味) and 60 prescriptions claimed to derive from Yī Yǐn's lost *Tāngyè jīngfǎ*. If accepted, it is the principal documentary witness to the lost pre-Hàn formulary substrate of the *Shānghán lùn*; reconstructed 1974 by 馬繼興 Mǎ Jìxìng from a pseudo-Dunhuang transmission.

## Important persons

- [[李杲]] Lǐ Gǎo (Dōngyuán, 1180–1251) — the Jīn founder of the *PíWèi* (補土) spleen-stomach school; author of [[KR3eh009]] and [[KR3eh015]], the doctrinal sources of the entire 內傷 framework that constitutes this division as a field. His *gānwēn chú dàrè* principle and *Bǔzhōng yìqì tāng* prescription remain operational in modern TCM.
- [[劉完素]] Liú Wánsù (Héjiān, c. 1110–1200) — the Jīn founder of the cooling-fire (寒涼) school and the first of the JīnYuán Four Masters; author of [[KR3eh047]], the foundational *sānxiāo* treatise. His doctrine is repeatedly invoked against the *wēnbǔ* tendency by the Lǐngnán polemic ([[KR3eh005]], [[KR3eh033]]).
- [[王好古]] Wáng Hǎogǔ (c. 1200–1264) — direct disciple of 張元素 and 李杲, author of the JīnYuán transitional *Yīnzhèng lüèlì* ([[KR3eh045]]).
- [[葛乾孫]] Gě Qiánsūn (Kějiǔ, 1305–1353) — Yuán Suzhou physician whose ten-prescription consumption formulary ([[KR3eh041]]) remained operational into the late Qīng.
- [[朱震亨]] Zhū Zhènhēng (Dānxī, 1281–1358) — the Yuán founder of the yīn-nourishing (滋陰) school; present in the division through pseudo-Dānxī works ([[KR3eh002]]) and through the Dānxī-tradition Míng systematisations of [[虞摶]] Yú Tuán ([[KR3eh006]]), [[王綸]] Wáng Lún / [[王肯堂]] Wáng Kěntáng ([[KR3eh014]]), and [[劉純]] Liú Chún ([[KR3eh021]]).
- [[虞摶]] Yú Tuán (1438–1517) — mid-Míng Dānxī-school systematiser; author of [[KR3eh006]], the principal Míng synthesis of the Dānxī tradition.
- [[薛己]] Xuē Jǐ (1487–1559) — Sūzhōu hereditary physician, founder of the Míng *Mìngmén* doctrinal application; author of [[KR3eh010]], the seminal case-record collection of the *wēnbǔ* lineage. The downstream tradition (Sūn Yīkuí → Zhào Xiànkě → Zhāng Jièbīn → Lǐ Zhōngzǐ) frames much of the late-Míng / Qīng *wēnbǔ* material in the division.
- [[王肯堂]] Wáng Kěntáng (1549–1613) — the great late-Míng synthesist of pre-1600 Chinese medicine; author of [[KR3eh011]] (內科 division) and [[KR3eh052]] (傷寒 division) of the *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng*; reviser of [[KR3eh014]]; cited as principal prescriptive source in [[KR3eh057]].
- [[武之望]] Wǔ Zhīwàng (1552–1629) — late-Míng author of the paired 108-juan *Jǐyáng gāngmù* ([[KR3eh024]]) for male internal medicine and the *Jǐyīn gāngmù* for women's medicine; the great Míng synthetic encyclopaedist of gender medicine.
- [[汪綺石]] Wāng Qǐshí (late Míng) — foundational systematic theorist of 虛勞 ([[KR3eh042]]); his three-root / six-cause nosology is the standard modern teaching framework on consumption.
- [[龔居中]] Gōng Jūzhōng (fl. late Wànlì) — late-Míng systematist of *tánhuǒ* / phlegm-fire pathology ([[KR3eh040]]); a standard *kanpō* source in Edo Japan.
- [[吳謙]] Wú Qiān (c. 1689–1748) — chief editor of the Qiánlóng imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn*; the *záběng xīnfǎ yàojué* ([[KR3eh004]]) section is the principal Qīng standard textbook of internal medicine.
- [[尤怡]] Yóu Yí (?–1749) — one of the most learned Qīng scholar-physicians of the Sūzhōu Zhòngjǐng-school orbit; author of [[KR3eh003]] (cf. Chao 2009 in Wilkinson §41.7.4).
- [[何夢瑤]] Hé Mèngyáo (1693–1764) — founder of the Lǐngnán internal-medicine polemic ([[KR3eh005]]); the principal Cantonese physician of the Qīng.
- [[陳念祖]] Chén Niànzǔ (Xiūyuán, 1753–1823) — the great Qīng medical populariser, author of *Yīxué sānzì jīng* and of [[KR3eh025]]; commentator on [[KR3eh041]].
- [[唐宗海]] Táng Zōnghǎi (1846–1897) — founder of the late-Qīng *ZhōngXī huìtōng* movement; author of [[KR3eh012]] (the foundational systematic treatise on blood-disorders) and [[KR3eh056]] (the dysentery mnemonic).
- [[陸懋修]] Lù Màoxiū (1818–1886) — the principal late-Qīng MèngHé school polemicist for *Shānghán* revival against *wēnbǔ* ([[KR3eh032]]).
- [[蔣寶素]] Jiǎng Bǎosù (1795–1873) — early systematist of *fúxié* 伏邪 latent-evil doctrine for warm-disease etiology and defender of 真中風 ([[KR3eh018]]).
- [[董汲]] Dǒng Jí (Northern Sòng, fl. 1080s–1090s) — author of [[KR3eh046]], the earliest specialised monograph on 腳氣 in the Chinese tradition.
- [[盧之頤]] Lú Zhīyí (Míng) — late-Míng critical *běncǎo* scholar and 瘧 commentator ([[KR3eh057]]; his major work catalogued at [[KR3e0089]] in the parent division).
- [[何炫]] Hé Xuàn (1662–1722) — the Qīngpǔ Héshì hereditary lineage's principal 虛勞 theorist ([[KR3eh036]]); the family line is one of the longest physician lineages of late-imperial China.
- [[何其偉]] Hé Qíwěi (1774–1837) — Qīngpǔ Héshì versifier ([[KR3eh017]]); historically notable for the opium-cessation prescriptions adopted by 林則徐 in 1839.
- [[今邨亮]] Imamura Ryōan (1814–1890) — Edo *kanpō* physician; principal theorist of the *suǐdoku* 水毒 doctrine of beriberi ([[KR3eh055]]).
- [[丹波元堅]] Tamba no Motokata (1795–1857) — Edo Taki-Tamba 多紀 *kōshōgaku* philologist; author of [[KR3eh008]], the apex of Japanese medical-philological compendia.
- [[大塚敬節]] Ōtsuka Keisetsu (1900–1980) — the principal 20th-century Japanese *koihō* / *kanpō* revivalist; author of [[KR3eh027]] (1933), the latest text in the division.

## Topics

- **The 內傷 / 外感 distinction.** The foundational discrimination of internally-engendered damage (spleen-stomach exhaustion, depletion) from externally-contracted affliction, articulated by Lǐ Gǎo in [[KR3eh009]] in response to the 1232 Mongol siege of Kāifēng (Hymes 2021). This distinction is the constitutive premise of the entire division: it defines *záběng* as a doctrinal field by separating it from the cold-damage and warm-disease frameworks belonging to externally-contracted pathology.
- **The 補土 spleen-stomach doctrine (PíWèi / Dōngyuán school).** Lǐ Gǎo's elevation of the spleen-stomach to *hòutiān zhī běn*, with its therapeutic corollaries (*Bǔzhōng yìqì tāng*, *gānwēn chú dàrè* — sweet-warm therapy clears severe heat), is the most pervasive single doctrinal substrate in the division ([[KR3eh009]], [[KR3eh015]] foundational; [[KR3eh002]] pseudonymously; [[KR3eh037]] via 周慎齋; [[KR3eh045]] direct disciple).
- **The 滋陰 yīn-nourishing line and its critics.** Zhū Dānxī's yīn-nourishing programme, transmitted through Yú Tuán ([[KR3eh006]]), Wāng Wénqǐ ([[KR3eh007]] in part), and the pseudo-Dānxī material ([[KR3eh002]]), is the doctrinal counterpole to *wēnbǔ*. The Lǐngnán school ([[KR3eh005]], [[KR3eh033]], [[KR3eh030]]) reactivates the Liú Héjiān cooling-fire and Dānxī yīn-nourishing therapeutics together against the *wēnbǔ* mainstream.
- **The 命門 / 溫補 line and its critics.** Xuē Jǐ's *Mìngmén* doctrine ([[KR3eh010]]) and the consequent *wēnbǔ* (warming-tonifying) therapeutic style — *Bāwèi wán*, modified 補中益氣湯, 桂附-based prescribing — is the principal late-Míng / early-Qīng line ([[KR3eh007]], [[KR3eh023]], [[KR3eh037]], [[KR3eh051]]). It is sharply criticised from the south (Lǐngnán: [[KR3eh005]], [[KR3eh033]], [[KR3eh030]]), from the Héshì family-school ([[KR3eh036]] — *wēnbǔ* actively worsens 虛勞), and from the MèngHé *Shānghán* revival ([[KR3eh032]]).
- **真中風 vs 內風.** A sustained 18th–19th-century controversy on wind-stroke etiology: is 中風 internally generated (the JīnYuán consensus of 內風 from *xuèxū* / *xiànghuǒ*, with the 左血右氣 corollary), or externally contracted (the position recovered in late Qīng)? Jiāng Lǐ ([[KR3eh038]], c. 1700–1735) rejects external; Xióng Hù ([[KR3eh035]], 1821) defends external in *piānkū* form; Jiǎng Bǎosù ([[KR3eh018]], 1840) defends external generally.
- **The 痰火 / phlegm-fire complex.** The Dānxī-derived doctrinal complex underlying *láozhài* (consumption), *kéxuè* (haemoptysis), *dàohàn* (night sweats), and wasting; systematised by Gōng Jūzhōng ([[KR3eh040]]) and downstream in [[KR3eh041]], [[KR3eh044]], [[KR3eh042]].
- **The three-root / six-cause nosology of *xūsǔn*.** Wāng Qǐshí's foundational reframing of consumption ([[KR3eh042]]): three organs as roots (lung, spleen, kidney), six etiologies (excess wine/sex, anxiety, garrulousness, overwork, sexual indulgence, post-pathogenic depletion). This framework remains the standard nosology of consumption in modern TCM teaching.
- **The 八綱 真假 problematic.** True-vs-apparent syndromes (真寒假熱, 真虛假實 etc.) — already embryonic in Xuē Jǐ ([[KR3eh010]]), systematised as a differential-diagnosis manual in Chén Shìduó's *Biànzhèng yùhán* ([[KR3eh016]], 1687–1707), a precursor to the modern eight-principle (*bāgāng*) differential.
- **燥熱 vs 下焦寒 etiology of 三消.** Liú Wánsù's foundational reframing of *xiāokě* / diabetes-cluster ([[KR3eh047]]) from the Sòng "lower-burner cold" reading to a unified dryness-heat 燥熱 doctrine, with cooling-moistening prescriptions; the cornerstone reframing on which all later 三消 thought rests.
- **水毒 / *suǐdoku* etiology of 腳氣.** Imamura Ryōan's late-Edo doctrinal innovation ([[KR3eh055]]): beriberi as a single endogenous toxic principle of nutritional / constitutional origin, summer-onset and urban-prevalent, read through *Jīnguì*'s 支飲 and 風水 framings. Transmitted into Meiji *kanpō* via [[KR3eh054]]; an instructive case of pre-thiamine East-Asian medical theorisation of what was emerging as the principal urban-Japanese deficiency disease (cf. Wilkinson §41.7.4 on late-imperial medicine, §41.7.5 on modern medicine).
- **中西匯通 (Sino-Western synthesis).** A doctrinal line crystallising in the late Qīng around Táng Zōnghǎi ([[KR3eh012]], [[KR3eh056]]) and continuing into the Republican era ([[KR3eh019]], [[KR3eh049]]). The Japanese *kanpō* revival ([[KR3eh027]]) is the parallel non-Chinese case. The texts engage Western anatomy (mesentery, cardiovascular system), bacteriology, and pharmacology (quinine, iron, cathartics) without abandoning the classical doctrinal frame. See Andrews 2013, Croizier 1968, Lei 2014 in Wilkinson §41.7.5.
- **The single-disease *zhuānshū* genre.** A characteristic genre of the division: focused monographs on a single condition (中風, 三消, 腳氣, 麻瘋, 癲狂, 瘧, 痢, 血證, 男科, 失眠). Reflects late-imperial specialisation within internal medicine and the broader Qīng-Republican movement toward differentiated clinical sub-fields.
- **Versified mnemonic pedagogy (*gējué* / *gēkuò*).** A pervasive MíngQīng pedagogical idiom in the division: imperial 七言歌訣 ([[KR3eh004]]), 七言歌括 family-school primer ([[KR3eh017]]), three-character verse ([[KR3eh056]]), 歌括 paratext ([[KR3eh044]]), enumerative 要數 digest ([[KR3eh031]]), question-and-answer 答問 form ([[KR3eh030]]), 醫話 polemic ([[KR3eh032]]).
- **Editorial transmission, family-schools, regional lineages.** Like the warm-disease canon collected in [[KR3eg]], the *záběng* corpus is heavily constituted through editorial transmission and family-school continuity: the Qīngpǔ Héshì lineage ([[KR3eh017]], [[KR3eh036]]); the Sūzhōu *wēnbǔ* hereditary line; the Xīn'ān Wùyuán school ([[KR3eh007]], [[KR3eh028]]); the MèngHé school ([[KR3eh032]]); the Lǐngnán polemical school ([[KR3eh005]], [[KR3eh030]], [[KR3eh033]]); the Lóngshā Jiāngshī ([[KR3eh038]]). See Chao 2009 (Sūzhōu physicians 1600–1850) and Grant 2003 (Huīzhōu) in Wilkinson §§41.7.3–4.
- **East-Asian medical circulation.** The Edo Taki-Tamba philological line ([[KR3eh008]]) and the late-Edo / Meiji / Shōwa Asada-Imamura-Yumoto-Ōtsuka *kanpō* circle ([[KR3eh026]], [[KR3eh027]], [[KR3eh054]], [[KR3eh055]]) form a continuous Japanese current parallel to the Chinese mainstream. The Republican-era Sino-Japanese exchange (the Yumoto-Ōtsuka teacher-pupil relation, the [[KR3eh027]] textbook) is the principal late case.

## Timeline

- **1085–1100** — *Jiǎoqì zhìfǎ zǒngyào* 腳氣治法總要 [[KR3eh046]] — Dǒng Jí's Northern-Sòng founding monograph on beriberi; opens the division chronologically.
- **1170–1232** — *Sānxiāo lùn* 三消論 [[KR3eh047]] — Liú Wánsù's foundational treatise on the *sānxiāo* / wasting-thirst cluster; the first of the JīnYuán Four Masters.
- **1232–1247** — *Nèiwài shāng biànhuò lùn* 內外傷辨惑論 [[KR3eh009]] — Lǐ Gǎo's foundational discrimination of internal damage from external affliction, born of the 1232 Kāifēng siege.
- **1243** — *Yīnzhèng lüèlì* 陰證略例 [[KR3eh045]] — Wáng Hǎogǔ's JīnYuán transitional monograph on *yīn*-syndromes of *Shānghán*.
- **1245–1249** — *Píwèi lùn* 脾胃論 [[KR3eh015]] — Lǐ Gǎo's most influential single treatise; the doctrinal core of the 補土 school.
- **c. 1300–1500** — *Huófǎ jīyào* 活法機要 [[KR3eh002]] — pseudonymous Dānxī-attributed *záběng* handbook (doctrinally YìshuǐDōngyuán).
- **1348–1879** — *Zēngdìng shíyào shénshū* 增訂十藥神書 [[KR3eh041]] — Gě Qiánsūn's 1348 ten-prescription consumption formulary with Chén Niànzǔ's Qīng commentary (printed 1879).

- **1529–1545** — *Nèikē zhāiyào* 內科摘要 [[KR3eh010]] — Xuē Jǐ's seminal *Mìngmén* case-record collection; opening of the Sūzhōu *wēnbǔ* line.
- **1580–1610** — *Yīxué yàoshù* 醫學要數 [[KR3eh031]] — Hú Wénhuàn's late-Wàn-lì enumerative pocket digest.
