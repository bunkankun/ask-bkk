# Fǎhuá jīng xuán-yì jí-lüè 法華經玄義輯略
*Compiled Outline of the Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sūtra*
by [[傳燈]] (Chuándēng / Yōu-xī Chuándēng, 錄)

## About the work

A single-*juan* abridgement and outline of Zhìyǐ's *Miào-fǎ liánhuá jīng xuán-yì* ([[KR6d0006]], T1716), recorded by the late-Ming Tiāntái master Yōu-xī Chuándēng 幽溪傳燈 (1554–1628), the principal architect of the late-Ming Tiāntái revival on Tiāntái-shān. The text is recorded in the front matter as composed at the *Yōu-xī sēng* 幽溪 retreat ("the *śramaṇa* of Yōu-xī of Tiāntái-shān, Chuán-dēng, recorded": 天台山幽溪沙門　傳燈　錄).

### Prefaces

The text has no separate preface; it opens directly with Chuándēng's editorial framing: "The Tiāntái Zhì-zhě Dà-shī used the five-fold profound-meaning to explain the title of the sūtra; the doctrinal purport beneath the title was thereby exhausted with no remainder. The five chapters are: (1) explaining the name; (2) elucidating the essence; (3) clarifying the purport; (4) discussing the function; (5) judging the doctrinal classification. In explaining these five there are 'common' and 'particular' aspects: the common is the seven-aspect joint explanation; the particular is the five chapters each separately set forth — analogous to the broad and concise modes for sharp and dull faculties." The text proceeds to extract and order the central definitions of each of the five chapters into a compact handbook.

### Abstract

Chuándēng's *Jí-lüè* belongs together with Zhìxù's *Jié-yào* ([[KR6d0010]]) as the two principal late-Ming abridgements of Zhìyǐ's *Xuán-yì*, but Chuándēng's work differs significantly in editorial method: rather than excerpting Zhìyǐ's text verbatim, Chuándēng paraphrases and synthesises, producing what is in effect a self-standing summary in his own voice rather than a condensed copy of the original. The text is consequently more accessible as an introduction but less useful as a study aid alongside the full *Xuán-yì*.

Yōu-xī Chuándēng was the central figure in the late-Ming revival of Tiāntái doctrinal study at Tiāntái-shān itself. Born to a Yè 葉 family in Pínghǎi 平海 in modern Zhèjiāng, he took monastic vows at the *Yīn-jīng-sì* 蔭靜寺; received the dharma from **Bǎijì Cǎo Tài-shī** 百際草大師 of Tiāntái; restored the **Yōu-xī Gāo-míng-sì** 幽溪高明寺 (whence his sobriquet *Yōu-xī*) on Tiāntái-shān; and produced a substantial corpus of Tiāntái commentaries — the *Wéimó wú-wǒ shū* 維摩無我疏 (16 *juan*), the *Lèngyán xuán-yì* 楞嚴玄義 (4 *juan*), the *Xìng-shàn-è lùn* 性善惡論 (6 *juan*), the *Tiāntái sì-jiào yí jí-zhù* 天台四教儀集註 (10 *juan*), the *Jìng-tǔ shēng-wú-shēng lùn* 淨土生無生論 (1 *juan*), and the present *Xuán-yì jí-lüè*. He was the principal Tiāntái dialogue-partner with the late-Ming Pure Land master Yúnqī Zhūhóng 雲棲祩宏 (1535–1615) and the chief late-Ming defender of the *xìng-jù-è* 性具惡 ("buddha-nature inherently includes evil") doctrine of Sòng Tiāntái scholasticism.

The dating of the *Jí-lüè* is uncertain — no internal date is given — but it most likely belongs to Chuándēng's mature productive period at Yōu-xī Gāo-míng-sì in the early seventeenth century, alongside his other major Tiāntái compendia.

## Other points of interest

Yōu-xī Chuándēng's *xìng-jù-è* polemic against Yún-qī Zhū-hóng — preserved most fully in his *Xìng-shàn-è lùn* 性善惡論 — was the most substantial late-Ming Tiāntái-Pure Land doctrinal exchange, and the *Jí-lüè*'s emphasis on the structural soundness of Zhìyǐ's *Xuán-yì* should be read in the context of Chuán-dēng's defense of Sòng Tiāntái positions against late-Ming Pure Land syncretism.
