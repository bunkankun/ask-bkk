# Bùtuìzhuǎn fǎlún jīng 不退轉法輪經
*Sūtra of the Non-Backsliding Dharma-Wheel*
Translator unknown (失譯), attributed to the Northern Liáng 北涼 catalogue.

## About the work

A four-*juan* anonymous early Chinese translation of the *Avaivartika-cakra-sūtra* — the second of the three Chinese translations of the same Indic source-text preserved in the canon. The Taishō cross-reference 「No. 267 [Nos. 266, 268]」 documents the parallel translations: T266 = Dharmarakṣa's *Fó-shuō Āwéiyuèzhì zhē jīng* ([[KR6d0105]]), T267 = the present anonymous translation, T268 = Zhì-yán's *Fó-shuō Guǎng-bó yán-jìng bù-tuì-zhuǎn lún jīng* ([[KR6d0107]]).

### Prefaces

The text in the Taishō recension carries the editorial note: *Sēngyòu lù yún Āngōng Liángtǔ yì jīng zài BěiLiáng lù dìèr yì* 僧祐錄云安公涼土異經在北涼錄第二譯 ("The *Sēngyòu lù* [the *Chū sānzàng jì jí* T2145] says: per Master Ān [Dàoān 道安, 312–385], in the Liáng-area divergent-sūtra catalogue of the Northern Liáng, [this is] the second translation"). This editorial framing attributes the translation to the **Northern Liáng** (397–439 CE) productive period and identifies the work as one of the *Liángtǔ yìjīng* 涼土異經 ("Liáng-area divergent sūtras") catalogued by Dàoān.

### Abstract

The *Bùtuìzhuǎn fǎlún jīng* is the second of the three Chinese translations of the Indic *Avaivartika-cakra-sūtra*. Its anonymous Liáng-period attribution places it in the early-fifth-century Northern Liáng productive context, when the Liáng courts at Liángzhōu sponsored substantial Buddhist translation activity that supplemented and frequently competed with the contemporary Cháng'ān translation bureau under Kumārajīva.

The work's four-*juan* extent (against Dharmarakṣa's three-*juan* in [[KR6d0105]]) suggests either a different recension of the Indic text or substantial expansion in translation. Comparison of the three Chinese translations against the Tibetan and any surviving Indic fragments would clarify the textual relationships, but no detailed comparative study has been published.

The dating is bracketed within the Northern Liáng productive period (397–439).
