# Fǎhuá jīng rù-shū 法華經入疏
*Inserted-Annotation Subcommentary on the Lotus Sūtra*
by [[道威]] (Dàowēi / Sìmíng Wēi, 入注 — interpolating annotator)

## About the work

A twelve-*juan* Sòng-period subcommentary on the Lotus Sūtra by Dào-wēi 道威 (DILA A001512), a Sì-míng 四明 (Níngbō) Buddhist master whose biographical details are largely unrecorded. The genre — *rù-shū* 入疏 ("inserted-into-subcommentary") — indicates a commentary in which Dào-wēi's own annotations are interpolated directly into the text of an existing earlier subcommentary, with the goal of producing a unified text in which original commentary and supplementary annotation can be read together. The work is preserved as X30n0600 in the *Xù-zàng-jīng*.

### Prefaces

The text in the X30n0600 recension carries no separate translator's preface. The work opens with the *Fǎhuá jīng rù-shū mù-cì* 妙法蓮華經入疏目次 (table of contents), arranged by chapter of the Lotus Sūtra in the standard twenty-eight-chapter division, and proceeds with the *Xù-pǐn dì-yī* 序品第一 ("Introductory Chapter, the First").

### Abstract

The *Rù-shū* — "inserted-subcommentary" — is a genre in which a later commentator's annotations are interpolated directly into the text of an earlier subcommentary. The base text Dào-wēi worked on is not securely identified in the surviving recension; on the basis of his Sì-míng affiliation it is most plausibly the standard *shān-jiā* commentarial corpus on the Lotus, possibly Zhīlǐ's [Tiāntái Sì-míng] *Pǔ-mén* commentaries ([[KR6d0047]], [[KR6d0049]]) extended through the *Wén-jù*-based exegesis. The twelve-*juan* extent corresponds to the standard division of Jí-zàng's *Yì-shū* ([[KR6d0024]]); Dào-wēi may have interpolated his annotations into a Tiāntái-school adaptation of Jí-zàng's structural framework.

The work is consequently of interest as a Sòng-period example of the *rù-shū* editorial genre — a Sòng innovation in commentarial production that anticipated the late-Míng combined-edition (*huì-běn*) genre by allowing readers to access an earlier text and a later commentator's annotations in unified form rather than in parallel separate texts. Dào-wēi's *Rù-shū* is one of the few surviving Sòng *rù-shū* productions on a major Mahāyāna sūtra.

The dating is uncertain. Dào-wēi's lifedates are not recorded; his productive period must postdate Zhīlǐ's mature work (early eleventh century) and predate the late-Sòng productive period of the *shān-jiā* lineage. A defensible bracket of c. 1000–1200 covers his probable active period.

## Other points of interest

The *rù-shū* editorial genre is parallel to the contemporary Confucian *fù-zhù* 附註 (attached-annotation) genre developed in the Northern-Sòng *Lǐ-xué* 理學 commentarial tradition: in both cases, a later commentator's annotations are interpolated into an earlier authoritative text rather than circulated as a separate parallel commentary. The Sòng tendency toward integrated commentary-and-base-text editions reflects a broader Sòng-period scholarly preference for unified rather than fragmentary access to authoritative material.
