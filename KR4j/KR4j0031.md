# Lǎn-kū cí 孏窟詞
 *Lyrics of the Lazy-Den*
by [[侯寘]] (撰)

## About the work

The *Lǎn-kū cí* 孏窟詞 is the one-*juǎn* Sìkù *cí* collection of Hóu Zhì [[侯寘]] (*fl. Shào-xīng* through *Qián-dào* / *Chún-xī*, c. 1141–1180; *zì* Yàn-zhōu 彥周, *hào* Lǎn-kū jūshì 孏窟居士), of Dōng-wǔ 東武 (modern Zhū-chéng, Shān-dōng). Chén Zhèn-sūn [[陳振孫]]'s *Shūlù jiětí* records him as having served under *Shào-xīng* as *Zhí xué-shì* Prefect of Jiàn-kāng, but the *Tíyào* shows from internal-evidence that Hóu lived on through *Qián-dào* and *Chún-xī*: the *Rén-wǔ yuán-dàn* one *cí* must be from *Rén-wǔ* / *Shào-xīng* 32 / 1162 (the year before *Lóng-xīng* / *Xiào-zōng*'s reign-name change); his *cí* for Zhāng Jìng-fū 張敬夫 (Zhāng Shì 張栻) and the Mid-Autumn *cí* to Liú Gōng-fù 劉恭甫 both refer to Xiào-zōng-era figures. Hóu was the nephew of Cháo Bǔ-zhī [[晁補之]]; the family had preserved the literary heritage of the *Yuán-yòu* old houses, and the volume's social network — its *cháng-chóu* with high-tier Southern-Sòng officials — reflects that pedigree. The *Tíyào* notes a *Yǎn ér méi · Xiào Yì-ān tǐ* 眼兒媚·效易安體 — one of the earliest documented self-conscious imitations of the Yì-ān-tǐ 易安體 of Lǐ Qīng-zhào [[李清照]] in the *cí* tradition.

### Tiyao

*Lǎn-kū cí*, one *juǎn*, by Hóu Zhì of the Sòng. Per Chén Zhèn-sūn's *Shūlù jiětí*: Zhì, *zì* Yàn-zhōu, a man of Dōng-wǔ; in *Shào-xīng*, as *Zhí xué-shì* he governed Jiàn-kāng. The collection has *Xì yòng Hè Fāng-huí (Hè Zhù) yùn jiàn-bié Zhū Shǎo-zhāng* — so the man is early-Southern-Sòng. The *Yǎn ér méi · Xiào Yì-ān tǐ* — *Yì-ān* is Lǐ Qīng-zhào's *hào*, also an early-*Shào-xīng* person; Hóu already calls it "imitating" — somewhat like the *Xiào Shěn Xià-xián tǐ* of Dù Mù and Lǐ Shāng-yǐn. He has also a *Shòu cí* for *Zhí gé* Zhāng Jìng-fū, a *Mid-Autumn* *cí* to *Shè-rén* Liú Gōng-fù — both Xiào-zōng-era figures; and a *Rén-wǔ yuán-dàn* *cí* — *Rén-wǔ* / *Shào-xīng* 32 / 1162, the year before Xiào-zōng's accession — so Hóu was still alive between *Qián-dào* and *Chún-xī*. Chén Zhèn-sūn merely names his official-year. Hóu was Cháo's nephew, retaining the *Yuán-yòu* old-family currents; hence his associates are all leading men, and his *cí* is gracefully refined, no wine-shop-and-music-hall *zān-xì láng-jí* manner. Though his name is not loud, among Southern-Sòng houses he must be counted a *zuò-zhě*. The *Wénxiàn tōngkǎo* records one *juǎn*, agreeing with the present. Máo Jìn [[毛晉]] cut it among the *Liùshí jiā cí*, with very loose proof-reading. The worst: *Qín-lóu yuè* is *Yì Qín-é* — from Lǐ Bái's *cí* line *Qín-é mèng duàn Qín-lóu yuè*, later it was renamed; the form is properly two-stanza (*shuāng-diào*); Jìn's cut drops a *dí* character from the end of the front-half and joins it to the back-half, making the tune appear to have a variant form — a clear error. Other instances of word-confusion (*Shuǐ-diào gē-tóu*'s *qīng* should not be flat; *Qīng-yù àn*'s *mù* repeats rhyme with the prior stanza; *rǎn-rǎn nián yuán* — *yuán* makes no sense): also miscut. The *Yáo tiān fèng cuì-huá yǐn* one *cí* especially full of errors, almost unreadable. No other text to collate against: corrections where possible, others left as is. — Compiled, *Qián-lóng* 44 / 1779, 10th month.

### Abstract

The transmitted *Lǎn-kū cí* descends through Máo Jìn's cutting. Modern editions: the *Quán Sòng cí* of Táng Guī-zhāng [[唐圭璋]] preserves around 91 *cí*. Hóu Zhì's biographical record is fragmentary: nephew of Cháo Bǔ-zhī (matrilineal Cháo connection); *Shào-xīng* prefectship of Jiàn-kāng; the *cháng-chóu* network includes Zhāng Shì [[張栻]] (*zì* Jìng-fū), Liú Gōng-fù 劉恭甫, and Zhū Shǎo-zhāng. The collection is best read as a useful mid-Southern-Sòng witness — refined private-friendship *cí* in a register continuous with the late-Northern-Sòng *Yuán-yòu* tradition the Cháo family preserved. The self-conscious *Yǎn ér méi · Xiào Yì-ān tǐ* is historically valuable as one of the earliest *cí* explicitly invoking Lǐ Qīng-zhào's manner as a category.

## Other points of interest

The early formal-imitation of *Yì-ān tǐ* documented in this collection is one of the foundational moments of Lǐ Qīng-zhào's canonization within Sòng literary culture: the *xiào* 效 (imitate) gesture was reserved for forms recognized as having their own distinctive prosodic-rhetorical identity, and that Hóu Zhì applied it to Lǐ within a generation of her death testifies to the speed of her recognition.
