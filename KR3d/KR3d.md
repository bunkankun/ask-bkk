# KR3d 農家類 — Nóngjiā lèi (Agricultural Treatises)

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3d 農家類 (*Nóngjiā lèi*) is the agricultural-treatises subdivision of the 子部 (Masters), gathering the comprehensive *nóng shū* 農書 — practical handbooks and imperially sponsored encyclopedias covering grain and rice cultivation, sericulture, horticulture, animal husbandry, water-control, agricultural implements, food preservation, and famine-relief botany. The classification has a continuous bibliographic history: from the Hàn 漢志 the genre was placed in section nine (*nóngshū*) of the Masters branch; by the Qīng *Sìkù quánshū* it sat in section four of the *zǐ-bù*, and the present KR3d retains that placement (Wilkinson, *Chinese History: A New Manual* §35.4).

The chronological span of the works actually collected here runs from the Northern Wèi *Qí mín yào shù* of the 530s — the first complete extant agricultural encyclopedia — through the Sòng-Yuán southern-rice and imperial-handbook traditions, the Míng famine-botany and Jesuit-influenced hydraulic literature, and culminates in the Qīng imperial *Qīn dìng shòu shí tōng kǎo* of 1742. Wilkinson (§35.4) notes that some 260 such treatises survive out of about 650 known titles, and characterises the genre as combining (i) quotation from earlier and now-lost agricultural works, (ii) hands-on experience by retired officials, magistrates, and estate-holders, and (iii) imperially commissioned popularisation. KR3d's 10 entries are a curated SKQS selection drawn precisely from these three streams.

## Important texts and text clusters

The ten works fall into four readily recognisable clusters.

**Foundational pre-Sòng treatise.**
- *Qí mín yào shù* 齊民要術 [[KR3d0001]] — Jiǎ Sīxié's Northern Wèi (533–544) encyclopedia, the first complete extant *nóng shū* and the cornerstone of the entire later tradition; about a third of every subsequent comprehensive treatise quotes from it.

**Sòng–Yuán southern-rice and imperial-handbook cluster.** The four medieval comprehensive treatises that together canonised the genre's two registers — private/regional and imperial/administrative.
- *Nóng shū* 農書 (Chén Fū) [[KR3d0002]] — the 3-juan 1149 Southern Sòng manual focused on Lower-Yangzi wet-rice cultivation, the first major treatise written from a southern-Chinese viewpoint.
- *Nóng-sāng jí yào* 農桑輯要 [[KR3d0003]] — the 1273/1286 *Sī-nóng-sī* compilation under Khubilai, the earliest surviving officially sponsored agricultural treatise, distributed empire-wide to local magistrates.
- *Nóng-sāng yī-shí cuō yào* 農桑衣食撮要 [[KR3d0004]] — Lǔ Míngshàn's 1314 calendar-organised handbook for magistrates acting as agricultural instructors, distinguished by being written by a Uyghur Yuán official.
- *Nóng shū* 農書 (Wáng Zhēn) [[KR3d0005]] — the 22-juan Yuán encyclopedia (preface 1313), whose third part *Nóng qì tú pǔ* 農器圖譜 is the most extensive pre-modern Chinese illustrated technical catalogue of agricultural implements, and whose printing history is famous for the earliest detailed description of wooden movable-type technology.

**Late-Míng famine-relief and Jesuit-encounter cluster.** Four Míng works marking the genre's two great Late-Míng innovations: famine-relief edible-plants botany, and the integration of European agricultural and hydraulic technology.
- *Jiù huāng běn cǎo* 救荒本草 [[KR3d0006]] — Zhū Sù's 1406 famine-botany pharmacopoeia, methodologically pioneering for shifting from medicinal to emergency-edibility classification.
- *Nóng zhèng quán shū* 農政全書 [[KR3d0007]] — Xú Guāngqǐ's 60-juan synthesis (published posthumously 1639), the largest comprehensive Míng agricultural encyclopedia, incorporating both the entire prior tradition and Jesuit-introduced European material.
- *Tài-xī shuǐ fǎ* 泰西水法 [[KR3d0008]] — the 1612 Xú Guāngqǐ–Sabatino de Ursis hydraulic-engineering translation (the SKQS catalog title 秦西水法 is a typographical slip for 泰西), introducing European water-lifting devices, pumps, and aqueduct construction.
- *Yě cài bó lù* 野菜博錄 [[KR3d0009]] — Bào Shān's ca. 1622 wild-vegetables reference, extending the Zhū Sù famine-botany tradition.

**Qīng imperial synthesis.**
- *Qīn dìng shòu shí tōng kǎo* 欽定授時通考 [[KR3d0010]] — the 78-juan Qiánlóng-commissioned encyclopedia (1737–1742) under È'ěr-tài and Zhāng Tíngyù, the culmination of the pre-modern Chinese agricultural-administrative tradition, drawn almost entirely from the earlier works listed above.

## Important persons

- [[賈思勰]] (Jiǎ Sīxié, late-5th–6th c.) — Northern Wèi *Tài-shǒu* of Gāo-yáng-jùn whose private experience and reading of prior (now-lost) treatises produced the foundational text of the entire later genre.
- [[陳旉]] (Chén Fū, 1076–1154) — Southern Sòng private author whose treatise gave the genre its first explicit wet-rice southern register.
- [[魯明善]] (Lǔ Míngshàn, fl. 1314–1330) — the Uyghur Yuán magistrate whose handbook fixed the *agricultural-calendar* form for the magistrate-as-instructor.
- [[王禎]] (Wáng Zhēn, fl. 1295–1313) — Yuán county magistrate whose illustrated *Nóng qì tú pǔ* is the principal pre-modern Chinese visual record of agricultural implements, and whose own printing of his treatise occasioned the first detailed description of wooden movable-type technology.
- [[朱橚]] (Zhū Sù, Prince of Zhōu, 1361–1425) — fifth son of Míng Tài-zǔ, founder of the famine-botany sub-tradition through his court-sponsored *Jiù huāng běn cǎo*.
- [[徐光啟]] (Xú Guāngqǐ, 1562–1633) — the central Late-Míng figure of the division, author or co-author of two KR3d entries ([[KR3d0007]], [[KR3d0008]]) and the principal mediator between the Chinese agricultural tradition and Jesuit-introduced European technical material.
- [[西洋熊三拔]] (Sabatino de Ursis, 1575–1620) — the Italian Jesuit whose collaboration with Xú Guāngqǐ produced the *Tài-xī shuǐ fǎ*, one of the principal works of Late-Míng Chinese-Western technical encounter.
- [[鄂爾泰]] (È'ěr-tài, 1677–1745) and [[張廷玉]] (Zhāng Tíngyù, 1672–1755) — the senior Yōngzhèng-Qiánlóng ministers who directed the imperial synthesis [[KR3d0010]] that closes the SKQS *Nóng-jiā* selection.

## Topics

- **Northern dry-field vs. southern wet-rice agriculture.** The single most persistent fault-line in the genre: *Qí mín yào shù* anchors the dry-land tradition of Guānzhōng and Shāndōng; Chén Fū's *Nóng shū* establishes the Lower-Yangzi wet-rice counter-tradition; Wáng Zhēn explicitly attempts to bridge the two. Wilkinson (§35.4) treats this north-south split as the structuring axis of the entire genre.
- **Private treatise vs. imperially sponsored manual (*quànnóng* 勸農).** Two registers coexist throughout: Jiǎ Sīxié, Chén Fū, and Wáng Zhēn write as informed private observers, while *Nóng-sāng jí yào*, *Nóng-sāng yī-shí cuō yào*, and *Shòu shí tōng kǎo* are official handbooks distributed through the magistrate network. The genre is one of the clearest Chinese cases of a continuous bureaucratic-pedagogical literature.
- **Compilation, quotation, and lost-text recovery.** Much of every comprehensive treatise is verbatim quotation from earlier (and frequently now-lost) works; the genre's transmission is consequently also a major preservation channel for pre-Hàn through Táng technical literature. About a third of *Nóng-sāng jí yào* is verbatim from *Qí mín yào shù*; *Shòu shí tōng kǎo* is "almost entirely culled" from *Nóng zhèng quán shū* (Wilkinson §35.4.9).
- **The agricultural calendar (*shí lìng* 時令 / *yuè lìng* 月令).** Many treatises organise material by lunar month, inheriting from the *Yuè lìng* of the *Lǐjì* and Cuī Shí's *Sì mín yuè lìng*; Lǔ Míngshàn's [[KR3d0004]] is the cleanest exemplar of this organisational principle inside KR3d.
- **Illustrated technology.** Wáng Zhēn's *Nóng qì tú pǔ* and the related *Gēng zhī tú* 耕織圖 tradition mark the genre's distinctive use of technical illustration; many of the implements depicted were still in use in early-20th-century rural China (Wilkinson §35.4.7 citing Hommel 1937).
- **Famine relief and applied botany.** Beginning with Zhū Sù's [[KR3d0006]] and continued by Bào Shān's [[KR3d0009]], the Míng adds a famine-emergency edibility register that is methodologically distinct from the older *běn cǎo* medical-pharmacognosy tradition.
- **The Late-Míng Jesuit encounter.** *Tài-xī shuǐ fǎ* and the European-derived material in *Nóng zhèng quán shū* mark the only point in the pre-modern Chinese agricultural literature where European technical knowledge enters the canon — a localised episode, but a historically conspicuous one.
