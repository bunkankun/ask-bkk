# Wēn-bìng tiáo-biàn 溫病條辨
*Detailed Analysis of Warm Diseases*
by [[吳塘]] (Wú Táng, *hào* Jū-tōng 鞠通, 1758–1836)

## About the work

The doctrinal *summa* of the southern *wēn-bìng* school in 6 *juǎn*, drafted by Wú Jū-tōng ca. Jiā-qìng 3 (1798) and printed Jiā-qìng 18 (1813). Together with Yè Tiān-shì's *Wēn-rè lùn* ([[KR3eg001]]), Wáng Mèng-yīng's *Wēn-rè jīng-wěi* ([[KR3eg008]]), and Wú Yǒu-xìng's *Wēn-yì lùn* ([[KR3eg004]]), the *Wēn-bìng tiáo-biàn* constitutes the **canonical four** of the Qīng warm-disease tradition; modern Chinese medicine further counts it among the **Four Great Classics** of TCM alongside the *Nèi-jīng*, *Shāng-hán lùn*, and *Jīn-guì yào-luè*.

Wú's central doctrinal contribution is the **three-burner** (*sān-jiāo* 三焦) staging of warm-disease pathology — proceeding from upper to lower, from shallow to deep — a parallel to (and clinical complement of) Yè Tiān-shì's *wèi-qì-yíng-xuè* (four-fen) staging. The two schemata together provide the modern *wēn-bìng* clinical apparatus.

### Abstract

The composition history is well documented from the prefaces. Wú had a complete draft by Jiā-qìng 3 (1798); he showed it to his friend Zhèng-bǎo 徵保 in Jiā-qìng 9 (1804); after another decade of revision, the book was printed in Jiā-qìng 18 (1813). The motivation, given in Wú's own preface, was the catastrophic experience of the 1793 Beijing epidemic, during which Wú had worked through the outbreak applying the southern *wēn-bìng* doctrinal frame to a northern epidemic context — an experience that demanded a doctrinally rigorous synthesis.

The six *juǎn*: (juǎn 1) wind-warm, warm-toxin, warm-epidemic, winter-warm, *fēng-wēn 風溫* and varieties of upper-burner warm disease; (juǎn 2) middle-burner; (juǎn 3) lower-burner; (juǎn 4) miscellaneous addenda on relevant clinical scenarios; (juǎn 5) the prescription apparatus; (juǎn 6) supplementary appendix on women and children.

The prescription apparatus is the book's enduring contribution: ***Yín-qiào sǎn*** 銀翹散, ***Sāng-jú yǐn*** 桑菊飲, ***Huò-xiāng zhèng-qì sǎn*** 藿香正氣散, ***Qīng-yíng tāng*** 清營湯, ***Qīng-gōng tāng*** 清宮湯, ***Xī-jiǎo dì-huáng tāng*** 犀角地黃湯, ***Ān-gōng niú-huáng wán*** 安宮牛黃丸 — all of these remain the operational core of modern TCM warm-disease therapy and were prominently deployed in 2003 (SARS) and 2020 (COVID-19) TCM treatment protocols.

Doctrinally, the work establishes the *wēn-bìng* tradition as an autonomous medical framework standing alongside *Shāng-hán*, no longer subordinate to it. The work has generated an immense commentary tradition; the standard Mín-guó-era critical edition is by Léi Shào-yì 雷少逸's son or pupil — see [[KR3eg011]] for the duplicate KR catalog entry on a different recension.

## Other points of interest

The Wú Jū-tōng prescription apparatus has the most enduring clinical relevance of any Qīng medical text — *Yín-qiào sǎn* remains the standard TCM "common cold and influenza" prescription in modern Chinese pharmacies, and *Ān-gōng niú-huáng wán* remains the principal TCM ICU stroke prescription. The book is also notable for Wú's explicit polemic against the *Shāng-hán*-extremist position that warm disease is merely a subtype of cold damage — a position that, by 1813, was untenable.
