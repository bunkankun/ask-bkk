## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3eg is the **warm-disease** (*wēn-bìng* 溫病) and **epidemic-disease** (*wēn-yì* 瘟疫) subdivision within the Kanripo medicine class (KR3e 醫家類, *yī-jiā lèi*, "Masters → Medicine"). All 50 texts in the division are drawn from a single early-modern Chinese-medical *cóngshū* (catalog source `hxwd`) that compiles the wēn-bìng canon together with adjacent epidemic-medical literature. The texts collected here address those acute, externally-contracted febrile conditions which the late-imperial Chinese-medical tradition came to recognise as etiologically distinct from the cold-injury (*Shāng-hán* 傷寒) framework systematised by Zhāng Jī 張機 in the late Hàn — a recognition first crystallised in the late Míng with [[吳有性]]'s *Wēn-yì lùn* ([[KR3eg004]], 1642) and elaborated across the Qīng into a doctrinal apparatus parallel to, and ultimately co-equal with, the *Shāng-hán lùn* tradition. Adjacent disease categories included in the same subdivision are summer-heat (*shǔ-bìng* 暑病), miasma-malaria (*zhàng-nüè* 瘴瘧), *shā* 痧 distension disorders, cholera-like *huò-luàn* 霍亂, bubonic plague (*shǔ-yì* 鼠疫), smallpox (*dòu-kē* 痘科), dysentery (*lì-jí* 痢疾), and assorted folk-medical pestilential categories.

The chronological span runs from the late Míng (鄭全望's 1609 *Zhàng-nüè zhǐ-nán*, 張鳳逵's 1623 *Shāng-shǔ quán-shū*, and Wú Yǒuxìng's 1642 *Wēn-yì lùn*) through the entire Qīng — during which the wēn-bìng synthesis matures in the Sūzhōu-Hángzhōu axis (Yè–Xuē–Wú Jūtōng–Wáng Mèng-yīng) and is then transmitted into a contested Republican-era doctrinal field — to the late Republican period (王德宣's 1935 *Wēn-bìng zhèng-zōng*). The division includes four Edo / Bakumatsu-period Japanese *kanpō* 漢方 contributions (KR3eg022, 025, 045, 046), all transmitted back to China via 湯本求真 Tāngběn Qiúzhēn's *Huáng Hàn yī-xué cóng-shū* 皇漢醫學叢書 (Shanghai, 1936), and several late-Qīng / Republican texts preserved through 裘慶元 Qiū Qìng-yún's *Sān-sān yī-shū* 三三醫書 (Hángzhōu, 1924). Wilkinson §41.7.4 (late-imperial medicine) and especially §41.7.6 (epidemics) frame the secondary literature; Marta Hanson's *Speaking of Epidemics in Chinese Medicine* (Routledge, 2011) is the standard English-language monograph and is the principal reference for almost every per-text note in this division. T. J. Hinrichs and Linda L. Barnes (eds.), *Chinese Medicine and Healing* (Harvard Belknap, 2013), pp. 156, 203–207 provides the standard concise English overview.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The canonical four Qīng *wēn-bìng* monographs.** The mid-Qīng synthesis crystallises around four foundational texts, all preserved here together with their principal commentaries:
- *Wēn-rè lùn* 溫熱論 ([[KR3eg001]]) of [[葉桂]] Yè Tiān-shì — the kernel-document of the *wèi-qì-yíng-xuè* 衛氣營血 four-fen staging doctrine. Critically annotated by Chén Guāng-sōng in *Wēn-rè lùn jiān-zhèng* ([[KR3eg016]]).
- *Shī-rè tiáo-biàn* 濕熱條辨 ([[KR3eg020]]) of [[薛雪]] Xuē Shēng-bái — foundational treatise on damp-warm pathology; versified by [[王旭高]] Wáng Xù-gāo in *Xuē shì shī-rè lùn gē-jué* ([[KR3eg015]]).
- *Wēn-yì lùn* 溫疫論 ([[KR3eg004]]) of [[吳有性]] Wú Yǒu-xìng — the late-Míng pestilential-qi (*lì-qì* 戾氣) doctrine, the source-document of the entire wēn-yì current.
- *Wài-gǎn wēn-bìng piān* 外感溫病篇 ([[KR3eg009]]) of [[陳平伯]] Chén Píng-bó — the canonical treatment of *fēng-wēn* 風溫 (wind-warm) and the locus classicus of cool-releasing therapy.

**The mid-Qīng *summa* texts.** *Wēn-bìng tiáo-biàn* 溫病條辨 ([[KR3eg010]]; alternate recension [[KR3eg011]]) of [[吳塘]] Wú Jūtōng establishes the *sān-jiāo* 三焦 (three-burner) staging doctrine and the prescription apparatus (*Yín-qiào sǎn* 銀翹散, *Sāng-jú yǐn* 桑菊飲, *Ān-gōng niú-huáng wán* 安宮牛黃丸) that remains operational in modern TCM. *Wēn-rè jīng-wěi* 溫熱經緯 ([[KR3eg008]]) of [[王士雄]] Wáng Mèng-yīng is the mid-19th-century compendium that transmitted Yè, Xuē, Chén, and Yú Lín into the late-Qīng curriculum and remains the principal modern textbook entry-point to the canonical four.

**The Wú–Dài–Liú line of *wēn-yì* extension.** Three generations adapt Wú Yǒuxìng's pestilential-qi doctrine to successive regional and clinical contexts: [[戴天章]] Dài Tiān-zhāng's *Guǎng wēn-yì lùn* ([[KR3eg023]], 1675, Jiāng-níng / Sūzhōu) supplies the *wǔ-jiān shí-jiá* 五兼十夾 syndrome schema and the working clinical apparatus; [[劉奎]] Liú Kuí's *Sōng-fēng shuō yì* ([[KR3eg039]], 1785, Shān-dōng) extends the framework to northern epidemic conditions; the late-Qīng / Republican re-edition by [[陸懋修]] Lù Mào-xiū and [[何廉臣]] Hé Liánchén appears as *Chóng-dìng Guǎng wēn-rè lùn* ([[KR3eg006]]). The Sūzhōu physician [[周揚俊]] Zhōu Yáng-jùn's earlier *Wēn-rè shǔ-yì quán-shū* ([[KR3eg032]], 1679) provides the conceptual bridge between Wú and the mature Yè-Tiān-shì synthesis. [[余霖]] Yú Lín's *Yì-zhěn yī-dé* ([[KR3eg031]], 1785) supplies the gypsum-centred *Qīng-wēn bài-dú yǐn* 清瘟敗毒飲 prescription, the parallel and partial alternative to Wú's *dà-huáng* purgative protocol.

**The summer-heat / *shǔ-bìng* cluster.** [[張鳳逵]] Zhāng Fèng-kuí's *Zēng-dìng Yè-píng Shāng-shǔ quán-shū* ([[KR3eg005]], base text 1623) is the first Chinese monograph dedicated specifically to summer-heat as a distinct disease category, with later Yè Tiān-shì-attributed annotations and a Dào-guāng-era expansion. [[李識候]] Lǐ Shí-hòu's *Shǔ-zhèng fā-yuán* ([[KR3eg047]]) traces the *Nèi-jīng* doctrinal sources.

**The latent-pathogen (*fú-qì* / *fú-xié*) cluster.** A late-Qīng / Republican doctrinal current concentrating on warm-disease conditions caused by pathogens dormant within the body before clinical manifestation: [[柳寶詒]] Liǔ Bǎoyí's *Wēn-rè féng-yuán* ([[KR3eg003]]), [[劉吉人]] Liú Jí-rén's *Fú-xié xīn-shū* ([[KR3eg028]]), and [[蔣樹杞]] Jiǎng Shù-qǐ's *Fú-wēn zhèng-zhì shí-yàn tán* ([[KR3eg027]]) — the three are transmitted together through Qiū Qìng-yún's *Sān-sān yī-shū* (1924).

**The cholera-response cluster.** Three mid-to-late-19th-century Chinese-medical responses to the Asian cholera pandemics that reached China in the 1820s: [[王士雄]] Wáng Mèng-yīng's *Suí-xī-jū chóng-dìng huò-luàn lùn* ([[KR3eg040]], 1838 / 1862) — the heat-aetiology argument, the most influential of the three; [[田宗漢]] Tián Zōng-hàn's *Yī-jì fú-yīn lùn* ([[KR3eg014]], 1854) — the *fú-yīn* analytical category; and [[許志]] Xǔ Zhì's *Huò-luàn rán-xī shuō* ([[KR3eg042]]).

**The southern-frontier / miasma-malaria cluster.** [[鄭全望]] Zhèng Quán-wàng's *Zhàng-nüè zhǐ-nán* ([[KR3eg038]], 1609) is the principal late-Míng monograph on miasma-malaria, continuing the Sòng *Zhàng-nüè wèi-shēng fāng* tradition and introducing climatic-historical reasoning about the northward expansion of the *zhàng* zone. [[郭志邃]] Guō Zhì-suì's *Shā-zhàng yù-héng* ([[KR3eg037]], 1675) is the canonical reference for the *shā* 痧 disease category. [[宋兆淇]] Sòng Zhào-qí's *Nán-bìng bié-jiàn* ([[KR3eg029]], 1878) makes explicit the geographic-particularist argument that southern Jiāng-nán climate produces a clinical picture requiring its own doctrinal framework.

**Plague.** [[羅汝蘭]] Luó Rǔ-lán's *Shǔ-yì yuē-biān* ([[KR3eg041]], 1891–1894) is the principal Chinese-medical monograph composed in immediate response to the 1890–1894 Guǎng-dōng outbreak that, transmitted to Hong Kong in May 1894, initiated the Third Plague Pandemic.

**The Edo / Bakumatsu Japanese *kanpō* cluster.** Four Japanese contributions transmitted to China through 湯本求真 Yumoto Kyūshin (1876-194)'s *Huáng Hàn yī-xué cóng-shū* (Shanghai, 1936): [[秋吉質]] Akiyoshi Tadashi's *Wēn-yì lùn shī-píng* ([[KR3eg022]]), an Edo commentary on Wú Yǒuxìng; [[源元凱]] Minamoto Genkai's *Wēn-bìng zhī yán-jiū* ([[KR3eg025]], 1788–1810), incorporating the Tenmei 8 (1788) Kyoto epidemic and the original rigid / soft pathogen (*gō-ja* / *jū-ja* 剛邪 / 柔邪) doctrinal innovation; [[高島久貫]] Takashima Hisanuki's *Xiè-yì xīn-lùn* ([[KR3eg045]]) on the Bunsei 5 (1822) and Ansei 5 (1858) cholera waves; [[池田瑞仙]] Ikeda Zuisen's *Dòu-kē biàn-yào* ([[KR3eg046]], 1780–1816), the principal Edo treatise on smallpox, by the Bakufu Medical Academy specialist.

**The pedagogical / mnemonic cluster.** Three *gē-jué* / *gē-kuò* versifications of the canonical wēn-bìng monographs by [[王旭高]] Wáng Xù-gāo: *Xuē shì shī-rè lùn gē-jué* ([[KR3eg015]]) of *Shī-rè tiáo-biàn*; *Wú Yòu-kě wēn-yì lùn gē-kuò* ([[KR3eg024]]) of Wú Yǒuxìng; *Wēn-yì míng-biàn gē-jué* ([[KR3eg033]]) of Dài Tiān-zhāng's *Guǎng wēn-yì lùn* (under the pirated-edition title). Plus the late-Qīng pedagogical primers [[婁傑]] Lóu Jié's *Wēn-bìng zhǐ-nán* ([[KR3eg007]], 1903), [[馬宗元]] Mǎ Zōng-yuán's *Wēn-bìng biàn-zhèng* ([[KR3eg019]]), and [[周魁]] Zhōu Kuí's *Wēn-zhèng zhǐ-guī* ([[KR3eg030]]).

**The late-Qīng / Republican critique and Sino-Western integration cluster.** Three sharp internal critiques of the Wú-Jūtōng-Wáng-Mèng-yīng *wēn-bìng* mainstream from the *zūn-jīng-pài* 尊經派 (revere-the-classics) and *Shāng-hán*-ist positions: [[周岩]] Zhōu Yán's *Liù-qì gǎn-zhèng yào-yì* ([[KR3eg018]], 1880–1905); [[王德宣]] Wáng Dé-xuān's *Wēn-bìng zhèng-zōng* ([[KR3eg012]], 1935); [[沈麟]] Shěn Lín's *Chóng-dìng wēn-rè jīng-jiě* ([[KR3eg013]], Republican). Plus Sino-Western integrationist treatises: [[吳瑞甫]] Wú Ruì-fǔ's *Zhōng-xī wēn-rè chuàn-jiě* ([[KR3eg021]], 1920) and [[劉裁吾]] Liú Cái-wú's *Jìng-bìng yǔ nǎo-mó-yán quán-shū* ([[KR3eg044]]).

## Important persons

- [[葉桂]] Yè Tiān-shì (1666–1745) — the foundational mid-Qīng Sūzhōu physician of the *wēn-bìng* tradition; *Wēn-rè lùn* ([[KR3eg001]]) is the doctrinal kernel of the entire later canon.
- [[薛雪]] Xuē Shēng-bái (1681–1770) — Yè's contemporary in Sūzhōu; the foundational treatise on damp-warm ([[KR3eg020]]).
- [[吳有性]] Wú Yǒuxìng (1582–1652) — the late-Míng author of the pestilential-qi doctrine ([[KR3eg004]]); the most-commented-upon source-text in the division.
- [[吳塘]] Wú Jūtōng (1758–1836) — the *summa*-author of the southern *wēn-bìng* school, *Wēn-bìng tiáo-biàn* ([[KR3eg010]]); his prescription apparatus is the operational core of modern TCM warm-disease therapy.
- [[王士雄]] Wáng Mèng-yīng (1808–1868) — the mid-19th-century editor and theorist who consolidated the canonical four into the standard *Wēn-rè jīng-wěi* ([[KR3eg008]]) and produced the principal Chinese-medical treatise on cholera ([[KR3eg040]]).
- [[戴天章]] Dài Tiān-zhāng (1644–1722) — the principal early-Qīng extender of Wú Yǒuxìng's framework into a working clinical apparatus ([[KR3eg023]]).
- [[陳平伯]] Chén Píng-bó (late Qián-lóng / Jiā-qìng) — author of the canonical *fēng-wēn* monograph ([[KR3eg009]]).
- [[余霖]] Yú Lín (1723–1795) — author of the gypsum-centred *Yì-zhěn yī-dé* ([[KR3eg031]]); his *Qīng-wēn bài-dú yǐn* prescription remains in active TCM use today.
- [[周揚俊]] Zhōu Yáng-jùn (late 17th c.) — the early-Qīng Sūzhōu transitional figure between Wú Yǒuxìng and Yè Tiān-shì ([[KR3eg032]]).
- [[郭志邃]] Guō Zhì-suì (mid-17th c.) — the Jiā-xīng physician who established the *shā* category as a doctrinally rigorous clinical entity ([[KR3eg037]]).
- [[雷豐]] Léi Fēng (1833–1888) — the Qú-zhōu physician of the late-Qīng Zhèjiāng wing of the *wēn-bìng* tradition ([[KR3eg002]]).
- [[柳寶詒]] Liǔ Bǎoyí (1842–1901) — the principal late-Qīng theorist of latent-qi (*fú-qì*) warm disease ([[KR3eg003]]).
- [[王旭高]] Wáng Xù-gāo (1798–1862) — the Wúxī physician whose *gē-jué* / *gē-kuò* mnemonic versifications ([[KR3eg015]], [[KR3eg024]], [[KR3eg033]]) defined the standard mid-Qīng pedagogical idiom.
- [[何廉臣]] Hé Liánchén (1861–1929) — the leading Shàoxīng-school (*Yuè-pài* 越派) editor of the late-Qīng / Republican period; principal contributor to [[KR3eg006]] and [[KR3eg034]].
- [[羅汝蘭]] Luó Rǔ-lán (late Qīng) — the Lián-jiāng physician whose *Shǔ-yì yuē-biān* ([[KR3eg041]]) documents the 1890–1894 Guǎng-dōng plague onset.
- [[鄭全望]] Zhèng Quán-wàng (late Míng) — the Jiāng-xī physician who composed the principal late-Míng miasma-malaria monograph ([[KR3eg038]]).
- [[源元凱]] Minamoto Genkai (Edo) — the principal Edo-period *kanpō* warm-disease theorist; his rigid / soft pathogen distinction ([[KR3eg025]]) is the most substantial Japanese doctrinal contribution in the division.
- [[池田瑞仙]] Ikeda Zuisen (1734–1816) — the Bakufu Medical Academy smallpox specialist whose *Dòu-kē biàn-yào* ([[KR3eg046]]) is one of the principal Edo *kanpō* texts on smallpox.
- [[王德宣]] Wáng Dé-xuān (民國) — the Hú-nán State Medical College professor whose *Wēn-bìng zhèng-zōng* ([[KR3eg012]], 1935) is the sharpest internal Republican-era critique of the Qīng *wēn-bìng* canon.
- [[吳瑞甫]] Wú Ruì-fǔ (1872–1952) — the Xià-mén physician whose *Zhōng-xī wēn-rè chuàn-jiě* ([[KR3eg021]], 1920) is the principal early-Republican Sino-Western integrationist warm-disease treatise; principal warm-disease text of the Mín-nán medical tradition.

## Topics

- **The *Shāng-hán* / *wēn-bìng* relation.** The defining doctrinal question of the entire division: is warm-disease etiologically distinct from cold-injury (the position of Wú Yǒuxìng, Yè Tiān-shì, Wú Jūtōng, and the southern *wēn-bìng* mainstream), or is it a sub-category within the *Shāng-hán* framework (the position of the *zūn-jīng-pài* in the late Qīng and of the Republican-era *Shāng-hán*-ists Zhōu Yán, Wáng Dé-xuān, and Shěn Lín)? Wáng Mèng-yīng's *Wēn-rè jīng-wěi* ([[KR3eg008]]) attempts to retire the polemic by treating both as valid frameworks for different etiological situations; [[陸子賢]] Lù Zǐ-xián's *Liù-yīn tiáo-biàn* ([[KR3eg017]]) is the principal late-Qīng cold-warm integrationist statement.
- **Pestilential-qi (*lì-qì* 戾氣 / *zá-qì* 雜氣) doctrine.** Wú Yǒuxìng's claim that epidemic warm disease is caused by a distinct pestilential agent — entering through mouth and nose, lodging in the *mó-yuán* 膜原 — is the foundational theoretical innovation of the late-Míng / early-Qīng *wēn-yì* tradition. Dài Tiān-zhāng ([[KR3eg023]]) supplies the working clinical apparatus; Liú Kuí ([[KR3eg039]]) extends it northward; Minamoto Genkai ([[KR3eg025]]) refines and partially refutes it from a Japanese clinical position.
- **The four-fen versus three-burner staging.** Yè Tiān-shì's *wèi-qì-yíng-xuè* 衛氣營血 staging (in [[KR3eg001]]) and Wú Jūtōng's *sān-jiāo* 三焦 staging (in [[KR3eg010]]) constitute the parallel staging systems of the mature Qīng *wēn-bìng* doctrinal apparatus. They are not in competition but provide complementary clinical frames.
- **Latent-qi versus newly-contracted (*fú-qì* 伏氣 vs *xīn-gǎn* 新感) pathogens.** The principal interpretive divide running through the late-Qīng *wēn-bìng* current: the southern Sūzhōu line (Yè–Wú Jūtōng–Wáng Mèng-yīng) emphasises newly-contracted pathogens; the latent-pathogen line (Liǔ Bǎoyí [[KR3eg003]], Liú Jí-rén [[KR3eg028]], Léi Fēng [[KR3eg002]], Jiǎng Shù-qǐ [[KR3eg027]]) emphasises pathogens dormant within the body and emerging seasonally.
- **Geographic and climatic particularism.** The argument that southern Jiāng-nán climate produces clinical pictures requiring distinct doctrinal frameworks — implicit in Yè–Xuē–Wú Jūtōng, made explicit in [[宋兆淇]] Sòng Zhào-qí's *Nán-bìng bié-jiàn* ([[KR3eg029]]). The geographical argument extends to the *zhàng* / miasma category as the disease-imagination of the southern frontier (Zhèng Quán-wàng [[KR3eg038]]; cf. Yang 2010, Baldanza 2022 in Wilkinson §41.7.6) and to the long-term southward shift of climatic boundaries (Lee et al. 2017).
- **Doctrinal response to novel epidemics.** The successive arrival of novel disease categories — cholera (1820s), bubonic plague (1890s) — provided clinical tests of the Qīng *wēn-bìng* apparatus. Wáng Mèng-yīng's heat-aetiology argument for cholera ([[KR3eg040]]) and Luó Rǔ-lán's wēn-bìng-framed response to plague ([[KR3eg041]]) are the principal cases. Hanson 2011 reads the entire division as a study in the *geographic imagination* through which a doctrinal tradition expanded its categorical reach.
- **Cool-acrid release and gypsum-centred therapy.** The signature therapeutic principles of the wēn-bìng school: cool-acrid (rather than hot-acrid) surface-release (locus classicus in Chén Píng-bó [[KR3eg009]]); heavy-gypsum *qì*-level heat-clearing (locus classicus in Yú Lín [[KR3eg031]]); *Yín-qiào sǎn* 銀翹散 and *Sāng-jú yǐn* 桑菊飲 as the standard surface-cooling prescriptions (Wú Jūtōng [[KR3eg010]]).
- **Editorial transmission, pseudepigraphy, and pirated editions.** The late-Qīng wēn-bìng canon is constituted as much by editorial transmission as by primary composition. Wáng Mèng-yīng's flagging of doubtful Chén / Xuē attribution ([[KR3eg009]], [[KR3eg020]]) is one of the locus classicus *kǎo-jù* 考據 cases in Chinese medical philology; the Dài Tiān-zhāng / *Wēn-yì míng-biàn* false-attribution-and-correction story ([[KR3eg023]], [[KR3eg033]]) is another. The successive editorial layers in *Chóng-dìng Guǎng wēn-rè lùn* ([[KR3eg006]]) — Dài 1675, Lù Mào-xiū 1864, Hé Liánchén 1909 — exemplify the late-Qīng practice of continuous re-edition.
- **Sino-Japanese exchange.** Edo and Bakumatsu Japanese *kanpō* texts ([[KR3eg022]], [[KR3eg025]], [[KR3eg045]], [[KR3eg046]]) are transmitted to Republican-era China through Tāngběn Qiúzhēn's 1936 *Huáng Hàn yī-xué cóng-shū*; the round-trip is one of the more striking East-Asian medical-bibliographic patterns of the early 20th century.
- **Sino-Western integration in the Republican era.** The wēn-bìng tradition's encounter with bacteriological epidemiology and Western internal medicine: [[吳瑞甫]] Wú Ruì-fǔ ([[KR3eg021]]) on warm-disease, [[劉裁吾]] Liú Cái-wú ([[KR3eg044]]) on meningitis as *jìng-bìng* 痙病, [[何廉臣]] Hé Liánchén ([[KR3eg034]]) on damp-warm seasonal epidemics. The polemical-defensive counterpoint is in [[蔣樹杞]] Jiǎng Shù-qǐ ([[KR3eg027]]) and [[沈麟]] Shěn Lín ([[KR3eg013]]).

## Timeline

- **1609** — *Zhàng-nüè zhǐ-nán* 瘴瘧指南 [[KR3eg038]] — late-Míng monograph on miasma-malaria, occasioned by the 1602 epidemic; opens the division chronologically.
- **1623–1854** — *Zēng-dìng Yè-píng Shāng-shǔ quán-shū* 增訂葉評傷暑全書 [[KR3eg005]] — Zhāng Fèng-kuí's first Chinese monograph on summer-heat as a distinct disease category, with later Yè-attributed annotations and a Dào-guāng expansion.
- **1642** — *Wēn-yì lùn* 溫疫論 [[KR3eg004]] — Wú Yǒuxìng's response to the 1641 Chóng-zhēn epidemic; foundation of the entire later wēn-bìng / wēn-yì tradition.
- **1675** — *Guǎng wēn-yì lùn* 廣瘟疫論 [[KR3eg023]] — Dài Tiān-zhāng's clinicisation of Wú Yǒuxìng; the *wǔ-jiān shí-jiá* schema.
- **1675–1678** — *Shā-zhàng yù-héng* 痧脹玉衡 [[KR3eg037]] — Guō Zhì-suì establishes the *shā* category as a doctrinally rigorous clinical entity.
- **1675–1909** — *Chóng-dìng Guǎng wēn-rè lùn* 重訂廣溫熱論 [[KR3eg006]] — Dài Tiān-zhāng's text in successive Lù-Mào-xiū / Hé-Liánchén editorial layers.
- **1679** — *Wēn-rè shǔ-yì quán-shū* 溫熱暑疫全書 [[KR3eg032]] — Zhōu Yáng-jùn's early-Qīng Sūzhōu treatise; the conceptual bridge between Wú Yǒuxìng and Yè Tiān-shì.
- **1740–1770** — *Shī-rè tiáo-biàn* 濕熱條辨 [[KR3eg020]] — Xuē Shēng-bái's foundational treatise on damp-warm pathology.
- **1746–1792** — *Wēn-rè lùn* 溫熱論 [[KR3eg001]] — Yè Tiān-shì's foundational text; the kernel of the four-fen staging doctrine.
