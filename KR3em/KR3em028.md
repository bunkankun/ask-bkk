# Hóu-kē jí-yè 喉科集腋
*Gleanings on Laryngology*
by [[沈青芝]] Shěn Qīng-zhī (late-Qīng physician of Lì-yáng 溧陽).

## About the work

A two-*juǎn* late-Qīng laryngology compendium — *shàng-juǎn* covering theory, needling technique, and the diagnostic typology of severe and acute throat conditions, *xià-juǎn* covering the prescriptions. The author's stated aim is the practical preservation of life in the face of epidemic *bái-hóu fēng* 白喉風 outbreaks, supplemented with a comprehensive review of the inherited laryngology corpus. The text combines orthodox doctrinal teaching (the *yān-hóu* anatomical division, the *zhēn-fǎ* 針法 at the standard hand- and face-acupoints — *shǎo-shāng* 少商, *shāng-yáng* 商陽, *zhōng-chōng* 中衝, *guān-chōng* 關衝, *shǎo-chōng* 少衝, *jiá-chē* 頰車, *qū-chí* 曲池, *hé-gǔ* 合谷 — with a *zhú-rì xuè-shén* 逐日血神 daily-prohibition calendar restricting the use of each acupoint), the typology of severe conditions (*hóu-bì* 喉痹, *hóu-fēng* 喉風, *chán-hóu fēng* 纏喉風, *bái-hóu fēng* 白喉風, *làn-hóu shā* 爛喉痧, *yīn-xū hóu-lóng* 陰虛喉嚨), and a detailed discussion of the 19th-century *bái-hóu* epidemics.

### Prefaces

The work is prefaced by a *xiǎo-xù* 小序 by Fāng Yàn-zhāo 方燕昭 (*zì* Bó-róng 伯融, of Dìng-yuǎn 定遠), the author's friend of ten years' standing, dated *Guāng-xù* 7 *xīn-sì* 辛巳, first month of autumn (1881), written "at the Wēn-cuì xuān 溫翠軒 of his temporary residence at Hán-shàng" 邗上 (Yáng-zhōu 揚州). Fāng records that he had repeatedly asked Shěn for his laryngology methods without success until Shěn, declaring himself aged, finally entrusted him with the volume saying "the heart's-blood of my entire life is in this." A subsequent self-preface (*zì xù* 自序) by Shěn Qīng-zhī himself, dated *Guāng-xù* *gēng-yín* 庚寅 (1890) mid-winter, signed "from the Jú-jǐng Shān-fáng 橘井山房 at Guǎng-líng 廣陵", recounts the *Qián-lóng* 54 (1789) outbreak of *bái-hóu fēng* in the north, the *Xián-fēng* 6 (1856) outbreak that swept both north and south, and the catastrophic *Guāng-xù* *gēng-yín* (1890) outbreak in the Yáng-zhōu region in which "of every ten or twenty cases, only two or three survived." Shěn reports having declined an offer of payment from Fāng for the publication of the work, electing to release it freely to the world.

### Abstract

*Hóu-kē jí-yè* is one of the major late-Qīng laryngology compendia, and — alongside [[KR3em027]] *Bái-hóu quán-shēng jí* of [[李紀方]] (1882) — one of the principal indigenous-medical responses to the diphtheria epidemics that swept south China in the 1880s and 1890s. Shěn Qīng-zhī's etiological theory is distinctive in late-imperial laryngology: he argues that *bái-hóu fēng* arises when the "lung — which belongs to metal and whose colour is white — falls under conditions of summer drought combined with secondary yīn-damp infection, like dry wood that grows mushrooms after long time"; hence "wind-drying drugs do not save, only cooling-moistening prescriptions can preserve life." He explicitly criticizes the use of *shēng-tí xīn-wēn fēng-zào suān-sè* 升提辛溫風燥酸澀 (uplifting/pungent-warm/wind-drying/sour-astringent) classes of drug in *bái-hóu*, and grounds his own prescribing in the transformations of the *lóng-dǎn xiè-gān tāng* 龍膽瀉肝湯, *bái-hǔ tāng* 白虎湯, *pǔ-jì xiāo-dú yǐn* 普濟消毒飲, *xī-jiǎo dì-huáng tāng* 犀角地黃湯, and *huáng-lián jiě-dú tāng* 黃連解毒湯, modified to exclude exactly those four prohibited drug-classes.

Shěn Qīng-zhī is not recorded in CBDB and his life-dates are not securely fixed. He was a senior physician of Lì-yáng 溧陽 (Jiāng-sū) practising in the Zhèn-jiāng / Yáng-zhōu region; his self-preface implies a working life of "several decades" reaching from the 1850s through the 1890 epidemic. The composition window is therefore set at 1860 (a defensible *terminus a quo* for sustained adult practice) – 1890 (the date of his self-preface, the latest stratum in the received text). The preface by Fāng Yàn-zhāo gives a *terminus ante quem* of 1881 for an earlier draft of the work, but the integrated *shàng-xià* 上下 two-*juǎn* recension carrying the 1890 *gēng-yín* preface is what survives in the hxwd witness.

## Other points of interest

The text's "*shí-nán* 十難 [ten difficulties]" discussion — listing ten clinical problems in differential diagnosis of *bái-hóu* — is cited approvingly as "a method that may stand as the model for later students." Shěn's pointed dating of the three major *bái-hóu* epidemics he had heard of or lived through (1789, 1856, 1890) is among the more precise epidemic-chronicling passages in the late-Qīng medical corpus.
