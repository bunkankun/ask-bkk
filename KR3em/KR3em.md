# KR3em — Wǔguān kē 五官科

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3em is the *wǔguān kē* 五官科 ("five sense-organs") sub-division of `KR3e` 醫家類 (Medicine), itself the principal medical sub-section of `KR3` 子部 (Masters). It collects late-imperial and early-Republican Chinese specialty monographs on diseases of the **eye** (*yǎnkē* 眼科), the **throat and larynx** (*hóukē* 喉科), and the **mouth and teeth** (*kǒuchǐ* 口齒). The selection criterion is editorial rather than canonical: every one of the 39 cataloged entries carries the source code `hxwd`, i.e. the *Hǎiwài huíliú zhōngyī shànběn gǔjí cóngshū* 海外回流中醫善本古籍叢書 ("Returned-from-Overseas Collectanea of Chinese Medical Rare Books and Old Editions"), a modern reprint series of rare medical books — many of them recovered from Japanese and Korean libraries — that fall outside the *Sìkù quánshū* canon. There is no WYG-edition material in this sub-division and no *tíyào* sections in the per-text notes; per project convention the front-matter section is *Prefaces* instead.

Chronologically the collection spans the YuánMíng transition (the *Yuánjī qǐwēi* 原機啟微 of 1370–1391, [[KR3em001]]) to the final years of the Qīng and the early Republic (the *Hóukē jiāxùn* 喉科家訓 of 1918–1924, [[KR3em038]]; the *Hóukē jīnyuè quánshū* 喉科金鑰全書 of *Xuāntǒng* 3 / 1911, [[KR3em034]]). The bulk of the corpus is Qīng (28 of 39 entries). Three doctrinal currents organise the field: (1) the Indo-Buddhist couching-for-cataract surgical tradition repackaged in the *Lóngmù lùn* 龍木論 group of pseudo-Sūn Sīmiǎo eye texts; (2) the JīnYuán *qìxuè / zàngfǔ* aetiological synthesis of the Dānxī school as applied to ophthalmology by [[倪維德]] Ní Wéidé and re-canonised in the imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* 醫宗金鑑 ([[KR3em007]]); and (3) the family-and-region transmissions of throat medicine that crystallised in the Yóu 尤, Zhāng 張, Jiāo 焦, and Zhèng 鄭 lineages of the JiāngNán *qīshíèr zhǒng yānhóu* (72-throat-disorder) genre, and which were forced into a programmatic recasting by the late-nineteenth-century diphtheria (*báihóu* 白喉) epidemics. Wilkinson (Section 41, "Medicine"; §41.3) treats specialty disciplines like ophthalmology and laryngology as a late development of the secular medical bibliography, paralleling the rise of clan-transmitted *jiāchuán* 家傳 medical practice in MíngQīng JiāngNán; Hinrichs and Barnes (2013, pp. 78, 109–113, 196, 343–345) supply the scholarly anchor for the Indo-Buddhist surgical strand and for the late-Qīng diphtheria literature.

## Important texts and text clusters

**Ophthalmology — JīnYuán aetiological synthesis.**
- *Yuánjī qǐwēi* 原機啟微 [[KR3em001]] (YuánMíng; 倪維德, 1370–1391) — earliest fully extant Chinese ophthalmology monograph organised aetiologically rather than symptomatically, the principal carrier of the Dānxī school in eye medicine.
- *Yīzōng jīnjiàn · Yǎnkē xīnfǎ yàojué* 醫宗金鑑·眼科心法要訣 [[KR3em007]] (Qīng; 吳謙 et al., 1742–1749) — the imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* ophthalmology section in verse-mnemonic form, the standard Qīng curriculum text.

**Ophthalmology — *Lóngmù lùn* pseudo-Sūn / Nāgārjuna cluster.** A four-text complex transmitting the Indo-Buddhist couching-surgery tradition under Sūn Sīmiǎo's borrowed prestige:
- *Mìchuán yǎnkē Lóngmù lùn* 秘傳眼科龍木論 [[KR3em003]] (Míng print; attributed to [[龍樹]] Nāgārjuna and [[葆光道人]]) — the core *Lóngmù* text;
- *Yínhǎi jīngwēi* 銀海精微 [[KR3em011]] (attrib. [[孫思邈]], received form SòngMíng) — the 81-disorder companion (a parallel WYG recension is at `[[KR3e0014]]`);
- *Míngmù zhìbǎo* 明目至寶 [[KR3em002]] (Míng) and *Yǎnkē mìjué* 眼科秘訣 [[KR3em004]] (early Qīng) — affiliated 72-disorder formularies.

**Ophthalmology — surgical and Wú-region.** *Shěnshì yáohán* 審視瑤函 [[KR3em010]] (late Míng; 傅仁宇) is the most operatively oriented late-Míng eye treatise, preserving golden-needle cataract couching procedure in detail.

**Ophthalmology — Qīng synthesis and pedagogy.** *Mùjīng dàchéng* 目經大成 [[KR3em006]] (黃庭鏡, 1741–1805) and *Yínhǎi zhǐnán* 銀海指南 [[KR3em012]] (顧錫, 1807–1810) are eighteenth- and nineteenth-century syntheses of the inherited doctrine for clinical use.

**Ophthalmology — Japanese kanpō outlier.** *Yǎnkē jǐnnáng* 眼科錦囊 [[KR3em005]] (本荘普一 / Honjō Fuichi, 1831–1837) is the only late-Edo *kanpō* 漢方 work in the subdivision.

**Laryngology — Xīn'ān 新安 / Chónglóu 重樓 tradition.**
- *Chónglóu yùyuè* 重樓玉鑰 [[KR3em017]] (Qīng; 鄭宏綱, 1750–1787) — founding work of the *yǎngyīn qīngfèi* 養陰清肺 doctrine of throat treatment;
- *Chónglóu yùyuè xùbiān* 重樓玉鑰續編 [[KR3em018]] (Qīng; 方成培) — the continuation that consolidated the doctrine.

**Laryngology — family-and-clan transmissions.**
- Yóu-clan 尤氏: *Yóushì hóuzhèng zhǐnán* 尤氏喉症指南 [[KR3em021]] (Míng; 尤仲仁) and *Yóushì hóukē mìshū* 尤氏喉科秘書 [[KR3em022]] (Qīng; 尤乘) — the Sūzhōu Yóu lineage;
- Jiāo-clan 焦氏: *Jiāoshì hóukē zhěnmì* 焦氏喉科枕秘 [[KR3em023]] — canonical 72-disorder framework;
- Bāo-clan 包氏: *Bāoshì hóuzhèng jiābǎo* 包氏喉證家寶 [[KR3em025]] — family-manual reception of the same framework;
- Zhāng Zōngliáng's *Hóukē zhǐzhǎng* 喉科指掌 [[KR3em024]] (1700–1757) — the most-reprinted Qīng laryngology, with *liùwèi tāng* 六味湯 as its base prescription, and its companion *Yānhóu mìjí* 咽喉秘集 [[KR3em026]].

**Laryngology — *báihóu* 白喉 (diphtheria) cluster.** A late-Qīng epidemic-response corpus, recognised in the secondary literature (Hinrichs and Barnes 2013, pp. 343–345) as the principal indigenous response to nineteenth-century diphtheria outbreaks:
- *Báihóu tiáobiàn* 白喉條辨 [[KR3em020]] (陳葆善, 1893–1909) — Wēnzhōu *wēnbìng*-framework treatise;
- *Báihóu quánshēng jí* 白喉全生集 [[KR3em027]] (李紀方, 1860–1882) — landmark Húnán manual, exported as the "Chinese specific" reported in the Australian medical press in 1874;
- *Hóukē jíyè* 喉科集腋 [[KR3em028]] (沈青芝) — the lung-as-metal aetiology of diphtheria;
- *Báihóu jiéyào hébiān* 白喉捷要合編 [[KR3em031]] (1867–1907) — combined essentials;
- *Báihóu biànzhèng* 白喉辨證 [[KR3em032]] (王裕慶, 1880–1902) — polemical Húnán anti-cooling text in explicit dialogue with [[KR3em027]].

**Laryngology — late Qīng / Republican syntheses and survivors.** *Hóukē dàchéng* 喉科大成 [[KR3em033]] (馬渭齡), *Hóukē jīnyuè quánshū* 喉科金鑰全書 [[KR3em034]] (袁仁賢, 1911), *Hóukē xīnfǎ* 喉科心法 [[KR3em036]], and *Hóukē jiāxùn* 喉科家訓 [[KR3em038]] (刁步忠, 1918–1924). *Chóngdìng nángmì hóushū* 重訂囊秘喉書 [[KR3em030]] and *Yānhóu màizhèng tōnglùn* 咽喉脈證通論 [[KR3em029]] (校刊 by [[許槤]]) preserve earlier anonymous material recovered in the Qīng.

**Mouth and teeth (口齒).** *Kǒuchǐ lèiyào* 口齒類要 [[KR3em039]] (Míng; 薛己, 1528–1559) is the canonical late-imperial treatise and the only major one in the corpus; *Zǒumǎ jígān zhēnfāng* 走馬急疳真方 [[KR3em040]] (pseudo-Sòng) is a short pseudepigraphic paediatric-cancrum-oris formulary. *Hóushé bèiyào mìzhǐ* 喉舌備要秘旨 [[KR3em041]] is a hybrid throat-and-tongue work.

**Cough as a throat-adjacent topic.** *Kélùn jīngzhǐ* 咳論經旨 [[KR3em037]] (凌德, late Qīng) is filed under throat medicine but is doctrinally a *xūsǔn* / *tányǐn* digest synthesising *Wēnbìng* and *Jīnguì* sources.

## Important persons

- [[倪維德]] Ní Wéidé (1303–1377): Wúzhōng physician of the YuánMíng transition; carrier of the Dānxī school's ophthalmological line; author of [[KR3em001]] and the foundational figure for aetiological eye medicine.
- [[孫思邈]] Sūn Sīmiǎo (581–682): the Táng *yàowáng* whose authorship is borrowed by the *Lóngmù* eye-text cluster ([[KR3em002]], [[KR3em004]], [[KR3em011]]); historically present in the division only as a pseudepigraphic anchor.
- [[龍樹]] Lóngshù (Nāgārjuna): Indo-Buddhist eponymous "author" of [[KR3em003]], standing for the Indian couching-surgery tradition that entered China via Buddhist translations.
- [[傅仁宇]] Fù Rényǔ (late Míng): Nánjīng surgical ophthalmologist; author of [[KR3em010]].
- [[吳謙]] Wú Qiān (1689–1748): chief compiler of the imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn*; under his name the ophthalmology section [[KR3em007]] entered the standard Qīng medical curriculum.
- [[黃庭鏡]] Huáng Tíngjìng (1741–1805): author of the eighteenth-century ophthalmological synthesis [[KR3em006]].
- [[薛己]] Xuē Jǐ (1487–1559): the great Jiā-jìng-era Sūzhōu *wēnbǔ* physician; author of [[KR3em039]] (the canonical mouth-and-teeth treatise) and re-editor of [[KR3em001]].
- [[鄭宏綱]] Zhèng Hónggāng (Méijiàn 梅澗, 1727–1787): Xīn'ān physician; founder of the *yǎngyīn qīngfèi* doctrine in throat medicine through [[KR3em017]].
- [[方成培]] Fāng Chéngpéi: continuator of the *Chónglóu* tradition with [[KR3em018]].
- [[尤仲仁]] Yóu Zhòngrén (Míng) and [[尤乘]] Yóu Chéng (Qīng): founders and continuators of the Sūzhōu Yóu-clan throat-medicine line ([[KR3em021]], [[KR3em022]]).
- [[張宗良]] Zhāng Zōngliáng (mid-Qīng): author of [[KR3em024]], the most-circulated Qīng laryngology, and probable redactor of [[KR3em026]].
- [[李紀方]] Lǐ Jìfāng (mid-late Qīng): Húnán physician; author of the landmark anti-diphtheria *Báihóu quánshēng jí* [[KR3em027]].
- [[陳葆善]] Chén Bǎoshàn (late Qīng / Republican): Wēnzhōu physician; author of the *wēnbìng*-framework diphtheria treatise [[KR3em020]].
- [[許槤]] Xǔ Lián (1787–1862): philologist-official and editor; recovered and printed the anonymous *Yānhóu màizhèng tōnglùn* [[KR3em029]].
- [[本荘普一]] Honjō Fuichi (early-19th-c. Japan): late-Edo *kanpō* ophthalmologist; author of [[KR3em005]] and the division's only Japanese contributor.

## Topics

- **Indigenous vs. Indo-Buddhist ophthalmology.** The *wǔlún bākuò* 五輪八廓 ("five wheels and eight quadrants") topographic symbolism of the *Lóngmù* tradition versus the JīnYuán *zàngfǔ / qìxuè* aetiology promoted by [[KR3em001]] and canonised by [[KR3em007]]. The struggle is explicit in the editorial framing of [[KR3em001]]: "the eye is the chief manifestation of the qì and blood of the five viscera and six bowels — surely it cannot be confined to the vulgar *wǔlún bākuò* schema alone."
- **Indo-Buddhist couching for cataract.** Operative needle-couching (*jīnzhēn bōzhàng* 金針撥障) for internal cataract enters the Chinese literate medical record through the Buddhist-translation channel of the Táng; by the late Míng the *Yínhǎi jīngwēi* [[KR3em011]], the *Lóngmù lùn* [[KR3em003]], and the *Yuánjī qǐwēi* [[KR3em001]] all describe the procedure in physician-controlled language. Discussed in Hinrichs and Barnes (2013), pp. 78 and 196.
- **The 72-throat-disorder genre (*qīshíèr zhǒng yānhóu zhèng* 七十二種咽喉症).** A taxonomy that organises [[KR3em023]], [[KR3em024]], [[KR3em025]], [[KR3em026]], and their relatives, with *liùwèi tāng* 六味湯 as the universal opening prescription. The genre is a vehicle of family- and region-transmitted clinical knowledge rather than a state-sanctioned curriculum.
- **The *báihóu* / *wēnbìng* controversy.** Late-Qīng diphtheria epidemics force a doctrinal split between cooling/yīn-nourishing *yǎngyīn qīngfèi* (descended from [[KR3em017]] and developed in [[KR3em027]] and [[KR3em020]]) and warming/expelling readings ([[KR3em032]]). The corpus is a textbook case of indigenous epidemic doctrine evolving under pressure from a specific bacterial pathogen, decades before its identification by Klebs and Loeffler.
- **Family transmission (*jiāchuán* 家傳) and regional schools.** Throat medicine in particular is structured by lineage: the Yóu in Sūzhōu, the Bāo and Jiāo, the Diāo, the Húnán Lǐ line, the Xīn'ān ZhèngFāng line. Wilkinson §41 treats this as the dominant social form of specialty medicine in the late empire.
- **Pseudepigraphy and prestige-borrowing.** Sūn Sīmiǎo, Nāgārjuna, and "the Yellow Perfected" (*Huáng zhēnrén* 黃真人, [[KR3em019]]) serve as borrowed names for technically expert but socially marginal traditions. Editorial recovery work — Xǔ Lián for [[KR3em029]], Yáng Lóngjiǔ for [[KR3em030]] — frequently restores anonymous YuánMíng material to scholarly circulation through Qīng prefaces.
- **Pedagogical verse and mnemonic.** Both [[KR3em007]] and many of the throat manuals encapsulate their doctrine in *yàojué* 要訣 (rhymed mnemonic formulae), a pedagogical genre Wilkinson (§41.3.1, §29.4.9.1) treats as central to the transmission of imperial-period technical knowledge.
- **Cross-divisional reception.** [[KR3em007]] is the *yǎnkē* fascicle of the imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* whose body resides at `[[KR3e0090]]`; [[KR3em011]] has a parallel WYG recension at `[[KR3e0014]]`; [[KR3em039]] is the *kǒuchǐ* monograph excerpted from [[薛己]]'s collected medical works at `[[KR3e0070]]`. The subdivision functions as a specialty annex to the main `KR3e` medical canon, not as a free-standing tradition.
