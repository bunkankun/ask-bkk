# KR1f 孝經類 — Classics, Classic of Filial Piety

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR1f gathers the *Xiàojīng* 孝經 (*Classic of Filial Piety*) and its commentarial tradition, plus the closely-modelled apocryphal *Zhōngjīng* 忠經 (*Classic of Loyalty*). It is the sixth subdivision of the *jīngbù* 經部 (KR1, Classics) and corresponds exactly to the *Xiàojīng* branch of the *Sìkù quánshū* classification (Wilkinson §28.5, [Box 161]). The canonical work itself is the shortest of the *Thirteen Classics* — c. 1,800 graphs in 18 short *zhāng* 章 (Wilkinson §28.4.4) — a Confucian–Zēngzǐ dialogue on filial piety (*xiào* 孝) as the root of moral order and the foundation of government. From the Hàn through the late Qīng it was the standard primer with which children in China, Korea, Japan, and Vietnam began their classical education (Wilkinson §28.4.4), and it survives in two competing recensions whose dispute organizes the entire division: a *jīnwén* 今文 ("Current Script") recension in 18 *zhāng* transmitted by Yán Zhī 顏芝 and canonized by the Táng emperor Xuánzōng in 722–743, and a *gǔwén* 古文 ("Ancient Script") recension in 22 *zhāng* attributed to Kǒng Ānguó 孔安國 and preserved largely in Japan (Wilkinson §28.7.1; see [[KR1f0001]], [[KR1f0003]]).

Three layers shape the division's commentarial history: (1) a Táng imperial layer crystallizing the *jīnwén* recension under Xuánzōng's *yùzhù* 御注 of 743, with Lù Démíng's 陸德明 phonetic glosses and Xíng Bǐng's 邢昺 *zhèngyì* 正義 of 1001 producing the *Shísān jīng zhùshū* form ([[KR1f0002]], [[KR1f0004]]); (2) a Sòng–Yuán Daoxue layer in which Sīmǎ Guāng and Fàn Zǔyǔ revived the *gǔwén* ([[KR1f0005]]) and Zhū Xī's *Kānwù* 刋誤 of 1186 ([[KR1f0006]]) restructured the text into "1 *jīng* + *zhuàn*" form and excised 223 characters, transmitted onward by Dǒng Dǐng ([[KR1f0007]]) and Wú Chéng ([[KR1f0008]]); (3) a Míng–Qīng layer in which late-Míng philosophical readings ([[KR1f0009]], [[KR1f0010]]), two Qīng imperial commentaries ([[KR1f0011]], [[KR1f0012]]), Qīng kǎozhèng polemics ([[KR1f0013]]), and the Edo Japanese rediscovery of the Kǒng-attributed *gǔwén zhuàn* ([[KR1f0003]]) all contend with one another. The *Xiàojīng* is also the only Confucian classic annotated by four reigning emperors — Táng Xuánzōng, Sòng Zhēnzōng, Qīng Shùnzhì, and Qīng Yōngzhèng — paralleling the imperial annotation of the *Dàodé jīng* 道德經 (Wilkinson §65451).

## Important texts and text clusters

- **The canonical text.** *Xiàojīng* 孝經 [[KR1f0001]] — the bare 18-*zhāng* *jīnwén* recension, the philological starting-point of the entire division and the form in which children in East Asia memorized the work for two millennia.

- **The Táng imperial / Sòng *zhèngyì* line.** *Xiàojīng* 孝經 [[KR1f0002]] — Táng Xuánzōng's *yùzhù* of 722, revised 743, the recension that eclipsed all rival commentaries and was carved on the *Shítái Xiàojīng* 石臺孝經 stele in 745. *Xiàojīng zhùshū* 孝經注疏 [[KR1f0004]] — Xíng Bǐng's 1001 *zhèngyì* trimming Yuán Xíngchōng's 元行冲 c. 745 sub-commentary onto the imperial *zhù*, the form in which the work entered the *Shísān jīng zhùshū* and the *Sìkù quánshū*. Together these two are the canonical Táng–Sòng form of the *Xiàojīng*.

- **The Sòng *gǔwén* revival.** *Gǔwén Xiàojīng zhǐjiě* 古文孝經指解 [[KR1f0005]] — Sīmǎ Guāng's *zhǐjiě* (c. 1054) appended to Fàn Zǔyǔ's *shuō* (c. 1085), the primary Sòng witness to the 22-chapter *gǔwén* text independent of the post-1731 Japanese line, and the principal pre-Zhū defence of the *gǔwén* against the established *jīnwén*.

- **The ZhūXī editorial line.** *Xiàojīng kānwù* 孝經刋誤 [[KR1f0006]] — Zhū Xī's 1186 restructuring of the *gǔwén* into "1 *jīng* + 14 *zhuàn*" with 223 characters excised, the most consequential single intervention in the *Xiàojīng* canon after the Táng imperial commentary; *Xiàojīng dàyì* 孝經大義 [[KR1f0007]] (Dǒng Dǐng, c. 1290–1305) and *Xiàojīng dìngběn* 孝經定本 [[KR1f0008]] (Wú Chéng, 1303) are the two Yuán expositions that turned Zhū's polemic into a stable curricular text and carried the *Kānwù* recension into the Yuán–Míng examination world.

- **The Edo *gǔwén* recovery.** *Gǔwén Xiàojīng Kǒngshì zhuàn* 古文孝經孔氏傳 [[KR1f0003]] — the Japanese-preserved *gǔwén* 22-chapter recension with the Kǒng-attributed *zhuàn*, fixed in 1731 by Dazai Shundai 太宰純 and reintroduced into China in 1776; assessed as a Sòng-or-later Japanese fabrication by the *Sìkù* editors but a vital independent witness to pre-Xuánzōng textual states.

- **The late-Míng philosophical commentary.** *Xiàojīng jízhuàn* 孝經集傳 [[KR1f0010]] — Huáng Dàozhōu's 1638–1643 prison-and-imprisonment commentary, the most ambitious philosophical reading of the work in the late Míng and the one that explicitly executes Zhū Xī's never-realized *wàizhuàn* 外傳 programme.

- **The Qīng imperial pair.** *Yùdìng Xiàojīng zhù* 御定孝經注 [[KR1f0011]] — Shùnzhì's 1656 commentary, deliberately avoiding both the *jīnwén / gǔwén* dispute and the ZhūXī *Kānwù*; *Yùzuǎn Xiàojīng jízhù* 御纂孝經集註 [[KR1f0012]] — Yōngzhèng's 1727 compact compilation modelled on Zhū Xī's *Sìshū zhāngjù jízhù*, the standard ceremonial Qīng recension. The pair (with the unincluded 100-*juàn* Kāngxī *Xiàojīng yǎnyì* 孝經衍義) constitutes the high-Qīng imperial *Xiàojīng* programme.

- **The Qīng kǎozhèng critique.** *Xiàojīng wèn* 孝經問 [[KR1f0013]] — Máo Qílíng's polemical *wèndá* of c. 1685–1716, the most important Qīng critique of the ZhūXī editorial tradition and a key document of early kǎozhèngxué.

- **The fragmentary outlier.** *Xiàojīng shùzhù* 孝經述註 [[KR1f0009]] — Xiàng Bīn's early-Míng commentary, lost from circulation and recovered by the *Sìkù* editors from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* 永樂大典; included as the principal early-Míng witness in the division.

- **The companion apocryphon.** *Zhōngjīng* 忠經 [[KR1f0014]] — the pseudepigraphic *Classic of Loyalty* in 18 *zhāng*, attributed to Mǎ Róng 馬融 with a Zhèng Xuán-attributed commentary, actually a late-Táng or early-Sòng imitation of the *jīnwén Xiàojīng*. Excluded from the *Sìkù* but bibliographically paired with the *Xiàojīng* since the Sòng *Tōngzhì* 通志.

## Important persons

- [[孔子]] — traditional attributed author of the *Xiàojīng*'s spoken half; the *Hàn shū yìwén zhì* says "Zhòngní spoke and Zēngzǐ wrote it down."
- [[曾子]] — traditional attributed redactor (or, in a subsidiary attribution, author); the disciple of Confucius in the canonical opening dialogue.
- [[孔安國]] — pseudepigraphic author of the *gǔwén zhuàn* (see [[KR1f0003]]), the figurehead of the Old-Text tradition in the *Xiàojīng* sub-canon.
- [[鄭玄]] — author of the lost Hàn *jīnwén* commentary that Xuánzōng's *yùzhù* displaced; also the spurious commentator attached to the pseudepigraphic *Zhōngjīng* [[KR1f0014]].
- [[玄宗]] — Táng emperor (Lǐ Lóngjī 李隆基, 685–762, r. 712–756); his imperial *yùzhù* of 743 (see [[KR1f0002]]) is the recension that canonized the *jīnwén* and dominated all later commentary.
- [[陸德明]] — author of the *Jīngdiǎn shìwén* 經典釋文 (c. 583); supplied the phonetic glosses (*yīnyì* 音義) preserved in [[KR1f0004]] and the prefatory list of *Xiàojīng* commentators.
- [[邢昺]] — Sòng compiler of the *Xiàojīng zhèngyì* of 1001 (see [[KR1f0004]]), redacting Yuán Xíngchōng's lost Táng *zhèngyì* onto Xuánzōng's commentary.
- [[司馬光]] — author of the Sòng *Gǔwén Xiàojīng zhǐjiě* (see [[KR1f0005]]); the principal high-Sòng scholar-official willing to defend the textual integrity of the *gǔwén* line.
- [[范祖禹]] — Northern Sòng historian and Sīmǎ Guāng's closest political ally; his *Gǔwén Xiàojīng shuō* of c. 1085 is bound with Sīmǎ Guāng's *zhǐjiě* in [[KR1f0005]].
- [[朱熹]] — author of the *Xiàojīng kānwù* of 1186 (see [[KR1f0006]]), the single most consequential editorial intervention in the *Xiàojīng* tradition after the Táng imperial commentary.
- [[董鼎]] — Yuán Daoxue scholar; his *Xiàojīng dàyì* (see [[KR1f0007]]) supplied the explanatory commentary Zhū Xī had never written, anchoring the *Kānwù* recension in the Yuán curriculum.
- [[吳澄]] — leading Yuán Confucian; his *Xiàojīng dìngběn* (see [[KR1f0008]]) is the second great Yuán reorganization, applying Zhū's structural method to a *jīnwén* base.
- [[太宰純]] — Edo Confucian (Dazai Shundai, 1680–1747, pupil of Ogyū Sorai); his 1731 fixing of the *Gǔwén Xiàojīng Kǒngshì zhuàn* (see [[KR1f0003]]) is the form in which the Japanese-preserved Old Text reached Qīng China.
- [[項霦]] — early-Míng *àncháshī qiānshì* of Jiāngxī; author of the recovered *Xiàojīng shùzhù* (see [[KR1f0009]]).
- [[黃道周]] — late-Míng Hànlín scholar and Lóngwǔ-court martyr; his *Xiàojīng jízhuàn* (see [[KR1f0010]]) is the high-water mark of philosophical *Xiàojīng* commentary.
- [[世祖]] — Qīng Shùnzhì emperor (1638–1661); his *Yùdìng Xiàojīng zhù* of 1656 (see [[KR1f0011]]) is the first Qīng imperial *Xiàojīng* commentary.
- [[蔣赫德]] — Han-banner Grand Secretary; the actual compiler of the Shùnzhì commentary (see [[KR1f0011]]).
- [[世宗胤禛]] — Qīng Yōngzhèng emperor (1678–1735); his *Yùzuǎn Xiàojīng jízhù* of 1727 (see [[KR1f0012]]) supersedes Shùnzhì's for ceremonial purposes.
- [[毛奇齡]] — early-Qīng kǎozhèng polemicist; his *Xiàojīng wèn* (see [[KR1f0013]]) is the most important Qīng refutation of the SòngYuán editorial tradition.
- [[李清植]] — Qīng-period *kǎozhèng* collator whose collation notes are appended in the *Sìkù* recension of [[KR1f0004]].
- [[馬融]] — Eastern Hàn scholar (79–166); pseudepigraphic author of the *Zhōngjīng* (see [[KR1f0014]]); the attribution is post-Hàn invention.

## Topics

- ***Jīnwén* 今文 vs. *gǔwén* 古文.** The defining controversy of the division. The 18-chapter *jīnwén* recension transmitted by Yán Zhī and the 22-chapter *gǔwén* recension attributed to Kǒng Ānguó (containing the additional *Guīmén* 閨門 chapter on familial relations and three further chapter-divisions) became canonized as rivals already in the Suí (Wáng Shào, Liú Xuàn) and Táng (Liú Zhījī, Sīmǎ Zhēn); Xuánzōng's 722 commentary closed the dispute by adopting the *jīnwén*; Sòng *Daoxue* scholars (Sīmǎ Guāng, Fàn Zǔyǔ, Zhū Xī) revived the *gǔwén*; Yuán commentators (Dǒng Dǐng, Wú Chéng) split between them; the Edo recovery of the Kǒng-attributed *zhuàn* (Dazai 1731) reopened the question; Qīng kǎozhèng (Wú Chéng's character-count argument, repeated by Máo Qílíng) effectively dissolved it by showing that the substantive textual differences are minor (under thirty characters plus the *Guīmén* chapter).

- **Imperial annotation of the canon.** The *Xiàojīng* is the only Confucian classic annotated by four reigning emperors — Táng Xuánzōng, Sòng Zhēnzōng, Qīng Shùnzhì, Qīng Yōngzhèng — paralleling the imperial annotation of the *Dàodé jīng* (Wilkinson §65451). The recurrence is not coincidental: the *Xiàojīng*'s extension of filial piety from family to state (*shì jūn* 事君 modelled on *shì qīn* 事親) made it the natural Confucian text for an emperor to take in hand. The succession Shùnzhì 1656 ⇒ Kāngxī *yǎnyì* 100 *juàn* ⇒ Yōngzhèng 1727 *jízhù* registers an explicit Qīng imperial *Xiàojīng* programme.

- **Editorial liberty vs textual conservation.** Zhū Xī's *Kānwù* of 1186 excised 223 characters of the canon — the most aggressive Daoxue editorial intervention in any *jīng*, going further even than his rearrangement of the *Dàxué* 大學. The post-Zhū tradition split: Dǒng Dǐng and Wú Chéng accepted and worked from the edited recension; Huáng Dàozhōu, the Qīng imperial commentaries, and especially Máo Qílíng repudiated it. The *Sìkù* editors' own *tíyào* on [[KR1f0013]] explicitly endorses Máo's standpoint ("standing firmly on the inherited text and refusing to license the corruption of the ancient canon"), making the *Xiàojīng* sub-canon one of the clearest sites at which the kǎozhèng critique of *Daoxue* editorial liberty can be observed in action.

- **The *Xiàojīng* as primer and political instrument.** From the Hàn imperial school onward the *Xiàojīng* was the entry text of the curriculum (Wilkinson §24.2.1 cites the *Sìmín yuè lìng* 四民月令: in the eleventh month "*the young children read the* Xiàojīng *and the* Lúnyǔ"), shorter and more accessible than the *Wǔjīng*, and pedagogically prior to the *Sìshū*. The Qīng imperial commentaries (especially Yōngzhèng's compact *jízhù*) and works like the *Shèng yù guǎng xùn* 聖諭廣訓 of 1724 made this primer function a central element of Qīng administrative ideology — see Kutcher (1999) on *xiào* as instrument of state ideology in the Qīng.

- **Cross-canon imitation.** The *Xiàojīng* generated a small genre of formal imitations: the *Zhōngjīng* 忠經 (see [[KR1f0014]]), pseudonymously ascribed to Mǎ Róng, extending the model from filial piety to political loyalty; the *Nǚ xiàojīng* 女孝經 ascribed to "Lady née Zhèng" c. 730 (Wilkinson §11790; Murray 1988, 1990), extending it to women's filial duty. These imitations are not in the *Sìkù* but were bibliographically paired with the *Xiàojīng* in the Sòng *Tōngzhì* and the Yuán *Wénxiàn tōngkǎo*.

- **The *Guīmén* 閨門 chapter and dynastic taboo.** The 24-character *Guīmén* chapter peculiar to the *gǔwén* (on family relations within the home) was excised in the Táng *jīnwén* recension; YuánMíng commentators (Xióng Hé in his preface to [[KR1f0007]], Sūn Běn) read this as a deliberate court-taboo response to Wǔ Zétiān 武則天 and Wéi Hòu 韋后 — an interpretation explicitly refuted at length by the *Sìkù* editors in the *Tíyào* to [[KR1f0004]]. The dispute is a useful indicator of how moralized readings of textual history were contested in the Qīng evidential-scholarship project.

## Timeline

- c. 350–200 BCE — *Xiàojīng* 孝經 [[KR1f0001]] — the canonical 18-*zhāng* text, cited by name in the *Lǚshì chūnqiū* 呂氏春秋 (239 BCE) (Wilkinson §28.4.4).
- 722–743 — *Yùzhù Xiàojīng* 御注孝經 [[KR1f0002]] — Táng Xuánzōng's imperial commentary, carved on the *Shítái Xiàojīng* stele in 745; closes the *jīnwén / gǔwén* dispute for the Táng-and-after canon.
- 700–1000 — *Zhōngjīng* 忠經 [[KR1f0014]] — pseudonymous formal imitation of the *jīnwén Xiàojīng*; latest by the time of its earliest Sòng citation.
- 1001 — *Xiàojīng zhùshū* 孝經注疏 [[KR1f0004]] — Xíng Bǐng's *zhèngyì* completed under Sòng imperial commission; the form that enters the *Shísān jīng zhùshū*.
- 1054–1086 — *Gǔwén Xiàojīng zhǐjiě* 古文孝經指解 [[KR1f0005]] — Sīmǎ Guāng's exposition (presented to Rénzōng in 1054 and again later) plus Fàn Zǔyǔ's *shuō* of c. 1085; the principal Sòng witness to the *gǔwén* text.
- 1186 — *Xiàojīng kānwù* 孝經刋誤 [[KR1f0006]] — Zhū Xī's restructuring and 223-character excision; the watershed event of post-Táng *Xiàojīng* history.
- c. 1290–1305 — *Xiàojīng dàyì* 孝經大義 [[KR1f0007]] — Dǒng Dǐng's exposition of the ZhūXī *Kānwù* recension, published with Xióng Hé's preface in Dàdé 9 (1305).
- 1303 — *Xiàojīng dìngběn* 孝經定本 [[KR1f0008]] — Wú Chéng's reorganization of the *jīnwén* on the "1 *jīng* + *zhuàn*" model; the second great Yuán recension.
- 1369–1395 — *Xiàojīng shùzhù* 孝經述註 [[KR1f0009]] — Xiàng Bīn's lost early-Míng commentary, recovered from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn*.
- 1638–1643 — *Xiàojīng jízhuàn* 孝經集傳 [[KR1f0010]] — Huáng Dàozhōu's prison-period philosophical commentary, executing Zhū Xī's never-realized "outer tradition" plan.
- 1656 — *Yùdìng Xiàojīng zhù* 御定孝經注 [[KR1f0011]] — the Shùnzhì imperial commentary, compiled by Jiǎng Hèdé; first of the Qīng imperial pair.
- c. 1685–1716 — *Xiàojīng wèn* 孝經問 [[KR1f0013]] — Máo Qílíng's *wèndá* polemic against Zhū Xī and Wú Chéng; principal early-Qīng kǎozhèng critique.
- 1727 — *Yùzuǎn Xiàojīng jízhù* 御纂孝經集註 [[KR1f0012]] — the Yōngzhèng imperial *jízhù* on the model of Zhū Xī's *Sìshū zhāngjù jízhù*; the standard Qīng ceremonial recension.
- 1731 — *Gǔwén Xiàojīng Kǒngshì zhuàn* 古文孝經孔氏傳 [[KR1f0003]] — Dazai Shundai's Edo edition of the Japanese-preserved Old-Text *zhuàn*, reintroduced into China in 1776 and included in the *Sìkù* in 1781 (the underlying Japanese material is older, but not securely datable; the received recension is at this point).
