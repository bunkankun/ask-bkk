# Shěn-shì zūn-shēng shū 沈氏尊生書
*Master Shěn's Book on Honouring Life*
by [[沈金鰲]] Shěn Jīn-áo (*zì* Qín-yuán 芹源, *hào* Zūn-shēng lǎo-rén 尊生老人 / Rè-tiān cǎo-táng 熱天草堂, 1717–1776, Wú-xī 無錫, Cháng-zhōu prefecture, Jiāng-sū).

## About the work

A 72-*juǎn* comprehensive medical compendium — actually a *cóng-shū* 叢書 (collected works) of eight separate but interrelated medical treatises — composed by the polymath Wú-xī physician Shěn Jīn-áo over decades and conventionally dated to *Qián-lóng* 38 / 1773 (the date of the publication preface). The eight constituent works, organised as a unified curriculum, are:

1. ***Mài-xiàng tǒng-lèi*** 脈象統類 (1 *juǎn*, the opening *juǎn-shǒu* 卷首 of the present hxwd recension): Shěn's pulse-classification monograph, organising the 28 (or 30, in some recensions) classical pulse-categories under the six grand rubrics *fú-chén-chí-shù-huá-sè* 浮沉遲數滑澀 — the basis of Shěn's diagnostic-system reform.

2. ***Zhū mài zhǔ-bìng shī-tú*** 諸脈主病詩圖 (1 *juǎn*): the verse mnemonic supplement to the *Mài-xiàng*.

3. ***Zá-bìng yuán-liú xī-zhú*** 雜病源流犀燭 (30 *juǎn*): the great miscellany of internal-medicine pathomechanism — Shěn's principal doctrinal-and-clinical work, organised by disease-category with detailed *yuán-liú* 源流 (etiology-and-development) discussion.

4. ***Shāng-hán lùn gāng-mù*** 傷寒論綱目 (16 *juǎn*): Shěn's systematic re-organisation of Zhāng Zhòng-jǐng's *Shāng-hán lùn* under the *gāng-mù* (outline-and-items) format, with a heavy assimilation of Wú Jū-tōng's *Wēn-bìng tiáo-biàn* protocols.

5. ***Yào-yào fēn-jì*** 藥要分劑 (10 *juǎn*): Shěn's materia-medica, organised by the *qī-fāng shí-jì* 七方十劑 (seven-formula-types, ten-formulary-functions) classification of the Sòng tradition.

6. ***Fù-kē yù-rěn*** 婦科玉尺 (6 *juǎn*): obstetrics-and-gynaecology — Shěn's principal women's-medicine work, widely cited in subsequent Qīng gynaecological literature.

7. ***Yòu-kē shì-jīng-bǎo lù*** 幼科釋謎 (6 *juǎn*): paediatrics.

8. ***Yào-fāng dài-lěi*** 藥方代累 (2 *juǎn*): a closing formulary appendix.

(The *Sì-kù quán-shū* committee characterised the *Zūn-shēng shū* as one of the most ambitious individually-authored medical compendia of the high Qīng.)

The work's title — *zūn-shēng* 尊生 ("honouring life") — invokes the *Zhuāng-zǐ* 莊子 *Shàn-shēng* 善生 ("benefiting life") tradition and the early-Qīng *yǎng-shēng* 養生 literature, but Shěn's emphasis is firmly clinical rather than longevity-philosophical: the doctrinal preliminaries, the pulse-diagnosis, and the formulary together constitute a complete clinical-pedagogical curriculum.

Shěn's clinical-doctrinal position is an eclectic synthesis: he draws on the late-Míng / early-Qīng *Mìng-mén* / *xiān-tiān* tradition (Xuē Jǐ → Zhào Xiàn-kě → Zhāng Jiè-bīn) for the constitutional-fortifying framework; on the *Sì dà-jiā* for the differentiation of clinical patterns; on Yè Tiān-shì and Wú Jū-tōng for the *wēn-bìng* protocols; and on his own clinical experience for the specific drug-and-formula recommendations. The eclecticism is characteristic of the high-Qīng (Qián-lóng-era) mainstream medical curriculum.

### Abstract

The 1773 *editio princeps* dating of the unified *Zūn-shēng shū* is the consensus modern figure; individual constituent works were composed over a longer period (the *Zá-bìng yuán-liú xī-zhú* and *Mài-xiàng* are documentable to the 1760s). Shěn was a *jǔ-rén* (provincial-examination graduate) of *Qián-lóng* 9 / 1744 but never advanced further in the examination track; he practised medicine in Wú-xī and acquired major patronage from the Qián-lóng-era Cháng-zhōu cultural establishment.

CBDB has Shěn Jīn-áo at c_personid 0099034.

The work was reprinted multiple times in the late Qīng and early Republican periods and remains a standard reference in Chinese-medical curricula today.

## Translations and research

*No full European-language translation of the* Shěn-shì zūn-shēng shū *located*. Particular chapters of the *Fù-kē yù-rěn* have been studied: Charlotte Furth, *A Flourishing Yin: Gender in China's Medical History 960–1665* (California, 1999), and Yi-Li Wu, *Reproducing Women: Medicine, Metaphor, and Childbirth in Late Imperial China* (California, 2010). For Shěn's place in high-Qīng Jiāng-nán medicine see Marta Hanson, *Speaking of Epidemics* (Routledge, 2011), and Volker Scheid, *Currents of Tradition* (Eastland, 2007).

## Other points of interest

The work's 72-*juǎn* scope and integrated curricular organisation make it one of the half-dozen most-ambitious individually-authored Chinese medical compendia (alongside the *Zhèng-zhì zhǔn-shéng* 證治準繩 of Wáng Kěn-táng, the *Jǐng-yuè quán-shū* of Zhāng Jiè-bīn, the *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn* 醫宗金鑑 imperial compilation, and the *Féng-shì jǐn-náng mì-lù* of Féng Zhào-zhāng).
