## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR3er` 綜合醫書類 (*Zōng-hé yī-shū lèi*, Comprehensive Medical Works) is the sub-division of `KR3e` 醫家類 (Masters → Medicine) that gathers works treating the whole field of medicine rather than a single specialty — synthetic doctrinal treatises (*yī-lùn* 醫論), encyclopaedic compendia (*yī-tǒng dà-quán* 醫統大全, *yī-xué gāng-mù* 醫學綱目), curriculum primers (*yī-xué rù-mén* 醫學入門, *yī-xué sān-zì jīng* 醫學三字經), case-and-essay miscellanies, and the great clinical encyclopaediae produced by individual masters from the Suí through the Republican period. Where `KR3e` proper preserves the Sì-kù-quán-shū imperial-library mainstream of Chinese medicine (`source: WYG`), every text in `KR3er` instead derives from the *Hǎi-wài huí-liú zhōng-yī shàn-běn gǔ-jí cóng-shū* 海外回流中醫善本古籍叢書 (`source: hxwd`), the modern repatriation series edited by Zhèng Jīnshēng 鄭金生 et al. (Běijīng: Rén-mín wèi-shēng chū-bǎn-shè, 2003) that has returned to Chinese-language circulation rare *shàn-běn* witnesses preserved in Japanese, Korean, and European holdings. Many of the works gathered here have WYG-edition counterparts in `KR3e` proper — Cháo Yuánfāng's *Zhū-bìng yuán-hòu lùn* [[KR3er002]] vs. [[KR3e0012]], Sūn Sīmiǎo's *Qiānjīn* corpus [[KR3er088]] [[KR3er090]] vs. [[KR3e0013]], Wáng Tāo's *Wài-tái mì-yào* [[KR3er091]] vs. [[KR3e0015]], Xú Yànchún's *Yùjī wēi-yì* [[KR3er036]] vs. [[KR3e0068]], Yú Chāng's *Yī-mén fǎ-lǜ* [[KR3er007]] vs. [[KR3e0092]] — and the principal scholarly interest of `KR3er` therefore lies in its alternative editorial witnesses (often Japanese Edo or Korean reprints from Sòng or Yuán recensions lost in mainland China) and in its admission of three genres the Sì-kù editors excluded: late-imperial pseudepigrapha (the *Huá Tuó nèi-zhào tú* cluster [[KR3er012]] [[KR3er119]] [[KR3er125]]), Wàn-lì commercial *tōng-shū* primers, and post-1911 Republican works.

The genre's intellectual frame in Endymion Wilkinson's *Chinese History: A New Manual* is Section 41 (Medicine), in particular §41.3 (the Sòng–Qīng synthetic and clinical literature), §41.4 (Self-cultivation, where the boundary with `KR3eo` lies), and §41.5 (Modernisation), which covers the late-Qīng / Republican Chinese-Western synthesis (*zhōng-Xī huì-tōng* 中西匯通) of Táng Zōng-hǎi 唐宗海 [[KR3er063]], Wáng Yǒu-zhōng 王有忠 [[KR3er117]], and Zhāng Xī-chún 張錫純 [[KR3er071]]. The transmission tradition runs from the Suí imperial encyclopaedia of Cháo Yuánfāng, through the Táng synthesis attempted by Sūn Sīmiǎo and Wáng Tāo, the late-Hàn / Sòng pseudepigraphic *Zhōng-zàng jīng* [[KR3er012]] cluster, the Jīn-Yuán doctrinal-school monuments of Liú Wánsù [[KR3er011]], Zhāng Cóngzhèng [[KR3er064]], Lǐ Gǎo [[KR3er041]] [[KR3er062]] [[KR3er120]] and Zhū Zhènhēng [[KR3er017]] [[KR3er018]] [[KR3er024]] [[KR3er027]] [[KR3er042]] (the four masters re-printed here from Japanese-preserved Yuán-Míng witnesses), the Míng commercial-publishing *yī-xué tǒng* programme of Xú Chūn-fǔ [[KR3er031]], Wáng Lún [[KR3er040]], the Lóu-Yīng-Lǐ-Tǐng axis [[KR3er072]] [[KR3er074]], and the Gōng-shì 龔氏 family of Jīn-xī [[KR3er029]] [[KR3er037]] [[KR3er039]] [[KR3er084]] [[KR3er085]]; through the late-Míng warming-tonifying monuments of Zhāng Jiè-bīn [[KR3er073]] and Lǐ Zhōng-zǐ [[KR3er079]] [[KR3er083]]; the early-Qīng critical-clinical compendia of Yú Chāng [[KR3er007]], Zhāng Lù [[KR3er043]], Féng Zhào-zhāng [[KR3er070]], Chén Shì-duó [[KR3er057]] [[KR3er077]], and Huáng Yuán-yù [[KR3er047]]; the mid-Qīng programme of Xú Dà-chūn [[KR3er086]], Chéng Guó-péng [[KR3er048]], and Shěn Jīn-áo [[KR3er065]] [[KR3er109]]; and the late-Qīng / Republican syntheses of Chén Niàn-zǔ [[KR3er009]] [[KR3er045]] [[KR3er059]] [[KR3er066]], Lín Pèi-qín [[KR3er061]], Fèi Bó-xióng [[KR3er049]], Zhāng Xī-chún [[KR3er071]], Zhāng Shòu-yí [[KR3er112]], and Péng Zǐ-yì [[KR3er116]]. The chronological span (610 — c. 1947) is the widest of any `KR3e*` sub-division.

## Important texts and text clusters

**Suí–Táng synthesis.** The two great pre-Sòng systematisations of Chinese clinical knowledge are present here in hxwd-edition witnesses:
- *Zhū-bìng yuán-hòu lùn* 諸病源候論 [[KR3er002]] — Cháo Yuánfāng's 610 imperial Suí encyclopaedia of disease aetiology, the foundational *bìng-yīn* / *bìng-jī* reference for the entire post-Suí tradition.
- *Bèi-jí qiān-jīn yào-fāng* 備急千金要方 [[KR3er088]], *Qiān-jīn yì-fāng* 千金翼方 [[KR3er090]], and the separately-printed *Dà-yī jīng-chéng* 大醫精誠 [[KR3er089]] — Sūn Sī-miǎo's mid-Táng *Qiān-jīn* corpus, the great Táng synthesis and the locus of the *Dà-yī jīng-chéng* essay (the canonical Chinese statement of medical ethics).
- *Wài-tái mì-yào* 外臺秘要 [[KR3er091]] — Wáng Tāo's 752 compendium, the great Táng anthology preserving quotations from now-lost early formularies.
- *Yī-xīn-fāng* 醫心方 [[KR3er092]] — Tān-bō Yasuyori 丹波康賴's 984 Heian-Japanese compendium that quotes from Suí–Táng Chinese works otherwise lost; included here as the principal *jí-yì* 輯佚 source for the early imperial mainstream.

**The *Huá Tuó* pseudepigrapha cluster.** Three works circulating under Huá Tuó's name, all in fact late-imperial:
- *Huá-shì zhōng-zàng jīng* 華氏中藏經 [[KR3er012]] — Sòng compilation; the most influential of the *Huá Tuó* pseudepigrapha and the principal source for the doctrine of *bā-yào* 八要 / *yīn-yáng* / *xū-shí* pulse-and-symptom matching that runs through the post-Sòng synthetic tradition.
- *Xīn-kè Huà Tuó nèi-zhào tú* 新刻華佗內照圖 [[KR3er125]] (Hú Wén-huàn, 1603) and *Xuán-mén mài-jué nèi-zhào tú* 玄門脈訣內照圖 [[KR3er119]] — the late-Míng / early-Qīng anatomical-diagram pseudepigrapha; witnesses to the *zàng-fǔ* visualisation tradition.

**Sòng synthesis and the three-causes aetiology.** *Sān-yīn jí-yī bìng-zhèng fāng-lùn* 三因極一病證方論 [[KR3er055]] (Chén Yán, 1174) — the systematic articulation of the three-causes (內因 / 外因 / 不內外因) aetiology that becomes the standard Sòng doctrinal frame; here as hxwd reprint paralleling the WYG [[KR3e0041]]. *Lèi-zhèng pǔ-jì běn-shì fāng xù-jí* 類證普濟本事方續集 [[KR3er108]] — Xǔ Shū-wēi's continuation of his own *Běn-shì fāng* (the WYG-edition main work is [[KR3e0032]]). *Biǎn-què xīn-shū* 扁鵲心書 [[KR3er020]] (Dòu Cái, 1146) — Southern-Sòng pseudepigraphic moxibustion-and-warming-tonifying treatise.

**The four Jīn-Yuán masters re-edited.** All four schools are represented here from Japanese-preserved or Korean *shàn-běn* witnesses:
- Liú Wánsù — *Sù-wèn xuán-jī yuán-bìng shì* 素問玄機原病式 [[KR3er011]] (the *hán-liáng* school).
- Zhāng Cóngzhèng — *Rú-mén shì-qīn* 儒門事親 [[KR3er064]] (the *gōng-xié* attacking-purging school; cf. [[KR3e0051]]).
- Lǐ Gǎo — *Yī-xué fā-míng* 醫學發明 [[KR3er041]], *Lán-shì mì-cáng* 蘭室秘藏 [[KR3er062]], *Dōng-yuán shì-xiào fāng* 東垣試効方 [[KR3er120]] (the *pí-wèi* spleen-stomach school; cf. [[KR3e0052]]–[[KR3e0054]]).
- Wáng Hǎo-gǔ (Lǐ Gǎo's principal disciple) — *Cǐ-shì nán-zhī* 此事難知 [[KR3er030]], *Yī-lěi yuán-róng* 醫壘元戎 [[KR3er033]], *Bān-lùn cuì-yīng* 斑論萃英 [[KR3er034]] (cf. [[KR3e0055]]–[[KR3e0056]]).
- Zhū Zhèn-hēng (Dān-xī) — *Jīn-guì gōu-xuán* 金匱鉤玄 [[KR3er017]], *Dān-xī xīn-fǎ* 丹溪心法 [[KR3er018]] (with Chéng Chōng's Míng recompilation) and its supplement *Dān-xī xīn-fǎ fù-yú* 丹溪心法附餘 [[KR3er019]], *Píng-zhì huì-cuì* 平治會萃 [[KR3er024]], *Dān-xī zhì-fǎ xīn-yào* 丹溪治法心要 [[KR3er027]], *Dān-xī shǒu-jìng* 丹溪手鏡 [[KR3er042]], and the spurious *Mài-yīn zhèng-zhì* 脈因證治 [[KR3er010]] (a Qīng compilation under Zhū's name).
- Luó Tiān-yì (Lǐ Gǎo's disciple) — *Wèi-shēng bǎo-jiàn* 衛生寶鑑 [[KR3er052]], the principal Yuán-period synthesis of the *pí-wèi* school's clinical practice.

**Míng encyclopaedic compendia.** Five 100-juan-class or near-100-juan-class synthetic works define the Míng genre of *yī-xué dà-chéng* 醫學大成:
- *Gǔ-jīn yī-tǒng dà-quán* 古今醫統大全 [[KR3er031]] (Xú Chūn-fǔ, 1556, 100 juan) — the principal Míng *tǒng-dà-quán* and the first work to bring the entire field under a single architectural frame.
- *Yī-xué gāng-mù* 醫學綱目 [[KR3er074]] (Lóu Yīng, 1396, 40 juan) — the earliest Míng synthetic *gāng-mù* covering doctrine, diagnosis, and clinical practice.
- *Yùjī wēi-yì* 玉機微義 [[KR3er036]] (Xú Yàn-chún and Liú Chún, 1396, 50 juan) — the great early-Míng reorganisation of Zhū Zhèn-hēng's clinical doctrine (cf. [[KR3e0068]]).
- *Yī-xué rù-mén* 醫學入門 [[KR3er072]] (Lǐ Tǐng, 1575, 8 juan) — the most influential Míng synthetic curriculum, organised as an Inner-Collection / Outer-Collection didactic structure and used as the standard textbook in Chosŏn Korea and Edo Japan as well as Míng China.
- *Zhèng-zhì zhǔn-shéng* (not in this division) and *Jǐng-yuè quán-shū* 景岳全書 [[KR3er073]] (Zhāng Jiè-bīn, 1640, 64 juan) — the central monument of the Míng *wēn-bǔ* (warming-tonifying) synthesis; cf. [[KR3e0086]].

**The Gōng-shì 龔氏 family of Jīn-xī (Jiāngxī).** Five generations of commercially-published synthetic compendia, the dominant Wàn-lì-era brand of practical medicine:
- *Gǔ-jīn yī-jiàn* 古今醫鑑 [[KR3er037]] (16-juan editorial recension by Gōng Tíng-xián, 1576–1589) and its initial-cut *chū-kān běn* 初刊本 [[KR3er038]] (8-juan, Gōng Xìn) — the father's and son's joint work.
- *Wàn-bìng huí-chūn* 萬病回春 [[KR3er039]] (Gōng Tíng-xián, 1587) — the most popular Míng clinical primer.
- *Shòu-shì bǎo-yuán* 壽世保元 [[KR3er029]] (Gōng Tíng-xián, 1615) — the late-life synthesis.
- *Yún-lín shén-gòu* 雲林神彀 [[KR3er084]] and *Jì-shì quán-shū* 濟世全書 [[KR3er085]] (Gōng Tíng-xián, 1591, 1616) — popular-medicine compendia.

**Late-Míng warming-tonifying programme.** Three works define the Mìng-mén / *wēn-bǔ* doctrinal cluster:
- *Yī-guàn* 醫貫 [[KR3er001]] (Zhào Xiàn-kě, 1617) — the systematic articulation of the *Mìng-mén* doctrine that displaces the heart as the body's "ruler" and installs the formless *zhēn-jūn* of the *Mìng-mén* in its place. The polemical target of [[徐大椿]] Xú Dà-chūn's later *Yī-guàn biān* 醫貫砭 ([[KR3eq048]]).
- *Jǐng-yuè quán-shū* 景岳全書 [[KR3er073]] (Zhāng Jiè-bīn, 1640) — the most complete clinical articulation of the *wēn-bǔ* programme; doctrinally close to the *Yī-guàn*.
- *Yī-zōng bì-dú* 醫宗必讀 [[KR3er083]] (Lǐ Zhōng-zǐ, 1637) and *Shān-bǔ yí-shēng wēi-lùn* 刪補頤生微論 [[KR3er079]] — the parallel Lǐ Zhōng-zǐ synthesis.

**Early- and mid-Qīng critical syntheses.** The Qīng response to the late-Míng *wēn-bǔ* extremism:
- *Yī-mén fǎ-lǜ* 醫門法律 [[KR3er007]] (Yú Chāng, 1658) — the *Shāng-hán* reformer's prescriptive code of clinical practice (cf. [[KR3e0092]]).
- *Zhāng-shì yī-tōng* 張氏醫通 [[KR3er043]] (Zhāng Lù, 1695) — the standard early-Qīng synthetic clinical encyclopaedia.
- *Féng-shì jǐn-náng mì-lù* 馮氏錦囊秘錄 [[KR3er070]] (Féng Zhào-zhāng, 1686–1702, 50 juan) — the comprehensive late-Kāng-xī clinical synthesis.
- *Biàn-zhèng lù* 辨證錄 [[KR3er077]] and *Shí-shì mì-lù* 石室秘錄 [[KR3er057]] (Chén Shì-duó, 1687) — the polemically distinctive Chén Shì-duó synthesis claiming dream-revelation from Lǚ Dòng-bīn and Qí Bó.
- *Sì-shèng xīn-yuán* 四聖心源 [[KR3er047]] (Huáng Yuán-yù, 1754) — the systematic Qián-lóng-era articulation of the *zhōng-qì* 中氣 (central-qì) school grounded in a return to Zhāng Jī.
- *Jǐng-yuè quán-shū fā-huī* 景岳全書發揮 [[KR3er082]] (attributed to Yè Guì, c. 1700–1745) — the Qīng *wēn-bìng*-school polemical critique of the late-Míng *wēn-bǔ* programme.

**Mid-Qīng curriculum and the return-to-the-Classics movement.** *Lán-tái guǐ-fàn* 蘭臺軌範 [[KR3er086]] (Xú Dà-chūn, 1764) — the mid-Qīng curricular synthesis (cf. [[KR3e0097]]). *Yī-xué xīn-wù* 醫學心悟 [[KR3er048]] (Chéng Guó-péng, 1732) — the most widely-circulated mid-Qīng clinical primer, organising treatment by the *bā-fǎ* 八法 (eight methods). *Zá-bìng yuán-liú xī-zhú* 雜病源流犀燭 [[KR3er065]] and *Shěn-shì zūn-shēng shū* 沈氏尊生書 [[KR3er109]] (Shěn Jīn-áo, 1773) — the late-Qián-lóng aetiology-and-treatment synthesis. *Lèi-zhèng zhì-cái* 類證治裁 [[KR3er061]] (Lín Pèi-qín, 1839) — the standard late-Qīng clinical handbook.

**Chén Xiū-yuán (Chén Niàn-zǔ) and the late-Qīng popularisation.** Chén Niàn-zǔ's *Yī-xué sān-zì jīng* 醫學三字經 [[KR3er059]] (1804) is the canonical Three-Character-Classic-style medical primer for popular memorisation; his *Yī-xué shí-zài-yì* 醫學實在易 [[KR3er045]] (1808), *Yī-yī ǒu-lù* 醫醫偶錄 [[KR3er066]] (1812), and *Bǐ-huā yī-jìng* 筆花醫鏡 [[KR3er009]] (Jiāng Qiū, 1824 — a contemporary work in the same primer tradition) define the late-Qīng popular curriculum.

**Republican-era and Chinese-Western synthesis.** *Yī-lín gǎi-cuò* 醫林改錯 [[KR3er008]] (Wáng Qīng-rèn, 1830) — the proto-scientific anatomical critique based on Wáng's observation of corpses at Luán-zhōu plague pits and execution-grounds. *Jiǎn-míng zhōng-Xī huì-cān yī-xué tú-shuō* 簡明中西匯參醫學圖說 [[KR3er117]] (Wáng Yǒu-zhōng) — late-Qīng integrationist. *Yī-xué jiàn-néng* 醫學見能 [[KR3er063]] (Táng Zōng-hǎi, 1873) — the principal *zhōng-Xī huì-tōng* synthesiser. *Yī-xué zhōng-zhōng cān-xī lù* 醫學衷中參西錄 [[KR3er071]] (Zhāng Xī-chún, 1909–1934) — the magnum opus of Republican-era Chinese medicine. *Zhōng-fēng jiào-quán* 中風斠詮 [[KR3er112]] (Zhāng Shòu-yí, 1917) — the early-Republican critical re-thinking of stroke aetiology. *Yuán-yùn-dòng de gǔ Zhōng-yī xué* 圓運動的古中醫學 [[KR3er116]] (Péng Zǐ-yì, 1921–1947) — the Yún-nán-school *yuán-yùn-dòng* (circular-motion) systematisation, still in active TCM use.

**Case-and-essay miscellanies.** A late-Míng / Qīng / Republican sub-tradition of short-form writing on doctrine illustrated by clinical cases: *Yī-jīng mì-zhǐ* 醫經秘旨 [[KR3er105]] (Jiāng Zhī-lán, 1662), *Yī-jīn yī-fá* 醫津一筏 [[KR3er103]] (Jiāng Zhī-lán, 1662), *Yī-xué tǐ-yòng* 醫學體用 [[KR3er099]], *Qí-xiào yī-shù* 奇效醫述 [[KR3er127]] (Niè Shàng-héng, 1620), *Yī-zhōng yī-dé* 醫中一得 [[KR3er097]] (1803), *Guò-tíng lù-cún* 過庭錄存 [[KR3er111]] (Cáo Cún-xīn), and *Jīng-lì zá-lùn xù* 經歷雜論序 [[KR3er110]].

## Important persons

- [[巢元方]] (Cháo Yuánfāng, fl. 605–615) — Suí imperial physician, lead compiler of 《諸病源候論》 [[KR3er002]] (cf. [[KR3e0012]]); foundational *bìng-yīn* systematiser.
- [[孫思邈]] (Sūn Sī-miǎo, c. 581–682) — the central Táng clinician, author of the *Qiānjīn* corpus [[KR3er088]] [[KR3er090]] and of the *Dà-yī jīng-chéng* [[KR3er089]] medical-ethics statement.
- [[王燾]] (Wáng Tāo, c. 670–755) — author of 《外臺秘要》 [[KR3er091]] (cf. [[KR3e0015]]), the great Táng compendium preserving lost Suí-Táng formularies.
- [[丹波康賴]] (Tān-bō Yasuyori, 912–995) — Heian-Japanese physician, compiler of 《醫心方》 [[KR3er092]], the principal pre-Sòng Chinese-medical *jí-yì* source preserving Suí-Táng works lost in China.
- [[華佗]] (Huà Tuó, c. 145 – 208) — pseudepigraphic cover-author of the *Zhōng-zàng jīng* [[KR3er012]] and the *nèi-zhào tú* cluster [[KR3er119]] [[KR3er125]]; canonical anchor for the "lost-anatomy" tradition of late-imperial visualisation.
- [[陳言]] (Chén Yán, 1131–1189) — formulator of the three-causes aetiology in 《三因極一病證方論》 [[KR3er055]] (cf. [[KR3e0041]]).
- [[許叔微]] (Xǔ Shū-wēi, 1080–1154) — Sòng *Shāng-hán* expositor, here represented by the *Běn-shì fāng xù-jí* [[KR3er108]].
- [[劉完素]] (Liú Wán-sù, c. 1110–1200) — founder of the *Héjiān* school (*hán-liáng* cooling-cold), 《素問玄機原病式》 [[KR3er011]].
- [[張從正]] (Zhāng Cóng-zhèng, 1156–1228) — the attacking-purging master, 《儒門事親》 [[KR3er064]].
- [[李杲]] (Lǐ Gǎo, 1180–1251) — *Dōng-yuán*, founder of the *pí-wèi* school; here in 《醫學發明》 [[KR3er041]], 《蘭室秘藏》 [[KR3er062]], 《東垣試效方》 [[KR3er120]].
- [[王好古]] (Wáng Hǎo-gǔ, c. 1200 – c. 1264) — Lǐ Gǎo's principal disciple; three works [[KR3er030]] [[KR3er033]] [[KR3er034]] continuing the *pí-wèi* programme.
- [[羅天益]] (Luó Tiān-yì, c. 1220–1290) — Lǐ Gǎo's senior pupil, author of 《衛生寶鑑》 [[KR3er052]] and editor of Lǐ Gǎo's late works.
- [[朱震亨]] (Zhū Zhèn-hēng, 1281–1358) — *Dān-xī*, founder of the *zī-yīn* school and most prolific Yuán doctrinal author; principal Dān-xī works re-edited from Japanese / Korean witnesses appear at [[KR3er017]] [[KR3er018]] [[KR3er019]] [[KR3er024]] [[KR3er027]] [[KR3er042]].
- [[戴思恭]] (Dài Sī-gōng, 1324–1405) — Zhū Zhèn-hēng's foremost disciple and Míng imperial physician; 《秘傳證治要訣及類方》 [[KR3er068]].
- [[劉純]] (Liú Chún, 1340 – c. 1412) — Wáng Lǚ's student; author of the influential primer 《醫經小學》 [[KR3er051]] and continuer of *Yùjī wēi-yì* [[KR3er036]].
- [[樓英]] (Lóu Yīng, 1320–1389) — author of 《醫學綱目》 [[KR3er074]], the earliest Míng synthetic *gāng-mù*.
- [[徐春甫]] (Xú Chūn-fǔ, 1520–1596) — author of 《古今醫統大全》 [[KR3er031]], 100-juan, the principal Míng synthetic encyclopaedia, and convenor of the late-Míng *Yī-xué huì* 醫學會 medical society.
- [[王綸]] (Wáng Lún, c. 1453–1510) — Míng imperial physician, author of 《明醫雜著》 [[KR3er040]], the standard late-Míng synthesis.
- [[李梃]] (Lǐ Tǐng, fl. 1573–1619) — author of 《醫學入門》 [[KR3er072]], the curriculum most widely adopted abroad.

## Topics

The division is organised around several overlapping intellectual currents:

**The genre of "comprehensive medical writing" (綜合醫書).** The defining bibliographical category of `KR3er` is also its principal intellectual content. From the Suí *Yuán-hòu lùn* and the Táng *Qiān-jīn* / *Wài-tái* through the Míng *yī-tǒng dà-quán* and *gāng-mù*, the Qīng *yī-tōng* and *zūn-shēng* compilations, and the Republican synthesis attempts, a single genre-history runs unbroken: the periodic attempt to bring the whole of Chinese medical knowledge under a single architectural frame. Xú Chūn-fǔ's *Gǔ-jīn yī-tǒng dà-quán* [[KR3er031]] is the central monument of this tradition; Lóu Yīng's *Yī-xué gāng-mù* [[KR3er074]] is its earliest Míng systematic attempt; and the Republican-era *Yī-xué zhōng-zhōng cān-xī lù* [[KR3er071]] is its latest pre-modern instantiation.

**The Mìng-mén / wēn-bǔ controversy.** The late-Míng warming-tonifying programme of Zhào Xiàn-kě [[KR3er001]], Zhāng Jiè-bīn [[KR3er073]], and Lǐ Zhōng-zǐ [[KR3er083]] [[KR3er079]] is in this division balanced by its Qīng polemical critics — Yè Guì's *Jǐng-yuè quán-shū fā-huī* [[KR3er082]], Xú Dà-chūn's *Yī-guàn biān* (in [[KR3eq048]], outside this division), and the implicit critique of Yú Chāng's *Yī-mén fǎ-lǜ* [[KR3er007]]. The 命門 (gate-of-life) doctrine, displacing the heart from the position of *jūn-zhǔ* 君主 ("ruler") of the body, is the principal doctrinal innovation of late-Míng synthetic medicine, with consequences across the entire Qīng tradition.

**The Jīn-Yuán school re-edition programme.** A distinguishing feature of `KR3er` is the depth of its Jīn-Yuán-master coverage in alternative shàn-běn witnesses: every school is represented from Japanese-preserved Yuán or early-Míng editions, often filling lacunae or correcting variants in the WYG-edition transmission preserved in `KR3e` proper. This is most pronounced for the *Dān-xī* corpus (six works in `KR3er` vs. three in `KR3e`) and the *Dōng-yuán* corpus (three works in `KR3er`).

**The bā-fǎ (eight methods) and clinical pattern-recognition.** Chéng Guó-péng's *Yī-xué xīn-wù* [[KR3er048]] articulates the *bā-fǎ* — *hàn* 汗 (sweat) / *tù* 吐 (vomit) / *xià* 下 (purge) / *hé* 和 (harmonise) / *wēn* 溫 (warm) / *qīng* 清 (cool) / *xiāo* 消 (resolve) / *bǔ* 補 (tonify) — that becomes the canonical Qīng frame for organising treatment. Most of the late-Qīng clinical primers ([[KR3er061]] [[KR3er023]] [[KR3er086]] [[KR3er045]]) presuppose this organisation.

**Pseudepigraphy and the recovery of "lost antiquity".** The recurrent late-imperial appeal to Huá Tuó, Biǎn Què, and other Hàn-and-earlier figures as cover-authors for late-Míng / Qīng compilations is well-represented here ([[KR3er012]] [[KR3er020]] [[KR3er119]] [[KR3er125]] [[KR3er105]] etc.). The phenomenon is bibliographically transparent — the works are demonstrably late — but doctrinally consequential, since it allows the late-imperial reorganisations of medicine to claim canonical Hàn-Sòng authority.

**Chinese-Western synthesis (zhōng-Xī huì-tōng).** A late-Qīng / Republican movement responding to the introduction of Western anatomy, physiology, and pharmacology into Chinese medical practice. The principal figures here are Wáng Qīng-rèn (a proto-anatomist independent of Western input, [[KR3er008]]), Táng Zōng-hǎi [[KR3er063]], Wáng Yǒu-zhōng [[KR3er117]], and Zhāng Xī-chún [[KR3er071]], with Péng Zǐ-yì [[KR3er116]] as a counterposition that insists on a purely Chinese cosmological frame.

**The hxwd source and the politics of "repatriated heritage".** The conceptual frame of the *Hǎi-wài huí-liú zhōng-yī shàn-běn gǔ-jí cóng-shū* — that rare witnesses preserved abroad (especially in Japanese Edo-period reprints and Korean Chosŏn-period editions) constitute a recoverable Chinese national-medical heritage — is itself a topic of modern historiography. Edo Japan in particular preserved many Yuán-Míng commercial medical editions in better shape than mainland China after the Wàn-lì-Qián-lóng print runs were exhausted, and the hxwd programme has had material consequences for textual scholarship since 2003.
