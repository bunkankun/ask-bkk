## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR3er` 綜合醫書類 (*Zōnghé yīshū lèi*, Comprehensive Medical Works) is the sub-division of `KR3e` 醫家類 (Masters → Medicine) that gathers works treating the whole field of medicine rather than a single specialty — synthetic doctrinal treatises (*yīlùn* 醫論), encyclopaedic compendia (*yītǒng dàquán* 醫統大全, *yīxué gāngmù* 醫學綱目), curriculum primers (*yīxué rùmén* 醫學入門, *yīxué sānzì jīng* 醫學三字經), case-and-essay miscellanies, and the great clinical encyclopaediae produced by individual masters from the Suí through the Republican period. Where `KR3e` proper preserves the Sìkùquánshū imperial-library mainstream of Chinese medicine (`source: WYG`), every text in `KR3er` instead derives from the *Hǎiwài huíliú zhōngyī shànběn gǔjí cóngshū* 海外回流中醫善本古籍叢書 (`source: hxwd`), the modern repatriation series edited by Zhèng Jīnshēng 鄭金生 et al. (Běijīng: Rénmín wèishēng chūbǎnshè, 2003) that has returned to Chinese-language circulation rare *shànběn* witnesses preserved in Japanese, Korean, and European holdings. Many of the works gathered here have WYG-edition counterparts in `KR3e` proper — Cháo Yuánfāng's *Zhūbìng yuánhòu lùn* [[KR3er002]] vs. [[KR3e0012]], Sūn Sīmiǎo's *Qiānjīn* corpus [[KR3er088]] [[KR3er090]] vs. [[KR3e0013]], Wáng Tāo's *Wàitái mìyào* [[KR3er091]] vs. [[KR3e0015]], Xú Yànchún's *Yùjī wēiyì* [[KR3er036]] vs. [[KR3e0068]], Yú Chāng's *Yīmén fǎlǜ* [[KR3er007]] vs. [[KR3e0092]] — and the principal scholarly interest of `KR3er` therefore lies in its alternative editorial witnesses (often Japanese Edo or Korean reprints from Sòng or Yuán recensions lost in mainland China) and in its admission of three genres the Sìkù editors excluded: late-imperial pseudepigrapha (the *Huá Tuó nèizhào tú* cluster [[KR3er012]] [[KR3er119]] [[KR3er125]]), Wànlì commercial *tōngshū* primers, and post-1911 Republican works.

The genre's intellectual frame in Endymion Wilkinson's *Chinese History: A New Manual* is Section 41 (Medicine), in particular §41.3 (the Sòng–Qīng synthetic and clinical literature), §41.4 (Self-cultivation, where the boundary with `KR3eo` lies), and §41.5 (Modernisation), which covers the late-Qīng / Republican Chinese-Western synthesis (*zhōngXī huìtōng* 中西匯通) of Táng Zōnghǎi 唐宗海 [[KR3er063]], Wáng Yǒuzhōng 王有忠 [[KR3er117]], and Zhāng Xīchún 張錫純 [[KR3er071]]. The transmission tradition runs from the Suí imperial encyclopaedia of Cháo Yuánfāng, through the Táng synthesis attempted by Sūn Sīmiǎo and Wáng Tāo, the late-Hàn / Sòng pseudepigraphic *Zhōngzàng jīng* [[KR3er012]] cluster, the JīnYuán doctrinal-school monuments of Liú Wánsù [[KR3er011]], Zhāng Cóngzhèng [[KR3er064]], Lǐ Gǎo [[KR3er041]] [[KR3er062]] [[KR3er120]] and Zhū Zhènhēng [[KR3er017]] [[KR3er018]] [[KR3er024]] [[KR3er027]] [[KR3er042]] (the four masters re-printed here from Japanese-preserved YuánMíng witnesses), the Míng commercial-publishing *yīxué tǒng* programme of Xú Chūnfǔ [[KR3er031]], Wáng Lún [[KR3er040]], the LóuYīngLǐTǐng axis [[KR3er072]] [[KR3er074]], and the Gōngshì 龔氏 family of Jīnxī [[KR3er029]] [[KR3er037]] [[KR3er039]] [[KR3er084]] [[KR3er085]]; through the late-Míng warming-tonifying monuments of Zhāng Jièbīn [[KR3er073]] and Lǐ Zhōngzǐ [[KR3er079]] [[KR3er083]]; the early-Qīng critical-clinical compendia of Yú Chāng [[KR3er007]], Zhāng Lù [[KR3er043]], Féng Zhàozhāng [[KR3er070]], Chén Shìduó [[KR3er057]] [[KR3er077]], and Huáng Yuányù [[KR3er047]]; the mid-Qīng programme of Xú Dàchūn [[KR3er086]], Chéng Guópéng [[KR3er048]], and Shěn Jīnáo [[KR3er065]] [[KR3er109]]; and the late-Qīng / Republican syntheses of Chén Niànzǔ [[KR3er009]] [[KR3er045]] [[KR3er059]] [[KR3er066]], Lín Pèiqín [[KR3er061]], Fèi Bóxióng [[KR3er049]], Zhāng Xīchún [[KR3er071]], Zhāng Shòuyí [[KR3er112]], and Péng Zǐyì [[KR3er116]]. The chronological span (610 — c. 1947) is the widest of any `KR3e*` sub-division.

## Important texts and text clusters

**Suí–Táng synthesis.** The two great pre-Sòng systematisations of Chinese clinical knowledge are present here in hxwd-edition witnesses:
- *Zhūbìng yuánhòu lùn* 諸病源候論 [[KR3er002]] — Cháo Yuánfāng's 610 imperial Suí encyclopaedia of disease aetiology, the foundational *bìngyīn* / *bìngjī* reference for the entire post-Suí tradition.
- *Bèijí qiānjīn yàofāng* 備急千金要方 [[KR3er088]], *Qiānjīn yìfāng* 千金翼方 [[KR3er090]], and the separately-printed *Dàyī jīngchéng* 大醫精誠 [[KR3er089]] — Sūn Sīmiǎo's mid-Táng *Qiānjīn* corpus, the great Táng synthesis and the locus of the *Dàyī jīngchéng* essay (the canonical Chinese statement of medical ethics).
- *Wàitái mìyào* 外臺秘要 [[KR3er091]] — Wáng Tāo's 752 compendium, the great Táng anthology preserving quotations from now-lost early formularies.
- *Yīxīnfāng* 醫心方 [[KR3er092]] — Tānbō Yasuyori 丹波康賴's 984 Heian-Japanese compendium that quotes from Suí–Táng Chinese works otherwise lost; included here as the principal *jíyì* 輯佚 source for the early imperial mainstream.

**The *Huá Tuó* pseudepigrapha cluster.** Three works circulating under Huá Tuó's name, all in fact late-imperial:
- *Huáshì zhōngzàng jīng* 華氏中藏經 [[KR3er012]] — Sòng compilation; the most influential of the *Huá Tuó* pseudepigrapha and the principal source for the doctrine of *bāyào* 八要 / *yīnyáng* / *xūshí* pulse-and-symptom matching that runs through the post-Sòng synthetic tradition.
- *Xīnkè Huà Tuó nèizhào tú* 新刻華佗內照圖 [[KR3er125]] (Hú Wénhuàn, 1603) and *Xuánmén màijué nèizhào tú* 玄門脈訣內照圖 [[KR3er119]] — the late-Míng / early-Qīng anatomical-diagram pseudepigrapha; witnesses to the *zàngfǔ* visualisation tradition.

**Sòng synthesis and the three-causes aetiology.** *Sānyīn jíyī bìngzhèng fānglùn* 三因極一病證方論 [[KR3er055]] (Chén Yán, 1174) — the systematic articulation of the three-causes (內因 / 外因 / 不內外因) aetiology that becomes the standard Sòng doctrinal frame; here as hxwd reprint paralleling the WYG [[KR3e0041]]. *Lèizhèng pǔjì běnshì fāng xùjí* 類證普濟本事方續集 [[KR3er108]] — Xǔ Shūwēi's continuation of his own *Běnshì fāng* (the WYG-edition main work is [[KR3e0032]]). *Biǎnquè xīnshū* 扁鵲心書 [[KR3er020]] (Dòu Cái, 1146) — Southern-Sòng pseudepigraphic moxibustion-and-warming-tonifying treatise.

**The four JīnYuán masters re-edited.** All four schools are represented here from Japanese-preserved or Korean *shànběn* witnesses:
- Liú Wánsù — *Sùwèn xuánjī yuánbìng shì* 素問玄機原病式 [[KR3er011]] (the *hánliáng* school).
- Zhāng Cóngzhèng — *Rúmén shìqīn* 儒門事親 [[KR3er064]] (the *gōngxié* attacking-purging school; cf. [[KR3e0051]]).
- Lǐ Gǎo — *Yīxué fāmíng* 醫學發明 [[KR3er041]], *Lánshì mìcáng* 蘭室秘藏 [[KR3er062]], *Dōngyuán shìxiào fāng* 東垣試効方 [[KR3er120]] (the *píwèi* spleen-stomach school; cf. [[KR3e0052]]–[[KR3e0054]]).
- Wáng Hǎogǔ (Lǐ Gǎo's principal disciple) — *Cǐshì nánzhī* 此事難知 [[KR3er030]], *Yīlěi yuánróng* 醫壘元戎 [[KR3er033]], *Bānlùn cuìyīng* 斑論萃英 [[KR3er034]] (cf. [[KR3e0055]]–[[KR3e0056]]).
- Zhū Zhènhēng (Dānxī) — *Jīnguì gōuxuán* 金匱鉤玄 [[KR3er017]], *Dānxī xīnfǎ* 丹溪心法 [[KR3er018]] (with Chéng Chōng's Míng recompilation) and its supplement *Dānxī xīnfǎ fùyú* 丹溪心法附餘 [[KR3er019]], *Píngzhì huìcuì* 平治會萃 [[KR3er024]], *Dānxī zhìfǎ xīnyào* 丹溪治法心要 [[KR3er027]], *Dānxī shǒujìng* 丹溪手鏡 [[KR3er042]], and the spurious *Màiyīn zhèngzhì* 脈因證治 [[KR3er010]] (a Qīng compilation under Zhū's name).
- Luó Tiānyì (Lǐ Gǎo's disciple) — *Wèishēng bǎojiàn* 衛生寶鑑 [[KR3er052]], the principal Yuán-period synthesis of the *píwèi* school's clinical practice.

**Míng encyclopaedic compendia.** Five 100-juan-class or near-100-juan-class synthetic works define the Míng genre of *yīxué dàchéng* 醫學大成:
- *Gǔjīn yītǒng dàquán* 古今醫統大全 [[KR3er031]] (Xú Chūnfǔ, 1556, 100 juan) — the principal Míng *tǒngdàquán* and the first work to bring the entire field under a single architectural frame.
- *Yīxué gāngmù* 醫學綱目 [[KR3er074]] (Lóu Yīng, 1396, 40 juan) — the earliest Míng synthetic *gāngmù* covering doctrine, diagnosis, and clinical practice.
- *Yùjī wēiyì* 玉機微義 [[KR3er036]] (Xú Yànchún and Liú Chún, 1396, 50 juan) — the great early-Míng reorganisation of Zhū Zhènhēng's clinical doctrine (cf. [[KR3e0068]]).
- *Yīxué rùmén* 醫學入門 [[KR3er072]] (Lǐ Tǐng, 1575, 8 juan) — the most influential Míng synthetic curriculum, organised as an Inner-Collection / Outer-Collection didactic structure and used as the standard textbook in Chosŏn Korea and Edo Japan as well as Míng China.
- *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng* (not in this division) and *Jǐngyuè quánshū* 景岳全書 [[KR3er073]] (Zhāng Jièbīn, 1640, 64 juan) — the central monument of the Míng *wēnbǔ* (warming-tonifying) synthesis; cf. [[KR3e0086]].

**The Gōngshì 龔氏 family of Jīnxī (Jiāngxī).** Five generations of commercially-published synthetic compendia, the dominant Wàn-lì-era brand of practical medicine:
- *Gǔjīn yījiàn* 古今醫鑑 [[KR3er037]] (16-juan editorial recension by Gōng Tíngxián, 1576–1589) and its initial-cut *chūkān běn* 初刊本 [[KR3er038]] (8-juan, Gōng Xìn) — the father's and son's joint work.
- *Wànbìng huíchūn* 萬病回春 [[KR3er039]] (Gōng Tíngxián, 1587) — the most popular Míng clinical primer.
- *Shòushì bǎoyuán* 壽世保元 [[KR3er029]] (Gōng Tíngxián, 1615) — the late-life synthesis.
- *Yúnlín shéngòu* 雲林神彀 [[KR3er084]] and *Jìshì quánshū* 濟世全書 [[KR3er085]] (Gōng Tíngxián, 1591, 1616) — popular-medicine compendia.

**Late-Míng warming-tonifying programme.** Three works define the Mìngmén / *wēnbǔ* doctrinal cluster:
- *Yīguàn* 醫貫 [[KR3er001]] (Zhào Xiànkě, 1617) — the systematic articulation of the *Mìngmén* doctrine that displaces the heart as the body's "ruler" and installs the formless *zhēnjūn* of the *Mìngmén* in its place. The polemical target of [[徐大椿]] Xú Dàchūn's later *Yīguàn biān* 醫貫砭 ([[KR3eq048]]).
- *Jǐngyuè quánshū* 景岳全書 [[KR3er073]] (Zhāng Jièbīn, 1640) — the most complete clinical articulation of the *wēnbǔ* programme; doctrinally close to the *Yīguàn*.
- *Yīzōng bìdú* 醫宗必讀 [[KR3er083]] (Lǐ Zhōngzǐ, 1637) and *Shānbǔ yíshēng wēilùn* 刪補頤生微論 [[KR3er079]] — the parallel Lǐ Zhōngzǐ synthesis.

**Early- and mid-Qīng critical syntheses.** The Qīng response to the late-Míng *wēnbǔ* extremism:
- *Yīmén fǎlǜ* 醫門法律 [[KR3er007]] (Yú Chāng, 1658) — the *Shānghán* reformer's prescriptive code of clinical practice (cf. [[KR3e0092]]).
- *Zhāngshì yītōng* 張氏醫通 [[KR3er043]] (Zhāng Lù, 1695) — the standard early-Qīng synthetic clinical encyclopaedia.
- *Féngshì jǐnnáng mìlù* 馮氏錦囊秘錄 [[KR3er070]] (Féng Zhàozhāng, 1686–1702, 50 juan) — the comprehensive late-Kāng-xī clinical synthesis.
- *Biànzhèng lù* 辨證錄 [[KR3er077]] and *Shíshì mìlù* 石室秘錄 [[KR3er057]] (Chén Shìduó, 1687) — the polemically distinctive Chén Shìduó synthesis claiming dream-revelation from Lǚ Dòngbīn and Qí Bó.
- *Sìshèng xīnyuán* 四聖心源 [[KR3er047]] (Huáng Yuányù, 1754) — the systematic Qián-lóng-era articulation of the *zhōngqì* 中氣 (central-qì) school grounded in a return to Zhāng Jī.
- *Jǐngyuè quánshū fāhuī* 景岳全書發揮 [[KR3er082]] (attributed to Yè Guì, c. 1700–1745) — the Qīng *wēnbìng*-school polemical critique of the late-Míng *wēnbǔ* programme.

**Mid-Qīng curriculum and the return-to-the-Classics movement.** *Lántái guǐfàn* 蘭臺軌範 [[KR3er086]] (Xú Dàchūn, 1764) — the mid-Qīng curricular synthesis (cf. [[KR3e0097]]). *Yīxué xīnwù* 醫學心悟 [[KR3er048]] (Chéng Guópéng, 1732) — the most widely-circulated mid-Qīng clinical primer, organising treatment by the *bāfǎ* 八法 (eight methods). *Zábìng yuánliú xīzhú* 雜病源流犀燭 [[KR3er065]] and *Shěnshì zūnshēng shū* 沈氏尊生書 [[KR3er109]] (Shěn Jīnáo, 1773) — the late-Qián-lóng aetiology-and-treatment synthesis. *Lèizhèng zhìcái* 類證治裁 [[KR3er061]] (Lín Pèiqín, 1839) — the standard late-Qīng clinical handbook.

**Chén Xiūyuán (Chén Niànzǔ) and the late-Qīng popularisation.** Chén Niànzǔ's *Yīxué sānzì jīng* 醫學三字經 [[KR3er059]] (1804) is the canonical Three-Character-Classic-style medical primer for popular memorisation; his *Yīxué shízàiyì* 醫學實在易 [[KR3er045]] (1808), *Yīyī ǒulù* 醫醫偶錄 [[KR3er066]] (1812), and *Bǐhuā yījìng* 筆花醫鏡 [[KR3er009]] (Jiāng Qiū, 1824 — a contemporary work in the same primer tradition) define the late-Qīng popular curriculum.

**Republican-era and Chinese-Western synthesis.** *Yīlín gǎicuò* 醫林改錯 [[KR3er008]] (Wáng Qīngrèn, 1830) — the proto-scientific anatomical critique based on Wáng's observation of corpses at Luánzhōu plague pits and execution-grounds. *Jiǎnmíng zhōngXī huìcān yīxué túshuō* 簡明中西匯參醫學圖說 [[KR3er117]] (Wáng Yǒuzhōng) — late-Qīng integrationist. *Yīxué jiànnéng* 醫學見能 [[KR3er063]] (Táng Zōnghǎi, 1873) — the principal *zhōngXī huìtōng* synthesiser. *Yīxué zhōngzhōng cānxī lù* 醫學衷中參西錄 [[KR3er071]] (Zhāng Xīchún, 1909–1934) — the magnum opus of Republican-era Chinese medicine. *Zhōngfēng jiàoquán* 中風斠詮 [[KR3er112]] (Zhāng Shòuyí, 1917) — the early-Republican critical re-thinking of stroke aetiology. *Yuányùndòng de gǔ Zhōngyī xué* 圓運動的古中醫學 [[KR3er116]] (Péng Zǐyì, 1921–1947) — the Yún-nán-school *yuányùndòng* (circular-motion) systematisation, still in active TCM use.

**Case-and-essay miscellanies.** A late-Míng / Qīng / Republican sub-tradition of short-form writing on doctrine illustrated by clinical cases: *Yījīng mìzhǐ* 醫經秘旨 [[KR3er105]] (Jiāng Zhīlán, 1662), *Yījīn yīfá* 醫津一筏 [[KR3er103]] (Jiāng Zhīlán, 1662), *Yīxué tǐyòng* 醫學體用 [[KR3er099]], *Qíxiào yīshù* 奇效醫述 [[KR3er127]] (Niè Shànghéng, 1620), *Yīzhōng yīdé* 醫中一得 [[KR3er097]] (1803), *Guòtíng lùcún* 過庭錄存 [[KR3er111]] (Cáo Cúnxīn), and *Jīnglì zálùn xù* 經歷雜論序 [[KR3er110]].

## Important persons

- [[巢元方]] (Cháo Yuánfāng, fl. 605–615) — Suí imperial physician, lead compiler of 《諸病源候論》 [[KR3er002]] (cf. [[KR3e0012]]); foundational *bìngyīn* systematiser.
- [[孫思邈]] (Sūn Sīmiǎo, c. 581–682) — the central Táng clinician, author of the *Qiānjīn* corpus [[KR3er088]] [[KR3er090]] and of the *Dàyī jīngchéng* [[KR3er089]] medical-ethics statement.
- [[王燾]] (Wáng Tāo, c. 670–755) — author of 《外臺秘要》 [[KR3er091]] (cf. [[KR3e0015]]), the great Táng compendium preserving lost SuíTáng formularies.
- [[丹波康賴]] (Tānbō Yasuyori, 912–995) — Heian-Japanese physician, compiler of 《醫心方》 [[KR3er092]], the principal pre-Sòng Chinese-medical *jíyì* source preserving SuíTáng works lost in China.
- [[華佗]] (Huà Tuó, c. 145 – 208) — pseudepigraphic cover-author of the *Zhōngzàng jīng* [[KR3er012]] and the *nèizhào tú* cluster [[KR3er119]] [[KR3er125]]; canonical anchor for the "lost-anatomy" tradition of late-imperial visualisation.
- [[陳言]] (Chén Yán, 1131–1189) — formulator of the three-causes aetiology in 《三因極一病證方論》 [[KR3er055]] (cf. [[KR3e0041]]).
- [[許叔微]] (Xǔ Shūwēi, 1080–1154) — Sòng *Shānghán* expositor, here represented by the *Běnshì fāng xùjí* [[KR3er108]].
- [[劉完素]] (Liú Wánsù, c. 1110–1200) — founder of the *Héjiān* school (*hánliáng* cooling-cold), 《素問玄機原病式》 [[KR3er011]].
- [[張從正]] (Zhāng Cóngzhèng, 1156–1228) — the attacking-purging master, 《儒門事親》 [[KR3er064]].
- [[李杲]] (Lǐ Gǎo, 1180–1251) — *Dōngyuán*, founder of the *píwèi* school; here in 《醫學發明》 [[KR3er041]], 《蘭室秘藏》 [[KR3er062]], 《東垣試效方》 [[KR3er120]].
- [[王好古]] (Wáng Hǎogǔ, c. 1200 – c. 1264) — Lǐ Gǎo's principal disciple; three works [[KR3er030]] [[KR3er033]] [[KR3er034]] continuing the *píwèi* programme.
- [[羅天益]] (Luó Tiānyì, c. 1220–1290) — Lǐ Gǎo's senior pupil, author of 《衛生寶鑑》 [[KR3er052]] and editor of Lǐ Gǎo's late works.
- [[朱震亨]] (Zhū Zhènhēng, 1281–1358) — *Dānxī*, founder of the *zīyīn* school and most prolific Yuán doctrinal author; principal Dānxī works re-edited from Japanese / Korean witnesses appear at [[KR3er017]] [[KR3er018]] [[KR3er019]] [[KR3er024]] [[KR3er027]] [[KR3er042]].
- [[戴思恭]] (Dài Sīgōng, 1324–1405) — Zhū Zhènhēng's foremost disciple and Míng imperial physician; 《秘傳證治要訣及類方》 [[KR3er068]].
- [[劉純]] (Liú Chún, 1340 – c. 1412) — Wáng Lǚ's student; author of the influential primer 《醫經小學》 [[KR3er051]] and continuer of *Yùjī wēiyì* [[KR3er036]].
- [[樓英]] (Lóu Yīng, 1320–1389) — author of 《醫學綱目》 [[KR3er074]], the earliest Míng synthetic *gāngmù*.
- [[徐春甫]] (Xú Chūnfǔ, 1520–1596) — author of 《古今醫統大全》 [[KR3er031]], 100-juan, the principal Míng synthetic encyclopaedia, and convenor of the late-Míng *Yīxué huì* 醫學會 medical society.
- [[王綸]] (Wáng Lún, c. 1453–1510) — Míng imperial physician, author of 《明醫雜著》 [[KR3er040]], the standard late-Míng synthesis.
- [[李梃]] (Lǐ Tǐng, fl. 1573–1619) — author of 《醫學入門》 [[KR3er072]], the curriculum most widely adopted abroad.
- [[龔信]] (Gōng Xìn, fl. 1567–1572) and [[龔廷賢]] (Gōng Tíngxián, c. 1522–1619) — the principal late-Wàn-lì commercial-medical authors of the Jīnxī Gōngshì family ([[KR3er029]] [[KR3er037]] [[KR3er038]] [[KR3er039]] [[KR3er084]] [[KR3er085]]).
- [[趙獻可]] (Zhào Xiànkě, c. 1573 – c. 1644) — author of 《醫貫》 [[KR3er001]], systematic articulation of the *Mìngmén* doctrine.
- [[張介賓]] (Zhāng Jièbīn, 1563–1640) — *Jǐngyuè*, principal Míng *wēnbǔ* thinker; 《景岳全書》 [[KR3er073]] (cf. [[KR3e0086]]).

## Topics

The division is organised around several overlapping intellectual currents:

**The genre of "comprehensive medical writing" (綜合醫書).** The defining bibliographical category of `KR3er` is also its principal intellectual content. From the Suí *Yuánhòu lùn* and the Táng *Qiānjīn* / *Wàitái* through the Míng *yītǒng dàquán* and *gāngmù*, the Qīng *yītōng* and *zūnshēng* compilations, and the Republican synthesis attempts, a single genre-history runs unbroken: the periodic attempt to bring the whole of Chinese medical knowledge under a single architectural frame. Xú Chūnfǔ's *Gǔjīn yītǒng dàquán* [[KR3er031]] is the central monument of this tradition; Lóu Yīng's *Yīxué gāngmù* [[KR3er074]] is its earliest Míng systematic attempt; and the Republican-era *Yīxué zhōngzhōng cānxī lù* [[KR3er071]] is its latest pre-modern instantiation.

**The Mìngmén / wēnbǔ controversy.** The late-Míng warming-tonifying programme of Zhào Xiànkě [[KR3er001]], Zhāng Jièbīn [[KR3er073]], and Lǐ Zhōngzǐ [[KR3er083]] [[KR3er079]] is in this division balanced by its Qīng polemical critics — Yè Guì's *Jǐngyuè quánshū fāhuī* [[KR3er082]], Xú Dàchūn's *Yīguàn biān* (in [[KR3eq048]], outside this division), and the implicit critique of Yú Chāng's *Yīmén fǎlǜ* [[KR3er007]]. The 命門 (gate-of-life) doctrine, displacing the heart from the position of *jūnzhǔ* 君主 ("ruler") of the body, is the principal doctrinal innovation of late-Míng synthetic medicine, with consequences across the entire Qīng tradition.

**The JīnYuán school re-edition programme.** A distinguishing feature of `KR3er` is the depth of its Jīn-Yuán-master coverage in alternative shànběn witnesses: every school is represented from Japanese-preserved Yuán or early-Míng editions, often filling lacunae or correcting variants in the WYG-edition transmission preserved in `KR3e` proper. This is most pronounced for the *Dānxī* corpus (six works in `KR3er` vs. three in `KR3e`) and the *Dōngyuán* corpus (three works in `KR3er`).

**The bāfǎ (eight methods) and clinical pattern-recognition.** Chéng Guópéng's *Yīxué xīnwù* [[KR3er048]] articulates the *bāfǎ* — *hàn* 汗 (sweat) / *tù* 吐 (vomit) / *xià* 下 (purge) / *hé* 和 (harmonise) / *wēn* 溫 (warm) / *qīng* 清 (cool) / *xiāo* 消 (resolve) / *bǔ* 補 (tonify) — that becomes the canonical Qīng frame for organising treatment. Most of the late-Qīng clinical primers ([[KR3er061]] [[KR3er023]] [[KR3er086]] [[KR3er045]]) presuppose this organisation.

**Pseudepigraphy and the recovery of "lost antiquity".** The recurrent late-imperial appeal to Huá Tuó, Biǎn Què, and other Hàn-and-earlier figures as cover-authors for late-Míng / Qīng compilations is well-represented here ([[KR3er012]] [[KR3er020]] [[KR3er119]] [[KR3er125]] [[KR3er105]] etc.). The phenomenon is bibliographically transparent — the works are demonstrably late — but doctrinally consequential, since it allows the late-imperial reorganisations of medicine to claim canonical HànSòng authority.

**Chinese-Western synthesis (zhōngXī huìtōng).** A late-Qīng / Republican movement responding to the introduction of Western anatomy, physiology, and pharmacology into Chinese medical practice. The principal figures here are Wáng Qīngrèn (a proto-anatomist independent of Western input, [[KR3er008]]), Táng Zōnghǎi [[KR3er063]], Wáng Yǒuzhōng [[KR3er117]], and Zhāng Xīchún [[KR3er071]], with Péng Zǐyì [[KR3er116]] as a counterposition that insists on a purely Chinese cosmological frame.

**The hxwd source and the politics of "repatriated heritage".** The conceptual frame of the *Hǎiwài huíliú zhōngyī shànběn gǔjí cóngshū* — that rare witnesses preserved abroad (especially in Japanese Edo-period reprints and Korean Chosŏn-period editions) constitute a recoverable Chinese national-medical heritage — is itself a topic of modern historiography. Edo Japan in particular preserved many YuánMíng commercial medical editions in better shape than mainland China after the WànlìQiánlóng print runs were exhausted, and the hxwd programme has had material consequences for textual scholarship since 2003.
