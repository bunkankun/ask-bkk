# Nèijīng bó yì 內經博議
*Wide-Ranging Discussions on the Inner Classic*
by [[羅美]] (Luó Měi, *fl.* 1675, 清) — author

## About the work

The *Nèijīng bó yì* in four juan is an early-Kāngxī series of discursive essays on selected topics from the *Sùwèn* and *Língshū* by **Luó Měi** 羅美 (*zì* Dàn-shēng 澹生, *hào* Dōng-yì 東逸), an early-Qing physician of Xīnzhōu 新洲 (Ānhuī). The work is dated to *Kāngxī* 14 = 1675. Unlike a continuous commentary, it consists of about thirty topical essays — 陰陽論, 五行論, 藏象論, 經絡論, 病能論, *yùn-qì* 運氣論, 治法論, 脈論, and so on — each developing a single doctrinal point through citation of *Nèijīng* and post-classical evidence.

### Prefaces

The hxwd source opens (`KR3ea046_000.txt`) with an *appended* essay rather than the proper authorial preface: *Fù lù: Zhāng Zǐ-hé jiǔ qì gǎn jí lùn* 附錄張子和九氣感疾論 — an appendix-style attachment of Zhāng Zǐ-hé's 張子和 (Zhāng Cōng-zhèng 張從正, 1156–1228, one of the four Jīn-Yuán masters) essay on the nine *qì*-related disease categories. Zhāng Zǐ-hé's text, in its turn, glosses the *Sùwèn*'s *Jǔ tòng lùn* 舉痛論 doctrine of disease arising from the disturbance of *qì* — anger reverses *qì* upward producing vomiting of blood and *flank-thoroughfare-collapse* (飧泄); joy slackens *qì*; sorrow makes the heart-system constrict and the lung lobes lift; etc. Luó's editorial inclusion of Zhāng's essay at the head of his own work signals a deliberate doctrinal alignment with the Jīn-Yuán "*qì-jī* / *xiǎo-fá*" school of Liú Wánsù [[劉完素]] and Zhāng Cōng-zhèng.

### Abstract

Luó Měi is recorded in the *Sìkù quánshū* tíyào on the *Nèijīng bó yì* as 新洲羅美 (no further biographical data). The work was admitted to the *Sìkù* and is conventionally classed with Wāng Áng's [[KR3ea039]] *Lèi zuǎn yuē zhù* and the Lǚshān *Jí zhù* ([[KR3ea008]]) as one of the three serious early-Qīng systematic engagements with *Nèijīng* doctrine. Luó's distinctive contribution is the discursive-essay form (博議 = "wide-ranging discussion") which allows for sustained doctrinal argument outside the line-by-line commentary genre. His clinical orientation is broadly Liú-Hé-jiān (cooling) tempered by Sōngjiāng-school *Mìng-mén* warming.
