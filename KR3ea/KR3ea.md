## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR3ea` 醫經 (*Yī jīng*, "Medical Classics") is the first sub-division of `KR3e` 醫家類 (Masters — Medicine) and gathers the foundational early-imperial canon of Chinese medical doctrine together with its full commentarial afterlife. The two anchor texts are the *Huángdì nèijīng* 黃帝內經 — transmitted in its two halves *Sùwèn* 素問 and *Língshū* 靈樞, plus the parallel Táng recension *Tàisù* 太素 — and the *Bāshíyī Nànjīng* 八十一難經 ("Classic of eighty-one difficulties"), conventionally attributed to Qín Yuèrén 秦越人 (the legendary Biǎn Què 扁鵲) but actually a Hàn compilation. To these the division adds the lost *Huángdì wàijīng* 黃帝外經 (KR3ea067) and the Mǎwáng-dūi excavated medical corpus (KR3ea066), the latter giving manuscript witnesses for the pre-*Nèijīng* state of medical writing in the late Warring States and early Western Hàn. Endymion Wilkinson (*Chinese History: A New Manual*, Section 41.3.1, "General Works") treats the *Huángdì nèijīng* and the *Nán-jīng* together as the indispensable starting-point for any historian working on Chinese medicine — a framing this overview adopts, since every other text in the division is in some sense a commentary on, abridgement of, mnemonic for, or polemical re-arrangement of these two works.

Chronologically the division spans more than two millennia. The Mǎwáng-dūi silk and bamboo medical texts (KR3ea066) are c. late 4th to mid-2nd century BCE; the *Sùwèn* and *Língshū* reached their received state by the late Warring States or early Hàn; the *Nànjīng* is a Hàn compilation; the *Tàisù* survives in [[楊上善]] Yáng Shàngshàn's high-Táng commentary of 666–683 (KR3ea031); [[王冰]] Wáng Bīng's 762 redaction of the *Sùwèn* (KR3ea001) and [[林億]] Lín Yì's Northern-Sòng 校正醫書局 collation of 1057–1069 (KR3ea003) fixed the standard recensions still used today. Three doctrinal moments then drive the commentarial tradition: the Jīn-Yuán *wǔyùn liùqì* 五運六氣 revival (Liú Wánsù in this division at [[KR3ea015]], [[KR3ea017]], [[KR3ea045]]); the late-Míng to early-Qīng comprehensive commentaries of [[馬蒔]] Mǎ Shī, [[吳崐]] Wú Kūn, [[張志聰]] Zhāng Zhìcōng, and [[張介賓]] Zhāng Jièbīn; and the high-Qīng critical–philological turn carried by [[徐大椿]] Xú Dàchūn, [[黃元御]] Huáng Yuányù, the Edo *Tanba* lineage of [[丹波元簡]] / [[丹波元堅]] / [[丹波元胤]], and the late-Qīng evidential scholars [[胡澍]] Hú Shù, [[俞樾]] Yú Yuè, and [[陸懋修]] Lù Màoxiū. The division closes with [[唐宗海]] Táng Zōnghǎi's pioneering Chinese–Western synthesis (KR3ea044, 1892) and [[張驥]] Zhāng Jì's *Yào yuè* of 1924 (KR3ea042).

## Important texts and text clusters

**The base classics.** Three early-imperial works are the doctrinal centre of the division.
- *Huángdì nèijīng Sùwèn* 黃帝內經素問 [[KR3ea001]] — the dialogic cosmological-physiological canon in 24 juan / 81 篇, transmitted in [[王冰]] Wáng Bīng's 762 redaction. The hxwd witness is the Jiājìng 顧從德 1550 facsimile of the Northern-Sòng校正醫書局 print.
- *Bāshíyī Nànjīng* 八十一難經 [[KR3ea054]] — 81 *nán* on pulse diagnosis, channels, viscera, pathology, *shū*-points, and acupuncture method; the principal early companion-classic to the *Nèijīng*, source of the *cùn-guān-chǐ* 寸關尺 pulse system and of the right-kidney-as-*mìng-mén* 命門 doctrine.
- *Huángdì nèijīng Tài sù* 黃帝內經太素 [[KR3ea031]] — [[楊上善]] Yáng Shàngshàn's Táng (666–683) re-arrangement of the *Nèijīng* corpus into thirty topical juan, lost in Sòng China but preserved in Japan and re-introduced by [[蕭延平]] Xiāo Yán-píng in 1924; the only independent surviving pre-Wáng-Bīng witness to the *Sùwèn* / *Língshū* text.

**The Sòng校正醫書局 recensions.** [[林億]] Lín Yì and his colleagues [[高保衡]] Gāo Bǎohéng and [[孫奇]] Sūn Qí re-edited the *Sùwèn* in 1057–1069: *Chóng-guǎng bǔ-zhù Huángdì nèijīng Sùwèn* 重廣補注黃帝內經素問 [[KR3ea003]], with the supplementary *Yí piān* 遺篇 ("missing chapters" — 刺法論 and 本病論) restored from late-Táng witnesses. The same bureau's *Língshū* recension was lost in the Southern Sòng and only re-published in 1155 by Shǐ Sōng 史崧, the version that survives.

**Excavated and lost classics.** The *Mǎwáng-dūi jiǎn-bó* 馬王堆簡帛 [[KR3ea066]] (tomb closed 168 BCE) preserves fifteen medical texts on silk and bamboo — among them the early channel-and-cauterization manuals *Zú-bì shíyī-mài jiǔ-jīng* 足臂十一脈灸經, *Yīn-yáng shíyī-mài jiǔ-jīng* 陰陽十一脈灸經, the *Wǔshí'èr bìngfāng* 五十二病方, and yangsheng / sexual-cultivation texts — and is the principal witness to the state of medical writing just before the *Nèijīng* reached its received form. The *Huángdì wàijīng* 黃帝外經 [[KR3ea067]] is the lost companion-classic to the *Nèijīng* listed in the *Hàn shū yìwén zhì*; the related entry *Wàijīng wēiyán* 外經微言 [[KR3ea051]] is a late-17th-century Qīng pseudepigraphic recasting by [[陳士鐸]] Chén Shìduó.

**The Sòng *yùnqì* school and Liú Wánsù.** [[劉完素]] Liú Wánsù (*Héjiān*) inaugurates the systematic Sòng-Jīn recovery of the *Sùwèn*'s *wǔyùn liùqì* 五運六氣 cosmological-clinical doctrine. Three of his works in this division — *Sùwèn yào-zhǐ lùn* 素問要旨論 [[KR3ea015]], *Sùwèn bìng-jī qì-yí bǎo-mìng jí* 素問病機氣宜保命集 [[KR3ea017]], and *Huángdì Sùwèn xuān-míng lùn fāng* 黃帝素問宣明論方 [[KR3ea045]] — make him the most heavily-represented commentator on the *Sùwèn* in KR3ea after Wáng Bīng himself. The pseudepigraphic *Sùwèn liùqì xuán-zhū mì-yǔ* 素問六氣玄珠密語 [[KR3ea016]] transmits a closely-related Táng-rooted *yùnqì* tradition under Wáng Bīng's name. The pedagogical follow-up tradition is preserved in [[汪機]] Wāng Jī's *Yùnqì yì-lǎn* 運氣易覽 [[KR3ea050]] (Míng); [[吳謙]] Wú Qiān et al.'s imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn · Yùnqì yào-jué* 醫宗金鑑·運氣要訣 [[KR3ea018]] (1742); [[王旭高]] Wáng Xùgāo's mid-Qīng *Yùnqì zhèngzhì gē-jué* 運氣證治歌訣 [[KR3ea019]]; and the three Lù Màoxiū *Yùnqì* works [[KR3ea047]]–[[KR3ea049]].

**The late-Yuán to late-Míng *Nèijīng* commentaries.** [[滑壽]] Huá Shòu's abridgement *Dú Sùwèn chāo* 讀素問鈔 [[KR3ea011]] (c. 1355–1370, with [[汪機]] Wāng Jī's Míng continuation) is the first sustained systematic re-organisation of the *Sùwèn*. [[馬蒔]] Mǎ Shī's *Huángdì nèijīng Língshū zhù-zhèng fā-wēi* 黃帝內經靈樞注證發微 [[KR3ea035]] (1580–1586) is the **first comprehensive systematic commentary on the *Língshū***, decisive for opening the difficult acumoxa half of the *Nèijīng* to broad readership. [[吳崐]] Wú Kūn's *Sùwèn Wú zhù* 素問吳注 [[KR3ea014]] (1594) and [[張介賓]] Zhāng Jièbīn's monumental *Lèijīng* 類經 [[KR3ea036]] (1624 — re-organising both *Sùwèn* and *Língshū* under twelve topical headings, with 11-juan *tú-yì* and 4-juan *fù-yì* appendices) are the late-Míng benchmarks. [[李中梓]] Lǐ Zhōngzǐ's compact pedagogical *Nèijīng zhī-yào* 內經知要 [[KR3ea038]] (1642) became the most-used Míng-Qīng curriculum primer.

**The early- and mid-Qīng commentaries.** [[張志聰]] Zhāng Zhìcōng's collaborative *Lǚ-shān jiǎng-táng* 侶山講堂 commentaries — *Sùwèn jí zhù* [[KR3ea008]] (1670) and *Língshū jí zhù* [[KR3ea025]] (1672) — set the doctrinal standard against which most subsequent *Nèijīng* commentaries position themselves. Parallel projects: [[姚紹虞]] Yáo Shàoyú's *Sùwèn jīng-zhù jié-jiě* 素問經注節解 [[KR3ea005]] (1671); [[高士宗]] Gāo Shìzōng's *Huángdì Sùwèn zhí-jiě* 黃帝素問直解 [[KR3ea007]] (1695); [[羅美]] Luó Měi's *Nèijīng bó-yì* 內經博議 [[KR3ea046]] (1675); [[汪昂]] Wāng Áng's *Sùwèn Língshū lèi-zuǎn yuē-zhù* 素問靈樞類纂約注 [[KR3ea039]] (1689); [[薛雪]] Xuē Xuě's *Yī jīng yuán-zhǐ* 醫經原旨 [[KR3ea041]] (1754); [[沈又彭]] Shěn Yòupéng's *Yī jīng dú* 醫經讀 [[KR3ea043]] (1764).

**The Huáng Yuányù programme.** [[黃元御]] Huáng Yuányù (1705–1758), the *Sì-shèng* 四聖 ("Four Sages": Huángdì, Qí-bó, Yuè-rén, Zhòng-jǐng) systematizer, produced an integrated re-interpretation of all four medical Classics. In this division: *Sù-líng wēi-yùn* 素靈微蘊 [[KR3ea053]] (1740), *Sì-shèng xuán-shū* 四聖懸樞 [[KR3ea052]] (1749–1752), *Sùwèn xuán-jiě* 素問懸解 [[KR3ea013]] (1753), *Língshū xuán-jiě* 靈樞懸解 [[KR3ea027]] (1756), and *Nànjīng xuán-jiě* 難經懸解 [[KR3ea063]] (1753). With [[徐大椿]] Xú Dàchūn's *Nànjīng jīng-shì* 難經經釋 [[KR3ea059]] (1727) these works are the high-Qīng systematic recovery of the medical Classics as an integrated doctrinal whole.

**The Edo *Tanba* lineage and 19th-century philology.** Three generations of the Edo *Tài-yī yuàn* Tanba family produced critical commentaries that are essential to modern Chinese-medical textual scholarship: [[丹波元簡]] Tanba no Mototane's *Sùwèn shí* 素問識 [[KR3ea010]] (1806) and *Língshū shí* 靈樞識 [[KR3ea026]] (1806–1810); [[丹波元堅]] Tanba no Motokata's *Sùwèn shào-shí* 素問紹識 [[KR3ea012]] (1846–1854); [[丹波元胤]] Tanba no Mototsugu's *Nànjīng shū-zhèng* 難經疏證 [[KR3ea062]] (1819). In late-Qīng China the same critical impulse is carried by [[胡澍]] Hú Shù's *Sùwèn jiào-yì* 黃帝內經素問校義 [[KR3ea006]] (1860s) and [[俞樾]] Yú Yuè's *Nèijīng biàn-yán* 內經辨言 [[KR3ea034]] (1881–1899), which apply the *Gāo-yóu* school's evidential method to the medical Classics; [[陸懋修]] Lù Màoxiū's three *Yùnqì*-and-lexicon works [[KR3ea047]]–[[KR3ea049]] (1879–1884); [[周學海]] Zhōu Xuéhǎi's *Nèijīng píng-wén* 內經評文 [[KR3ea032]] (1898), a literary-prosodic commentary; and [[章楠]] Zhāng Nán's clinically-oriented *Líng-sù jié-zhù lèi-biān* 靈素節注類編 [[KR3ea040]] (1834). [[唐宗海]] Táng Zōnghǎi's *Zhōng-Xī huì-tōng yī jīng jīng-yì* 中西匯通醫經精義 [[KR3ea044]] (1892) is the first systematic attempt at a Chinese-Western synthesis of the *Nèijīng* anatomy and physiology, and [[張驥]] Zhāng Jì's *Nèijīng yào-yuè* 內經藥瀹 [[KR3ea042]] (1924) closes the division as a late witness to the same critical-philological tradition.

**The *Nànjīng* tradition.** The standard commentary line after the base text [[KR3ea054]] runs through: the Yuán *Nànjīng jí-zhù* 難經集註 [[KR3ea056]] of Wáng Hànlín 王翰林 (see [[王九思]]); [[李駉]] Lǐ Jiǒng's Southern-Sòng *Huángdì bāshíyī Nànjīng zǎn-tú jù-jiě* 黃帝八十一難經纂圖句解 [[KR3ea065]] (1269); [[滑壽]] Huá Shòu's authoritative Yuán *Nànjīng běn-yì* 難經本義 [[KR3ea060]] / [[KR3ea061]] (1361 — the *Sìkù quánshū* preferred witness); the late-Míng popularised *Qiè Wáng-shì mìchuán tú-zhù bāshíyī Nànjīng pínglín* [[KR3ea064]]; the three high-Qīng commentaries of Xú Dàchūn [[KR3ea059]], [[丁錦]] Dīng Jǐn ([[KR3ea055]] and [[KR3ea057]]), and Huáng Yuányù [[KR3ea063]]; and the late-Qīng *Nànjīng zhèng-yì* 難經正義 of [[葉霖]] Yè Lín [[KR3ea058]] (1895).

**Late-Míng vernacular epitomes.** [[胡文煥]] Hú Wénhuàn's parallel pair *Sùwèn xīn-dé* 素問心得 [[KR3ea020]] and *Língshū xīn-dé* 靈樞心得 [[KR3ea028]] (c. 1592–1604) are popular-print epitomes for a broad late-Míng readership; the Kamakura-period Japanese *Yī-jiā qiān-zì wén* 醫家千字文 of Korenori Tokitoshi [[惟宗時俊]] [[KR3ea033]] (1287–1304) is a foreign witness to the same medical-Classic curriculum.

## Important persons

- [[王冰]] (Wáng Bīng, *fl.* 762) — Táng redactor of the *Sùwèn*; supplied the seven great treatises on *yùnqì*; the base text and commentary of [[KR3ea001]] and the foundation of all later *Nèijīng* study.
- [[林億]] (Lín Yì, *fl.* 1057–1069) — head of the Northern-Sòng 校正醫書局; with [[高保衡]] and [[孫奇]] produced the *Chóng-guǎng bǔ-zhù* recension of the *Sùwèn* [[KR3ea003]] still in use.
- [[楊上善]] (Yáng Shàngshàn, *fl.* 666–683) — Táng commentator on the *Tàisù* [[KR3ea031]], whose 30-juan topical re-arrangement is the only independent surviving pre-Wáng-Bīng witness to the *Nèijīng* corpus.
- [[秦越人]] (Qín Yuèrén, conventional *Biǎn Què*; pseudepigraphic) — traditional author of the *Bāshíyī Nànjīng* [[KR3ea054]].
- [[劉完素]] (Liú Wánsù, c. 1110–1200) — Jīn founder of the *Hé-jiān* / cooling-cold school and inaugurator of the systematic Sòng recovery of *wǔyùn liùqì*; author of three works in this division ([[KR3ea015]], [[KR3ea017]], [[KR3ea045]]).
- [[滑壽]] (Huá Shòu, *fl.* late 14th c.) — produced both the earliest sustained *Sùwèn* abridgement [[KR3ea011]] and the canonical Yuán *Nànjīng běn-yì* [[KR3ea060]].
- [[馬蒔]] (Mǎ Shī, *fl.* 1580–1586) — first comprehensive commentator on the *Língshū* [[KR3ea035]]; opens the acumoxa half of the *Nèijīng* to broad scholarly study.
- [[吳崐]] (Wú Kūn, 1551–1620) — Wǎnlì-era Huīzhōu commentator on the *Sùwèn* [[KR3ea014]]; one of the three benchmark late-Míng to early-Qīng *Nèijīng* commentaries.
- [[張介賓]] (Zhāng Jièbīn, 1563–1640) — author of the *Lèijīng* [[KR3ea036]], the most ambitious topical re-presentation of the *Nèijīng* in the entire tradition.
- [[張志聰]] (Zhāng Zhìcōng, 1610–1674) — leader of the Hángzhōu *Lǚ-shān jiǎng-táng*; principal compiler of the collaborative *Sùwèn jí zhù* [[KR3ea008]] and *Língshū jí zhù* [[KR3ea025]] — the early-Qīng standard.
- [[李中梓]] (Lǐ Zhōngzǐ, 1588–1655) — author of the most-used pedagogical primer *Nèijīng zhī-yào* [[KR3ea038]].
- [[黃元御]] (Huáng Yuányù, 1705–1758) — *Sì-shèng* systematizer; produced an integrated re-reading of *Sùwèn* [[KR3ea013]], *Língshū* [[KR3ea027]], *Nànjīng* [[KR3ea063]], plus [[KR3ea052]] and [[KR3ea053]].
- [[徐大椿]] (Xú Dàchūn, 1693–1771) — high-Qīng critic and call-to-return programmatist; in this division through *Nànjīng jīng-shì* [[KR3ea059]].
- [[陳士鐸]] (Chén Shìduó, c. 1627–1707) — Qīng author / transmitter of the pseudepigraphic *Wàijīng wēi-yán* [[KR3ea051]], the only post-classical witness to the lost *Wàijīng* tradition.
- [[汪昂]] (Wāng Áng, 1615–c. 1699) — popular Qīng medical encyclopaedist; author of *Sùwèn Língshū lèi-zuǎn yuē-zhù* [[KR3ea039]], a widely-circulated topical anthology.
- [[薛雪]] (Xuē Xuě, 1681–1770) — mid-Qīng Suzhou physician of the early *wēnbìng* circle; author of *Yī jīng yuán-zhǐ* [[KR3ea041]].

- [[章楠]] (Zhāng Nán, *fl.* 1834) — clinically-oriented mid-Qīng topical re-organizer of *Sùwèn* and *Língshū* [[KR3ea040]].

- [[蕭延平]] (Xiāo Yán-píng, *fl.* 1897–1924) — late-Qīng / Republican collator of the *Tàisù* from the Japanese Ninna-ji manuscript [[KR3ea031]].
- [[惟宗時俊]] (Korenori Tokitoshi, 1196–1274) — Kamakura *Tài-yī* physician; author of the medical mnemonic primer *Yī-jiā qiān-zì wén* [[KR3ea033]].
- [[胡文煥]] (Hú Wénhuàn, *fl.* late 16th – early 17th c.) — late-Míng Hángzhōu publisher and editor; produced the popular twin epitomes [[KR3ea020]] and [[KR3ea028]].
- [[高保衡]] (Gāo Bǎohéng) and [[孫奇]] (Sūn Qí) — Lín Yì's principal colleagues in the 1057–1069 校正醫書局 *Sùwèn* recension [[KR3ea003]].
- [[顧從德]] (Gù Cóngdé, *fl.* mid-16th c.) — Jiājìng-era Shànghǎi publisher of the 1550 facsimile woodblock edition of the Sòng校正 *Sùwèn* [[KR3ea001]].

## Topics

The division is organised around a number of long-running debates and interpretive problems.

**The *Sùwèn*–*Língshū* problem.** Whether the two halves of the *Nèijīng* form a single coherent doctrinal work, or two redactionally and topically distinct compilations later associated, is the single most important framing question in *Nèijīng* studies. The Mǎwáng-dūi excavated channel-and-cauterization manuals [[KR3ea066]] suggest a long pre-*Nèijīng* prehistory in which the channel-acumoxa material (later *Língshū*) developed semi-independently from the diagnostic-cosmological-pulse material (later *Sùwèn*). The *Tàisù* [[KR3ea031]] redaction merges them under topical headings; the commentarial tradition oscillates between treating them together (Zhāng Jièbīn [[KR3ea036]], Zhāng Zhìcōng's paired commentaries [[KR3ea008]] and [[KR3ea025]], Wāng Áng [[KR3ea039]], Lǐ Zhōngzǐ [[KR3ea038]], Zhāng Nán [[KR3ea040]]) and treating them separately (Mǎ Shī's pair, Huáng Yuányù's pair, the Tanba *shí* / *shào-shí* pair).

**The seven great treatises and *wǔyùn liùqì* 五運六氣.** Wáng Bīng's 762 addition of *Sùwèn* j. 19–22 (the so-called "seven great treatises") supplied the cosmological-epidemiological doctrine of the five circulatory phases and six climatic influences. From [[劉完素]] Liú Wánsù onward this becomes the central doctrinal kernel of *Nèijīng* studies: it generates the formularies of [[KR3ea015]], [[KR3ea017]], [[KR3ea045]], the Sòng systematisations *Sùwèn rù-shì yùn-qì lùn-ào* (in [[KR3e]]) and *Yùnqì xuán-zhū* [[KR3ea016]], the Míng *Yùnqì yì-lǎn* [[KR3ea050]] of Wāng Jī, the imperial *Yùnqì yào-jué* [[KR3ea018]] of Wú Qiān, the mid-Qīng *Yùnqì zhèngzhì gē-jué* [[KR3ea019]], and Lù Màoxiū's triple [[KR3ea047]]–[[KR3ea049]].

**Critical philology and the recovery of the original text.** From [[滑壽]] Huá Shòu's 1361 *Nànjīng běn-yì* [[KR3ea060]] onward, the search for the *běn-yì* 本義 ("original meaning") of the medical Classics is a continuous critical project: the late-Yuán abridgement-and-correction tradition (Huá's *Dú Sùwèn chāo* [[KR3ea011]]), the Edo Tanba commentaries with their integration of Japanese-preserved Tàisù evidence ([[KR3ea010]] [[KR3ea012]] [[KR3ea026]] [[KR3ea062]]), and the *Gāo-yóu*-school late-Qīng evidential philology of Hú Shù [[KR3ea006]] and Yú Yuè [[KR3ea034]] are the principal moments. [[徐大椿]] Xú Dàchūn's *Nànjīng jīng-shì* [[KR3ea059]] is the programmatic statement that *Nèijīng* doctrine is the standard against which the *Nànjīng* must be measured — a position [[黃元御]] Huáng Yuányù pushes still further by treating all four medical Classics as a single doctrinal whole [[KR3ea013]] [[KR3ea027]] [[KR3ea052]] [[KR3ea053]] [[KR3ea063]].

**The *mìng-mén* 命門 doctrine.** *Nànjīng* 36 located the *mìng-mén* in the right kidney, against the *Nèijīng*'s placement between the kidneys (or in the *xīn-bāo* 心包). This becomes one of the most-debated doctrinal problems in late-Míng to Qīng medicine and is a recurrent motif in nearly every *Sùwèn*-and-*Nànjīng* commentary in the division.

**The Chinese-Western synthesis.** [[唐宗海]] Táng Zōnghǎi's *Zhōng-Xī huì-tōng yī jīng jīng-yì* [[KR3ea044]] is the first systematic attempt to read the *Nèijīng*'s organ and channel doctrine alongside late-Qīng Western anatomical knowledge. Although later regarded as transitional and methodologically uneven, it is the historical starting-point of the *Zhōng-Xī yī huì-tōng* movement.

**Excavated medicine and the prehistory of the *Nèijīng*.** The Mǎwáng-dūi corpus [[KR3ea066]], together with the Zhāng-jiā-shān, Shuāng-bāo-shān, Lǎo-guān-shān, and Tiān-huí *Biǎn-què* manuscripts (not collected here), has transformed the question of how the medical Classics came into being. The presence of the entry in KR3ea signals the now-standard view that the *Nèijīng* must be read as the late-Warring-States / early-Hàn synthesis of an older multi-stranded technical tradition.

**The lost *Wàijīng* problem.** The *Huángdì wàijīng* [[KR3ea067]] was lost in antiquity; the *Wàijīng wēi-yán* [[KR3ea051]] is universally regarded by modern scholarship as a late-17th-century forgery. The pair stands as a reminder that the surface continuity of the *Nèijīng* tradition conceals at least one major canonical work that was already gone by the Suí.

## Timeline

- c. 300–168 BCE — *Mǎwáng-dūi jiǎn-bó* 馬王堆簡帛 [[KR3ea066]] — late-Warring-States to early-Western-Hàn medical manuscripts, witnesses to the pre-*Nèijīng* state of channel, drug, and self-cultivation writing.
- *Late Warring States – Western Hàn* — formation of the *Sùwèn* and *Língshū* (transmitted as [[KR3ea001]]).
- *c. 1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE* — *Bāshíyī Nànjīng* 八十一難經 [[KR3ea054]] — Hàn pseudepigraphic compilation under Qín Yuèrén / Biǎn Què's name.
- *c. 200 BCE – 100 CE* — *Huángdì wàijīng* 黃帝外經 [[KR3ea067]] — lost; bibliographic ghost in the *Hàn shū yìwén zhì*.
- 666–683 — *Huángdì nèijīng Tài sù* 黃帝內經太素 [[KR3ea031]] — Yáng Shàngshàn's high-Táng 30-juan topical re-arrangement of the *Nèijīng* with full commentary; survives only via the Japanese Ninna-ji 仁和寺 manuscript.
- 762 — *Huángdì nèijīng Sùwèn* 黃帝內經素問 [[KR3ea001]] — Wáng Bīng's restoration recension; supplies the seven great *yùnqì* treatises and the base commentary.
- *Late Táng – Northern Sòng* (received recension) — *Sùwèn liùqì xuán-zhū mì-yǔ* 素問六氣玄珠密語 [[KR3ea016]] — pseudepigraphic *yùnqì* treatise transmitted under Wáng Bīng's name.
- 1057–1069 — *Chóng-guǎng bǔ-zhù Huángdì nèijīng Sùwèn* 重廣補注黃帝內經素問 [[KR3ea003]] — Lín Yì's 校正醫書局 collation; the standard scholarly recension.
- 1172–1200 — Liú Wánsù's *Sùwèn* programme: *Huángdì Sùwèn xuān-míng lùn fāng* 黃帝素問宣明論方 [[KR3ea045]], *Sùwèn yào-zhǐ lùn* 素問要旨論 [[KR3ea015]], *Sùwèn bìng-jī qì-yí bǎo-mìng jí* 素問病機氣宜保命集 [[KR3ea017]] — the Jīn-Yuán *wǔyùn liùqì* revival.
- 1269 — *Huángdì bāshíyī Nànjīng zǎn-tú jù-jiě* 黃帝八十一難經纂圖句解 [[KR3ea065]] — Lǐ Jiǒng's Southern-Sòng *Nànjīng* commentary with diagrams.
- 1287–1304 — *Yī-jiā qiān-zì wén* 醫家千字文 [[KR3ea033]] — Korenori Tokitoshi's Kamakura medical mnemonic primer.
- *Yuán* — *Nànjīng jí-zhù* 難經集註 [[KR3ea056]] — Wáng Hànlín's compiled *Nànjīng* commentary (later re-printed in the Míng).
- c. 1355–1370 — *Dú Sùwèn chāo* 讀素問鈔 [[KR3ea011]] — Huá Shòu's *Sùwèn* abridgement (with later Wāng Jī continuation).
