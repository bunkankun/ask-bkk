# Hùshēnmìng jīng 護身命經
*Sūtra for Protecting Body and Life* (variant recension)
Anonymous Chinese composition; survives in a Dūnhuáng manuscript from the Nakamura 中村 collection.

## About the work
A divergent one-fascicle recension of the apocryphal *Hùshēnmìng jīng*, related to but textually distinct from [[KR6u0001]] (T85n2865). The opening of the manuscript is broken; the surviving body presents twelve "noble wisdoms" (十二聖慧), an attendant set of vows, and an injunction that *bǐqiūnī*, *upāsaka*, *upāsikā*, kings' sons, and palace women who maintain these twelve practices will be reborn in good circumstances. The shared kernel with T2865 is the framing situation — the Buddha's deathbed instruction at the twin *śāla* trees — and the homiletic insistence on copying, reciting, and circulating the scripture as the operative meritorious act.

### Abstract
The Tàishō editors took this text from a manuscript privately held in the Nakamura collection, distinct from the Pelliot manuscript that underlies [[KR6u0001]]. Hobogirin (no. 2866) records the source as "Coll. Nakamura" and notes the cross-reference to no. 2865. CANWWW (`div09.xml`) lists T85N2865 and T85N2866 as parallel related texts. The shared title and shared deathbed-injunction frame, but partly non-overlapping content, point to two stages or two regional variants of a single Dūnhuáng-circulating apocryphon rather than two unrelated compositions. Pre-Táng catalogues do not register the work; later catalogues subsume scriptures of this title under the rubric of doubtful or forged sūtras (偽疑). Makita (1976) treats T2865 and T2866 together as a single apocryphal cluster. As with most Dūnhuáng yiwei scriptures of this type, the social function — talismanic, protective, life-extending — is more securely reconstructed than the date or place of composition, although a Northern-Dynasties to Suí–Táng window is the consensus bracket.
