# Zhèn-chuān jí 震川集
*Thunder-Stream Collection*
by [[歸有光]] (撰), [[歸莊]] (編)

## About the work
The literary collection of **Guī Yǒu-guāng** 歸有光 (1506–1571), *zì* **Xī-fǔ** 熙甫 / **Kāi-fǔ** 開甫, *hào* **Zhèn-chuān** 震川 (also **Xiàng-jí** 項脊), of **Kūn-shān** 昆山 (Sū-zhōu, Jiāng-sū). Jiā-jìng 19 (1540) *jǔ-rén*; not *jìn-shì* until **Jiā-jìng 44** (1565, *yǐ-chǒu*) — only at age 60 *sui*; *Cháng-shú zhī-xiàn*, later *Nán-jīng Tài-pú sì chéng*. Guī is the principal master of the **Tang-Sòng-pài** prose in the lineage that runs **Wáng Shèn-zhōng** [[KR4e0185]] → **Táng Shùn-zhī** [[KR4e0190]] → **Guī Yǒu-guāng** → **Máo Kūn**; he is the *foremost* prose master of the entire Míng dynasty in the Qīng *Tóng-chéng* school's retrospective canon and one of the most-imitated *gǔ-wén* writers in Chinese literary history. The textual history of the *Zhèn-chuān jí* is exceptionally complex: an early 20-*juǎn* cutting by Guī's cousin **Guī Dào-chuán** 歸道傳 and a 32-*juǎn* cutting by Guī's sons **Guī Zǐ-hù** 歸子祜 and **Guī Zǐ-níng** 歸子寧; both were partly lost in the *kòu-biàn* (rebel-disorder, the late-Míng peasant-and-Manchu wars); the Kāng-xī **yǐ-mǎo** (1675) consolidated edition is by Guī's grandson **Guī Xuán-gōng** 歸玹珙 (with Guī Yǒu-guāng's grand-nephew **Guī Zhuāng** 歸莊's involvement), based on the *fán-lì* (editorial principles) drafted by Guī Zhuāng. **Qián Qiān-yì** 錢謙益 also worked on a partial edition of Guī's prose. The WYG-base text is the Kāng-xī 1675 consolidated edition.

### Tiyao
*No tiyao found in source.* The source file is SBCK, not the WYG with *Sìkù* tíyào. The principal documentary content of the source is the **Guī Xuán-gōng** 1675 (Kāng-xī *yǐ-mǎo*) editor's preface — translated in part as follows. "The *Zhèn-chuān xiān-shēng wén-jí* has circulated in the empire for over a hundred years; those who know-the-art-of-prose all treasure-store it. My late-father (i.e. Guī Zhuāng) old-stored two editions — one in 20 *juǎn*, one in 32 *juǎn* — both lost in the *kòu-biàn* (Manchu invasion). I, following **Chén Bǎi-shǐ** the *xiāng-jūn* (Chen Mingxia, the *xiāng-guó* (Premier)) saw [an edition] he had pointed-and-read of 20 *juǎn*; broadly searched and obtained two-editions which I now have on my shelves. The 20-*juǎn* is what Guī's cousin **Dào-chuán** cut. The 32-*juǎn* is what Guī's son **Zǐ-hù** and **Zǐ-níng** cut. *Yǒu wú shēn-hù, huò yí yǒu zá-é yú qí jiān* (their having-and-not-having mutually-overlap; or it is feared there is *zá-é* (mixed-errors) among them); also heard from **Qián Mù-zhāi zōng-bó** (Qián Qiān-yì) that *xiān-shēng yí-wén shàng duō* (the master's surviving prose is still much). I formerly worked with his descendant **Xuě-ān** at the *Lǐ-bù* (Board of Rites); Xuě-ān..." [continuing the editor's account]. The same preface notes that **Qián Qiān-yì** (號 Mù-zhāi 牧齋) had told Guī Xuán-gōng that there were more *yí-wén* in circulation than the family had collected. The colophon also records the *fán-lì* (editorial principles) history: Guī Zhuāng's draft *fán-lì* had been frequently revised but never fixed; the present editor (Guī Xuán-gōng's nephew) discovered an 8-rule *chāo-běn* (manuscript) at the cutter's workshop, and at his uncle's house found a 5-rule version; he combined them and preserved the previously-circulated **Qián Mù-zhāi**-drafted *fán-lì* at the front for reference.

### Abstract
Guī Yǒu-guāng of Kūn-shān is the canonical *gǔ-wén* master of the entire Míng dynasty in the retrospective Qīng *Tóng-chéng* (Tóng-chéng) school's canon, and the most-imitated *biéjí* prose writer in subsequent Chinese literary history. His career was unusually delayed — *jǔ-rén* 1540 but not *jìn-shì* until 1565, at age 60 *sui* — and the celebrated short-prose pieces (*Xiàng-jí xuān zhì* 項脊軒志, *Xiān-bǐ shì-lüè* 先妣事略, *Hán-huā zàng zhì* 寒花葬志, etc.) were almost entirely composed during his pre-*jìn-shì* decades at Kūn-shān. He is the central master in the lineage **Wáng-Táng** [[KR4e0185]] [[KR4e0190]] → **Guī Yǒu-guāng** → **Máo Kūn** → (Qīng) **Fāng Bāo** / **Yáo Nài** of *Tóng-chéng*; his prose's *qīng-yuǎn* (crisp-far) domestic intimacy is the antitype of the **Hòu Qī Zǐ** archaist *Qín-Hàn / shèng-Táng* imitation program. **Wáng Shì-zhēn** in late life conceded Guī's superiority — see the portrait-*zàn* *Fēng xíng shuǐ shàng, huàn wéi wén-zhāng* preserved in *Dú-shū hòu* [[KR4e0202]].

The textual transmission is exceptionally well documented in the editor's preface preserved in the source: the *kòu-biàn* (Manchu invasion) disrupted both family editions; **Qián Qiān-yì** participated in collecting and editing supplementary materials and drafted a *fán-lì*; the family's authorized *fán-lì* (by Guī Zhuāng) circulated only in damaged manuscript form; the Kāng-xī 1675 edition is a consolidated reconstruction. The WYG-base text is 30 *juǎn* — between the family-edited 20 and 32 *juǎn* of the earlier separate cuttings.

Date bracket: 1540 (Guī's *jǔ-rén* — establishing the *terminus a quo* of substantial preserved writing) — 1675 (Kāng-xī consolidated edition). Active composition window: 1530–1571 (Guī's death). CBDB 34731 confirms 1506–1571.

## Other points of interest
The *Xiàng-jí xuān zhì* 項脊軒志, a short prose memoir of the Guī family studio and the deaths of Guī's mother and grandmother, is one of the most-anthologized single prose pieces in subsequent Chinese literary history — the canonical example of Míng *gǔ-wén qíng-jǐng* (intimate-domestic prose).
