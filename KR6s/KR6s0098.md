# Xù Zhēnyuán shìjiào lù 續貞元釋教錄
*A Continuation of the Zhēn-yuán Catalog of Buddhist Teaching*
by [[恒安]] (集)

## About the work

A single-juan **Southern Tang (南唐) Buddhist canonical bibliographic supplement**, compiled by **Héngān** 恒安 in **Bǎodà** 保大 3, the *yǐsì* year = **945 CE**. The work supplements **Yuánzhào's** *Zhēnyuán xīndìng shìjiào mùlù* ([[KR6s0097]], 800 CE) with the post-800 translation activity through the late Tang and Five-Dynasties periods. The byline reads **西都右街報恩禪院取經禪大德恒安集** ("Composed by Héngān, *qǔjīng chán dàdé* of Bàoēn Chán Cloister, the right street of the Western Capital"). Preserved at T55 no. 2158.

### Prefaces

The text opens with the title-and-byline:

> ***DàTáng Bǎodà yǐsìsuì xù Zhēnyuán shìjiào lù, yī juàn*** 大唐保大乙巳歲續貞元釋教錄一卷 ("The Great Tang Bǎodà *yǐsì* year Continuation of the Zhēnyuán Catalog of Buddhist Teaching, one juan").

Then the body of the text begins with the bibliographic-historical narrative:

> *Hearing that **Mātaṅga** entered the Hàn — the sound-and-teaching first reached unto Cīna. Through the operation of the **sage Tang** — the Sanskrit canons flourished and rounded into the sea-canon. And again, our **Xuán-zōng huáng-dì** [Tang Xuán-zōng] reigned over the world-bounds, in the year **Kāi-yuán** 18, *gēng-wǔ*: the **Western Capital Xī Chóng-fú-sì** *śramaṇa* **Zhì-shēng** composed the **Kāi-yuán shì-jiào lù** in **20 juan**, titled the **Kāi-yuán-lù canon**, gathering Mahāyāna and Hīnayāna *jīng* / *lǜ* / *lùn* and the worthy-and-sage biographical collections, totaling **5,048…**.*

[The text continues with extensive bibliographic-historical narration through the post-800 translation activity.]

### Abstract

Authorship and date: composed by **Héngān** 恒安 (DILA does not preserve a separate authority entry; otherwise unattested) at the **Bàoēn Chán Cloister** 報恩禪院 in the **Western Capital** = **Chángān**, in **Bǎodà** 保大 3 = **945 CE**.

The dating is significant: **Bǎodà** is the era-name of the **Southern Tang** (南唐) regional dynasty (937–975) under **Yuánzōng** 元宗 **Lǐ Jǐng** 李璟 (r. 943–961) — making the work a **Southern-Tang regional** Buddhist canonical bibliography, produced by Buddhist scholars in the Southern-Tang-controlled lower-Yangtze and Jiang-nan region during the chaotic Five-Dynasties period. The use of "**Tang**" in the title — Bǎodà *yǐsì*-year **DàTáng** — reflects the Southern Tang's claim to be the legitimate continuation of the original Tang dynasty (which had collapsed in 907 in the central plain, but which the Southern Tang regime claimed to have preserved in the south).

*notBefore* = **945**, *notAfter* = **945** (firmly dated). Catalog dynasty 南唐.

The work documents the **post-Yuán-zhào (post-800) translation activity** through the late Tang into the early Five-Dynasties period — a substantial expansion of the canonical roster covering ~150 years of additional translation output, including:

- Late-Tang Esoteric translations (post-Bù-kōng / Prajñā generations).
- Five-Dynasties translation activity in the Southern Tang and other regional courts.
- Liturgical, ritual, and commentarial canonical-additions.

The 1-juan brief format makes this a focused supplement rather than a comprehensive restatement — paralleling **Dàoxuān's** 1-juan supplement [[KR6s0089]] to his own main *DàTáng nèidiǎn lù* ([[KR6s0088]]).

## Other points of interest

The Southern-Tang Bàoēnyuàn provenance places the work in the **Southern-Tang Buddhist scholarly establishment** that was a principal pre-Sòng preserver of Tang-period Buddhist culture during the chaotic Five-Dynasties decade. The Southern Tang's Buddhist patronage was substantial and continuous, and produced significant canonical-bibliographic and translation activity that supplied the post-conquest **Sòng** dynasty (which absorbed the Southern Tang in 975 / 976) with much of its inherited Buddhist scholarly apparatus — including catalogs like this one, which fed directly into the early-Sòng **Kāibǎo canon-printing project** of 971–983.
