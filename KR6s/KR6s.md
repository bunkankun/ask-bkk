### Scope and scholarly tradition

KR6s 事彙部類 (*Shìhuì bù lèi*) is the catch-all "subject-collected" division of the Kanripo edition of the Chinese Buddhist canon. It assembles the auxiliary, paratextual, encyclopaedic, and bibliographically-organized materials that the medieval and early-modern canon editors did not place in the doctrinal divisions (*sūtra*, *vinaya*, *abhidharma*) proper. In Taishō terms it spans large parts of vols 53–55 (T2121–T2184) plus selected items from T54's heterodox section (T2137–T2144) and T85's *gǔyì* / *yísì* 古逸／疑似 section (Dunhuang manuscripts and dubious texts), supplemented by Korean (高麗藏 → 嘉興藏 → 卍續藏) and Japanese imperial-era recensions for items that did not enter the Taishō main canon. The matching CANWWW divisions are `div22.xml` (事彙部, encyclopedic compendia and lexicographical works), `div24.xml` (外教部, non-Buddhist religions), `div18.xml` (目錄部, canon catalogs), `div25.xml` (悉曇部, Siddham studies), and `div09.xml` (古逸部全．疑似部, Dunhuang and dubious-text recoveries). The division covers chronologically from the early Liáng (the *Fān fányǔ* 翻梵語 [[KR6s0018]] and the 510s anthological projects) to the high Qīng (the Yōngzhèng 雍正 imperial Chán anthologies of the 1730s [[KR6s0068]]–[[KR6s0071]]).

Generically the division is the most heterogeneous in the canon: a single shelf brings together (1) topical Buddhist *lèi-shū* 類書 (encyclopedias arranged by subject), (2) numerical-categories *fǎshù* 法數 lexicons of doctrine, (3) the great *yīnyì* 音義 phonological-semantic glossaries that are the principal philological apparatus to the canon, (4) Sanskrit-script and Indo-Chinese translation aids (*Xītán* 悉曇 primers, Sanskrit thousand-character classics, bilingual word-lists), (5) the 古逸部 recoveries of Dūnhuáng ritual, popular, and *biànwén* 變文 literature, (6) imperial-author Buddhist literary collections — Sòng Tàizōng, Míng Tàizōng (the Yǒnglè 永樂 emperor), and Qīng Shìzōng (the Yōngzhèng emperor) — that the editors chose not to scatter into the doctrinal divisions of their original genres, (7) the 外教部 corpus of Sāṃkhya, Vaiśeṣika, Daoist apocrypha, Manichaean, and Nestorian-Christian texts that the Táng canon transmitted under the same shelf as Buddhist material, (8) the 目錄部 series of canon catalogs from the Liáng *Chū sānzàng jì jí* 出三藏記集 [[KR6s0084]] to the Míng *Yuèzàng zhījīn* 閱藏知津 [[KR6s0104]], and (9) the parallel record of Japanese pilgrim-importation catalogs (*qǐnglái mùlù* 請來目錄) running from Saichō (805) and Kūkai (806) through the *nittō hakke* 入唐八家. The principal scholarly traditions a reader must keep in view are accordingly four: (a) Buddhist encyclopedism modelled on the secular *lèi-shū* (Bǎochàng → Dào-shì → late-Míng compilers), (b) Sino-Indian philology — *yīnyì*, *Xītán*, *fānyì míngyì* — as a continuous Táng-Sòng project of preserving Sanskrit sound and translation equivalence, (c) the *jīnglù* 經錄 / canon-catalog tradition as the metadata-and-version-control layer of Chinese Buddhism (the *Kāiyuán shìjiào lù* 開元釋教錄 [[KR6s0093]] is the load-bearing center of the entire canonical organization), and (d) East-Asian transmission philology, where the Japanese 請來 catalogs and the Korean *Gāolìguó xīndiāo... biélù* 高麗國新雕大藏校正別錄 [[KR6s0133]] together document how the canon moved beyond the Chinese world.

### Important texts and text clusters

- **Topical Buddhist encyclopedias (*lèi-shū*).** *Jīnglǜ yìxiàng* 經律異相 [[KR6s0001]] (Bǎochàng, 508–516) is the earliest substantial Chinese Buddhist *lèi-shū* and the direct prototype for *Fǎyuàn zhūlín* 法苑珠林 [[KR6s0002]] (Dào-shì, 668), the great hundred-juan Táng encyclopedia. *Zhūjīng yàojí* 諸經要集 [[KR6s0003]] (Dào-shì, 656–660) is its companion abridgement. *Shìshì yàolǎn* 釋氏要覽 [[KR6s0005]] (Dào-chéng, 1019–1020) is the standard Sòng one-volume reference for monastic life and terminology. *Dàzàng yīlǎn* 大藏一覽 [[KR6s0006]] (Chén Shí, late Wànlì) and *Yuèzàng zhījīn* 閱藏知津 [[KR6s0104]] (Ǒu-yì Zhì-xù 蕅益智旭, 1654) are the two great late-Míng readers' guides to the canon as a whole.
- **Numerical-categories lexicons (*fǎshù*).** *Dà Míng sānzàng fǎshù* 大明三藏法數 [[KR6s0007]] (Yīrú 一如, 1417–1419, by Yǒnglè edict) is the standard topically-arranged numerical lexicon, in 50 juan; *Jiàoshèng fǎshù* 教乘法數 [[KR6s0008]] (Yuán-jìng 圓瀞, c. 1426–1431) and its Qīng re-edition *Chóngdìng jiàoshèng fǎshù* [[KR6s0009]] (Chāo-hǎi 超海, 1734–1735) carry the same genre forward.
- **Phonological-semantic glossaries (*yīnyì*).** *Yīqiè jīng yīnyì* 一切經音義 [[KR6s0010]] (Xuán-yìng 玄應, 645–656) inaugurates the genre; *Xīnyì Dàfāngguǎng fó Huáyán jīng yīnyì* 新譯大方廣佛華嚴經音義 [[KR6s0011]] [[KR6s0012]] (Huì-yuàn 慧苑, c. 700–720) is the dedicated Huáyán companion; the magnum opus is Huì-lín's 慧琳 hundred-juan *Yīqiè jīng yīnyì* [[KR6s0013]] (783–807) — the single most important Táng philological work for the canon — extended by Xī-lín's 希麟 *Xù yīqiè jīng yīnyì* [[KR6s0014]] (980–990, Liáo); the Hòu-Jìn *Xīnjí zàngjīng yīnyì suíhán lù* 新集藏經音義隨函錄 [[KR6s0015]] (Kě-hóng 可洪, 932–940) is its principal alternative organized by canonical case-position. *Shàoxīng chóngdiāo dàzàng yīn* 紹興重雕大藏音 [[KR6s0016]] (Chǔ-guān 處觀, c. 1070s–1090s) and *Běijīng wǔ dà bù zhíyīn huìyùn* 北京五大部直音會韻 [[KR6s0017]] (Jiǔ-yǐn 久隱, 1522–1543) are the Sòng and Míng continuations.
- **Sanskrit script and translation aids.** *Fān fányǔ* 翻梵語 [[KR6s0018]] (anonymous Liáng) is the earliest Sanskrit-glossary in the canon. *Fānyì míngyì jí* 翻譯名義集 [[KR6s0019]] (Fǎ-yún 法雲, 1136–1143) is the standard Sòng dictionary of translated terms. *Xītán zìjì* 悉曇字記 [[KR6s0020]] (Zhì-guǎng 智廣, 780–806) is the foundational Chinese primer of Siddham. The Sanskrit-Chinese bilingual cluster — *Fànyǔ qiānzìwén* 梵語千字文 [[KR6s0021]] [[KR6s0022]] (Yì-jìng 義淨, 695–713), *Táng Fàn wénzì* 唐梵文字 [[KR6s0023]] (Quán-zhēn 全真), *Fànyǔ zámíng* 梵語雜名 [[KR6s0024]] (Lǐ-yán 禮言), *Táng Fàn liǎngyǔ shuāngduì jí* 唐梵兩語雙對集 [[KR6s0025]] (Sēng Dáduō-niè-duō 僧怛多蘖多 with Bōluó-jùnà-míshě-shā 波羅瞿那彌捨沙) — preserves the Táng working-vocabulary of the translation bureaus.
- **The Dūnhuáng cluster (T85 古逸部・疑似部).** [[KR6s0026]] through [[KR6s0056]] are the Dūnhuáng manuscript fragments that the Taishō editors classified as "ancient lost / dubious." They include doctrinal compendia (*Dàshèng jīng zuǎn yàoyì* [[KR6s0026]], *Dàshèng èrshíèr wèn běn* [[KR6s0027]]), liturgical and ritual texts (penitential liturgies [[KR6s0033]] [[KR6s0046]]–[[KR6s0048]], *upoṣadha* and bodhisattva-precept formulae [[KR6s0043]]–[[KR6s0045]], dedication and vow texts [[KR6s0038]]–[[KR6s0040]] [[KR6s0049]]), the *biànwén* 變文 transformation-text *Dà Mùjiānlián míngjiān jiùmǔ biànwén bìng tú* 大目乾連冥間救母變文并圖 [[KR6s0050]], the verse-encomium *Quánzhōu qiānfó xīnzhù zhū zǔshī sòng* 泉州千佛新著諸祖師頌 [[KR6s0053]], the Tibetan-period Shāzhōu inscription for Hé-shàng Hóng-biàn 洪辯 [[KR6s0054]] (850–869), and — separate from the Taishō recension but included here — the Wáng Fán-zhì poetry corpus [[KR6s0055]] [[KR6s0134]]. Together this cluster is the principal canonical witness for the religious life of medieval Dūnhuáng.
- **Imperial-author Buddhist literary collections (御製).** Three layers: (i) Sòng Tàizōng 宋太宗 (Zhào Jiǒng 趙炅, r. 976–997) — six poetry / prose collections [[KR6s0057]] *Yùzhì liánhuá xīnlún huíwén jìsòng*, [[KR6s0058]] *Yùzhì mìzàng quán*, [[KR6s0059]] *Yùzhì xiāoyáo yǒng*, [[KR6s0060]] *Yùzhì yuánshí*, [[KR6s0061]] *Yùzhì fó fù*, [[KR6s0062]] *Yùzhì quányuán gē* — the foundational corpus of explicitly imperial Chinese Buddhist literature; (ii) Míng Tàizōng (Yǒnglè 永樂 emperor, Zhū Dì 朱棣, r. 1402–1424) — *Dà Míng Tàizōng yùzhì xùzàn wén* [[KR6s0063]], the Buddha-name liturgical works *Zhū fó shìzūn rúlái púsà zūnzhě shénsēng míng jīng* [[KR6s0064]] and *Zhū fó míngchēng gēqū* [[KR6s0065]], and the *Wénshū zàn* / *Shìjiā Móuní fó zàn* encomia [[KR6s0066]] [[KR6s0067]]; (iii) Qīng Shìzōng (Yōngzhèng 雍正 emperor, r. 1722–1735) — the polemical *Yùzhì jiǎnmó biànyì lù* 御製揀魔辨異錄 [[KR6s0068]] against the Hànyuè 漢月 line of Línjì Chán, the *Yùxuǎn yǔlù* 御選語錄 anthology [[KR6s0069]], and the digest editions *Yùlù Zōngjìng dàgāng* [[KR6s0070]] and *Yùlù jīnghǎi yī dī* [[KR6s0071]] of the *Zōng-jìng-lù* and the canon — the most ambitious imperial intervention in Chán historiography in late imperial China.
- **The 外教部 (non-Buddhist religions in the canon).** *Jīn qīshí lùn* 金七十論 [[KR6s0072]] (Paramārtha 真諦, 557–569) is the Chinese translation of the Sāṃkhya *Suvarṇasaptati* / *Sāṃkhyakārikā* commentary, the principal Chinese-Buddhist source on Sāṃkhya. *Shèngzōng shí jùyì lùn* 勝宗十句義論 [[KR6s0073]] (Huì-yuè 慧月, tr. Xuán-zàng 玄奘, 645–664) is the parallel Vaiśeṣika treatise. *Lǎozǐ huàhú jīng* 老子化胡經 [[KR6s0074]] is the Dūnhuáng witness to the Daoist "conversion of the barbarians" polemic — the central textual irritant of the Buddho-Daoist controversy ([[KR6r0149]]). *Zhuāngzǐ nèipiān zhù* 莊子內篇註 [[KR6s0075]] (Hānshān Dé-qīng 憨山德清, c. 1593–1623) is the late-Míng Chán commentary on Zhuāng-zǐ, included as a Buddhist work despite its subject. *Gāoshàng Yùhuáng běnxíng jí jīng suǐ* [[KR6s0076]] and *Wúshàng Yùhuáng xīnyìn jīng* [[KR6s0077]] are Daoist Jade-Emperor texts preserved in the *Fāngshān shíjīng* 房山石經 (here in the *Fāng-shān* recension, F29). The Manichaean cluster — *Móníjiào xiàbù zàn* 摩尼教下部讚 [[KR6s0078]], *Móní guāngfó jiàofǎ yí luè* 摩尼光佛教法儀略 [[KR6s0079]] (Fú-duō-dàn 拂多誕, presented to the Táng court 731), *Bōsījiào cánjīng* 波斯教殘經 [[KR6s0080]] — and the Nestorian-Christian (景教 *jǐngjiào*) cluster — *Xùtīng Míshīsuǒ jīng* 序聽迷詩所經 [[KR6s0081]] (the *Sūtra of Jesus the Messiah*), *Jǐngjiào sānwēi méngdù zàn* 景教三威蒙度讚 [[KR6s0082]] (the *Gloria*), and *Dà-Qín jǐngjiào liúxíng zhōngguó bēisòng* 大秦景教流行中國碑頌 [[KR6s0083]] (Jǐng-jìng 景淨, 781, the Xī'ān Nestorian stele) — together constitute the canon's principal record of medieval Chinese encounters with West-Asian religions.
- **Canon catalogs (目錄), Chinese sequence.** *Chū sānzàng jì jí* 出三藏記集 [[KR6s0084]] (Sēngyòu 僧祐, 510–518) is the earliest extant Chinese Buddhist *jīnglù* and the foundation of all later catalogs. The Suí-Táng sequence runs through the three *Zhòngjīng mùlù* 眾經目錄 [[KR6s0085]] (Fǎ-jīng 法經, 594), [[KR6s0086]] (Yàn-cōng 彥琮, 602), [[KR6s0087]] (Jìng-tài 靜泰, 663–666), Dào-xuān's *Dà-Táng nèidiǎn lù* 大唐內典錄 [[KR6s0088]] and *Xù...* [[KR6s0089]] (664–667), Jìng-mài's *Gǔjīn yìjīng tú jì* 古今譯經圖紀 [[KR6s0090]] and Zhì-shēng's *Xù...* [[KR6s0091]] (730), and Míng-quán's Wǔ-Zhōu-court *Dà-Zhōu kāndìng zhòngjīng mùlù* 大周刊定眾經目錄 [[KR6s0092]] (695). The 730 *Kāiyuán shìjiào lù* 開元釋教錄 [[KR6s0093]] (Zhì-shēng 智昇), with its abridgement *Lüèchū* [[KR6s0094]] and the working-document *Dà-Táng Kāiyuán shìjiào guǎngpǐn lìzhāng* [[KR6s0095]] (Xuán-yì 玄逸), is the load-bearing standard catalog for the Chinese canon thereafter. The *Zhēnyuán* 貞元 era continuations — Yuán-zhào's 圓照 *Dà-Táng Zhēnyuán xù Kāiyuán shìjiào lù* [[KR6s0096]] (794) and *Zhēnyuán xīndìng shìjiào mùlù* [[KR6s0097]] (800), and Héng-ān's *Xù Zhēnyuán shìjiào lù* [[KR6s0098]] (945, Southern Táng) — close the Táng sequence. The Sòng catalog series — *Tiānshèng shìjiào zǒnglù* [[KR6s0099]] (Wéi-jìng 惟淨, 1023–1031), *Dà-zhōng-xiángfú fǎbǎo lù* [[KR6s0100]] (Yáng Yì 楊億, 1011–1013), *Jǐng-yòu xīnxiū fǎbǎo lù* [[KR6s0101]] (Lǚ Yí-jiǎn 呂夷簡, 1034–1037) — documents the Northern-Sòng official translation bureau. *Dàzàng shèngjiào fǎbǎo biāomù* [[KR6s0102]] (Wáng Gǔ 王古, Yuán) and *Dà Míng chóngkān sānzàng shèngjiào mùlù* [[KR6s0103]] (Wànlì Jìng-shān recension) carry the genre into the Yuán-Míng. The genre culminates in *Yuèzàng zhījīn* 閱藏知津 [[KR6s0104]] (Ǒu-yì Zhì-xù, 1654), the late-Míng readers'-guide to the canon.
- **Japanese pilgrim-importation catalogs (*qǐnglái mùlù*).** The 805 catalogs of Saichō 最澄 — *Chuánjiào dàshī jiānglái Tāizhōu lù* [[KR6s0105]] and *... Yuèzhōu lù* [[KR6s0106]] — and the 806 *Yù qǐnglái mùlù* [[KR6s0107]] of Kūkai 空海 inaugurate a continuous Heian-era genre. The *nittō hakke* 入唐八家 ("eight Heian pilgrims to Táng") sequence runs through the Jōgyō 常曉 catalog [[KR6s0109]] (839), Engyō 圓行 [[KR6s0110]] (839), Ennin 圓仁 [[KR6s0111]]–[[KR6s0113]] (839–847), Eun 惠運 [[KR6s0114]] [[KR6s0115]] (847), Enchin 圓珍 [[KR6s0116]]–[[KR6s0120]] (858), Shūei 宗叡 [[KR6s0121]] [[KR6s0122]] (865), and the *Lùwài jīng děng mùlù* [[KR6s0123]] miscellany; the seventh of the eight, the foundational *Gēnběn dàhéshàng zhēnjì cèzǐ děng mùlù* [[KR6s0108]], records Kūkai-school manuscript apparatus. Annen's 安然 *Zhū āshélí zhēnyán mìjiào bùlèi zǒnglù* 諸阿闍梨真言密教部類總錄 [[KR6s0124]] (885–902) consolidates the esoteric portion. Together this corpus is the principal documentary source for what 9th-century Chinese Buddhism actually moved out of the empire.
- **Japanese sectarian (六宗) sub-catalogs.** Yuán-chāo's (Enchō 圓超) *Huāyán zōng zhāngshū bìng yīnmíng lù* [[KR6s0125]], Xuán-rì's (Gennichi 玄日) *Tiāntāi zōng zhāngshū* [[KR6s0126]], Ān-yuǎn's (An'on 安遠) *Sānlùn zōng zhāngshū* [[KR6s0127]], Píng-zuò's (Heiso 平祚) *Fǎxiàng zōng zhāngshū* [[KR6s0128]] (all c. 950–1100), Zōshun 藏俊's *Zhùjìn Fǎxiàng zōng zhāngshū* [[KR6s0129]] (12th c.), and Yǒng-wěn's (Eion 榮穩) *Lǜ zōng zhāngshū* [[KR6s0130]] are the school-by-school sub-catalogs of the commentarial literature held in Heian-era Japanese monastic libraries. Eichō 永超's *Dōngyù chuándēng mùlù* 東域傳燈目錄 [[KR6s0131]] (1094) is the consolidated successor.
- **Korean canon catalogs.** Ŭich'ŏn 義天's *Xīnbiān zhūzōng jiàozàng zǒnglù* 新編諸宗教藏總錄 [[KR6s0132]] (1090) is the canonical record of the Goryeo *Sok-jang* 續藏 supplementary canon project and the most important pre-modern East-Asian catalog of commentarial literature. Sugi 守其's *Gāolìguó xīndiāo dàzàng jiàozhèng biélù* 高麗國新雕大藏校正別錄 [[KR6s0133]] (1236–1248) is the critical-collation apparatus to the Goryeo *Tripiṭaka* (the *Koryŏ Taejanggyŏng* 高麗大藏經 second carving), and the foundation document of all modern critical editions of the Chinese canon (the Taishō uses the Goryeo as base text).

### Important persons

- [[寶唱]] — Bǎochàng (early 6th c.), Liáng court compiler of *Jīnglǜ yìxiàng* [[KR6s0001]]; founder of the Buddhist *lèi-shū* genre.
- [[僧祐]] — Sēngyòu (445–518), Liáng cataloguer-historian, author of *Chū sānzàng jì jí* [[KR6s0084]] — the founding text of the *jīnglù* genre. (Also the principal early apologetic anthologist; see [[KR6r]].)
- [[真諦]] — Paramārtha (499–569), Chén translator of *Jīn qīshí lùn* [[KR6s0072]] (the *Suvarṇasaptati*); the canon's principal source on Indian Sāṃkhya.
- [[法經]] — Fǎ-jīng (fl. late 6th c.), compiler of the 594 Suí *Zhòngjīng mùlù* [[KR6s0085]] — the first Chinese catalog organized by doctrinal classification rather than by translation chronology.
- [[彥琮]] — Yàn-cōng (557–610), Suí cataloguer and translation theorist, author of the 602 *Zhòngjīng mùlù* [[KR6s0086]] and of the celebrated *bā-bèi* 八備 (eight requirements of a translator) discussion.
- [[玄應]] — Xuán-yìng (fl. 645–656), Táng phonologist, founder of the Buddhist *yīnyì* genre with [[KR6s0010]].
- [[玄奘]] — Xuán-zàng (602–664), translator of *Shèngzōng shí jùyì lùn* [[KR6s0073]] — the canon's principal Vaiśeṣika source.
- [[道宣]] — Dào-xuān (596–667), Vinaya patriarch and the period's most prolific cataloguer, author of *Dà-Táng nèidiǎn lù* [[KR6s0088]] and its continuation [[KR6s0089]]. (His historiographical and apologetic work belongs to [[KR6r]].)
- [[道世]] — Dào-shì (?–683), Táng encyclopaedist, author of the great hundred-juan *Fǎyuàn zhūlín* [[KR6s0002]] and of *Zhūjīng yàojí* [[KR6s0003]].
- [[義淨]] — Yì-jìng (635–713), India-pilgrim and translator, author of *Fànyǔ qiānzìwén* [[KR6s0021]] [[KR6s0022]]. (His pilgrimage and Vinaya works belong to [[KR6r]] and [[KR6k]].)
- [[慧苑]] — Huì-yuàn (early 8th c.), Fǎzàng's disciple, author of *Xīnyì Dàfāngguǎng fó Huáyán jīng yīnyì* [[KR6s0011]] [[KR6s0012]] — the foundational Huáyán philological apparatus.
- [[智廣]] — Zhì-guǎng (late 8th – early 9th c.), Táng monk, author of *Xītán zìjì* [[KR6s0020]] — the foundational Chinese Siddham primer transmitted to Japan.
- [[智昇]] — Zhì-shēng (fl. 730), Táng cataloguer; the *Kāiyuán shìjiào lù* [[KR6s0093]] and its abridgement [[KR6s0094]] together fix the standard organization of the canon used down to the present (Taishō section-numbering ultimately descends from it).
- [[慧琳]] — Huì-lín (737–820), Táng phonologist, author of the hundred-juan *Yīqiè jīng yīnyì* [[KR6s0013]] — the single most important pre-modern Chinese Buddhist philological work.
- [[圓照]] — Yuán-zhào (8th–9th c.), Táng cataloguer of the *Zhēnyuán* updates [[KR6s0096]] [[KR6s0097]].
- [[最澄]] — Saichō (767–822), founder of Japanese Tendai; author of the 805 *jiānglái* catalogs [[KR6s0105]] [[KR6s0106]].
- [[空海]] — Kūkai (774–835), founder of Japanese Shingon; author of *Yù qǐnglái mùlù* [[KR6s0107]] (806).
- [[圓仁]] — Ennin (794–864), Tendai pilgrim; author of three pilgrimage catalogs [[KR6s0111]]–[[KR6s0113]] and of the *Nittō guhō junrei kōki* travel diary (not in this division).
- [[圓珍]] — Enchin (814–891), Tendai pilgrim; author of the five-catalog cluster [[KR6s0116]]–[[KR6s0120]] (858).

### Topics

- **Buddhist encyclopedism and the *lèi-shū* tradition.** The 6th-century *Jīnglǜ yìxiàng* [[KR6s0001]] → 7th-century *Fǎyuàn zhūlín* [[KR6s0002]] → 11th-century *Shìshì yàolǎn* [[KR6s0005]] → 17th-century *Dàzàng yīlǎn* [[KR6s0006]] / *Yuèzàng zhījīn* [[KR6s0104]] sequence is a deliberate Buddhist appropriation of the secular *lèi-shū* form (Liú Xiàng, *Yìwén lèijù*, *Tàipíng yùlǎn*) for the management of canonical material; the canon, on this account, is something to be navigated by topic and synopsis as much as read straight through.
- **The *yīnyì* (sound-and-sense) tradition as canonical philology.** Xuán-yìng → Huì-lín → Xī-lín → Kě-hóng → Chǔ-guān → Jiǔ-yǐn ([[KR6s0010]]–[[KR6s0017]]) constitutes the most sustained pre-modern East Asian project of philological-lexicographical apparatus to a single textual corpus, and the principal source for the historical phonology of Middle Chinese as much as for canonical reading.
- **The *Xītán* / *fānyì míngyì* project.** The Sino-Indian translation aids ([[KR6s0018]]–[[KR6s0025]]) and *Fānyì míngyì jí* [[KR6s0019]] together attempt to keep the Sanskrit substrate of the canon visible in the Chinese reception — both as a script (Siddham as a memorized inventory of dharanis and seed-syllables) and as a translation-equivalence dictionary. Through Kūkai and the Heian Mikkyō transmission this project becomes load-bearing for Japanese esoteric Buddhism.
- **The 經錄 / canon catalog as version control.** From Sēngyòu (510s) through Zhì-shēng's 730 *Kāiyuán* standardization to Sugi's 1230s *Goryeo* collation and Zhì-xù's 1654 reader's guide, the catalog tradition is the metadata layer that makes the Chinese canon a canon at all — fixing what counts, what is dubious, what is lost, and what is duplicated. The *Kāiyuán shìjiào lù* [[KR6s0093]] is the load-bearing standard down to the Taishō.
- **East Asian transmission and the Japanese 請來 record.** The catalogs of Saichō, Kūkai, Ennin, Eun, Enchin, Shūei, and Annen ([[KR6s0105]]–[[KR6s0124]]) are the principal documentary record of which texts moved out of Táng China into Heian Japan, and at what pace. Together with the Korean *Sok-jang* catalog of Ŭich'ŏn [[KR6s0132]] and the Goryeo-canon collation of Sugi [[KR6s0133]], they constitute the East-Asian extension of the Chinese canon-catalog tradition.
- **Imperial patronage and imperial authorship of Buddhist texts.** Three discrete moments — Sòng Tàizōng's 980s–990s self-presentation as a Buddhist sovereign-author ([[KR6s0057]]–[[KR6s0062]]), the Yǒnglè-emperor's early-Míng commissioning programme ([[KR6s0063]]–[[KR6s0067]] plus [[KR6s0007]]), and the Yōngzhèng-emperor's 1730s polemical-anthological intervention into Chán historiography ([[KR6s0068]]–[[KR6s0071]]) — define the principal late-imperial axis of state engagement with the canon.
- **The "外教" question: religions of the West preserved in a Buddhist shelf.** The Sāṃkhya, Vaiśeṣika, Daoist apocryphal, Manichaean, and Nestorian-Christian texts ([[KR6s0072]]–[[KR6s0083]]) — preserved in Táng-period canonical company because the editors classed them with translated Indo-Iranian doctrinal literature — make this division the canon's principal record of medieval Chinese encounter with non-Buddhist religious systems beyond Daoism and Confucianism. The Nestorian stele [[KR6s0083]] (781) and the Manichaean court-presentation [[KR6s0079]] (731) are the centrepiece documents.
- **Dūnhuáng popular Buddhism in the canon.** The T85 *gǔyì* / *yísì* cluster ([[KR6s0026]]–[[KR6s0056]]) opens the canon to a class of materials — penitential and *upoṣadha* liturgies, vow and merit-dedication formulae, *biànwén* transformation-texts, vernacular poetry, local hagiographies — that the doctrinal divisions systematically excluded. The cluster is the principal canonical witness for the religious life of medieval Dūnhuáng outside the elite monastic establishments.
- **Wáng Fán-zhì and vernacular Buddhist poetry.** The two recensions of the Wáng Fán-zhì poetry corpus [[KR6s0055]] [[KR6s0134]] are the canon's principal record of popular vernacular Buddhist verse, recovered almost entirely from Dūnhuáng witnesses and substantially recovered into modern editions only in the 20th century.
