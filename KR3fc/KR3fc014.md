# Xiángmíng suànfǎ 詳明算法
 *Detailed-and-Clear Mathematical Methods*
by [[安止齋]] (撰), [[何平子]] (合撰)

## About the work

A late-Yuán practical-mathematical primer in 4 *juàn* by Ān Zhǐzhāi 安止齋 and the contemporary mathematician Hé Píngzǐ 何平子, dated *Hóngwǔ* 6 (1373) by the surviving preface — squarely at the YuánMíng transition, with the catalog placement under Yuán reflecting the work's substantive Yuán-period content and intellectual lineage. The work was among the most widely-circulating Chinese mathematical primers of the late-Yuán and early-Míng, repeatedly reprinted and transmitted across East Asia to Korea and Japan.

### Abstract

The *Xiángmíng suànfǎ* belongs to the late-Yuán / early-Míng tradition of practical-mathematical pedagogy oriented toward the abacus (*suànpán* 算盤) rather than the counting-rod (*suànchóu* 算籌). It systematizes elementary arithmetic — integer and fraction operations, multiplication and division shortcuts, the standard rules of three and false position — and provides worked-problem applications to commercial, taxation, and surveying contexts. Its principal pedagogical innovation is the systematic presentation of the *jiǔjiǔ* multiplication table (九九歌) and the related *kǒujué* 口訣 (oral-formula) mnemonics for abacus computation; these mnemonics, established in the *Xiángmíng* in essentially their later-standard form, would become the universal pedagogical apparatus of late-imperial Chinese abacus arithmetic.

The work is doctrinally derivative from Dīng Jù's [[KR3fc013]] *Dīng Jù suànfǎ* (1355) and from the now-lost late-Yuán mathematical compilations of the southeastern Jiāngzhè region; its substantive contribution is presentational and pedagogical rather than mathematical. But its very accessibility and conservatism made it the dominant arithmetical primer of the late-Yuán / early-Míng decades, displacing the high algebraic tradition of [[朱世傑]] Zhū Shìjié's *Sìyuán yùjiàn* ([[KR3fc017]]) from active circulation as a working text. [[程大位]] Chéng Dàwèi's 1592 [[KR3fc027]] *Suànfǎ tǒngzōng* explicitly identifies the *Xiángmíng* as a principal source for its abacus-oriented presentation.

Authorship and date: the work is generally cataloged under joint authorship by Ān Zhǐzhāi and Hé Píngzǐ; the relative contributions are not clearly differentiated by the preface. The 1373 date places the work in the second Hóngwǔ year, at the very beginning of the Míng. Its Yuán placement in the present catalog reflects its substantive lineage rather than its chronological position. *NotBefore* and *notAfter* are both fixed at 1373.

The work was transmitted to Korea early in the Chosŏn period and became one of the principal mathematical texts of the Chosŏn-state mathematical-curriculum (*sàn-hak* 算學) civil-service examination; from Korea it was further transmitted to Edo-period Japan, where it was incorporated into seventeenth-century *wasan* 和算 (traditional Japanese mathematics) primers.
