# Sān-jiǎo hé-jiào shù 三角和較術
 *Sum-and-Difference Methods in Triangle Geometry*
by [[項名達]] (撰)

## About the work

[[項名達]] Xiàng Míngdá's (1789–1850) treatise in 4 *juàn* on the *hé-jiào* 和較 (sum-and-difference) methods in *sān-jiǎo* 三角 (triangle) geometry — the application of identities expressing one side or angle of a triangle in terms of sums and differences of the others to solve trigonometric problems.

### Abstract

The *hé-jiào* methods in trigonometry are the systematic application of the *sum-and-difference identities* — sin(A±B), cos(A±B), and their generalisations to sides and angles of a general triangle — to solve oblique-triangle problems. In the European tradition, these identities are central to Renaissance and post-Renaissance plane and spherical trigonometry (Vièta, Napier, etc.). In the Chinese tradition they entered via the Jesuit translation programme and the imperially-commissioned *Lǜ-lì yuān-yuán* 律曆淵源 (1722–1723).

Xiàng Míngdá's 4-*juàn* treatise presents a systematic exposition of the *hé-jiào* methods applied across the plane-triangle problem-domain. The treatise extends well beyond the *Lǜ-lì yuān-yuán* presentation to cover the systematic derivation of higher-order trigonometric identities (including some that arise naturally from the cyclotomic-series approach of [[KR3fc069]] *Xiàng-shù yī-yuán*).

The work is closely tied to Xiàng Míngdá's broader theoretical project: where the *Xiàng-shù yī-yuán* develops the cyclotomic *infinite-series* expansions for trigonometric functions, the *Sān-jiǎo hé-jiào shù* develops the *finite identities* (sum-and-difference relations) that govern the same functions. The two works together cover the analytic and the synthetic sides of the trigonometric apparatus.

Dating: Xiàng Míngdá lived 1789–1850. *notBefore* 1820 (mature productive period); *notAfter* 1850.
