# Gē-yuán mì-lǜ jié-fǎ 割圈密率捷法
 *Quick Method of Circle-Cutting Refined Ratios (Trigonometric Power Series)*
by [[安圖]] (Mínggatu, 撰) and [[陳際新]] (續, completed posthumously)

## About the work

The principal Chinese-language eighteenth-century mathematical treatise, in 6 *juàn* — the systematic exposition of the trigonometric power-series expansions transmitted to [[安圖]] Mínggatu (d. 1765) by the Jesuit mathematician Pierre Jartoux (Dù Démeǐ 杜德美, 1668–1720) during their joint Bureau-of-Astronomy work in the 1710s. Incomplete at Mínggatu's death in 1765; put through final editing and publication by his pupil [[陳際新]] Chén Jìxīn and others in the closing decade of Qián-lóng (c. 1775). The catalog records "extent: 6卷" and lists co-authorship 安圖[清]陳際新等續.

### Abstract

The work is the foundational document of Chinese-language analytical mathematics — the first systematic presentation in Chinese of the infinite-series methods (the power-series for sine, cosine, arc-sine, and related inverse functions) that constituted the early stages of European calculus. Pierre Jartoux had received the relevant series from his European correspondents (these are essentially the Newton-Leibniz series for trigonometric functions, transmitted to Jartoux through his Jesuit network) and had communicated three principal series to Mínggatu: the series for arc-length in terms of chord, the series for chord in terms of arc, and the series for *zhèng-shǐ* 正矢 (versed sine) in terms of arc. Jartoux had stated the series without proof.

Mínggatu's substantive contribution was twofold:

(a) **Combinatorial proofs**: Mínggatu supplied detailed combinatorial proofs for each series, working in a style adapted from the [[朱世傑]] Zhū Shìjié *duǒ-jī* 垛積 figurate-summation tradition of the Yuán. His proofs proceed by interpreting the series coefficients as combinatorial-figurate sums (sums of products of integers from successive natural-number sequences), then establishing the series identity by direct combinatorial reasoning. This is the principal substantive original contribution of Chinese mathematics in the eighteenth century, and it represents a remarkable indigenous theoretical-mathematical achievement.

(b) **Systematic extension**: where Jartoux had supplied three series, Mínggatu systematically extended the framework to nine related trigonometric series (the *jiǔ shù* 九術 "nine procedures" enumerated in the work): series for the arc in terms of chord, the chord in terms of arc, the versed-sine in terms of arc, the chord in terms of versed-sine, etc. — covering essentially all the elementary trigonometric inverse relations.

The work is structured as: *juàn* 1, the basic combinatorial-figurate methodology (essentially restating the Zhū Shìjié *duǒ-jī gē* material in updated form, with the new series as immediate applications); *juàn* 2–6, systematic derivation of each of the nine series with full combinatorial proof.

The work is remarkable on several counts. Substantively, it is the most theoretically-sophisticated Chinese mathematical work of the eighteenth century — the only Chinese mathematical production of that period to make a substantive original contribution to mathematical theory rather than primarily exposition of imported European content. Methodologically, it represents the most successful fusion of indigenous and imported mathematical traditions in pre-modern China: where most Sino-European mathematical productions of the period work primarily within one or the other tradition, Mínggatu's work genuinely combines them — taking the imported series-expansion results and grounding them in indigenous combinatorial-figurate methodology. Historically, it shows that the late-imperial Mongol-Manchu component of the Qīng mathematical establishment was not merely receptive to imported European learning but capable of substantive original work integrating European and East-Asian methodologies.

Dating: Mínggatu's productive period at the Bureau extends from c. 1712 to his death in 1765; the bulk of the *Gē-yuán mì-lǜ jié-fǎ* was composed in the middle decades of that period, with completion by Chén Jìxīn c. 1775. *NotBefore* set at 1730 (allowing for Mínggatu's mature productive period); *notAfter* at 1775 (Chén Jìxīn's editorial completion).

## Other points of interest

The *Gē-yuán mì-lǜ jié-fǎ* is the unique substantive original mathematical contribution from the eighteenth-century Chinese mathematical establishment — and remarkably, it comes from a Mongol-banner mathematician working at the imperial Bureau of Astronomy rather than from the Hàn-Chinese mathematical scholars who dominated the literary record. The work's existence is one of the strongest pieces of documentary evidence against the conventional view that eighteenth-century Chinese mathematics was a period of stagnation; in the *Gē-yuán mì-lǜ jié-fǎ* the most theoretically-advanced mathematics of the eighteenth century was being done in Beijing as well as in Europe.
