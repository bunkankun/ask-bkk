## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR3eu` is the last sub-division of `KR3e` 醫家類 (Masters — Medicine). Unlike its sister sub-divisions `KR3ea`–`KR3et`, which are organised by medical *genre* (醫經, 診法, 本草, 傷寒金匱, 婦科, 幼科, 養生, etc.), `KR3eu` is organised by *source and transmission*: every entry derives from the modern PRC reprint series *Hǎiwài huíguī Zhōngyī shàn-běn gǔ-jí cóng-shū* 海外回歸中醫善本古籍叢書 ("Excellent Editions of Chinese Medical Books Returned from Overseas," shorthand `hxwd` in the catalog meta), edited by Zhèng Jīnshēng 鄭金生 and his colleagues at the China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences in the 2000s. The corpus the series recovers is dominated by Japanese holdings — both Chinese late-Míng to late-Qīng medical works as preserved in Edo / Meiji *kanpō* 漢方 libraries, and the original Japanese *kanpō* scholarship those libraries produced from the late-17th century down to the early Shōwa era. A minority of entries are independent works whose only Kanripo classification is here; the majority duplicate works that are also separately catalogued in the genre-classified sub-divisions of `KR3e` (see the cross-references in the per-text notes, e.g. [[KR3eu044]] / KR3eq052; [[KR3eu050]] / [[KR3er094]]; [[KR3eu051]] / [[KR3eg025]]; [[KR3eu054]] / [[KR3el019]]; [[KR3eu057]] / [[KR3eg046]]; [[KR3eu070]] / KR3eq055; [[KR3eu073]] / KR3ec079; etc.). The sub-division therefore functions as the *hxwd* / Japan-transmission index for the medical corpus: a reader scanning `KR3eu` is scanning the part of Chinese medicine that travelled, was preserved, and was extended overseas, primarily in Edo Japan.

Chronologically the division spans roughly seven centuries: from [[錢乙]] Qián Yǐ's Sòng pediatric *Qiánshì xiǎo'ér zhí jué* 錢氏小兒直訣 [[KR3eu009]] (1119, in [[薛己]] Xuē Jǐ's later commentary), through the late-Míng to early-Qīng comprehensive corpora of [[薛己]] Xuē Jǐ ([[KR3eu002]]–[[KR3eu008]]), [[王肯堂]] Wáng Kěntáng ([[KR3eu011]]–[[KR3eu015]]) and [[吳謙]] Wú Qiān et al. ([[KR3eu016]]–[[KR3eu037]]), to the high-Edo *Kohōha* 古方派 / Ancient-Formula school of [[吉益爲則]] Yoshimasu Tōdō ([[KR3eu063]], [[KR3eu065]], [[KR3eu069]], [[KR3eu072]]) and the Edo *Igakukan* 醫學館 Tamba-family *kǎojù* lineage ([[KR3eu041]], [[KR3eu044]], [[KR3eu046]], [[KR3eu047]], [[KR3eu049]], [[KR3eu061]], [[KR3eu064]], [[KR3eu067]]), and finally to the Shōwa-era pharmacological and historical writings of [[大塚敬節]] Ōtsuka Keisetsu [[KR3eu056]], [[富士川遊]] Fujikawa Yū [[KR3eu076]], [[中尾萬三]] Nakao Manzō [[KR3eu075]], [[峰下鐵雄]] Mineshita Tetsuo [[KR3eu074]], and [[久保田晴光]] Kubota Seimitsu [[KR3eu073]] of the South Manchurian Railway / Mantetsu pharmacological apparatus. Endymion Wilkinson, *Chinese History: A New Manual* § 41 ("Medicine"), treats the *kanpō* tradition as the most important extra-Chinese branch of pre-modern Chinese-medical scholarship — both because the Tamba family's *Yījí kǎo* [[KR3eu044]] is the indispensable single bibliography of pre-modern Chinese medical literature, and because Edo / Meiji *kanpō* preserved Chinese medical works (e.g. the Sòng校正 medical-books-bureau prints, Wáng Bīng *Sùwèn* recensions, Sūn Sīmiǎo *Qiānjīn fāng* witnesses) lost in their Chinese transmission-line. This sub-division is the principal locus in `KR3e` for that overseas line.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The Xuē Jǐ corpus (mid-Míng *wēnbǔ* benchmark).** [[薛己]] Xuē Jǐ (1487–1559), the Sūzhōu *Tàiyīyuàn* physician who became the central figure of the mid-Míng *wēnbǔ* 溫補 ("warming-and-tonifying") synthesis, is represented in `KR3eu` by his collected case-cum-formulary *Xuēshì yīàn* 薛氏醫案 [[KR3eu002]], his internal-medicine *Nèikē zhāi yào* 內科摘要 [[KR3eu003]], the pediatric *Bǎo yīng cuì yào* 保嬰粹要 [[KR3eu004]], the orthopaedic *Zhèngtǐ lèi yào* 正體類要 [[KR3eu006]], the *Lìyáng jī yào* 癘瘍機要 [[KR3eu007]] on leprosy and ulcers, and his annotated edition of [[陳自明]] Chén Zìmíng's gynaecology *Jiàozhù Fùrén liáng fāng* 校註婦人良方 [[KR3eu008]]. He also stands behind the recension of [[錢乙]] Qián Yǐ's pediatric *Qiánshì xiǎo'ér zhí jué* [[KR3eu009]] and [[陳自明]] Chén Zìmíng's external-medicine *Wàikē jīng yào* [[KR3eu010]] as transmitted in `KR3eu`. The Xuē Jǐ corpus was the most-imported Chinese mid-Míng medical literature in Edo Japan and the principal proximate Chinese-language source for the early-Edo *Gōseihōha* 後世方派 ("Later-Formula school" — those who followed JīnYuán and MíngQīng doctrinal synthesis as against the *Kohōha*'s call to return to Zhāng Zhòngjǐng).

**The Wáng Kěntáng *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng* sub-corpus.** [[王肯堂]] Wáng Kěntáng's late-Míng systematic clinical compendium *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng* 證治準繩 (1597–1607) is here represented by the head-entry [[KR3eu011]] and by the four major topical sub-parts: *Lèifāng* 類方 (categorical prescriptions) [[KR3eu012]], *Shānghán* 傷寒 [[KR3eu013]], *Nǚkē* 女科 [[KR3eu014]], and *Yòukē* 幼科 [[KR3eu015]] — the last being the most extensive late-Míng systematic pediatric handbook, and a principal Chinese-language source for Edo *kanpō* pediatrics.

**The *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* sub-corpus.** [[吳謙]] Wú Qiān et al.'s Qiánlóng-imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* 醫宗金鑑 (1742–1749) — the standard examination-curriculum compendium of Qīng official medicine — is the most heavily-represented Chinese work in the division: head-entries at [[KR3eu016]] and [[KR3eu027]] (two recension witnesses preserved in *hxwd*), and the topical sub-parts *Dìngzhèng Zhòngjǐng quánshū* (Shānghán + Jīnguì corrected commentaries) [[KR3eu017]] [[KR3eu026]] [[KR3eu028]] [[KR3eu029]], *Shānbǔ míngyī fānglùn* [[KR3eu018]] [[KR3eu030]], *Yùnqì yàojué* [[KR3eu019]], *Zábìng xīnfǎ yàojué* [[KR3eu020]] [[KR3eu031]], *Fùkē xīnfǎ yàojué* [[KR3eu021]] [[KR3eu032]], *Yòukē zábìng xīnfǎ* [[KR3eu033]], *Dòuzhěn xīnfǎ yàojué* [[KR3eu022]] [[KR3eu034]], *Yǎnkē xīnfǎ yàojué* [[KR3eu023]] [[KR3eu035]], *Cìjiǔ xīnfǎ yàojué* (acupuncture-moxibustion) [[KR3eu024]] [[KR3eu036]], *Wàikē xīnfǎ yàojué* (external medicine) [[KR3eu025]], and *Zhènggǔ xīnfǎ yàozhǐ* (bone-setting) [[KR3eu037]]. The double-listing for many sub-parts reflects two separate Japanese-preserved recensions in the *hxwd* series.

**Late-Qīng comprehensive works.** [[鄭玉壇]] Zhèng Yùtán's *Tóngyuán yīshū · Xiǎo'érkē* 彤園醫書·小兒科 [[KR3eu038]] (Jiāqìng-era pediatrics) and [[黃朝坊]] Huáng Cháofāng's twin *Jīnguì qǐyuè* 金匱啟鑰 — *Fùkē* [[KR3eu039]] and *Yǎnkē* [[KR3eu040]] (gynaecology and ophthalmology) — close the Chinese block of the division.

**The Tamba (Taki) *Igakukan* lineage** is the doctrinal centre of the Japanese block. Three generations of the Edo *Tàiyī-yuàn* / *Igakukan* 醫學館 directors produced the most important Japanese critical philology of the Chinese medical Classics ever written.
- [[丹波元簡]] Tamba no Mototane (1755–1810), the founder, is represented by *Màixué jíyào* 脈學輯要 [[KR3eu061]] (1795 — the standard Edo synthesis of Chinese pulse doctrine), *Jiùjí xuǎnfāng* 救急選方 [[KR3eu064]] (emergency formulary), and *Yīshèng* 醫賸 [[KR3eu067]] (his collected reading-notes / philological miscellany, the *Eindringtisch* of Edo medical *kǎojù*). He is also the *始撰* of [[KR3eu044]].
- [[丹波元堅]] Tamba no Motokata (1795–1857), Mototane's younger son, contributed *Sùwèn shào shí* 素問紹識 [[KR3eu041]] (continuing his father's *Sùwèn shí* — commenting on the *Huángdì Nèijīng Sùwèn*), *Shānghán guǎng yào* 傷寒廣要 [[KR3eu046]] (the most comprehensive Edo *Shānghán* compendium), *Shānghán lùn shù yì* 傷寒論述義 [[KR3eu047]] (exposition of the *Shānghán lùn*), and *Jīnguì yùhán yàolüè shù yì* 金匱玉函要略述義 [[KR3eu049]] (exposition of the *Jīnguì yàolüè*).
- [[丹波元胤]] Tamba no Motoin (1789–1827), Mototane's elder son, completed the family's collective magnum opus *Zhōngguó yījí kǎo* 中國醫籍考 [[KR3eu044]] (1810–1827; printed 1831–32) — an 80-juan critical bibliography of Chinese medical literature from the Hàn to the late Míng, covering some 3,000 works, that remains the indispensable single reference for the philology of Chinese medical books down to the present.

**The *Kohōha* 古方派 / Ancient-Formula school.** The Edo doctrinal counterpart to the *Igakukan* philological lineage. [[吉益爲則]] Yoshimasu Tōdō (1702–1773) and his disciples advocate radical return to the formulas of [[張機]] Zhāng Zhòngjǐng's *Shānghán lùn* and *Jīnguì yàolüè* against all post-Hàn doctrine. Tōdō is represented by *Lèijù fāng* 類聚方 [[KR3eu063]] (1762 — categorical re-arrangement of Zhòngjǐng's formulas), the paired *Jiāshú fāng yǔ Fāng jí* 家塾方與方極 [[KR3eu065]], the polemical *Yī duàn yǔ Chì yī duàn* 醫斷與斥醫斷 [[KR3eu069]] (Tsuruoki Gen'itsu 鶴沖元逸's recording of Tōdō's *Medical Pronouncements* together with the rebuttal), and most famously the *Yào zhēng* 藥徵 [[KR3eu072]] (1771–84 — the *locus classicus* of *Kohōha* drug-doctrine, asserting that each drug is to be verified solely against its empirical *zhèng* / pattern in the *Shānghán* / *Jīnguì*, against all *Běncǎo gāngmù* literary tradition). Second-generation *Kohōha* texts in `KR3eu`: [[中神琴溪]] Nakagami Kinkei's *Shēngshēngtáng zhìyàn* 生生堂治驗 [[KR3eu070]] (1804 case-records); [[田中榮信]] Tanaka Eishin's *Chángshā zhènghuì* 長沙證彙 [[KR3eu050]] (1790, prefaced by Tōdō's son 吉益南涯); [[中西惟忠]] Nakanishi Iyū's *Shānghán zhī yánjiū* 傷寒之研究 [[KR3eu045]] (1789); [[川越正淑]] Kawagoe Seishuku's *Shānghán yòngyào yánjiū* 傷寒用藥研究 [[KR3eu048]] (1797); and [[賀谷壽]] Kayano Ju's *Qízhèng fāng* 奇正方 [[KR3eu062]] (Kanripo holding is header-only).

**Specialist Edo *kanpō* innovations.** Several Edo *kanpō* physicians produced specialist monographs that have no Chinese precedent and represent original Japanese clinical contributions.
- *Chǎnlùn* 產論 [[KR3eu055]] — [[賀川玄悅]] Kagawa Genetsu (1700–1777), 1765. The foundational Edo obstetrics treatise, presenting the *Kagawa-ryū* 賀川流 head-down delivery doctrine, instrumental delivery techniques, and version manoeuvres. The most influential pre-modern Japanese obstetrical work.
- *Dòukē biànyào* 痘科辨要 [[KR3eu057]] — [[池田瑞仙]] Ikeda Zuisen (1734–1816), 1807–11. The principal Edo monograph on smallpox treatment, by the foremost family-tradition specialist of the period.
- *Méilì xīnshū* 黴癘新書 [[KR3eu058]] — [[片倉元周]] Katakura Genshū (1751–1822), 1786–88. Major Edo monograph on syphilis and leprosy; the doctrinal manifesto preface was contributed by Tamba Genkan in 1788.
- *Qīngnáng suǒtàn* 青囊瑣探 [[KR3eu068]] — Katakura Genshū again, 1801–02, miscellany of clinical observations.
- *Zhōngguó jiēgǔ túshuō* 中國接骨圖說 [[KR3eu054]] — [[二宮獻]] Ninomiya Ken (1754–1827), 1807–08. The first major Japanese orthopaedic-manipulation treatise; *zhōngguó* 中國 in the title refers to the Chūgoku region of western Honshū, not to China. Integrates the *kuatsu* / *shi-katsu kenpō* manual-manipulation tradition with classical bone-setting.
- *Xuǎnzhēn sānyào jí* 選針三要集 [[KR3eu060]] — [[杉山和一]] Sugiyama Waichi (1614–1694), 1680–94. Founding text of the Sugiyama school of blind-acupuncturists and of the *kuda-bari* 管針 ("tube-needle") technique that is the principal pre-modern Japanese contribution to acupuncture practice.
- *Wēnbìng zhī yánjiū* 溫病之研究 [[KR3eu051]] — [[源元凱]] Minamoto Genkai, 1788–1811. Major Edo response to the Wú Yǒuxìng / *wēnbìng* tradition, prompted by the catastrophic 1788 Kyoto epidemic.

**Late-Edo philological and clinical miscellanies.** Beyond the Tamba *Yīshèng*, the division preserves several other major *kanpō* clinical-philological miscellanies: [[原昌克]] Hara Masakatsu's *Cóngguì ǒujì* 叢桂偶記 [[KR3eu071]] (1800); [[中川成章]] Nakagawa Seishō's *Zhèngzhì zhāi yào* 證治摘要 [[KR3eu043]] (1820–50); [[平井庭石]] Hirai Teiseki's *Gǔfāng fēnliàng kǎo* 古方分量考 [[KR3eu066]] (1793 — measure-and-dose philology for ancient formulas).

**Late-Edo / Meiji historical and integrative writing.** [[今邨亮]] Imamura Ryōan's *Yīshì qǐyuán* 醫事啟源 [[KR3eu042]] (1862) is one of the most comprehensive Edo historical surveys of the origins of Chinese medicine — by an author who in the early Meiji actively defended *kanpō* against the official Meiji programme of replacement by German biomedicine. [[佐藤利信]] Satō Toshinobu's *Zhēnxué tōnglùn* 針學通論 [[KR3eu059]] (1880–1910) is the Meiji-era reformulation of acupuncture instruction. [[高島久貫]] Takashima Hisatsura's *Xièyì xīnlùn* 瀉疫新論 [[KR3eu052]] (1858–79) and [[岡田昌春]] Okada Masaharu's *Jiǎoqì gàilùn* 腳氣概論 [[KR3eu053]] (1879) document the late-Edo / early-Meiji *kanpō* responses to the cholera and beriberi epidemics that accompanied the Meiji opening.

**Republican-era / Shōwa Japanese pharmacology and history.** [[富士川遊]] Fujikawa Yū's *Zhōngguó yīyào lùnwén jí* 中國醫藥論文集 [[KR3eu076]] is the collected essays of the founding modern historian of Japanese medicine. [[大塚敬節]] Ōtsuka Keisetsu's *Zhōngguó érkē yījiàn* 中國兒科醫鑑 [[KR3eu056]] (1936) is one of the foundational texts of the early-Shōwa *kanpō* revival. [[久保田晴光]] Kubota Seimitsu's *Hànyào yánjiū gāngyào* 漢藥研究綱要 [[KR3eu073]], [[峰下鐵雄]] Mineshita Tetsuo's *Lùróng zhī yánjiū* 鹿茸之研究 [[KR3eu074]], and [[中尾萬三]] Nakao Manzō's *Zhōngguó yào yībǎizhǒng zhī huàxué shíyàn* 中國藥一百種之化學實驗 [[KR3eu075]] represent the early-Shōwa Japanese colonial-pharmacological project (centred on the South Manchurian Railway *chōsabu* and related institutions in northeast China) of subjecting the Chinese materia medica to modern chemical and pharmacological scrutiny.

**The *Pǔjì fāng* pediatric chapter.** [[朱橚]] Zhū Sù's *Pǔjì fāng · Yīnghái mén* 普濟方·嬰孩門 [[KR3eu001]] — a stand-alone *hxwd* reprint of the pediatric subsection (j. 358–406) of the great early-Míng imperial-princely formulary [[KR3e0067]] — preserves the most extensive late-medieval Chinese pediatric corpus of the late-14th / early-15th century and is the proximate Chinese source for both the Xuē Jǐ pediatric works and for the Wáng Kěntáng *Yòukē* [[KR3eu015]].

**The Yuán ophthalmological foundation.** [[倪維德]] Ní Wéidé's *Yuánjī qǐwēi* 原機啟微 [[KR3eu005]] (1370–1377) is the first systematic Yuán-era Chinese ophthalmological treatise and the principal Chinese-language source for ophthalmology in the *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* and in the Edo *kanpō* ophthalmological tradition.

## Important persons

- [[朱橚]] (Zhū Sù, 1361–1425) — early-Míng prince of Zhōu and chief patron-compiler of the *Pǔjì fāng*; the pediatric chapter [[KR3eu001]] is the standalone *hxwd* witness.
- [[倪維德]] (Ní Wéidé, 1303–1377) — Yuán Sūzhōu physician; first systematic ophthalmologist of the late imperial era, in this division through [[KR3eu005]].
- [[錢乙]] (Qián Yǐ, c. 1032–1113) — Sòng pediatrician of imperial *Tàiyīyuàn*; founder of the systematic *xiǎo'érkē* 小兒科 tradition; in this division via the *Qiánshì xiǎo'ér zhí jué* [[KR3eu009]] (in Xuē Jǐ's later commentary).
- [[陳自明]] (Chén Zìmíng, c. 1190–1270) — Sòng external-medicine and gynaecology systematizer; in this division through *Wàikē jīng yào* [[KR3eu010]] and Xuē Jǐ's annotated edition of his *Fùrén liángfāng* [[KR3eu008]].
- [[薛己]] (Xuē Jǐ, 1487–1559) — mid-Míng *wēnbǔ* / *bǔtǔ* synthesizer and Sūzhōu *Tàiyīyuàn* physician; the most heavily-represented single author of the division ([[KR3eu002]]–[[KR3eu004]], [[KR3eu006]]–[[KR3eu008]]) and the central late-Míng author for Edo *Gōseihōha* reception.
- [[王肯堂]] (Wáng Kěntáng, 1549–1613) — late-Míng *jìnshì* turned scholar-physician; author of the systematic *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng* corpus [[KR3eu011]]–[[KR3eu015]], the principal Chinese clinical compendium of the late Wǎnlì era.
- [[吳謙]] (Wú Qiān, c. 1689–1748) and [[劉裕鐸]] (Liú Yùduó, c. 1700–1773) — chief editors of the Qiánlóng-imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* [[KR3eu016]]–[[KR3eu037]], the standard examination-curriculum compendium of Qīng official medicine.
- [[鄭玉壇]] (Zhèng Yùtán, fl. 1796–1820) — Húnán Liúyáng physician; author of the *Tóngyuán yīshū* family of clinical handbooks [[KR3eu038]].
- [[黃朝坊]] (Huáng Cháofāng, fl. 1840–1900) — late-Qīng compiler of the *Jīnguì qǐyuè* family [[KR3eu039]] [[KR3eu040]].
- [[杉山和一]] (Sugiyama Waichi, 1614–1694) — blind Edo acupuncturist; founder of the *Sugiyama-ryū* and inventor of the *kuda-bari* 管針 technique; in this division through [[KR3eu060]].
- [[吉益爲則]] (Yoshimasu Tōdō, 1702–1773) — central figure of the Edo *Kohōha* / Ancient-Formula school; author of [[KR3eu063]], [[KR3eu065]], [[KR3eu072]] and stimulus for [[KR3eu069]].
- [[賀川玄悅]] (Kagawa Genetsu, 1700–1777) — founder of the Kagawa-school of Japanese obstetrics; author of the *Chǎnlùn* [[KR3eu055]].
- [[丹波元簡]] (Tamba no Mototane / Taki no Mototane, 1755–1810) — founder of the Edo *Igakukan* 醫學館 critical-philological lineage; author of [[KR3eu061]], [[KR3eu064]], [[KR3eu067]] and *始撰* of the family magnum opus [[KR3eu044]]. The single most influential pre-modern Japanese medical philologist.
- [[丹波元胤]] (Tamba no Motoin, 1789–1827) — elder son of Mototane; *續撰* and posthumous completer of the *Yījí kǎo* [[KR3eu044]].
- [[丹波元堅]] (Tamba no Motokata, 1795–1857) — younger son of Mototane; major *Shānghán* and *Jīnguì* philologist of the next generation, in this division through [[KR3eu041]], [[KR3eu046]], [[KR3eu047]], [[KR3eu049]].
- [[池田瑞仙]] (Ikeda Zuisen / Ikeda Dokumi 池田獨美, 1734–1816) — leading family-tradition smallpox specialist of late Edo; author of [[KR3eu057]].
- [[片倉元周]] (Katakura Genshū / *Kakuryō* 鶴陵, 1751–1822) — Kagawa-school obstetrician-physician; author of the syphilis-and-leprosy monograph [[KR3eu058]] and the clinical miscellany [[KR3eu068]].
- [[二宮獻]] (Ninomiya Ken / *Hikoka* 彥可, 1754–1827) — medical officer of Hamada *han* in Iwami; author of the first systematic Japanese bone-setting treatise [[KR3eu054]].
- [[中神琴溪]] (Nakagami Kinkei, 1744–1833) — Kyoto *Seiseidō* clinician; second-generation *Kohōha* teacher; case-records compiled by his pupil [[小野遜]] Ono Son [[KR3eu070]].
- [[原昌克]] (Hara Masakatsu / *Nan'yō* 南陽, 1753–1820) — late-Edo physician and acupoint philologist (best known for the *Jīngxué huìjiě* in `KR3ee`); in this division through the miscellany [[KR3eu071]].
- [[今邨亮]] (Imamura Ryōan 今村了庵, 1814–1890) — senior shogunal physician and Meiji-era defender of *kanpō*; author of the historical survey [[KR3eu042]].
- [[源元凱]] (Minamoto Genkai, fl. 1788–1811) — Kyoto physician of the *wēnbìng* response tradition; author of [[KR3eu051]].
- [[中西惟忠]] (Nakanishi Iyū, fl. 1789) — *Kohōha* *Shānghán* author [[KR3eu045]].
- [[田中榮信]] (Tanaka Eishin, fl. 1790) — Banshū / Harima *Kohōha* systematizer [[KR3eu050]].
- [[川越正淑]] (Kawagoe Seishuku, fl. 1797) — *Kohōha* drug-research [[KR3eu048]].
- [[中川成章]] (Nakagawa Seishō, fl. 1820–50) — late-Edo clinical-handbook compiler [[KR3eu043]].
- [[平井庭石]] (Hirai Teiseki, fl. 1793) — Edo philologist of ancient-formula dosages [[KR3eu066]].
- [[賀谷壽]] (Kayano Ju, fl. 1750–1850) — *Kohōha* formulary author [[KR3eu062]] (Kanripo holding header-only).
- [[鶴沖元逸]] (Tsuruoki Gen'itsu, fl. 1747–1759) — pupil-redactor of Yoshimasu Tōdō; recorded the *Yī duàn* [[KR3eu069]].

## Topics

The division is organised by a different principle from its sister sub-divisions (genre); but the texts collected nevertheless cluster around a coherent set of recurring problems.

**The Edo *Kohōha* / *Gōseihōha* polarity.** The single most important Edo medical-doctrinal debate. The *Gōseihōha* 後世方派 ("Later-Formula school") follows the JīnYuán and Míng synthetic doctrine — especially the Xuē Jǐ / Zhāng Jièbīn *wēnbǔ* line and the *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* curriculum — and is represented in `KR3eu` by the large blocks of imported Chinese works [[KR3eu002]]–[[KR3eu040]]. The *Kohōha* 古方派 ("Ancient-Formula school"), founded by Nagoya Gen'i and brought to its radical form by Yoshimasu Tōdō, calls for return to Zhāng Zhòngjǐng's *Shānghán* / *Jīnguì* corpus alone, against all post-Hàn doctrine. The polemical *Yī duàn yǔ Chì yī duàn* [[KR3eu069]] is the most direct surviving record of this contest, and the *Yào zhēng* [[KR3eu072]] is the school's most influential single text. The Tamba *Igakukan* lineage's position is a third one — neither doctrinal *Kohōha* nor doctrinal *Gōseihōha* but a *kǎojù*-philological reconstruction of all Chinese medical sources on their own evidential terms.

**Critical philology of the Chinese medical Classics.** The Edo *Igakukan* lineage applied to Chinese medical literature the same critical-philological method that the Edo *Kogaku* 古学 and Mito-school philologists were applying to the Confucian Classics: collation of multiple recensions, *kǎozhèng* on dates, persons, and transmission, recovery of lost works from quotations. The fruits of this method are the Tamba *Sùwèn shí* 素問識 (in `KR3ea`), *Língshū shí* 靈樞識 (in `KR3ea`), *Shānghán lùn jíyì* (in `KR3ef`), the *Sùwèn shàoshí* [[KR3eu041]], the *Shānghán guǎng yào* [[KR3eu046]] and *Shānghán lùn shù yì* [[KR3eu047]], the *Jīnguì yùhán yàolüè shù yì* [[KR3eu049]], and — encompassing all the rest — the bibliographical *Yījí kǎo* [[KR3eu044]]. Late-Qīng Chinese critical philology (Hú Shù, Yú Yuè) draws directly on this Edo tradition. The Tamba miscellanies *Yīshèng* [[KR3eu067]] and Hara Masakatsu's *Cóngguì ǒujì* [[KR3eu071]] are the *Sitz im Leben* of this scholarly practice — the reading-notes of a working physician-philologist.

**Japanese clinical innovations without Chinese precedent.** Several Edo specialist traditions developed clinical-technical practices that have no equivalent in the imperial-Chinese inheritance and constitute genuine Japanese contributions: Kagawa-school obstetrics with mechanical version manoeuvres ([[KR3eu055]]); the Sugiyama *kuda-bari* tube-needle technique for acupuncture ([[KR3eu060]]); the Ninomiya-school orthopaedic manipulation integrated with *jūjutsu* / *kuatsu* ([[KR3eu054]]); the Ikeda smallpox family-tradition with its 50-year clinical longitudinal experience ([[KR3eu057]]); and Katakura's joint *méilì* taxonomy of syphilis-and-leprosy ([[KR3eu058]]).

**The reception of *wēnbìng* doctrine in Japan.** Edo Japan periodically faced major epidemics (smallpox, measles, cholera, dysentery, beriberi). The reception of Wú Yǒuxìng's *Wēnyì lùn* and the Qīng *wēnbìng* school in Japan is documented in [[KR3eu051]] (Minamoto Genkai's response to the 1788 Kyoto epidemic), and the late-Edo / Meiji epidemic-response tradition continues through [[KR3eu052]] (cholera, 1858–79) and [[KR3eu053]] (beriberi, 1879).

**Kanpō historiography and the early-Meiji crisis.** With the 1874 Meiji government decree replacing *kanpō* with German biomedicine as official medicine, Japanese *kanpō* enters its long crisis. The early-Meiji historical works — Imamura's *Yīshì qǐyuán* [[KR3eu042]] in 1862, then Fujikawa Yū's foundational historiography [[KR3eu076]] — are both contributions to scholarship and political defences of the *kanpō* tradition. Ōtsuka Keisetsu's 1936 *Zhōngguó érkē yījiàn* [[KR3eu056]] documents the early-Shōwa *kanpō* revival's methodological compromise with biomedicine and the strategy of repackaging *Kohōha* therapeutics for a biomedical readership.

**Modern pharmacology of Chinese drugs.** The Shōwa-era pharmacological writings ([[KR3eu073]] [[KR3eu074]] [[KR3eu075]]) reflect the Japanese colonial / *Mantetsu* programme of subjecting the inherited Chinese materia medica to modern chemical analysis — a project carried out largely in northeast China during the 1930s and continued post-war in Japanese and Chinese pharmaceutical research.

**The *hxwd* reprint enterprise as a sub-topic in itself.** Every text in `KR3eu` carries the implicit historical claim that the *Hǎiwài huíguī Zhōngyī shàn-běn gǔ-jí cóng-shū* enterprise has restored to Chinese-language scholarship a body of medical literature — both Chinese-authored works preserved overseas and Japanese-authored *kanpō* literature — that pre-1949 PRC scholarship had largely lost access to. The cross-references from `KR3eu` to other genre-sub-divisions of `KR3e` (and the residue of texts catalogued only here) record the editorial decisions of that enterprise.
