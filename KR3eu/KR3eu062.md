# Qí-zhèng fāng 奇正方
 *Formulas, Extraordinary and Regular*
by [[賀谷壽]] (撰)

## About the work

The *Qí-zhèng fāng* 奇正方 (Japanese reading *Kisei hō*) is a late-Edo Japanese formulary attributed in the catalog meta to [[賀谷壽]] **Kayano Ju** 賀谷壽, a physician of the *Ko-iho* 古醫方 (Ancient-Formula School) tradition descended from [[吉益爲則]] **Yoshimasu Tōdō** 吉益東洞 (1702–1773). The work is independently catalogued in the *hxwd* series as [[KR3ed095]] in the present knowledgebase, where it survives in the same header-only state.

### Abstract

The Kanripo digital source for this text (`KR3eu062_001.txt`) is **only the six-line file header** — title, KR id, date stamp, BASEEDITION marker, and an empty JUAN line — with **no preface or body text** preserved. The parallel listing at [[KR3ed095]] is in identical state. The catalog meta gives the author as 日本·賀谷壽 ("Japan-Kayano Ju") and source *hxwd*, with no dynasty bracket.

The title term *qí-zhèng* 奇正 ("irregular and orthodox") is borrowed from the *Sūn-zǐ bīng-fǎ* 孫子兵法 distinction between *zhèng-bīng* (orthodox tactical formations) and *qí-bīng* (extraordinary, surprise manoeuvres) and is one of [[吉益爲則]] Yoshimasu Tōdō's signature theoretical devices for organising formulary practice in his school. In the Tōdō scheme, the *zhèng-fāng* are Zhāng Zhòng-jǐng's canonical *Shāng-hán* / *Jīn-guì* formulas, the *qí-fāng* are the extraordinary or supplementary prescriptions devised for the cases that the canonical formulas do not cover — a logical complement, not a doctrinal opposition. Kayano's work title and *Ko-iho*-school affiliation place him squarely in the Tōdō lineage, plausibly in the **mid-to-late Edo period (ca. 1750–1850)**; no tighter bracket is defensible without the actual text body.

Kayano Ju does not appear in the standard Edo medical biographical dictionaries (*Nihon-i-shi* 日本醫史, *Kanpō no rinshō* 漢方の臨牀 biographical indices) and is essentially known only from the title-line of this work and its inclusion in the **Shanghai *Huáng-Hàn yī-xué cóng-shū*** 皇漢醫學叢書 (Shanghai: Shìjiè Shūjú, 1936) — the source of the *hxwd*-series text.
