### Scope and scholarly tradition

KR2a 正史類 (*Zhèngshǐ lèi*, "Standard Histories") collects the canonical dynastic histories of imperial China together with their classical commentaries, supplements, and works of textual rectification. The category is the head of KR2 史部 (Histories), and within the *Sìkù quánshū* taxonomy it occupies the most prestigious position among historical genres: every other history-section subcategory — annals (編年), unofficial history (別史), miscellaneous history (雜史), biography (傳記), geography (地理), bureaucratic monographs (職官), bibliography (目錄), and so on — is defined in part by its difference from, or supplement to, the *zhèngshǐ*. The defining textual form is the *jìzhuàn tǐ* 紀傳體 ("annals-and-biographies form") modelled on Sīmǎ Qiān's 司馬遷 *Shǐjì* 史記 and Bān Gù's 班固 *Hànshū* 漢書, in which a court chronicle (*běnjì* 本紀) is followed by chronological tables (*biǎo* 表), topical treatises (*zhì* 志 / *shū* 書), and exemplary biographies (*lièzhuàn* 列傳). Wilkinson (§49) traces how the term *zhèngshǐ* itself was first applied to single-dynasty *jìzhuàn* histories in the Liáng 梁 (502–57), and how dynastic sponsorship of an "official" history of the previous dynasty became, from the early Táng onwards, one of the principal devices by which a new regime affirmed its claim to *zhèngtǒng* 正統 (legitimate succession; see Wilkinson §49.5, §A.2.6).

The thirty-nine texts in this division cover the full chronological span of the imperial era, from the *Shǐjì* (composed under Hàn Wǔdì 漢武帝 in the late 2nd / early 1st century BCE) to the *Qīng shǐ gǎo* 清史稿 (drafted 1914–27 by holdover Qīng officials under the Republic). They divide cleanly into three layers: (i) the twenty-four *zhèngshǐ* canonized in the *Sìkù* selection plus the Republican-era *Qīng shǐ gǎo* — twenty-five core histories in all, one per legitimate dynasty (with *Sānguó zhì* 三國志 covering the Three Kingdoms collectively and three parallel pairs of *Tángshū* / *Wǔdài shǐ* / *Hòu Hànshū*-with-treatises representing successive recensions); (ii) the great early commentaries that became inseparable from their host texts — Péi Yīn's 裴駰 *Jíjiě* 集解, Sīmǎ Zhēn's 司馬貞 *Suǒyǐn* 索隱, and Zhāng Shǒujié's 張守節 *Zhèngyì* 正義 on the *Shǐjì*; Yán Shīgǔ's 顏師古 commentary on the *Hànshū*; Péi Sōngzhī's 裴松之 commentary on the *Sānguó zhì*; Lǐ Xián's 李賢 commentary on the *Hòu Hànshū*; (iii) Sòng– and Qīng–era works of *kǎoyì* 考異, *bǔyí* 補遺, *jiūmiù* 糾謬 and *biànwù* 辨誤 — corrections, supplements, and tabular reconstructions that became part of the apparatus through which the histories were read.

### Important texts and text clusters

**The Sì shǐ 四史 ("Four Histories") cluster.** The first four *zhèngshǐ* — *Shǐjì*, *Hànshū*, *Hòu Hànshū*, and *Sānguó zhì* — are by long convention treated as a unit, both because they cover the formative imperial period (Hàn through Three Kingdoms) and because they are the most stylistically and historiographically accomplished members of the genre. Wilkinson (§59) treats them together as the foundational corpus of historical Chinese.

- *Shǐjì* 史記 [[KR2a0001]] — Sīmǎ Qiān's universal history from the Yellow Thearch to Hàn Wǔdì, the prototype of every later *zhèngshǐ* and the origin of the *jìzhuàn* form. Carried in the WYG together with the three Táng-era commentaries below as the *Sānjiā zhù* 三家注 *Shǐjì*.
- *Shǐjì jíjiě* 史記集解 [[KR2a0002]] — Péi Yīn's mid-5th-century compilation of Hàn–Six-Dynasties glosses on the *Shǐjì*, the first of the *Sānjiā zhù* and the channel through which most pre-Táng *Shǐjì* scholarship survives.
- *Shǐjì suǒyǐn* 史記索隱 [[KR2a0003]] — Sīmǎ Zhēn's Táng commentary, especially valued for its critical engagement with Péi Yīn and for the *Bǔ Sānhuáng běnjì* 補三皇本紀.
- *Shǐjì zhèngyì* 史記正義 [[KR2a0004]] — Zhāng Shǒujié's Táng commentary, the most geographically detailed of the three and the principal source for Táng-period topographical identifications of *Shǐjì* place names.
- *Qián Hànshū* 前漢書 [[KR2a0007]] — Bān Gù's history of the Western Hàn, the first single-dynasty *zhèngshǐ* and the model of organization (annals + tables + treatises + biographies, all bounded by one dynasty) followed by every successor.
- *Hòu Hànshū* 後漢書 [[KR2a0009]] — Fàn Yè's Liú-Sòng compilation covering the Eastern Hàn, transmitted with the eight *zhì* (treatises) of Sīmǎ Biāo's 司馬彪 *Xù Hàn shū* 續漢書 (annotated by Liú Zhāo 劉昭) substituted for the lost monographs Fàn Yè had planned, and with Lǐ Xián's 李賢 Táng commentary.
- *Sānguó zhì* 三國志 [[KR2a0012]] — Chén Shòu's late-3rd-century history of Wèi, Shǔ, and Wú, indispensable for its expansion in the early 5th century by Péi Sōngzhī, whose commentary preserves more than two hundred lost works in extenso.

**Comparative and corrective Sòng scholarship on the Sì shǐ.** The Sòng marked the first sustained scholarly engagement with the foundational histories as objects of *kǎozhèng* 考證.

- *Bān-Mǎ yìtóng* 班馬異同 [[KR2a0008]] — Ní Sī's 倪思 systematic juxtaposition of the *Shǐjì* and *Hànshū*, registering Bān Gù's silent edits to passages he inherited from Sīmǎ Qiān, with marginal evaluation by Liú Chénwēng 劉辰翁.
- *Bǔ Hòu Hànshū niánbiǎo* 補後漢書年表 [[KR2a0010]] — Xióng Fāng's 熊方 reconstruction of the chronological tables Fàn Yè never compiled, supplying for the *Hòu Hànshū* the *biǎo* layer that the *Shǐjì* / *Hànshū* model required.
- *Liǎng Hàn kānwù bǔyí* 兩漢刋誤補遺 [[KR2a0011]] — Wú Rénjié's 吳仁傑 textual corrections to both *Hàn* histories.

**The Tang single-dynasty Histories (Eight Histories of the early Táng).** Between 629 and 656 the Táng court produced or supervised eight successive *zhèngshǐ* covering every regime from Jìn to Suí, fixing the model of imperially commissioned historiography that lasted to the end of the dynastic era.

- *Jìnshū* 晉書 [[KR2a0015]] — compiled under Fáng Xuánlíng 房玄齡 by an editorial board appointed by Tàizōng 太宗 in 644, drawing on (and superseding) more than a dozen earlier Jìn histories.
- *Sòngshū* 宋書 [[KR2a0016]] — Shěn Yuē's 沈約 history of the Liú-Sòng, completed already in the Liáng (487/488) and incorporated into the canon as the standard account of that dynasty.
- *Nán Qí shū* 南齊書 [[KR2a0017]] — Xiāo Zǐxiǎn 蕭子顯 of the Liáng on the dynasty in which his own ancestor reigned.
- *Liáng shū* 梁書 [[KR2a0018]] — drafted by Yáo Chá 姚察 (Liáng/Chén) and completed by his son Yáo Sīlián 姚思廉 under Táng auspices.
- *Chénshū* 陳書 [[KR2a0019]] — companion volume by the same Yáo father–son line, completed in 636.
- *Wèishū* 魏書 [[KR2a0020]] — Wèi Shōu's 魏收 controversial Northern-Qí history of the Tuòbá Bei-Wèi, written in 551–54.
- *Běi Qí shū* 北齊書 [[KR2a0021]] — begun by Lǐ Délín 李德林 in the Suí, finished by his son Lǐ Bǎiyào 李百藥 in 636.
- *Zhōu shū* 周書 [[KR2a0022]] — Lìnghú Défēn's 令狐德棻 history of the Bei-Zhōu (629–636).
- *Suí shū* 隋書 [[KR2a0023]] — annals/biographies under Wèi Zhēng 魏徵 (629–636) and the encyclopaedic *Wǔdài shǐzhì* 五代史志 (treatises) under Zhǎngsūn Wújì 長孫無忌 (656), the latter covering all of the Liáng, Chén, Bei-Qí, Bei-Zhōu and Suí jointly.
- *Nán shǐ* 南史 [[KR2a0024]] and *Běi shǐ* 北史 [[KR2a0025]] — Lǐ Yánshòu's 李延壽 single-handed synthesis (completed 659/676) of the eight separate Southern- and Northern-Dynasty histories into two continuous regional narratives.

**The Old / New histories pairs.** Twice in the canon a Sòng-era replacement was deemed insufficient to displace its predecessor, so both versions were retained.

- *Jiù Tángshū* 舊唐書 [[KR2a0026]] — completed under Liú Xù 劉昫 in 945 (Hòu Jìn) from late-Táng veritable records.
- *Xīn Tángshū* 新唐書 [[KR2a0027]] — recompiled 1044–60 under Ōuyáng Xiū 歐陽修 and Sòng Qí 宋祁 to remedy what the Sòng court regarded as the older work's prolixity and uneven literary register.
- *Xīn Tángshū jiūmiù* 新唐書糾謬 [[KR2a0028]] — Wú Zhěn's 吳縝 detailed enumeration of factual errors in the *Xīn Tángshū*, the single most cited Sòng critique of an imperially sponsored history.
- *Jiù Wǔdài shǐ* 舊五代史 [[KR2a0029]] — Xuē Jūzhèng's 薛居正 official history of the Five Dynasties (974); lost in the Yuán/Míng transmission and reconstructed under Shào Jìnhán 邵晉涵 from *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* 永樂大典 quotations during the *Sìkù* project.
- *Xīn Wǔdài shǐ* 新五代史 [[KR2a0030]] — Ōuyáng Xiū's privately drafted alternative, presented posthumously in 1077 and praised for its terse *Chūnqiū* 春秋-style historical judgements.
- *Wǔdài shǐ zuǎnwù* 五代史纂誤 [[KR2a0031]] — Wú Zhěn again, this time on the *Xīn Wǔdài shǐ*; the two *jiūmiù* / *zuǎnwù* works are the locus classicus of Sòng critical historiography.

**The Yuán Three Histories and after.** The compilation of the *Sòng*, *Liáo*, and *Jīn* histories under Toqto'a in the 1340s settled the question of legitimate succession by granting all three rival regimes a *zhèngshǐ* of their own — the so-called *gè yǔ zhèngtǒng* 各與正統 ("each is granted legitimacy") solution.

- *Sòng shǐ* 宋史 [[KR2a0032]] — at 496 *juǎn* the longest of the *zhèngshǐ*, compiled in barely two years (1343–45) and widely criticized for its uneven coverage and editorial rush.
- *Liáo shǐ* 遼史 [[KR2a0033]] and *Jīn shǐ* 金史 [[KR2a0035]] — companion pieces of the same Toqto'a project, the *Jīn shǐ* generally regarded as the most carefully edited of the three.
- *Liáo shǐ shíyí* 遼史拾遺 [[KR2a0034]] — Lì È's 厲鶚 mid-Qīng supplement gathering material that the rushed Yuán compilation had omitted.
- *Yuán shǐ* 元史 [[KR2a0036]] — produced under Sòng Lián 宋濂 in just 188 days (1369–70) at the start of the Míng, the most hastily compiled of all the *zhèngshǐ* and the one most extensively rewritten in later private histories.
- *Qīndìng Liáo-Jīn-Yuán sān shǐ Guóyǔ jiě* 欽定遼金元三史國語解 [[KR2a0037]] — Qiánlóng's 乾隆 1781–82 imperially commissioned glossary that re-transcribed the Khitan, Jurchen, and Mongol names of the three conquest-dynasty histories into a "corrected" Manchu-based standard, a project of great political symbolism but considerable scholarly damage.
- *Míng shǐ* 明史 [[KR2a0038]] — begun in 1679, presented in 1739 under Zhāng Tíngyù 張廷玉; the longest gestation of any *zhèngshǐ* and, by general modern consensus, the most carefully edited.
- *Qīng shǐ gǎo* 清史稿 [[KR2a0039]] — Zhào Ěrxùn's 趙爾巽 Republican-era draft (1914–27), printed in haste in 1928 and deliberately styled "draft" (*gǎo* 稿) because it was never imperially ratified; included here in the SBCK edition rather than the WYG, since it postdates the *Sìkù* by more than a century.

**Three Kingdoms supplementary scholarship.**

- *Sānguó shǐ biànwù* 三國史 辨誤 [[KR2a0013]] — anonymous corrections to the *Sānguó zhì*, of uncertain Sòng–Míng date.
- *Sānguó zhì bǔzhù* 三國志補注 [[KR2a0014]] — Háng Shìjùn's 杭世駿 mid-Qīng supplement to Péi Sōngzhī's annotation, gathering further citations from Táng and Sòng encyclopaedias.

### Important persons

- [[司馬遷]] — author of the *Shǐjì*, originator of the *jìzhuàn* form on which every subsequent *zhèngshǐ* is patterned.
- [[班固]] — author of the *Hànshū*, who fixed the single-dynasty scope as the standard frame of dynastic history.
- [[陳壽]] — compiler of the *Sānguó zhì*, the first *zhèngshǐ* to treat a period of disunion as a single subject.
- [[范曄]] — compiler of the *Hòu Hànshū*, whose programme of *lùn* 論 and *zàn* 贊 set the template for historical comment within the genre.
- [[裴松之]] — Liú-Sòng commentator on the *Sānguó zhì* whose annotation, by preserving lost works in long quotations, came to be regarded as historiographical scholarship of a fundamentally new kind.
- [[裴駰]] — Péi Sōngzhī's son and the *Jíjiě* commentator on the *Shǐjì*, transmitter of Hàn–Six-Dynasties *Shǐjì* learning.
- [[司馬貞]] — Táng *Suǒyǐn* commentator on the *Shǐjì* and author of the *Bǔ Sānhuáng běnjì*.
- [[張守節]] — Táng *Zhèngyì* commentator on the *Shǐjì*.
- [[顏師古]] — Táng commentator on the *Hànshū*, whose glosses became the standard text-and-commentary edition of that work.
- [[李賢]] — Táng prince and commentator on the *Hòu Hànshū*.
- [[沈約]] — Liáng compiler of the *Sòngshū*.
- [[蕭子顯]] — Liáng compiler of the *Nán Qí shū*.
- [[魏收]] — Bei-Qí compiler of the *Wèishū*, whose authorial bias gave the work its unhappy nickname *huì shǐ* 穢史 ("the foul history").
- [[房玄齡]] — Táng chief minister who supervised the *Jìnshū* project for Tàizōng.
- [[姚思廉]] — completer of the *Liáng shū* and *Chénshū* on the basis of his father Yáo Chá's drafts.
- [[李百藥]] — completer of the *Běi Qí shū*.
- [[令狐德棻]] — compiler of the *Zhōu shū* and one of the architects of the Táng programme of state-sponsored historiography.
- [[魏徵]] — chief editor of the *Suí shū* annals and biographies and supervising editor of the Liáng / Chén / Bei-Qí cluster.
- [[李延壽]] — Táng historian who alone wrote the *Nán shǐ* and *Běi shǐ*.
- [[劉昫]] — Hòu Jìn editor of record for the *Jiù Tángshū*.
- [[歐陽修]] — co-editor of the *Xīn Tángshū* and sole compiler of the *Xīn Wǔdài shǐ*, the central figure of Northern-Sòng historiographical reform.
- [[宋祁]] — Ōuyáng Xiū's co-editor on the *Xīn Tángshū*, responsible chiefly for the biographies.
- [[薛居正]] — Sòng minister who oversaw the *Jiù Wǔdài shǐ*.
- [[邵晉涵]] — *Sìkù* editor whose reconstruction restored the *Jiù Wǔdài shǐ* to the canon from *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* fragments.
- [[托克托]] (Toqto'a) — Yuán grand councillor who chaired the joint compilation of the *Sòng shǐ*, *Liáo shǐ*, and *Jīn shǐ*.
- [[宋濂]] — chief compiler of the *Yuán shǐ* in the opening months of the Míng.
- [[張廷玉]] — presenter of the *Míng shǐ* to the Qiánlóng court in 1739.
- [[高宗弘曆]] (the Qiánlóng emperor) — sponsor of the *Liáo-Jīn-Yuán Guóyǔ jiě* and ultimate patron of the *Sìkù* programme that fixed the canon of twenty-four.
- [[趙爾巽]] — chief editor of the *Qīng shǐ gǎo*.
- [[吳縝]] — Sòng critical historian whose *Xīn Tángshū jiūmiù* and *Wǔdài shǐ zuǎnwù* are the earliest sustained works of *kǎoyì* applied to *zhèngshǐ*.

### Topics

- **The *jìzhuàn* 紀傳 form and its alternatives.** Why the annals-and-biographies model, rather than the chronicle (*biānnián* 編年, KR2b) form of the *Zuǒzhuàn* and *Zīzhì tōngjiàn*, became the dynastic norm; what the inclusion or omission of *biǎo* 表 and *zhì* 志 entails for periods (notably Eastern Hàn, Three Kingdoms, Liáng, Chén, Northern Qí, Northern Zhōu) where the original compiler omitted one or both.
- **State sponsorship and *zhèngtǒng* 正統.** The link between imperial commissioning of a "previous-dynasty" history and the new dynasty's claim to legitimacy; the deliberate doubling of *Tángshū* and *Wǔdài shǐ*; the political force of the *gè yǔ zhèngtǒng* settlement applied to the Sòng / Liáo / Jīn triad and contested by Zhū Xī 朱熹 in his *Tōngjiàn gāngmù* 通鑑綱目 (Wilkinson §A.2.6).
- **The annotative supplement as historical scholarship.** The Péi-Sōngzhī model — substantive commentary that doubles the size of the host text by quoting lost sources verbatim — and its later imitators (Lǐ Xián, Háng Shìjùn).
- **The *kǎoyì* 考異 tradition on the *zhèngshǐ*.** From Wú Zhěn's Sòng *jiūmiù* / *zuǎnwù*, through the *Sìkù* *kǎozhèng* 考證 layer attached to every WYG history (visible in this division as the dozens of *kǎozhèng* persons in each text's frontmatter), to the great Qīng critical apparatus of Qián Dàxīn 錢大昕 and others.
- **The relation between the *Shǐjì* and the *Hànshū*.** Where Bān Gù copies, adapts, or silently revises Sīmǎ Qiān, and the implications for early-imperial source criticism — the explicit subject of the *Bān-Mǎ yìtóng*.
- **Reconstruction and recovery.** The *Jiù Wǔdài shǐ* as the paradigmatic case of a *zhèngshǐ* re-assembled from encyclopaedia quotations after centuries of loss; *bǔbiǎo* 補表 supplements (Xióng Fāng, Lì È) as a recurring scholarly response to the structural lacunae of the post-*Hàn* histories.
- **Translation, transcription, and the conquest dynasties.** The Qiánlóng *Guóyǔ jiě*'s retrospective standardization of Khitan, Jurchen, and Mongol names in the *Liáo*, *Jīn*, and *Yuán* histories — a politically motivated philological intervention that long obscured the underlying transcriptions and is now treated by modern scholarship as a textual problem in its own right.
- **The unresolved status of the *Qīng shǐ gǎo*.** A draft never imperially ratified, partially banned by the Republican Government in 1929, and not formally counted as the twenty-fifth *zhèngshǐ* — included here as the natural endpoint of the genre but explicitly marked as provisional.
