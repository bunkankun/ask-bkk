# Zhèngshū lèi 政書類 — Works relating to government administration

## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR2m` 政書類 is the *zhèngshū* (works relating to government administration) subdivision of the History branch (`KR2` 史部) of the *Sìkù quánshū* 四庫全書. As Wilkinson observes in his Chapter 51, the *Sìkù* category — credited by the editors to Qián Pǔ 錢溥 (*jìnshì* 1439) in his *Mìgé shūmù* 秘閣書目, and previously called *jiùshì* 舊事 in the *Qīlù* 七錄 and *Suízhì* 隋志, or *gùshì* 故事 in TángSòng catalogs — gathers the topical-encyclopedic, administrative, and regulatory texts that lay outside the annals-biography (*jìzhuàn* 紀傳) and chronicle (*biānnián* 編年) genres but were nevertheless central to the practical operation of imperial government. The 57 works collected here — the same 57 listed by Wilkinson in his Table 73 — comprise the comprehensive institutional histories (the *Sāntōng* 三通 / *Shítōng* 十通), the dynasty-by-dynasty *huìyào* 會要 and *huìdiǎn* 會典 compendia, the ritual codes (*wǔlǐ* / *lǐqì túshì*), the famine-relief literature (*huāngzhèng* 荒政), the military-administration treatises, the major imperial law codes, and a small but historically pivotal group of works on construction, printing, and dynastic ceremonial.

The scholarly tradition is unusually long-running and self-conscious. Dù Yòu's 杜佑 *Tōngdiǎn* (801) established a nine-fold topical taxonomy — *shíhuò* 食貨, *xuǎnjǔ* 選舉, *zhíguān* 職官, *lǐ* 禮, *yuè* 樂, *bīng* 兵, *xíng* 刑, *zhōujùn* 州郡, *biānfáng* 邊防 — that every later *zhèngshū* author had to engage with, whether by extension (Mǎ Duānlín's 馬端臨 *Wénxiàn tōngkǎo*, which added five new *kǎo*), continuation (the eighteenth-century Qiánlóng commissions), or polemical critique (Zhèng Qiáo 鄭樵 in the *Tōngzhì*, whose own twenty *lüè* were classified by the *Sìkù* editors not here but as a *biéshǐ* 別史). The chronological span runs from a Late-Hàn institutional handbook (the *Hàn guān jiùyí* 漢官舊儀) at one end to Qiánlóng-era court compilations (the *Bāxún wànshòu shèngdiǎn* of 1792) at the other; the densest concentration is in the Sòng (*huìyào*, ritual codes, *Tōngkǎo*) and the Qīng (the imperially-commissioned continuations and the *Dà Qīng huìdiǎn* / *Dà Qīng lǜlì* / *Bāqí tōngzhì* state-machinery compendia).

## Important texts and text clusters

**The *Sāntōng* 三通 and their Qīng continuations (the *Jiǔtōng* 九通 backbone).** The three canonical encyclopedic institutional histories and the imperially-commissioned eighteenth-century extensions that, taken together, were eventually packaged as the *Shítōng* 十通.

- *Tōngdiǎn* 通典 [[KR2m0001]] — Dù Yòu's 801 foundation of the genre, in 200 *juǎn*; the canonical framework on which the entire *Shítōng* series rests.
- *Wénxiàn tōngkǎo* 文獻通考 [[KR2m0009]] — Mǎ Duānlín's 1307/1319 extension of the *Tōngdiǎn* through 1224, in 348 *juǎn* and 24 *kǎo*; the longest and most-used of the three.
- *Qīndìng xù wénxiàn tōngkǎo* 欽定續文獻通考 [[KR2m0014]] — Qiánlóng-era continuation covering 1224–1644.
- *Huángcháo wénxiàn tōngkǎo* 皇朝文獻通考 [[KR2m0015]] — Qīng-only extension covering 1644 to the 1730s.
- *Qīndìng xù tōngdiǎn* 欽定續通典 [[KR2m0016]] — Qiánlóng continuation of the *Tōngdiǎn* through 1644.
- *Qīndìng huángcháo tōngdiǎn* 欽定皇朝通典 [[KR2m0017]] — Qīng-only *Tōngdiǎn* covering 1644–1736.
- *Qīndìng huángcháo tōngzhì* 欽定皇朝通志 [[KR2m0018]] — Qīng-only continuation of the *Tōngzhì*. (The *Tōngzhì* itself is classified by the *Sìkù* as a *biéshǐ*, not in this subdivision.)

**The *Huìyào* 會要 cluster (single-dynasty institutional digests).** Compilations that, unlike the comprehensive *Shítōng*, cover the institutions of one dynasty in topical order.

- *Táng huìyào* 唐會要 [[KR2m0002]] — Wáng Pǔ's 961 compilation in 100 *juǎn*; the indispensable institutional reference for Táng administrative history.
- *Wǔdài huìyào* 五代會要 [[KR2m0003]] — Wáng Pǔ's parallel digest of the Five-Dynasties period in 30 *juǎn*.
- *Xī Hàn huìyào* 西漢會要 [[KR2m0006]] and *Dōng Hàn huìyào* 東漢會要 [[KR2m0007]] — Xú Tiānlín's early-thirteenth-century pair of reverse-engineered *huìyào* for the two Hàn (the *Huìyào* genre having not existed under the Hàn itself).

**The *Huìdiǎn* 會典 cluster (MíngQīng administrative law codes).** Successor format to the *huìyào*, organized by agency rather than by topic.

- *Míng huìdiǎn* 明會典 [[KR2m0010]] — Xú Pǔ and Lǐ Dōngyáng's *Hóngzhì* 10 (1497) imperial compilation in 180 *juǎn*; the standard Míng administrative-law reference.
- *Qīndìng Dà Qīng huìdiǎn* 欽定大清會典 [[KR2m0012]] and *Qīndìng Dà Qīng huìdiǎn zélì* 欽定大清會典則例 [[KR2m0013]] — the two-volume Qīng equivalent: a regulatory code paired with case-precedent supplements.

**Imperial law codes proper.** Two of the *Sìkù*'s most consequential single titles sit here:

- *Táng lǜ shūyì* 唐律疏義 [[KR2m0054]] — the *Yǒnghuī* code of 653 with the official *shūyì* sub-commentary; the model for every later East-Asian penal code through the *Dà Qīng lǜlì*. Wilkinson calls it "the first code to survive in its entirety."
- *Dà Qīng lǜlì* 大清律例 [[KR2m0055]] — the 1740/1768 Qīng code in 47 *juǎn*, the terminal imperial code of the tradition.

**Ritual codes and ceremonial.** A dense cluster, since *zhèngshū* in *Sìkù* practice subsumes both administrative and ritual regulation.

- *Dà Táng kāiyuán lǐ* 大唐開元禮 [[KR2m0021]] — Xiāo Sōng's 732 *wǔlǐ* ritual code for the Táng court.
- *Zhènghé wǔlǐ xīnyí* 政和五禮新儀 [[KR2m0023]] — Zhèng Jūzhōng's Sòng counterpart (Zhènghé era).
- *Míng jí lǐ* 明集禮 [[KR2m0028]] — Xú Yīkuí's 1369 Míng ritual code, in 53 *juǎn*.
- *Qīndìng Dà Qīng tōnglǐ* 欽定大清通禮 [[KR2m0035]] — the Qiánlóng-era Qīng comprehensive ritual code.
- *Huángcháo lǐqì túshì* 皇朝禮器圖式 [[KR2m0036]] — the illustrated catalog of state ritual objects (1759).
- *Shàoxī zhōuxiàn shìdiàn yítú* 紹熙州縣釋奠儀圖 [[KR2m0024]] — Zhū Xī's diagram of the Confucian sacrificial ceremony for prefectural and county schools.
- *Qīndìng Mǎnzhōu jìshén jìtiān diǎnlǐ* 欽定滿洲祭神祭天典禮 [[KR2m0038]] — the Manchu-tradition ritual code, an unusual non-Hàn entry in the *Sìkù*.

**Famine relief (*huāngzhèng* 荒政).** A small but distinct sub-genre that emerged in the Southern Sòng and was canonized by Qīng officials.

- *Jiùhuāng huómín shū* 救荒活民書 [[KR2m0044]] — Dǒng Wěi's 1184/1196 foundational famine-relief treatise.
- *Bǔ huáng kǎo* 捕蝗考 [[KR2m0047]] — Chén Fāngshēng's late-Míng/early-Qīng locust-extermination manual.
- *Huāngzhèng cóngshū* 荒政叢書 [[KR2m0048]] — Yú Sēn's 1690 compilation of canonical *huāngzhèng* texts.
- *Qīndìng Kāngjì lù* 欽定康濟錄 [[KR2m0049]] — the imperially-edited Qiánlóng-era famine-relief manual.

**Military administration (*bīngzhì* 兵制 / *mǎzhèng* 馬政).** Historical-systematic treatments of military institutions.

- *Lìdài bīngzhì* 歷代兵制 [[KR2m0050]] — Chén Fùliáng's Sòng survey from Zhōu *xiāngsuì* through Sòng standing-army problems.
- *Bǔ Hàn bīngzhì* 補漢兵志 [[KR2m0051]] — Qián Wénzǐ's Sòng supplement to the missing *Hànshū* military treatise.
- *Mǎ zhèng jì* 馬政紀 [[KR2m0052]] — Yáng Shíqiáo's Míng treatise on horse-administration.
- *Qīndìng Bāqí tōngzhì* 欽定八旗通志 [[KR2m0053]] — Qiánlóng's monumental institutional history of the Manchu Eight Banners in 342 *juǎn*.

**Dynastic-event commemoration.** Imperially commissioned records of Qīng court occasions.

- *Xìnglǔ shèngdiǎn* 幸魯盛典 [[KR2m0033]] — Kāngxī's 1684 imperial visit to the Qūfù Kǒng temple.
- *Wànshòu shèngdiǎn chūjí* 萬壽盛典初集 [[KR2m0034]] — Kāngxī's sixtieth-birthday celebrations (1713).
- *Qīndìng nánxún shèngdiǎn* 欽定南巡盛典 [[KR2m0039]] — Qiánlóng's southern tours.
- *Bāxún wànshòu shèngdiǎn* 八旬萬壽盛典 [[KR2m0040]] — Qiánlóng's eightieth-birthday celebrations (1790).

**Architecture, technology, and the *Kǎogōng* tradition.** Small but historically pivotal.

- *Yíngzào fǎshì* 營造法式 [[KR2m0056]] — Lǐ Jiè's 1103 Sòng construction-standards code, the single most important pre-modern Chinese architectural treatise.
- *Qīndìng Wǔyīng Diàn jùzhēnbǎn chéngshì* 欽定武英殿聚珍版程式 [[KR2m0057]] — Jīn Jiǎn's 1776 manual of the *Wǔyīng Diàn* movable wood-type printing program.
- *Áobō tú* 熬波圖 [[KR2m0045]] — Chén Chūn's illustrated Yuán-era technical treatise on salt evaporation.

**Posthumous-titles literature (*shìfǎ* 謚法).** A distinct minor genre.

- *Shìfǎ* 謚法 [[KR2m0022]] — Sū Xún's Sòng treatise on the rules of posthumous naming.
- *Míng chén shì kǎo* 明臣謚考 [[KR2m0030]] and *Míng shì jì huìbiān* 明謚紀彙編 [[KR2m0031]] — Bào Yìngáo's and Guō Liánghàn's late-Míng catalogs of Míng official posthumous titles.

**Antiquarian and case-specific institutional studies.** Single-issue scholarly monographs.

- *Hàn guān jiùyí* 漢官舊儀 [[KR2m0020]] — Wèi Hóng's late-first-century outline of Hàn court offices; the earliest text in the division.
- *Hàn zhì kǎo* 漢制考 [[KR2m0008]] — Wáng Yīnglín's Sòng study of Hàn institutions.
- *Qīguó kǎo* 七國考 [[KR2m0011]] — Dǒng Shuō's MíngQīng study of Warring-States institutions.
- *Yuáncháo diǎngù biānnián kǎo* 元朝典故編年考 [[KR2m0019]] — Sūn Chéngzé's chronologically arranged study of Yuán institutions.
- *Lìdài jiànyuán kǎo* 歷代建元考 [[KR2m0041]] — Zhōng Yuānyìng's reign-title compendium.
- *Běijiāo pèiwèi zūn xī xiàng yì* 北郊配位尊西向議 [[KR2m0042]] — Máo Qílíng's polemical brief on suburban-sacrifice ritual orientation.
- *Miào zhì túkǎo* 廟制圖考 [[KR2m0043]] — Wàn Sītóng's illustrated study of ancestral-temple regulation.

## Important persons

- [[杜佑]] — Mid-Táng grand councillor; through the *Tōngdiǎn* he created the *zhèngshū* genre and its enduring topical taxonomy.
- [[馬端臨]] — Yuán scholar; in the *Wénxiàn tōngkǎo* he produced the largest and most-cited single work in the division.
- [[王溥]] — Early-Sòng prime minister; compiled the *Táng huìyào* and *Wǔdài huìyào*, establishing the *huìyào* format as a viable single-dynasty companion to comprehensive *Shítōng* coverage.
- [[徐天麟]] — Southern-Sòng scholar; reverse-engineered the *Xī Hàn huìyào* and *Dōng Hàn huìyào* to fill the pre-Tang gap in the *huìyào* tradition.
- [[王應麟]] — Late-Sòng polymath; *Hàn zhì kǎo* applied his philological skills to recovering Hàn-period institutions.
- [[長孫無忌]] — Early-Táng chief minister; led the imperial commission that produced the *Yǒnghuī* code with its *shūyì* sub-commentary — the foundational document of East-Asian law.
- [[蕭嵩]] — Mid-Táng grand secretary; supervised compilation of the *Dà Táng kāiyuán lǐ*, the standard Táng ritual code.
- [[朱熹]] — Sòng *dàoxué* master; in this division represented by his diagram of the prefectural-school sacrificial ceremony, a key text in the institutionalization of the *Wénmiào* cult.
- [[陳傅良]] — Yǒngjiā utilitarian-school philosopher; the *Lìdài bīngzhì* is a canonical Southern-Sòng analysis of military institutional decline.
- [[董煟]] — Southern-Sòng magistrate; the *Jiùhuāng huómín shū* founded the *huāngzhèng* genre.
- [[李誡]] — Sòng Imperial Workshops official; the *Yíngzào fǎshì* is the definitive pre-modern Chinese architectural treatise.
- [[蘇洵]] — Northern-Sòng essayist (one of the *Sān Sū*); his *Shìfǎ* is the standard Sòng treatment of posthumous naming.
- [[徐溥]] / [[李東陽]] — Mid-Míng grand secretaries; produced the *Míng huìdiǎn* under imperial commission.
- [[高宗弘曆]] (the Qiánlóng emperor) — Sponsor and nominal author of the largest cluster of texts in the division: the *Sāntōng* continuations, the *Bāqí tōngzhì*, *Dà Qīng tōnglǐ*, *Lǐqì túshì*, *Guócháo gōng shǐ*, and the *shèngdiǎn* records of his court occasions.
- [[嵇璜]], [[阿桂]], [[允祹]], [[允祿]] — Qīng grand-secretary-level supervisors of the great Qiánlóng-era compilations.
- [[孫承澤]] — Late-Míng / early-Qīng official-turned-scholar; the *Yuáncháo diǎngù biānnián kǎo* exemplifies the early-Qīng antiquarian recovery of pre-Míng administrative history.
- [[毛奇齡]] / [[萬斯同]] — Early-Qīng evidential-scholarship pioneers; both contributed short polemical monographs on disputed points of state ritual.
- [[衞宏]] — Eastern-Hàn court scholar; the *Hàn guān jiùyí* preserves a near-contemporary outline of Hàn central-government offices.

## Topics

The division's principal interpretive problems run along several axes.

**The structure of comprehensive coverage.** The fundamental methodological problem of *zhèngshū* is how to organize the institutions of imperial government across the entire historical span. Dù Yòu's nine-topic *Tōngdiǎn* taxonomy (food/finance, selection, office, ritual, music, military, punishments, local administration, border defense) became the default; Mǎ Duānlín added five new *kǎo*; the *huìyào* and *huìdiǎn* formats abandoned cross-dynastic synthesis for one-dynasty agency-by-agency comprehensiveness. The choice of structure was itself an argument about which aspects of governance were primary and how institutions developed historically.

**Ritual versus administration.** The *Sìkù* placement of imperial ritual codes (*Kāiyuán lǐ*, *Zhènghé wǔlǐ xīnyí*, *Míng jí lǐ*, *Dà Qīng tōnglǐ*) alongside law codes and fiscal compendia is itself a substantive claim — that imperial ritual is a branch of governance, not a separate "religious" domain. The same logic puts *Mǎnzhōu jìshén jìtiān diǎnlǐ* (Manchu shamanic ritual) and *Shàoxī zhōuxiàn shìdiàn yítú* (Confucian school sacrifice) in the same subdivision.

**The standing-army problem and military-institutional decline.** From Chén Fùliáng's *Lìdài bīngzhì* through the Qīng *Bāqí tōngzhì*, *zhèngshū* authors return repeatedly to the question of how military institutions degrade — the swollen post-*Huángyòu* Sòng armies, the Míng *wèisuǒ* system's collapse, the eighteenth-century Banner's loss of effectiveness — and what historical models of compact, militia-rooted armies might be recovered.

**Famine relief as administrative philosophy.** The *huāngzhèng* cluster, beginning with Dǒng Wěi and canonized in Yú Sēn's *Huāngzhèng cóngshū* and the Qīng *Kāngjì lù*, treats famine response not as ad-hoc emergency action but as a discipline with its own historical canon, institutional vocabulary (granaries, tax-remission, work-relief, locust extermination), and ethical foundation in classical "nourishing the people" doctrine.

**Legal codification and East-Asian transmission.** The *Táng lǜ shūyì* — *Sìkù*-classified here under *zhèngshū* rather than separately — is the source from which the *Sòng xíngtǒng*, the Korean and Japanese *ritsuryō*, the Vietnamese Lê code, and ultimately the *Dà Qīng lǜlì* all descend. Within this division the law-code cluster is small (two titles) but disproportionately consequential for comparative East-Asian legal history.

**Technical knowledge as state knowledge.** The presence of *Yíngzào fǎshì*, *Áobō tú*, and *Wǔyīng Diàn chéngshì* in the History branch (not in *zǐbù* / Masters) reflects a *Sìkù* judgment that engineering and craft regulation, when promulgated by the throne, become part of the institutional record proper rather than artisanal *zǐ* literature.

**Antiquarian recovery of lost institutions.** Wáng Yīnglín's *Hàn zhì kǎo*, Xú Tiānlín's *LiǎngHàn huìyào* pair, Sūn Chéngzé's Yuán-institutions study, and Wàn Sītóng's temple-regulation diagram all belong to a long-running project of using textual evidence to reconstruct institutional configurations no longer documented in extant codes.
