# Guócháo Gōng Shǐ 國朝宮史
*Records of the Reigning Dynasty's Palace*
by [[高宗弘曆]] (敕撰)

## About the work
The Qīng dynasty's imperial palace-records reference, the disciplined Qīng counterpart to the *Míng gōng shǐ* ([[KR2m0032]]). Originally commissioned in *Qiánlóng* 7 (1742); revised and enlarged in *Qiánlóng* 24 (1759), with completion two years later (1761). 36 *juǎn* in six gates: *xùnyù* (instructional decrees of successive reigns); *diǎnlǐ* (inner-court protocols, dress, and parades, with explicit cross-reference to the *Huìdiǎn* for outer-court matters); *gōngdiàn* (palaces, with positions, scale, imperial inscriptions, and tablets); *jīngfèi* (expenditures, banquets, dress, food, and implement counts); *guānzhì* (inner-court office system, including eunuch ranks and duties, with merits, faults, rewards, and punishments); *shūjí* (books held in the inner court, with abstracts, excluding Buddhist scriptures kept in the *Jìngyù* 淨域).

### Tiyao
By imperial command of *Qiánlóng* 7 (1742). In *Qiánlóng* 24 (1759), since the original was thin, ordered to be expanded; completed two years later. Six gates:

1) *Xùnyù* (instructional decrees): the sage-instructions of successive reigns and the August Emperor's decrees, recorded reverently to display the household-discipline.

2) *Diǎnlǐ* (rituals): inner-court ceremonial regulations, dress, and parade-equipage. Outer-court rituals already detailed in the *Huìdiǎn* are abbreviated.

3) *Gōngdiàn* (palaces): arranged by position, with scale carefully detailed; imperial-brushed inscriptions, couplets, and various poetry are all reverently recorded.

4) *Jīngfèi* (expenditures): tribute, banquet, dress, food, and implement counts—detailed without omission.

5) *Guānzhì* (offices): inner-court staff numbers, ranks, and duties; merits, faults, rewards, and punishments graded.

6) *Shūjí* (books): the volumes held in the inner-court library, listed by classification with abstracts. Limited to works of substance for cosmic and political ordering; Buddhist scriptures kept in the *Jìngyù* (a separate sacred building) are excluded.

Reverently reading the imperial decrees on the editing of this work, one sees the emperor's careful insistence on the establishing of foundations and the issuing of clear instructions, as the basis from which ten-thousand-times-ten-thousand generations should follow. The Way of self-cultivation, family-management, state-governance, and world-pacification is fully present here.

### Abstract
The *Guócháo gōng shǐ* deliberately positions itself as a counterpart to the *Míng gōng shǐ* ([[KR2m0032]]) but as a model of discipline rather than warning. Where the *Míng gōng shǐ* is a memoir of late-Míng inner-court excess, the *Guócháo gōng shǐ* is a regulated catalog: every protocol, expenditure, building, office, and book is recorded with precision and rectitude. The contrast is intended; the *Sìkù* tíyào's framing of both works makes the rhetorical point.

The dating bracket reflects the two compilation phases: 1742 first commissioning; 1761 expanded completion. The work was reissued throughout the Qīng (a *Guócháo gōng shǐ xùbiān* 國朝宮史續編 of 1813 carries the record forward through Jiāqìng), making it the principal documentary source for Qīng inner-court life across the entire dynasty.

The work is widely used in modern Qīng-court studies. Wilkinson (*Chinese History: A New Manual*, §66.4.6) cites it among the principal Qīng administrative-reference works.

## Other points of interest
The exclusion of Buddhist scriptures from the *shūjí* gate—they "are kept in the *Jìngyù*"—reflects the Qīng inner-court's spatial division between Hàn classical/historical works and the imperial-Buddhist library at the *Jìngyǔn* (purified vessels) chamber within the Forbidden City, an architectural distinction that maps onto the bibliographic.
