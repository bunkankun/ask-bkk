## Scope and scholarly tradition

The *jìshì běnmò* 紀事本末 ("from beginning to end of an event", topically arranged) class collects works in the third of the three great forms of Chinese historiography — the form invented in 1173–74 when the Southern Sòng scholar Yuán Shū 袁樞 took Sīmǎ Guāng's annalistic *Zīzhì tōngjiàn* 資治通鑑 ([[KR2k0001]]), gathered the dispersed entries of each event into 239 self-contained, individually titled narratives, and so produced the genre-founding *Tōngjiàn jìshì běnmò* ([[KR2c0001]]). Wilkinson, *Chinese History*, ch. 50, sets the genre alongside the older *biānnián* 編年 (annals, KR2b) and *jìzhuàn* 紀傳 (annals-biographies, the *Histories* in [[KR2a]]) and notes that, with one exception, *jìshì běnmò* writers tended to rearrange existing material rather than open new sources — the function of the form is navigational, making the dynastic record tractable to readers.

The Sìkù compilers placed under this rubric three distinct kinds of work: (i) topical rearrangements of pre-existing year-by-year or biographical records (Yuán Shū's archetype, the Sòng/Yuán/Míng *jìshì běnmò* of [[KR2c0007]], [[KR2c0008]], [[KR2c0020]], and the *Zuǒzhuàn* treatments of [[KR2c0003]] and [[KR2c0022]]); (ii) topically organised regional and frontier histories ([[KR2c0005]], [[KR2c0006]], [[KR2c0019]]); and (iii) the *fānglüè* 方略 / *jìlüè* 紀略 — the imperially-commissioned Qīng campaign histories that Wilkinson treats separately at §66.6.1 as a special form of episodic history but which the *Sìkù* curators folded into the *jìshì běnmò* class. The *fānglüè* dominate the late-Qīng portion of the division: ten of the twenty-three works in KR2c are imperial campaign histories of operations between 1673 and 1788. The division thus runs in compass from 270 (the Jìn-period commentary on the *Yì Zhōushū* preserved in [[KR2c0002]]) to 1789, with its centre of gravity in two periods: the Southern Sòng founding moment of the genre (1173–1235) and the Qiánlóng-era *fānglüè* office (1750–1789).

## Important texts and text clusters

**The founding work and its Classical extensions.** *Tōngjiàn jìshì běnmò* 通鑑紀事本末 [[KR2c0001]] — Yuán Shū's 239-topic rearrangement of Sīmǎ Guāng, the matrix from which every other work in the division descends. *Chūnqiū Zuǒzhuàn shìlèi shǐmò* 春秋左傳事類始末 [[KR2c0003]] (Zhāng Chōng 章沖, 1185) — the earliest application of the method to a Classic, written nine years after Yuán Shū's enterprise saw print. *Zuǒzhuàn jìshì běnmò* 左傳紀事本末 [[KR2c0022]] (Gāo Shìqí 高士奇, 1690) — the much larger 53-*juǎn* Kāngxī replacement, organised by state rather than by year, that supersedes Zhāng Chōng's slighter book. *Yì shǐ* 繹史 [[KR2c0021]] (Mǎ Sù 馬驌, c. 1670–75) — the great 160-*juǎn* topical antiquity-history of the Three Augusts through the fall of Qín, distinctive within the genre for preserving its source-texts whole and so anticipating evidential-research method.

**The Sòng–Yuán–Míng "trilogy".** *Sòngshǐ jìshì běnmò* 宋史紀事本末 [[KR2c0007]] (Féng Qí 馮琦 / Chén Bāngzhān 陳邦瞻, 1605) — the standard topical key to the unwieldy *Sòngshǐ*. *Yuánshǐ jìshì běnmò* 元史紀事本末 [[KR2c0008]] (Chén Bāngzhān, 1606) — the immediate sequel by the same hand. *Míngshǐ jìshì běnmò* 明史紀事本末 [[KR2c0020]] (Gǔ Yìngtài 谷應泰, 1655–58) — the principal topical history of the Míng, unique within the genre for being substantively independent of any prior dynastic history (it was written eighty years before the official *Míngshǐ* itself). Wilkinson singles Gǔ out as the one *jìshì běnmò* writer who added new evidence rather than rearranging an existing record.

**The Sòng documentary compilation.** *Sānzhāo běiméng huìbiān* 三朝北盟會編 [[KR2c0004]] (Xú Mèngxīn 徐夢莘, presented 1194) — the 250-*juǎn* documentary compendium on Sòng–Jīn relations 1117–1161, sitting in this class on the strength of its part-by-part topical structure (Zhèngxuān, Jìngkāng, Yánxīng) but operating as a documentary archive rather than a narrative history; Wilkinson (§62.2 #11) ranks it second only to Lǐ Xīnchuán's *Jiànyán yǐlái xìnián yàolù* ([[KR2k0008]]) among Southern-Sòng private histories.

**Regional and frontier histories.** *Shǔ jiàn* 蜀鑑 [[KR2c0005]] (Guō Yǔndǎo 郭允蹈, 1235–36) — the strategic-topographical military history of Sìchuān across twelve centuries. *Yánjiǎo jìwén* 炎徼紀聞 [[KR2c0006]] (Tián Rǔchéng 田汝成, 1558) — the southwest-frontier critique. *Diāncháo* 滇考 [[KR2c0019]] (Féng Sū 馮甦, 1665) — the Yúnnán topical history. *Suíkòu jìlüè* 綏寇紀略 [[KR2c0018]] (Wú Wěiyè 吳偉業, 1652 onward) — the late-Míng poet-historian's twelve-piece account of the peasant rebellions and the fall of the Míng.

**The Qīng imperial campaign histories (*fānglüè* / *jìlüè*).** *Píngdìng sānnì fānglüè* 平定三逆方略 [[KR2c0009]] (Lè Déhóng 勒德洪, 1682–86) — the precedent-setting first imperially-commissioned Qīng *fānglüè*, the model for everything after. *Qīnzhēng pīngdìng shuòmò fānglüè* [[KR2c0010]] (Wēn Dá 溫達, 1708–10) — the Kāngxī Galdan campaign, the first to carry an imperial preface. *Qīndìng pīngdìng Jīnchuān fānglüè* [[KR2c0011]] (Lái Bǎo 來保, 1750–52, on the First Jīnchuān war) and the comprehensive *Píngdìng liǎng Jīnchuān fānglüè* [[KR2c0013]] (Āguì 阿桂, 1781–89, on both Jīnchuān wars). *Píngdìng zhǔn'gā'ěr fānglüè* 平定準噶爾方略 [[KR2c0012]] (Fù Héng 傅恆, 1763–70) — 173 *juǎn* on the destruction of the Dzungar Khanate and the conquest of Xīnjiāng. The cluster of late-Qiánlóng *jìlüè* on smaller suppressions: the Wáng Lún sectarian rising in Shāndōng [[KR2c0014]], the Sū Sìshísān Hui Muslim rising at Lánzhōu [[KR2c0015]], the Tián Wǔ rising at Shífēngbǎo [[KR2c0016]], the Lín Shuǎngwén rising on Taiwan [[KR2c0017]]. *Píngtái jìlüè* 平臺紀略 [[KR2c0023]] (Lán Dǐngyuán 藍鼎元, 1722–32) — the eyewitness account of the suppression of the Zhū Yīguì revolt of 1721, the only non-imperially-commissioned campaign history in the Qīng cluster.

**Outlier.** *Jízhǒng Zhōushū* 汲冢周書 [[KR2c0002]] (Kǒng Cháo 孔晁, c. 270–290) — the *Yì Zhōushū* with Jìn-period commentary, traditionally a *bié shǐ* / *zá shǐ* but placed here in the *Sìbù cóngkān* curators' arrangement.

## Important persons

- [[袁樞]] — Southern Sòng scholar (1131–1205) whose 1173–74 rearrangement of the *Zīzhì tōngjiàn* founded the genre; everything else in the division is a derivative or a response.
- [[司馬光]] — author of the *Zīzhì tōngjiàn* whose annalistic record provided Yuán Shū with his raw material; not himself a *jìshì běnmò* writer but the indispensable upstream source.
- [[章沖]] — Sòng scholar who in 1185 made the first application of Yuán Shū's method to a Classic, the *Chūnqiū / Zuǒzhuàn*, in [[KR2c0003]].
- [[徐夢莘]] — Southern Sòng documentary compiler (1126–1207) whose *Sānzhāo běiméng huìbiān* [[KR2c0004]] is the principal Sòng-side archive of the catastrophic Sòng–Jīn decades.
- [[馮琦]] and [[陳邦瞻]] — late-Wànlì Hànlín designer and provincial *jìnshì* completer of the *Sòngshǐ jìshì běnmò* [[KR2c0007]]; Chén alone produced its sequel *Yuánshǐ jìshì běnmò* [[KR2c0008]] within the year.
- [[谷應泰]] — Shùnzhì-era Provincial Education Commissioner whose *Míngshǐ jìshì běnmò* [[KR2c0020]] (1655–58) is, per Wilkinson, the one work in the genre that contributes substantively new evidence, having been written before the official *Míngshǐ* itself.
- [[馬驌]] — early-Qīng polymath (1621–1673) whose 160-*juǎn* *Yì shǐ* [[KR2c0021]] is the most ambitious classical-antiquity history in the division, employing the source-preserving method that distinguishes it from rearrangements.
- [[高士奇]] — the most powerful Hàn close minister of the Kāngxī court, whose 1690 *Zuǒzhuàn jìshì běnmò* [[KR2c0022]] is the standard *jìshì běnmò* of the *Zuǒzhuàn*.
- [[吳偉業]] — late-Míng / early-Qīng poet (Méicūn 梅村), whose *Suíkòu jìlüè* [[KR2c0018]] gives the division its principal Míng-loyalist treatment of the rebellions that brought down the dynasty.
- [[勒德洪]] — Manchu Grand Secretary, chief compiler-of-record of the precedent-setting *Píngdìng sānnì fānglüè* [[KR2c0009]] of 1682–86 — the work that established the *fānglüè* form for the remainder of the dynasty.
- [[溫達]], [[來保]], [[傅恒]], [[阿桂]] — the senior Manchu officials who served as chief compilers-of-record on the great Kāngxī- and Qiánlóng-era *fānglüè*: respectively the Galdan campaign [[KR2c0010]], the First Jīnchuān [[KR2c0011]], the Dzungar conquest [[KR2c0012]], and the combined Jīnchuān + several late-Qiánlóng frontier *jìlüè* [[KR2c0013]] [[KR2c0015]]. Āguì in particular is the connecting figure across the late-Qiánlóng campaign-history office.
- [[于敏中]] and [[舒赫德]] — late-Qiánlóng Grand Secretaries who together with Āguì ran the campaign-history office through the 1770s and 1780s ([[KR2c0014]]).
- [[田汝成]], [[郭允蹈]], [[馮甦]], [[藍鼎元]] — the principal individual compilers, outside the imperial *fānglüè* office, of regional and frontier *jìshì běnmò* on YúnnánGuìzhōu, Sìchuān, Yúnnán, and Taiwan respectively.

## Topics

- **The invention of a third historiographical form.** The division is anchored on Yuán Shū's recognition that pure *biānnián* fragments an event across years and pure *jìzhuàn* fragments it across persons; the *jìshì běnmò* method gathers each event into one place while preserving chronology within. Every text in the division is, at base, an experiment in this method.
- **Rearrangement vs. original research.** The deep methodological question Wilkinson raises (§50): does *jìshì běnmò* generate new historical knowledge, or only re-package an existing record? The division supplies both kinds of answer: most works ([[KR2c0007]], [[KR2c0008]], [[KR2c0022]]) are rearrangements of a single prior text; a minority ([[KR2c0020]], [[KR2c0021]], [[KR2c0018]]) draw on an open source-base and are substantively new.
- **Topical arrangement of the Classics.** A distinct sub-tradition treats the *Zuǒzhuàn* as if it were a *biānnián* like the *Tōngjiàn* and applies Yuán Shū's method to it ([[KR2c0003]], [[KR2c0022]]). Mǎ Sù's *Yì shǐ* extends the same logic across the entire pre-imperial corpus.
- **Documentary compendium vs. narrative history.** The *Sānzhāo běiméng huìbiān* [[KR2c0004]] sits in the division on the strength of its three-part topical structure but is documentary, not narrative — Xú Mèngxīn juxtaposes contradictory sources without adjudicating. The contrast with Yuán Shū's purely narrative *jìshì běnmò* defines two poles of the genre.
- **The *fānglüè* institution.** The Qīng court's establishment of a standing *fānglüè* / *jìlüè* compilation office, beginning with the Three Feudatories campaign of 1673–81 and reaching maturity in the late-Qiánlóng campaign-history bureau, gives the division its single largest internal cluster (10 of 23 works). The *fānglüè* are documentary, day-by-day records of imperial military operations; they are tightly bound to the *Shíquán wǔgōng* 十全武功 ("Ten Complete Military Victories") cycle that the Qiánlóng emperor cultivated as legitimating ideology.
- **Frontier history and the southwest.** A surprisingly large share of the regional *jìshì běnmò* concerns the southern and southwestern marches: Tián Rǔchéng on the *yánjiǎo* southwest [[KR2c0006]], Guō Yǔndǎo on Sìchuān [[KR2c0005]], Féng Sū on Yúnnán [[KR2c0019]], Lán Dǐngyuán on Taiwan [[KR2c0023]], and the Qīng *fānglüè* on Jīnchuān [[KR2c0011]] [[KR2c0013]] and Taiwan [[KR2c0017]]. The cluster is a window on three centuries of central-court engagement with Tibetan-speaking *tǔsī*, Hui Muslim communities, southern non-Hàn peoples, and the Tài-speaking polities at the Burmese border.
- **Sectarian uprisings and the *jìlüè* genre's selectivities.** The Wáng Lún ([[KR2c0014]]), Sū Sìshísān ([[KR2c0015]]), and Tián Wǔ ([[KR2c0016]]) *jìlüè* are the principal documentary entry points into the late-Qiánlóng court's understanding of *Bāguà jiào* / White Lotus and Naqshbandiyya Jahriyya mass movements; Susan Naquin's analyses of how the *jìlüè* editorial line systematically distorts the underlying record (overstating Manchu valour, suppressing official negligence) have been taken as paradigmatic for the *fānglüè* genre.

## Timeline

- **270–290 — *Jízhǒng Zhōushū* 汲冢周書 [[KR2c0002]]** — Kǒng Cháo's Jìn-period commentary on the *Yì Zhōushū*; the chronologically earliest work in the division but a generic outlier.
- **1173–1175 — *Tōngjiàn jìshì běnmò* 通鑑紀事本末 [[KR2c0001]]** — Yuán Shū's founding work; printed at Yánzhōu in 1174, the textual matrix from which the genre takes its name.
- **1185 — *Chūnqiū Zuǒzhuàn shìlèi shǐmò* 春秋左傳事類始末 [[KR2c0003]]** — Zhāng Chōng's first application of the method to a Classic.
- **1194 — *Sānzhāo běiméng huìbiān* 三朝北盟會編 [[KR2c0004]]** — Xú Mèngxīn's documentary compendium of Sòng–Jīn relations, presented to the throne in Shàoxī 5.
- **1235–1236 — *Shǔ jiàn* 蜀鑑 [[KR2c0005]]** — Guō Yǔndǎo's strategic-military history of Sìchuān, with critical evaluation appended to each event.
- **1550–1558 — *Yánjiǎo jìwén* 炎徼紀聞 [[KR2c0006]]** — Tián Rǔchéng's fourteen essays on the southwestern frontier, dated by self-preface to Jiājìng 37 (1558).
- **1605 — *Sòngshǐ jìshì běnmò* 宋史紀事本末 [[KR2c0007]]** — Féng Qí / Chén Bāngzhān's continuation of Yuán Shū's enterprise into the Sòng dynasty proper.
- **1606 — *Yuánshǐ jìshì běnmò* 元史紀事本末 [[KR2c0008]]** — Chén Bāngzhān's immediate sequel covering the Yuán.
- **1652–1672 — *Suíkòu jìlüè* 綏寇紀略 [[KR2c0018]]** — Wú Wěiyè's twelve-piece *jìshì běnmò* on the late-Míng peasant rebellions, composed in retirement at Jiāxīng.
- **1655–1658 — *Míngshǐ jìshì běnmò* 明史紀事本末 [[KR2c0020]]** — Gǔ Yìngtài's principal topical history of the Míng, written eighty years before the official *Míngshǐ*.
- **1665 — *Diāncháo* 滇考 [[KR2c0019]]** — Féng Sū's topical history of Yúnnán in 37 pieces, composed during his judicial service at Yǒngchāng.
- **1670–1675 — *Yì shǐ* 繹史 [[KR2c0021]]** — Mǎ Sù's 160-*juǎn* topical antiquity-history of pre-imperial China, the most ambitious work in the division.
- **1682–1686 — *Píngdìng sānnì fānglüè* 平定三逆方略 [[KR2c0009]]** — Lè Déhóng's precedent-setting first imperial Qīng *fānglüè*, on the suppression of the Three Feudatories.
- **1690 — *Zuǒzhuàn jìshì běnmò* 左傳紀事本末 [[KR2c0022]]** — Gāo Shìqí's 53-*juǎn* state-by-state Kāngxī treatment of the *Zuǒzhuàn*, superseding Zhāng Chōng.
- **1708–1710 — *Qīnzhēng pīngdìng shuòmò fānglüè* 親征平定朔漠方略 [[KR2c0010]]** — Wēn Dá's official record of Kāngxī's three personal expeditions against Galdan, the first *fānglüè* with an imperial preface.
- **1722–1732 — *Píngtái jìlüè* 平臺紀略 [[KR2c0023]]** — Lán Dǐngyuán's eyewitness account of the Zhū Yīguì revolt of 1721; the *jìlüè* itself dated by self-preface to Yōngzhèng 10 (1732).
- **1750–1752 — *Qīndìng pīngdìng Jīnchuān fānglüè* 平定金川方略 [[KR2c0011]]** — Lái Bǎo's *fānglüè* of the First Jīnchuān war (1747–49); imperial preface dated Qiánlóng 17.
- **1763–1770 — *Píngdìng zhǔn'gā'ěr fānglüè* 平定準噶爾方略 [[KR2c0012]]** — Fù Héng's 173-*juǎn* record of the destruction of the Dzungar Khanate and the conquest of Xīnjiāng (operations 1755–59); imperial preface early spring 1770.
- **1775–1777 — *Qīndìng jiǎopǔ Línqīng nìfěi jìlüè* 剿捕臨清逆匪紀略 [[KR2c0014]]** — Shū Hèdé and Yú Mǐnzhōng's *jìlüè* of the Wáng Lún sectarian rising of 1774.
- **1781–1789 — *Qīndìng Lánzhōu jìlüè* 蘭州紀略 [[KR2c0015]]** — Āguì's *jìlüè* of the Sū Sìshísān Hui Muslim rising of 1781.
- **1781–1789 — *Píngdìng liǎng Jīnchuān fānglüè* 平定兩金川方略 [[KR2c0013]]** — Āguì's combined treatment of both Jīnchuān wars, *Sìkù* edition presented Qiánlóng 54.
- **1784–1789 — *Qīndìng Shífēngbǎo jìlüè* 石峰堡紀略 [[KR2c0016]]** — *jìlüè* of the second great Naqshbandiyya Jahriyya rising in Gānsù (Tián Wǔ / Mǎ Mínglái, 1784).
- **1788–1789 — *Qīndìng pīngdìng Táiwān jìlüè* 平定臺灣紀略 [[KR2c0017]]** — *jìlüè* of the Lín Shuǎngwén / Zhuāng Dàtián Tiāndìhuì rising of 1786–88, the seventh of Qiánlóng's *Shíquán wǔgōng*.
