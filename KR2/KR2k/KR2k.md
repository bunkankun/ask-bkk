# KR2k 地理類 (Dìlǐ lèi) — Histories: Geography

## Scope and scholarly tradition

The *Dìlǐ* 地理 ("Geography") category of the *Sìkù quánshū* is the largest single sub-category of the *Shǐbù* 史部 (152 titles in the Wényuāngé recension) and the principal home of the imperial Chinese geographical, hydrographical, urban-historical, ethnographic, and travel literatures. As Wilkinson notes (§16.3), the bibliographical placement of *dìlǐ* in the History branch is one of the durable structural features of the *sìbù* classification, retained even in modern PRC library systems (§75.2); it reflects the inherited assumption that Chinese geographical writing existed not as a freestanding natural science but as an instrument of statecraft — providing incoming officials, military planners, ritual specialists, and historiographers with the *yán'gé* 沿革 (administrative-historical genealogy of place), *jiāngyù* 疆域 (boundaries), *shānchuān* 山川 (topography), *fēngsú* 風俗 (customs), and *wùchǎn* 物產 (products) of every administered unit in the empire (Wilkinson §16.4.1–2).

The genre's textual ancestry runs from the *Yúgòng* 禹貢 of the *Shàngshū* and the *Shānhǎi jīng* 山海經 (Wilkinson §16.3.1), through the *dìlǐ zhì* 地理志 treatises of the *Hànshū* and subsequent standard histories (§16.3.2), to the freestanding empire-wide comprehensive gazetteers (*zǒngzhì* 總志 / *yītǒng zhì* 一統志) and the vast Sòng–Qīng *fāngzhì* 方志 (local-gazetteer) literature. Within the Sìkù scheme the 152 titles of KR2k follow the canonical ten-fold sub-arrangement that the editors imposed across the division: 宮殿簿 (palace registers, [[KR2k0001]]–[[KR2k0002]]); 總志 (empire-wide gazetteers, [[KR2k0003]]–[[KR2k0009]]); 都會郡縣 (cities, prefectures, counties — the bulk of *fāngzhì* proper, [[KR2k0010]]–[[KR2k0057]]); 河渠 (waterways, [[KR2k0058]]–[[KR2k0080]]); 邊防 (frontier defence, [[KR2k0081]]–[[KR2k0082]]); 山川 (mountains and waters, [[KR2k0083]]–[[KR2k0089]]); 古蹟 (ancient monuments and antiquarian topographies, [[KR2k0090]]–[[KR2k0103]]); 雜記 (miscellaneous regional records, [[KR2k0104]]–[[KR2k0132]]); 遊記 (travel diaries, [[KR2k0133]]–[[KR2k0135]]); and 外紀 (foreign records, [[KR2k0136]]–[[KR2k0152]]). The chronological span runs from Jī Hán's 嵇含 early-4th-century botanical *Nánfāng cǎomù zhuàng* [[KR2k0104]] to the Qiánlóng-era imperial provincial *tōngzhì* of 1789, covering the entire imperial documentary tradition of place-writing in China.

## Important texts and text clusters

### The empire-wide comprehensive gazetteers (總志)

- *Yuánhé jùnxiàn (tú)zhì* 元和郡縣(圖)志 [[KR2k0003]] — Lǐ Jífǔ's 李吉甫 (758–814) Yuánhé-era (813–814) gazetteer of the entire empire, the earliest extant separately-circulating empire-wide geography (Wilkinson §16.3.3 #1; §61.3.3). The *Yuánhé* sets the template — *yán'gé* prologue, then prefecture-by-prefecture survey of borders, products, and population — that all later *yītǒng zhì* will inherit.
- *Tàipíng huányǔ jì* 太平寰宇記 [[KR2k0004]] — Yuè Shǐ's 樂史 (930–1007) early-Sòng (979–987) compilation, the first comprehensive gazetteer of the unified Sòng empire and the work that introduces the inclusion of biographies, literary works, and ethnographic material into the *zǒngzhì* genre (Wilkinson §16.3.3 #2). The two great empire-wide compilations of the long 11th–13th c. that follow it — *Yuánfēng jiǔyù zhì* 元豐九域志 [[KR2k0005]] (Wáng Cún 王存, 1080) and *Yúdì guǎngjì* 輿地廣記 [[KR2k0006]] (Ōuyáng Mín 歐陽忞, 1111–1117) — extend its template into the high-Sòng administrative state.
- *Fāngyú shènglǎn* 方輿勝覽 [[KR2k0007]] — Zhù Mù's 祝穆 1239 Southern-Sòng compilation organized by *lù* 路, the principal Southern-Sòng cultural-geographical synthesis and the only major Sòng *zǒngzhì* still preserved in its full original form.
- *Míng yītǒng zhì* 明一統志 [[KR2k0008]] — Lǐ Xián's 李賢 imperially-commissioned 90-juan compendium of 1461, the standard Míng comprehensive gazetteer; and *Dà Qīng yītǒng zhì* 大清一統志 [[KR2k0009]] — the 424-juan Hé-Shēn 和珅-supervised Qiánlóng-era revision (1764–1784) of the Kāngxī and Yōngzhèng *Yītǒng zhì* projects, the largest single work in the division and the standard imperial-Qīng repository (Wilkinson §16.3.3 #6).

### Sòng–Yuán prefectural gazetteers and the *fāngzhì* tradition (都會郡縣)

The Sòng *fāngzhì* of [[KR2k0010]]–0027 are the principal surviving witnesses to a genre that produced over 1,000 titles in the Southern Sòng alone, of which fewer than 30 are extant (Wilkinson §16.4.1, §62.3.3.2). The cluster anchors the historical geography of the Jiāng-Zhè and Mǐn cultural cores:
- *Wújùn tújīng xùjì* 吳郡圖經續記 [[KR2k0010]] — Zhū Chángwén's 朱長文 1084 Sūzhōu prefectural continuation, the earliest extant work in the long Sūzhōu-prefectural-gazetteer series.
- The Línān / Hángzhōu cluster — *Qiándào Línān zhì* 乾道臨安志 [[KR2k0011]] (1169–1173) and *Xiánchún Línān zhì* 咸淳臨安志 [[KR2k0022]] (1268–1274), the two principal Southern-Sòng capital gazetteers.
- *Chúnxī Sānshān zhì* 淳熙三山志 [[KR2k0012]] — Liáng Kèjiā 梁克家, Fúzhōu 1179–1182; the earliest extant prefectural gazetteer of Fújiàn.
- *Wújùn zhì* 吳郡志 [[KR2k0013]] — Fàn Chéngdà's 范成大 50-juan Sūzhōu gazetteer (1192–1229).
- *Xīn'ān zhì* 新安志 [[KR2k0014]] (Huīzhōu, 1175), *Shàn lù* 剡錄 [[KR2k0015]] (Shèngzhōu, 1214–1215), *Huìjī zhì* 會稽志 [[KR2k0016]] (1201–1225), *Chìchéng zhì* 赤城志 [[KR2k0017]] (Tāizhōu, 1214–1223), *Bǎoqìng Sìmíng zhì* 寶慶四明志 [[KR2k0018]] (Níngbō, 1226–1227), and *Jǐngdìng Jiànkāng zhì* 景定建康志 [[KR2k0021]] (Nánjīng, 1261) — together the canonical Southern-Sòng prefectural-gazetteer set. As Wilkinson notes (§16.4.1), the first detailed *fánlì* (compilation rules) extant in the genre are in *Jǐngdìng Jiànkāng zhì*.
- Yuán continuations — *Zhìyuán Jiāhé zhì* 至元嘉禾志 [[KR2k0023]], *Yányòu Sìmíng zhì* 延祐四明志 [[KR2k0025]], *Qí chéng* 齊乘 [[KR2k0026]] (Yú Qīn 于欽's 1339 Shāndōng provincial gazetteer, the earliest extant provincial-level *fāngzhì*), and *Zhìdà Jīnlíng xīnzhì* 至大金陵新志 [[KR2k0027]] (Zhāng Xuàn 張鉉, 1344).

### The high-Qīng imperial gazetteer programme (欽定 and 通志)

- *Qīndìng Rèhé zhì* 欽定熱河志 [[KR2k0035]], *Qīndìng Rìxià jiùwén kǎo* 欽定日下舊聞考 [[KR2k0036]] (the imperial-Qīng standard topography of Běijīng), *Qīndìng Mǎnzhōu yuánliú kǎo* 欽定滿洲源流考 [[KR2k0038]], *Qīndìng Huángyú Xīyù túzhì* 欽定皇輿西域圖志 [[KR2k0039]], and *Qīndìng Shèngjīng tōngzhì* 欽定盛京通志 [[KR2k0040]] — the late-Qiánlóng imperial-commissioned topographies of the Manchu dynastic homeland, the Mongol-Manchurian frontier, Xīnjiāng, and the Manchu second capital, jointly the documentary monument of the multi-ethnic Qīng-imperial geographical project.
- The 16 provincial *tōngzhì* of [[KR2k0041]]–[[KR2k0056]] (*Jīfǔ tōngzhì* through *Guìzhōu tōngzhì*) — the Qing imperial provincial-gazetteer programme, commissioned by edicts of Kāngxī 1672 and Yōngzhèng 1728 that ordered every province to compile a *fāngzhì* (Wilkinson §16.4.1). Sixty-five percent of all *fāngzhì* extant before 1912 were compiled in the Qīng (46 percent of those in the 18th century); these 16 imperial provincial *tōngzhì*, all completed between 1729 and 1742, are the apex of that programme.
- *Tiānxià jùnguó lìbìng shū* 天下郡國利病書 [[KR2k0037]] — Gù Yánwǔ's 顧炎武 (1613–1682) Ming-loyalist historical geography of strategic advantages and weaknesses, composed 1639–1662 from over a thousand local gazetteers; the founding monument of *kǎojù* historical geography and Wilkinson's first item under "Empire-wide Geographies" (§16.3.4 #1). Its admission to the *Sìkù* — despite its author's anti-Manchu posture and his close relations with the resistance — is the principal sign that the Qiánlóng court treated him as the standard-setter of late-imperial historical-geographical scholarship.

### The hydraulic and watercourse tradition (河渠)

- *Shuǐjīng zhù* 水經注 [[KR2k0058]] — Lì Dàoyuán's 酈道元 (d. 527) Northern-Wèi commentary on the anonymous 3rd-c. *Shuǐjīng*, the foundational monument of Chinese historical hydrology and one of the great prose works of the medieval period (Wilkinson §16.3.1 closing note; §15.7.1). It is followed in the cluster by the two principal Qīng reconstructions — Shěn Bǐngxùn's 沈炳巽 *Shuǐjīngzhù jíshì dìngé* [[KR2k0059]] and Zhào Yīqīng's 趙一清 *Shuǐjīngzhù shì* [[KR2k0060]] — the texts at the centre of the famous Dài Zhèn 戴震 – Zhào Yīqīng *kǎojù* plagiarism controversy.
- The Sòng–Míng water-control compendia — *Wúzhōng shuǐlì shū* 吳中水利書 [[KR2k0061]] (Shàn È 單鍔, 1059–1091), *Héfáng yīlǎn* 河防一覽 [[KR2k0066]] (Pān Jìxún 潘季馴, 1591), *Wúzhōng shuǐlì quánshū* 吳中水利全書 [[KR2k0071]] (Zhāng Guówéi 張國維, 1636–1639) — and the Qīng monumental *Xíngshuǐ jīnjiàn* 行水金鑑 [[KR2k0078]] (Fù Zéhóng 傅澤洪, 175 juan, 1725), the standard imperial-Qīng reference for the Yellow River, Grand Canal, and Huái systems.
- *Shuǐdào tígāng* 水道提綱 [[KR2k0079]] — Qí Zhàonán's 齊召南 1761 comprehensive empire-wide hydrography on the new Kāngxī geodetic basis.

### Frontier defence (邊防)

- *Chóuhǎi túbiān* 籌海圖編 [[KR2k0081]] — Zhèng Ruòzēng's 鄭若曾 1562 anti-*wōkòu* coastal-defence atlas compiled under Hú Zōngxiàn 胡宗憲, the standard Míng work on coastal military geography.
- *Zhèng Kāiyáng zázhù* 鄭開陽雜著 [[KR2k0082]] — Zhèng Ruòzēng's collected coastal-defence essays, the companion piece.

### Mountains, antiquarian topographies, and famous sites (山川, 古蹟)

- *Luòyáng qiélán jì* 洛陽伽藍記 [[KR2k0090]] — Yáng Xuànzhī's 楊衒之 547 Buddhist-monastic topography of post-Northern-Wèi Luòyáng, one of the great literary monuments of the early-medieval period and the principal source for the religious and urban history of Luòyáng under Xuānwǔ and Xiàomíng.
- *Chángān zhì* 長安志 [[KR2k0092]] — Sòng Mǐnqiú's 宋敏求 20-juan Sòng-era historical topography of the Hàn-Táng capital, and its illustrated companion *Chángān zhì tú* 長安志圖 [[KR2k0096]] (Lǐ Hǎowén 李好文, 1346); the standard repository for the historical urbanism of Cháng'ān.
- *Luòyáng míngyuán jì* 洛陽名園記 [[KR2k0093]] — Lǐ Géfēi's 李格非 turn-of-the-12th-c. record of nineteen private gardens of late-Běi-Sòng Luòyáng; a foundational document of Chinese garden history.
- *Guānzhōng shèngjì túzhì* 關中勝蹟圖志 [[KR2k0103]] — Bì Yuán's 畢沅 (1730–1797) 1776 illustrated antiquarian gazetteer of the Wèihé basin, written during his Shǎn-Gān viceregal tenure; the culmination of the late-Qiánlóng antiquarian-topographical tradition.

### Regional ethnography and urban memoirs (雜記)

- *Nánfāng cǎomù zhuàng* 南方草木狀 [[KR2k0104]] — Jī Hán's 嵇含 (263–306) work on the plants and products of Lǐngnán; the earliest dated text in the division and the foundational document of Chinese ethnobotany.
- *Jīng-Chǔ suìshí jì* 荊楚嵗時記 [[KR2k0105]] — Zōng Lǐn's 宗懍 mid-6th-c. Liáng work, the foundational document of the imperial-Chinese annual-festival cycle. (Note: the more comprehensive Sòng anthology *Suìshí guǎngjì* 歲時廣記 of Chén Yuánjìng 陳元靚 is in [[KR2j0001]], not KR2k.)
- The four great urban memoirs of the late Sòng — *Dōngjīng mènghuá lù* 東京夢華錄 [[KR2k0111]] (Mèng Yuánlǎo, 1147; memoir of Northern-Sòng Kāifēng), *Dūchéng jìshèng* 都城紀勝 [[KR2k0117]] (1235), *Mèngliáng lù* 夢梁錄 [[KR2k0118]] (Wú Zìmù, 1274), and *Wǔlín jiùshì* 武林舊事 [[KR2k0119]] (Zhōu Mì, 1280–1290; memoir of Southern-Sòng Línān). Together they form the most concentrated body of urban-historical material in pre-modern Chinese literature and the principal anglophone-translated cluster of the division.
- *Guìhǎi yúhéng zhì* 桂海虞衡志 [[KR2k0115]] (Fàn Chéngdà 范成大, 1175) and *Lǐngwài dàidá* 嶺外代答 [[KR2k0116]] (Zhōu Qùfēi 周去非, 1178) — the two great Southern-Sòng Lǐngnán ethnographies, indispensable for the social, mineral, and material history of the southwestern frontier.

### Travel diaries (遊記)

- *Yóu Chéngnán jì* 遊城南記 [[KR2k0133]] (Zhāng Lǐ 張禮, 1086) — the earliest classical *yóujì* preserved in the *Sìkù*.
- *Xú Xiákè yóujì* 徐霞客遊記 [[KR2k0135]] — Xú Hóngzǔ's 徐宏祖 (1587–1641) thirty-year travel diaries, the most famous travel-literature work in Chinese (Wilkinson §14.4) and the textual monument of Late-Míng empirical geography.

### Foreign records (外紀)

- *Fóguó jì* 佛國記 [[KR2k0136]] — Fǎxiǎn's 法顯 414–416 account of his pilgrimage to India through Central Asia, the earliest extant first-person Chinese account of South and Inner Asia.
- *Dà Táng Xīyù jì* 大唐西域記 [[KR2k0137]] — Xuánzàng's 玄奘 and Biànjī's 辯機 646 report of Xuánzàng's 17-year journey across the Western Regions and India; the single most consequential pre-modern Chinese ethnographic source on Central Asia and South Asia and the primary archaeological source for Buddhist site identification on the subcontinent.
- *Xuānhé fèngshǐ Gāolí tújīng* 宣和奉使高麗圖經 [[KR2k0138]] — Xú Jīng's 徐兢 1124 embassy report on Goryeo Korea, the standard pre-Mongol source on the peninsula.
- *Zhūfān zhì* 諸蕃志 [[KR2k0139]] (Zhào Rǔshì 趙汝适, 1225) and *Dǎoyí zhìlüè* 島夷誌略 [[KR2k0142]] (Wāng Dàyuān 汪大淵, 1349–1351) — the two great Sòng–Yuán maritime-trade gazetteers of the South China Sea / Indian Ocean world (the Quánzhōu / Fújiàn customs-superintendent tradition).
- *Zhēnlà fēngtǔ jì* 眞臘風土記 [[KR2k0141]] — Zhōu Dáguān's 周達觀 1297 Cambodia account, the unique surviving eyewitness description of the Khmer Angkor court.
- *Zhífāng wàijì* 職方外紀 [[KR2k0146]] (Ài Rúlüè / Giulio Aleni, 1623), and *Kūnyú túshuō* 坤輿圖說 [[KR2k0150]] (Nán Huáirén / Ferdinand Verbiest, 1672) — the Jesuit world geographies that the Sìkù compilers admitted to the canon, the central textual evidence for the 17th-c. encounter between European and Chinese cartography.
- *Yìyù lù* 異域錄 [[KR2k0151]] — Tulišen's 圖理琛 1715–1721 Manchu/Chinese embassy report on the Volga Torghut Mongols, the unique 18th-c. eyewitness account of the Caspian-steppe Mongols and their Russian context.
- *Huáng Qīng zhígòng tú* 皇清職貢圖 [[KR2k0149]] (Fù Héng 傅恒 et al., 1751–1763) and *Hǎiguó wénjiàn lù* 海國聞見錄 [[KR2k0152]] (Chén Lúnjiǒng 陳倫炯, 1730) — the high-Qīng visual-ethnographic and maritime-geographical syntheses with which the division closes.

## Important persons

- [[酈道元]] (d. 527) — Northern-Wèi Vice Censor-in-Chief, author of the *Shuǐjīng zhù* [[KR2k0058]]; the founding figure of Chinese historical hydrography.
- [[李吉甫]] (758–814) — Táng grand councilor and author of the *Yuánhé jùnxiàn zhì* [[KR2k0003]]; the architect of the empire-wide-gazetteer genre.
- [[樂史]] (930–1007) — early-Sòng author of *Tàipíng huányǔ jì* [[KR2k0004]]; the figure who broadens the *zǒngzhì* into a literary-geographical compendium.
- [[范成大]] (1126–1193) — Southern-Sòng polymath author of *Wújùn zhì* [[KR2k0013]] and *Guìhǎi yúhéng zhì* [[KR2k0115]]; the model of the high-Sòng literatus-administrator-geographer.
- [[祝穆]] — 13th-c. Fújiàn literatus, author of *Fāngyú shènglǎn* [[KR2k0007]]; the principal Southern-Sòng cultural-geographical synthesizer.
- [[孟元老]], [[吳自牧]], [[周密]] — the three authors of the great Sòng urban memoirs ([[KR2k0111]], [[KR2k0118]], [[KR2k0119]]); together the foundational figures of the Chinese urban-memoir tradition.
- [[釋法顯]] (ca. 337 – ca. 422) and [[玄奘]] (ca. 602–664), with his disciple [[辯機]] — the two pilgrim authors of [[KR2k0136]] and [[KR2k0137]]; the canonical figures of pre-modern Chinese foreign ethnography.
- [[周達觀]] (fl. 1297) — Yuán embassy member to Angkor, author of *Zhēnlà fēngtǔ jì* [[KR2k0141]].
- [[徐宏祖]] / 徐霞客 (1587–1641) — Late-Míng traveler and diarist; the canonical figure of Chinese travel literature.
- [[潘季馴]] (1521–1595) — Míng Yellow-River conservancy commissioner and author of *Héfáng yīlǎn* [[KR2k0066]]; the standard-setter of imperial-Chinese hydraulic engineering doctrine.
- [[顧炎武]] (1613–1682) — Ming-loyalist polymath, author of *Tiānxià jùnguó lìbìng shū* [[KR2k0037]]; founder of *Hànxué pài* / *kǎojù* historical geography (Wilkinson §16.3.4).
- [[酈道元]], [[戴震]] (1724–1777) — Dài Zhèn is the principal Sìkù editor of the *Shuǐjīng zhù* and the central figure of the celebrated *kǎojù* controversy over the *jīngzhù* separation (see [[KR2k0058]], [[KR2k0059]], [[KR2k0060]]).
- [[畢沅]] (1730–1797) — Shǎn-Gān viceroy, antiquarian-topographer, author of *Guānzhōng shèngjì túzhì* [[KR2k0103]]; the late-Qiánlóng synthesis of *Hànxué* historical topography and viceregal patronage.
- [[艾儒略]] (Giulio Aleni, 1582–1649) and [[南懷仁]] (Ferdinand Verbiest, 1623–1688) — the Jesuit authors whose world geographies ([[KR2k0146]], [[KR2k0150]]) the Sìkù admitted to the canon; the bridge between European and Chinese cartography in the 17th c.
- [[和珅]], [[于敏中]], [[英廉]], [[傅恒]] — the senior Qing court officials who headed the great imperial gazetteer commissions of the Qiánlóng era ([[KR2k0009]], [[KR2k0035]]–[[KR2k0040]], [[KR2k0149]]).

## Topics

- **The *fāngzhì* as instrument of governance.** The *Sìkù* editors retained the inherited classification of geography under *shǐbù* because, as Wilkinson §16.4.1 emphasizes, the gazetteer was first and foremost an administrator's handbook — the rules of avoidance forbade officials from serving in their own home region, so every new magistrate or prefect had to learn the locality from its gazetteer. Almost every text in [[KR2k0010]]–0056 was either commissioned by, or actively used by, the local administration. The 1672 and 1728 imperial edicts mandating provincial *tōngzhì* are the proximate cause of the dominance of Qīng *fāngzhì* in the division.
- **The empire-wide *yītǒng zhì* programme.** From the *Tàipíng huányǔ jì* through the *Da-Yuán da-yītǒng zhì* (lost in part) and the *Dà Míng yītǒng zhì* [[KR2k0008]] to the *Dà Qīng yītǒng zhì* [[KR2k0009]], each unifying dynasty signaled its claim to *tiānxià* by commissioning a single empire-wide gazetteer (Wilkinson §16.3.3). The Sìkù sub-cluster preserves the standard recension of every surviving member of the sequence except the Yuán entry.
- **Historical hydrology and the *Shuǐjīng zhù* tradition.** The 23-text 河渠 cluster ([[KR2k0058]]–0080) preserves the entire long arc from Lì Dàoyuán to the late-Qiánlóng imperial Yellow-River programmes. The *kǎojù* reconstruction of the *Shuǐjīng zhù* in the 18th century (Quán Zǔwàng, Zhào Yīqīng, Shěn Bǐngxùn, Dài Zhèn) and the Dài–Zhào plagiarism controversy is one of the most documented affairs in early-modern Chinese textual scholarship.
- **The Sòng prefectural-gazetteer renaissance.** Of the 1,000+ known Southern-Sòng *fāngzhì*, fewer than 30 survive; the Sòng cluster of [[KR2k0010]]–0022 contains the bulk of them, including the two great Línān gazetteers ([[KR2k0011]], [[KR2k0022]]) and the canonical Sūzhōu set ([[KR2k0010]], [[KR2k0013]]). These works embody the Southern-Sòng shift Wilkinson describes (§16.4.1) — from military-administrative reportage to gentry biography, literary citation, and cultural display.
- **Late-Sòng urban nostalgia.** The cluster [[KR2k0111]], [[KR2k0117]], [[KR2k0118]], [[KR2k0119]] preserves the most concentrated body of urban-cultural-historical writing in the pre-modern Chinese tradition, all four works composed retrospectively after the loss of their respective capitals (Kāifēng to the Jīn; Línān to the Mongols). They are the documentary basis of almost every modern study of Sòng urban culture, popular entertainment, ritual calendar, and material life.
- **Foreign-records and the boundary of *tiānxià*.** The 外紀 cluster [[KR2k0136]]–[[KR2k0152]] traces the genealogy of Chinese knowledge of the outer world — Buddhist pilgrim accounts of South and Central Asia (Fǎxiǎn, Xuánzàng), Sòng–Yuán maritime ethnography (*Zhūfān zhì*, *Dǎoyí zhìlüè*, *Zhēnlà fēngtǔ jì*), Míng tributary embassies (*Cháoxiǎn fù* [[KR2k0143]], *Cháoxiǎn zhì* [[KR2k0148]]), Jesuit world geographies ([[KR2k0146]], [[KR2k0150]]), and the high-Qīng visual-ethnographic synthesis ([[KR2k0149]]). It registers, within a single Sìkù sub-cluster, the entire pre-modern Chinese geographical imagination of what lay beyond the *zhífāng* world.
- **The Ming-loyalist historiographical legacy.** That Gù Yánwǔ's *Tiānxià jùnguó lìbìng shū* [[KR2k0037]] was admitted to the Sìkù but his *Zhàoyù zhì* and Gù Zǔyǔ's *Dúshǐ fāngyú jìyào* (the third great Qīng work of historical geography) were excluded — Wilkinson §16.3.4 makes the political-bibliographical point sharp — illustrates the Qiánlóng court's selective accommodation of *kǎojù* historical geography. The Qing imperial gazetteer programme inherited the methods of the Ming loyalists while editing out the politically resistant works.
- **The Jesuit world-geographies in the Sìkù.** The admission of [[KR2k0146]] (Aleni 1623) and [[KR2k0150]] (Verbiest 1672) to the *Sìkù* — alongside the rejection of most early-Qīng Christian writings — reflects the editors' technical regard for Jesuit cartography and astronomy even as they kept doctrinal Christianity outside the canon. The two works are the principal Sìkù-canonized European-language-derived geographies.
- **Encyclopedism and the imperial gazetteer.** The flowering of the *qīndìng* genre ([[KR2k0035]]–[[KR2k0040]], [[KR2k0072]], [[KR2k0088]], [[KR2k0089]], [[KR2k0149]]) under Qiánlóng converted the *fāngzhì* from a working administrative reference into a state-ideological monument; many were produced in the same Hànlín / Wǔyīngdiàn editorial pipeline that produced the imperial *yùdìng* literary and *yuèlìng* anthologies (see [[KR2j]] for the cognate *Shílìng* category).
