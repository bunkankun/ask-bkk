# Huáng Gōng shísì zhōu jì 黃恭十四州記
*Huang Gong's Records of the Fourteen Provinces*
by [[黃恭]] (Huáng Gōng, dates unknown) — *zhuàn* 撰

## About the work

A fragmentary geographic text attributed to one Huang Gong 黃恭, whose dates and biographical details are unknown. The work is reconstructed from citations in Tang-Song encyclopedias (《藝文類聚》, 《太平御覽》, 《水經注》). The surviving passages focus on the etymology and institutional history of Chinese administrative units: the *jùn* 郡 (commandery), the *xiàn* 縣 (county), the *xiāng* 鄉 (township), and the *cìshǐ* 刺史 (regional inspector). The title's "fourteen provinces" (*shísì zhōu* 十四州) may represent an administrative count characteristic of the Han-to-Wei transition.

### Abstract

The surviving fragments of the *Huáng Gōng shísì zhōu jì* are entirely philological and institutional in character. The *jùn* passage: "The commandery (*jùn* 郡) derives from 'lord' (*jūn* 君); the commandery governor monopolizes authority, the ritual between lord and minister is thereby elevated…" The *xiàn* passage explains the county name as derived from *xián* 弦 ("bowstring" = straight), noting the obligation of lower-level administrators to uphold law without bending. The passage on *xiāng* 鄉 notes that Qin converted dependent territories (*fùyōng* 附庸) into *xiāng*. The *cìshǐ* passage explains the regional inspector title: *cì* 刺 means "to investigate and report," and *shǐ* 史 means "an imperial envoy." 

This content is parallel to material in KR2k0157 (闞駰 [[KR2k0157]] *Shísān zhōu zhì*) and KR2k0200 ([[KR2k0200]] *Shísān zhōu jì*), suggesting a common tradition of administrative-geography etymology texts. Huang Gong's identity remains obscure; the title does not appear in the *Suí shū · Jīngjí zhì*, and the bibliographic evidence for this work is limited to the encyclopedia citations that constitute its surviving text.

The dating window 200–600 is broad and reflects only the latest secure limit: the encyclopedias citing the text are Tang-period. The content is consistent with a Wei-Jin or Southern-dynasty composition.
