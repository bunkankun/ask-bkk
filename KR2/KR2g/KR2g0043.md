# Qīndìng wàifān Měnggǔ Huíbù wánggōng biǎozhuàn 欽定外藩蒙古回部王公表傳
*Imperially Ordered Genealogical Tables and Biographies of the Outer-Vassal Mongol and Muslim Princes and Dukes*
imperially commissioned by [[高宗弘曆]] (敕撰)

## About the work
A 120-*juàn* imperially commissioned compilation of genealogical tables and biographies of the *wàifān* 外藩 (outer-vassal) Mongol and Eastern-Turkestan Muslim (*Huíbù* 回部) princes and dukes who held titles under the Qīng. The first imperial edict commanding the compilation is dated *Qiánlóng* 44 (*qīyuè* 29 = 29th day of the seventh month of 1779). A second edict of the same year (ninth month, second day) extended the program to the post-pacification Eastern Turkestan (*Huíbù*) princes who had been enfeoffed since their submission to the Qīng. The work was to be compiled by the *Guóshǐguǎn* in collaboration with the *Lǐfānyuàn* 理藩院, in three-language (*qīng-Hàn-Měnggǔ* — Manchu, Chinese, Mongolian) parallel-text format; one *biǎozhuàn* per *bùluò* (banner-confederation), with separate biographies appended for the most prominent princes within each. The catalog meta gives the compilation date as *Qiánlóng* 44 (1779). The 120 *juàn* are organized by *bù* (confederation): *Kēěrqìn* 科爾沁, *Zhālàitè* 扎賚特, *Dùěrbótè* 杜爾伯特, *Guōěrluósī* 郭爾羅斯 (Khorchin and related); *Kālāqìn* 喀喇沁, *Tǔmòtè* 土默特 (Eastern Mongolian); *Áohàn* 敖漢, *Nàimàn* 奈曼, *Bālín* 巴林, *Zhālǔtè* 扎嚕特, *Ālǔ Kēěrqìn* 阿嚕科爾沁 (Inner Mongolian); etc., extending through the *Khalkha* (Outer Mongolian), *Oirat*, *Khoit*, and other confederations and concluding with the *Huíbù* of Kashgar, Yarkand, Khotan, etc. The work is the foundational documentary compilation of the Qīng's *fānbù* (vassal) nobility and the most important single source for the prosopography of the Qing's Inner Asian elite.

### About the imperial-edict program
The first edict (*Qiánlóng* 44, seventh month, 29th day, 1779) opens with the rationale: "Our state, founding the dynasty and unifying the realm, has had the 49 banners of Mongolia and the *Khalkha* outer-banners of *Zhāsàkè* 扎薩克 all serving as defensive vassals, generations of unflinching loyalty, the central and the outer one family, far transcending all earlier ages. From *Tàizǔ* and *Tàizōng* through *Huángzǔ* (Kāngxī), *Huángkǎo* (Yōngzhèng), and the present reign, those Mongol princes and dukes who have rendered conspicuous merit are too many to count. Lest their meritorious deeds go unsignalled, the *Guóshǐguǎn* in collaboration with the *Lǐfānyuàn* shall examine the deeds and lineages of each Mongol *Zhāsàkè* in detail. One *biǎozhuàn* per confederation; for those princes whose deeds are conspicuous, one separate biography per man within the confederation's *biǎozhuàn*. The whole shall be compiled in three-language parallel-text (*qīng-Hàn-Měnggǔ*) and presented chapter by chapter for imperial review. After approval, this work and the *Zōngshì wánggōng biǎozhuàn* ([[KR2g0042]]) shall be entered into the *Sìkù quánshū* in the Chinese script alone; each confederation shall be given a three-language fascicle of its own *biǎozhuàn* and individual *zhuàn* for distribution among its descendants, that they may know their fathers' deeds." The second edict (*Qiánlóng* 44, ninth month, 2nd day) extends the same treatment to the *Huíbù* princes enfeoffed since the conquest of Eastern Turkestan in 1759.

### Abstract
The *Qīndìng wàifān Měnggǔ Huíbù wánggōng biǎozhuàn* is the most ambitious work of the Qiánlóng court's mid-late prosopographical program — a 120-*juàn* compilation comprehensively documenting the Mongol and Eastern Turkestan Muslim *wàifān* nobility of the Qīng. Composition was commanded *Qiánlóng* 44 (1779) and proceeded in stages, with the work likely completed and presented over the next several years (final fascicle by *Qiánlóng* 47 / 1782). The catalog meta records "*Qiánlóng* 44" as the headline date. The work's three-language (Manchu/Chinese/Mongolian) parallel-text format reflects the *Lǐfānyuàn* / *Guóshǐguǎn* collaboration; the WYG copy preserves the Chinese-only version per the imperial edict's specification. The work is the foundational source for the Qīng-period prosopography of the Mongol-Manchu-Muslim Inner Asian elite and is consulted in modern times by all serious historians of the Qīng's Inner Asian frontiers (Crossley, Elliott, Atwood).

## Other points of interest
The work's three-language parallel-text format (Manchu, Chinese, Mongolian) makes it a textbook example of late-Qián-lóng *qīngwén* (Manchu-language) imperial historiography. The deliberate distribution of single-confederation fascicles to each *bù* — so that the descendants might "know their fathers' deeds" — is also one of the more striking instances of the Qiánlóng court's active management of *wàifān* identity through textual production.
