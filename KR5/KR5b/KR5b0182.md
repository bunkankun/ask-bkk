# Jīnlù yánshòu shèjiào yí 金籙延壽設醮儀
*Longevity-Extending Offering-Rite of the Golden-Register Fast*

## About the work

A one-fascicle *shèjiào yí* 設醮儀 (set-up of the offering rite) appended in the Míng *Zhèngtǒng dàozàng* to the close of the Dù Guāngtíng **Jīnlù** longevity sequence ([[KR5b0178]]–[[KR5b0181]]). The catalog entry labels it Míng; following Schipper & Verellen (*Taoist Canon* 2: 996–997, John Lagerwey), it is a Sòng-to-Míng adaptation that recasts the TángWǔdài *qíshòu Jīnlù* in the standardised SòngYuán *jiào* 醮 format, in which the final imperial offering is dedicated to longevity (*yánshòu* 延壽).

### Abstract

The text opens with the *wǔxīng* 五星 (Five Planets) opening verse — *Wǔxīng lièzhào huànmíng wǔfāng* 五星列照煥明五方 — which became the standard exorcistic-protective hymn for late-medieval and early-modern *jiào* offerings (see also [[KR5b0192]] j. 1). The enumeration of the celestial recipients includes by name many late additions characteristic of post-Sòng pantheons (*Shénxiāo Yùqīng chángshēng dàdì* 神霄玉清長生大帝, *Mǎ–Zhào–Guān–Wēn sì dàyuánshuài* 馬趙關温四大元帥, the four marshals of the Shénxiāo 神霄 tradition), confirming the Sòng / Míng dating proposed by Lagerwey.

The catalog meta assigns the work to 明 without naming an author; in fact the redactional history follows the same anonymous, accretional pattern as several other *Jīnlù* expansions in DZ 494–498 (cf. [[KR5b0183]]–[[KR5b0192]]). The composition window is conservatively bracketed by the YuánMíng transition (c. 1300) and the Zhèngtǒng compilation of the *Daozang* (1444).
