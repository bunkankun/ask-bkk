## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR5i collects the **Dàozàng jíyào** 道藏輯要, the principal Qīng-era abridgment-and-expansion of the Daoist Canon, in two superimposed strata: the **原版道藏輯要** (Yuánbǎn DZJY, base-edition tag `YPP`), compiled by **Jiǎng Yǔpǔ** 蔣予蒲 (= Jiǎng Yuántíng 蔣元庭, 1755–1819) and printed in Beijing in 1806/1809; and the **重刊道藏輯要** (Chóngkān DZJY, base-edition tag `CK-KZ`), the substantially enlarged re-engraving completed at the **Èrxiānān** 二仙菴 and **Qīngyánggōng** 青羊宮 of Chéngdū in 1906 under the abbacy of **Hé Lóngxiāng** 賀龍驤. The Kanripo recension preserves all 109 works in DZJY's final received state, with the YPP / CK-KZ stratification visible in each per-text frontmatter. The division has no WYG (Wényuàngé Sìkù) counterpart: the Sìkù compilers excluded the Daoist Canon and its supplements from the 子部, so DZJY's entry into Kanripo is via its own block-print line, not via any imperial bibliographic sanction (cf. the parallel situation for [[KR5h]] *Xù Dàozàng*).

DZJY differs from the *Zhèngtǒng Dàozàng* ([[KR5a]]–[[KR5g]]) and its Wànlì supplement ([[KR5h]]) in three editorial respects that shape the whole division. First, the selection is decisively **Lǚ-zǔ-centric and Quánzhēn / Lóngmén in orientation**: a large bloc of the texts are Lǚ Dòngbīn 呂洞賓 *fújī* 扶乩 (planchette) productions ([[KR5i0049]], [[KR5i0050]], [[KR5i0051]], [[KR5i0053]], [[KR5i0054]], [[KR5i0055]], [[KR5i0047]], [[KR5i0048]], [[KR5i0045]]), and the institutional precept and liturgy corpus ([[KR5i0097]]–[[KR5i0099]], [[KR5i0090]]–[[KR5i0091]]) anchors the collection on the post-1656 Wáng Chángyuè revival at the Báiyúnguàn 白雲觀. Second, **inner alchemy** (*nèidān* 內丹) is the dominant practice register — the *Cāntóngqì* and *Wùzhēnpiān* lineages, the Wǔ Liǔ pài 伍柳派 dossier, the women's-alchemy (*nǚdān*) corpus and the *Jīnhuá* / *Tàiyǐ jīnhuá zōngzhǐ* tradition are all present in their late-imperial received form. Third, DZJY enacts an explicit **Three-Teachings synthesis** by canonizing several non-Daoist works — Zhōu Dūnyí's 太極圖說 and 通書 with Zhū Xī's annotation ([[KR5i0079]]–[[KR5i0080]]), Shào Yōng's 皇極經世書 and 擊壤集 ([[KR5i0081]]–[[KR5i0082]]), and Zhūgě Liàng's collected works ([[KR5i0078]]) — alongside Buddhist-register expositions like Wǔ Shǒuyáng's *Xiān fó hé zōng* ([[KR5i0066]]). The geographic centre of gravity is **Sìchuān**: the texts emerge from, or were re-canonized through, the Chéngdū Lóngmén network around the Èrxiānān and the Sìchuān Lǚzǔ planchette altars, making DZJY the principal documentary witness to nineteenth-century West-China Daoist institutional life.

## Important texts and text clusters

- **The Lǚzǔ planchette dossier (*Chúnyáng* corpus).** The single largest cluster in the division: *Lǚzǔ běnzhuàn* 呂祖本傳 [[KR5i0045]], *Lǚdì wénjí* 呂帝文集 [[KR5i0049]], *Lǚdì shījí* 呂帝詩集 [[KR5i0050]], *Chúnyáng sānshū* 純陽三書 [[KR5i0051]], *Yǔlù dàguān* 語錄大觀 [[KR5i0053]], *Wēiyán zhāiyào* 微言摘要 [[KR5i0054]], *Lǚdì shèngjì jìyào* 呂帝聖蹟紀要 [[KR5i0055]], *Tóngcān jīng* 同參經 [[KR5i0047]], and *Wǔjīng hébiān* 五經合編 [[KR5i0048]]. Together this bloc constitutes the **canonical anthology of the Lǚzǔ cult as it operated through Qīng planchette altars** — Liǔ Shǒuyuán 柳守元 is the recurring editorial hand, and the corpus defines DZJY's editorial centre of gravity.
- **The Wǔ Liǔ pài 伍柳派 inner-alchemy core.** *Tiānxiān zhènglǐ zhílùn* 天仙正理直論 [[KR5i0067]] and its *Qiǎnshuō* abridgement [[KR5i0068]], *Xiānfó hézōng yǔlù* 仙佛合宗語錄 [[KR5i0066]], *Wǔzhēnrén dāndào jiǔpiān* 伍真人丹道九篇 [[KR5i0070]], and *Jīndān yàojué* 金丹要訣 [[KR5i0069]] — the foundational dossier of the **Wǔ Shǒuyáng** 伍守陽 (c. 1574–1644) → Liǔ Huáyáng 柳華陽 inner-alchemical lineage, which became the most widely-printed and most-translated late-imperial *nèidān* tradition (Cleary's *Daoist Yoga* renders its derivative form).
- **The *Cāntóngqì* / *Wùzhēnpiān* commentarial tradition.** *Cāntóngqì chǎnyōu* 參同契闡幽 [[KR5i0037]] and *Wùzhēnpiān chǎnyōu* 悟真篇闡幽 [[KR5i0059]], the paired commentaries of **Zhū Yuányù** 朱元育 (Kāngxī era); *Cāntóngqì fēnzhāngzhù* 參同契分章註 [[KR5i0038]] by the Yuán master **Chén Zhìxū** 陳致虛; the foundational *Jīndān sìbǎizì* 金丹四百字 [[KR5i0058]] of **Zhāng Bóduān** 張伯端 (c. 1075–1085); *Wǔpiān língwén* 五篇靈文 [[KR5i0062]] (the Jīn-era short scripture of the Quánzhēn founders' alchemical insight); and the Sòng *Rùyàojìng* 入藥鏡 [[KR5i0039]] of [[崔希範]] — collectively the classical inner-alchemical curriculum of late-imperial Daoism.
- **The *Jīnhuá* / *Golden Flower* and Qīnghuáshàngdì planchette compendia.** *Jīnhuá zōngzhǐ* 金華宗旨 [[KR5i0046]] (the DZJY recension of the work that reached Europe in 1929 as Richard Wilhelm's *Das Geheimnis der Goldenen Blüte*); *Tiānxiān jīndān xīnfǎ* 天仙金丹心法 [[KR5i0056]]; *Chàngdào zhēnyán* 唱道真言 [[KR5i0030]] (the great Yōng-zhèng-era *Qīnghuáshàngdì* planchette compendium, datable to 3 May 1729); *Yǎngzhēn jí* 養真集 [[KR5i0072]], *Yùquán* 玉詮 [[KR5i0073]], and *Zhēnquán* 真詮 [[KR5i0074]] — the late-imperial cultivation digests.
- **The women's alchemy (*nǚdān* 女丹) corpus.** *Sūn Bùèr yuánjūn fǎyǔ* 孫不二元君法語 [[KR5i0063]], *Sūn Bùèr yuánjūn chuánshù dāndào mìshū* 孫不二元君傳述丹道祕書 [[KR5i0064]], and the late-Qīng *Nǚdān hébiān* 女丹合編 [[KR5i0109]] — the principal vehicle of female inner-alchemy in the post-1700 Daoist tradition.
- **The Quánzhēn / Lóngmén institutional dossier.** The three-altar ordination block: *Sāntán yuánmǎn tiānxiān dàjiè lüèshuō* 三壇圓滿天仙大戒略說 [[KR5i0097]], *Chūzhēn jièlǜ* 初真戒律 [[KR5i0098]] (transmitted by **Wáng Chángyuè** 王常月 at the Báiyúnguàn on 31 March 1656 — the founding moment of the Qīng Quánzhēn institutional revival), and *Zhōngjí jiè* 中極戒 [[KR5i0099]]. Paired daily-liturgy manuals: *Qīngwēi hóngfàn dàomén gōngkè* 清微宏範道門功課 [[KR5i0090]] and *Tàishàng xuánmén gōngkè jīng* 太上玄門功課經 [[KR5i0091]]. Sacred-geography documentation of the DZJY's own institutional home: *Èrxiānān bēijì* 二仙菴碑記 [[KR5i0100]], *Qīngyánggōng bēijì* 青羊宮碑記 [[KR5i0101]], and *Qīngchéngshān jì* 青城山記 [[KR5i0103]].
- **The high-canonical scripture set.** *Yuánshǐ dàdòng yùjīng* 元始大洞玉經 [[KR5i0001]] and its 1709 expanded recension [[KR5i0003]] (Shàngqīng 大洞 tradition); *Gāoshàng yùhuáng běnxíng jíjīng* 高上玉皇本行集經 [[KR5i0015]] with its 1809 Jiǎng-circle commentary [[KR5i0016]] (Jade-Emperor scripture); the cluster of *Gāoshàng yùhuáng xīnyìn* recensions [[KR5i0017]]–[[KR5i0020]]; the *Huángtíng* 黃庭 inner / outer scriptures [[KR5i0010]]–[[KR5i0012]]; and the *Yùshū bǎojīng* 玉樞寶經 [[KR5i0035]] of the Sòng Shénxiāo 神霄 thunder-rite tradition.
- **The *Tàishàng gǎnyìng piān* moral-merit anchor.** *Tàishàng gǎnyìng piān jiānzhù* 太上感應篇箋注 [[KR5i0013]] and *Tàishàng gǎnyìng piān jízhù* 太上感應篇集注 [[KR5i0014]] — the two principal Qīng commentaries on the most-printed *shànshū* 善書 of late-imperial China, accompanied by *Yīnzhìwén zhù* 陰騭文註 [[KR5i0087]] and the merit-ledger pair *Shíjiè gōngguò gé* 十戒功過格 [[KR5i0095]] and *Jǐngshì gōngguò gé* 警世功過格 [[KR5i0096]].
- **The Wénchāng cult dossier.** *Wénchāng dìjūn běnzhuàn* 文昌帝君本傳 [[KR5i0083]], *Wéndì xiàojīng* 文帝孝經 [[KR5i0084]] (with Qiū Jùn's 1492 preface), *Wénchāng yánsì jīng* 文昌延嗣經 [[KR5i0086]] (dated Yōngzhèng 12 = 1734), and the *Yùqīng zànhuà jiǔtiān yǎnzhèng xīnyìn* pair [[KR5i0021]]–[[KR5i0022]] — DZJY's witness to the late-imperial literature-god cult that mediated Daoism to the *shìdàfū* class.
- **The Guāndì and Dòumǔ cults.** *Sānjiè fúmó Guānshèng dìjūn zhōngxiào zhōngyì zhēnjīng* 三界伏魔關聖帝君忠孝忠義真經 [[KR5i0088]] and *Guānshèng dìjūn běnzhuàn* 關聖帝君本傳 [[KR5i0089]] for the Guān-Yǔ cult; the Nine-August / Dòumǔ cluster [[KR5i0023]]–[[KR5i0026]].
- **The *Yīnfú jīng* commentarial cluster.** The base text in two recensions [[KR5i0031]]–[[KR5i0032]]; the precisely-dated 1772 *Yīnfú xuánjiě* 陰符玄解 [[KR5i0033]] of **Fàn Yíbīn** 范宜賓; and the *Tàigōng Yīnfú jīng* 太公陰符經 attribution [[KR5i0061]] — together documenting the eighteenth-century Liáoníng / Sìchuān Lóngmén reading of the canonical *Yīnfú*.
- **The Three-Teachings inclusions.** *Tàijí túshuō* 太極圖說 [[KR5i0079]] and *Tōngshū* 通書 [[KR5i0080]] of **Zhōu Dūnyí** 周敦頤 (Sòng, with Zhū Xī's annotation); *Huángjí jīngshìshū* 皇極經世書 [[KR5i0081]] and *Jīrǎng jí* 擊壤集 [[KR5i0082]] of **Shào Yōng** 邵雍 (Sòng); and *Hàn chéngxiàng Zhūgě Zhōngwǔhóu jí* 漢丞相諸葛忠武侯集 [[KR5i0078]] of **Zhūgě Liàng** 諸葛亮 (compiled 1632 by his 36th-generation descendant Zhūgě Xī 諸葛羲). Together they enact DZJY's editorial commitment to *sānjiào héyī* by canonizing Sòng *Lǐxué* and Hàn loyalty-and-martial figures *as Daoist material*.
- **The Lǎo-Zhuāng commentaries.** *Tàishàng xuányuán Dàodé jīng jiě* 太上玄元道德經解 [[KR5i0005]] (Lǚzǔ planchette commentary); *Tàishàng Dàodé bǎozhāng yì* 太上道德寶章翼 [[KR5i0006]] (Chéng Yǐníng 程以甯, paired with [[KR5i0036]]); *Dàodé jīng shìcí* 道德經釋辭 [[KR5i0007]] (Wáng Yīqīng 王一清, 1592–1597, late-Míng Wǔdāngshān milieu); and *Nánhuá zhēnjīng zhùshū* 南華真經註疏 [[KR5i0036]] (Chéng Yǐníng, *guǐyǒu* spring 1693). The latter is the high-water mark of inner-alchemical *Zhuāngzǐ* hermeneutics in DZJY.
- **The Zhāng Sānfēng compendium.** *Zhāng Sānfēng xiānshēng quánjí* 張三丰先生全集 [[KR5i0071]] — the standard late-imperial collected works of the legendary Wǔdāngshān patriarch, edited by Wāng Xīlíng 汪錫齡 (1723).
- **The Five-Dynasties and Sòng inclusions.** *Huàshū* 化書 [[KR5i0042]] of **Tán Qiào** 譚峭 in its second canonical recension (cf. the *Xù Dàozàng* recension at [[KR5h0047]]); *Qióngguǎn Bái zhēnrén jí* 瓊琯白真人集 [[KR5i0060]] (the 1594 collected works of the Southern-Sòng *Nánzōng* 南宗 patriarch **Bái Yùchán** 白玉蟾); and *Jīnbì gǔwén Lónghǔ shàngjīng* 金碧古文龍虎上經 [[KR5i0029]] in **Péng Hàogǔ's** 彭好古 1599 critical recension.
- **The Jìngmíng dossier.** *Tàishàng língbǎo jìngmíng zōngjiào lù* 太上靈寶淨明宗教錄 [[KR5i0041]] — the Qīng-recension institutional record of the Xǔ Xùn-centred Jìngmíng 淨明 tradition out of Jiāngxī.
- **The repentance and *zhāijiào* 齋醮 liturgical bloc.** *Chànfǎ dàguān* 懺法大觀 [[KR5i0076]] (32-part repentance compendium), *Sānbǎo wànlíng fǎchàn* 三寶萬靈法懺 [[KR5i0077]], *Tàishàng wújí dàdào yánshòu jífú xiāojié bǎochàn* [[KR5i0104]], and the *Tàishàng wújí dàdào sānshíliùbù zūnjīng* opening / commentary pair [[KR5i0107]]–[[KR5i0108]] — DZJY's witness to the Qīng performative-liturgical tradition out of which the *Èrxiānān* engraving house operated. The hungry-ghost-feeding rite is in *Qīngxuán jìliàn tiěguàn húshí* 青玄濟煉鐵罐斛食 [[KR5i0106]].

## Important persons

- [[蔣予蒲]] (Jiǎng Yǔpǔ, *zì* Jiǎng Yuántíng 元庭, 1755–1819) — Qīng *jìnshì* and high official; **compiler of the original 1806/1809 *Dàozàng jíyào*** in Beijing, and the editor whose Lǚ-zǔ-circle affiliations define the division's selection principle. Cf. Mori Yuria, "*Daozang jiyao* and Quanzhen Daoism in the Qing Dynasty."
- [[賀龍驤]] (Hé Lóngxiāng) — Sìchuān Lóngmén abbot at the **Èrxiānān** 二仙菴 in Chéngdū; principal editor of the 1906 **重刊道藏輯要**, the substantially expanded re-engraving from which most CK-KZ-tagged texts derive.
- [[呂洞賓]] (Lǚ Dòngbīn) — the **dominant attribution-figure of the whole division**: 25+ works carry his name as planchette-revealer, commentator, or biographical subject. DZJY is, in editorial substance, the canonical Qīng anthology of the Lǚzǔ cult.
- [[柳守元]] (Liǔ Shǒuyuán) — recurring editorial / compilatorial hand across the Lǚzǔ planchette corpus; appears as compiler-preface-writer on six DZJY entries and is the principal organising figure of the *Chúnyáng* dossier.
- [[伍守陽]] (Wǔ Shǒuyáng, c. 1574–1644) — **founder of the Wǔ Liǔ pài** 伍柳派; principal author of [[KR5i0066]], [[KR5i0067]], [[KR5i0068]], [[KR5i0070]]. The Wǔ Liǔ corpus is the most widely-circulated late-imperial inner-alchemical exposition.
- [[伍守虛]] (Wǔ Shǒuxū) — Wǔ Shǒuyáng's brother; collator and annotator across the Wǔ Liǔ dossier.
- [[王常月]] (Wáng Chángyuè, 1622–1680) — **Lóngmén institutional founder**; transmitted the *Chūzhēn jièlǜ* [[KR5i0098]] at the Báiyúnguàn on 31 March 1656, instituting the three-altar ordination system that organizes [[KR5i0097]]–[[KR5i0099]]. His preface is one of the foundational documents of Qīng Quánzhēn institutional history.
- [[朱元育]] (Zhū Yuányù) — Kāng-xī-era inner-alchemical commentator; author of the paired *Cāntóngqì chǎnyōu* [[KR5i0037]] and *Wùzhēnpiān chǎnyōu* [[KR5i0059]], the most-cited Qīng commentaries on the two foundational *nèidān* classics.
- [[程以甯]] (Chéng Yǐníng) — Kāng-xī-era *Lǎo-Zhuāng* commentator from the same milieu; author of [[KR5i0006]] *Dàodé bǎozhāng yì* and [[KR5i0036]] *Nánhuá zhēnjīng zhùshū* (*guǐyǒu* spring 1693), the latter the principal Qīng *Zhuāngzǐ* commentary in DZJY.
- [[王一清]] (Wáng Yīqīng, *hào* Tǐwùzǐ 體無子, *fl.* 1592–1597) — late-Wàn-lì Wǔdāngshān commentator; author of *Dàodé jīng shìcí* [[KR5i0007]] and of the 1592 critical recension of *Huàshū* [[KR5i0042]] that established the modern attribution to Tán Qiào.
- [[彭好古]] (Péng Hàogǔ) — late-Wàn-lì Húběi inner-alchemy editor; 1599 critical recension of *Jīnbì gǔwén Lónghǔ shàngjīng* [[KR5i0029]] is the clearest specimen of late-Míng Húběi inner-alchemical exegesis in DZJY.
- [[範宜賓]] / [[范宜賓]] (Fàn Yíbīn, *hào* Yīzhōngzǐ) — Shěn-yáng-based mid-Qián-lóng Lóngmén figure; 5 July 1772 *Yīnfú xuánjiě* [[KR5i0033]] documents the eighteenth-century Liáoníng / Sìchuān Lóngmén commentarial network.
- [[張清夜]] (Zhāng Qīngyè, *hào* Zìmù dàorén 自牧道人) — early-Qīng Chéngdū *Zǐyángdòng* 紫陽洞 abbot; author of *Yīnfú fāmì* (the antecedent to [[KR5i0033]]) and a foundational figure of the Sìchuān Lóngmén network that produced DZJY itself.
- [[張伯端]] (Zhāng Bóduān, 987–1082) — founder of the Sòng Nánzōng 南宗 inner-alchemical lineage; author of *Jīndān sìbǎizì* [[KR5i0058]] and the underlying *Wùzhēn piān* whose Qīng commentary forms [[KR5i0059]].
- [[白玉蟾]] (Bái Yùchán, *fl.* early 13th c.) — Southern-Sòng Nánzōng fifth patriarch; *Qióngguǎn Bái zhēnrén jí* [[KR5i0060]] is his standard Míng-Qīng collected works.
- [[譚峭]] (Tán Qiào, *fl.* c. 880–950) — Five-Dynasties Daoist philosopher; *Huàshū* [[KR5i0042]] in this division's recension.
- [[孫不二]] (Sūn Bùèr, 1119–1182) — Quánzhēn Seven-Perfected female patriarch; principal attribution-figure of the *nǚdān* tradition in [[KR5i0063]], [[KR5i0064]].
- [[周敦頤]] (Zhōu Dūnyí, 1017–1073) — Sòng Neo-Confucian founder; included here for *Tàijí túshuō* [[KR5i0079]] and *Tōngshū* [[KR5i0080]] under the Three-Teachings synthesis.
- [[邵雍]] (Shào Yōng, 1011–1077) — Sòng cosmologist and image-numerologist; *Huángjí jīngshìshū* [[KR5i0081]] and *Jīrǎng jí* [[KR5i0082]].
- [[朱熹]] (Zhū Xī, 1130–1200) — annotator of Zhōu Dūnyí's two works as preserved in [[KR5i0079]]–[[KR5i0080]] (canonical *jízhù*).
- [[諸葛亮]] (Zhūgě Liàng, 181–234) — Hàn-Sānguó statesman; corpus at [[KR5i0078]], included on the Daoist tradition's claim that he alone among Hàn figures grasped the *Yīnfú jīng*.
- [[諸葛羲]] (Zhūgě Xī) — Zhūgě Liàng's 36th-generation descendant; 1632 compiler of the *Wǔhóu jí*.
- [[張三丰]] (Zhāng Sānfēng) — legendary Wǔdāngshān patriarch; subject and attributed author of the *Zhāng Sānfēng xiānshēng quánjí* [[KR5i0071]].
- [[文昌帝君]] (Wénchāng dìjūn) — deified literature-god and principal *fújī* speaker of the Wénchāng cult dossier ([[KR5i0021]], [[KR5i0083]]–[[KR5i0084]], [[KR5i0086]], [[KR5i0087]]); the matrix of late-imperial *shànshū* moral-merit literature.
- [[關帝]] (Guāndì = Guānyǔ 關羽) — Hàn martial-loyalty figure deified as principal warrior-deity of the late-imperial state cult; principal speaker in [[KR5i0088]]–[[KR5i0089]].
- [[蔣國祚]] (Jiǎng Guózuò) — Jiǎng Yǔpǔ's elder kinsman; co-annotator of the *Gǎnyìngpiān* commentary [[KR5i0013]].
- [[朱珪]] (Zhū Guī, 1731–1807) — Jiā-qìng-era Grand Secretary; Jiǎng Yǔpǔ's principal political patron, named as 敬校 on [[KR5i0013]] and elsewhere — his patronage is the political circumstance under which the 1806 DZJY could be printed at all.
- [[彭定求]] (Péng Dìngqiú, 1645–1719) — Kāng-xī-era Hànlín scholar and pious Daoist layman; cited as re-printer on [[KR5i0014]].
- [[申兆定]] (Shēn Zhàodìng) — 1764 postface-writer of *Tiānxiān zhènglǐ zhílùn* [[KR5i0067]], providing the explicit 1622 → 1639 → 1669 → 1719 → 1764 print history of the Wǔ Liǔ corpus.
- [[陳致虛]] (Chén Zhìxū) — Yuán inner-alchemist; *Cāntóngqì fēnzhāngzhù* [[KR5i0038]].
- [[崔希範]] (Cuī Xīfàn, late Táng) — author of the foundational *Rùyàojìng* [[KR5i0039]].
- [[周玄貞]] (Zhōu Xuánzhēn) — late-Míng compiler of the Jade-Emperor scripture's principal collected-commentary tradition (cf. [[KR5h0009]]); contextual figure for the *Yùhuáng* cluster [[KR5i0015]]–[[KR5i0016]].

## Topics

- **Spirit-writing (*fújī* 扶乩 / *fùluán* 扶鸞) as canonization mechanism.** A defining feature of DZJY: a large bloc of the "Qīng" texts are planchette-revealed scriptures whose attributed speakers are deified Táng-Sòng patriarchs (Lǚ Dòngbīn, Xǔ Xùn, Zhāng Sānfēng, Zhōnglí Quán) speaking centuries after their natural lives. The mechanism is not occasional but the principal mode by which new scripture entered the late-imperial Daoist canon. The Wén-chāng-cult bloc [[KR5i0021]] and the *Chàngdào zhēnyán* [[KR5i0030]] are paradigm cases; the dating policy across the division — *terminus a quo* = consolidation of the planchette cult (post-1700), *terminus ad quem* = inclusion in DZJY (1809 or 1906) — directly reflects this.
- **The Lǚzǔ cult as editorial centre.** No other figure even approaches Lǚ Dòngbīn's dominance across DZJY's frontmatter. The Lǚ-zǔ-circle planchette altars of late-imperial Sìchuān, Jiāngnán, and the capital are the editorial matrix of the entire division; reading DZJY without that frame collapses much of the editorial logic.
- **Inner alchemy (*nèidān* 內丹) as central practice.** Where the Míng *Zhèngtǒng Dàozàng* gave roughly equal weight to ritual / liturgy and to inner cultivation, DZJY tilts decisively toward inner alchemy. The *Cāntóngqì* / *Wùzhēnpiān* commentarial tradition, the Wǔ Liǔ corpus, the women's-alchemy materials, and the *Jīnhuá* / *Tàiyǐ jīnhuá zōngzhǐ* compendia together form the doctrinal core, and the standard late-imperial *nèidān* curriculum *is* the DZJY *nèidān* curriculum.
- **Three-Teachings synthesis (*sānjiào héyī* 三教合一).** The most-distinctive editorial commitment: the Sòng Neo-Confucian classics ([[KR5i0079]]–[[KR5i0082]]) are canonized *as Daoist material*, with Zhū Xī as their annotator; Wǔ Shǒuyáng's [[KR5i0066]] uses Chán Buddhist *yuánjué* as a paired absolute with Daoist *xiāntiān yīqì*; and Zhūgě Liàng's loyalty-and-martial corpus [[KR5i0078]] is incorporated as Daoist ancestry. This is not an accident of bricolage but a programmatic claim.
- **The Quánzhēn / Lóngmén institutional turn and the 1656 Báiyúnguàn revival.** The precept-and-liturgy bloc ([[KR5i0090]]–[[KR5i0091]], [[KR5i0097]]–[[KR5i0099]]) anchors the division to the post-Wáng-Cháng-yuè Lóngmén institutional revival. Sīchuān (Èrxiānān, Qīngyánggōng, Qīngchéngshān, Zǐyángdòng) is the geographic centre, and the local-stele texts [[KR5i0100]], [[KR5i0101]], [[KR5i0103]] make DZJY itself a documentary witness to its own institutional home.
- **The popular-cult pantheon at the doorstep of the literati.** Wénchāng, Guāndì, the Jade-Emperor / 玉皇 cult, Dòumǔ, and the *Tàishàng gǎnyìng piān* moral-merit tradition all appear in DZJY in their state-of-the-art Qīng forms. The division is therefore a primary source for the religious culture of late-imperial gentry life, not only for monastic Daoism.
- **Editorial layering: YPP (1806/1809) vs. CK-KZ (1906).** Every per-text frontmatter records the base-edition tag. Reading the division by edition reveals two editorial moments separated by a century: Jiǎng Yǔpǔ's Beijing-court-supported selection of 1806/1809, vs. Hé Lóngxiāng's Chéngdū institutional re-engraving of 1906 (which absorbs the supplement-track materials including [[KR5i0104]], [[KR5i0107]]–[[KR5i0109]]). Most analytic claims about DZJY must be relativised to which stratum a text belongs in.
- **Women's alchemy as a recognised sub-tradition.** The *Sūn Bùèr* materials [[KR5i0063]]–[[KR5i0064]] and the late-Qīng *Nǚdān hébiān* [[KR5i0109]] establish *nǚdān* as a self-aware, lineage-documented sub-tradition with its own boundary-policing against "*yīnyáng* schools" of sexual yoga ([[KR5i0109]] *Pángmén lù* polemic).
- **The Sìchuān provenance of the received text.** The 1906 re-engraving was produced at Chéngdū institutions, and several DZJY-only items (the local-stele texts; the Sìchuān provincial-Daoist anthology [[KR5i0094]]) point unambiguously to a Sìchuān editorial home. The Beijing 1806 stratum and the Chéngdū 1906 stratum jointly define the geography of late-imperial Daoist institutional Daoism in the period that immediately preceded the early-Republican Daoist reform.
