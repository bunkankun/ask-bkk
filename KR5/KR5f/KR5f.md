# Tài qīng bù 太清部 — Great Clarity Section of the *Zhèng tǒng Dào zàng*

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR5f corresponds to the **Tài qīng bù** 太清部, the third of the **Four Supplements** (*sì fǔ* 四輔) that complete the **Three Caverns** (*sān dòng* 三洞) bibliographic frame of the *Zhèng tǒng Dào zàng* 正統道藏 (1445). Within the canon's textile of *Dòng zhēn* 洞真, *Dòng xuán* 洞玄, *Dòng shén* 洞神 — *Tài xuán* 太玄, *Tài píng* 太平, *Tài qīng* 太清, *Zhèng yī* 正一, the Tài qīng bù was originally intended as the supplement to the *Dòng shén* cavern, providing the **alchemical-scriptural corpus** of the Highest Clarity (*Tài qīng*) revelations of the third/fourth century — the foundational tradition of operative alchemy (*wài dān* 外丹) systematized in Gě Hóng's *Bào pǔ zǐ*. By the Míng compilation, however, the section had drifted into a **catch-all collection** for philosophical, military, technical, and ethical works that did not fit elsewhere in the canon's classificatory scheme — most notably a remarkable cluster of pre-Qín and Hàn masters whose principal manuscript witnesses were preserved here precisely because mainstream Confucian transmission lapsed. The 25 texts of KR5f (DZ 1167–1191) span roughly fifteen centuries, from late-Warring-States philosophical writings to late-Yuán neo-Confucian cosmology.

The classification reflects the long Daoist self-understanding as the canonical home of *all* indigenous Chinese learning marginalized by the Confucian curriculum: the canonical Mòhist text, the principal text of the School of Names, the major Legalist compendium, the chief Hàn syncretic encyclopaedia, the standard Hàn imitation of the *Yì*, the central Eastern-Jìn alchemical treatise, and the foundational *nèi dān* manual of the ZhōngLǚ lineage are all here, jostling with brief TángSòng meditation scriptures and Sòng pseudepigraphical military classics. The section thus functions less as a doctrinally coherent grouping than as a **bibliographic reservoir** — and for several of the works housed here (the *Mò zǐ* especially) the Daozang witness is the most important single textual source for the modern critical tradition.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The popular-morality founder.**

- *Tài shàng gǎn yìng piān* 太上感應篇 [[KR5f0001]] — the single most influential Chinese morality tract, opening the section as DZ 1167 and seeding the entire late-imperial **morality-ledger** (*gōng guò gé* 功過格) tradition.

**Pre-Qín and Hàn philosophical masters preserved here (a remarkable cluster).**

- *Mò zǐ* 墨子 [[KR5f0010]] — the foundational text of Mòhism, whose Daozang witness is the most important manuscript witness of the work for the modern critical tradition.
- *Hán fēi zǐ* 韓非子 [[KR5f0011]] — the great Warring-States Legalist synthesis.
- *Gōng sūn Lóng zǐ* 公孫龍子 [[KR5f0006]] — the foundational text of the *Míng jiā* 名家 (School of Names), source of the "white horse is not horse" paradox.
- *Yǐn wén zǐ* 尹文子 [[KR5f0007]] — companion text of the *Míng jiā* / HuángLǎo current.
- *Hé guān zǐ* 鶡冠子 [[KR5f0009]] — Warring-States / early-Hàn HuángLǎo political-strategic text, transmitted here with **Lù Diàn 陸佃's Northern-Sòng commentary**; the Mǎ wáng duī finds vindicated its authenticity against long-standing forgery charges.
- *Yù zǐ* 鬻子 [[KR5f0005]] — short pseudepigraphical text ascribed to the Zhōu counselor Yù Xióng; received recension Hàn–Six Dynasties.
- *Huái nán hóng liè jiě* 淮南鴻烈解 [[KR5f0018]] — the *Huái nán zǐ*, Liú Ān's great Hàn syncretic-encyclopaedic compendium, with the composite "Xǔ Shèn" commentary.
- *Jí zhù Tài xuán jīng* 集註太玄經 [[KR5f0017]] — Yáng Xióng's 81-tetragram Hàn imitation of the *Yì jīng*, with accumulated Hàn–Sòng commentaries.

**The Gě Hóng / Bào pǔ zǐ corpus** — the alchemical heart of the section.

- *Bào pǔ zǐ nèi piān* 抱朴子內篇 [[KR5f0019]] — Gě Hóng's foundational defence of operative alchemy, *shén xiān* 神仙, and esoteric practice; one of the most important works in the entire Daoist canon.
- *Bào pǔ zǐ wài piān* 抱朴子外篇 [[KR5f0021]] — the same author's 50-*juàn* Confucian-social complement.
- *Bào pǔ zǐ bié zhǐ* 抱朴子別旨 [[KR5f0020]] — a Táng supplementary text attached to the corpus.

**Inner alchemy** (*nèi dān*) **and esoterica.**

- *Mì chuán Zhèng yáng zhēn rén Líng bǎo bì fǎ* 祕傳正陽真人靈寶畢法 [[KR5f0025]] — the foundational Northern-Sòng *nèi dān* text of the ZhōngLǚ 鍾呂 transmission (Zhōnglí Quán → Lǚ Dòng bīn), and the concluding text of the entire Tài qīng bù (DZ 1191) — and thus of the Four Supplements.
- *Yīn dān nèi piān* 陰丹內篇 [[KR5f0023]] — short SòngYuán treatise on the *yīn* / passive aspect of the elixir work.
- *Tuó yuè zǐ* 橐籥子 [[KR5f0022]] — Sòng philosophical-cosmological treatise on the *Dào dé jīng*'s "bellows" image.

**Daoist devotional scriptures** (a small but doctrinally significant group).

- *Tài shàng Lǎo jūn zhōng jīng* 太上老君中經 [[KR5f0002]] — one of the earliest surviving Daoist meditation-scriptures; late Hàn; foundational for the Huáng tíng 黃庭 visualization tradition.
- *Tài shàng Lǎo jūn qīng jìng xīn jīng* 太上老君清靜心經 [[KR5f0003]] — TángSòng heart-scripture in the *Qīng jìng jīng* lineage.
- *Tài shàng Lǎo jūn shuō shàng qī miè zuì jí fú miào jīng* 太上老君說上七滅罪集福妙經 [[KR5f0004]] — Táng sin-confession and merit-accumulation scripture.
- *Tiān jī jīng* 天機經 [[KR5f0024]] — Táng esoteric-cosmological scripture.

**Sòng military classics in the Daozang.**

- *Sūn zǐ zhù jiě* 孫子註解 [[KR5f0014]] and *Sūn zǐ yí shuō* 孫子遺說 [[KR5f0015]] — two late-Northern-Sòng commentarial works on the *Sūn zǐ bīng fǎ*.
- *Huáng shí gōng sù shū* (兩本) [[KR5f0012]] / [[KR5f0013]] — two recensions of the Sòng pseudepigraphical *Sù shū* 素書 ascribed to the Hàn immortal Huáng shí gōng (legendary transmitter of strategy to Zhāng Liáng).

**SòngYuán cosmology.**

- *Tiān yuán fā wēi* 天原發微 [[KR5f0016]] — Bào Yúnlóng's late-Southern-Sòng / Yuán cosmological-diagrammatic synthesis on the *Yì* together with Shào Yōng 邵雍 and Zhū Xī 朱熹.
- *Zǐ huá zǐ* 子華子 [[KR5f0008]] — late-Northern-Sòng / early-Southern-Sòng pseudepigraphical philosophical miscellany ascribed to the Spring-and-Autumn figure Chéng Běn.

## Important persons

- [[葛洪]] (Gě Hóng, 283–343) — author of the entire *Bào pǔ zǐ* corpus and the central figure of the section; pivotal theorist of operative alchemy and Eastern-Jìn Daoist learning.
- [[揚雄]] (Yáng Xióng, 53 BCE – 18 CE) — Western-Hàn polymath; author of the *Tài xuán jīng* preserved here.
- [[許慎]] (Xǔ Shèn, d. *c.* 124) — Eastern-Hàn philologist (compiler of the *Shuō wén jiě zì*); the composite *Huái nán zǐ* commentary in this section is transmitted under his name.
- [[墨翟]] (Mò Dí, *c.* 470–391 BCE) — eponymous founder of Mòhism; the *Mò zǐ* preserved here is the most important manuscript witness for the modern textual tradition.
- [[韓非]] (Hán Fēi, *c.* 280–233 BCE) — synthesizer of Warring-States Legalism; his *Hán fēi zǐ* is in this section.
- [[公孫龍]] (Gōngsūn Lóng) — Warring-States School-of-Names logician; the *Gōng sūn Lóng zǐ* is preserved here.
- [[尹文]] (Yǐn Wén) — Warring-States School-of-Names / HuángLǎo figure; the *Yǐn wén zǐ* is here.
- [[鍾離權]] (Zhōnglí Quán, Zhèng yáng zhēn rén) — patriarch of the ZhōngLǚ inner-alchemical transmission; the *Líng bǎo bì fǎ* concluding the section is ascribed to him.
- [[李昌齡]] (Lǐ Chānglíng, 937–1008) — Northern-Sòng compiler / editor of the *Tài shàng gǎn yìng piān*.
- [[鮑雲龍]] (Bào Yúnlóng, 1226–1296) — late-Southern-Sòng / Yuán cosmologist; author of *Tiān yuán fā wēi*.
- [[陸佃]] (Lù Diàn, 1042–1102) — Northern-Sòng scholar (a student of Wáng Ān shí); his commentary on the *Hé guān zǐ* is the received transmission of that text.
- [[祝洞天]] (Zhù Dòngtiān) — Sòng author of *Tuó yuè zǐ*; representative of Sòng philosophical Daoism.

## Topics

- **The Daoist canon as bibliographic refuge.** The Tài qīng bù is the principal site of the well-known phenomenon by which texts marginalized in the Confucian curriculum — Mòhism, the School of Names, Legalism, the HuángLǎo current — were preserved through Daoist transmission. The modern critical text of the *Mò zǐ* depends decisively on the Daozang witness, and several of the smaller masters (*Hé guān zǐ*, *Yǐn wén zǐ*, *Gōng sūn Lóng zǐ*) would be far more attenuated without it.
- **The Tài qīng alchemical lineage.** The section's nominal subject — the *Tài qīng* scriptural revelation of operative alchemy (third/fourth century) — is represented above all by the *Bào pǔ zǐ* trilogy. The deeper history is the trajectory from **operative alchemy** (*wài dān* 外丹, elixir-compounding) of the Six Dynasties to the **inner alchemy** (*nèi dān* 內丹) systematization of the Sòng, traced here from Gě Hóng [[KR5f0019]] through to the ZhōngLǚ *Líng bǎo bì fǎ* [[KR5f0025]].
- **The morality-book revolution.** The opening text — *Tài shàng gǎn yìng piān* [[KR5f0001]] — inaugurated a new genre of Daoist-flavoured popular ethics that would dominate late-imperial religious-moral practice through the morality-ledger (*gōng guò gé* 功過格) tradition and shape MíngQīng popular religion across denominational lines.
- **Pseudepigraphy in the section.** A striking proportion of the texts are pseudepigraphical attributions to figures of high antiquity — *Yù zǐ* to Yù Xióng of the Zhōu, *Zǐ huá zǐ* to Chéng Běn of the Spring-and-Autumn, *Huáng shí gōng sù shū* to the Hàn immortal Huáng shí gōng, *Líng bǎo bì fǎ* to Zhōnglí Quán of the Hàn — typically masking Sòng compilation. The dating policy in this division consistently follows the **received recension** rather than the legendary ascription.
- **Hàn syncretism and its Sòng inheritors.** *Huái nán zǐ*, *Tài xuán jīng*, and the Mòhist / Míngjiā / HuángLǎo texts represent the Hàn syncretic moment; the Sòng texts of the section (*Hé guān zǐ* commentary, *Sù shū*, *Sūn zǐ* commentaries, *Tiān yuán fā wēi*) represent successive re-readings of that inheritance from the standpoint of Sòng neo-Confucian and Daoist cosmology.
