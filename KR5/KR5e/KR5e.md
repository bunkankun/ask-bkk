# KR5e 太平部 *Tàipíng bù* — Daoist Canon → Great Peace

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR5e 太平部 (*Tàipíng bù*, "Great Peace") collects 68 works (DZ 1101 – DZ 1166 in the Schipper / Verellen *Concordance du Tao-tsang* numbering, with DZ 1101 split into the three subitems 1101a/b/c that yield [[KR5e0001]] – [[KR5e0003]]) — the second of the *sì fǔ* 四輔 ("Four Supplements") of the *Zhèngtǒng Dàozàng* 正統道藏 (1445), which sit immediately after the *sāndòng* 三洞 ("Three Caverns" — [[KR5a]] 洞真, [[KR5b]] 洞玄, [[KR5c]] 洞神) and the first supplement [[KR5d]] 太玄部. Wilkinson §29.4.4 records the canon's tripartite-with-supplements structure but flags the by-now familiar dislocation: the three Caverns are *schools*, the twelve sections 十二類 (本文, 神符, 玉訣, 靈圖, 譜錄, 戒律, 威儀, 方法, 衆術, 記傳, 讚頌, 表奏) are a cross-cutting *genre* axis, and the four supplements were classically meant to attach to one Cavern apiece — *Tàixuán* to 洞真 / Shàngqīng, *Tàipíng* to 洞玄 / Língbǎo, *Tàiqīng* to 洞神 / Sānhuáng-cum-Lǎozǐ, and *Zhèngyī* across all three (Schipper & Verellen, *Taoist Canon* 1: 14–22, 30–40). In the *Tàipíng bù* the school-supplement scheme is visible only in the surviving openings: the section is named after, and opens with, the *Tàipíng jīng* 太平經 [[KR5e0002]] — the foundational scripture of the late-Hàn *Tài píng dào* 太平道 / Yellow Turban movement, traditionally taken as the "Língbǎo *ante litteram*" by the canon-editors — and what follows is *de facto* an archive of whatever the Sòng and Míng compilers could not house in [[KR5a]] / [[KR5b]] / [[KR5c]] / [[KR5d]] but judged canonical.

The actual contents fall into six unequal strata: (i) the **Tàipíng jīng nucleus** that gives the section its name (4 titles, [[KR5e0001]] – [[KR5e0004]]: the Táng epitome *Tàipíng jīng chāo*, the fragmentary main scripture, the preface *Fù wén xù*, and the Táng digest *Shèng jūn mì zhǐ*); (ii) the **Jìngmíng 淨明 revelation corpus** (8 titles, [[KR5e0005]] – [[KR5e0012]]) — the scriptural and ritual archive of the Sòng-Yuán *Jìngmíng zhōngxiào dào* 淨明忠孝道 movement centred on the cult of [[許遜]] Xǔ Xùn at Xīshān 西山 (Jiāngxī), culminating in [[黃元吉]]'s 1327 *Jìngmíng zhōngxiào quánshū* [[KR5e0012]]; (iii) a substantial **Six-Dynasties – Táng doctrinal and systematic-theology block** ([[KR5e0013]] – [[KR5e0041]]) that holds the major Táng *Chóngxuán* 重玄 ("Twofold Mystery") scripture *Běn jì jīng* 本際經 [[KR5e0013]], the foundational Daoist apologetic / systematic manuals *Dàomén jīng fǎ xiāng chéng cì xù* [[KR5e0030]] by [[潘師正]] (Pān Shīzhèng), *Dàojiào yì shū* [[KR5e0031]] by [[孟安排]] (Mèng Ānpái), and *Dòngxuán Língbǎo xuán mén dà yì* [[KR5e0026]], and the great early-medieval **Daoist encyclopedias** *Wúshàng bìyào* 無上祕要 [[KR5e0040]] (compiled under Běi-Zhōu imperial sponsorship c. 577–589) and *Sāndòng zhū náng* 三洞珠囊 [[KR5e0041]] by [[王懸河]] (Wáng Xuánhé) — by far the densest concentration of pre-Quánzhēn systematic Daoist literature anywhere in the canon; (iv) [[羅隱]]'s late-Táng political-Daoist treatise *Tàipíng liǎng tóng shū* [[KR5e0037]], inserted into the section on the strength of its title alone; (v) the **Quánzhēn 全真 literary archive** (22 titles, [[KR5e0042]] – [[KR5e0063]]) — the largest single deposit of the Wáng Chóngyáng-Seven Patriarchs *yǔlù* 語錄 and *shī cí* 詩詞 corpus in the *Dàozàng*, holding the *Chóngyáng quánzhēn jí* [[KR5e0055]] and three further Wáng Chóngyáng anthologies, [[馬鈺]] (Mǎ Yù / Dānyángzǐ)'s *Jiànwù jí* [[KR5e0044]] / *Dòngxuán jīnyù jí* [[KR5e0051]] / *Dānyáng shén guāng càn* [[KR5e0052]], and the collected works of [[丘處機]], [[譚處端]], [[王處一]], [[郝大通]], [[劉處玄]], and [[尹志平]]; (vi) a small but consequential **Daoist medical block** ([[KR5e0064]] – [[KR5e0067]]) anchored by [[孫思邈]] (Sūn Sīmiǎo)'s *Bèijí qiānjīn yàofāng* 備急千金要方 [[KR5e0065]]; and (vii) the closing early-Míng Thunder-Rites ritual compendium *Fǎ hǎi yí zhū* 法海遺珠 [[KR5e0068]].

The chronological span runs from the Eastern-Hàn core of the *Tàipíng jīng* itself (c. 100–250, [[KR5e0002]]) through to the early-Míng *Jíjiù xiān fāng* 急救仙方 [[KR5e0066]] and *Fǎ hǎi yí zhū* [[KR5e0068]] (both c. 1444–1445, finished just before the *Zhèngtǒng Dàozàng* itself was printed). Distribution by composition midpoint is sharply bimodal: a Hàn–Six-Dynasties nucleus dominated by the *Tàipíng jīng* and a cluster of Liùcháo Língbǎo scriptures ([[KR5e0016]] [[KR5e0017]] [[KR5e0020]] [[KR5e0021]] [[KR5e0022]]) plus the *Wúshàng bìyào* (577–589); a dense Táng peak (c. 30 titles, c. 600–900) carrying the *Chóngxuán*-philosophical scriptures, the Táng systematic-theology manuals, the great encyclopedias, and the *Qiānjīn yàofāng*; a Sòng-Yuán surge (c. 30 titles, c. 1100–1330) dominated by two distinct currents — the **Jìngmíng** scriptures and the **Quánzhēn** *yǔlù*/poetry corpus that documents the founding generation and immediate successors of [[王嚞]] Wáng Chóngyáng (Wáng Zhé, 1113–1170); and a sparse early-Míng tail terminating with the *Fǎ hǎi yí zhū*. The section is therefore best read not as a coherent textual school but as the *Dàozàng*'s **Yellow-Turban / Jìngmíng / Quánzhēn overflow basin** — bracketed at one end by the foundational late-Hàn revelatory text that gives the supplement its name, at the other by the early-Míng Thunder-Rites archive.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The *Tàipíng jīng* nucleus — the section's etymological anchor.**
- *Tài píng jīng* 太平經 [[KR5e0002]] — anonymous, Eastern-Hàn core (2nd c.) with extensive Six-Dynasties expansion. **One of the foundational scriptures of Chinese Daoism**, the cornerstone text of the earliest organized Daoist movement, traditionally associated with the Yellow Turban / Five-Pecks-of-Rice context and extensively quoted in the *Hòu Hàn shū* as the doctrinal source of the *Tài píng dào* 太平道 of Zhāng Jué 張角 (184 CE rebellion). The received text is defective: 57 *juàn* survive of an original 170. Doctrinal core: *tàipíng* 太平 (a utopian millennial socio-cosmic order), *chéng fù* 承負 (inherited moral-cosmic burden across generations), *sān qì* 三氣 (the three cosmic pneumas), and a prototype of Daoist ritual confession.
- *Tài píng jīng chāo* 太平經鈔 [[KR5e0001]] — a Táng 10-*juàn* abridgement organized by the sexagenary 甲乙丙丁戊己庚辛壬癸 cycle, preserving in condensed form much of the scripture's content now lost in the main copy. Together with [[KR5e0003]] *Fù wén xù* (the preface) and [[KR5e0004]] *Shèng jūn mì zhǐ* (a Táng doctrinal digest), the three DZ 1101 a/b/c items and DZ 1102 constitute the **entire surviving transmission of the Tàipíng-corpus** in the *Dàozàng*.

**The Jìngmíng 淨明 revelation cluster.** Eight scriptures and one anthology ([[KR5e0005]] – [[KR5e0012]]) documenting the **Jìngmíng zhōngxiào dào** 淨明忠孝道 ("Way of Pure Illumination, Loyalty, and Filiality"), a Daoist movement centred on the cult of [[許遜]] Xǔ Xùn (the deified third-century Jìn official) at Xīshān 西山 (Jiāngxī), reaching mature form in the 12th–14th centuries. The cluster's distinctive theology is a **Confucian-Daoist synthesis** in which *zhōngxiào* 忠孝 (loyalty and filiality) is the path to *jìngmíng* 淨明 (pure illumination).
- *Tài shàng Língbǎo Jìngmíng dòngshén shàng pǐn jīng* [[KR5e0005]] — the opening scripture of the cluster, defining its cosmological-doctrinal frame.
- The *Yùzhēn shū / Dào yuán zhèng yìn / Tiān zūn shuō yù wēn / Shǒu rù sì guī / Jiǔ xiān shuǐ / Zhōng huáng bā zhù* scriptures [[KR5e0006]] – [[KR5e0011]] — the supporting Sòng-Yuán Jìngmíng scriptural archive.
- *Jìngmíng zhōngxiào quánshū* 淨明忠孝全書 [[KR5e0012]] — compiled by [[黃元吉]] Huáng Yuánjí (1270–1324) with an introduction dated 1327. **The foundational compilation of the Yuán-period Jìngmíng school**, the authoritative anthology of scripture, ritual, doctrine, and hagiography around the Xǔ Xùn cult.

**The *Běn jì jīng* and the Táng *Chóngxuán* 重玄 ("Twofold Mystery") current.**
- *Tài xuán zhēn yī běn jì miào jīng* 太玄真一本際妙經 [[KR5e0013]] — anonymous, early Táng (c. 600–630), 10 *juàn*. **The single most influential Táng Daoist philosophical-soteriological scripture**, articulating the *Chóngxuán* metaphysics through dialogues between Tàishàng Lǎojūn 太上老君 and a celestial retinue. Extensively quoted in Táng canonical compilations; substantially recovered from Dūnhuáng 敦煌 manuscripts (parts of the original were lost from the *Dàozàng* copy).

**The Táng systematic-theology / apologetic manuals.** The densest concentration of Daoist *yìxué* 義學 outside [[KR5a]] in the canon.
- *Lù xiānshēng dào mén kē lüè* 陸先生道門科略 [[KR5e0029]] — [[陸修靜]] Lù Xiūjìng (406–477). The earliest text in this cluster, by the great Liùcháo Daoist liturgist who first systematized the *Dàozàng* into the *sāndòng* scheme.
- *Dào mén jīng fǎ xiāng chéng cì xù* 道門經法相承次序 [[KR5e0030]] — [[潘師正]] Pān Shīzhèng (Táng, 680–684). Written for emperor Gāozōng 高宗.
- *Dào jiào yì shū* 道教義樞 [[KR5e0031]] — [[孟安排]] Mèng Ānpái (Táng, c. 680–710). Systematic 10-*juàn* manual of Daoist doctrine; partly digests the earlier *Dòngxuán Língbǎo xuán mén dà yì* [[KR5e0026]].
- *Dòngxuán Língbǎo sāndòng fèngdào kē jiè yíng shǐ* 洞玄靈寶三洞奉道科戒營始 [[KR5e0027]] — attributed to [[金明七真]] Jīnmíng-Qīzhēn (early Táng). **The foundational regulatory-liturgical compendium of Táng Daoist monasticism** (translated by Kohn, *The Daoist Monastic Manual*, OUP 2004).
- *Dào diǎn lùn* [[KR5e0032]], *Tài shàng miào fǎ běn xiāng jīng* [[KR5e0033]], *Shàng qīng dào lèi shì xiāng* [[KR5e0034]] by [[王懸河]] Wáng Xuánhé, *Dòngxuán Língbǎo zuǒ xuán lùn* [[KR5e0038]] — further Táng doctrinal treatises.
- *Yī qiè dào jīng yīn yì miào mén yóu qǐ* 一切道經音義妙門由起 [[KR5e0025]] — [[史崇]] Shǐ Chóng et al., 712–713. Compiled at the order of Táng Ruìzōng 睿宗; the philological-bibliographic preface of an unrealized Táng *Dàozàng* compilation.

**The great early-medieval Daoist encyclopedias.** The two densest reference works in the section.
- *Wúshàng bìyào* 無上祕要 [[KR5e0040]] — anonymous, Běi-Zhōu, c. 577–589. **The earliest substantial Daoist encyclopedia**, compiled under imperial sponsorship in 100 *juàn* (about a third has survived); the principal source for reconstructing the state of pre-Táng Daoist scripture.
- *Sāndòng zhū náng* 三洞珠囊 [[KR5e0041]] — [[王懸河]] Wáng Xuánhé, Táng (c. 680–710), 10 *juàn*. **The principal Táng-era topical anthology of Daoist scripture**, organized by doctrinal headings and quoting extensively from sources now lost.

**[[羅隱]]'s political-Daoist treatise.** *Tài píng liǎng tóng shū* 太平兩同書 [[KR5e0037]] — Luó Yǐn (833–909), late Táng. A two-*juàn* political treatise on the unification of Confucian and Daoist statecraft, included in the section on the strength of its *tàipíng* title and its placement in the *zhūzǐ* 諸子 (masters) tradition Luó Yǐn worked within.

**The Quánzhēn 全真 literary archive — the largest single Wáng Chóngyáng-and-disciples deposit in the *Dàozàng*.** Twenty-two collections spanning four generations of Northern-Lineage Quánzhēn 全真 Daoism.
- *Founding patriarch — [[王嚞]] Wáng Zhé (Wáng Chóngyáng, 1113–1170)*: *Chóng yáng quánzhēn jí* 重陽全真集 [[KR5e0055]] (his principal *shī cí* anthology), *Chóng yáng jiào huà jí* 重陽教化集 [[KR5e0056]], *Chóng yáng fēn lí shí huà jí* 重陽分梨十化集 [[KR5e0057]] (recording the ten allegorical "pear-dividing" exchanges with Mǎ Yù), and *Chóng yáng zhēn rén jīn guān yù suǒ jué* 重陽真人金關玉鎖訣 [[KR5e0058]] (a practical *neidān* instruction set). [[KR5e0060]] *Chóng yáng zhēn rén shòu Dān yáng èr shí sì jué* preserves twenty-four instructions Wáng Chóngyáng transmitted to Mǎ Dānyáng.
- *The Seven Patriarchs (七真) — first generation of disciples*: [[馬鈺]] Mǎ Yù (Mǎ Dānyáng, 1123–1184) — *Jiànwù jí* 漸悟集 [[KR5e0044]], *Dòngxuán jīn yù jí* 洞玄金玉集 [[KR5e0051]], *Dānyáng shén guāng càn* 丹陽神光燦 [[KR5e0052]] (1175). [[譚處端]] Tán Chùduān (1123–1185) — *Shuǐ yún jí* 水雲集 [[KR5e0062]]. [[劉處玄]] Liú Chùxuán (1147–1203) — *Xiān lè jí* 仙樂集 [[KR5e0043]]. [[丘處機]] Qiū Chùjī (1148–1227) — *Pán xī jí* 磻溪集 [[KR5e0061]] (his major collection from the years before the audience with Činggis Qaγan). [[王處一]] Wáng Chùyī (1142–1217) — *Yún guāng jí* 雲光集 [[KR5e0054]]. [[郝大通]] Hǎo Dàtōng (1140–1212) — *Tài gǔ jí* 太古集 [[KR5e0063]].
- *Second and third generations*: [[尹志平]] Yǐn Zhìpíng (1169–1251) — *Bǎo guāng jí* 葆光集 [[KR5e0048]]. [[姬志真]] Jī Zhìzhēn (Yuán) — *Yún shān jí* 雲山集 [[KR5e0042]] (1265). [[郭志誠]] Guō Zhìchéng (Yuán) — *Xī yún jí* 西雲集 [[KR5e0049]]. Plus the further Quánzhēn-affiliated collections *Cǎo táng jí* by [[王丹桂]] [[KR5e0045]], *Xuán xū zǐ míng zhēn jí* by [[玄沖師]] [[KR5e0047]], *Zì rán jí* [[KR5e0046]], *Wù zhēn jí* [[KR5e0053]], and the Southern-Sòng *Wù zhāi xiān sheng wén jí* of [[楊至質]] [[KR5e0050]].
- *Pre-Quánzhēn alchemical instruction*: *Mǎ Zì rán jīn dān kǒu jué* 馬自然金丹口訣 [[KR5e0059]] — [[馬自然]] (Northern Sòng).

**The Daoist-affiliated medical block.**
- *Sūn zhēnrén bèijí qiānjīn yàofāng* 孫真人備急千金要方 [[KR5e0065]] — [[孫思邈]] Sūn Sīmiǎo (early-Táng), composed c. 650, revised by Sòng editors in the 11th century (the *Dàozàng* copy follows the Sòng recension in 93 *juàn*). **The foundational clinical encyclopedia of the Táng**: diagnostics, obstetrics-paediatrics, internal medicine, acupuncture, materia medica, and emergency treatment, anchored by Sūn's dictum that human life is worth more than a thousand pieces of gold. The matching index [[KR5e0064]] is a Sòng-era *mùlù* to the same work.
- *Xiān chuán wài kē mì fāng* 仙傳外科祕方 [[KR5e0067]] — [[趙宜真]] Zhào Yízhēn (Yuán–early Míng, d. 1382). A Daoist-affiliated surgical and external-medicine manual.
- *Jí jiù xiān fāng* 急救仙方 [[KR5e0066]] — anonymous early Míng (c. 1444–1445), an emergency-prescription manual.

**The closing Thunder-Rites compendium.** *Fǎ hǎi yí zhū* 法海遺珠 [[KR5e0068]] — anonymous, early Míng (by 1445), 46 *juàn*. **The concluding text of the *Tàipíng bù***: a major ritual compendium collecting Daoist practices of the *léi fǎ* 雷法 (Thunder Rites) tradition and allied currents (the Tiānxīn 天心, Shénxiāo 神霄, and Qīngwéi 清微 ritual lineages).

## Important persons

- [[陸修靜]] (Lù Xiūjìng, 406–477) — the Liùcháo Daoist liturgist whose codification of the *sāndòng* scheme is the structural ancestor of the entire *Dàozàng*; here for *Lù xiānshēng dào mén kē lüè* [[KR5e0029]].
- [[孫思邈]] (Sūn Sīmiǎo, early Táng) — the *yào wáng* 藥王 ("King of Medicine"), author of *Bèijí qiānjīn yàofāng* [[KR5e0065]]; canonized into the *Dàozàng* as a Daoist *zhēnrén* on the strength of his self-cultivation profile.
- [[潘師正]] (Pān Shīzhèng, 583–682) — Shàngqīng patriarch and tutor to Táng Gāozōng 高宗; author of *Dàomén jīng fǎ xiāng chéng cì xù* [[KR5e0030]].
- [[孟安排]] (Mèng Ānpái, fl. c. 700) — compiler of the foundational Daoist systematic-theology manual *Dào jiào yì shū* [[KR5e0031]].
- [[王懸河]] (Wáng Xuánhé, late 7th – early 8th c.) — Táng Daoist priest at Chéngdū 成都; compiler of two of the section's principal reference works, the topical encyclopedia *Sāndòng zhū náng* [[KR5e0041]] and the doctrinal repertory *Shàng qīng dào lèi shì xiāng* [[KR5e0034]].
- [[史崇]] (Shǐ Chóng, fl. 712–713) — editor of *Yī qiè dào jīng yīn yì miào mén yóu qǐ* [[KR5e0025]], commissioned by Táng Ruìzōng.
- [[羅隱]] (Luó Yǐn, 833–909) — late-Táng Hángzhōu literatus, author of *Tài píng liǎng tóng shū* [[KR5e0037]] — the single literati-political text in the section.
- [[許遜]] (Xǔ Xùn) — the deified Jìn-period (3rd–4th c.) administrator at the cultic centre of the Sòng-Yuán Jìngmíng movement; not the author of anything in this section, but the iconographic and ritual anchor of the entire Jìngmíng cluster [[KR5e0005]] – [[KR5e0012]].
- [[黃元吉]] (Huáng Yuánjí, 1270–1324) — Yuán Jìngmíng patriarch and compiler of the school's foundational anthology *Jìngmíng zhōngxiào quánshū* [[KR5e0012]].
- [[王嚞]] (Wáng Zhé, Wáng Chóngyáng, 1113–1170) — founder of Quánzhēn 全真 Daoism, the dominant Daoist movement of the post-Sòng period; four of his anthologies are preserved here ([[KR5e0055]] – [[KR5e0058]]) plus a fifth disciple-transmission record [[KR5e0060]].
- [[馬鈺]] (Mǎ Yù / Mǎ Dānyáng, 1123–1184) — senior of the Quánzhēn Seven Patriarchs, husband of Sūn Bù'èr 孫不二; three substantial collections [[KR5e0044]] [[KR5e0051]] [[KR5e0052]] — the largest single-author Quánzhēn deposit in the section.
- [[丘處機]] (Qiū Chùjī, 1148–1227) — Quánzhēn patriarch later summoned in 1219 by Činggis Qaγan to Central Asia; his *Pán xī jí* [[KR5e0061]] documents the pre-1219 Shǎnxī phase of his career.
- [[王處一]] (Wáng Chùyī, 1142–1217), [[譚處端]] (Tán Chùduān, 1123–1185), [[郝大通]] (Hǎo Dàtōng, 1140–1212), [[劉處玄]] (Liú Chùxuán, 1147–1203) — further Seven-Patriarch Quánzhēn masters, each represented by one or two collections.
- [[尹志平]] (Yǐn Zhìpíng, 1169–1251) — Quánzhēn third-generation patriarch and Qiū Chùjī's principal successor; author of *Bǎo guāng jí* [[KR5e0048]].
- [[趙宜真]] (Zhào Yízhēn, d. 1382) — Yuán–Míng Jìngmíng-affiliated Daoist physician; author of *Xiān chuán wài kē mì fāng* [[KR5e0067]].
- [[侯善淵]] (Hóu Shànyuān, Jīn) — Northern-school Daoist commentator and exegete; author of *Shàng qīng tài xuán jiàn jiè lùn* [[KR5e0039]].

## Topics

- **The *Tàipíng* utopia and its afterlife.** *Tàipíng* 太平 (Great Peace) as the foundational Daoist conception of a millennial socio-cosmic order under a sage-ruler, registered in the *Tàipíng jīng* cluster and replayed in muted form in [[羅隱]]'s *Tàipíng liǎng tóng shū* [[KR5e0037]] (a Confucian-Daoist political treatise) and in the *Shàng fāng Língbǎo wú jí zhì dào kāi huà zhēn jīng* [[KR5e0035]] / *Shàng fāng jūn tiān yǎn fàn zhēn jīng* [[KR5e0036]] pair. The *Tàipíng* concept is the section's nominal organizing principle, even where the actual contents have drifted far away from it.
- **The *Tàipíng jīng* and the origins of organized Daoism.** The text's role as the doctrinal source of the late-Hàn *Tài píng dào* 太平道 of Zhāng Jué 張角 and its 184 CE Yellow Turban rebellion remains the principal question driving modern study of the scripture (Hendrischke 2006). The Hàn core / Six-Dynasties expansion / Táng epitome layering is the textual problem.
- **Chóngxuán 重玄 ("Twofold Mystery") Daoist philosophy.** A Táng metaphysical-soteriological current that re-reads the *Lǎozǐ* through *Mádhyamaka*-influenced double-negation logic; the *Běn jì jīng* [[KR5e0013]] is the *Tàipíng bù*'s principal scripture in this current, complemented by the systematic-theology manuals of [[孟安排]] and [[潘師正]] (Assandri 2009).
- **Daoist systematic theology and the codification of *yìxué* 義學.** The *Dào jiào yì shū* [[KR5e0031]], *Dòngxuán Língbǎo xuán mén dà yì* [[KR5e0026]], and *Dào diǎn lùn* [[KR5e0032]] together constitute the Táng project of building a Daoist counterpart to Buddhist *abhidharma*-style systematic doctrine, in conscious response to the structural sophistication of Táng Buddhist scholasticism.
- **Daoist monasticism in the Táng.** The *Fèng dào kē jiè yíng shǐ* [[KR5e0027]] and *Dào xué kē yí* [[KR5e0028]] are the principal sources for reconstructing the institutional shape of Táng Daoist monasteries — refectory rules, ordination steps, the ritual day, the textual curriculum (Kohn 2003, 2004).
- **The early Daoist encyclopedic tradition.** *Wúshàng bìyào* (Běi Zhōu) [[KR5e0040]] and *Sāndòng zhū náng* (Táng) [[KR5e0041]] together hold the bulk of the *Dàozàng*'s pre-Song reference-encyclopedic literature; both quote extensively from scriptures since lost, and are therefore indispensable for any reconstruction of pre-Táng Daoism.
- **The Jìngmíng 淨明 Confucian-Daoist synthesis.** The Sòng-Yuán Jìngmíng movement's distinctive blending of Confucian *zhōngxiào* 忠孝 (loyalty, filiality) with Daoist *jìngmíng* 淨明 (pure illumination), in the cultic frame of the [[許遜]] Xīshān tradition (Akizuki 1978; Boltz 1987).
- **The Quánzhēn 全真 literary self-documentation.** The single largest topic in the section by volume. The Wáng Chóngyáng / Seven Patriarchs *shī cí* anthologies and *yǔlù* records are the principal primary sources for the rise of Quánzhēn Daoism in Jīn-period northern China and its consolidation under Mongol patronage; they document the *neidān* 內丹 (internal alchemy) practice tradition, the eremitic-yet-evangelical social style, and the lyric-poetry idiom Quánzhēn adopted for popular preaching (Marsone 2010; Goossaert 2001).
- **Daoism and medicine.** The *Bèijí qiānjīn yàofāng* [[KR5e0065]] and the *Xiān chuán wài kē mì fāng* [[KR5e0067]] register the long-running convergence of Daoist self-cultivation, alchemy, and clinical medicine — a convergence already visible in [[孫思邈]]'s reputation as both *yào wáng* and Daoist *zhēnrén* (Sivin 1968, 1995; Unschuld 1986).
- ***Léi fǎ* 雷法 Thunder Rites.** The closing *Fǎ hǎi yí zhū* [[KR5e0068]] documents the synthesis of the major Sòng-Yuán new-ritual lineages (Tiānxīn 天心, Shénxiāo 神霄, Qīngwéi 清微) into the early-Míng exorcistic-thunder repertoire that defined late-imperial Daoist ritual practice (Skar 2000; Boltz 1987).
