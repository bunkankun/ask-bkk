# Huánglù jiǔyáng fànqì dēngyí 黃籙九陽梵炁燈儀

*Yellow Register Lamp Ritual for the Brahman Energies of the Nine Yáng*

Anonymous Sòng–Yuán Daoist *dēngyí* 燈儀, eleven folios, preserved in the *Zhèngtǒng Dàozàng* 正統道藏 (DZ 0211 / CT 211 = TC 210), 洞真部 威儀類, in the same Dàozàng fascicle as *[[KR5a0213|DZ 212 Huánglù jiǔzhī dēngyí]]*.

## About the work

An eleven-folio *Huánglù* 黃籙 (Yellow Register) lamp-liturgy for the "Brahman Energies of the Nine Yáng" (*Jiǔyáng fànqì* 九陽梵氣), understood here as the *qì* of the Nine Heavens, which illuminate the underworld and enable the souls of the deceased to ascend. The rite invokes a suite of presiding deities — Tàiyǐ jiùkǔ tiānzūn 太乙救苦天尊, the Jiǔtiān shēngshén shàngdì 九天生神上帝, the Jiǔyáng fànqì tiānjūn 九陽梵炁天君, the *jiǔzhōu shèlìng* 九州社令 territorial deities, and the full complement of underworld officials of the Nine Depths (*Jiǔyōu dìyù* 九幽地獄) — then, at each of the nine cardinal stations, summons the *Jiùkǔ tiānzūn* 救苦天尊 of that direction, lights a lamp, and chants the corresponding hymn drawn from *[[KR5a0318|DZ 318 Dòngxuán língbǎo zìrán jiǔtiān shēngshén zhāngjīng]]*.

### Prefaces

*No separate preface in the source; the opening invocation serves as the introduction.* The text opens: "Burning incense in offering to Tàiyǐ jiùkǔ tiānzūn; the Jiǔtiān shēngshén shàngdì; the Jiǔyáng fànqì tiānjūn; the lords of the nine province-soils; all the underworld officials and administrators of the Nine Depths; and the entire holy assembly. Reverently: the primordial origin is the source, where the three *qì* are stored in the Great Beginning; the numinous light contains its radiance, and spreads the nine lights through the roof of the sky. The great mass once divided, the Primordial Beginning unfolded the chart; divine substance beneficently illumined the *qì* that divides the netted realms, and grace flowed down to the earth. The *fǎ* arose to deliver men…"

### Abstract

John Lagerwey, in Schipper & Verellen eds., *The Taoist Canon* (2004) 2:968–969 (§3.B.1, Zhèngyī), glosses the "Nine Yáng Brahman Energies" as the *qì* of the Nine Heavens, held to light the underworld and enable the ascent of the souls of the dead. The liturgical skeleton consists of directional invocations to the *Jiǔkǔ tiānzūn* with lamp-lightings, each followed by the appropriate hymn drawn from [[KR5a0318|DZ 318]]. The frontmatter brackets composition broadly 1100–1300, within the normal Sòng-era *Huánglù* ritual cycle.
