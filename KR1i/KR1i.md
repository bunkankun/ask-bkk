# Yuè lèi 樂類 — Classics: Music

## Scope and scholarly tradition

The *Yuè lèi* 樂類 sits inside the *Jīng-bù* 經部 (Classics) as the systematic literature on ritual music — pitch-pipe theory (*lǜ-lǚ* 律呂), instrument construction, modal practice, court song repertoire, and the philological and metaphysical commentary tradition that grew up around them. It is, by Sìkù convention, the smallest of the seven *jīng-bù* subdivisions, and its scholarly horizon is conditioned by a peculiar absence: as Wilkinson notes (§34.2), of the original Six Classics it was the *Yuè* 樂 itself that the Hàn "mislaid," with the consequence that the entire premodern Chinese music classic-tradition is, in effect, an ongoing reconstructive enterprise. Authors work from surviving fragments embedded in the *Lǐ jì Yuè jì* 禮記·樂記, the music monographs of the standard histories ("seventeen of the *Histories* have treatises on music and seven on tuning standards" — Wilkinson §34.2), the music chapters of the encyclopaedic *lèi-shū*, and the live tradition of imperial *yǎ-yuè* 雅樂 ceremony. The Sìkù compilers therefore treat the genre as canon-adjacent rather than canon-internal: the works gathered here are the *jīng-zhuàn* 經傳 ("classic-and-commentary") of a classic that was never in fact transmitted.

The corpus collected at WYG runs from Northern Sòng (1053) to mid-Qián-lóng (mid-18th century), 22 works in all (19 currently cataloged). Three constants run across the seven centuries: (1) repeated imperial commissions to standardize the *zhōng-lǜ* 鐘律 system for court ritual — the Sòng *Huáng-yòu* reform, the Kāngxī *Lǜ-lǚ zhèng-yì* and its sequels; (2) the Cài-Zhū *xiàng-shù* 象數 tradition descended from Cài Yuán-dìng 蔡元定 ([[KR1i0003]]), which gave the genre its standard mathematical idiom; and (3) the polemical drive — most visible in [[KR1i0009]] *Yuè-lǜ quán shū* and the late-Qīng *kǎo-zhèng* treatises — to anchor music theory in observable measurement rather than speculative number, culminating in Zhū Zàiyù's mathematically explicit equal temperament (Wilkinson §34.2.3).

## Important texts and text clusters

**The Northern Sòng imperial monuments.** Two works survive complete from the official Sòng music reforms.
- *Huáng-yòu xīn yuè tú jì* 皇祐新樂圖記 [[KR1i0001]] — the illustrated official record of the 1053 *zhōng-lǜ* recension under Sòng Rénzōng, the foundational Northern-Sòng artefact of imperial *yuè-lǜ* design.
- *Yuèshū* 樂書 [[KR1i0002]] — Chén Yáng's 200-juan encyclopaedic treatise (presented 1101–1106), the largest single work in the division and the principal anthology of music passages from the Three Rites and the rest of the canon.

**The Cài-Zhū *Dàoxué* tradition.** The interpretive backbone of the Sòng-Yuán-Míng portion of the division.
- *Lǜ-lǚ xīn shū* 律呂新書 [[KR1i0003]] — Cài Yuán-dìng's foundational two-juan treatise (1187), written in collaboration with Zhū Xī; the standard reference text for every later author in the division.
- *Lǜ-lǚ chéng shū* 律呂成書 [[KR1i0006]] — Liú Jǐn's 1347 systematic Yuán synthesis of Cài's system with Péng Sī's *Huáng-zhōng lǜ shuō*.
- *Yuàn-luò zhì yuè* 苑洛志樂 [[KR1i0007]] — Hán Bāngqí's 20-juan Míng treatise that opens with a "direct exposition" of Cài's *Xīn shū* and then reconstructs the six legendary court dances by *wǔ-dé* element-correspondence.
- *Zhōng-lǜ tōng-kǎo* 鐘律通考 [[KR1i0008]] — Ní Fù's 1526 mid-Míng synthesis, doctrinally orthodox to Cài but less polemically forceful than [[KR1i0007]].

**Reconstruction-of-lost-music projects.** The recurrent enterprise of supplying-what-was-lost (*bǔ wáng* 補亡).
- *Sè pǔ* 瑟譜 [[KR1i0004]] — Xióng Pénglái's six-juan reconstruction (early Yuán) of the *sè* 瑟 zither tradition for the temple-hall *yòu-gē* 侑歌, with the most detailed pre-Míng performance-practice notation for any single classical instrument.
- *Sháo-wǔ jiǔ-chéng yuè-bǔ* 韶舞九成樂補 [[KR1i0005]] — Yú Zǎi's diagrammatic reconstruction of the legendary *Sháo* 韶 court dance.
- *Gǔ yuè jīng zhuàn* 古樂經傳 [[KR1i0013]] — Lǐ Guāngdì's Kāngxī-era reconstruction of the lost *Yuè jīng* itself, by extracting the music-officer chapters from the *Zhōu lǐ* and treating them as a recovered *Jīng* glossed by the *Lǐ jì Yuè jì*.
- *Gǔ yuè shū* 古樂書 [[KR1i0014]] — Yìng Huīqiān's two-juan early-Qīng treatise, distinguished by its precise reconstruction of the *Kǎo-gōng jì* bell-shape geometry.

**Zhū Zàiyù and the discovery of equal temperament.** The intellectual high-water mark of the genre and one of the few premodern Chinese mathematical achievements with global comparative significance (Wilkinson §34.2.3).
- *Yuè-lǜ quán shū* 樂律全書 [[KR1i0009]] — the 42-juan collected works of Zhū Zàiyù (presented 1606), within which the *Lǜ-lǚ jīng-yì* 律呂精義 (1584–1596) gives the mathematically explicit derivation of *shí-èr píng-jūn lǜ* 十二平均律 — the twelfth root of two computed to twenty-five-decimal-place accuracy, a decade before Simon Stevin's parallel European derivation.
- *Lǜ-lǚ chǎn-wēi* 律呂闡微 [[KR1i0021]] — Jiāng Yǒng's mid-Qīng exposition of Zhū's mathematical method, recomputing it with Zǔ Chōng-zhī's high-precision *mì-lǜ* 密率 (π = 22/7, refined).

**The Máo Qílíng trilogy and its school.** The most polemically distinctive late-Kāngxī cluster.
- *Shèng-yù yuè-běn jiě-shuō* 聖諭樂本解說 [[KR1i0015]] — Máo's clause-by-clause commentary on Kāngxī's *Sacred Edict* on the *jìng* 1 / *wéi* 3 and *gé bā xiāng shēng* doctrines.
- *Huáng-yán dìng-shēng lù* 皇言定聲錄 [[KR1i0016]] — the eight-juan theoretical core, propounding Máo's distinctive *jiǔ shēng qī diào* 九聲七調 ("nine tones and seven modes") doctrine.
- *Jìng-shān yuè-lù* 竟山樂錄 [[KR1i0017]] — Máo's most radical work, dismissing the entire pre-Sòng *sān-fēn sǔn-yì* tradition as fiction on the basis of a (lost) Tang flute-fingering chart.
- *Lǐ-shì xué-yuè lù* 李氏學樂錄 [[KR1i0018]] — the disciple Lǐ Gōng's record of his studies with Máo, the only systematic exposition of Máo's seven-tone *qì-sè* 器色 fingering doctrine.

**Mid-Qīng *kǎozhèng* music theory.** The empirical, philologically rigorous treatises that close the division.
- *Yuè-lǜ biǎo-wēi* 樂律表微 [[KR1i0019]] — Hú Yànshēng's eight-juan four-part treatise (length-pitch / tone discrimination / mode construction / instrument examination), arguing that the entire *biàn-lǜ* 變律 (derived-pitch) apparatus from Jīng Fáng to Cài Yuán-dìng is unnecessary.
- *Lǜ-lǚ xīn lùn* 律呂新論 [[KR1i0020]] — Jiāng Yǒng's most theoretically precise work, deriving every doctrinal proposition from empirical sources (the *qín* string-fret nodes, actually-cut pitch-pipes, the volumetric basis of the Zhōu *fǔ* 鬴 measure) and rejecting *biàn-lǜ*, *sān-fēn sǔn-yì*, and wind-watching as insufficiently anchored.
- *Qín zhǐ* 琴旨 [[KR1i0022]] — Wáng Tǎn's two-juan systematic treatise on *qín-lǜ* 琴律 (zither-string pitch theory), held by the Sìkù compilers to be the most accomplished mid-Qīng work in this niche.

(Three further texts — KR1i0010 *Yù zhì Lǜ-lǚ zhèng-yì* of Kāngxī, KR1i0011 *Hòu biān* of the Qián-lóng court, and KR1i0012 *Qīn-dìng Shī jīng yuè-pǔ quán shū* — are present in the catalog but not yet cataloged here. These are the principal Qīng imperial music codifications and form the official counterpart to the private treatises listed above.)

## Topics

- **The status of the *Yuè jīng*.** Whether the music classic existed before the Qín book-burning, whether anything substantive of it can be recovered from the *Lǐ jì Yuè jì* and the *Zhōu lǐ* music chapters, and what counts as a defensible reconstruction. The defining debate of the division; foregrounded most explicitly by [[李光地]] in [[KR1i0013]] but implicit in every reconstruction project.
- ***Lǜ-lǚ* 律呂 and the mathematics of pitch.** The derivation of the twelve pitch-pipes from the *huáng-zhōng* 黃鐘, the *sān-fēn sǔn-yì* 三分損益 (cut-and-add-by-thirds) generating procedure, and the inability of *sān-fēn* to close the circle and return to the starting pitch — the mathematical problem that motivates the *biàn-lǜ* 變律 (derived-pitch) tradition (Jīng Fáng, Qián Lè-zhī, Cài Yuán-dìng) and that [[朱載堉]] resolved by abandoning *sān-fēn* altogether for the twelfth root of two.
- ***Biàn-lǜ* and the *èr biàn shēng*.** Whether derived pitches and the two derived tones (*biàn-gōng*, *biàn-zhǐ*) are necessary at all. Defended by Cài Yuán-dìng [[KR1i0003]]; rejected by [[KR1i0019]] and by Jiāng Yǒng [[KR1i0020]].
- **Imperial reform vs. private scholarship.** The repeated tension between standardizing court ritual music by imperial fiat (the Sòng *Huáng-yòu*, the Kāngxī *Zhèng-yì*) and the private reconstructive scholarship that critiques and extends it. Most pointed in Máo Qílíng's positioning of his trilogy as commentary on Kāngxī's own *Sacred Edict*.
- **Empirical anchoring.** The mid-Qīng *kǎo-zhèng* turn that requires music-theoretical claims be tied to measurable phenomena: cut pipes, *qín* fret nodes (*huī* 徽), the volumetric basis of the Zhōu *fǔ*. Definitive in Jiāng Yǒng [[KR1i0020]] and Hú Yànshēng [[KR1i0019]].
- **The *Sháo* and the legendary court dances.** The recurrent attempt to reconstruct the music and choreography of the legendary sage-king dances (*Yún-mén*, *Xián-chí*, *Dà-zhāng*, *Dà-xià*, *Dà-sháo*, *Dà-hù*), known by name only from the *Zhōu guān*. Pursued by Yú Zǎi [[KR1i0005]], Hán Bāngqí [[KR1i0007]], and Zhū Zàiyù [[KR1i0009]] (the last with detailed dance-step diagrams that, as Wilkinson §34.2 notes, are the first such surviving for Chinese ritual dance).
- **Notation and instrumental practice.** The *gōng-chě* 工尺 fingering tradition, the *qín* and *sè* zither traditions, and the question whether any pre-Sòng music can in fact be performed. Foregrounded by Xióng Pénglái [[KR1i0004]], Máo Qílíng [[KR1i0017]], Lǐ Gōng [[KR1i0018]], and Wáng Tǎn [[KR1i0022]].

- c. 1730–1755 — *Qín zhǐ* 琴旨 [[KR1i0022]] — Wáng Tǎn's mid-Qīng *qín*-pitch treatise.
