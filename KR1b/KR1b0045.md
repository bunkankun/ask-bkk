# Rì jiǎng Shū jīng jiě yì 日講書經解義
*Daily-Lecture Exposition of the Documents Classic*
by [[庫勒納]] (fèng chì biān 奉敕編) and [[葉方藹]] (fèng chì zhuàn 奉敕撰)

## About the work
The official Kāngxī-court daily-lecture (*rì jiǎng* 日講) commentary on the *Shàngshū* 尚書 (KR1b0001) in 13 *juǎn*, compiled between Kāngxī 17 / 1678 and Kāngxī 19 / 1680 by an imperial commission under Kuldena 庫勒納 and [[葉方藹]] (Yè Fāng-ǎi, 1629–1682). The work was prepared for the Kāngxī emperor's personal *jīng yán* 經筵 study sessions, then printed with an imperial preface (Kāngxī 19 / 1680.IV.12) and promulgated to officials throughout the empire as the authoritative Kāngxī-era *Shū* commentary. It is the institutional precursor to the much larger *Yùdìng Shū jīng zhuàn shuō huì zuǎn* 御定書經傳說彙纂 (later Kāngxī era), which became the *Yùdìng* imperial standard for the Yōngzhèng-Qiánlóng *Shū* curriculum.

The Kāngxī emperor's preface lays out the work's doctrinal program with unusual clarity: the *Shàngshū* records the *zhì fǎ* 治法 ("methods of government") of the four dynasties (Yú-Xià-Shāng-Zhōu), but these are grounded in *xīn fǎ* 心法 ("methods of the heart-mind") whose three core virtues are *jìng* 敬 (reverence), *chéng* 誠 (sincerity), and *zhōng* 中 (the mean). The preface catalogs canonical phrases under each of these three headings (*qīn míng* 欽明, *yín gōng* 寅恭, *zhī jù* 祗懼, *dí wèi* 迪畏 are *jìng*-class; *yǔn sài* 允塞, *zhì xián* 至諴, *yī dé* 一德, *dūn xìn* 惇信 are *chéng*-class; *yì zhì shì lǐ zhì xīn* 義制事禮制心, *chén qián gāng kè gāo míng róu kè* 沈潛剛克高明柔克, *kuān ér yǒu zhì cōng-róng yǐ hé* 寬而有制從容以和 are *zhōng*-class), and classifies the canonical sage-kings by their relation to the *xīn fǎ*: Yáo, Shùn, Yǔ, and Wén are "*xìng zhī*" (innate); Tāng, Wǔ, Gāozōng are "*shēn zhī*" (embodied); Tài Jiǎ and Chéng Wáng are "*kùn ér xué zhī*" (forced and learned); Tài Kāng, Jié, and Zhòu are "*bèi ér qù zhī*" (deviated and abandoned). This four-fold classification is one of the cleaner late-Imperial statements of the post-Cài Shěn *xīnfǎ* doctrine of *Shàngshū* canonical-political theology.

### Tiyao
*Imperially Authorized Sìkù Quánshū. [Classics, division 2.]*
*Rì jiǎng Shū jīng jiě yì. [Books-class.]*

**Précis.** Your servants etc. respectfully submit: the *Rì jiǎng Shū jīng jiě yì* in thirteen *juǎn* — the Kāngxī-era Director-General Kuldena, Yè Fāng-ǎi and others, by imperial commission — gathered the *jīng yán* lecture-notes and edited them into a finished book. The work began in the spring of Kāngxī 17 (1678) and was completed in summer of Kāngxī 19 (1680), with an imperial preface promulgating it. We respectfully consider that our Imperial Ancestor Rén-huángdì [the Kāngxī emperor] grasped the transmission of the *jīng-yī* 精一 [doctrine] and unfolded the [Hóng fàn's] *dàng-píng* zenith — gathering up the great accomplishment of the *dào* of the two emperors and the three kings — yet still commissioned the Confucian officials to come on alternating days to lecture, and where the subtle and profound matters could not all be exhaustively penetrated by the various ministers, his sage utterance was earnestly thorough and personally elucidative. Day after day accumulated, year after year compiled, to make this book. As for the explanations of the various Confucians from Hàn, Táng, and after, [the work] picked up their finest essence — like the small drop and the [grain of] dust adding to ocean and mountain. Truly, that the *Jūn* and *Shī* unite into one is a thing made eternally manifest. Respectfully submitted, Qiánlóng 40 / 1775, ninth month.

— Director-General, Jì Yún 紀昀, Lù Xīxióng 陸錫熊, Sūn Shìyì 孫士毅. — Director of Final Collation, Lù Fèichí 陸費墀.

### Abstract
The *Rì jiǎng Shū jīng jiě yì* is the institutional Kāngxī-court statement of *Shàngshū* doctrine, prepared as the imperial daily-lecture text and promulgated through the empire as the standard. Its institutional position is significant: in the post-1313 *Cài-zhuàn* curricular tradition, the imperial center had progressively substituted its own commentaries for Cài Shěn's, with the Hóngwǔ *Huì xuǎn* (KR1b0036, 1394) taking the first step (correcting Cài on 66 specific points), the Yǒnglè *Dàquán* (KR1b0037, 1415) re-canonizing Cài, and now the Kāngxī *Rì jiǎng* (1680) and the later *Yùdìng Huì zuǎn* re-establishing imperial-court arbitration over Cài via the synthesizing daily-lecture format.

The composition window is fixed (1678–1680): formal commission Kāngxī 17 / 1678.II.2; completion and imperial preface Kāngxī 19 / 1680.IV.12. The submission to the *Sìkù* was Qiánlóng 40 / 1775, in the early wave of *Sìkù* *Shū lèi* entries.

The doctrinal program — set out in the imperial preface — is the *xīnfǎ* 心法 / *zhìfǎ* 治法 distinction, with the three core virtues (*jìng*, *chéng*, *zhōng*) anchoring the *xīnfǎ*. This is essentially the Cài-Shěn / Zhū-Xī orthodox reading of the *Shàngshū* — the *Dà Yǔ mó* sixteen-character formula (*rén xīn wéi wēi, dào xīn wéi wéi, wéi jīng wéi yī, yǔn zhí jué zhōng*) is the unstated foundation — but the imperial preface is unusually explicit in cataloging which canonical phrases belong to which doctrinal category. The four-fold classification of sage-kings (innate / embodied / forced / deviated) gives the doctrine a graduated psychological-political application that the Sòng-Yuán-Míng Cài-zhuàn tradition had largely left implicit.

The *Sìkù tíyào* is unusually deferential — emphasizing the Kāngxī emperor's personal contribution and casting the human compilers' role as merely supportive. This is the standard reverential mode for imperial-commission entries; the *tíyào* does not engage the work's substantive readings (as it would for a private commentary) but only the institutional fact of its production. The closing formula — "*jūn shī tǒng yī*" 君師統一 ("the sovereign and the teacher are one") — is the classical-political topos asserting that the emperor as classical lecturer-on-his-own-canon-curriculum embodies both political and pedagogical authority.

The work served as the immediate model for the parallel *Rì jiǎng Sì shū jiě yì* 日講四書解義 (already published in 1677, before the *Shū* project's commission), the *Rì jiǎng Yìjīng jiě yì* 日講易經解義 (Kāngxī 22 / 1683), and other Kāngxī-era imperial daily-lecture commentaries on the canon. As a group these works represent the Kāngxī court's institutional re-claiming of canonical-curricular authority from the late-Míng commercial cram-book industry that had developed around the post-Yǒnglè *Dàquán* Cài-zhuàn monopoly.

## Translations and research
*No substantial Western-language translation* of the *Rì jiǎng Shū jīng jiě yì* is known. For the Kāngxī-era *jīng yán* daily-lecture institution see Lì Wèi 黎偉, *Qīngdài jīng yán zhì-dù yánjiū* 清代經筵制度研究 (Beijing: Renmin University, 2010); for Kāngxī's classical-cultural project broadly see Jonathan Spence, *Emperor of China: Self-Portrait of K'ang-hsi* (New York: Knopf, 1974), and Lawrence D. Kessler, *K'ang-hsi and the Consolidation of Ch'ing Rule, 1661–1684* (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1976). Elman, *A Cultural History of Civil Examinations in Late Imperial China* (Berkeley: University of California Press, 2000).

## Other points of interest
The Kāngxī imperial preface's catalog of canonical phrases under the three *xīnfǎ* virtues (*jìng*, *chéng*, *zhōng*) is one of the cleaner late-Imperial doxographic statements available; modern scholars of the post-Cài-Shěn *xīnfǎ* tradition regularly cite it as the canonical synopsis of the doctrine. The four-fold classification of sage-kings (innate / embodied / forced / deviated) similarly provides a clean teaching-friendly schema.

The use of the *jīng yán* daily-lecture mechanism as a vehicle for compiling official imperial commentaries was a Kāngxī-era institutional innovation: previous emperors had received daily lectures, but the systematic editorial collection of those lectures into printed and promulgated books — and the use of this format as the carrier of new official-court doctrine — is a distinctively Kāngxī move. The *Rì jiǎng Shū jīng jiě yì* is one of the cleanest examples of this institutional form.
