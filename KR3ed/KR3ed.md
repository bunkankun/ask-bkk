## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR3ed` 方書類 (*Fāngshū lèi*, "Formularies") is the fourth sub-division of `KR3e` 醫家類 (Masters — Medicine) and collects, with very few exceptions, the entire specialist literature of Chinese medical prescription-books — *fāng* 方 ("formulas", "recipes"): the practical record of drug-combinations, dosages, preparation methods, and indications that constitute the working pharmacopoeia of clinical practice. All 151 entries are sourced from the supplementary 華夏文獻 (*hxwd*) database rather than the Wényuāngé *Sìkù quánshū*. The split is editorial, not conceptual: the Qiánlóng-era Sìkù compilers absorbed the canonical great formularies (*Bèijí qiānjīn yàofāng*, *Wàitái mìyào*, the Sòng imperial *Tàipíng shènghuì fāng* / *Shèngjì zǒnglù* / *Júfāng*, Zhū Sù's *Pǔjì fāng*) into the main `KR3e` medical sub-category, but the vast bulk of the genre — Sòng family formularies (家藏方), YuánMíngQīng experiential formularies (經驗方 / 驗方), Qīng pedagogical formulary-commentaries (方論 / 方解 / 歌訣), Qīng popular and itinerant-medicine collections (*Chuànyǎ* 串雅 etc.), and the Edo Japanese *kohō-ha* 古方派 formulary tradition — was either excluded from the Sìkù as un-orthodox / 巾箱小本 or simply post-dated it. The hxwd supplement accordingly preserves the bulk of the genre in this division. Endymion Wilkinson's *Chinese History: A New Manual* discusses the Chinese formulary tradition under Section 41.3.3, treating the imperial Sòng formularies (*Tàipíng shènghuì fāng* and *Shèngjì zǒnglù*) and Zhū Sù's Míng *Pǔjì fāng* as the three principal monuments of the encyclopaedic strand, and noting the bibliographic-historical importance of the Mǎwángduī *Wǔshí'èr bìngfāng* [[KR3ed001]] as the earliest extant Chinese recipe text. Hinrichs and Barnes 2013 frame the genre more broadly: from Gě Hóng's *Zhǒuhòu* [[KR3ed002]] onward, Chinese physicians produced formularies in two parallel streams — encyclopaedic state compilations on the one hand, and shorter accessible *yìjiǎnfāng* 易簡方 / 備急方 / *jīngyànfāng* 經驗方 designed for laypeople and lower-rank physicians on the other — and this two-fold split structures the corpus collected here.

Chronologically the division spans roughly two-and-a-quarter millennia. The anchor work is the anonymous Mǎwángduī *Wǔshí'èr bìngfāng* [[KR3ed001]] (Warring-States–early Western Hàn, copied onto silk by 168 BCE), the earliest excavated Chinese recipe corpus. From Gě Hóng's Eastern-Jìn *Zhǒuhòu bèijí fāng* [[KR3ed002]] (c. 320–343) the tradition is continuous: WèiJìnNánBěiCháo emergency manuals, SuíTáng systematic formularies, Sòng imperial encyclopaedias and family formularies, Yuán medical-lineage works, Míng imperial and clinical syntheses, and a particularly thick Qīng and Republican stratum of pedagogical, popular, and experiential formularies. The principal commentarial and transmissional traditions a reader must keep in mind are: (i) the HànJìnSuíTáng *fāngshū* tradition of emergency / household formularies inaugurated by Gě Hóng and continued by Yáo Sēngyuán [[KR3ed113]], Chén Yánzhī [[KR3ed006]], and (in attribution) Sūn Sīmiǎo and Huà Tuó; (ii) the Northern-Sòng imperial-pharmacy programme that produced the three great state formularies — *Tàipíng shènghuì fāng* [[KR3ed007]], *Tàipíng huìmín héjì júfāng* [[KR3ed011]], and *Shèngjì zǒnglù* [[KR3ed012]] — and standardised Chinese pharmaceutical practice for the next eight centuries; (iii) the Sòng family-and-literati formulary tradition (Xǔ Shūwēi [[KR3ed015]], Yán Yònghé [[KR3ed022]], Hóng Zūn [[KR3ed016]], Sū Shì / Shěn Kuò [[KR3ed009]]); (iv) the Míng imperial encyclopaedia and the late-imperial popular formulary (Zhū Sù [[KR3ed036]], Hú Yíng [[KR3ed035]]); (v) the Qīng pedagogical-commentary tradition of Wāng Áng [[KR3ed076]] [[KR3ed083]] and his followers Wú Yílù [[KR3ed052]], Chén Niànzǔ [[KR3ed053]] [[KR3ed084]]–[[KR3ed086]], and Wáng Tàilín [[KR3ed087]]–[[KR3ed090]], which created the 21-category formulary curriculum still followed by modern *zhōngyī*; and (vi) the Edo Japanese *kohō-ha* 古方派 of Yoshimasu Tōdō [[KR3ed096]] [[KR3ed097]] [[KR3ed100]] and the Tanba-family critical scholarship of Tanba Genkan [[KR3ed098]] [[KR3ed101]] and Tanba Genken [[KR3ed093]], which together carried the *Shānghán*-*Jīnguì* formulary tradition into early-modern East Asian medicine.

## Important texts and text clusters

**Excavated and early-medieval formularies.** The anchor texts of the genre.
- *Wǔshí'èr bìngfāng* 五十二病方 [[KR3ed001]] — anonymous Warring-States to early-Hàn silk manuscript recovered 1973 at Mǎwángduī, the earliest extant Chinese recipe collection.
- *Zhǒuhòu bèijí fāng* 肘後備急方 [[KR3ed002]] — Gě Hóng's "emergency at-the-elbow" formulary, supplemented by Táo Hóngjǐng's 補闕 and Yáng Yòngdào's 附廣, the prototype of all later emergency / household formularies.
- *Xiǎopǐn fāng* 小品方 [[KR3ed006]] — Chén Yánzhī's late-fifth-century domestic formulary, partly recovered from Japanese sources.
- *Jíyàn fāng* 集驗方 [[KR3ed113]] — Yáo Sēngyuán's Northern-Zhōu imperial formulary, lost in China and reconstructed from Japanese citations.
- *Yīluè chāo* 醫略抄 [[KR3ed101]] — Tanba no Masatada's 11th-century Heian medical anthology, witness to the Japanese reception of SuíTáng formulary literature.

**Northern-Sòng imperial state formularies — the "three great state compilations".** The Sòng court's investment in standardising medical knowledge produced the three largest pre-Pǔjì formularies in Chinese history.
- *Tàipíng shènghuì fāng* 太平聖惠方 [[KR3ed007]] — Wáng Huáiyǐn and Chén Zhāoyù et al., 982 / printed 992; 100 *juǎn*, 16,834 recipes; the first major Sòng state formulary.
- *Tàipíng huìmín héjì júfāng* 太平惠民和劑局方 [[KR3ed011]] — the standard dispensing manual of the Sòng state pharmacies, repeatedly revised 1078–1208, source of *sìjūnzǐtāng*, *sìwùtāng*, *liùwèi dìhuángwán*, *píngwèisǎn* and the bulk of the modern *zhōngyī* core curriculum.
- *Shèngjì zǒnglù* 聖濟總錄 [[KR3ed012]] — Huīzōng's imperial encyclopaedia in 200 *juǎn* and c. 20,000 recipes, with Huīzōng's own *Shèngjì jīng* in 42 chapters as the theoretical introduction; the largest single Northern-Sòng medical compilation.
- *Shèngjì zǒnglù zuǎnyào* 聖濟總錄纂要 [[KR3ed013]] — Chéng Lín's 1681 Qīng epitome that put the *Zǒnglù* back into circulation after centuries of neglect.

**Sòng family and literati formularies.** A dense cluster characteristic of Sòng literati medical practice.
- *Bójì fāng* 博濟方 [[KR3ed008]] — Wáng Gǔn (1041–1048).
- *SūShěn liángfāng* 蘇-沈良方 [[KR3ed009]] — the joint Sū Shì–Shěn Kuò formulary, witness to elite literati-physician culture.
- *Shǐ Zàizhī fāng* 史載之方 [[KR3ed010]] — Shǐ Kān's Northern-Sòng aetiology-and-prescription manual.
- *Pǔjì běnshì fāng* 普濟本事方 [[KR3ed015]] — Xǔ Shūwēi's case-and-prescription compendium, the most influential Sòng *fāngzhèng* combined-genre work.
- *Hóngshì jíyàn fāng* 洪氏集驗方 [[KR3ed016]] — Hóng Zūn's 1170 family formulary.
- *Yángshì jiācáng fāng* 楊氏家藏方 [[KR3ed017]] — Yáng Tǎn's 1178 home-formula collection.
- *Quánshēng zhǐmí fāng* 全生指迷方 [[KR3ed019]] — Wáng Kuàng (early Southern Sòng), partly preserved through *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* recovery.
- *Shìzhāi bǎiyī xuǎnfāng* 是齋百一選方 [[KR3ed027]] — Wáng Qiú, 1196.
- *Chuánxìn shìyòng fāng* 傳信適用方 [[KR3ed028]] — Wú Yànkuí, 1180.
- *Lèibiān Zhūshì jíyàn yīfāng* 類編朱氏集驗醫方 [[KR3ed030]] — Zhū Zuǒ, 1265.
- *Wēn Yǐnjū hǎishàng xiānfāng* 溫隱居海上仙方 [[KR3ed129]] and its *Hòují* [[KR3ed136]] — Wēn Dàmíng's late-Sòng verse-formularies, ancestors of the Qīng *gējué* tradition.
- *Yèshì lùyàn fāng* 葉氏錄驗方 [[KR3ed130]] — Yè Dàlián, 1186–1204.
- *Lǚshě bèiyào fāng* 旅舍備要方 [[KR3ed111]] — Dǒng Jí's specialised traveller's emergency formulary.

**Specialised Sòng works** (gynaecology, paediatrics, regional epidemics, traumatology).
- *Fùrén dàquán liángfāng* 婦人大全良方 [[KR3ed020]] — Chén Zìmíng (1237), the foundational systematic gynaecology of the imperial era.
- *Rénzhāi zhízhǐ fānglùn* 仁齋直指方論 [[KR3ed021]] — Yáng Shìyíng (1264), a synthetic clinical formulary that strongly influenced the late-Sòng-Yuán curriculum.
- *Yánshì jìshēng fāng* 嚴氏濟生方 [[KR3ed022]] — Yán Yònghé's *Píwèi*-oriented Southern-Sòng formulary, source of *guīpítāng* 歸脾湯.
- *Lǐngnán wèishēng fāng* 嶺南衛生方 [[KR3ed031]] — Lǐ Qiú and Zhāng Zhìyuǎn's specialised compendium of southern *zhàngqì* 瘴氣 / malaria therapeutics, a key witness to Sòng regional epidemiology.
- *Xiānshòu lǐshāng xùduàn mìfāng* 仙授理傷續斷秘方 [[KR3ed004]] — Lìn Dàorén's 9th-century traumatology / bone-setting manual, the earliest specialist orthopaedic text in the corpus.
- *Qiānjīn bǎoyào* 千金寶要 [[KR3ed018]] — Guō Sī's 1124 condensation of Sūn Sīmiǎo's *Qiānjīn* into a portable lay-physician formulary.

**Yuán formularies.** The Yuán-government pharmacy and the new ethnic-medical synthesis.
- *Yùyàoyuàn fāng* 御藥院方 [[KR3ed025]] — Xǔ Guózhēn et al., the standard formulary of the Yuán imperial pharmacy (御藥院).
- *Ruìzhútáng jīngyàn fāng* 瑞竹堂經驗方 [[KR3ed023]] / Sìkù recension [[KR3ed024]] — Shātúmùsū's Mongol-physician formulary, important for the integration of Yuán "Huíhuí" 回回 pharmaceutical knowledge.
- *Shìyī déxiào fāng* 世醫得效方 [[KR3ed026]] — Wēi Yìlín, 1337–1345; the great Yuán family-physician formulary, with significant surgery and orthopaedic content.

**The Míng imperial *Pǔjì fāng*.** The single largest medical compendium in Chinese history.
- *Pǔjì fāng* 普濟方 [[KR3ed036]] — Zhū Sù (Prince of Zhōu), c. 1390–1406; 426 *juǎn*, c. 61,739 recipes, organised into 1,960 *mén*. Larger than *Shèngjì zǒnglù* by a factor of three and preserving vast quantities of SòngYuán formulary literature now otherwise lost.
- *Pǔjì fāng · yīnghái mén* 普濟方·嬰孩門 [[KR3ed037]] — the paediatric extract that circulated independently in the late Míng.

**Míng practical and specialist formularies.**
- *Wèishēng yìjiǎn fāng* 衛生易簡方 [[KR3ed035]] — Hú Yíng, 1410, an imperially-sponsored *yìjiǎn* (simple-and-easy) collection.
- *Qíxiào liángfāng* 奇效良方 [[KR3ed038]] — Dǒng Sù and Fāng Xián, 1449–1470, the largest single Míng synthesis between *Pǔjì fāng* and *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng*.
- *Yīfāng kǎo* 醫方考 [[KR3ed039]] — Wú Kūn, 1584; the first systematic *fāngyì* (formula-meaning) analytical commentary, foundational for the Qīng pedagogical tradition.
- *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng · lèifāng* 證治準繩·類方 [[KR3ed044]] — Wáng Kěntáng's late-Míng clinical encyclopaedia's formulary section.
- *Zǔjì* 祖劑 [[KR3ed042]] — Shī Pèi, 1640; an analytic genealogy of *fāng* by descent from canonical "ancestor-formulas" 祖方, a major late-Míng innovation.
- *Lǔfǔ jìnfāng* 魯府禁方 [[KR3ed041]], *Zhòngxìng xiānfāng* 種杏仙方 [[KR3ed141]] — Gōng Tíngxián's two influential Wànlì practical formularies.
- *Běncǎo dānfāng* 本草單方 [[KR3ed142]] — Miào Xīyōng's 1622–33 rearrangement of Lǐ Shízhēn's *Gāngmù* into single-drug *fāng* form.
- *Wàikē jīngyàn fāng* 外科經驗方 [[KR3ed123]] — Xuē Jǐ's external-medicine formulary.

**Qīng pedagogical formulary-commentary — the central late-imperial cluster.** This is the genre that fixed the modern *zhōngyī* curriculum.
- *Yīfāng jíjiě* 醫方集解 [[KR3ed076]] / duplicate [[KR3ed077]] — Wāng Áng, 1682; the most successful Chinese formulary commentary of the entire late-imperial period, the direct ancestor of the modern 21-category clinical-formulary curriculum.
- *Tāngtóu gējué* 湯頭歌訣 [[KR3ed083]] — Wāng Áng's 1694 verse mnemonic, the canonical memorisation manual of pre-modern Chinese medical training.
- *Yīzōng jīnjiàn · shānbǔ míngyī fānglùn* 醫宗金鑑·刪補名醫方論 [[KR3ed074]] — Wú Qiān et al., 1742, the formulary-discussion section of the imperial Qiánlóng compendium that defined the Qīng medical examinations.
- *Gǔjīn míngyī fānglùn* 古今名醫方論 [[KR3ed075]] — Luó Měi, 1675; the influential anthology of master-physicians' formula-discussions on which Wáng Áng built.
- *Chéngfāng qièyòng* 成方切用 [[KR3ed052]] — Wú Yílù, 1761; the Qiánlóng-era successor and rival to Wāng Áng.
- *Jiàngxuěyuán gǔfāng xuǎnzhù* 絳雪園古方選注* [[KR3ed078]] — Wáng Zǐjiē, 1731; a *gǔfāng* (ancient-formula) commentary in the *Shānghán*–*Jīnguì* line.
- *Yīfāng lùn* 醫方論 [[KR3ed079]] — Fèi Bóxióng, 1865; the most critical of the Qīng formulary-commentaries, often polemical against Wāng Áng's compromises.
- *Jīngfāng lìshì* 經方例釋 [[KR3ed081]] — Mò Méishì's late-Qīng exposition of the Hàn classical formulas.
- *Lèizhèng pǔjì běnshì fāng shìyì* 類證普濟本事方釋義 [[KR3ed082]] — Yè Guì's 1745 commentary on Xǔ Shūwēi (= [[KR3ed015]]).

**The verse-formulary 歌訣 cluster.** Mnemonic *gējué* arranged for memorisation was the central pedagogical genre of nineteenth-century *zhōngyī* education.
- *Shífāng gēkuò* 時方歌括 [[KR3ed084]], *Chángshā fāng gēkuò* 長沙方歌括 [[KR3ed085]] (= *Shānghán* mnemonic, commentary on [[KR3a0011]]), *Jīnguì fāng gēkuò* 金匱方歌括 [[KR3ed086]] (= *Jīnguì* mnemonic, commentary on [[KR3a0012]]) — Chén Niànzǔ's three companion mnemonics (1801–1830), the standard nineteenth-century pedagogical set.
- *Yīfāng zhèngzhì huìbiān gējué* 醫方證治彙編歌訣 [[KR3ed087]], *Yīfāng gēkuò* 醫方歌括 [[KR3ed088]], *Zēngdìng yīfāng gējué* 增訂醫方歌訣 [[KR3ed089]], *Tuìsī jí lèifāng gēzhù* 退思集類方歌註 [[KR3ed090]] — Wáng Tàilín's four mnemonic works, the largest single-author verse-formulary corpus in the division.
- *Zhěnyàn yīfāng gēkuò* 診驗醫方歌括 [[KR3ed091]] — Zuòxiàoshānrén's nineteenth-century manuscript mnemonic.
- *Chéngfāng biàndú* 成方便讀 [[KR3ed092]] — Zhāng Bǐngchéng, 1904, the last great Qīng pedagogical formulary.
- *Shífāng miàoyòng* 時方妙用 [[KR3ed053]] and *Shíjì biǎo* 十劑表 [[KR3ed054]] (Bāo Chéng) — the *shíjì* "ten therapeutic categories" pedagogical scheme.

**Qīng experiential / popular formularies (經驗方 / 驗方).** A dense late-imperial stratum.
- *Chuànyǎ nèiwài biān* 串雅內外編 [[KR3ed105]] / [[KR3ed107]] and *Chuànyǎ bǔ* [[KR3ed106]] — Zhào Xuémǐn's 1759 "refined pedlar's" formulary, the unique surviving witness to itinerant-physician (走方) medicine, supplemented by Lǔ Zhào in 1825.
- *Yànfāng xīnbiān* 驗方新編 [[KR3ed108]] — Bào Xiāng'áo's 1846 compendium, the most widely-printed late-Qīng popular formulary, reprinted into the twentieth century.
- *Wàizhì shòushì fāng* 外治壽世方 [[KR3ed118]] — Zōu Cúngàn's 1877 *wàizhì* (external-treatment) formulary, the principal Qīng monument of the external-therapy school.
- *Wáng Shìxióng* 王士雄 quartet — *Jīmíng lù* 雞鳴錄 [[KR3ed124]], *Qiánzhāi jiǎnxiào fāng* 潛齋簡效方 [[KR3ed138]], *Sìkē jiǎnxiào fāng* 四科簡效方 [[KR3ed139]], *Jiàodìng Yuàntǐ yīhuà liángfāng* 校訂願體醫話良方 [[KR3ed140]] — the central nineteenth-century experiential-formulary corpus, by the leading Qīng warm-pathogen physician (Mèngyīng 孟英).
- *Jīngyàn dānfāng huìbiān* 經驗丹方彙編 [[KR3ed112]], *Huíshēng jí* 回生集 [[KR3ed120]], *Liángpéng huìjí jīngyàn shénfāng* 良朋彙集經驗神方 [[KR3ed147]], *Huìzhítáng jīngyàn fāng* 惠直堂經驗方 [[KR3ed115]] — Qīng *jīngyànfāng* collections aimed at the general literate household.
- *Lùzhútáng jíyàn fāng* 菉竹堂集驗方 [[KR3ed148]] — Yáo Rǔxún's late-Míng family formulary.
- *Wéntáng jíyàn fāng* 文堂集驗方 [[KR3ed119]], *Línzhèng yīdé fāng* 臨證一得方 [[KR3ed128]], *Língyàn liángfāng huìbiān* 靈驗良方彙編 [[KR3ed150]] — typical Qīng *jīngyànfāng* products of literati-physician practice.

**Republican-era formularies.**
- *Běncǎo jiǎnyào fāng* 本草簡要方 [[KR3ed121]] — Zhāng Zōngxiáng, 1943–45.
- *Dīng Gānrén xiānshēng jiāchuán zhēnfāng* 丁甘仁先生家傳珍方 [[KR3ed061]] — the home-formulae of Dīng Gānrén, founder of the Shànghǎi *Mènghé* 孟河 school.
- *Jīnshì ménzhěn fāngàn* 金氏門診方案 [[KR3ed070]] — Jīn Zǐjiǔ's clinical case-and-formula records.
- *Línglín língfāng* 凌臨靈方 [[KR3ed143]] — Líng Xiǎowǔ.
- *Pūxī mìchuán jiǎnyàn fāng* 䱐溪秘傳簡驗方 [[KR3ed149]] — Lù Jǐnsuì, 1918.
- *Dānfāng zhī yánjiū* 丹方之研究 [[KR3ed144]] — Oka-nishi Tameto's 1930s bibliographic survey of single-drug formula literature.

**The Edo Japanese *kohō-ha* and Tanba-family cluster.** A coherent Japanese sub-tradition embedded in the division.
- *Lèijù fāng* 類聚方 [[KR3ed096]], *Fāngjī* 方機 [[KR3ed097]], *Jiāshú fāng yǔ fāngjí* 家塾方與方極 [[KR3ed100]] — Yoshimasu Tōdō (吉益爲則, 1702–1773), founder of the *kohō-ha* (古方派, "ancient-formula school") that built its clinical doctrine on the Hàn *Shānghán*–*Jīnguì* canon against SòngYuán doctrinal medicine.
- *Yàozhì tōngyì* 藥治通義 [[KR3ed093]] — Tanba Genken (丹波元堅, 1795–1857), the principal Edo synthesis of drug-therapy theory.
- *Jiùjí xuǎnfāng* 救急選方 [[KR3ed098]] — Tanba Genkan (丹波元簡, 1755–1810), with appendix by Yu-kawa Tan.
- *Gǔfāng fēnliàng kǎo* 古方分量考 [[KR3ed102]] — Hirai Niwa-ishi, 1793, the standard Edo study of ancient-formula dosing.
- *Fāngjì cídiǎn* 方劑辭典 [[KR3ed094]] — Mizuhashi Yoshikoto, 1808.
- *Qízhèng fāng* 奇正方 [[KR3ed095]] — Kaya Hisashi.
- *Míngjiā fāngxuǎn* 名家方選 [[KR3ed099]] — Yamada Genrin, 1780.

**Pseudepigraphic and anonymous works.** Of note for the discriminating reader.
- *Huà Tuó shénfāng* 華佗神方 [[KR3ed005]] — anachronistic Qīng pseudepigraphic compilation under Huà Tuó's name; the persons-frontmatter (東漢) gives the attributed dynasty, but the genuine composition window is 1700–1762.
- *Sūn Zhēnrén hǎishàng fāng* 孫真人海上方 [[KR3ed003]] — verse formulary in Sūn Sīmiǎo's name, in fact compiled c. 1100–1572.
- *Dàxiǎo zhūzhèng fānglùn* 大小諸證方論 [[KR3ed080]] — attributed to Fù Shān but largely a Qīng compilation.
- *Jíjiù xiānfāng* 急救仙方 [[KR3ed033]], *Rúyī xīnjìng* 儒醫心鏡 [[KR3ed063]], *Jíjiù biànfāng* 急救便方 [[KR3ed103]], *Jǐshì shényàn liángfāng* 濟世神驗良方 [[KR3ed109]], *Guàizhèng qífāng* 怪證奇方 [[KR3ed135]], *Yìshì jīngyàn liángfāng* 益世經驗良方 [[KR3ed133]] — anonymous compilations of SòngQīng date with no defensible author attribution.

## Important persons

- [[葛洪]] (Gě Hóng, 283–343) — author of *Zhǒuhòu bèijí fāng* [[KR3ed002]], the prototype emergency formulary and the model for all later 備急 / 急救 formularies in the division.
- [[陶弘景]] (Táo Hóngjǐng, 456–536) — supplementer of *Zhǒuhòu*, whose 補闕 enlarged the Gě Hóng core for medieval transmission.
- [[陳延之]] (Chén Yánzhī, fl. 454–473) — author of the partially-recovered *Xiǎopǐn fāng* [[KR3ed006]].

## Topics

The division is organised around a number of overlapping debates, genres, and reading-strategies.

**Imperial state formularies versus private and family formularies.** Sòng state-pharmacy compilations ([[KR3ed007]] [[KR3ed011]] [[KR3ed012]]) impose drug standardisation and uniform compounding on a national network of dispensaries; the private *jiācángfāng* 家藏方 ([[KR3ed017]]) and *jíyànfāng* 集驗方 ([[KR3ed016]] [[KR3ed148]]) preserve the actual prescribing practice of named physicians and literati. Chinese pharmaceutical history reads the two streams against each other: institutional standardisation versus family-craft transmission.

**Formula as therapeutic taxonomy: the 21-category Wāng Áng scheme.** Wāng Áng's *Yīfāng jíjiě* ([[KR3ed076]]) organises *fāng* under twenty-one therapeutic categories (補養之劑、發表之劑、湧吐之劑、攻裏之劑、表裏之劑、和解之劑、理氣之劑、理血之劑、祛風之劑、祛寒之劑、清暑之劑、利濕之劑、潤燥之劑、瀉火之劑、除痰之劑、消導之劑、收澀之劑、殺蟲之劑、明目之劑、癰瘍之劑、經產之劑) — the conceptual scheme is then carried by *Tāngtóu gējué* ([[KR3ed083]]), *Chéngfāng qièyòng* ([[KR3ed052]]), the *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* formulary section ([[KR3ed074]]), the *gējué* cluster ([[KR3ed084]]–[[KR3ed092]]), and on into the modern *fāngjìxué* 方劑學 textbook. This is the principal achievement of the Qīng formulary tradition and the conceptual armature of the entire late-imperial-and-modern field.

**Mnemonic verse formularies (歌訣 / 歌括).** From Wāng Áng's *Tāngtóu gējué* (1694) the verse-formulary becomes an autonomous pedagogical genre, climaxing in Chén Niànzǔ's *Shānghán* / *Jīnguì* / *shífāng* trilogy ([[KR3ed084]]–[[KR3ed086]]) and Wáng Tàilín's four-work cluster ([[KR3ed087]]–[[KR3ed090]]). Sòng verse-formularies (Wēn Dàmíng [[KR3ed129]] [[KR3ed136]]) anticipate the genre; Zhāng Bǐngchéng's 1904 *Chéngfāng biàndú* [[KR3ed092]] closes it.

**Emergency and household formularies (急救方 / 備急方 / 易簡方).** A continuous lineage from Gě Hóng ([[KR3ed002]]) through Dǒng Jí ([[KR3ed111]]), Sòng *Jíjiù xiānfāng* ([[KR3ed033]]), Zhāng Shíchè ([[KR3ed034]]), Hú Yíng ([[KR3ed035]]), Wáng Zhèngyán's *Yìngjí liángfāng* ([[KR3ed137]]), Tanba Genkan's *Jiùjí xuǎnfāng* ([[KR3ed098]]), and the Qīng popular emergency formularies ([[KR3ed103]] [[KR3ed109]] [[KR3ed122]]) — the genre of last-resort domestic medicine, distinguished by accessibility, low cost, and reliance on common drugs.

**Experiential / tested formularies (經驗方 / 驗方 / 神效方).** A dense Qīng stratum: Bào Xiāng'áo's *Yànfāng xīnbiān* ([[KR3ed108]]) and Wáng Shìxióng's quartet ([[KR3ed124]] [[KR3ed138]]–[[KR3ed140]]) are the canonical anchors; surrounding them are dozens of literati-physician *jīngyànfāng* collections ([[KR3ed112]] [[KR3ed115]] [[KR3ed117]] [[KR3ed119]] [[KR3ed120]] [[KR3ed131]] [[KR3ed133]] [[KR3ed134]] [[KR3ed146]] [[KR3ed147]] [[KR3ed150]] etc.). The genre's epistemological premise — *fāng* validated by reported clinical success rather than by classical authority — is the most consequential late-imperial development in the field.

**Single-drug formulas (單方 / 丹方).** A specialised genre that selects high-efficacy single-ingredient remedies: Miào Xīyōng's *Běncǎo dānfāng* [[KR3ed142]] is the Míng anchor; Qián Jùn's *Jīngyàn dānfāng huìbiān* [[KR3ed112]] and Oka-nishi Tameto's Republican bibliographic *Dānfāng zhī yánjiū* [[KR3ed144]] mark the genre's later trajectory.

**Itinerant medicine and the *Chuànyǎ* tradition.** Zhào Xuémǐn's *Chuànyǎ* ([[KR3ed105]] [[KR3ed107]]; supplement [[KR3ed106]]) is the unique surviving textual witness to the *zǒufāngyī* 走方醫 / pedlar physicians who operated outside the literate medical establishment, transmitting their formulas orally and in secret. The text is a key historical source for Qīng popular medical practice.

**External-treatment formularies (外治方).** Zōu Cúngàn's *Wàizhì shòushì fāng* [[KR3ed118]] is the central monument; the genre — plasters, ointments, fumigations, moxibustion, acupressure — develops as a coherent therapeutic alternative to internal-decoction medicine in the late Qīng.

**Pedagogical / formal-analytic formulary commentary (方論 / 方解 / 方義).** Wú Kūn's *Yīfāng kǎo* (1584, [[KR3ed039]]) inaugurates the analytical genre; Luó Měi ([[KR3ed075]]), Wāng Áng ([[KR3ed076]]), Wú Yílù ([[KR3ed052]]), Fèi Bóxióng ([[KR3ed079]]), and Mò Méishì ([[KR3ed081]]) carry it forward; Shī Pèi's *Zǔjì* ([[KR3ed042]]) develops the parallel *zǔfāng* 祖方 genealogical method.

**Specialised genres.** Gynaecology (Chén Zìmíng [[KR3ed020]]); paediatrics (the *Pǔjì fāng · yīngháimén* [[KR3ed037]]); traumatology (Lìn Dàorén [[KR3ed004]]); regional epidemic medicine (*Lǐngnán wèishēng fāng* [[KR3ed031]], *Chóngdìng shāyì zhǐmí* [[KR3ed069]]); external medicine (Xuē Jǐ [[KR3ed123]]); military-emergency medicine (Luó Shìyáo's *Xíngjūn fāngbiàn biànfāng* [[KR3ed068]]); palace pharmacy (Tàiyīyuàn imperial formulary [[KR3ed055]]).

**The Edo Japanese *kohō-ha* and the bilateral formulary tradition.** Yoshimasu Tōdō's polemical return to the Hàn *Shānghán*–*Jīnguì* canon ([[KR3ed096]] [[KR3ed097]] [[KR3ed100]]) generates a distinctive Japanese reading of Chinese formulary literature, which is then re-imported back into China through nineteenth-century Sino-Japanese book exchange; the Tanba family ([[KR3ed098]] [[KR3ed093]] [[KR3ed101]]) constitutes the parallel philological-recovery side of the same tradition. Yáo Sēngyuán's *Jíyàn fāng* [[KR3ed113]] and Tanba no Masatada's *Yīluè chāo* [[KR3ed101]] are the principal beneficiaries: lost in China, recovered through Japanese transmission.
