# Tàipíng Huìmín Héjì Júfāng 太平惠民和劑局方
*Recipes of the Imperial Pharmacy for the People's Welfare in the Reign of Great Peace*
compiled by the imperial pharmacy office (太平惠民和劑局) under successive Sòng imperial commissions

## About the work

The *Tàipíng huìmín héjì júfāng* — commonly *Héjì júfāng* 和劑局方 or simply *Júfāng* 局方 — is the **standard Sòng-Yuán formulary for state-pharmacy dispensing** and one of the most clinically influential medical books in the entire Chinese tradition. Originally compiled in 元豐 (1078–1085) by Chén Chéng 陳承, Péi Zōngyuán 裴宗元, and Chén Shīwén 陳師文 of the *Tài-yī jú* (Imperial Medical Office), the book served as the dispensing manual for the Northern Sòng's national network of state pharmacies (*Hùimín-jú* 惠民局 and *Héjì-jú* 和劑局). Each entry gives a recipe in standardised form — drug list, weights, preparation, dosing, indications — designed for industrial-scale compounding and uniform distribution. The work was repeatedly revised and expanded under subsequent reign-periods (大觀 1107, 紹興 1148, 寶慶 1226), each redaction adding recipes that had proved effective in pharmacy practice. The Southern-Sòng received text in 10 *juǎn* (with an appended *Zhǐnán zǒng-lùn* 指南總論 of theoretical principles in 3 *juǎn*) preserves the cumulative revisions.

The work's central importance — apart from its clinical scope — is its role in **standardising Chinese pharmaceutical recipes for institutional dispensing**: dosages are calibrated for adult, paediatric, pregnant, and elderly patients; preparation methods are uniformly specified; raw vs prepared drug states are explicit. Many Chinese clinical recipes still in use today — *bā-zhèng-sǎn* 八正散, *píng-wèi-sǎn* 平胃散, *xiāng-shā liù-jūnzǐ-tāng* 香砂六君子湯, *sì-jūnzǐ-tāng* 四君子湯, *sì-wù-tāng* 四物湯, *liù-wèi dì-huáng wán* 六味地黃丸, *bǎo-hé-wán* 保和丸 — descend in their standard form from the *Júfāng*.

### Prefaces

The hxwd recension carries multiple imperial prefaces from successive Sòng revisions, the most important being:

- 紹興十八年戊辰 (1148) imperial decree authorising the *Tàipíng huìmín héjì jú-fāng* title and the consolidated 10-*juǎn* form.
- 大觀年間 (1107–1110) imperial preface formalising the work as the *Júfāng* and authorising its empire-wide distribution to provincial pharmacies.
- 寶慶元年 (1225–1226) preface of the final Southern-Sòng revision, the *Zhǐnán zǒng-lùn* 指南總論 supplement, by Xǔ Hóng 許洪, which articulates the theoretical principles underlying the empirical formulae.

### Abstract

The *Júfāng* is the formal endpoint of a state-pharmacy project initiated under Sòng Shénzōng 神宗 in the 熙寧 (1068–1077) reform era, when the imperial government began acquiring, standardising, and distributing pharmaceuticals as a public-welfare service. The *Huìmín-jú* 惠民局 and *Héjì-jú* 和劑局 networks of state pharmacies were established to dispense subsidised or free medicines, and the *Júfāng* was the dispensary manual. The work was originally short — the 1078 redaction by Chén Chéng et al. had only c. 300 recipes — but the cumulative Southern-Sòng revisions enlarged it to 788 recipes (10 *juǎn*) by the *Bǎoqìng* 1226 redaction. Some 27 of the recipes are *fù-zǐ* 附子 (aconite)-based and stand among the earliest standardised Chinese pharmaceutical formulae for chronic *yáng-xū* (yang-deficiency) conditions.

The work's **institutional significance** is unmatched in pre-modern Chinese medicine: it institutionalised state pharmacy as a function of governance, codified recipe-formulary as a regulatory genre, and established a pattern of state pharmaceutical quality-control later emulated in Yuán, Ming, and Qing dynasty institutions. Its **clinical significance** is also exceptional: by the late Sòng, physicians were complaining that the *Júfāng* recipes had become a clinical default applied uncritically — a complaint that drove the polemic-physicians of the Jin-Yuán transition (Lǐ Gǎo, Zhū Zhèn-hēng) to articulate alternative theoretical foundations.

The work's authorship is **collective and institutional** — no single named author is more than a coordinating figure. The hxwd transmission records the official compiling office (太平惠民和劑局, "the Imperial Pharmacy for the People's Welfare") rather than an individual; the catalog meta correctly follows this practice.

## Other points of interest

The Jīn-Yuán physician Lǐ Gǎo 李杲 (1180–1251)'s *Nèi-wài shāng biàn-huò lùn* 內外傷辨惑論 includes a sustained polemic against indiscriminate *Júfāng* use — the so-called *Júfāng zhī bì* 局方之弊 ("ills of the *Júfāng*") debate that shaped the doctrinal divisions of the Jin-Yuán medical schools. Modern scholarship (Goldschmidt, Hanson, Wú Yītì 吳以德) treats this debate as a foundational moment in the differentiation of Chinese medical schools.
