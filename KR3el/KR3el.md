# KR3el — Shāng-kē 傷科 (Traumatology, Wound-Medicine and Bone-Setting)

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3el is the traumatology sub-division of KR3e 醫家類, itself part of the Masters (KR3 子部). The genre covers what Chinese medicine has called *shāng-kē* 傷科 — the diagnosis and treatment of acute mechanical injury: open wounds caused by edged weapons (*jīn-chuāng* 金瘡), fall-blow injuries (*diē-dǎ* 跌打 / *diē-sǔn* 跌損), bone fractures and joint dislocations (*zhèng-gǔ* 正骨, *jiē-gǔ* 接骨), arrow-extraction (*chū-zú* 出鏃), strangulation and battlefield trauma, and the downstream syndromes of internal blood-stasis (*yū-xuè* 瘀血) and *jīng* / fright (*zhèn-jīng* 鎮驚). Its practical apparatus comprises three intersecting families of technique: manual reduction (*shǒu-fǎ* 手法), wound-dressing pharmacology (the standard *Táo-huā sǎn* 桃花散 / *Hēi-lóng sǎn* 黑龍散 / *Fēng-liú sǎn* 風流散 dressing-trio plus haemostatic, blood-quickening, and flesh-generating powders), and acupoint-based forensic-prognostic mapping (*xué-dào* 穴道 — the "fatal-point" atlases inherited from the Sòng forensic *Xǐ-yuān lù* 洗冤錄 and elaborated by the Qīng martial-arts lineages into the *diǎn-xué* 點穴 tradition). The chronological centre of gravity is the Míng–Qīng arc; the corpus collected here runs from Xuē Jǐ's foundational [[KR3el018]] *Zhèng-tǐ lèi-yào* (1529) to Zhào Lián's late-Qīng synthesis [[KR3el005]] *Shāng-kē dà-chéng* (1891), with the late-Edo [[KR3el019]] *Zhōng-guó jiē-gǔ tú-shuō* (1808) of Ninomiya Ken included as a Sino-Japanese branch of the same lineage.

The principal English-language framing of this genre is the section "Therapeutic Massage, Dit Da Jow, and Bone Setting" in Hinrichs and Barnes (eds.), *Chinese Medicine and Healing: An Illustrated History* (Harvard, 2013), pp. 326–328, which establishes the social-historical key to the corpus: traumatology was for most of the late-imperial period the lineage-protected, low-status manual specialty of martial-arts teachers, village physicians (*xiāng-yī* 鄉醫), Buddhist Chán monks, and military bone-setters — the same "*chuāng-yī*" 瘡醫 / *jì-jī-jiā* 技擊家 milieu that elite *yī-jīng*-trained literati-physicians refused to enter. The polemical history of the division is therefore the history of the discipline's slow integration into the learned medical canon: Xuē Jǐ's [[KR3el018]] *Zhèng-tǐ lèi-yào* (1529) is the first systematic Míng attempt to treat *zhèng-tǐ* as a coupled internal–external pathology requiring elite *yī-lǐ* reasoning; the imperial *Yīzōng jīn-jiàn*'s [[KR3el015]] *Zhèng-gǔ xīn-fǎ yào-zhǐ* (1742–49) institutionalises the eight *Zhèng-gǔ bā-fǎ* manipulation-techniques as state-canonical curriculum; and the canonical Qīng synthetic triad — [[KR3el013]] *Shāng-kē bǔ-yào* (1808), [[KR3el011]] *Shāng-kē huì-zuǎn* (1815), [[KR3el005]] *Shāng-kē dà-chéng* (1891) — completes the integration of practitioner experience with classical channel-and-tendon (*jīng-luò* 經絡) theory and Wáng Qīng-rèn's blood-stasis paradigm. Endymion Wilkinson, *Chinese History: A New Manual*, Section 41 ("Medicine"), supplies the general bibliographic context for the medical *jiā*; Wilkinson himself does not discuss *shāng-kē* as a specialty, and Hinrichs and Barnes therefore remains the principal first-tier English-language reference for this division.

## Important texts and text clusters

The division's nineteen titles (one of which, KR3el006, is a duplicate-title placeholder for [[KR3el005]] *Shāng-kē dà-chéng* and is not separately catalogued) fall into recognisable clusters that the overview must keep distinct.

- **The Míng foundation.** [[KR3el018]] *Zhèng-tǐ lèi-yào* 正體類要 ([[薛己]] Xuē Jǐ, 1529) — the only Míng-period specialist monograph on bone-setting / traumatology, and explicitly identified by the imperial *Yīzōng jīn-jiàn* editors of 1749 as the *sole* prior specialist work in the field. Its two *juǎn* — sixty-some entries of principles and treatments, plus a large *yī-àn* 醫案 (clinical case-history) collection — establish the principle that traumatology is a coupled external–internal pathology requiring *shǒu-fǎ* manipulation alongside *yī-lǐ* medical reasoning. Source of every subsequent Qīng *shāng-kē* manual.

- **The Míng Buddhist-monastic verse-handbook tradition.** [[KR3el010]] *Diē-sǔn miào-fāng* 跌損妙方 ([[異遠真人]] Yì-yuǎn Zhēn-rén, 1523, in manuscript for three centuries before [[孫應科]] Sūn Yìng-kē's *Dào-guāng* 16 / 1836 print edition) — the founding text of the so-called Shàolín-Chán *shāng-kē* tradition. Its versified *Yòng-yào gē* 用藥歌 (drug-use song) — with *guī-wěi* 歸尾 / *shēng-dì* 生地 / *bīng-láng* 檳榔 / *chì-sháo* 赤芍 as the "four ruler-drugs" and region-specific add-ons — became canonical and is paraphrased in nearly every subsequent Qīng manual.

- **The late-Míng pseudepigraphic prestige-attribution stratum.** Three closely related anonymous compilations of military-tradition wound-medicine circulating under prestige-names: [[KR3el001]] *Mì-chuán Liú Bówēn jiā-cáng jiē-gǔ jīn-chuāng jìn-fāng* 秘傳劉伯溫家藏接骨金瘡禁方 and [[KR3el003]] *Jīn-chuāng mì-chuán jìn-fāng* 金瘡秘傳禁方 (both attributed to [[劉基]] Liú Jī / Liú Bówēn, 1427–1644 bracket); [[KR3el008]] *Shàolín zhēn-chuán shāng-kē mì-fāng* 少林真傳傷科秘方 (attributed to the Shàolín monastery, Qīng); and [[KR3el012]] *Diē-dǎ sǔn-shāng fāng* 跌打損傷方 (attributed to a "Yáng Chéng-bó" 楊成博 martial-arts lineage, partner-text of [[KR3ee051]] in the adjoining sub-division). All four share the same dressing-trio, the same "five fatal signs" rubric, and the same anatomical *shàng / zhōng / xià-bù* prescription scheme — strong evidence for a shared late-Míng military-medical archetype.

- **The two anonymous Qīng practitioner manuals.** [[KR3el002]] *Jiē-gǔ shǒu-fǎ* 接骨手法 (anonymous, post-Wōkòu Jiāng-nán, ca. 1550–1800) — a seven-character-verse joint-by-joint reduction manual; [[KR3el009]] *Jīn-chuāng diē-dǎ jiē-gǔ yào-xìng mì-shū* 金瘡跌打接骨藥性秘書 (anonymous, 1600–1900) — distinctive for its opening *Jīn-chuāng fù* 金瘡賦 Five-Phase essay framing trauma as a Metal-Phase / autumn / lung pathology. To these is appended [[KR3el004]] *Diē-dǎ mì-fāng* 跌打秘方 (anonymous Qīng, 1600–1900), an unattributed *diǎn-xué*-tradition compilation.

- **The imperial Qīng canon.** [[KR3el015]] *Yīzōng jīn-jiàn · Zhèng-gǔ xīn-fǎ yào-zhǐ* 醫宗金鑑·正骨心法要旨 ([[吳謙]] Wú Qiān and [[劉裕鐸]] Liú Yùduó, imperial commission 1742–49) — the bone-setting and traumatology chapter (originally juǎn 87–90) of the *Yù-zuǎn Yīzōng jīn-jiàn* ([[KR3e0090]]), extracted and circulated as a self-standing four-juǎn primer. Establishes the eight *Zhèng-gǔ bā-fǎ* 正骨八法 (*mō*-*jiē*-*duān*-*tí*-*àn*-*mó*-*tuī*-*ná* — palpation, joining, raising, lifting, pressing, rubbing, pushing, grasping) that have remained the curriculum standard from the *Tài-yī-yuàn* to the contemporary TCM training schools, plus the standardised reduction-instrument repertoire. The principal vehicle through which the imperial Qīng codified *shāng-kē* as a formal medical specialty.

- **The Qīng synthetic triad.** Three works built directly on the imperial canon and routinely treated as the conceptual spine of late-Qīng traumatology: [[KR3el013]] *Shāng-kē bǔ-yào* 傷科補要 ([[錢秀昌]] Qián Xiù-chāng, 1808) — a pedagogical condensation of the imperial *yào-zhǐ* into thirty-six rules and versified mnemonics, integrating the *Xǐ-yuān lù* forensic acupoint atlas with clinical reduction; [[KR3el011]] *Shāng-kē huì-zuǎn* 傷科彙纂 ([[胡廷光]] Hú Tíng-guāng, 1815) — at 12 *juǎn* and ca. 254,000 characters, the largest single-author Qīng compendium, fusing the imperial canon with the family-transmitted *Chén-shì jiē-gǔ shū* 陳氏接骨書 and with Xuē Jǐ's [[KR3el018]]; and [[KR3el005]] *Shāng-kē dà-chéng* 傷科大成 ([[趙濂]] Zhào Lián, 1891) — a single-juǎn but exceptionally dense late-Qīng synthesis that integrates Wáng Qīng-rèn's *xuè-yū* blood-stasis paradigm into traumatology and includes one of the earliest formal Chinese-medicine attestations of the capillary-refill test.

- **The Wù-yuán / Jiāng-Zhè practitioner manuals.** [[KR3el007]] *Jiāng-shì shāng-kē xué* 江氏傷科學 and its parallel recension [[KR3el017]] *Shāng-kē fāng-shū* 傷科方書 ([[江考卿]] Jiāng Kǎo-qīng of Wù-yuán 婺源, 1840; first printed 1924 by Qiū Jì-shēng 裘吉生 in the *Sān-sān yī-bào* 三三醫報) — the principal mid-19th-century manual of the *xiāng-yī* (village-doctor) traumatology tradition, with the signature *Duàn-sǐ zhèng mì-jué* 斷死證秘訣 and *Mì-shòu bù-zhì fǎ* 秘受不治法 lists. [[KR3el016]] *Diē-dǎ sǔn-shāng huí-shēng jí* 跌打損傷回生集 ([[胡青崑]] Hú Qīng-kūn, 1856) — a three-juǎn Hú-family compilation distinguished by its methodological *diē* vs. *dǎ* (fall vs. blow) doctrine and the order-of-treatment rule (treat injury then *zhèn-jīng*, or vice versa). [[KR3el014]] *Jiù-shāng mì-zhǐ* 救傷秘旨 ([[趙蘭亭]] Zhào Lán-tíng of Tiān-tāi, prefaced 1851, published 1852 by [[管頌聲]] Guǎn Sòng-shēng) — methodologically notable as the first work to *break with* the *mì-cáng* close-keeping tradition of the martial-arts lineage-houses and openly publish their secret prescriptions; bound together by its 1852 publishers with [[KR3el010]] *Diē-sǔn miào-fāng* to give the Huáng-yán region a self-contained traumatology canon.

- **The Sino-Japanese branch.** [[KR3el019]] *Zhōng-guó jiē-gǔ tú-shuō* 中國接骨圖說 / *Seikotsuhan* 正骨範 ([[二宮獻]] Ninomiya Ken of Hamada-han / Iwami, 1808) — the first systematic Japanese orthopaedic-manipulation treatise, synthesising the imperial Qīng *Zhèng-gǔ xīn-fǎ yào-zhǐ* with the *jujutsu* / *kuatsu* 活法 martial-arts revival-technique tradition received from Yoshihara Kyōin 吉原杏隱 at Nagasaki and with Dutch (*rangaku* 蘭學) anatomical sources. Its 15 *mǔ-fǎ* + 36 *zǐ-fǎ* + 150 *róu-fǎ* schema with pictorial-mnemonic names (*Tàn-zhū* 探珠 probing-pearl, *Yuè-yú* 躍魚 leaping-fish, *Xióng-gù* 熊顧 bear-looking-back, *Luán-xiáng* 鸞翔 phoenix-soaring, etc.) vastly refines the imperial eight-method scheme; the work re-entered the Chinese-medicine canon through the late-Qīng / Republican *hxwd* repatriation movement, the same channel that brought [[KR3el001]], [[KR3el003]], [[KR3el008]], and several other overseas-preserved manuscripts back into circulation.

## Important persons

- [[薛己]] (Xuē Jǐ, 1487–1559) — Sūzhōu imperial physician; author of [[KR3el018]] *Zhèng-tǐ lèi-yào* (1529), the only Míng specialist monograph in this division and the founding text of the integrated *nèi-wài jiān-zhì* approach to traumatology.
- [[異遠真人]] (Yì-yuǎn Zhēn-rén, *fl.* Jiā-jìng / 1523) — Chán monk of the Zhèn-guó Chán-sì in Gāo-yóu (Jiāngsū); compiler of [[KR3el010]] *Diē-sǔn miào-fāng* (1523) and *de facto* originator of the Buddhist-monastic verse-handbook tradition of *shāng-kē*.
- [[孫應科]] (Sūn Yìng-kē, *fl.* early 19th c.) — Gāo-yóu literatus who, having broken his upper arm in 1833 and been cured by the methods of [[KR3el010]], underwrote its first printed edition of 1836 — a key moment in the public diffusion of the Yì-yuǎn Zhēn-rén tradition.
- [[吳謙]] (Wú Qiān, *fl.* early Qián-lóng) and [[劉裕鐸]] (Liú Yùduó, *fl.* early Qián-lóng) — co-directors of the imperial editorial commission that produced [[KR3el015]] *Zhèng-gǔ xīn-fǎ yào-zhǐ* (1742–49); architects of the imperial codification of the eight *Zhèng-gǔ bā-fǎ* and of *shāng-kē* as a state-canonical specialty.
- [[錢秀昌]] (Qián Xiù-chāng, *zì* Sōng-xī, *fl.* early-Jiā-qìng) — author of [[KR3el013]] *Shāng-kē bǔ-yào* (1808); the principal Qīng pedagogue of the imperial canon, who reduced the *yào-zhǐ* to thirty-six teachable rules and introduced an early wooden-splint internal-fixation technique (*yáng-mù jiē-gǔ fǎ* 楊木接骨法).
- [[胡廷光]] (Hú Tíng-guāng, *zì* Qíng-chuān, *hào* Yào-shān, *fl.* Jiā-qìng) — third-generation physician of the Xiāo-shān (Hángzhōu) Hú family; compiler of [[KR3el011]] *Shāng-kē huì-zuǎn* (1815), the largest Qīng synthetic compendium and the principal vehicle by which classical *jīng-luò* theory was integrated with bedside bone-setting.
- [[江考卿]] (Jiāng Kǎo-qīng, *zì* Guó-xīng, *hào* Ruì-píng, b. ca. 1770, *fl.* mid-Dào-guāng) — Wù-yuán (Jiāng-xī) village traumatologist; author of [[KR3el007]] / [[KR3el017]] (1840), the most influential mid-19th-century *xiāng-yī* traumatology manual.
- [[趙蘭亭]] (Zhào Lán-tíng, *fl.* early Xián-fēng) — Tiān-tāi (Zhèjiāng) practitioner; compiler of [[KR3el014]] *Jiù-shāng mì-zhǐ* (1851–52); methodologically distinguished as the first traumatologist to break with the *mì-cáng* secret-keeping tradition, and a parallel disseminator of Western smallpox vaccination in Tiān-tāi.
- [[管頌聲]] (Guǎn Sòng-shēng, *zì* Gēng-táng, *fl.* mid-Xián-fēng) — Huáng-yán publisher of the 1852 *editio princeps* of [[KR3el014]], bound with [[KR3el010]] to form a regional traumatology canon.
- [[胡青崑]] (Hú Qīng-kūn, *hào* Làng-fēng, *fl.* mid-Xián-fēng) — Nán-yì physician; compiler of [[KR3el016]] *Diē-dǎ sǔn-shāng huí-shēng jí* (1856), articulator of the *diē* / *dǎ* methodological distinction and of an early form of the acute psychological-shock doctrine in traumatology.
- [[趙濂]] (Zhào Lián, *zì* Zhú-quán, *fl.* late Guāng-xù) — Dāntú (Jiāngsū) practitioner; author of [[KR3el005]] *Shāng-kē dà-chéng* (1891), the most widely cited single late-Qīng *shāng-kē* manual and a principal source for the modern PRC TCM curriculum in traumatology.
- [[二宮獻]] (Ninomiya Ken, *zì* Hikoka, 1754–1827) — Hamada-han medical officer (Iwami, modern Shimane); author of [[KR3el019]] *Seikotsuhan* / *Zhōng-guó jiē-gǔ tú-shuō* (1808); founder of the *seikotsu* 整骨 lineage that runs through the Meiji period into modern Japanese orthopaedic surgery.
- [[丹波元簡]] (Tamba no Genkan, 1755–1810) — shogunal medical officer and Edo bibliographer; author of the 1808 preface to [[KR3el019]] and a principal Sino-Japanese conduit for the imperial Qīng *Yīzōng jīn-jiàn* in late-Edo medical circles.
- [[劉基]] (Liú Jī / Liú Bówēn, 1311–1375, attributed) — early-Míng founding counsellor; pseudepigraphic attribution of [[KR3el001]] and [[KR3el003]]. Not a historical author of medical works, but the prestige-name through which late-Míng military-medical compilations entered the *shāng-kē* canon.

## Topics

- ***Shǒu-fǎ* 手法 — the eight *Zhèng-gǔ bā-fǎ*.** *Mō* 摸 (palpation), *jiē* 接 (joining), *duān* 端 (raising), *tí* 提 (lifting), *àn* 按 (pressing), *mó* 摩 (rubbing), *tuī* 推 (pushing), *ná* 拿 (grasping). Programmatically canonised in the imperial [[KR3el015]] *Zhèng-gǔ xīn-fǎ yào-zhǐ* (1742–49) as the formal taxonomy of manual reduction; absorbed into [[KR3el011]], [[KR3el013]], and [[KR3el005]]; expanded by [[KR3el019]] into a 15-mother / 36-child / 150-kneading nomenclature under pictorial-mnemonic Japanese names. The Qīng eight-method scheme remains the curriculum standard of present-day Chinese-medicine traumatology.

- **The dressing-trio and the wound-pharmacology formulary.** *Táo-huā sǎn* 桃花散 (haemostatic), *Hēi-lóng sǎn* 黑龍散 (anti-inflammatory closure), and *Fēng-liú sǎn* 風流散 (wound-filling) — the canonical three powders shared across [[KR3el001]], [[KR3el003]], [[KR3el007]], [[KR3el008]], [[KR3el017]] and most of the pseudepigraphic / lineage manuscripts. With added haemostatic *Zhǐ-xuè sǎn* 止血散, flesh-generating *Shēng-jī sǎn* 生肌散, anaesthetic *má-yào* 麻藥 protocols (one of the very few pre-modern Chinese surgical-anaesthesia traditions), and the late-Qīng mineral-medicine *shēng-jiàng* 升降 sublimate-precipitate preparations of [[KR3el008]], the dressing pharmacology constitutes the most stable inheritance of the genre across three centuries.

- **The *xué-dào* / *diǎn-xué* fatal-acupoint tradition.** A head-to-foot atlas of points whose injury is prognostically grave or lethal: *xìn-mén* 囟門 (anterior fontanelle), *tài-yáng* 太陽, *tiān-zhù* 天柱, *tū-gǔ* 突骨, *xīn-wō* 心窩 (xiphoid), *hǎi-dǐ* 海底 (perineum), *qì-mén* 氣門 / *xuè-hǎi* 血海 (precordium), *wěi-lǘ* 尾閭, *dān-tián* 丹田, *bǎi-láo* 百勞 and others. Inherited from the Sòng forensic *Xǐ-yuān lù* of Sòng Cí, integrated into traumatology by [[KR3el013]] *Shāng-kē bǔ-yào*, and elaborated by the martial-arts lineages (*Yáng Chéng-bó* in [[KR3el012]], the "Liú Bówēn" tradition in [[KR3el001]] / [[KR3el003]], and most fully [[KR3el005]] *Shāng-kē dà-chéng*) into the *diǎn-xué* 點穴 doctrine that injury to a specific point produces a stereotyped downstream syndrome.

- **Pseudepigraphy and prestige-economics.** A defining structural feature of the genre. Three closely-related Míng / Qīng pseudepigraphic streams — the "Liú Bówēn imperial-secret" tradition ([[KR3el001]], [[KR3el003]]), the Shàolín-monastery attribution ([[KR3el008]]), and the "Yáng Chéng-bó" martial-arts-lineage attribution ([[KR3el012]], [[KR3ee051]]) — each affix a founding-strategist or martial-monastery name to anonymous compilations of lineage-protected wound-medicine. As argued in the discussion of [[KR3el001]], the pseudepigraphic *jìn* 禁 ("forbidden / palace-secret") framing is itself a genre marker, not a historiographical claim — and the *Yòng-yào gē* of [[KR3el001]], attributing its formula to the Hàn-founding strategist Zhāng Liáng 張良, makes the prestige-economics explicit: the secret of healing battle-wounds is presented as the secret transmission of the founding strategist of the dynasty.

- **The Wáng Qīng-rèn *xuè-yū* paradigm in traumatology.** Wáng Qīng-rèn 王清任 (1768–1831) and his blood-stasis (*xuè-yū lùn* 血瘀論) doctrine reshape traumatology pharmacology in the second half of the Qīng. *Huó-xuè huà-yū* 活血化瘀 (blood-quickening / stasis-resolving) drugs — *táo-rén* 桃仁, *hóng-huā* 紅花, *sū-mù* 蘇木, *liú-jì-nú* 劉寄奴, *gǔ-suì-bǔ* 骨碎補 — come to dominate the formulary of [[KR3el007]], [[KR3el014]], [[KR3el016]], and [[KR3el005]]. The integration is most explicit in [[KR3el005]] *Shāng-kē dà-chéng* (1891), where channel-guide (*yǐn-jīng-yào* 引經藥) prescriptions distribute blood-quickening drugs by body-region. *Yū-xuè* (stasis-blood) vs. *wáng-xuè* (lost-blood) — closed vs. open injury — becomes the primary differential of late-Qīng traumatology, with closed injuries receiving *nèi-gōng* (internal attack) and open injuries *bǔ-érháng* (tonify-while-quickening).

- ***Mì-cáng* 秘藏 secret-transmission vs. open publication.** The genre's defining social-historical tension. Most surviving *shāng-kē* texts present themselves as fragments of lineage-protected knowledge: family manuscripts ([[KR3el011]], [[KR3el016]]), monastic transmissions ([[KR3el010]]), martial-arts pseudepigrapha ([[KR3el001]], [[KR3el003]], [[KR3el008]], [[KR3el012]]). The methodological turning point comes with [[KR3el014]] *Jiù-shāng mì-zhǐ* (1851–52), in which Zhào Lán-tíng deliberately breaks with the *jì-jī-zhī jiā* secret-keeping convention and publishes the prescriptions he had collected from itinerant martial-arts traumatologists, citing the mid-19th-century Jiāng-Zhè *shàn-tang* 善堂 charitable-medicine movement as the model. Republican-era publication of [[KR3el007]] in the *Sān-sān yī-bào* (1924) completes the transition.

- **The Confucianisation of *shāng-kē*.** The standing late-imperial argument — running from Xuē Jǐ's [[KR3el018]] (1529) through the imperial [[KR3el015]] (1742–49) to Hú Tíng-guāng's [[KR3el011]] (1815) — that traumatology is *not* a *jiāng-hú* manual-craft but a learned medical specialty requiring *jīng-luò* anatomy, four-diagnoses (*sì-zhěn* 四診) including pulse-method, *Língshū* / *Sùwèn* aetiology, and the elite *yī-àn* case-history form. The *Sìkù tíyào* to the parent *Yīzōng jīn-jiàn* ([[KR3e0090]]) cites Xuē Jǐ's [[KR3el018]] explicitly as the sole prior specialist book and the imperial supplement aims to fill the lacuna — a programmatic statement of the state's role in canonising the discipline.

- **Sino-Japanese transmission and the Edo synthesis.** [[KR3el019]] *Seikotsuhan* (1808) is the most striking case in the corpus: a Japanese physician synthesises the imperial Qīng *Zhèng-gǔ xīn-fǎ yào-zhǐ* with the *jujutsu* / *kuatsu* martial-arts revival-technique tradition of Yoshihara Kyōin and with Dutch *rangaku* anatomical sources, producing a three-stream integration that re-enters Chinese-language circulation via the late-Qīng / Republican *hxwd* repatriation movement.

- ***Diē* (fall) vs. *dǎ* (blow) — psychological-shock doctrine.** [[KR3el016]] *Diē-dǎ sǔn-shāng huí-shēng jí* (1856) articulates an unusually clear pre-modern Chinese doctrine of acute psychological shock: a patient who *falls* sustains injury first and is then frightened (treat the injury, then *zhèn-jīng*); a patient who is *attacked* is frightened first and then sustains injury (calm the fright, then treat the injury). The methodological distinction anticipates the modern concept of psychological-physiological co-pathology of trauma.

## Timeline

- **1523** — *Diē-sǔn miào-fāng* 跌損妙方 ([[異遠真人]] Yì-yuǎn Zhēn-rén) [[KR3el010]] — Chán-monk founding text of the Buddhist-monastic verse-handbook tradition; circulated in manuscript until printed in 1836 by [[孫應科]] Sūn Yìng-kē.
- **1529** — *Zhèng-tǐ lèi-yào* 正體類要 ([[薛己]] Xuē Jǐ) [[KR3el018]] — the only Míng-period specialist monograph on traumatology; foundational *nèi-wài jiān-zhì* doctrine and source of every subsequent Qīng manual.
- **1427–1644** — *Mì-chuán Liú Bówēn jiā-cáng jiē-gǔ jīn-chuāng jìn-fāng* 秘傳劉伯溫家藏接骨金瘡禁方 ([[劉基]] Liú Jī, attributed) [[KR3el001]] — late-Míng pseudepigraphic military-medicine manual, dated by its 1427 preface; *terminus ante quem* the fall of the Míng.
- **1427–1644** — *Jīn-chuāng mì-chuán jìn-fāng* 金瘡秘傳禁方 ([[劉基]] Liú Jī, attributed) [[KR3el003]] — parallel recension of the "Liú Bówēn imperial-secret" stream.
- **c. 1550–1800** — *Jiē-gǔ shǒu-fǎ* 接骨手法 (anonymous) [[KR3el002]] — post-Wōkòu Jiāng-nán bone-setting verse-handbook; conservative bracket.
- **c. 1600–1900** — *Jīn-chuāng diē-dǎ jiē-gǔ yào-xìng mì-shū* 金瘡跌打接骨藥性秘書 (anonymous) [[KR3el009]] — anonymous late-imperial Five-Phase / drug-property compilation.
- **c. 1600–1900** — *Diē-dǎ mì-fāng* 跌打秘方 (anonymous) [[KR3el004]] — anonymous Qīng *diǎn-xué* compilation; *terminus a quo* from the elaboration of the *diǎn-xué* framework.
- **c. 1644–1911** — *Diē-dǎ sǔn-shāng fāng* 跌打損傷方 ([[楊成博]] Yáng Chéng-bó, attributed) [[KR3el012]] — Qīng martial-arts-lineage 72-point lethal-anatomy manual; partner-text of [[KR3ee051]].
- **c. 1700–1900** — *Shàolín zhēn-chuán shāng-kē mì-fāng* 少林真傳傷科秘方 (Shàolín-attributed) [[KR3el008]] — Qīng martial-medical compilation with *shēng-jiàng* mineral-chemistry appendix.
- **1742–1749** — *Yīzōng jīn-jiàn · Zhèng-gǔ xīn-fǎ yào-zhǐ* 醫宗金鑑·正骨心法要旨 ([[吳謙]] Wú Qiān, [[劉裕鐸]] Liú Yùduó) [[KR3el015]] — imperial Qián-lóng codification; sets the eight *Zhèng-gǔ bā-fǎ* as state-canonical curriculum.
- **1808** — *Shāng-kē bǔ-yào* 傷科補要 ([[錢秀昌]] Qián Xiù-chāng) [[KR3el013]] — first member of the Qīng synthetic triad; condenses the imperial *yào-zhǐ* into thirty-six rules.
- **1808** — *Zhōng-guó jiē-gǔ tú-shuō* 中國接骨圖說 / *Seikotsuhan* 正骨範 ([[二宮獻]] Ninomiya Ken) [[KR3el019]] — late-Edo Sino-Japanese-Dutch synthesis; founder of modern Japanese *seikotsu*.
- **1815** — *Shāng-kē huì-zuǎn* 傷科彙纂 ([[胡廷光]] Hú Tíng-guāng) [[KR3el011]] — twelve-juǎn Qīng compendium; the most systematic single-author work in the division.
- **1840** — *Jiāng-shì shāng-kē xué* 江氏傷科學 ([[江考卿]] Jiāng Kǎo-qīng) [[KR3el007]] — Wù-yuán village-doctor manual; circulated in manuscript until first printed in 1924.
- **1840** — *Shāng-kē fāng-shū* 傷科方書 ([[江考卿]] Jiāng Kǎo-qīng) [[KR3el017]] — parallel recension of [[KR3el007]] (the Kanripo corpus's policy of including both extant recensions).
- **1851–1852** — *Jiù-shāng mì-zhǐ* 救傷秘旨 ([[趙蘭亭]] Zhào Lán-tíng, ed. [[管頌聲]] Guǎn Sòng-shēng) [[KR3el014]] — first manual deliberately to break with the lineage *mì-cáng* secret-keeping tradition; published in 1852 bound with [[KR3el010]].
- **1856** — *Diē-dǎ sǔn-shāng huí-shēng jí* 跌打損傷回生集 ([[胡青崑]] Hú Qīng-kūn) [[KR3el016]] — three-juǎn Hú-family compilation; articulates the *diē* vs. *dǎ* psychological-shock doctrine.
- **1891** — *Shāng-kē dà-chéng* 傷科大成 ([[趙濂]] Zhào Lián) [[KR3el005]] — final member of the Qīng synthetic triad; integrates Wáng Qīng-rèn's blood-stasis paradigm; the most-cited late-Qīng manual in the modern PRC TCM traumatology curriculum.
