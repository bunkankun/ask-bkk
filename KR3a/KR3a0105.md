# Yù dìng xiào jīng yǎn yì 御定孝經衍義
*Imperial-Approved Extension of the Meaning of the* Xiào jīng
imperially commissioned; supervised by [[葉方藹]] (Yè Fāng-ǎi, 1629–1682, 清, 監修) and [[張英]] (Zhāng Yīng, 1637–1708, 清, 監修); compiled-in-chief by [[韓菼]] (Hán Tǎn, 1637–1704, 清, 編纂)

## About the work

A 100-juan imperial extension-and-elaboration of the *Xiào jīng* (Classic of Filial Piety), commissioned by Shùnzhì in *Shùnzhì* 13 (1656) — extending the *yǎn-yì* tradition that began with Zhēn Déxiù's *Dà-xué yǎn-yì* (KR3a0058) — and completed under Kāngxī in *Kāngxī* 21 (1683) after a long compositional history. The work is the largest single Qīng-period imperial-pedagogical treatise in the SKQS *Rújiā* and the largest *yǎn-yì*-genre work after Qiū Jùn's *Dà-xué yǎn-yì bǔ* (KR3a0080, 160 juan). Yè Fāng-ǎi and Zhāng Yīng supervised; Hán Tǎn led the compilation. The work draws extensively from classical and historical sources, with sustained imperial-court attention to *xiào* as the foundation of *zhì-tiān-xià* — the Confucian-orthodox extension of family-ethical practice into political-administrative principle.

### Abstract

The *Yù dìng xiào jīng yǎn yì* is the major Qīng imperial pedagogical treatise on *xiào*, completed under Kāngxī's continuing direction across nearly three decades from Shùnzhì's initial commission. The composition window spans 1656 (Shùnzhì commission) to 1683 (Kāngxī completion). The frontmatter brackets to 1656–1683.

The substantive structure follows the *yǎn-yì* tradition: *xiào jīng* core text → topical extension across imperial-pedagogical categories. Within the broader *yǎn-yì* genre, this work joined Zhēn Déxiù's *Dà-xué yǎn-yì*, Qiū Jùn's *Dà-xué yǎn-yì bǔ*, Xià Liángshèng's *Zhōng yōng yǎn yì* (KR3a0093), and Zhàn Ruòshuǐ's *Gé wù tōng* (KR3a0094) as the canonical imperial-pedagogical works of the Sòng-Yuán-Míng-Qīng tradition.

The bibliographic record: SKQS *Zǐ-bù — Rújiā lèi*; *Wén-yuān-gé shū-mù*. Substantial late-imperial reception via the imperial state-school system and diplomatic adoption in Korean / Japanese / Vietnamese curricula.
