# Yù dìng xiào jīng yǎn yì 御定孝經衍義
*Imperial-Approved Extension of the Meaning of the* Xiào jīng
imperially commissioned; supervised by [[葉方藹]] (Yè Fāngǎi, 1629–1682, 清, 監修) and [[張英]] (Zhāng Yīng, 1637–1708, 清, 監修); compiled-in-chief by [[韓菼]] (Hán Tǎn, 1637–1704, 清, 編纂)

## About the work

A 100-juan imperial extension-and-elaboration of the *Xiào jīng* (Classic of Filial Piety), commissioned by Shùnzhì in *Shùnzhì* 13 (1656) — extending the *yǎnyì* tradition that began with Zhēn Déxiù's *Dàxué yǎnyì* ([[KR3a0058]]) — and completed under Kāngxī in *Kāngxī* 21 (1683) after a long compositional history. The work is the largest single Qīng-period imperial-pedagogical treatise in the SKQS *Rújiā* and the largest *yǎnyì*-genre work after Qiū Jùn's *Dàxué yǎnyì bǔ* ([[KR3a0080]], 160 juan). Yè Fāngǎi and Zhāng Yīng supervised; Hán Tǎn led the compilation. The work draws extensively from classical and historical sources, with sustained imperial-court attention to *xiào* as the foundation of *zhìtiānxià* — the Confucian-orthodox extension of family-ethical practice into political-administrative principle.

### Abstract

The *Yù dìng xiào jīng yǎn yì* is the major Qīng imperial pedagogical treatise on *xiào*, completed under Kāngxī's continuing direction across nearly three decades from Shùnzhì's initial commission. The composition window spans 1656 (Shùnzhì commission) to 1683 (Kāngxī completion). The frontmatter brackets to 1656–1683.

The substantive structure follows the *yǎnyì* tradition: *xiào jīng* core text → topical extension across imperial-pedagogical categories. Within the broader *yǎnyì* genre, this work joined Zhēn Déxiù's *Dàxué yǎnyì*, Qiū Jùn's *Dàxué yǎnyì bǔ*, Xià Liángshèng's *Zhōng yōng yǎn yì* ([[KR3a0093]]), and Zhàn Ruòshuǐ's *Gé wù tōng* ([[KR3a0094]]) as the canonical imperial-pedagogical works of the SòngYuánMíngQīng tradition.

The bibliographic record: SKQS *Zǐbù — Rújiā lèi*; *Wényuāngé shūmù*. Substantial late-imperial reception via the imperial state-school system and diplomatic adoption in Korean / Japanese / Vietnamese curricula.
