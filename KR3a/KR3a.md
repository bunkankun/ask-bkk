# KR3a 儒家類 — Confucian Writers

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3a sits at the head of the *Sìkù* Masters Branch (子部) and gathers the works that the Qīng editors classed as *Rújiā* 儒家 — Confucian thinkers and statecraft writers whose books do not belong to the canonical *jīng* corpus in `KR1`. Following Wilkinson (§73.2.2; cf. *Box 284* and §28.7.3), the *Rújiā* category is the oldest of the fourfold *zǐbù* divisions, traceable to the *Hànshū yìwén zhì* "Confucian writers" rubric (53 titles) and re-anchored under the *Sìkù* as the leading subdivision of the Masters Branch on the strength of its post-Sòng *Lǐxué* expansion. The works collected here therefore form a single, continuous tradition reaching from the late Warring States *zǐ*-books that survived the early imperial canon-formation (only *Xúnzǐ* [[KR3a0002]] is pre-Hàn) through Hàn ministerial writings, Wèi-Jìn *zhū-zǐ*, Suí-Táng *jiā-xùn* / imperial-instructional works, and — overwhelmingly — the Sòng-Yuán-Míng-Qīng *Lǐxué* corpus that became the orthodoxy of late-imperial scholarship and education.

The chronological span runs from c. 280 BCE (the working life of Xún Kuàng) to the mid-eighteenth century (the Qiánlóng-era imperial readings, e.g. *Rì zhī huì shuō* [[KR3a0104]], and Lǐ Guāngdì's *Róng-cūn yǔ-lù* [[KR3a0114]]). The principal transmissional traditions a reader must keep in mind are three: (1) the textual reconstruction of pre-Tāng *zǐ*-books from quotation-fragments in *lèishū* and commentary, of which `KR3a0119`–`KR3a0158` are *jíyì* reconstructions from CHANT 漢籍電子文獻; (2) the editorial canon-formation of Northern Sòng *daoxué* in Zhū Xī's editorial workshop (the *Yí-shū* / *Wài-shū* / *Cuì-yán* of the Chéng brothers, the *Jìn-sī lù* with Lǚ Zǔqiān, and the posthumous *Zhū-zǐ yǔ-lèi*) — see Wilkinson §28.7.3; and (3) the late-imperial *Yǎn-yì* tradition of *Dà-xué*-based imperial pedagogy launched by Zhēn Déxiù (*Dà-xué yǎn-yì* [[KR3a0058]]) and continued by Qiū Jùn, the Yǒng-lè court, and the Kāng-Qián throne. The Sòng-Yuán-Míng *xué'àn* genre noted by Wilkinson (§28.7.3, Box 162) — Huáng Zōngxī's *Sòng-Yuán xué'àn* and *Míng-rú xué'àn* — provides the standard biographical-doctrinal map across most of the persons gathered here.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The Hàn–Wèi *zǐ*-books.** The pre-Sòng layer is dominated by the *Xúnzǐ* [[KR3a0002]] — the only Warring-States survival, in Yáng Liàng's 818 recension — together with the Hàn ministerial writings: *Xīnyǔ* [[KR3a0004]] (Lù Jiǎ, on the lessons of Qín's collapse), *Xīnshū* [[KR3a0005]] (Jiǎ Yì), *Yán-tiě lùn* [[KR3a0006]] (Huán Kuān's record of the 81 BCE court debate on the salt-and-iron monopolies), *Shuōyuàn* [[KR3a0007]] and *Xīnxù* [[KR3a0008]] (Liú Xiàng's didactic anthologies), *Yáng-zǐ Fǎ yán* [[KR3a0009]] (Yáng Xióng's *Lúnyǔ*-modelled re-canonisation), *Qiánfū lùn* [[KR3a0010]] (Wáng Fú's late-Hàn statecraft), *Shēn jiàn* [[KR3a0011]] (Xún Yuè), and *Zhōng lùn* [[KR3a0012]] (Xú Gàn). The *Kǒngzǐ jiāyǔ* [[KR3a0001]] is the most disputed work of the entire division — almost certainly Wáng Sù's mid-third-century reconstruction, designed to provide canonical ammunition against Zhèng Xuán, though revisionist scholarship since the 1980s has recovered authentic pre-Wèi strata. *Kǒng cóngzǐ* [[KR3a0003]] is the comparable pseudepigraphic problem from the Hàn-Wèi transition.

**The Suí–Táng transitional cluster.** *Fù-zǐ* [[KR3a0013]] (Fù Xuán, Jìn), *Zhōng shuō* [[KR3a0014]] (Wáng Tōng's posthumously compiled Suí-Táng dialogues modelled on the *Lúnyǔ*), and the Táng-period *Dì fàn* [[KR3a0016]] of Tàizōng provide the bridge between Hàn *zǐ* and Sòng *Lǐxué*. *Xù Mèngzǐ* [[KR3a0017]] and *Shēnméng-zǐ* [[KR3a0018]] by Lín Shènsī, and *Sùlǚ-zǐ* [[KR3a0019]] by Zhāng Hú, document the late-Táng Confucian revival that Wilkinson (§28.7.3) traces to Hán Yù, Lǐ Áo, and Liǔ Zōngyuán.

**The Northern Sòng *daoxué* founding corpus.** This is the doctrinal core of the division. Zhāng Zǎi's writings — *Zhāng-zǐ quán-shū* [[KR3a0026]] and *Zhāng-zǐ yǔ-lù* [[KR3a0027]] — together with the *Zhèng méng* and its Míng-Qīng commentaries *Zhù jiě Zhèng méng* [[KR3a0028]] (Lǐ Guāngdì) and *Zhèng méng chū yì* [[KR3a0029]] (Wáng Zhí) record the *qì*-monist branch. The Chéng brothers' sayings, edited by Zhū Xī in 1168, form the foundational *Èr-Chéng yí-shū* [[KR3a0030]], *Èr-Chéng wài-shū* [[KR3a0031]], and Yáng Shí's *Èr-Chéng cuì-yán* [[KR3a0032]]. Zhōu Dūnyí's two foundational pieces — the *Tài-jí tú shuō* and the *Xī míng* (the latter Zhāng Zǎi's, conventionally paired with Zhōu's) — survive in this division only through commentaries: Cáo Duān's Ming-period *Tài-jí tú shuō shù-jiě* [[KR3a0023]], *Xī-míng shù-jiě* [[KR3a0024]], and *Tōng-shū shù-jiě* [[KR3a0025]]; and the Ming "*chāo shì*" 抄釋 set by Lǚ Nán — *Zhōu-zǐ chāo shì* [[KR3a0089]], *Zhāng-zǐ chāo shì* [[KR3a0090]], *Èr-Chéng-zǐ chāo shì* [[KR3a0091]], *Zhū-zǐ chāo shì* [[KR3a0092]].

**The Zhū Xī corpus and its editorial canon.** This is the largest single cluster. The *Zhū-zǐ yǔ-lèi* [[KR3a0047]] (Lí Jìngdé's integrated 1270 recension of the disciples' *yǔ-lù*) is the principal source for Zhū's mature thought. The *Jìn-sī lù* [[KR3a0042]] (1175, Zhū Xī and Lǚ Zǔqiān) is the canonical Northern-Sòng *daoxué* anthology; it accumulated two Qīng *jí-zhù* — Máo Xīnglái's [[KR3a0043]] and Jiāng Yǒng's [[KR3a0044]]. Zhū Xī's other writings in the division include the *Yán-píng dá wèn* [[KR3a0041]] (correspondence with his teacher Lǐ Tóng), *Zá xué biàn* [[KR3a0045]] (polemic against Buddhist-Daoist syncretism), *Yù-dìng Xiǎo-xué jí-zhù* [[KR3a0046]] (with Chén Xuǎn's commentary), and the editorial *Jīng-jì wén héng* [[KR3a0057]]. The Sòng editorial workshop's wider reach is represented by Liú Qīngzhī's *Jiè zǐ tōng lù* [[KR3a0048]] and Zhāng Hóng / Qí Xī's *Zhū-zǐ dú-shū fǎ* [[KR3a0070]]. The Kāngxī court's *Yù zuǎn Zhū-zǐ quán shū* [[KR3a0108]] (1713–15, by Lǐ Guāngdì and Xióng Cìlǚ) made the Zhū corpus the official Qīng *Lǐxué* canon.

**The Lǚ Zǔqiān and Hú Hóng cluster.** *Lì-zé lùn shuō jí lù* [[KR3a0052]] preserves Lǚ Zǔqiān's lectures at his Lì-zé Academy; *Shào yí wài zhuàn* [[KR3a0051]] supplements the *Lǐjì* "Shào yí" with classical exemplars. Hú Hóng's *Zhī yán* [[KR3a0049]] is the *Hú-Xiāng* school's foundational text, methodologically distinct from the Cheng-Zhu mainstream.

**The Zhēn Déxiù / Qiū Jùn imperial-pedagogical *Yǎn-yì* tradition.** *Dà-xué yǎn-yì* [[KR3a0058]] (1229–34) is the most influential Southern-Sòng imperial-instructional work; the structurally complementary *Xī-shān dú-shū jì* [[KR3a0059]], *Xīn jīng* [[KR3a0060]], and *Zhèng jīng* [[KR3a0061]] complete the Zhēn corpus. Qiū Jùn's *Dà-xué yǎn-yì bǔ* [[KR3a0080]] (1488, 160 *juǎn*) supplied the *zhì guó* and *píng tiān-xià* outer-*tiáo-mù* treatment Zhēn had deliberately omitted. The pair became the canonical late-imperial imperial-instructional dyad, with parallel reception in Chosŏn Korea and Tokugawa Japan. Xià Liángshèng's *Zhōng yōng yǎn yì* [[KR3a0093]] and the Qīng *Yù dìng xiào jīng yǎn yì* [[KR3a0105]] extend the *Yǎn-yì* genre to the *Zhōngyōng* and *Xiàojīng*.

**Sòng glossaries, commonplaces, and reading-records.** *Běixī zì yì* [[KR3a0065]] (Chén Chún, 1216–23) is the standard Northern-Sòng *Lǐxué* technical glossary; *Huáng-shì rì chāo* [[KR3a0064]] (Huáng Zhèn) is one of the most-cited reading-records of the entire tradition; *Xìng-lǐ qún-shū jù-jiě* [[KR3a0067]] (Xióng Jié and Xióng Gāngdà) anthologises the doctrinal corpus.

**The Yǒng-lè *Xìng-lǐ dà-quán* and the Míng Cheng-Zhu mainstream.** *Xìng-lǐ dà quán shū* [[KR3a0078]] (1414–15) is the foundational Míng *Lǐxué* compendium, the imperial textbook of *Lǐxué* in the *Guó-zǐ-jiàn* and provincial *guān-xué* system. The early- and mid-Ming Cheng-Zhu mainstream is represented by Xuē Xuān's *Dú shū lù* [[KR3a0079]], Hú Jūrén's *Jū yè lù* [[KR3a0081]], Cáo Duān's foundational commentaries on the Northern Sòng masters ([[KR3a0023]]–[[KR3a0025]]), Lǚ Nán's *Jīng-yě-zǐ nèi piān* [[KR3a0087]] and *chāo shì* set, Zhāng Jiǔsháo's *Lǐ-xué lèi biān* [[KR3a0077]], and Luó Qīnshùn's *Kùn-zhī jì* [[KR3a0084]] — the most influential Míng *qì*-monist response to Wáng Yáng-míng's *Xīnxué*.

**Late-Ming *shèn-dú* and Donglin-adjacent moral pedagogy.** *Liú-zǐ yí-shū* [[KR3a0097]] and *Rén pǔ* [[KR3a0098]] of Liú Zōngzhōu are the canonical late-Ming *shèn-dú* texts, foundational for the early-Qīng Huáng Zōngxī / Chén Què / Zhāng Lǚxiáng tradition. Huáng Dàozhōu's *Róng tán wèn yè* [[KR3a0099]] and Lǚ Kūn's *Shēn yín yǔ zhāi* [[KR3a0096]] sit in the same late-Ming moral-cultivation register.

**Domestic-instruction and *jiā-xùn* texts.** *Jiā fàn* [[KR3a0020]] (Sīmǎ Guāng), *Yuán-shì shì fàn* [[KR3a0040]] (Yuán Cǎi), *Nèi xùn* [[KR3a0076]] (Empress Xú of the Yǒng-lè court), *Wēn-shì mǔ xùn* [[KR3a0100]], *Xiàng-shì jiā shuō* [[KR3a0062]], and the Yuán *Chéng-shì jiā-shú dú-shū fēn-nián rì-chéng* [[KR3a0033]] (Chéng Duānlǐ) are the principal *Rújiā*-classed household-instruction texts.

**The Qīng imperial-Confucian canon.** *Yù dìng zī zhèng yào lǎn* [[KR3a0101]] (Shùnzhì), *Shèng yù guǎng xùn* [[KR3a0102]] (Yōngzhèng's 1724 amplification of Kāngxī's sixteen maxims, read monthly at *xiāng yuē* convocations throughout the empire), *Shèngzǔ Rén-huángdì tíng-xùn gé yán* [[KR3a0103]], *Rì zhī huì shuō* [[KR3a0104]] (Qiánlóng), *Yù dìng nèi-zé yǎn yì* [[KR3a0106]], *Yù zuǎn xìng-lǐ jīng yì* [[KR3a0107]] (Kāngxī, 1714–15, by Lǐ Guāngdì — the deliberate abridgment of the Yǒng-lè *Dà-quán*), *Qīn dìng zhí zhōng chéng xiàn* [[KR3a0109]], and *Yù lǎn jīng-shǐ jiǎng yì* [[KR3a0110]] constitute the official Qīng *Lǐxué* corpus.

**Early- and mid-Qīng scholar-officials.** *Sī biàn lù jí yào* [[KR3a0111]] (Lù Shìyí, edited by Zhāng Bóxíng), *Dú Zhū suí bǐ* [[KR3a0115]], *Sān-yú-táng shèng yán* [[KR3a0116]], and *Sōng-yáng chāo cún* [[KR3a0117]] (Lù Lǒngqí), and *Róng-cūn yǔ-lù* [[KR3a0114]] (Lǐ Guāngdì) are the principal early-Qīng Cheng-Zhu-loyalist sources outside the imperial commissions.

**The CHANT *jíyì* fragments (`KR3a0119`–`KR3a0158`).** This second layer — added to the division from sources outside the WYG *Sìkù* — comprises forty *jíyì* reconstructions of Three-Kingdoms / Jìn / Nán-Běi-Cháo *zhū-zǐ* whose full texts were lost between the Táng and the Sòng but quotation-fragments of which survive in *lèishū* (*Yìlín*, *Yìwén lèijù*, *Tàipíng yùlǎn*, etc.). The cluster includes *Zhōushēngzǐ yàolùn* [[KR3a0119]], the two *Zhèng-lùn* by Wáng Sù [[KR3a0129]] and Yuán Zhǔn [[KR3a0130]], *Qiáozǐ fǎxùn* [[KR3a0123]] of Qiáo Zhōu of Shǔ, Yuán Zhǔn's *Yuán-zǐ zhèng-shū* [[KR3a0124]], Gàn Bǎo's *Gān-zǐ* [[KR3a0154]] (the same Gàn Bǎo as the *Sōu shén jì* compiler), and the rare Wú-period texts *Tōngyǔ* [[KR3a0122]], *Lùzǐ* [[KR3a0137]], *Tángzǐ* [[KR3a0135]], and *Zhūgězǐ* [[KR3a0146]] (Zhūgě Kè, son of Zhūgě Jǐn).

## Important persons

- [[荀況]] — Warring-States master, the only pre-Hàn author in the division; *Xúnzǐ* [[KR3a0002]] grounds the *Rújiā* category historically.
- [[揚雄]] — Hàn philosopher and lexicographer, author of *Yáng-zǐ Fǎ yán* [[KR3a0009]] and the pivotal early model of a *Lúnyǔ*-style re-canonisation.
- [[劉向]] — Hàn imperial-archive editor and compiler of *Shuōyuàn* [[KR3a0007]] and *Xīnxù* [[KR3a0008]]; foundational to the early-imperial *Rújiā* anthology tradition.
- [[王肅]] — Wèi-period commentator whose contested *Kǒngzǐ jiāyǔ* [[KR3a0001]], *Wáng-zǐ zhèng-lùn* [[KR3a0129]] and *Wáng Shǐ xīnshū* [[KR3a0121]] are the principal anti-Zhèng-Xuán Confucian works in the division.
- [[王通]] — Suí Confucian who, through his posthumously compiled *Zhōng shuō* [[KR3a0014]], became one of the announced precursors of the Sòng Confucian revival.
- [[張載]] — Northern-Sòng founder, the principal *qì*-monist source; *Zhāng-zǐ quán-shū* [[KR3a0026]], *Zhāng-zǐ yǔ-lù* [[KR3a0027]], and (in commentary) *Zhèng méng* dominate the Zhāng cluster.
- [[程顥]] and [[程頤]] — Northern-Sòng founders of *Luò-xué*; the *Èr-Chéng yí-shū* / *wài-shū* / *cuì-yán* [[KR3a0030]]–[[KR3a0032]] is the most-cited source-text of the entire Sòng-Yuán-Míng *Lǐxué* corpus.
- [[朱熹]] — the dominant figure of the division by volume and influence: editorial work on the Chéng brothers, sole or joint authorship of *Jìn-sī lù* [[KR3a0042]], *Zhū-zǐ yǔ-lèi* [[KR3a0047]], *Yán-píng dá wèn* [[KR3a0041]], *Zá xué biàn* [[KR3a0045]], *Xiǎo-xué* [[KR3a0046]], *Jīng-jì wén héng* [[KR3a0057]], etc.
- [[呂祖謙]] — Southern-Sòng co-editor of *Jìn-sī lù* [[KR3a0042]]; author of *Lì-zé lùn shuō jí lù* [[KR3a0052]] and *Shào yí wài zhuàn* [[KR3a0051]].
- [[眞德秀]] — author of *Dà-xué yǎn-yì* [[KR3a0058]], *Xī-shān dú-shū jì* [[KR3a0059]], *Xīn jīng* [[KR3a0060]], *Zhèng jīng* [[KR3a0061]]; founder of the late-imperial imperial-pedagogical *Yǎn-yì* tradition.
- [[陳淳]] — author of *Běixī zì yì* [[KR3a0065]], the standard Sòng-Yuán-Míng *Lǐxué* technical glossary.
- [[黃震]] — author of *Huáng-shì rì chāo* [[KR3a0064]], a foundational late-Sòng reading-record.
- [[呂柟]] — Míng Cheng-Zhu scholar, author of the four *chāo shì* anthologies [[KR3a0089]]–[[KR3a0092]] and *Jīng-yě-zǐ nèi piān* [[KR3a0087]].
- [[羅欽順]] — Míng *qì*-monist author of *Kùn-zhī jì* [[KR3a0084]], the major Cheng-Zhu response to Wáng Yáng-míng.
- [[丘濬]] — author of *Dà-xué yǎn-yì bǔ* [[KR3a0080]], the canonical Míng continuation of Zhēn Déxiù.
- [[薛瑄]] and [[胡居仁]] — exemplary early- and mid-Ming Cheng-Zhu scholars (*Dú shū lù* [[KR3a0079]] and *Jū yè lù* [[KR3a0081]]).
- [[劉宗周]] — late-Ming founder of the *shèn-dú* school; *Liú-zǐ yí-shū* [[KR3a0097]] and *Rén pǔ* [[KR3a0098]] are the canonical sources, central to the early-Qīng Huáng Zōngxī tradition.
- [[李光地]] — Kāngxī-court Cheng-Zhu loyalist whose imperially commissioned *Yù zuǎn xìng-lǐ jīng yì* [[KR3a0107]] and *Yù zuǎn Zhū-zǐ quán shū* [[KR3a0108]] established the Qīng official *Lǐxué* canon; his private *Róng-cūn yǔ-lù* [[KR3a0114]] and *Zhù jiě Zhèng méng* [[KR3a0028]] supplement the imperial commissions.
- [[陸隴其]] — early-Qīng Cheng-Zhu purist, author of *Dú Zhū suí bǐ* [[KR3a0115]], *Sān-yú-táng shèng yán* [[KR3a0116]], *Sōng-yáng chāo cún* [[KR3a0117]].
- [[陸世儀]] — author of *Sī biàn lù jí yào* [[KR3a0111]], one of the major early-Qīng systematic Confucian treatises.

## Topics

The principal interpretive problems running through the division are these:

1. **Cheng-Zhu *Lǐxué* versus *Xīnxué*.** The orthodox *daotong* line — Zhōu Dūnyí → Zhāng Zǎi → the two Chéngs → Zhū Xī — is the dominant trajectory in this *zǐ-bù* category. The Lù Jiǔyuán / Wáng Yáng-míng line of *Xīnxué* is largely classified in `KR3` elsewhere, but the division registers the dispute directly through Luó Qīnshùn's *Kùn-zhī jì* [[KR3a0084]] and indirectly through the Wàn-lì-period writings of Lǚ Kūn, Liú Zōngzhōu, and the early-Qīng Cheng-Zhu loyalists Lù Lǒngqí and Lǐ Guāngdì.

2. **The *daotong* and the late-Táng / Northern-Sòng revival.** Wilkinson (§28.7.3) traces the *Lǐxué* origin to the late-Táng revival of Hán Yù, Lǐ Áo, and Liǔ Zōngyuán; the division registers the immediate consequences in Lín Shènsī, Zhāng Hú, Sīmǎ Guāng (*Jiā fàn* [[KR3a0020]]), Fàn Zǔyǔ (*Dì xué* [[KR3a0021]]), and Wáng Kāizǔ (*Rú zhì biān* [[KR3a0022]]).

3. **Statecraft and imperial pedagogy.** The *Yǎn-yì* / *Yǎn-yì bǔ* / *Zhōng yōng yǎn yì* tradition, together with the Qīng *Yù dìng / Yù zuǎn* corpus and the *Shèng yù guǎng xùn* [[KR3a0102]], constitutes the Confucian statecraft strand classified here rather than in `KR3i` 政書 — works that are doctrinally Confucian rather than administratively documentary.

4. **Cultivation, *shèn dú*, and moral self-examination.** The Liú Zōngzhōu line of *Rén pǔ* / *Jì guò gé* moral-bookkeeping is the apex of this strand within KR3a; the earlier *Jiè zǐ tōng lù* [[KR3a0048]], *Xīn jīng* [[KR3a0060]], *Jiā fàn* [[KR3a0020]], and *Yuán-shì shì fàn* [[KR3a0040]] provide the longer pedagogical-tradition background.

5. **Reconstruction of lost *zhū-zǐ*.** The forty CHANT *jíyì* entries (`KR3a0119`–`KR3a0158`) raise problems of fragment-attribution, *lèishū*-citation reliability, and the relationship between the lost Six-Dynasties *zhū-zǐ* and the Sòng catalogue tradition.

6. **The pseudepigraphic problem.** *Kǒngzǐ jiāyǔ* [[KR3a0001]] and *Kǒng cóngzǐ* [[KR3a0003]] are the canonical cases — Wáng-Sù-era reconstructions that nevertheless preserve genuine pre-Wèi material recovered from excavated manuscripts since the 1970s.

7. **The dialectic between Yǒng-lè *Dà-quán* and Kāngxī *Jīng yì*.** The Qīng court's deliberate replacement of the Yǒng-lè *Xìng-lǐ dà quán shū* [[KR3a0078]] by Lǐ Guāngdì's abridged *Yù zuǎn xìng-lǐ jīng yì* [[KR3a0107]] is the most explicit late-imperial canon-formation moment in the division and is reflected in the SKQS *tíyào*'s consistently dismissive treatment of the Yǒng-lè compendia.
