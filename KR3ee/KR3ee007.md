# Zhēnjiǔ jíchéng 針灸集成
*Compendium of Acupuncture and Moxibustion*
by [[廖潤鴻]]

## About the work
A four-juan late-Qīng acupuncture compendium by the Húnán official Liào Rùnhóng 廖潤鴻 (*hào* Sōngpǔ 松圃; later director of the Imperial Astronomical Office in Beijing), completed and printed in the winter of Tóngzhì 同治十三年 (1874, *jiǎxū*). The work is one of the most influential late-Qīng acupuncture handbooks because Liào reformatted the *Yīzōng jīnjiàn cìjiǔ xīnfǎ yàojué* 醫宗金鑑 · 刺灸心法要訣 ([[KR3ee015]], 1742, the imperial Qīng acupuncture curriculum) into a portable five-syllable verse mnemonic with full point-by-point indications, then supplemented it with copperplate reproductions of the bronze-figure acupoint atlas (the *Tóngrén* 銅人 diagrams, after Wáng Wéiyī, [[KR3ee056]]) at handbook scale "for the convenience of the desktop reader." The text comprises: (1) the verse mnemonic *Kǎozhèng zhōushēn xuéfǎ gē* 考正周身穴法歌 ([[KR3ee032]] in stand-alone form), (2) the indications and clinical applications, (3) the reproduced acupoint diagrams, and (4) chronoacupuncture and apotropaic-day tables.

### Abstract
Liào Rùnhóng's preface (reproduced as `KR3ee007_000.txt`) gives the genesis of the work clearly: serving in Beijing for five years, frustrated at age forty by the lack of progress in his official career and aware that he had a "weak memory" (餘性健忘), Liào set himself to memorizing the *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* acupuncture sections by recasting them as five-character verses (*yùn yǐ wǔyán* 韻以五言); he then "near at hand on his own body, constantly traced and palpated, until he forgot to sleep or eat" (近取諸身，時嘗尋按，至忘寢食). The result, the *Zhēnjiǔ jíchéng*, was prepared as a personal aid but published at the urging of his bookseller-friend Ráo Sōngpǔ 饒松圃, who saw its didactic value for beginners. The work belongs to the same Tóngzhì–Guāngxù late-Qīng acupuncture-revival moment as Liào's own *Miǎnxuétáng zhēnjiǔ jíchéng* 勉學堂針灸集成 ([[KR3ee037]]) and is widely cited in Republican-period acupuncture textbooks.
