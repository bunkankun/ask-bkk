# Zhēnjiǔ dàchéng 針灸大成
*Great Compendium of Acupuncture and Moxibustion*
by [[楊繼洲]]

## About the work
The single most influential acupuncture work in the Chinese medical tradition: a ten-juan late-Míng synthesis compiled by the imperial physician Yáng Jìzhōu 楊繼洲 (1522–1620, of Sānqú 三衢, Zhèjiāng) on the foundation of his family's hereditary *Wèi-shēng zhēnjiǔ xuán-jī mì-yào* 衛生針灸玄機秘要 and printed in Wàn-lì 29 (1601). The opening *Zhēn-dào yuán-liú* 針道源流 ("The genealogical source of the way of needling") chapter (`KR3ee027_001.txt`) gives a long historical survey: the *Sùwèn* 12-juan canon, traditionally ascribed to Yellow-Emperor / Qí-bó dialogue but identified by Liú Xiàng with "the various sons of the Hán family" and by Chéng-zǐ with "the end of the Warring States" — analogous in transmission to the *Lǐjì*, gathered by Hàn Confucians; the *Nànjīng* 13-juan attributed to Qín Yuèrén; the lost commentaries of Lǚ Bówàng, Wáng Wéiyī, Yú-shì, Jì-Qíqīng, Yáng Xuán-cāo, Lǚ Guǎng, Wáng Zōngzhèng, Zhōu Zhònglì, Lǐ Zǐyě; the *Jiǎyǐ jīng* of Huángfǔ Mì ([[KR3ee005]]); the *Tài-sù jīng* of Yáng Shàng-shàn; the *Tóngrén tú-jīng* of Wáng Wéiyī ([[KR3ee056]]); the *Yùlóng jīng* of Wáng Guóruì ([[KR3ee008]]); the *Zī-shēng jīng* of Wáng Zhízhōng ([[KR3ee020]]); the *Zhēnjiǔ dàquán* of Xú Fèng ([[KR3ee002]]); the *Jùyīng* of Gāo Wǔ ([[KR3ee014]]); etc. The body of the *Dàchéng* then incorporates virtually the entire pre-existing acupuncture corpus, with Yáng's editorial annotations and his own clinical cases.

### Tiyao
The *Sìkù quánshū zǒngmù tíyào* (*Zǐbù · Yījiā lèi*) carries a tiyao for the *Dàchéng* and praises its comprehensiveness; it remarks that Yáng's work "fully encompasses everything from the Yellow Emperor and Qí-bó to the present day, omitting nothing essential" (上自岐黃下迄今日，要無遺漏).

### Abstract
The *Zhēnjiǔ dàchéng* is the single most influential acupuncture text in East Asian history. Yáng Jìzhōu's editorial method is encyclopedic: virtually the entire pre-existing Sòng-Yuán-Míng acupuncture corpus is absorbed, with Yáng's *àn* 案 ("editorial note") interpolations marking his own clinical experience and his critical comparisons. The work is divided into ten juan: juan 1 the source-tradition essays and the canonical didactic verses; juan 2 the *Biāo-yōu fù* ([[KR3ee017]]) with Yáng's annotated commentary; juan 3 the *Yùlóng gē* (after [[KR3ee008]]) and the chronoacupuncture verses; juan 4 the channel-trajectories with diagrams; juan 5 the head-to-foot acupoint catalog; juan 6–7 the clinical-syndrome chapters with point-selection; juan 8 the special-needling and moxibustion techniques (the *Sòng-ér jiā chuán zhēn-fǎ* 宋兒家傳針法 etc.); juan 9 paediatric needling; juan 10 Yáng's own clinical case-records. The work was reprinted dozens of times in the late Míng and Qīng, translated and adapted into Japanese and Korean acupuncture curricula, and is the principal source for almost all modern Chinese-medicine acupuncture pedagogy. The first edition (1601) was a Píng-yáng commercial print; the SKQS recension is from a Qīng *Yī-tǒng zhèng-mài* 醫統正脈 reprint.

## Other points of interest
The *Zhēnjiǔ dàchéng* is the single most-translated Chinese medical text into modern European languages and the principal source-text of all modern East-Asian acupuncture practice.
