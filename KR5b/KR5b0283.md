# Shè-shēng zuǎn-lù 攝生纂錄
*Anthological Record of Methods for Caring for Life*

## About the work

An anonymous Táng-period single-juǎn anthology of *shè-shēng* 攝生 (caring-for-life) methods, organised thematically under *Dǎo-yǐn* 導引 (gymnastic), *Tù-nà* 吐納 (breath-and-air), *Shí-jì* 食忌 (dietary taboos) and other sections, drawn from a wide range of earlier source-texts including the legendary attributions to *Chì-sōng-zǐ* 赤松子.

### Abstract

The opening *Dǎo-yǐn piān* 導引篇 (Gymnastic Chapter) begins with the *Chì-sōng-zǐ zuò-yǐn fǎ* 赤松子坐引法 ("Master Red-Pine's Seated-Stretching Method"): "*Cháng-guì liǎng-shǒu xiàng-qián* 長跪兩手向前" ("Kneel long, both hands forward, fingers spread"); follow with kneeling with hands at the waist; then with the right hand pulling backward and the left hand at the abdomen; in successive alternations. Performed regularly, "this Method makes the ears and eyes acute, prolongs the years, increases the life-span; the hundred ailments will not arise."

The text continues with quotations from the *Yǎng-shēng yào-jí* 養生要集 (a now-lost Six-Dynasties / Táng compendium): "the *Dǎo-yǐn jīng* 導引經 says — at clear-dawn before rising, first knock the teeth twice-seven times, close the eyes and clench the fists, rinse the throat with saliva, swallow it three times, hold the breath to the extremity, then slowly thrice tap the heels…". Subsequent sections cover seated and standing postures, the morning bedside protocol, the *fú-qì* 服氣 (qì-circulation) sequences and the *shí-jì* (dietary cautions) catalogues.

Schipper & Verellen (*Taoist Canon* 2: 357–358, Catherine Despeux) date the work to the Táng — primarily because of its repertory of citations and its Tang-era anatomical idiom — and treat it as a key intermediary between the early-medieval *yǎng-shēng* tradition and the Sòng-period systematisation.
