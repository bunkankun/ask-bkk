# Guǎng-chéng jí 廣成集
*Collected Works of [the Master] Guǎng-chéng*
by [[杜光庭]] (撰)

## About the work

A seventeen-juǎn collection of the ritual prose of [[杜光庭]] (Dù Guāng-tíng, 850–933), late-Táng and Former-Shǔ 前蜀 Daoist polymath, the most prolific and institutionally central Daoist writer of the early tenth century. The collection takes its title from Dù's honorific *Guǎng-chéng xiān-shēng* 廣成先生 ("Master Guǎng-chéng"), bestowed by the Former-Shǔ emperor Wáng Yǎn 王衍 in 923. The first juǎn signature reads *"Shàng-dū Tài-qīng-gōng nèi gōng-fèng yìng-zhì wén-zhāng dà-dé cì-zǐ Dù Guāng-tíng zhuàn"* 上都太清宫内供奉應制文章大徳賜紫杜光庭撰 ("Composed by Dù Guāng-tíng, Master of Great Virtue *cì-zǐ*-robed, in-residence imperial-edict-respondent-of-literary-pieces at the Tài-qīng Palace of the Upper Capital"), preserving his late-Tang court titulature.

### Abstract

The work is the major surviving compendium of late-Tang and Five-Dynasties Daoist ritual prose: *biǎo* 表 (memorials to the throne), *zhāng* 章 (petitions to the celestial bureaucracy), *cí* 詞 (announcements), *bēi* 碑 (stelae), *shù* 述 (orations), *jì* 記 (records), and *wén* 文 (general prose), composed by Dù Guāng-tíng on behalf of his patrons — the late-Tang court (Xī-zōng 僖宗 and Zhāo-zōng 昭宗), the Former-Shǔ court (Wáng Jiàn 王建 and Wáng Yǎn 王衍), and a wide range of regional officials and individual patrons — for use at *zhāi-jiào* altars and at cult-site dedications. The collection is therefore both a literary monument and the principal documentary source for the institutional history of late-Tang and Former-Shǔ state Daoism.

The opening pieces show the typical range:

- *Dài Táo Fú tài-bǎo xiū Jìn-kǒu huà qǐng-é biǎo* 代陶福太保修濜口化請額表 ("On behalf of *tài-bǎo* Táo Fú, a memorial requesting an imperial plaque for the restoration of the Jìn-kǒu *huà*"). Jìn-kǒu *huà* 濜口化 was the eighteenth of the twenty-four *huà* (parishes) of the original Five-Pecks-of-Rice diocesan system, where the Hàn-period immortal Chén Ān-shì 陳安世 was said to have ascended in broad daylight. Damaged in late-Tang military disorder, the *huà* is restored, and Dù — speaking on behalf of the patron *tài-bǎo* Táo Fú — requests imperial issue of a plaque (*é* 額) under the historic name *Jìn-kǒu* and a re-titling as *Shèng-shòu* 聖夀 ("Sagely Longevity"). The memorial cites the cult's classical Daoist references (Chén Ān-shì, the local *Bāo-nǚ xiān* 褒女仙) and frames the restoration as a service to imperial longevity.
- *Xiè xīn-diàn xiū jīn-lù dào-chǎng biǎo* 謝新殿修金籙道場表 ("Memorial of thanks for the imperial *Jīn-lù* ritual held at the New Hall"), a seven-day-seven-night *jīn-lù zhāi* 金籙齋 (the highest Daoist liturgical observance) held in the imperial palace's new hall and reported by the chief officiant Hé Chōng-huī 何冲徽 and twenty other Daoists.

Subsequent juǎn cover: state petitions for rainfall, harvest, and military success; thanksgivings for imperial favours; petitions on behalf of named officials and consorts at moments of personal crisis (illness, bereavement, infertility, demonic affliction); petitions for the deceased of the imperial family; petitions on behalf of regional officials of Shǔ; and a substantial body of *jì* and *bēi* recording the foundation, restoration, or expansion of specific Daoist establishments. The collection is therefore the most fully documented body of late-Tang and Former-Shǔ Daoist ritual practice surviving today, far surpassing the abbreviated petition-formularies of [[KR5b0320]] in literary elaboration and historical specificity.

The dates of individual pieces, where given, range across Dù's adult career (ca. 880–933). Many can be precisely dated by their references to identifiable historical events (the Huáng Cháo rebellion, the founding of Former Shǔ in 907, Wáng Yǎn's 923 investiture ritual that bestowed Dù's *Guǎng-chéng* title, the 924 *Jīn-lù zhāi* at Chéng-dū, etc.), making the *Guǎng-chéng jí* one of the most precisely dated bodies of medieval Chinese Daoist literature. Schipper & Verellen (*Taoist Canon* 2: 854, Franciscus Verellen) treat it as the central documentary anchor of late-Tang Daoist studies.

## Other points of interest

The collection is one of the richest sources for the documentary history of late-Tang and Former-Shǔ political life and is regularly mined for non-Daoist historical detail (the personal vicissitudes of named officials and consorts, the chronology of military campaigns, the cultural politics of the Shǔ regional court). The *Guǎng-chéng jí* is also the principal documentary witness to the formalisation of the *jīn-lù zhāi* 金籙齋 as the highest state-Daoist liturgy, a status that Dù's redactions essentially codified and that would persist through the Sòng and beyond.
