# KR5b 洞玄部 *Dòngxuán bù* — Daoist Canon → Cavern of Mystery

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR5b 洞玄部 (*Dòngxuán bù*, "Cavern of Mystery") collects 324 works (DZ 317 onward in the Schipper / Verellen *Concordance du Tao-tsang* numbering) — the second of the *sāndòng* 三洞 ("Three Caverns") under which the *Zhèngtǒng Dàozàng* 正統道藏 (1445) is arranged, sitting between [[KR5a]] *Dòngzhēn bù* (洞真部, Shàngqīng-flagged) and KR5c *Dòngshén bù* (洞神部, Sānhuáng-flagged). Notionally the *Dòngxuán bù* is the canonical home of the **Língbǎo 靈寶** tradition — the second of the three medieval revelation-cycles — and the early-fifth-century *Old Língbǎo* scriptures of Gě Cháofǔ 葛巢甫 ([[KR5b0002]], [[KR5b0072]], [[KR5b0233]] and others) do indeed constitute its early *běnwén* core. But, as in *Dòngzhēn bù*, by the time the canon was fixed in the Míng the school boundaries had collapsed: the *Dòngxuán bù* opens not with an Old-Língbǎo text but with the early-Míng [[KR5b0001]] *Hóng'ēn língjì zhēnjūn miàojīng*, placed at DZ 317 because the Yǒnglè emperor [[朱棣]] had prefaced and patronised it in 1420 (Schipper & Verellen, *Taoist Canon* 2: 1210). What follows is a thousand-year stratification: Eastern-Jìn Língbǎo and *Huángtíng* revelations, a thick Táng commentarial and liturgical layer, the great SòngYuán liturgical compendia of the WúYuè *fǎshī* tradition, the Northern-Sòng *Tiānxīn* and Southern-Sòng *Jìngmíng* exorcistic-therapeutic schools, and a late stratum of Míng court-sponsored liturgy.

The division is organised internally by the canonical 十二類 (*shíèr lèi*, "twelve sections") that cut each *Cavern* into the same genre sequence: 本文 (original scriptures), 神符 (talismans), 玉訣 (jade instructions / commentary), 靈圖 (numinous charts), 譜錄 (genealogies and biographies), 戒律 (precepts), 威儀 (liturgies), 方法 (methods), 衆術 (various arts), 記傳 (records and hagiography), 讚頌 (hymns and encomia), 表奏 (memorials). The boundaries in KR5b are clean and contiguous: 本文 [[KR5b0001]]–0071, 神符 0072–0079, 玉訣 0080–0112, 靈圖 0113–0125, 譜錄 0126–0137, 戒律 0138–0148, 威儀 0149–0248, 方法 0249–0274, 衆術 0275–0294, 記傳 0295–0311, 讚頌 0312–0319, 表奏 0320–0324. The 威儀 (liturgy) section is by far the largest at 100 titles, mirroring the *Dòngxuán bù*'s historic identification as the liturgical heart of the canon.

The chronological span runs from the late-Hàn / Wèi *Huángtíng wàijǐng yùjīng* [[KR5b0016]] (before 255) and the late-fourth-century Lingbao revelations of c. 397–402 ([[KR5b0002]], [[KR5b0072]]), through the great LiúSòng codification by [[陸修靜]] (406–477), the late-Táng / Former-Shǔ liturgical and hagiographical œuvre of [[杜光庭]] (850–933), the central Southern-Sòng *Huánglù* compendium of [[蔣叔輿]] and [[留用光]] ([[KR5b0211]], completed 1223), the central Yuán liturgical syntheses of [[寧全真]] and [[林靈真]] ([[KR5b0150]], 321 *juàn*), and a Míng terminus in the Yǒnglè-era Hóng'ēnLíngjì cycle (1417–1424). Date distribution by composition midpoint is broadly trimodal: a Six-Dynasties stratum (4th–6th c., ~45 works, principally the Lingbao revelations), a thick Táng layer (7th–9th c., ~80 works), and a dominant SòngYuánMíng liturgical mass (10th–15th c., ~190 works).

## Important texts and text clusters

**The opening Míng-cult Hóng'ēn Língjì block.**
- *Língbǎo tiānzūn shuō Hóng'ēn língjì zhēnjūn miàojīng* 靈寶天尊說洪恩靈濟真君妙經 [[KR5b0001]] — the opening text of the *Dòngxuán bù* (DZ 317), a Yǒnglè-prefaced cult-scripture for the deified Xú brothers (Xú Zhīzhèng 徐知證 and Xú Zhī'è 徐知諤); the placement at DZ 317 reflects 1417–1420 imperial patronage rather than antiquity.
- The Hóng'ēnLíngjì 洪恩靈濟 liturgical cycle: [[KR5b0152]]–[[KR5b0160]] (the *zìrán xíngdào yí*, *jífú sùqǐ yí*, *zǎo-/wǔ-/wǎncháo yí*, *qíxiè shèjiào kē*, *lǐyuàn wén*, *qīzhèng xīngdēng yí*, and *shìshí*) — a complete late-imperial Daoist cult-cycle for the Áofēng 鰲峰 *Língjì gōng*, plus [[KR5b0221]] *Shéngōng miàojì zhēnjūn lǐwén*. Together one of the most fully preserved Míng court-sponsored cult-corpora in the canon.

**Old Língbǎo revelations and codification (c. 397–477).**
- *Dòngxuán língbǎo zìrán jiǔtiān shēngshén zhāngjīng* 洞玄靈寶自然九天生神章經 [[KR5b0002]] — the cosmogonic core of the early Língbǎo cycle, position 5 of Lù Xiūjìng's *Língbǎo jīngmù*, locus classicus for the *Tiānbǎo / Língbǎo / Shénbǎo = sāndòng = sānqīng* equation that becomes axiomatic Daoist doctrine.
- *Tàishàng língbǎo wǔfú xù* 太上靈寶五符序 [[KR5b0072]] — Eastern-Jìn anthology of Tàiqīng 太清 (Hàn–Wèi) material on the Five Talismans, the mountain-retreat practice, and Yǔ-mythography; position 20 of the canonical Língbǎo list and one of the principal repositories of pre-Língbǎo Tàiqīng Daoism (Kaltenmark, Bokenkamp).
- *Dòngxuán língbǎo yùlù jiǎnwén sānyuán wēiyí zìrán zhēnjīng* 洞玄靈寶玉籙簡文三元威儀自然真經 [[KR5b0233]] — c. 400; one of Lù Xiūjìng's catalogued Lingbao revelations, the protocol-text for the *Sānyuán* 三元 (Three Officers) zhāi.
- Lù Xiūjìng's own four securely attributed codifications: [[KR5b0033]] *Fǎzhú jīng*, [[KR5b0094]] *Zhòngjiǎn wén*, [[KR5b0227]] *Guāngzhú jièfá dēng zhùyuàn yí*, [[KR5b0231]] *Shòudù yí* — the foundational stratum of the early Lingbao liturgy.
- The wider Six-Dynasties Lingbao layer: [[KR5b0003]] [[KR5b0004]] [[KR5b0006]] [[KR5b0008]] [[KR5b0014]] [[KR5b0028]]–[[KR5b0032]] [[KR5b0036]] [[KR5b0040]] [[KR5b0053]] [[KR5b0054]] [[KR5b0065]] [[KR5b0118]] [[KR5b0139]]–[[KR5b0143]] [[KR5b0235]] [[KR5b0314]] [[KR5b0316]] — together the densest concentration of pre-Sòng Lingbao scriptural material in the canon.

**The *Huángtíng jīng* 黃庭經 corpus.**
- *Tàishàng huángtíng nèijǐng yùjīng* 太上黃庭內景玉經 [[KR5b0015]] (Eastern Jìn, 317–420) and *Tàishàng huángtíng wàijǐng yùjīng* 太上黃庭外景玉經 [[KR5b0016]] (late Hàn / Wèi, before 255) — the two foundational visualisation-scriptures of the Daoist body-pantheon and the principal sources for the medieval *cúnshén* 存神 / *cúnsī* 存思 meditation tradition.
- The commentary cluster: [[KR5b0085]] *Huángtíng nèijǐng yùjīng zhù (yī)* (Liú Chùxuán 1147–1203), [[KR5b0086]] *Huángtíng nèijǐng yùjīng zhù (èr)* ([[白履忠]], 722–729), [[KR5b0087]] *Huángtíng nèiwài yùjǐng jīng jiě* (Jiǎng Shènxiū). Plus the matching *Huángtíng* anatomical chart [[KR5b0116]] of Hú Yīn 胡愔 (848).

**The *Jiǔtiān shēngshén* commentary cluster.** The single most heavily commented scripture of the *Dòngxuán* layer:
- [[KR5b0080]] *Jiěyì* 解義 ([[董思靖]], 1252), [[KR5b0081]] *Jiě* 解 (Wáng Xīcháo 王希巢, 1205), [[KR5b0082]] *Zhù* 註 (Huá Yángfù 華陽復, 1332, with [[張守清]]'s preface) — the three SòngYuán commentaries, all attached to [[KR5b0002]].

**The *Huánglù* 黃籙 mortuary-zhāi cycle.** The doctrinal and liturgical core of the late-imperial Daoist death rite:
- *Tàishàng huánglù zhāiyí* 太上黃籙齋儀 [[KR5b0210]] ([[杜光庭]], c. 900–933) — the late-Táng / Former-Shǔ codification that fixes the *Huánglù* form for the next millennium.
- *Wúshàng huánglù dàzhāi lìchéng yí* 無上黃籙大齋立成儀 [[KR5b0211]] (57 juǎn; [[蔣叔輿]] 編次, [[留用光]] 傳授; completed 1223) — the most comprehensive Southern-Sòng codification, integrating *léifǎ*, *Shénxiāo*, and *liàndù* into the *Huánglù* programme and the principal counterpart to Dù Guāngtíng's foundational manual.
- The Huánglù satellite-rite cluster [[KR5b0212]]–[[KR5b0220]] (the *jiùkǔ shízhāi*, *shíniàn*, *wǔlǎo dàowáng*, *jiǔyōu jiào*, and *Hétú yǎngxiè sānshíliù tiān / sānshíliù tǔhuáng* sequences) and the *Yùguì míngzhēn* repentance / declaration-of-merit triad [[KR5b0222]]–[[KR5b0224]] (the latter by [[杜光庭]]).

**The *Jīnlù* 金籙 and *Yùlù* 玉籙 zhāi cycles.** The two ritual programmes for living beneficiaries (the *jīnlù* for the imperial house and great clans; the *yùlù* for senior officers and the deceased of the elite):
- *Jīnlù* series [[KR5b0167]]–[[KR5b0192]] — twenty-six liturgies covering the *qǐtán*, *sùqǐ*, *qǐméng*, *bǔzhí shuōjiè*, *zǎo-/wǔ-/wǎncháo*, *chànfāng*, *jiětán*, *shèjiào*, *fàngshēng*, and *qídǎo* rites; [[KR5b0167]] *Qǐtán yí*, [[KR5b0174]] *Chànfāng yí*, and [[KR5b0192]] *Tóujiǎn yí* (compiled by [[張商英]], 1043–1121) are attributed to [[杜光庭]]'s and Northern-Sòng Daoist circles; [[KR5b0183]]–[[KR5b0191]] are Míng additions.
- *Yùlù* series [[KR5b0193]]–[[KR5b0209]] — seventeen liturgies for the *yùlù zīdù* and *yùlù dàzhāi* of one to three days, the latter [[KR5b0201]]–[[KR5b0208]] preserving the Sòng three-day form intact.

**The Ní Quánzhēn–Lín Língzhēn *Dōnghuá* liturgical synthesis.**
- *Língbǎo lǐngjiào jìdù jīnshū* 靈寶領教濟度金書 [[KR5b0150]] and its index [[KR5b0149]] *mùlù* — 321 *juàn*, the largest single-work liturgical compendium in the canon (one tenth of the entire *Zhèngtǒng Dàozàng*), foundational transmission attributed to [[寧全真]] (1101–1181) and given its final form by his eleventh-generation successor [[林靈真]] (1239–1302). Schipper & Verellen call it "the most important liturgical compendium in the Daoist canon"; it integrates Lingbao scriptural learning, Tiānxīn 天心 / *léifǎ*, *liàndù*, and the older *zhāi*-form into a single liturgical system.

**The *Tiānxīn zhèngfǎ* 天心正法 tradition.**
- *Shàngqīng tiānxīn zhèngfǎ* 上清天心正法 [[KR5b0271]] ([[鄧有功]], c. 1090–1116) and *Shàngqīng běijí tiānxīn zhèngfǎ* [[KR5b0272]] — the principal Northern-Sòng witnesses to the *Tiānxīn* exorcistic-therapeutic tradition founded on the 994 discovery of the *Tiānxīn mìshì* manuscript by Ráo Dòngtiān 饒洞天 (see [[KR5b0145]]) at Mt. Sānqīngxūwú. The *Tiānxīn* talismans and seals (the *Běijí qūxié yuàn yìn*, the *Dūtiān tǒngshè* seal) become the standard exorcistic equipment of the SòngYuán *fǎshī*. The associated *Tiānshū yuàn* administrative literature continues in [[KR5b0253]]–[[KR5b0257]] and [[KR5b0324]].

**The *Jìngmíng* 淨明 tradition.** The Southern-Sòng / Yuán *Filial-Way* school centred on Mt. Xīshān 西山 (Jiāngxī) and the cult of [[許遜]]:
- The doctrinal core [[KR5b0257]]–[[KR5b0270]]: [[KR5b0257]] *Jìngmíng tiānshūyuàn dūsī fǎyuàn xūzhī fǎwén*, [[KR5b0258]] *Zhōu Zhēngōng qǐqǐng huàyī* and [[KR5b0260]] *Huángsù shū shìyì mìjué* (both [[周方文]]), [[KR5b0259]] *Gāoshàng yuègōng tàiyīn yuánjūn xiàodào xiānwáng língbǎo jìngmíng huángsù shū* (notes by [[傅飛卿]]), [[KR5b0267]] *Jiǔlǎo shényìn fúmó mìfǎ* (Hé Shǒuzhèng, 1131), [[KR5b0268]]–[[KR5b0270]] the *Fēixiān dùrén jīngfǎ* triad attributed to [[許遜]]. Plus [[KR5b0323]] *Jìngmíng yuàn xíngqiǎn shì* — the principal liturgical-administrative manual of the school.
- The XǔXùn hagiographical foundation in the 譜錄 section: [[KR5b0124]] *Xǔ tàishǐ zhēnjūn túzhuàn*, [[KR5b0131]] *Xǔ zhēnjūn xiānzhuàn*, [[KR5b0132]] *Xīshān Xǔ zhēnjūn bāshíwǔ huà lù* (1247), [[KR5b0133]] *Xiàodào Wú Xǔ èr zhēnjūn zhuàn*.

**Internal-alchemical salvation: the *jìliàn* tradition.**
- *Tàijí jìliàn nèifǎ* 太極祭鍊內法 [[KR5b0251]] and *Yìlüè* 議略 [[KR5b0252]] (both [[鄭思肖]], 1241–1318) — the Sòng-loyalist Daoist's three-juǎn manual of inner *jìliàn*, presenting an internal-alchemical (*nèiliàn*) reinterpretation of an originally external public liturgical genre, with *chéng* 誠 (sincerity) as its Confucian-affected metaphysical foundation. Foundational for the YuánMíng *liàndù* tradition.

**Du Guangting's late-Táng / Former-Shǔ corpus.** Beyond the great liturgical works above, the *Dòngxuán bù* preserves [[杜光庭]]'s hagiographical / miracle-tale output in the 記傳 section: [[KR5b0295]] *Dàojiào língyàn jì* (after 905), [[KR5b0296]] *Lùyì jì* (921–925), [[KR5b0297]] *Shénxiān gǎnyù zhuàn* (after 904), [[KR5b0298]] *Lìdài chóngdào jì* (885), [[KR5b0304]] *Dòngtiān fúdì yuèdú míngshān jì* (901); and the petition / *biǎozòu* collections [[KR5b0321]] *Guǎngchéng jí* and [[KR5b0322]] *Tàishàng xuāncí zhùhuà zhāng* in the 表奏 section. Together one of the densest single-author concentrations in the entire Daoist canon.

**Mountain and *dòngtiān* gazetteers (記傳類).** [[KR5b0137]] *Nányuè xiǎolù* (Lǐ Chōngzhāo, 902), [[KR5b0305]] *Méixiān guān jì* (c. 1296), [[KR5b0306]] *Jīnhuá Chìsōng shān zhì* (Ní Shǒuyuē, 13th c.), [[KR5b0307]] *Xiāndū zhì* (1348), [[KR5b0308]] *Tiāntái shān zhì* (1368), [[KR5b0309]] *Lóngruìguàn Yǔxué Yángmíng dòngtiān tújīng* (Yè Shū, 1114), [[KR5b0310]] *Sìmíng dòngtiān Dānshān túyǒng jí* (Zēng Jiān, c. 1362), [[KR5b0311]] *Nányuè zǒngshèng jí* — the late-Sòng / Yuán sacred-site corpus that complements the [[KR5a]] *Máoshān zhì*.

**The *Wǔyuè zhēnxíng tú* 五嶽真形圖 cluster.** [[KR5b0074]] *Wǔyuè zhēnfú* (Táng, 神符類) and [[KR5b0125]] *Dòngxuán língbǎo wǔyuè gǔběn zhēnxíng tú* (Míng, 靈圖類, pseudepigraphically ascribed to Dōngfāng Shuò 東方朔) — the apotropaic mountain-charts that travel from medieval cosmography to Míng popular cult.

## Important persons

- [[陸修靜]] (Lù Xiūjìng, 406–477) — the foundational figure of the *Dòngxuán bù*: LiúSòng codifier of the Língbǎo corpus, whose 437 *Língbǎo jīngmù* fixed the canonical sequence underlying [[KR5b0002]], [[KR5b0072]], [[KR5b0233]] and many others, and whose four directly attributed liturgical texts ([[KR5b0033]] [[KR5b0094]] [[KR5b0227]] [[KR5b0231]]) anchor the early Lingbao zhāi tradition. The *sāndòng* classification under which the *Dòngxuán bù* is constituted is itself his.
- [[杜光庭]] (Dù Guāngtíng, 850–933) — the single most prolific author in the division: the foundational *Huánglù* manual [[KR5b0210]], parts of the *Jīnlù* series [[KR5b0167]]–[[KR5b0177]] and the *Yùguì míngzhēn* triad [[KR5b0222]]–[[KR5b0224]], the *Dòngyuān sānmèi shénzhòu* zhāi series [[KR5b0228]]–[[KR5b0230]], the five major late-Táng / Former-Shǔ hagiographical and miracle-tale works [[KR5b0295]]–[[KR5b0298]] [[KR5b0304]], and the two great petition collections [[KR5b0321]]–[[KR5b0322]]. His career, anchored at the courts of Tángxuānzōng and the Former Shǔ in Chéngdū, is the principal vehicle by which late-Táng Daoism passes into the SòngYuán liturgical tradition.
- [[陶弘景]] (Táo Hóngjǐng, 456–536) — patriarch of the Máoshān Shàngqīng tradition (more centrally represented in [[KR5a]]); in *Dòngxuán bù* the principal witness is his *Dēngzhēn yǐnjué* [[KR5b0105]] (492–514), and his pseudepigraphic *Chìwén dòngshén sānlù* [[KR5b0294]].
- [[葛玄]] (Gě Xuán, 164–244) — the Three-Kingdoms patriarch of the Gě-Xuán-line transmission claimed by the early Língbǎo school, hagiographical subject of [[KR5b0134]] *Tàijí Gě xiāngōng zhuàn* (Tán Sìxiān, 1377), and the named source-figure of the *jìliàn* tradition ([[KR5b0251]]).
- [[許遜]] (Xǔ Xùn, 239–374, legendary) — the deified Jìn official whose cult underwrites the *Jìngmíng* tradition; named subject of [[KR5b0124]] [[KR5b0131]]–[[KR5b0133]] and nominal author of [[KR5b0254]] [[KR5b0269]] [[KR5b0270]] [[KR5b0275]]–[[KR5b0276]].
- [[司馬承禎]] (Sīmǎ Chéngzhēn, 647–735) — Táng twelfth Shàngqīng patriarch, court Daoist under WǔzōngXuánzōng, author of [[KR5b0115]] *Hánxiàng jiànjiàn tú* and of the encomium [[KR5b0317]] *Shì dìchén Tóngbǎi zhēnrén zhēntú zàn*; ascribed compiler of [[KR5b0084]] *Dòngxuán língbǎo dìngguān jīng zhù*.
- [[白履忠]] (Bái Lǚzhōng, fl. 722–729) — Táng commentator on the *Huángtíng nèijǐng* [[KR5b0086]], whose commentary is the principal early surviving exposition of the inner-visualisation programme.
- [[李隆基]] (Lǐ Lóngjī, Tángxuānzōng, r. 712–756) — preface-author of the imperially-sponsored [[KR5b0120]] *Shàngfāng dàdòng zhēnyuán miàojīng pǐn*.
- [[閭丘方遠]] (Lǘqiū Fāngyuǎn, d. 902) — late-Táng Shàngqīng master and senior editor of the early-Sòng Daoist canonical project; appears as compiler at [[KR5b0077]].
- [[寧全真]] (Níng Quánzhēn, 1101–1181) — Southern-Sòng founder of the *Dōnghuá* line of Lingbao *dàfǎ*, weather-control specialist at Gāozōng's court, nominal transmitter of [[KR5b0150]].
- [[林靈真]] (Lín Língzhēn, 1239–1302) — the actual compiler of [[KR5b0150]] *Lǐngjiào jìdù jīnshū* and the architect of its 321-juǎn programme; the *fǎshī* who renders the entire late-imperial WúYuè Daoist liturgical achievement printable.
- [[蔣叔輿]] (Jiǎng Shūyú, 1162–1223) — editor of the 57-juǎn [[KR5b0211]] *Wúshàng huánglù dàzhāi lìchéng yí*, the canonical Southern-Sòng *Huánglù* compendium; receives the transmission from his master [[留用光]].
- [[留用光]] (Liú Yòngguāng, d. before 1223) — Southern-Sòng Five-Thunder rain-making Daoist of Mt. Lónghǔ 龍虎山, twice summoned to Xiàozōng's court; transmitter of the *Huánglù* programme to Jiǎng Shūyú; his biography by Gāo Wénhǔ 高文虎 is embedded in [[KR5b0211]].
- [[鄧有功]] (Dèng Yǒugōng, fl. before 1116) — Northern-Sòng compiler of [[KR5b0271]] *Shàngqīng tiānxīn zhèngfǎ*, the principal Sòng witness to the *Tiānxīn* tradition; also active as redactor of [[KR5b0145]].
- [[周方文]] (Zhōu Fāngwén) — Southern-Sòng / Yuán Jìngmíng patriarch, central compiler-redactor of the *Jìngmíng* doctrinal cluster ([[KR5b0258]] [[KR5b0260]] [[KR5b0262]] [[KR5b0323]]).
- [[鄭思肖]] (Zhèng Sīxiào, 1241–1318) — Sòng-loyalist (the *Suǒnán* who painted orchids without earth) and author of the *jìliàn* manual [[KR5b0251]]–[[KR5b0252]], one of the intellectually most striking syntheses of Daoist liturgy with Confucian moral seriousness in the SòngYuán canon.
- [[董思靖]] (Dǒng Sījìng, fl. 1252) — Sòng commentator on the *Jiǔtiān shēngshén jīng* [[KR5b0080]].
- [[張商英]] (Zhāng Shāngyīng, 1043–1121) — Northern-Sòng grand councillor and Daoist liturgist; compiler of [[KR5b0192]] *Jīnlù zhāi tóujiǎn yí*.
- [[黃公望]] (Huáng Gōngwàng, 1269–1354) — the Yuán painter, also a Daoist of the Jīn Yuèyán 金月巖 line; named transmitter at [[KR5b0281]] *Bàoyī hánsān mìjué*.
- [[趙宜真]] (Zhào Yízhēn, d. 1382) — YuánMíng transitional Daoist; author of [[KR5b0273]] *Língbǎo guīkōng jué*.
- [[朱棣]] (Zhū Dì, the Yǒnglè emperor, r. 1402–1424) — preface-author and patron of [[KR5b0001]], the cult-foundation of the Hóng'ēnLíngjì block that opens the entire division.

Sānqīngxūwú whose discovery founds the *Tiānxīn* tradition behind [[KR5b0145]] and [[KR5b0271]]–[[KR5b0272]], would belong on this list; person notes for both remain to be created.

## Topics

- **The *sāndòng* 三洞 / 十二類 architecture, and the dominance of liturgy.** Where [[KR5a]] *Dòngzhēn bù* leads with *běnwén* and is heaviest in *nèidān*-commentary (玉訣) and ritual-method (方法) materials, *Dòngxuán bù* is dominated by 威儀 (100 of 324 titles). The genre is the *zhāijiào* 齋醮 — the public Daoist liturgy in which the *fǎshī* 法師 ascends an altar, presents memorials to the celestial bureau, and rescues the dead. The historical reason is that the Lingbao tradition was, from Gě Cháofǔ and Lù Xiūjìng onward, the principal Daoist *liturgical* tradition of medieval China; by the SòngYuán the *Dòngxuán bù* had absorbed virtually the entire institutional liturgy of the Daoist *fǎshī*.

- **The three *lù* programmes — 金籙 / 玉籙 / 黃籙.** The trio of *Register* liturgies for, respectively, the imperial house, the ruling officialdom, and the dead: from late-Táng codification ([[KR5b0210]]) through the Southern-Sòng compendium ([[KR5b0211]]) and the Yuán *Dōnghuá* synthesis ([[KR5b0150]]) to the Míng expansions. The graded liturgical economy these three programmes articulate is one of the principal Daoist contributions to medieval and late-imperial Chinese social organisation — the rite by which the state, the elite, and the family negotiate their relations with the celestial bureaucracy and with the dead.

- **The Old-Lingbao revelation and the *Tiānbǎo / Língbǎo / Shénbǎo* triad.** The cosmogonic kernel of [[KR5b0002]] *Jiǔtiān shēngshén jīng* — the identification of the three primordial *qì* of the *Lónghàn / Chìmíng / Shànghuáng* eras with the Three Caverns of the canon itself — is the doctrinal step by which the early-fifth-century Lingbao movement secures its canonical position. The architecture of the entire *Dàozàng* is the textual aftermath of this single doctrinal manoeuvre, theorised by Kobayashi Masayoshi 1990.

- **The *Huángtíng* visualisation tradition.** The two *Huángtíng* scriptures [[KR5b0015]] [[KR5b0016]] and their commentary cluster [[KR5b0085]]–[[KR5b0087]] [[KR5b0116]] preserve the medieval Daoist body-pantheon — the named indwelling deities of the viscera, the *sānyī* 三一 (Three Ones), the *bājǐng* 八景 (Eight Effulgences) — and the *cúnsī* / *cúnshén* meditation programme that operates upon them. This is the principal cross-period thread running from late-Hàn Tàiqīng practice ([[KR5b0072]]) through Shàngqīng and Lingbao revelation into SòngYuán *nèidān*; the *Huángtíng* corpus is the single most quoted Daoist text in pre-modern Chinese poetry.

- **Northern-Sòng exorcistic-therapeutic ritualism: *Tiānxīn zhèngfǎ* and *léifǎ*.** The eleventh- and twelfth-century crystallisation of a new ritual paradigm in which the *fǎshī* commands the celestial bureaucracy directly, by talisman and seal, to drive out demons and heal the sick — embodied here in [[KR5b0271]]–[[KR5b0272]] (Tiānxīn), the Northern-Sòng Shénxiāo materials filtering into [[KR5b0091]] and the Léitíng-flavoured *Jīnlù* and *Yùlù* expansions [[KR5b0182]]–[[KR5b0192]], and the Southern-Sòng *Wǔléi fǎ* of [[留用光]] preserved in the biography embedded in [[KR5b0211]]. The Sòng *bǐjì* (cf. Hóng Mài, Yí Jiān zhì) corroborates this material from the secular side; cf. Davis, *Society and the Supernatural*.

- **Internal-alchemical re-reading of public liturgy: *jìliàn* and *liàndù*.** [[鄭思肖]]'s [[KR5b0251]] reinterprets the externally-oriented *jìliàn* mortuary rite as an inner-alchemical operation in which the officiant's own body becomes the alchemical furnace in which the souls of the dead are recompounded. The same conceptual move — from external liturgy to inner cultivation — is visible across the late-Sòng / Yuán *Dòngxuán bù* (cf. [[KR5b0091]] *Língbǎo dàliàn nèizhǐ xíngchí jīyào*) and is one of the great SòngYuán Daoist conceptual achievements, parallel to the Southern-Lineage *nèidān* reading of *Wùzhēn piān* in [[KR5a]].

- **The Jìngmíng synthesis: Filial Way, XǔXùn cult, and the politics of moral Daoism.** The Southern-Sòng / Yuán *Jìngmíng* tradition cluster ([[KR5b0257]]–[[KR5b0270]] [[KR5b0323]]) bonds the Daoist ritual programme to a *xiàodào* 孝道 ("Filial Way") moral programme centred on the cult of [[許遜]]. *Jìngmíng* is one of the principal Daoist responses to the moral pressure of post-Sòng Neo-Confucianism, and forms the doctrinal background to several major late-imperial Daoist movements.

- **Late-imperial cult absorption: Hóng'ēn Língjì.** Just as [[KR5a]] absorbs the regional Wénchāng, Yùhuáng, and Zhēnwǔ cults into its scriptural layer, *Dòngxuán bù* absorbs the Yǒnglè-sponsored Hóng'ēnLíngjì cult of the Xú brothers ([[KR5b0001]] + [[KR5b0152]]–[[KR5b0160]] + [[KR5b0221]]) and gives it pride of place at the head of the division. The pattern is by then the canonical signature of how regional cults gain canonical legitimacy under imperial patronage.
