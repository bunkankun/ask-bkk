# Dà-yún-shān-fáng wén-gǎo chū-jí 大雲山房文稿初集
*Prose Drafts from the Great Cloud Mountain Studio, Initial Collection*
by [[惲敬]] (撰)

## About the work

The initial-collection (*chū-jí* 初集) of the collected prose of [[惲敬]] Yùn Jìng (1757–1817, *zì* Zǐ-jū 子居, *hào* Jiǎn-tāng 簡堂, native of Yáng-hú 陽湖, Cháng-zhōu, Jiāng-sū), 4 juan, the principal *bié-jí* representing the Yáng-hú 陽湖 school of *gǔwén* prose. Yáng-hú is the second of the two great early-Jiāqìng *gǔwén* schools, sister-and-rival of Tóngchéng: founded by Yùn Jìng in partnership with [[張惠言]] Zhāng Huì-yán [[KR4f0066]], it diverges from Tóngchéng by integrating *Hàn-Wèi pián-sàn* (Han-Wei parallel-and-prose blend) into the *gǔwén* line, and by drawing more freely on the *zǐ* (philosophical-masters) literature alongside the canonical *jīng* and *shǐ*. The *chū-jí* is organized into *zá-zhù* 雜著 (philosophical-essay, modeled on the *zǐ-jiā* tradition), *xù-jì* 序記 (prefaces and records), *bēi-zhì* 碑志 (stelae and epitaphs), *chuán* 傳 (biographies), *dà-zhuàn* 大傳 (major biographies), *xiǎo-chuán* 小傳 / *wài-chuán* 外傳, *yí-shì* 遺事 / *shù* 述, and *mù-biǎo* 墓表 / *bì-jì* 壁記. The companion volumes — *Dà-yún-shān-fáng wén-gǎo èr-jí* (second collection) and *Dà-yún-shān-fáng wén-gǎo yán-shì* 言事 (memorials and administrative writings) — are bibliographically distinct sub-collections.

### Prefaces

Following the *mù-lù* (table of contents), the *chū-jí* contains an unusually elaborate *Tōng-lì* 通例 ("General Conventions") explaining Yùn Jìng's editorial principles in over twenty articles, each addressing one bibliographic-formal question (how to refer to oneself in different genres; how to record the names of contemporaries; how to handle stele-vs.-biography conventions for an ancestor's name; how to format the title-line for *bēi-zhì* listing rank, office, fief, and posthumous title; how Manchu and Mongol names should be treated; how mourning-place inscriptions differ from common stele-inscriptions; etc.). The *Tōng-lì* is itself a primary document of late-Qiánlóng / early-Jiāqìng bibliographic-genre theory: Yùn writes that *zá-zhù wén* (miscellaneous philosophical essays) belong to the *zhū-zǐ-jiā zhī liú* 諸子家之流 (lineage of the masters-school), citing the *Wáng Zǐ-yuān* method; that *xù-jì wén* uses the Sòng-style *bǐ rén yuē xián, zì-chēng yuē yú* (others called "worthy," self "unworthy") convention; that *bēi-zhì wén* follows Hàn-Wèi tradition by omitting the composer's name in the inscription itself, but recording it in the collection (per Hán Yù's method); that *chuán wén* following the biographies appends a *lùn yuē* (commentator's note) in the manner of Bān Gù 班固. The treatise distinguishes *dà-zhuàn* (major biography, treated as proto-historiographical and so omitted from the collection per Hán Yù's example with Lù Zhì and Yáng Chéng) from *xiǎo-chuán* / *wài-chuán* (lesser biographies, retained), and provides systematic conventions for recording *zì* and *hào*.

### Abstract

Yùn Jìng was a *jǔ-rén* of Qiánlóng 48 (1783) but never advanced to *jìnshì*. He served as a sub-prefect (*tóng-zhī* 同知) and then as a county magistrate in Jiāng-xī (in Wǔ-níng 武寧 and Nán-fēng 南豐), gaining a strong administrative reputation. His mature literary partnership with [[張惠言]] Zhāng Huì-yán — both natives of Yáng-hú, both *jǔ-rén* of 1783, both members of the same Cháng-zhōu *cí-fù* circle — produced the *Yáng-hú* prose school as a programmatic alternative to Tóngchéng. The crucial point of departure is the role of *pián* (parallel-prose) within *gǔwén*: Tóngchéng (especially [[姚鼐]]) had treated *pián* as fundamentally distinct from *gǔwén*; Yáng-hú reintegrates them, on the philological-historical ground that Hàn-Wèi prose itself was *pián-sàn jiān-xíng* 駢散兼行 (parallel-and-prose intermixed). The Yáng-hú position is closely allied with [[阮元]] Ruǎn Yuán's *Wén-yán shuō* 文言說 (in [[KR4f0062]]) and supplied the early-nineteenth-century theoretical basis for the *pián-wén* revival that culminated in [[袁枚]] Yuán Méi's followers and the late-Qīng Cháng-zhōu *cí pài*.

The *chū-jí*'s philosophical-essay (*zá-zhù*) juan contain Yùn Jìng's most ambitious theoretical writings, notably the *Sān dài yīn-yáng wǔ-xíng shuō* 三代陰陽五行說 (a *kǎo-zhèng* reconstruction of pre-Qín cosmology, arguing against the orthodox Sòng integrated *yīn-yáng wǔ-xíng* system); the *Shàng-shū gǔ-jīn wén shuō* 尚書古今文說 (on the Old-vs-New-Text *Shàng shū*); essays on *Lǎo Zhuāng* and the *Ǎn yǐ rán shēng-fēng* (sceptical-Daoist) tradition; and the *Sān guó zhì zhū rén shū hòu* 三國志諸人書後 (postfaces to *Sān guó zhì* biographies). The *xù-jì* and *bēi-zhì* juan contain his prose for the Yáng-hú / Cháng-zhōu network, including biographies of Zhāng Huì-yán and the early Cháng-zhōu *cí*-school masters.

Composition window: c. 1785 (Yùn's earliest mature prose, in his late twenties) through 1817 (his death in Cháng-zhōu). The *chū-jí* is the first of the three SBCK sub-collections; the *èr-jí* and *yán-shì* extend the recension. The *editio princeps* is the Dàoguāng-era imprint by Yùn's son and disciples.

## Other points of interest

The studio name *Dà-yún-shān-fáng* 大雲山房 ("Great Cloud Mountain Studio") refers to the Dà-yún Mountains south of Cháng-zhōu, near Yùn Jìng's ancestral village. The *Tōng-lì* — Yùn's bibliographic-formal manual — is one of the most systematic statements of premodern Chinese *bié-jí* compilation theory in any author's collected works; it is virtually a literary-bibliographic treatise in miniature. Scholars of Qing book-historical convention regularly cite it as documentation of the *gǔwén* school's self-conscious formalization in the early nineteenth century.
