# KR3en — Zhùyóu kē 祝由科 (Incantational Department, Talismanic-Incantational Healing)

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3en is the *zhùyóu kē* 祝由科 sub-division of `KR3e` 醫家類 (Medicine), itself the principal medical sub-section of `KR3` 子部 (Masters). The genre is the **talismanic-incantational healing tradition** — medicine performed by writing healing-graphs (符 *fú*, normally in cinnabar 朱砂 on yellow paper or traced in the air over the patient in *jiànjué* 劍訣 mudra) paired with whispered or recited verbal formulae (祝 / 咒 *zhù* / *zhòu*), and administering the *fú* either by ingestion of its ash dissolved in water (灰服) or by adhesive application as a charm (貼). The *locus classicus* is *Huángdì nèijīng · Sùwèn* 黃帝內經·素問 ([[KR3e0001]]) 13 (*Yíjīng biànqì lùn* 移精變氣論), "in remote antiquity, when treating illness, one had only to transfer the *jīng* and transform the *qì*: one called it *zhùyóu* 祝由 and that sufficed" (古之治病, 惟其移精變氣, 可祝由而已). The decisive institutional moment, however, comes more than a millennium later: in 1268 the Yuán *Tàiyīyuàn* 太醫院 reorganised state-recognised medicine into **十三科** (Thirteen Departments) — 大方脈, 小方脈, 婦人, 瘡瘍, 鍼灸, 眼, 口齒, 咽喉, 傷折, 金鏃, 按摩, 祝由, 禁科 — assigning *zhùyóu* the twelfth examinable specialty (cf. *Yuánshǐ · Bǎiguān zhì* 元史·百官志 [[KR2a0036]] and the early-Míng confirmation in *Dà Míng huìdiǎn · Tàiyīyuàn* [[大明會典]]). The bureaucratic concept *zhùyóu kē* — incantational medicine treated as a "scientific" departmental specialty rather than as Daoist liturgy proper — is post-Yuán; the entire textual corpus collected here follows that conceptual frame, and the term *zhùyóu kē* is itself unattested before the Yuán reorganisation.

The three works gathered under KR3en are not Sòng or Yuán imperial-curriculum textbooks (none survive) but **late-imperial folk-Daoist manuscripts** received in the Kanripo corpus through the *jicheng.tw* 漢學文典 digital collection, whose underlying physical witnesses are the *zhū-mò chāo-běn* 朱墨鈔本 (cinnabar-and-ink manuscript copies) held at the U.S. Library of Congress; the same manuscript family was independently reprinted in the *Hǎiwài huíliú zhōngyī shànběn gǔjí cóngshū* 海外回流中醫善本古籍叢書 (ed. 鄭金生 Zhèng Jīnshēng et al., Běijīng: Rénmín wèishēng chūbǎnshè, 2003), the principal modern editorial channel through which this corpus returned to Chinese-language circulation. The per-text notes accordingly use a *Prefaces* heading rather than *Tiyao* — no WYG-edition material is present and the LoC manuscript carriers transmit only the body of the work without surviving 序 / 跋 / 凡例 / 進表. Endymion Wilkinson, *Chinese History: A New Manual*, Section 41 ("Medicine"), gives the general bibliographic frame for the medical *jiā* but does not treat *zhùyóu* as a specialty; the principal first-tier English-language source for this division is therefore **Hinrichs and Barnes (eds.), *Chinese Medicine and Healing: An Illustrated History*** (Harvard, 2013), especially pp. 18–24 (Shāng exorcistic medicine), 63–69 (Celestial-Masters healing), 87–93 (Six-Dynasties talismanic medicine), and 99–101 (incantational practice within Daoist self-cultivation), supplemented by Michel Strickmann, *Chinese Magical Medicine* (Stanford, 2002). Together with [[KR3el]] (*shāngkē* 傷科 traumatology) — the other late-imperial folk-medical lineage division reprinted out of the LoC manuscript corpus — KR3en is the sub-division in which the *Tàiyīyuàn* curriculum's most ritually-marked specialty surfaces in the Kanripo corpus as living manuscript practice rather than as canonical doctrine.

## Important texts and text clusters

The division contains exactly three texts, all anonymous or pseudepigraphically attributed, all single-fascicle, all of the **late-imperial folk-Daoist talismanic-incantational genre** (1500–1900), and all received via the *jicheng.tw* / LoC manuscript channel. They do not form sub-clusters: each is a single representative of one *facet* of the larger *zhùyóu* tradition, and together they constitute the principal published modern witness to that corpus.

- ***Zhùyóu kē zhū fú mì* 祝由科諸符祕 [[KR3en001]]** ("Secret Talismans of the Incantational Department"), pseudepigraphically attributed to **[[張繼先]] Zhāng Jìxiān** (Xūjìng zhēnrén 虛靖真人, 1092–1127), thirtieth Celestial Master of the Lónghǔshān 龍虎山 Zhèngyī lineage. The *prestige-attribution* witness: the manuscript stakes its authority on the most famous *léifǎ* 雷法 (Thunder Rites) Celestial Master of the Sòng, but the term *zhùyóu kē* itself, the manuscript's codicology, and the absence of any Sòng or Yuán bibliographic record of the title firmly locate the received text in the **Míng-Qīng folk-Daoist transmission**, alongside other pseudepigraphic Zhāng-Jìxiān titles in the Dàozàng (cf. [[KR5b0272]], [[KR5b0306]], [[KR5h0021]]).
- ***Zhùyóu kē* 祝由科 [[KR3en002]]** ("The Incantational Department"), anonymous. The *generic* witness: where [[KR3en001]] claims Celestial-Master authority and [[KR3en003]] embeds itself in named cultic-medical lineages, this manuscript styles itself simply as the practical *zhùyóu* department itself, with paired *fú* / 祝 entries for 諸風, 諸瘧, 諸痢, 諸瘡, 蛇咬, 犬咬, 小兒驚, 婦人難產, 邪祟, and the *qīzhǒng kèxié* 七種客邪. The signal-to-noise specimen of the corpus.
- ***Zīfú děng Qítiānyī shísān kē zhìbìng yì zōng* 資福等齊天醫十三科治病一宗 [[KR3en003]]** ("The Heaven-Equalling Medicine of the Zīfú [Tradition] and Others — A Single Compendium for Treating Disease in the Thirteen Departments"), anonymous. The *cultic-lineage* witness: titles itself a "single compendium" (*yì zōng* 一宗) of the entire **十三科** under the combined banner of the **Zīfú 資福** pious-foundation lineage (Sòng-Yuán Pure-Land / Tiantai *zhuǎnlún* lineage extended into the late-imperial *shàntáng* 善堂 / 義館 charitable-dispensary network) and the **Qítiānyī 齊天醫** ("medicine that equals Heaven") cult of the **Medicine-King 藥王** (identified across cultic strata with [[孫思邈]] Sūn Sīmiǎo of [[KR3e0013]], with the Bhaiṣajya-rāja bodhisattva of the *Saddharma-puṇḍarīka*, or with deified late-imperial physician-saints). The most lineally explicit document of how the entire Yuán-Míng departmental taxonomy could be re-coordinated under a single folk-religious ritual presidency.

## Important persons

- **[[張繼先]] Zhāng Jìxiān** (1092–1127), Xūjìng zhēnrén 虛靖真人 — thirtieth Celestial Master of the Lónghǔshān Zhèngyī lineage; pseudepigraphic author of [[KR3en001]]. Not a historical author of the received text but the prestige-name through which late-imperial talismanic-medical compilations entered the *Zhèngyī* + *léifǎ* + *zhùyóu* triple lineage. The single named figure in the entire division.

The two remaining texts ([[KR3en002]], [[KR3en003]]) are anonymous and carry no author-attribution in the catalog meta or in the manuscripts as transmitted; their lineage information is **collective** rather than personal — the **Zīfú** pious-foundations and the **Qítiānyī** Medicine-King cult of [[KR3en003]], and the *Tàiyīyuàn* departmental curriculum in which all three works embed themselves. *Zhùyóu* literature is structurally a low-named genre: prestige attaches to lineage and ritual office, not to individual authorship, and the absence of a longer roll of named compilers is itself a defining feature of the division.

## Topics

- **The Yuán 十三科 (Thirteen-Departments) frame and the bureaucratisation of *zhùyóu*.** All three KR3en works present themselves under the rubric of the Yuán-Míng *Tàiyīyuàn* curriculum codified in 1268 and reconfirmed under the early Míng (cf. *Yuánshǐ · Bǎiguān zhì* [[KR2a0036]], *Dà Míng huìdiǎn · Tàiyīyuàn* [[大明會典]]). The conceptual move that makes the genre possible is the renaming of an inherited Daoist ritual technique as the twelfth *examinable specialty* — *zhùyóu kē*, alongside *dàfāngmài*, *xiǎofāngmài*, *fùrén*, *chuāngyáng*, *zhēnjiǔ*, *yǎn*, *kǒuchǐ*, *yànhóu*, *shāngzhé*, *jīnzú*, *ànmó* and *jìnkē*. This taxonomic positioning is the textual marker that distinguishes a *zhùyóu* manuscript from a generic Daoist talisman manual.
- **The *Sùwèn* 移精變氣 *locus classicus* and the literate frame.** The classical authorisation for the genre is *Huángdì nèijīng · Sùwèn* ([[KR3e0001]]) 13: "in remote antiquity, when treating illness, one had only to *transfer the jīng and transform the qì* — one called it *zhùyóu* and that sufficed". This single sentence is the literate-medical handle by which a body of village-ritual practice attaches itself to the *Huángdì* canon and acquires a place in the *Tàiyīyuàn* curriculum. The KR3en works quote or paraphrase it in their conceptual framings.
- **Talisman + incantation as a coded prescription.** The unit of practice across the corpus is the paired *fúzhòu* 符咒: a graph drawn in cinnabar (or traced in the air with *jiànjué* 劍訣 mudra), administered as ash-in-water (灰服) or as an applied charm (貼), accompanied by a short rhymed incantation. The talisman is **not** merely a religious object but a coded prescription cross-indexed to the official medical disease-categories — as [[KR3en002]] makes most explicit. This integration of *fúzhòu* with the *shísān kē* taxonomy is the **textual point at which Daoist liturgical and literate-medical classifications most directly converge** in late-imperial Chinese medicine.
- **Pseudepigraphic Celestial-Master attribution.** [[KR3en001]] ascribes itself to **Zhāng Jìxiān** 張繼先 (Xūjìng zhēnrén); the parallel late-imperial *zhùyóu* literature frequently invokes **Zhāng Dàolíng** ([[張道陵]]), the *Tàishàng dòngyuān shénzhòu jīng* [[KR5b0019]] tradition, and other Zhèngyī prestige-names. The pseudepigraphy is genre-marking rather than evidential: the attribution signals the work's claim to participate in the Celestial-Master *léifǎ* + *zhùyóu* combined lineage, not its actual date of composition.
- **Cultic-medical lineages — *Zīfú* foundations and the *Qítiānyī* Medicine-King cult.** [[KR3en003]] documents a second mode of late-imperial *zhùyóu* embedding: the **pious-foundation network** of Jiāngnán and Mǐn-Yuè temple-dispensaries (*sìyuàn* 寺院, *shàntáng* 善堂, *yìguǎn* 義館) running charitable-medicine work as religious-philanthropic activity, presided over by a cultic *yàowáng* 藥王 figure. *Qítiānyī* is one of several such regional designations of the Medicine-King cult, particularly developed in Jiāng-Zhè 江浙, Fújiàn 福建 and Yún-Guì 雲貴 villages from the late Míng onward.
- **The post-Yuán date-bracket and the manuscript carrier.** All three KR3en works carry the date bracket **1500–1900** in the per-text notes. The bracket is fixed at its early bound by the post-Yuán emergence of the *zhùyóu kē* terminology and at its late bound by the codicology of the surviving LoC manuscript carriers; none of the three texts can plausibly be dated more precisely than that. The works are received in the Kanripo corpus through the *jicheng.tw* digital editions of the LoC manuscripts, and reach a parallel modern paper circulation through the **海外回流 (hǎiwài huíliú)** repatriation channel — the same overseas-recovery editorial movement that brought several texts back into [[KR3el]] and [[KR3em]].
- **State demotion under the Qīng vs. continued village practice.** *Zhùyóu* was demoted under the Qīng (the *Qīngcháo wénxiàn tōngkǎo · Tàiyīyuàn* [[清朝文獻通考]] records its eventual deletion from the *Tàiyīyuàn* curriculum), but the practice flourished in the village ritual-healing economy. The KR3en manuscripts are among the few surviving direct witnesses to that practice in late-Qīng / early-Republican form, and constitute the **ethnographic rather than canonical** record of the talismanic-incantational layer of late-imperial Chinese popular medicine — the layer that the standard literate genres (*běncǎo* 本草, *fāngjì* 方劑, *zhēnjiǔ* 鍼灸) silently excluded.
- **The Daoist-canon and *léifǎ* hinterland.** The KR3en works sit at the edge of the Daoist textual world. The Zhèngtǒng *Dàozàng* preserves genuine Zhāng-Jìxiān works on **Thunder Rites** and exorcism (cf. [[KR5b0272]] *Sānshídài tiānshī Xūjìng zhēnjūn yǔlù* 三十代天師虛靖真君語錄, [[KR5b0306]] *Xūjìng chōnghé Xiānshēng Xú Shénwēng yǔlù*, [[KR5c0379]] *Míngzhēn pòwàng zhāngsòng*, [[KR5g0058]], and [[KR5h0021]]); the *zhùyóu* manuals draw on the same *Zhèngyī* + *Shénxiāo* 神霄 + *Tiānxīn* 天心 ritual repertoire but re-package it under a **medical** rubric rather than as Daoist liturgy. The boundary between the KR3en *zhùyóu* corpus and the KR5 Daoist canon is therefore taxonomic, not substantive.

## Timeline

- **c. 1500–1900** — *Zhùyóu kē zhū fú mì* 祝由科諸符祕 ([[張繼先]] Zhāng Jìxiān, attributed) [[KR3en001]] — pseudepigraphic Celestial-Master witness; the *prestige-attribution* facet of the corpus.
- **c. 1500–1900** — *Zhùyóu kē* 祝由科 (anonymous) [[KR3en002]] — generic anonymous *zhùyóu* practitioner manual; the bare *operational* facet of the corpus, with full disease-by-disease *fúzhòu* coverage.
- **c. 1500–1900** — *Zīfú děng Qítiānyī shísān kē zhìbìng yì zōng* 資福等齊天醫十三科治病一宗 (anonymous) [[KR3en003]] — pious-foundation / *Qítiānyī* Medicine-King cultic witness; the *lineage-cultic* facet of the corpus, organising the entire **十三科** under a single folk-religious presidency.

The corpus contains no precisely datable work: all three KR3en texts are anonymous or pseudepigraphic late-imperial manuscripts whose composition windows can only be bracketed by the post-Yuán emergence of the *zhùyóu kē* terminology (lower bound) and the codicology of the surviving LoC manuscript carriers (upper bound). The timeline therefore lists the three works in their structural-conceptual sequence (prestige-attribution → generic operational → lineage-cultic) rather than in a chronological order that the evidence does not support.
