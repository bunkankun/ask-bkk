# Bùkōng juànsuǒ tuóluóní jīng 不空羂索陀羅尼經
*Dhāraṇī Sūtra of the Unfailing Noose* (Amoghapāśa-dhāraṇī-sūtra)
by [[李無諂]] (Lǐ Wúchǎn, 譯)

## About the work

A one-fascicle Tang translation of the **Amoghapāśa-dhāraṇī-sūtra** produced by the lay translator **Lǐ Wúchǎn** ([[李無諂]]) of Lampāka (嵐波國, North India) at the **Fó-shòu-jì-sì** 佛授記寺 (Buddha-Prophecy Monastery) Translation Cloister in 700 CE — the third year of Wǔ Zétiān's Shèng-lì 聖曆 era — at the request of the Korean (Silla) monk **Míngxiǎo** 明曉 (= 明曉). The text is parallel to the Amoghapāśa cycle: T1093 ([[KR6j0301]], Jñānagupta), T1094 ([[KR6j0302]], Xuán-zàng), T1095 ([[KR6j0303]], Bodhiruci), and T1097 ([[KR6j0305]], Bǎo-sī-wéi). It is uniquely valuable as a witness to a slightly different recension of the Sanskrit *vorlage*: Lǐ Wúchǎn's text contains a more developed *vidyā*-apparatus and ritual procedure than the *hṛdaya*-form translations of Jñānagupta and Xuán-zàng.

### Prefaces

The Taishō text is preceded by an introductory **preface (序) by Bōlún 波崙**, *śramaṇa* of the Fú-shòu-sì 福壽寺 (Felicity-and-Longevity Monastery): *不空羂索陀羅尼經序，福壽寺沙門波崙撰* — "Preface to the *Amoghapāśa-dhāraṇī-sūtra*, composed by the *śramaṇa* Bōlún of the Fú-shòu Monastery." The preface celebrates the doctrinal scope of the work — that it "encompasses the two truths without remainder, exhausts cause and effect" (該二諦而無遺，括因果而斯盡) — extols the bodhisattva-mahāsattva Avalokiteśvara as the great compassion of the ten directions, and provides a condensed introduction to the cult of the *Amoghapāśa* (the "Unfailing Noose"). Bōlún records that he himself, though "feeble and dim of mind" (愚暗), has long been devoted to the Buddhadharma and travelled widely between the two capitals (兩京) seeking dharma-friends.

### Abstract

The opening narrative situates the discourse at the *kūṭāgāra* (重閣) of the *Sūrya-tilaka* (日輪頂) — a celestial vantage above Mt. Potalaka — with Avalokiteśvara as the discoursing bodhisattva and the standard Mahāyāna audience of bhikṣus, bodhisattvas, and divine retinue. The *mūla-mantra* (the *Amoghapāśa-hṛdaya*), the *vidyā*-formulae, the eighteen *siddhi*-applications, the maṇḍala-construction (a quadrate maṇḍala with the deity at the centre and four guardian-bodhisattvas at the cardinal points), the *abhiṣeka* procedure, and the closing dedication are all expounded. The text's distinguishing feature among the parallel Amoghapāśa translations is its preservation of a particularly elaborate *kuṇḍa* (火爐) and *homa* (護摩) ritual, suggesting access to a slightly later recension of the Sanskrit *vorlage* than the Jñānagupta and Xuán-zàng versions.

The dating is fixed by the DILA Buddhist Person Authority record (A000518) and the *Kāiyuán shìjiào lù* (T55n2154): *Shènglì sānnián gēngzǐ sānyuè* 聖曆三年庚子三月 — "Shènglì 3, *gēngzǐ* year, third month" = March 700 CE.
