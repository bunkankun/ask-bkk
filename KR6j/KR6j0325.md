# Píjùzhī púsà yībǎibā míng jīng 毘俱胝菩薩一百八名經
*Sūtra of the One-Hundred-and-Eight Names of the Bodhisattva Bhṛkuṭī* (Bhṛkuṭī-aṣṭottara-śata-nāma-sūtra)
by [[法天]] (Fǎtiān, Dharmadeva, 譯)

## About the work

A one-fascicle Northern Sòng *aṣṭottara-śata-nāma* hymn-sūtra to **Bhṛkuṭī** (毘俱胝 *Píjùzhī*, "the Frowning One" — a wrathful retinue-deity of Avalokiteśvara, depicted with three eyes and a frowning expression, holding the *triśūla* trident) translated by **Dharmadeva** ([[法天]]). The deity Bhṛkuṭī, like Tārā, is one of the principal female emanations of Avalokiteśvara in Indian Tantric Buddhism, conventionally pictured as the wrathful counterpart to Tārā's peaceful aspect. The text is parallel in form to the Tārā *aṣṭottara-śata-nāma* texts ([[KR6j0314]]–[[KR6j0316]]) translated in the same Sòng *Yìjīngyuàn* programme.

### Prefaces

The opening *namaskāra*: 歸命一切如來應供遍知覺。我今說此一切如來心真言。若有天人持明仙眾。歸命供養... — "I take refuge in all *Tathāgatas*, *Arhats*, *Samyak-sambuddhas*. Now I shall expound this *hṛdaya-mantra* of all *Tathāgatas*. If there be devas, humans, *vidyādharas*, or *ṛṣis* who take refuge and make offering..."

### Abstract

The text expounds the **one-hundred-and-eight names of Bhṛkuṭī** in *aṣṭottara-śata-nāma-stotra* form. Each of the deity's epithets — *Bhṛkuṭī*, *Trinētrā* ("Three-Eyed"), *Triśūla-pāṇi* ("Trident-Bearer"), *Mahā-yakṣiṇī*, *Vajra-vidyā-rāñjī*, etc. — is given as a *namaskāra* invocation. The text catalogues the iconographic specifications (the deity's three eyes, frowning expression, trident, and her position in the Avalokiteśvara *kalpa-rāja* maṇḍala), the *mūla-mantra* and the secondary *vidyā*-formulae, and the *siddhi*-applications particularly for the wrathful operations: protection against malevolent spirits, the binding of demons, and the destruction of supernatural obstacles.

The text is one of the principal Sòng-period Bhṛkuṭī witnesses and provides the canonical reference for the Bhṛkuṭī cult that, while less prominent than the Tārā cult in the Chinese tradition, was a continuing presence in the Sòng-period Esoteric devotional repertoire.
