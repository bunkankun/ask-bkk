# KR2n 目錄類 — Bibliographic and Epigraphic Catalogues

## Scope and scholarly tradition

*Mùlù* 目錄 — literally "tables and entries" — is the bibliographic-catalogue branch of the *shǐbù* 史部 (History division) in the Sìkù taxonomy. In Wilkinson's *Chinese History: A New Manual* it occupies §73.3 ("Catalogs of books & epigraphy"), and the *Sìkù quánshū* admits exactly 47 titles into the category — precisely the count of works collected in KR2n. The division splits into two internal *zhǔ* 屬 (sub-classes), reflected in the catalogue itself: the *jīngjí zhī shǔ* 經籍之屬 (catalogues of books, KR2n0001–KR2n0011) and the *jīnshí zhī shǔ* 金石之屬 (catalogues of inscriptions in bronze and stone, KR2n0012–KR2n0047). Both grew out of activities that flourished from the eleventh century onward: the systematic cataloguing of large book collections (imperial and private), and the antiquarian science of *jīnshí* 金石, born in the Northern Sòng with [[歐陽修]] (Ōuyáng Xiū) and made canonical by [[趙明誠]] (Zhào Míngchéng).

The book-catalogue strand within KR2n is the descendant of two earlier traditions discussed by Wilkinson at §73.2 — the *yìwén zhì* 藝文志 / *jīngjí zhì* 經籍志 chapters of the standard *Histories* (Wilkinson §73.3.1) — and the late-Liù-cháo / Suí *qìlù* 七錄-style classified catalogues. By the Sòng the Sìbù 四部 fourfold scheme (*jīng-shǐ-zǐ-jí* 經史子集; Wilkinson §73.3.2) had become normative, and the imperial palace catalogues collected here (Wilkinson §73.3.4) and the great private *jiětí* 解題 catalogues of the Southern Sòng (Wilkinson §73.3.5) are the most important examples of its operation outside the standard Histories. The epigraphic strand begins with two Northern-Sòng pioneers (the *Jígǔ lù* and *Jīnshí lù*; Wilkinson §55.1, §73.3 introduction) and develops, through Yuán and Míng compilation, into the mature *jīnshí* scholarship of late-Míng and early-Qīng Sūzhōu, Wàn Sītóng's circle, and the *kǎozhèng* 考證 evidential current that culminates in [[顧炎武]]'s three works on inscriptions and the stone classics.

## Important texts and text clusters

The 47 works fall into recognized clusters; entries are grouped accordingly.

**Sòng imperial and palace book catalogues.** [[KR2n0001]] *Chóngwén zǒngmù* 崇文總目 — the surviving fragment of the great Northern-Sòng imperial palace catalogue (originally 66 *juàn*, here 12), is the earliest catalogue of the imperial book collection of the period and the model for everything that follows.

**Sòng private *jiětí* catalogues.** Three Southern-Sòng works define the genre of the privately-compiled, annotated book catalogue:
- [[KR2n0002]] *Jùnzhāi dúshū zhì* 郡齋讀書志 by [[晁公武]] — the first major Chinese book catalogue to carry substantial *jiětí* 解題 notes on each title, with [[趙希弁]]'s continuation.
- [[KR2n0003]] *Suìchūtáng shūmù* 遂初堂書目 by [[尤袤]] — a slim list, but the first to record multiple editions and printing locations as a routine feature.
- [[KR2n0005]] *Zhízhāi shūlù jiětí* 直齋書錄解題 by [[陳振孫]] — the most ambitious annotated catalogue of the Southern Sòng, exhaustively cited by the Sìkù editors and the principal source for thousands of lost works.

**Sòng specialised bibliographies.** Two works treat one branch of literature in depth: [[KR2n0004]] *Zǐlüè* 子略 by [[高似孫]] (the *zǐbù* / philosophical Masters) and [[KR2n0006]] *Hàn Yìwénzhì kǎozhèng* 漢藝文志考證 by [[王應麟]] (a critical commentary on the *Hàn-shū* 藝文志 — Wilkinson §73.2.1).

**Míng imperial and palace book catalogues.** [[KR2n0007]] *Wényuàngé shūmù* 文淵閣書目 by [[楊士奇]] — the inventory of the early-Míng palace library at the Wényuàn Pavilion, and the chief source for what survived (and what was lost) of the Yuán-Míng book collections.

**Míng specialised bibliographies.** [[KR2n0008]] *Shòujīngtú yìlì* 授經圖義例 by [[朱睦㮮]] — an unusual visual-genealogical catalogue diagramming the transmission of the Five Classics.

**Qīng palace book catalogues.** [[KR2n0009]] *Qīndìng Tiānlù línláng shūmù* 欽定天祿琳琅書目 — the imperially commissioned catalogue of the Qiánlóng emperor's collection of rare Sòng-Yuán-Míng imprints, compiled by [[于敏中]], [[王際華]], and [[梁國治]]: the model imperial-collector catalogue of the eighteenth century.

**Qīng private bibliographies of earlier scholarship.** Two great early-Qīng works recover the bibliographic history of the preceding dynasties:
- [[KR2n0010]] *Qiānqǐngtáng shūmù* 千頃堂書目 by [[黃虞稷]] — the most complete bibliography of Míng publications, and the principal source for the *yìwén zhì* 藝文志 of the *Míngshǐ* 明史.
- [[KR2n0011]] *Jīngyì kǎo* 經義考 by [[朱彝尊]] — an exhaustive bibliography (297 *juàn* surviving) of all classical-studies scholarship from antiquity through the seventeenth century, and the standard reference for the Classics-commentary tradition.

**Northern-Sòng founders of *jīnshí***. [[KR2n0012]] *Jígǔ lù* 集古錄 by [[歐陽修]] and [[KR2n0013]] *Jīnshí lù* 金石錄 by [[趙明誠]] (with [[李清照]]) — the two foundational works that establish the rubbing-based, antiquarian study of inscriptions on bronze and stone as a discipline in its own right (Wilkinson §55.1).

**Sòng calligraphy-anthology scholarship.** A cluster centred on the famous *Chúnhuàgé tiè* 淳化閣帖 of 992 and its successors: [[KR2n0014]] *Fǎtiè kānwù* 法帖刋誤 by [[黃伯思]], [[KR2n0015]] *Fǎtiè shìwén* 法帖釋文 by [[劉次莊]], [[KR2n0019]] *Jiàngtiè píng* 絳帖平 by [[姜夔]], [[KR2n0021]] *Fǎtiè pǔxì* 法帖譜系 by [[曹士冕]], plus the two *Lántíng* monographs [[KR2n0022]] *Lántíng kǎo* 蘭亭考 and [[KR2n0023]] *Lántíng xùkǎo* 蘭亭續考 — the principal Sòng efforts at textual and genealogical criticism of engraved calligraphy.

**Sòng *jīnshí* scholarship after the founders.** [[KR2n0016]] *Zhòushǐ* 籀史 by [[翟耆年]] (antique-script inscriptions), and the great Hàn-clerical-script project of [[洪适]] in two parts ([[KR2n0017]] *Lìshì* 隸釋 and [[KR2n0018]] *Lìxù* 隸續), which remained the standard reference for Hàn stelae until the Qīng. With them, [[KR2n0020]] *Shíkè pūxù* 石刻鋪敍 by [[曾宏父]], [[KR2n0024]] *Bǎokè cóngbiān* 寶刻叢編 by [[陳思]], the geographical [[KR2n0025]] *Yúdì bēijì mù* 輿地碑記目 by [[王象之]], and the anonymous [[KR2n0026]] *Bǎokè lèibiān* 寶刻類編.

**Yuán–Míng *jīnshí* and calligraphy.** [[KR2n0027]] *Gǔkè cóngchāo* 古刻叢鈔 by [[陶宗儀]] preserves stone-inscription transcriptions otherwise lost. [[KR2n0028]] *Míngjì lù* 名蹟錄 by [[朱珪]] documents the famous early-Míng Sūzhōu collection. The Sūzhōu/Wú regional and antiquarian tradition continues in [[KR2n0029]] *Wúzhōng jīnshí xīnbiān* 吳中金石新編 by [[陳暐]] and [[KR2n0030]] *Jīnxiè línláng* 金薤琳琅 by [[都穆]]. Late-Míng additions include [[KR2n0031]] *Fǎtiè shìwén kǎoyì* 法帖釋文考異 by [[顧從義]], [[KR2n0032]] *Jīnshílín shídì kǎo* 金石林時地考 by [[趙均]], [[KR2n0033]] *Shímò juānhuā* 石墨鐫華 by [[趙崡]], and [[KR2n0034]] *Jīnshí shǐ* 金石史 by [[郭宗昌]].

**Qīng imperial calligraphy-anthology scholarship.** [[KR2n0035]] *Qīndìng chóngkè Chúnhuàgé tiè shìwén* 欽定重刻淳化閣帖釋文 — the imperially sponsored re-cutting and re-edition of the *Chúnhuàgé tiè* under Qiánlóng, supervised by [[于敏中]], [[王際華]], and [[裘日修]].

**Gù Yánwǔ's *jīnshí* trilogy.** [[KR2n0036]] *Qiúgǔ lù* 求古錄, [[KR2n0037]] *Jīnshí wénzì jì* 金石文字記, and [[KR2n0038]] *Shíjīng kǎo* 石經考 — three works by [[顧炎武]] that decisively introduced *kǎozhèng* 考證 evidential method into the inscription disciplines. Together they fix the empirical, rubbing-comparing, autopsy-required practice that defines the early-Qīng tradition.

**Early-Qīng *jīnshí* after Gù Yánwǔ.** [[KR2n0039]] *Wàn-shì Shíjīng kǎo* 萬氏石經考 by [[萬斯同]] (an immediate rival to Gù on the Stone Classics); regional and personal compilations [[KR2n0040]] *Láizhāi jīnshí kè kǎolüè* 來齋金石刻考略 by [[林侗]] and [[KR2n0041]] *Sōngyáng shíkè jíjì* 嵩陽石刻集記 by [[葉封]]; the broad gazetteer-like [[KR2n0042]] *Jīnshí wén kǎolüè* 金石文考略 by [[李光暎]]; and the clerical-script paleography of [[KR2n0043]] *Fēnlì ǒucún* 分隸偶存 by [[萬經]].

**Wáng Shù and the eighteenth-century calligraphic-textual-criticism school.** [[KR2n0044]] *Chúnhuà mìgé fǎtiè kǎozhèng* 淳化祕閣法帖考正 and [[KR2n0045]] *Zhúyún tíbá* 竹雲題跋 by [[王澍]] together set the standard for *kǎozhèng* applied to engraved calligraphic anthologies in the high Qing.

**Mid-Qīng inscription connoisseurship and the Stone Classics revisited.** [[KR2n0046]] *Jīnshí jīngyǎn lù* 金石經眼錄 — diagrams by [[褚峻]] with supplementary explanations by [[牛運震]] — and [[KR2n0047]] *Shíjīng kǎoyì* 石經考異 by [[杭世駿]] (a critical follow-up to both Gù and Wàn on the Stone Classics) close out the division.

## Important persons

- [[歐陽修]] (Ōuyáng Xiū, 1007–1072) — co-compiler of the *Chóngwén zǒngmù* and author of the *Jígǔ lù*; presides over both founding moments of the division (book-catalogue and *jīnshí*).
- [[王應麟]] (Wáng Yīnglín, 1223–1296) — author of the *Hàn Yìwénzhì kǎozhèng*; the great Southern-Sòng critic of the bibliographic tradition.
- [[晁公武]] (Cháo Gōngwǔ, ca. 1105–1180) — author of the first major Chinese annotated private book catalogue, the *Jùnzhāi dúshū zhì*.
- [[陳振孫]] (Chén Zhènsūn, ca. 1183 – after 1249) — author of the *Zhízhāi shūlù jiětí*, the most ambitious private book catalogue of the Southern Sòng.
- [[尤袤]] (Yóu Mào, 1127–1194) — author of the *Suìchūtáng shūmù*, the first private catalogue to track editions.
- [[楊士奇]] (Yáng Shìqí, 1365–1444) — chief compiler of the *Wényuàngé shūmù*, the inventory of the early-Míng palace library.
- [[黃虞稷]] (Huáng Yújī, 1629–1691) — author of the *Qiānqǐngtáng shūmù*, the principal bibliography of Míng publishing.
- [[朱彝尊]] (Zhū Yízūn, 1629–1709) — author of the *Jīngyì kǎo*, the most extensive bibliography of classical-studies scholarship ever compiled.
- [[趙明誠]] (Zhào Míngchéng, 1081–1129) and [[李清照]] (Lǐ Qīngzhào, 1084–ca. 1155) — joint compilers of the *Jīnshí lù*, the defining early *jīnshí* monograph.
- [[洪适]] (Hóng Kuò, 1117–1184) — author of the *Lìshì* and *Lìxù*, the canonical Sòng treatments of Hàn clerical-script stelae.
- [[姜夔]] (Jiāng Kuí, ca. 1155–1221) and [[黃伯思]] (Huáng Bósī, 1079–1118) — leading Sòng critics of the *Chúnhuàgé tiè* calligraphic anthology.
- [[陶宗儀]] (Táo Zōngyí, ca. 1329–ca. 1409) — Yuán-Míng transition figure whose *Gǔkè cóngchāo* preserves otherwise-lost inscriptions.
- [[顧炎武]] (Gù Yánwǔ, 1613–1682) — author of three of the most important early-Qīng *jīnshí* works (*Qiúgǔ lù*, *Jīnshí wénzì jì*, *Shíjīng kǎo*); decisive in introducing *kǎozhèng* method into the discipline.
- [[萬斯同]] (Wàn Sītóng, 1638–1702) — author of the rival *Wàn-shì Shíjīng kǎo*; a major figure of the Zhèjiāng historical school.
- [[王澍]] (Wáng Shù, 1668–1743) — leading high-Qīng calligrapher-philologist; author of two works in this division (*Chúnhuà mìgé fǎtiè kǎozhèng* and *Zhúyún tíbá*).
- [[于敏中]] (Yú Mǐnzhōng, 1714–1779) and [[王際華]] (Wáng Jìhuá, 1717–1776) — repeat imperial-commission editors who appear at the head of both the *Tiānlù línláng shūmù* and the re-cut *Chúnhuàgé tiè* — the institutional spine of Qiánlóng-era palace cataloguing.
- [[杭世駿]] (Háng Shìjùn, 1696–1773) — author of the *Shíjīng kǎoyì*, summing up early-Qīng scholarship on the Stone Classics.

## Topics

- **The architecture of the Sìbù (四部 *jīng-shǐ-zǐ-jí*) scheme** and the recurring negotiation, visible across these catalogues, of how to classify works that straddle its boundaries (Wilkinson §73.3.2).
- ***Jiětí* 解題 / *tíyào* 提要 annotation** — the development, from the Sòng onward, of the descriptive catalogue note as a tool of textual criticism: who wrote it, when, in how many *juǎn*, in what editions, with what defects. The high point in this division is [[KR2n0005]] *Zhízhāi shūlù jiětí*, prefiguring the Sìkù *tíyào* themselves.
- **Imperial vs. private cataloguing.** Imperial catalogues (Chóngwén, Wényuàngé, Tiānlù línláng; Wilkinson §73.3.4) record holdings; private catalogues (Jùnzhāi, Suìchūtáng, Zhízhāi, Qiānqǐngtáng; Wilkinson §73.3.5) record judgements. The complementarity is constant throughout the division.
- **Loss and recovery.** A great many works in KR2n exist only because they cite, in passing, books whose texts are otherwise lost. The category therefore functions as a meta-archive: [[KR2n0006]] *Hàn Yìwénzhì kǎozhèng* recovers the *yìwén zhì* of the *Hàn-shū*; [[KR2n0010]] *Qiānqǐngtáng shūmù* recovers Míng publishing; [[KR2n0011]] *Jīngyì kǎo* recovers the entire classical-commentary tradition.
- **The birth of *jīnshí***. The systematic, rubbing-based study of inscriptions on bronze and stone is a Northern-Sòng invention (Wilkinson §55.1, §73.3 introduction); [[KR2n0012]] *Jígǔ lù* and [[KR2n0013]] *Jīnshí lù* are the foundational documents.
- **Calligraphic anthologies (*fǎtiè* 法帖) as a textual problem.** The repeated re-cutting of the *Chúnhuàgé tiè* (992) and its rivals produced a critical literature concerned with attribution, ordering, and the corruption of individual characters across recensions: [[KR2n0014]], [[KR2n0015]], [[KR2n0019]], [[KR2n0021]], [[KR2n0031]], [[KR2n0035]], [[KR2n0044]], [[KR2n0045]].
- **The *Lántíng* problem.** The reception-history of the *Lántíng xù* 蘭亭序 by Wáng Xīzhī 王羲之 became a self-contained scholarly genre, represented here by [[KR2n0022]] and [[KR2n0023]].
- **Stone Classics scholarship.** The successive cuttings of the Confucian Classics in stone (Xī-Píng, Zhèng-Shǐ, Kāi-chéng, etc.) produced a tightly-connected sub-literature: [[KR2n0038]], [[KR2n0039]], [[KR2n0047]].
- **Regionalism.** Several Míng–Qīng catalogues are explicitly local: Sūzhōu ([[KR2n0029]], [[KR2n0030]]), Mount Sōng ([[KR2n0041]]). They prefigure the Qīng *jīnshí zhì* 金石志 chapters of provincial gazetteers.
- ***Kǎozhèng* applied to inscriptions.** The decisive shift from connoisseurial appreciation to philological evidential argument, traceable from [[顧炎武]] through [[萬斯同]], [[王澍]], and [[杭世駿]].
