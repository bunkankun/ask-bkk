## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR3eo` 養生類 (*Yǎng-shēng lèi*, Nourishing Life) is the sub-division of the Masters → Medicine branch (`KR3e` 醫家類) that gathers manuals on the prolongation of life, dietary therapy (*shí-liáo* 食療 / *shí-zhì* 食治), guided-gymnastic and breathing exercises (*dǎo-yǐn* 導引, *xíng-qì* 行氣), massage (*àn-mó* 按摩), inner-alchemical *xìng-mìng* 性命 cultivation, and the care of the aged (*yǎng-lǎo* 養老). Where `KR3e` proper preserves the clinical-medical mainstream from the *Sì-kù quán-shū* 四庫全書 imperial library (`source: WYG`), every text in `KR3eo` instead derives from the *Hǎi-wài huí-liú zhōng-yī shàn-běn gǔ-jí cóng-shū* 海外回流中醫善本古籍叢書 (`source: hxwd`), the modern repatriation series edited by Zhèng Jīnshēng 鄭金生 that has gathered Chinese medical *shàn-běn* preserved abroad (especially in Japan and Korea) and returned to circulation rare editions that the Qiánlóng-era Sì-kù editors had either rejected or never seen. The division therefore exhibits two distinguishing features: (i) a chronological centre of gravity shifted later than the `KR3e` clinical mainstream — Míng and Qīng witnesses dominate, with a strong Wàn-lì-era nucleus around the publisher–anthologist Hú Wénhuàn 胡文煥; and (ii) a generic latitude that admits non-canonical genres (illustrated *dǎo-yǐn* charts, cosmetic manuals, dietary almanacs, monthly almanacs, *xìng-mìng* alchemical primers) that the Sì-kù editors had relegated to 道家 or excluded altogether.

The genre's intellectual centre of gravity lies in Endymion Wilkinson's *Chinese History: A New Manual* Section 41.4 (Self-cultivation), particularly §41.4.2 (Longevity Techniques) and §41.4.3 (Fitness), with substantial overlap into §36.1 (Food). The transmission tradition runs from the Hàn–Wèi *xíng-qì* and *fáng-zhōng* 房中 fragments preserved in the Mǎwángduī silks and the Zhāngjiāshān *Yǐn shū* 引書 (sources outside this division), through the Suí–Táng synthesis attempted by Sūn Sīmiǎo 孫思邈, the Liǎng-Sòng anthological *yǎng-shēng* of Zhōu Shǒuzhōng 周守忠 and Chén Zhí 陳直, the Yuán imperial dietetics of Hū Sīhuì 忽思慧, the Míng *cóng-shū* programme of Hú Wénhuàn and the synthetic *yī-xué* projects of Wàn Quán 萬全, down to the Qīng dietary critique (Wáng Shìxióng 王士雄, Zhāng Mù 章穆), external therapy (Wú Shījī 吳師機), and the inner-alchemical popularisation that runs from Mǎ Qí 馬齊 to Wāng Dōngtíng 汪東亭. The Bodhidharma-attributed *Yì-jīn jīng* 易筋經 / *Xǐ-suǐ jīng* 洗髓經 corpus, late-Qīng to early-Republican in its received form, sits at the boundary with martial-arts literature (Wilkinson §26.15.1).

## Important texts and text clusters

**Foundational Táng witnesses.** Two extracts from Sūn Sīmiǎo's *Qiān-jīn* corpus stand at the head of the division:
- *Shè-yǎng zhěn-zhōng fāng* 攝養枕中方 [[KR3eo029]] — Sūn Sīmiǎo's "pillow-side" longevity manual, the foundational late-Táng synthesis of self-cultivation, breath-regulation, and pharmacology in a *yǎng-shēng* register.
- *Qiān-jīn shí-zhì* 千金食治 [[KR3eo038]] — the food-therapy chapter of the *Bèi-jí qiān-jīn yào-fāng*, the earliest systematic statement of the *yī-shí tóng-yuán* 醫食同源 principle that governs subsequent *shí-liáo* literature.

**The geriatric (養老) tradition of Chén Zhí 陳直.** The single most coherent cluster in the division, transmitted in four overlapping recensions:
- *Yǎng-lǎo fèng-qīn shū* 養老奉親書 [[KR3eo001]] — Chén Zhí's original Northern-Sòng monograph (1078–1085), the earliest extant Chinese geriatric work.
- *Shòu-qīn yǎng-lǎo shū* 壽親養老書 [[KR3eo016]] — Chén Zhí's four-juan original recension.
- *Shòu-qīn yǎng-lǎo xīn shū* 壽親養老新書 [[KR3eo031]] — Zōu Xuàn's Yuán expansion (c. 1297–1307) adding three further juan; the same text is preserved at [[KR3e0021]] in the WYG.
- *Ān-lǎo huái-yòu shū* 安老懷幼書 [[KR3eo032]] — a composite Sòng–Yuán–Míng recension supplemented by Huáng Yìngzǐ 黃應紫 with paediatric materials.

**Sòng anthological *yǎng-shēng*.** Zhōu Shǒuzhōng's two foundational compendia, both compiled in the Jiā-dìng 嘉定 era (1208 onward):
- *Yǎng-shēng lèi-zuǎn* 養生類纂 [[KR3eo022]] — the great Southern-Sòng topical anthology in 22 juan, re-issued by Hú Wénhuàn.
- *Yǎng-shēng yuè-lǎn* 養生月覽 [[KR3eo024]] — the standard month-by-month *yǎng-shēng* almanac.

**Yuán imperial dietetics and dietary literature.** The greatest single Mongol-Yuán contribution to the genre is the imperial dietary office's pair:
- *Yǐn-shàn zhèng-yào* 飲膳正要 [[KR3eo040]] — Hū Sīhuì's 1330 imperial dietary manual presented to Wén-zōng 文宗, with Yú Jí 虞集's preface; the most important pre-modern Chinese cookbook and the source from which the bulk of Mongol-Yuán culinary practice can be reconstructed.
- *Shí-liáo fāng* 食療方 [[KR3eo039]] — Hū's companion volume on food therapy proper.
- *Yǐn-shí xū-zhī* 飲食須知 [[KR3eo044]] — Jiǎ Míng's late-Yuán manual of dietary avoidances and incompatibilities; together with the *Cháo-shì bìng-yuán bǔ-yǎng xuān-dǎo fǎ* 巢氏病源補養宣導法 [[KR3eo033]] and the *Sān-yuán cān-zàn yán-shòu shū* 三元參贊延壽書 [[KR3eo012]] of Lǐ Péngfēi, it forms the Yuán *yǎng-shēng* corpus proper.

**Míng synthetic *yǎng-shēng*.** Two clusters dominate the Míng:
- The *qì*-monist literatus tradition: *Shè-shēng yào-yì* 攝生要義 [[KR3eo015]] by Wáng Tíngxiāng 王廷相 (writing as Hé-bīn zhàng-rén 河濱丈人, c. 1530–1544); the early-Míng *Hòu-shēng xùn-zuǎn* 厚生訓纂 [[KR3eo009]] (Zhōu Chén 周臣, 1549); and the *Yǎng-shēng lèi-yào* 養生類要 [[KR3eo004]] (Wú Zhènglún 吳正倫, 1564).
- The Wàn Quán 萬全 synthesis (Húběi family physician, 1549–1582): *Yǎng-shēng sì-yào* 養生四要 [[KR3eo025]] and the monumental versified clinical compendium *Bǎo-mìng gē-kuò* 保命歌括 [[KR3eo026]] (35 juan), the largest Míng *yǎng-shēng*-clinical synthesis in the division.

**The Hú Wénhuàn 胡文煥 publishing programme (Wàn-lì, c. 1590–1602).** Hú Wénhuàn — Hángzhōu literatus, *Wén-huì-táng* 文會堂 publisher, and the most prolific Wàn-lì-era anthologist — is responsible for an unusually large share of the division (eleven titles), preserving short *yǎng-shēng* and esoteric medical manuals that would otherwise be lost: the inner-cultivation *Lèi-xiū yào-jué* 類修要訣 [[KR3eo011]] and *Shè-shēng jí-lǎn* 攝生集覽 [[KR3eo014]]; the dietary *Yǎng-shēng shí-jì* 養生食忌 [[KR3eo042]]; the cosmetic-and-female-health *Xiāng-lián rùn-sè* 香奩潤色 [[KR3eo020]]; the *Tài-sù* pulse-prognostication pair *Tài-sù mài-jué mì-shū* 太素脈訣秘書 [[KR3eo018]] and *Tài-sù xīn-yào* 太素心要 [[KR3eo019]]; the medical-introductory *Yī-xué quán-yú* 醫學權輿 [[KR3eo027]]; and the two principal *dǎo-yǐn* manuals in the division, *Yǎng-shēng dǎo-yǐn mì-jí* 養生導引秘籍 [[KR3eo034]] and *Yǎng-shēng dǎo-yǐn fǎ* 養生導引法 [[KR3eo036]]. Hú's programme is the principal mechanism by which Sòng-Yuán *yǎng-shēng* short-form literature survived into the modern period.

**Illustrated *dǎo-yǐn* and the body of the immortal.** *Bǎo-shēng xīn-jiàn* 保生心鑑 [[KR3eo041]] (Tiěfēng Jūshì 鐵峰居士, 1505–1506), with 32 illustrated *chén-zhī* figures, is the principal Míng illustrated *dǎo-yǐn* manual in the division. *Jǐn-shēn jī-yào* 錦身機要 [[KR3eo010]] (Hùndùnzǐ 混沌子) and the *Xīn-yìn gàn-zhū jīng* 心印紺珠經 [[KR3eo021]] (Lǐ Tāngqīng 李湯卿, Yuán) complete the metaphysically inflected branch of the tradition. *Xiū-zhēn mì-yào* 修真秘要 [[KR3eo023]] (Wáng Cài 王蔡, 1513–1515) and *Fú-shòu dān-shū* 福壽丹書 [[KR3eo030]] (Gōng Jūzhōng 龔居中, 1610–1632) carry the inner-alchemical *xìng-mìng* programme into the late Míng.

**Qīng dietary and external therapy.** A late-imperial cluster of analytical, clinical-dietary, and external-therapy works:
- *Suí-xī-jū yǐn-shí pǔ* 隨息居飲食譜 [[KR3eo043]] (Wáng Shìxióng 王士雄, 1861) — the most influential nineteenth-century critical dietary manual, by the *Wēn-rè jīng-wěi* 溫熱經緯 author.
- *Tiáo-jí yǐn-shí biàn* 調疾飲食辯 [[KR3eo045]] (Zhāng Mù 章穆) and *Shí-wù jí-yào* 食物輯要 [[KR3eo046]] (Mù Shìxī 穆世錫) — the discriminating-dietary scholarship.
- *Jí-jiù guǎng-shēng jí* 急救廣生集 [[KR3eo047]] (Chéng Péngchéng 程鵬程, c. 1800–1830) and *Lǐ-yuè pián-wén* 理瀹駢文 [[KR3eo048]] (Wú Shījī 吳師機, 1864–1870) — the founding texts of the modern *wài-zhì-fǎ* 外治法 (external-therapy) sub-discipline; the latter is the canonical theoretical statement of plaster-and-poultice therapy.

**Qīng inner-alchemical popularisation and longevity manuals.** *Yǎng-shēng mì-zhǐ* 養生秘旨 [[KR3eo005]] (Mǎ Qí 馬齊, Kāng-xī Manchu Grand Secretary), *Shòu-shì qīng-biān* 壽世青編 [[KR3eo006]] (Yóu Chéng 尤乘, 1666–1690), *Shòu-shì chuán-zhēn* 壽世傳真 [[KR3eo002]] (Xú Wénbì 徐文弼, 1768–1771), *Fèng-shí zhǐ-yào* 奉時旨要 [[KR3eo008]] (Jiāng Hán 江涵), *Shòu-shì biān* 壽世編 [[KR3eo017]] (Qīng-pǔ Zhū jūn-zǐ 青浦諸君子), *Xiū Kūn-lún zhèng-yàn* 修崑崙證驗 [[KR3eo003]] (Tiān-xiū-zǐ 天休子, 1846–1847), and *Xìng-mìng yào-zhǐ* 性命要旨 [[KR3eo007]] (Wāng Dōngtíng 汪東亭, 1880s) — late-Qīng workshop on the inner-alchemy / *xìng-mìng* programme as transmitted to literate non-specialists.

**Massage and the Bodhidharma exercises.** *Dòng-gōng àn-mó mì-jué* 動功按摩秘訣 [[KR3eo035]] (the Wāng brothers Qǐshèng 啟聖 and Qǐxián 啟賢, eighteenth century) is the principal Qīng massage manual. *Dá-mó xǐ-suǐ yì-jīn jīng* 達摩洗髓易筋經 [[KR3eo037]] preserves, in early-twentieth-century editorial form, the late-imperial Bodhidharma-attributed *Yì-jīn jīng* / *Xǐ-suǐ jīng* corpus that links *yǎng-shēng* with the martial-arts tradition of the Shàolín-sì 少林寺.

## Important persons

- [[孫思邈]] (Sūn Sīmiǎo, c. 581–682) — the Táng polymath whose *Qiān-jīn* corpus supplies both the foundational *yǎng-shēng* methodology ([[KR3eo029]]) and the foundational dietary-therapy programme ([[KR3eo038]]).
- [[巢元方]] (Cháo Yuánfāng, fl. 605–615) — the Suí imperial physician whose disease-aetiology classic furnished the source-base for the Sòng-compiled *Cháo-shì bìng-yuán bǔ-yǎng xuān-dǎo fǎ* [[KR3eo033]].
- [[陳直]] (Chén Zhí, fl. 1078–1085) — Northern-Sòng magistrate of Xīng-huà-xiàn, the founder of Chinese geriatric medicine and the central author of the division's *yǎng-lǎo* cluster ([[KR3eo001]] [[KR3eo016]] [[KR3eo031]] [[KR3eo032]]).
- [[鄒鉉]] (Zōu Xuàn, fl. 1297–1307) — Yuán expander of Chén Zhí's geriatric corpus, responsible for the form in which it became canonical ([[KR3eo031]] [[KR3eo032]]).
- [[周守忠]] (Zhōu Shǒuzhōng, fl. 1208–1224) — Southern-Sòng anthologist whose two *yǎng-shēng* compendia ([[KR3eo022]] [[KR3eo024]]) supply the topical and calendrical templates for all later *yǎng-shēng* literature.
- [[李鵬飛]] (Lǐ Péngfēi, fl. 1288–1291) — Yuán author of the *Sān-yuán cān-zàn yán-shòu shū* [[KR3eo012]], the principal Yuán synthetic *yǎng-shēng* treatise.
- [[忽思慧]] (Hū Sīhuì, fl. 1325–1330) — Mongol-Yuán imperial dietary officer (*yǐn-shàn tài-yī* 飲膳太醫); author of [[KR3eo039]] [[KR3eo040]], the foundational pre-modern Chinese dietetic manuals.
- [[賈銘]] (Jiǎ Míng, c. 1269–1374) — Yuán-Míng centenarian, author of *Yǐn-shí xū-zhī* [[KR3eo044]], the standard manual of dietary incompatibilities.
- [[王廷相]] (Wáng Tíngxiāng, 1474–1544) — Míng Minister of War and *qì*-monist philosopher; author of [[KR3eo015]] under the sobriquet Hé-bīn zhàng-rén, the one work in the division that integrates *yǎng-shēng* into a coherent philosophical position.
- [[萬全]] (Wàn Quán, c. 1499–1582) — Húběi family physician; the most prolific Míng *yǎng-shēng*-clinical synthesiser ([[KR3eo025]] [[KR3eo026]]).
- [[胡文煥]] (Hú Wénhuàn, c. 1551–1607) — Hángzhōu literatus and Wén-huì-táng publisher; responsible for editing, printing, or re-issuing eleven titles in the division and for the survival of much of its short-form Sòng-Yuán content ([[KR3eo011]] [[KR3eo014]] [[KR3eo018]]–[[KR3eo020]] [[KR3eo022]] [[KR3eo027]] [[KR3eo034]] [[KR3eo036]] [[KR3eo042]]).
- [[鐵峰居士]] (Tiěfēng Jūshì, fl. 1505–1506) — the most consequential Míng illustrated *dǎo-yǐn* author ([[KR3eo041]]).
- [[龔居中]] (Gōng Jūzhōng, fl. 1610–1632) — late-Míng inner-alchemical and clinical compiler; *Fú-shòu dān-shū* [[KR3eo030]] is his principal *yǎng-shēng* work.
- [[馬齊]] (Mǎ Qí, 1652–1739) — Kāng-xī era Manchu Grand Secretary; his compiled *Yǎng-shēng mì-zhǐ* [[KR3eo005]] is a high-Qīng court-elite document of *yǎng-shēng* practice.
- [[尤乘]] (Yóu Chéng, c. 1620–1690) — Sū-zhōu physician; author of *Shòu-shì qīng-biān* [[KR3eo006]], a much-reprinted Kāng-xī *yǎng-shēng* manual.
- [[徐文弼]] (Xú Wénbì, fl. 1768–1771) — author of *Shòu-shì chuán-zhēn* [[KR3eo002]], the most popular Qián-lóng-era *yǎng-shēng* compendium.
- [[王士雄]] (Wáng Shìxióng, 1808–1868) — author of *Suí-xī-jū yǐn-shí pǔ* [[KR3eo043]] and the principal dietetic critic of the high-Qīng *Wēn-bìng* school.
- [[章穆]] (Zhāng Mù, fl. 1810–1830) — dietary discrimination ([[KR3eo045]]).
- [[程鵬程]] (Chéng Péngchéng, fl. 1800–1830) — author of *Jí-jiù guǎng-shēng jí* [[KR3eo047]], a pioneering external-therapy compendium.
- [[吳師機]] (Wú Shījī, 1806–1886) — founder of the modern *wài-zhì* (external-therapy) sub-discipline ([[KR3eo048]]).
- [[汪東亭]] (Wāng Dōngtíng, fl. 1880s) — late-Qīng inner-alchemical popularisation ([[KR3eo007]]).

## Topics

The division clusters around a small number of recurring problems and debates.

**Dietary therapy and *yī-shí tóng-yuán* 醫食同源.** The single most continuous thematic strand. From Sūn Sīmiǎo's *Qiān-jīn shí-zhì* [[KR3eo038]] through the Yuán imperial *Yǐn-shàn zhèng-yào* [[KR3eo040]] / *Shí-liáo fāng* [[KR3eo039]] and Jiǎ Míng's *Yǐn-shí xū-zhī* [[KR3eo044]], to the Míng *Yǎng-shēng shí-jì* [[KR3eo042]] and *Shí-wù jí-yào* [[KR3eo046]], and culminating in the Qīng analytical works of Wáng Shìxióng ([[KR3eo043]]) and Zhāng Mù ([[KR3eo045]]). The progression is from compiled list to discriminating critique. Wilkinson §36.1 #6 and §41.4.2 are the principal Western entry points.

**Guided gymnastics (導引) and breath-cultivation (行氣).** From the Sòng compilation of Cháo Yuánfāng's *dǎo-yǐn* materials ([[KR3eo033]]), through the illustrated Míng manuals ([[KR3eo041]]), the Hú Wénhuàn *dǎo-yǐn* programme ([[KR3eo034]] [[KR3eo036]]), the *àn-mó* manual of the Wāng brothers ([[KR3eo035]]), down to the Bodhidharma corpus ([[KR3eo037]]). Wilkinson §41.4.3 is the standard scholarly orientation; the modern translations of Livia Kohn and Vivienne Lo are the principal entry points for the Mǎwángduī–Zhāngjiāshān excavated background.

**Care of the elderly (養老).** The Chén Zhí–Zōu Xuàn lineage ([[KR3eo001]] [[KR3eo016]] [[KR3eo031]] [[KR3eo032]]) is the principal Chinese geriatric tradition before the modern era; its thesis that "food therapy surpasses pharmacotherapy for the aged" defines the field.

**Inner alchemy (內丹) and *xìng-mìng* 性命 cultivation.** The non-clinical pole of the division — works that read *yǎng-shēng* as a programme of inner-alchemical refinement rather than as preventive medicine. *Xīn-yìn gàn-zhū jīng* [[KR3eo021]], *Jǐn-shēn jī-yào* [[KR3eo010]], *Xiū-zhēn mì-yào* [[KR3eo023]], *Fú-shòu dān-shū* [[KR3eo030]], *Xiū Kūn-lún zhèng-yàn* [[KR3eo003]], *Xìng-mìng yào-zhǐ* [[KR3eo007]]. The boundary with the Dào-jiā 道家 sub-division is porous and inheritances run in both directions.

**Monthly and seasonal regimens.** The almanac branch — Zhōu Shǒuzhōng's *Yǎng-shēng yuè-lǎn* [[KR3eo024]] is the prototype; Jiāng Hán's *Fèng-shí zhǐ-yào* [[KR3eo008]] is the major Qīng exemplar. The cosmological premise is that *qì* flows differently through each lunar month and each of the twenty-four *jié-qì* solar nodes, and that diet, conduct, and exercise must vary accordingly.

**The literatus *yǎng-shēng* tradition.** A distinctive strand running from Sū Shì 蘇軾 (whose *Sū-Shěn liángfāng* [[KR3e0020]] sits in `KR3e` proper) through Wáng Tíngxiāng ([[KR3eo015]]) to the Qīng scholar-officials Mǎ Qí ([[KR3eo005]]) and the *Qīng-pǔ Zhū jūn-zǐ* circle ([[KR3eo017]]). These works are characterised by philosophical self-consciousness and prose elegance rather than by clinical-medical authority.

**External therapy (外治法).** The late-Qīng *wài-zhì-fǎ* programme — plaster, poultice, fumigation, acupressure as substitutes for internal pharmacotherapy. Chéng Péngchéng ([[KR3eo047]]) and Wú Shījī ([[KR3eo048]]) are the founding figures. Wú's *Lǐ-yuè pián-wén*, written entirely in parallel-prose, is the theoretical statement; it has acquired renewed interest in twentieth-century *zhōng-yī* practice.

**The *Tài-sù* pulse tradition (太素脈).** A heterodox Míng prognostic-pulse tradition that read fortune, character, and lifespan from pulse-pattern, preserved here in Hú Wénhuàn's pair ([[KR3eo018]] [[KR3eo019]]) — the principal extant Wàn-lì-era witnesses to a tradition that the orthodox Sì-kù *yī-jiā* sub-category explicitly excluded.

**Repatriation philology (海外回流).** A specifically modern topic: the division as a whole is the product of the Zhèng Jīnshēng-led *hxwd* publishing programme, which has returned to circulation Míng-Qīng *yǎng-shēng* editions long preserved only in Japanese (Naikaku Bunko, Sonkeikaku) and Korean (Kyujanggak) collections. The textual recensions transmitted in `KR3eo` are accordingly often earlier or fuller than the WYG / `KR3e` parallels — most strikingly for the Chén Zhí corpus ([[KR3eo001]] vs. [[KR3e0021]]).
