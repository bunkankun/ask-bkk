# Yǐn-shàn zhèng-yào 飲膳正要
*Essentials of Eating and Drinking*
by [[忽思慧]] Hū Sī-huì (Yuán imperial dietetic physician, CBDB 116207), with presentation memorial by [[常普蘭奚]] Cháng Pǔlánxī and imperial preface by [[虞集]] Yú Jí.

## About the work

The principal **Yuán-era dietetic-medical text** (1330) and one of the most important food-medical works in the Chinese tradition. In three juan: juan 1 covers the cultivational and dietetic principles, the basic categories of *zhū-yǎng zhī-dào* 諸養之道, *yùn-yāo* 妊娠 (pregnancy), *yǎng-yīng* 育嬰 (infant care), *yǐn-shí wèi-shēng* 飲食衛生 (food and drink hygiene), and *shí-xìng yí-jì* 食性宜忌 (dietary appropriateness and avoidance). Juan 2 contains the systematic dietary-prescription chapters — including the imperial-banquet recipes (the famous "*qī-shí-wǔ wèi*" 七十五味 catalogue of imperial banquet dishes), the dietary-pharmaceutical preparations, the herbal-decoction-based dishes, and the food-therapy section that is also separately extracted as [[KR3eo039]]. Juan 3 contains the systematic foodstuff treatises by category — grains, animals, fish, fruits, vegetables, condiments — each entry providing nature, taste, and dietetic-therapeutic application, drawing heavily on prior Chinese pharmacopoeia (the *Běn-cǎo* tradition) and supplementing with Yuán-specific items.

### Prefaces

The hxwd reprint preserves a *bá* 跋 (afterword) at the end of the work by **Zhāng Yuánjì** 張元濟 (1867–1959, the great modern bibliographer), dated *Mín-guó* 19 (1930) tenth month — narrating the modern publishing history. Zhāng notes that the work, in three juan by the Yuán Hū Sī-huì, contains: (i) a presentation memorial by **Cháng Pǔlánxī** dated *Tiān-lì* 3 = 1330; (ii) an imperial preface by **Yú Jí**; (iii) a Míng *Jǐng-tài*-era reprint preface (now lost). Zhāng describes the work as "the Yuán-period Yǐn-shàn-tài-yī's official text." He compares his witness (held by the *Bǐ Sòng-lóu* 皕宋樓 collection, attributed to Yuán print but actually Míng print of the *Jǐng-tài* era 1450–1457) against the Cháng-shú Qú-family *Tiě-qín-tóng-jiàn-lóu* 鐵琴銅劍樓 collection (also a *Jǐng-tài* print). Zhāng concludes: "Although its propositions do not entirely accord with medical truth, [the work] can be consulted to glimpse the popular preferences of the Yuán-period people. Among private-collection traditions, manuscript copies have been more frequent; none compares in trustworthiness to this printed edition. I had sought it for years; in the winter of 1928 I at last met with it at the *Jìng-jiā wén-kù* 靜嘉文庫 in Tōkyō, whence I was permitted to borrow for lithograph reprinting, satisfying a long-cherished wish."

The Zhāng Yuánjì *bá* — itself dated 1930 — is one of the principal modern documents of the recovery of the *Yǐn-shàn zhèng-yào* from Japanese collections in the early Republican period.

### Abstract

Hū Sī-huì's *Yǐn-shàn zhèng-yào* (1330) is the **single most important Yuán dietetic-medical text** and one of the most important pre-modern documents of cross-cultural Eurasian food medicine. The work integrates: (i) the **Chinese dietetic-medical tradition** descending from the *Sù-wèn*, Sūn Sī-miǎo's *Qiān-jīn shí-zhì*, and Mèng Shēn's *Shí-liáo běn-cǎo*; (ii) the **Mongol-Yuán imperial cuisine** — sheep-meat preparations, *qū-mí* fermented dairy beverages, kebab-style and roasted dishes that are distinctively non-Hàn; (iii) **Persian and Arabic culinary borrowings** introduced through the Yuán's transcontinental administrative reach — including spice-and-rosewater dishes and Central-Asian-origin preparations. The work is in this respect not merely Chinese but **Eurasian** — a unique synthesis arising from the specific cosmopolitan position of the Yuán court.

The presentation memorial by **Cháng Pǔlánxī** (a Hàn-Mongol official of the Imperial Diary department) and the imperial preface by **Yú Jí** (one of the foremost Yuán-period scholars and one of the principal compilers of the *Yuán jīng-shì dà-diǎn* 元經世大典) both attest to the work's status as **official imperial document**, intended for the regulation of the imperial table.

The hxwd recension follows Zhāng Yuánjì's 1930 reprint of the *Jǐng-tài* (1450–1457) Míng court edition — the recovery from the *Jìng-jiā wén-kù* (Seikadō Bunko) in Tōkyō being one of the principal modern philological events in the history of Yuán medical literature.

## Other points of interest

The work's **Mongol-Persian-Hàn synthesis** has made it a principal document for the historical anthropology of pre-modern Eurasian food, and the Buell-Anderson translation is one of the most-cited works in Anglophone scholarship on Yuán social history. Zhāng Yuánjì's recovery of the work from Japanese holdings in 1928–1930 is itself a significant event in the modern history of the *Sì-bù bèi-yào* / *Sì-bù cóng-kān* publishing-philological project.
