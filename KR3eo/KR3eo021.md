# Xīn-yìn gàn-zhū jīng 心印紺珠經
*Heart-Sealed Sapphire-Pearl Classic*
by [[李湯卿]] Lǐ Tāngqīng (Yuán medical scholar).

## About the work

A two-juan systematic theoretical introduction to medicine, framing the discipline as the cosmological derivative of *yīn-yáng wǔ-xíng* doctrine: the work opens with a cosmogonic "Origin of the Way Lineage" (*Yuán dào-tǒng* 原道統) tracing medicine from the primal partition of *hùn-dùn* through the differentiation of *yáng* (light, becoming Heaven) and *yīn* (heavy, becoming Earth), through the differentiation of the Five Phases, to the constitution of the human body as a microcosm of Heaven (round head) and Earth (square feet) with *qì-blood* corresponding to Heaven's *yīn-yáng* and *Five Viscera* corresponding to Heaven's Five Phases. The text then proceeds through the canonical medical-historical genealogy (Fú-xī's hexagrams; Shén-nóng's tasting of the herbs; Huáng-dì's dialogue with Qí-bó in the *Sù-wèn*; the eventual emergence of *biān-zhēn* needle-therapy and *tāng-yè* decoction therapy) before arriving at its substantive systematic exposition of medical doctrine.

The title's *xīn-yìn* 心印 ("heart-seal") borrows the Chán Buddhist idiom of mind-to-mind transmission, and *gàn-zhū* 紺珠 (sapphire-pearl) is a Tang allusion to a famed mnemonic device — together signifying a work designed to be inwardly digested and held in memory as the kernel of a discipline.

### Prefaces

The hxwd reprint preserves no separate xù. The work opens directly with the *Yuán dào-tǒng dì-yī* 原道統第一 ("On the Origin of the Way-Lineage, first") cosmogonic introduction, which functions as a doctrinal preface. The opening line — *dà zāi yī hū, qí lái yuǎn yǐ* 大哉醫乎，其來遠矣 ("Great indeed is medicine — its origin is remote!") — is one of the more memorable opening formulations of late-Yuán medical literature.

### Abstract

Lǐ Tāngqīng is one of the more obscure Yuán medical theorists; no biographical record survives beyond his self-presentation as the author of this work. The text is unattested in major Yuán bibliographic catalogs (*Yuán shǐ · Yì-wén-zhì*, *Sòng-shǐ xīn-biān*) but is referenced in early-Míng medical bibliographies — notably in the *Yī-shǐ* 醫史 of Lǐ Lián 李濂 (*Jiā-jìng* 9 = 1530) — which place its composition in the late Yuán. The work is consistent in style and doctrine with the **Lǐ Gāo / Zhū Zhèn-héng** tradition of the *Jīn-Yuán sì-dà jiā* 金元四大家 ("Four Great Masters of the Jīn-Yuán"), in particular the foregrounding of *Sù-wèn* runqi 運氣 cosmology that is characteristic of Lǐ Gāo (Dōng-yuán) and his school.

The work's principal contribution is its **systematic synthesis of *yùn-qì* doctrine** with the clinical medical curriculum: the entire opening chapter is a careful derivation of medical principle from cosmogonic principle, after which the substantive chapters proceed in order through the standard clinical-pedagogical topics (channel-and-collateral structure, viscera-bowel theory, *zàng-fǔ* pathology, prescribing principles). It is, in effect, one of the more lucid late-Yuán **theoretical textbooks of medicine**, intended for the formation of the medical student rather than for direct clinical reference.

**Note on transmission**: the hxwd source files preserve KR3eo021_001.txt and KR3eo021_003.txt but not the middle juan (_002) or front-matter (_000). The present recension is therefore incomplete; the *Sì-kù* tradition transmits the full work under the title *Xīn-yìn gàn-zhū jīng* in two juan (with the title sometimes appearing variantly as *Xīn-yìn miào-jīng* 心印妙經 in late-Míng reprintings).

## Other points of interest

The doctrinal positioning of the work — *yùn-qì* foreground, careful integration with the *Sì-dà jiā* clinical doctrine — makes it a useful late-Yuán witness to the synthesis-phase of the *Jīn-Yuán* medical revolution into the systematic-pedagogical genre that would become dominant in early Ming (*Yī-xué rù-mén* 醫學入門 of Lǐ Tǐng 1575, etc.).
