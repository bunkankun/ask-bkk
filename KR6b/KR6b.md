## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR6b corresponds to the 本緣部 *Běnyuán bù* ("Section on Original Conditions") of the Taishō canon, occupying Taishō volumes 3 and 4 (T152–T219). In the CANWWW structural division  this is the fourth division of the canon, sitting between the 寶積部 *Ratnakūṭa* collections (div03) and the 般若部 *Prajñāpāramitā* section (div05). The unifying generic category is **narrative scripture about prior lives or originary events** — *jātaka* tales of the *bodhisattva*'s past births, *avadāna* anthologies of edifying karmic biographies, full Buddha-biographies (*Buddhacarita* / *Lalitavistara*-class works and their Chinese variants), the *Dharmapada* / *Udānavarga* gnomic-verse traditions, and the various *pìyù* 譬喻 (parable) compilations. The Chinese title literally means "section on origins and conditioned causes," capturing the karmic-narrative logic that ties this generically heterogeneous material together.

Chronologically the division spans roughly thirteen centuries of Chinese translation and compilation, from Ān Shìgāo's mid-second-century *Tàizǐ Mùpò jīng* 太子慕魄經 ([[KR6b0019]], c. 148–170) through the great Northern-Sòng *Yìjīng yuàn* 譯經院 translation programme of c. 980–1001 (the [[KR6b0017|Shīhù]] / [[KR6b0048|Fǎxián]] / [[KR6b0070|Tiānxīzāi]] cluster). A single late-imperial commentarial extension — Lái Zhōu's 來舟 Yōngzhèng-era *Qiǎnzhù* on the *Xīndì-guān jīng* ([[KR6b0009]], [[KR6b0010]], [[KR6b0011]]) — carries the division into the eighteenth century. The principal transmissional traditions a reader must keep in mind are: (i) the Hàn–Wú southern translation lineage (Ān Shìgāo → Zhī Qiān → Kāng Sēnghuì), which produced the foundational Chinese *jātaka*-anthologies and the canonical *Dharmapada*; (ii) the Western-Jìn Dūnhuáng/Cháng'ān lineage of Zhú Fǎhù (Dharmarakṣa), responsible for an unusually large fraction of the division (at least seven works); (iii) the early-fifth-century Northern-Liáng (Dharmakṣema), Latter-Qín (Kumārajīva, Zhú Fóniàn), and LiúSòng (Guṇabhadra, Bǎoyún) translation programmes, which gave Chinese readers the *Buddhacarita*, the *Sūtrālaṃkāra*, and the *Udānavarga* with auto-commentary; and (iv) the Northern Sòng *Yìjīng yuàn*, which produced systematic re-translations of jātaka and parable material (T165, T173, T191, T213, T218, T219). One unusual feature of this division is the prominence of **Chinese editorial compilation** alongside translation: several of the most-influential works — [[KR6b0001|*Liù-dù jí jīng* (T152)]], [[KR6b0059|*Xiányú jīng* (T202)]], the four [[KR6b0061|*Zá pìyù jīng*]] anthologies — are sinophone editorial assemblages of Indic narrative material rather than translations of single Indic recensions.

## Important texts and text clusters

**Mahāyāna jātaka-anthologies.** The principal Mahāyāna-inflected *bodhisattva*-jātaka anthologies of the Chinese canon: [[KR6b0001]] *Liù-dù jí jīng* 六度集經 (T152, Kāng Sēnghuì, c. 247–280), the foundational Wú-period anthology organising 91 *bodhisattva*-tales under the six *pāramitā*s; [[KR6b0002]] *Púsà běnyuán jīng* 菩薩本緣經 (T153, Zhī Qiān); [[KR6b0003]] *Shēng jīng* 生經 (T154, Zhú Fǎhù); and [[KR6b0012]] *Púsà běnshēng mán lùn* 菩薩本生鬘論 (T160, Sòng), the Chinese version of Āryaśūra's *Jātakamālā*.

**Karuṇā-puṇḍarīka cluster.** [[KR6b0006]] *Bēihuā jīng* 悲華經 (T157, Dharmakṣema, 414–421) and its companion [[KR6b0007]] *Dàshèng bēifēntuólì jīng* 大乘悲分陀利經 (T158, anonymous Northern-dynasties), two Chinese versions of the *Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka-sūtra* — a Mahāyāna meta-jātaka in which Śākyamuni's vow-history is the principal narrative.

**Xīndì-guān cluster.** [[KR6b0008]] *Dàshèng běnshēng xīndì-guān jīng* 大乘本生心地觀經 (T159, Prajña, 810–811) and its three Qing-Dynasty commentaries by Lái Zhōu — [[KR6b0009]] *Xuánshì*, [[KR6b0010]] *Kēwén*, and [[KR6b0011]] *Qiǎnzhù*; the only post-Sòng works in the division.

**Royal-jātaka single sūtras.** A series of single-fascicle past-life narratives anchored on a virtuous king or prince, ranging from short Hàn-era survivals to Northern-Sòng re-translations: [[KR6b0013]] *Chángshòu wáng jīng*, [[KR6b0014]] *Jīnsè wáng jīng*, [[KR6b0015]] *Miàosè wáng yīnyuán jīng*, [[KR6b0017]] *Dǐngshēng wáng yīnyuán jīng*, [[KR6b0018]] *Yuèguāng púsà jīng*, [[KR6b0023]] *Tàizǐ Sūdánà jīng* (the Chinese Vessantara/Sudāna), [[KR6b0025]] *Fúlì tàizǐ yīnyuán jīng* (Puṇyabala). The 太子慕魄/墓魄 doublet — [[KR6b0019]] (Ān Shìgāo, c. 148–170) and [[KR6b0020]] (Zhú Fǎhù) — is one of the earliest dated Chinese-Buddhist narrative pairs.

**Śyāmaka recensions.** A remarkable four-fold survival of the *Śyāmaka-jātaka* — [[KR6b0026]], [[KR6b0027]], [[KR6b0028]], [[KR6b0029]] — preserving multiple Chinese recensions (Yáo-Qín and Qǐfú-Qín attributions to Shèngjiān 聖堅, plus an anonymous one) of the same filial-piety jātaka, one of the very few cases in the canon where so many recensions of a single short narrative are preserved side by side. The pair of 九色鹿 (Nine-Coloured Deer, [[KR6b0035]] / [[KR6b0036]]) and 鹿母 (Mother-Deer, [[KR6b0037]] / [[KR6b0038]]) recensions show similar Wú/Western-Jìn doublet-preservation patterns.

**Lalitavistara pair.** [[KR6b0042]] *Pǔyào jīng* 普曜經 (T186, Zhú Fǎhù, 308) and [[KR6b0043]] *Fāngguǎng dàzhuāngyán jīng* 方廣大莊嚴經 (T187, Divākara, 685) — the early-Jìn and Táng Chinese versions of the Mahāyāna *Lalitavistara*-class Buddha-biography, separated by some 380 years and one of the principal test-cases in comparative Chinese Buddhist translation studies.

**Buddhacarita and verse-biographies.** [[KR6b0049]] *Fó suǒxíng zàn* 佛所行讚 (T192, Dharmakṣema, 414–421), Aśvaghoṣa's *Buddhacarita* — the Chinese version preserves chapters of the Sanskrit *kāvya* that are now lost in the original — and its companion [[KR6b0050]] *Fó běnxíng jīng* (T193, Bǎoyún, LiúSòng).

**Hàn-era Buddha-biography pair.** [[KR6b0040]] *Xiūxíng běnqǐ jīng* 修行本起經 (T184, Zhú Dàlì + Kāng Mèngxiáng, 197 CE) and [[KR6b0053]] *Zhōng běnqǐ jīng* 中本起經 (T196, Tánguǒ + Kāng Mèngxiáng, 207 CE) — together a complete Eastern-Hàn Chinese Buddha-biography cycle and one of the earliest dated translations after Ān Shìgāo's. [[KR6b0041]] *Tàizǐ ruìyìng běnqǐ jīng* (T185, Zhī Qiān) is the Wú-period parallel.

**Comprehensive Suí Buddha-biography.** [[KR6b0047]] *Fó běnxíng jí jīng* 佛本行集經 (T190, Jñānagupta, 587–591) — at 60 *juàn* the most extensive Mahāyāna Buddha-biography in Chinese and the most important pre-Tang biographical encyclopedia of the Buddha. [[KR6b0048]] *Zhòngxǔ móhēdì jīng* 眾許摩訶帝經 (T191, Fǎxián 法賢, 980–1001) is its Northern-Sòng re-translation.

**Avadāna anthologies.** Four of the most-cited Chinese *avadāna*-collections: [[KR6b0057]] *Zhuànjí bǎiyuán jīng* 撰集百緣經 (T200, Zhī Qiān) — the Chinese *Avadāna-śataka*; [[KR6b0058]] *Dàzhuāngyán lùn jīng* 大莊嚴論經 (T201, Aśvaghoṣa via Kumārajīva, 405–412) — the *Sūtrālaṃkāra* / *Kalpanāmaṇḍitikā*; [[KR6b0059]] *Xiányú jīng* 賢愚經 (T202, Huìjué et al., 445–459) — compiled at Khotan/Gāochāng directly from oral *avadāna*-recitations and the source of the Tibetan *mDzangs blun*; [[KR6b0060]] *Zábǎozàng jīng* 雜寶藏經 (T203, Jíjiāyè + Tányào, 472) — a Mūlasarvāstivādin compilation foundational for East-Asian popular literature.

**Parable (譬喻) anthologies.** Five anthologies of *pìyù*-narratives showcasing the genre's development from the late Hàn through the Suí: [[KR6b0061]] *Zá pìyù jīng* (T204, attributed Zhī Lóujiāchèn / Lokakṣema, c. 178–200); [[KR6b0063]] *Jiù zá pìyù jīng* 舊雜譬喻經 (T206, Kāng Sēnghuì); [[KR6b0064]] *Zá pìyù jīng* (T207, Dàoluè) and [[KR6b0065]] *Zhòngjīng zhuànzá pìyù* (T208, Dàoluè/Kumārajīva); and the masterpiece [[KR6b0066]] *Bǎiyú jīng* 百喻經 (T209, Saṃghasena via Guṇavṛddhi, 492) — Lú Xùn's 1914 edition made this one of the foundational texts for modern Chinese literary reception of the Buddhist canon.

**Dharmapada / Udānavarga cluster.** The four-text gnomic-verse complex of the Chinese canon: [[KR6b0067]] *Fǎjù jīng* 法句經 (T210, Wéi Qínán / Zhī Qiān, 224) — the canonical Chinese *Dharmapada* with the famous translator's preface; [[KR6b0068]] *Fǎjù pìyù jīng* (T211, Fǎjù + Fǎlì) — verse and avadāna-narrative combined; [[KR6b0069]] *Chūyào jīng* 出曜經 (T212, Zhú Fóniàn, 398–399) — the most extensive Chinese *Udānavarga* with full prose auto-commentary; [[KR6b0070]] *Fǎjí yàosòng jīng* (T213, Tiānxīzāi, 980–1000) — the Northern-Sòng re-translation.

## Important persons

- [[安世高]] (c. 148–170 CE active) — Parthian monk, the earliest dated translator in the division (T167 *Tàizǐ Mùpò jīng*); his rendering of a single princely jātaka inaugurates the Chinese reception of *běnyuán* literature.
- [[支謙]] (active c. 222–253) — Wú-period translator of more works in this division than any other figure: at least nine, including the canonical *Dharmapada* (T210), the *Avadāna-śataka* (T200), the *Aṭṭhakavagga* (T198), and the foundational royal-jātakas (T169, T181a/b, T185, T214); his preface to the *Dharmapada* is the foundational document of pre-modern Chinese Buddhist translation theory.
- [[康僧會]] (d. 280) — Sogdian-Wú compiler of [[KR6b0001|*Liù-dù jí jīng* (T152)]] and the *Jiù zá pìyù jīng* (T206), credited with naturalising *bodhisattva*-ethics in classical-Chinese ethical discourse.
- [[竺大力]] and [[康孟詳]] — the Eastern-Hàn translation pair responsible for [[KR6b0040|T184]], [[KR6b0053|T196]], and [[KR6b0054|T197]], the foundational Buddha-biography and karmic-aetiology corpus of the Hàn.
- [[竺法護]] (Dharmarakṣa, c. 233–310) — Western-Jìn translator of at least seven works in this division (T154, T168, T170, T180, T182, T186, T199); the principal Western-Jìn conduit for *jātaka* and Buddha-biographical material.
- [[曇無讖]] (Dharmakṣema, 385–433) — Northern-Liáng translator of the *Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka* ([[KR6b0006|T157]]) and the *Buddhacarita* ([[KR6b0049|T192]]); the most consequential early-fifth-century translator of narrative material.
- [[鳩摩羅什]] (Kumārajīva, 344–413) — translator of the *Sūtrālaṃkāra* ([[KR6b0058|T201]]) and (with Dàoluè) of T208; the principal Latter-Qín influence on the division.
- [[竺佛念]] (active 365–410) — Latter-Qín translator of the *Udānavarga* with auto-commentary ([[KR6b0069|T212]]), the most-extensive Chinese gnomic-verse compendium.
- [[馬鳴菩薩]] (Aśvaghoṣa, c. late 1st – early 2nd c. CE) — attributed author of the *Buddhacarita* ([[KR6b0049|T192]]) and the *Sūtrālaṃkāra* ([[KR6b0058|T201]]); the most important Indic literary figure represented in the division.
- [[求那跋陀羅]] (Guṇabhadra, 394–468) — LiúSòng translator of the *Guòqù xiànzài yīnguǒ jīng* ([[KR6b0045|T189]]) and the *Dàyì jīng* ([[KR6b0031|T177]]).
- [[慧覺]] (5th c.) — Liáng-zhōu monk who led the eight-member Khotan-pilgrim delegation behind the *Xiányú jīng* ([[KR6b0059|T202]]), an unusual case of a *jīng* compiled from oral-discourse notes.
- [[闍那崛多]] (Jñānagupta, 523–600) — Suí translator of [[KR6b0047|T190]], the longest Mahāyāna Buddha-biography in Chinese.
- [[地婆訶羅]] (Divākara, 613–688) — Táng re-translator of the *Lalitavistara* ([[KR6b0043|T187]]).
- [[義淨]] (Yìjìng, 635–713) — Táng translator of the short royal-jātakas [[KR6b0015|T163]] and the parable-sūtra [[KR6b0074|T217]].
- [[般若]] (Prajña, 734–c. 810) — Kashmiri Táng translator of the *Xīndì-guān jīng* ([[KR6b0008|T159]]), the latest Mahāyāna translation of the division.
- [[施護]] (Dānapāla, fl. 980–1017) — Northern-Sòng translator at the *Yìjīng yuàn*; produced four of the late re-translations: [[KR6b0017|T165]], [[KR6b0025|T173]], [[KR6b0075|T218]], [[KR6b0076|T219]].
- [[法賢]] / [[天息災]] (d. 1001) — Northern-Sòng translator of [[KR6b0048|T191]] (Buddha-biography re-translation), [[KR6b0070|T213]] (the *Udānavarga* re-translation), and the short [[KR6b0018|T166]] and [[KR6b0073]].
- [[僧伽斯那]] (Saṃghasena, 4th–5th c.) — Indic compiler of the parables that became [[KR6b0066|T209]] *Bǎiyú jīng*.
- [[求那毘地]] (Guṇavṛddhi, d. 502) — Southern-Qí translator of [[KR6b0066|T209]] at Jiànkāng in 492 CE.
- [[來舟]] (Yōngzhèng-era Qīng) — the only post-Sòng author in the division, commentator on the *Xīndì-guān jīng* ([[KR6b0009]], [[KR6b0010]], [[KR6b0011]]).

## Topics

- **The 本緣 generic frame.** What unifies the division is the Indic karmic-narrative complex of *jātaka* (past-life of the *bodhisattva*), *avadāna* (karmically-conditioned biography of a virtuous being), *nidāna* (the conditioning narrative for a doctrinal point), and *upamā* / *dṛṣṭānta* (parable). The Chinese organising rubric 本緣 collapses these Indic distinctions; readers should treat them as a continuum rather than as sharply-bounded genres.
- **Translation vs. compilation.** Unlike the Āgamas (KR6a) or Prajñāpāramitā (KR6c), several of the most-influential works of KR6b are **Chinese editorial compilations** rather than translations of single Indic recensions: [[KR6b0001|T152]] (Kāng Sēnghuì's compilation under six-*pāramitā* rubric), [[KR6b0059|T202]] (Huìjué et al. assembled from Khotanese oral recitations), the [[KR6b0061|*Zá pìyù*]] complex. The boundary between translation and Chinese authorship is unusually porous here.
- **Multiple-recension preservation.** The division contains an unusual density of multiple-recension survivals of single short jātakas: four *Śyāmaka*-jātakas ([[KR6b0026]]–[[KR6b0029]]), two *Tàizǐ Mùpò* ([[KR6b0019]], [[KR6b0020]]), two *Jiǔsè lù* ([[KR6b0035]], [[KR6b0036]]), two *Lùmǔ* ([[KR6b0037]], [[KR6b0038]]). These doublet/triplet/quadruplet preservations are a key resource for the historical-linguistic study of pre-Tang Chinese Buddhist translation.
- **Buddha-biography textual problem.** Four parallel Buddha-biography traditions are preserved in Chinese — the Hàn 修行/中本起 cycle ([[KR6b0040]] + [[KR6b0053]]), the Wú *Tàizǐ ruìyìng* ([[KR6b0041]]), the *Lalitavistara* pair ([[KR6b0042]] / [[KR6b0043]]), the Mūlasarvāstivādin *Fó běnxíng jí* ([[KR6b0047]]), the *Buddhacarita* + *Fó běnxíng jīng* verse-pair ([[KR6b0049]] + [[KR6b0050]]), and the Sòng *Zhòngxǔ móhēdì jīng* ([[KR6b0048]]) — yielding a four-recension Chinese-canonical Buddha-biography problem whose comparative study is foundational for pre-modern Indic-Buddhist biographical reception.
- **The Dharmapada question.** The three-text Chinese *Dharmapada* / *Udānavarga* set ([[KR6b0067]], [[KR6b0069]], [[KR6b0070]]) preserves a Sarvāstivādin-tradition recension different from the Pāli *Dhammapada* in selection, ordering, and verse-count, and remains essential to comparative Buddhist gnomic-verse studies alongside the Gāndhārī (Brough), Sanskrit *Udānavarga* (Bernhard), and Pāli traditions.
- **Royal jātaka and ethical-political reading.** A substantial sub-set of the division ([[KR6b0013]]–[[KR6b0018]], [[KR6b0022]], [[KR6b0023]], [[KR6b0025]]) places the *bodhisattva* as a royal protagonist whose *dāna* / *kṣānti* / *vīrya* paradigms are inscribed into models of just kingship; this material was particularly important in the Chinese ethical-political reception of Buddhism (the Vessantara-narrative of [[KR6b0023]] is the most-cited example).
- **Vegetarianism / non-violence.** Several works ([[KR6b0016]] *Shīzǐ Sùtuósuō wáng duànròu jīng*; [[KR6b0039]] *Yīqièzhì guāngmíng xiānrén cíxīn yīnyuán bùshíròu jīng*) are textual ancestors of the Chinese-Buddhist meat-renunciation discourse that became central in the Liáng and afterwards.
- **Reception in popular literature.** The avadāna and parable anthologies — especially [[KR6b0059|T202]], [[KR6b0060|T203]], and [[KR6b0066|T209]] — were the principal canonical source for the Dūnhuáng *biàn-wén* 變文 transformation-text tradition (Mair 1989) and for East-Asian Buddhist popular-narrative literature more broadly; T202 became the basis of the Tibetan *mDzangs blun*.
