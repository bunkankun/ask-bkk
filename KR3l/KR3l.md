# KR3l 小說家類 — School of Minor Discourses

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3l 小說家類 is the *Sìkù* Masters Branch (子部) subdivision that gathers what later editors called *xiǎoshuō* 小說 in the pre-modern sense — anecdotal miscellanies (*bǐjì* 筆記), accounts of the strange (*zhìguài* 志怪), records of urban and court anecdote (*yìshì* 逸事, *suǒyán* 瑣言, *wénjiàn lù* 聞見錄), and the Tang-Sòng narrative experiments that modern literary history calls *chuánqí* 傳奇. The category descends from the *Hànshū yìwén zhì* "ten Schools" rubric — *xiǎoshuō jiā zhě liú, gài chū yú bàiguān* 小說家者流, 蓋出於稗官 ("the School of Minor Discourses derives from the petty officers") — and was re-anchored under the *Sìkù* as the trailing subdivision of the Masters Branch on the strength of an editorial decision the Qing reviewers themselves call grudging: *xiǎoshuō* sits at the bottom of *zǐbù* because, by the eighteenth century, neither the bureaucratic-realist *jìshì* register nor the *zhìguài* / *chuánqí* register fitted the orthodox classification cleanly, yet too much of the medieval and Sòng documentary record was preserved only here to permit exclusion. Wilkinson §53 ("*Biji*"), §53.3 ("*Biji xiaoshuo*"), and §60.3 (under *Shishuo xinyu*) together provide the standard English-language framing: the genre's pre-Han origins in *jiētán xiàngyǔ* 街談巷語 street-corner talk; Liú Zhījī's eighth-century re-classification of *záshù* 雜述 into ten sub-types (*piānjì*, *xiǎolù*, *yìshì*, *suǒyán*, *jùnshū*, *jiāshǐ*, *biézhuàn*, *zájì*, *dìlǐ*, *dūyì bù*; *Shǐtōng* 史通, Nèi 34, [Wilkinson Box 264]); the persistent failure of pre-modern Chinese taxonomy to distinguish "fiction" from "factual notes," because the same *bǐjì* routinely mixes both; and the modern May-Fourth re-purposing of *xiǎoshuō* to mean prose fiction in the European sense, an equation that maps poorly onto everything classified here.

The chronological span of the division runs from the *Shānhǎi jīng* 山海經 [[KR3l0090]] (a composite Warring-States-to-Hàn cosmographic-cum-marvel anthology) to *Liáozhāi zhìyì* 聊齋誌異 [[KR3l0130]] (Pú Sōnglíng, c. 1670–1715) — effectively, the whole arc of premodern Chinese anecdotal and *zhìguài* writing in literary Chinese. Three transmissional traditions a reader must keep in mind are: (1) the pre-Táng *zhìguài* corpus, much of which survives only as quotation-fragments in *lèishū* (*Yìwén lèijù*, *Tàipíng yùlǎn*, *Tàipíng guǎngjì*) and which the modern Lǔ Xùn 魯迅 *Gǔ xiǎoshuō gōu chén* 古小說鉤沈 reconstruction project brought back into circulation — the entries `[[KR3l0131]]`–`[[KR3l0169]]` are *jíyì* reconstructions of this kind; (2) the SòngYuánMíng *bǐjì* mainstream, the principal supplementary source for everything the Standard Histories chose not to record (cf. Wilkinson §62.3.11 on Sòng *bǐjì*); and (3) the late-imperial *zhìguài* literary revival that culminates in *Liáozhāi zhìyì* and is anticipated by *Yǒuyáng zázǔ* 酉陽雜俎 [[KR3l0125]] in the ninth century.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The *Shìshuō xīnyǔ* and the *shìshuō tǐ* tradition.** *Shìshuō xīnyǔ* 世說新語 [[KR3l0002]] (c. 430–444, compiled under [[劉義慶]] Liú Yìqìng; commentary by [[劉孝標]] Liú Xiàobiāo, 462–521) is the foundational anecdotal compilation of medieval China and the genre-prototype of the *shìshuō tǐ* 世說體 — some 1,130 short anecdotes on c. 600 persons of the late Hàn and WèiJìn organised into thirty-six character-appraisal categories from *Déxíng* 德行 to *Chóuxì* 仇隟. Liú Xiàobiāo's commentary, citing some 400 lost works, is itself one of the great survival-vectors for Six Dynasties biography. The division registers the *shìshuō tǐ* afterlife directly in *Yǔlín* 語林 [[KR3l0089]] (Hé Liángjùn 何良俊, Míng), *Táng yǔlín* 唐語林 [[KR3l0055]] (Wáng Dǎng 王讜, Sòng — a *shìshuō*-style re-categorisation of Táng anecdote), and the lost *Péizǐ yǔlín* 裴子語林 [[KR3l0135]] (Péi Qǐ 裴啟, fl. c. 362), the work whose forgery scandal Liú Yìqìng's commission was designed in part to displace.

**Pre-Táng *zhìguài* core.** *Sōushén jì* 搜神記 [[KR3l0099]] (Gān Bǎo 干寶, c. 317–336) is the canonical *zhìguài* anthology and the principal source of the genre's iconography of ghosts, foxes, dragons, and karmic retribution. The companion *Sōushén hòujì* 搜神後記 [[KR3l0100]] is conventionally ascribed to Táo Qián 陶潛 (365–427) but is almost certainly LiúSòng. *Yìyuàn* 異苑 [[KR3l0101]] (Liú Jìngshū 劉敬叔, LiúSòng), *Shíyí jì* 拾遺記 [[KR3l0098]] (Wáng Jiā 王嘉, late-fourth-century; supplemented by Xiāo Qǐ 蕭綺, Liáng), *Xù Qíxié jì* 續齊諧記 [[KR3l0102]] (Wú Jūn 吳均, Liáng), and *Huányuān jì* 還冤記 [[KR3l0103]] (Yán Zhītuī 顏之推, Suí) round out the surviving core. The pseudepigraphic Hàn cluster — *Shényì jīng* 神異經 [[KR3l0093]] (attributed to Dōngfāng Shuò 東方朔), *Hǎinèi shízhōu jì* 海內十洲記 [[KR3l0094]] (id.), *Hàn Wǔ gùshì* 漢武故事 [[KR3l0095]] and *Hàn Wǔdì nèizhuàn* 漢武帝內傳 [[KR3l0096]] (both attributed to Bān Gù 班固), and *Dòngmíng jì* 洞冥記 [[KR3l0097]] (attributed to Guō Xiàn 郭憲) — are uniformly Six-Dynasties or later pseudepigrapha that present a Hàn world reimagined through *fāngshì* and early Daoist mythography.

**The cosmographic and *bówù* tradition.** *Shānhǎi jīng* 山海經 [[KR3l0090]] (with Guō Pú's 郭璞 [276–324] standard commentary) and *Mù Tiānzǐ zhuàn* 穆天子傳 [[KR3l0092]] (id.) are the two Warring-States-to-Hàn cosmographic-cum-marvel survivors that the Sìkù editors placed in this division rather than under geography or history. *Bówù zhì* 博物志 [[KR3l0123]] (Zhāng Huá 張華, 232–300) and its Sòng continuation *Xù bówù zhì* 續博物志 [[KR3l0128]] (Lǐ Shí 李石, twelfth century) define the *bówù* "treatise on universal things" sub-genre. *Wú Rènchén's* 吳任臣 *Shānhǎi jīng guǎngzhù* 山海經廣注 [[KR3l0091]] is the standard early-Qīng (1667) re-annotation. *Xuánzhōng jì* 玄中記 [[KR3l0160]], attributed in part to Guō Pú, survives only in fragments.

**The Táng anecdotal *bǐjì*.** *Cháoyě qiānzài* 朝野僉載 [[KR3l0003]] (Zhāng Zhuó 張鷟, c. 700–730) is the earliest Táng court-and-country miscellany in the division; *Táng xīnyǔ* 唐新語 [[KR3l0004]] (Liú Sù 劉肅, 807) is its *shìshuō*-modelled re-organisation. *Táng guóshǐ bǔ* 唐國史補 [[KR3l0006]] (Lǐ Zhào 李肇, c. 813–825) is the most-cited Mid-Táng anecdotal supplement to the dynastic record. *Cì Liǔshì jiùwén* 次柳氏舊聞 [[KR3l0005]] (Lǐ Déyù 李德裕, 834), *Liú Bīnkè jiāhuà lù* 劉賓客嘉話錄 [[KR3l0007]] (Wéi Xuàn 韋絢, 856 — a record of Liú Yǔxī's 劉禹錫 conversation), *Yīnhuà lù* 因話錄 [[KR3l0008]] (Zhào Lín 趙璘), *Mínghuáng záilù* 明皇雜錄 [[KR3l0009]] (Zhèng Chǔhuì 鄭處誨, 855), *Dà Táng chuánzài* 大唐傳載 [[KR3l0010]], and *Jiàofāng jì* 教坊記 [[KR3l0011]] (Cuī Lìngqīn 崔令欽, on the imperial Music Bureau under Xuánzōng) define the High-Táng / Mid-Táng register. *Sōngchuāng záilù* 松窗雜錄 [[KR3l0013]], *Yúnxī yǒuyì* 雲谿友議* [[KR3l0014]], *Yùquán zǐ* 玉泉子 [[KR3l0015]], and *Yúnxiān záilù* 雲仙雜記 [[KR3l0016]] (a Sòng forgery placed under the name of Féng Zhì 馮贄) carry the genre into the Late Táng and across the early-Sòng forgery boundary. The Five Dynasties — *Táng zhāiyán* 唐摭言 [[KR3l0017]] (Wáng Dìngbǎo 王定保, c. 954, on the Táng examination system), *Zhōngcháo gùshì* 中朝故事 [[KR3l0018]], *Jīnhuá zǐ záibiān* 金華子雜編 [[KR3l0019]], *Kāiyuán Tiānbǎo yíshì* 開元天寶遺事 [[KR3l0020]] (Wáng Rényù 王仁裕, c. 940–956), and *Jiànjiè lù* 鑑誡錄 [[KR3l0021]] (Hé Guāngyuǎn 何光遠, Later Shǔ) — preserve the trauma of the Táng collapse from the perspective of officials who had served under both dynasties.

**The Táng *zhìguài* / *chuánqí* line.** *Jíyì jì* 集異記 [[KR3l0104]] (Xuē Yòngruò 薛用弱, c. 821–824), *Bóyì jì* 博異記 [[KR3l0105]] (Gǔ Shénzǐ 谷神子), *Qiándìng lù* 前定錄 [[KR3l0107]] (Zhōng Lù 鍾輅, 827–835), *Xuānshì zhì* 宣室志 [[KR3l0110]] (Zhāng Dú 張讀), *Jùtán lù* 劇談錄 [[KR3l0109]] (Kāng Pián 康駢, 877–895), *Dùyáng zábiān* 杜陽雜編 [[KR3l0106]] (Sū È 蘇鶚, 886), *Guìyuàn cóngtán* 桂苑叢談 [[KR3l0108]], *Táng quē shǐ* 唐闕史 [[KR3l0111]] (Gāo Yànxiū 高彥休), *Gānzé yáo* 甘澤謠 [[KR3l0112]] (Yuán Jiāo 袁郊), *Kāitiān chuánxìn jì* 開天傳信記 [[KR3l0113]], and *Xù yōuguài lù* 續幽怪錄 [[KR3l0126]] (Lǐ Fùyán 李復言, Bùyǔ 補語 to Niú Sēngrú's 牛僧孺 lost *Xuánguài lù*) constitute the corpus most directly continuous with what modern literary history calls Táng *chuánqí*. *Yǒuyáng zázǔ* 酉陽雜俎 [[KR3l0125]] (Duàn Chéngshì 段成式, c. 853–863) stands apart in scale and erudition — Wilkinson §61.3.11 notes that it would "well be taken for *xiǎoshuō*, although today it is classified as a *biji*" — and is the most-cited Táng miscellany of the entire post-Táng tradition.

**The Northern Sòng *bǐjì* mainstream.** This is the largest cluster in the division. *Sùshuǐ jìwén* 涑水記聞 [[KR3l0030]] (Sīmǎ Guāng 司馬光, c. 1070–1086) records the political-historical anecdotes Sīmǎ collected during *Zīzhì tōngjiàn* compilation but excluded as too informal for the *tōngjiàn* proper. *Guītián lù* 歸田錄 [[KR3l0032]] (Ōuyáng Xiū 歐陽修, 1067) is the canonical retirement-*bǐjì*. *Shéngshuǐ yàntán lù* 澠水燕談錄 [[KR3l0031]] (Wáng Pìzhī 王闢之, c. 1095), *Dōngzhāi jìshì* 東齋記事 [[KR3l0034]] (Fàn Zhèn 范鎮), *Qīngxiāng zájì* 青箱雜記 [[KR3l0035]] (Wú Chǔhòu 吳處厚), *Lóngchuān lüèzhì* 龍川畧志 [[KR3l0037]] (Sū Zhé 蘇轍, 1099), *Hòushān táncóng* 後山談叢 [[KR3l0038]] (Chén Shīdào 陳師道), *Sūngōng tánpǔ* 孫公談圃 [[KR3l0039]] (Sūn Shēng 孫升 / Liú Yánshì 劉延世), *Tányuàn* 談苑 [[KR3l0040]] (Kǒng Píngzhòng 孔平仲), and *Dōngxuān bǐlù* 東軒筆錄 [[KR3l0048]] (Wèi Tài 魏泰, c. 1086–1110, the principal hostile source on Wáng Ānshí's reforms) collectively cover political and personal anecdote across the late eleventh and early twelfth centuries. *Wáng Gǒng's* 王鞏 (fl. 1075–1104) three works — *Jiǎshēn zájì* 甲申雜記 [[KR3l0042]], *Wénjiàn jìnlù* 聞見近錄 [[KR3l0043]], *Suíshǒu zálù* 隨手雜錄 [[KR3l0044]] — form a single Sū Shì-circle trilogy of personal recollection. The Buddhist-monastic register is represented by *Shì Wényíng's* 釋文瑩 two works *Xiāngshān yělù* 湘山野錄 [[KR3l0045]] and *Yùhú yěshǐ* 玉壺野史 [[KR3l0046]]. *Hóuqīng lù* 侯鯖錄 [[KR3l0047]] (Zhào Lìngzhì 趙令畤), *Bózhái biān* 泊宅編 [[KR3l0049]] (Fāng Sháo 方勺), *Tiěwéishān cóngtán* 鐵圍山叢談 [[KR3l0051]] (Cài Tāo 蔡絛, son of Cài Jīng 蔡京, written during exile after the Northern-Sòng collapse), and *Mòkè huīxī* 墨客揮犀 [[KR3l0054]] complete the Northern-Sòng corpus.

**The Northern–Southern Sòng transition cluster.** *Wénjiàn lù* 聞見錄 [[KR3l0066]] (Shào Bówēn 邵伯溫, 1132–1134) and its continuation *Wénjiàn hòu lù* 聞見後錄 [[KR3l0069]] (Shào Bó 邵博, fl. 1138) preserve the founding *Lǐxué* circle of Shào Yōng 邵雍 alongside the political record of the late BěiSòng. *Qīngbō zázhì* 清波雜志 [[KR3l0067]] (Zhōu Huī 周煇, c. 1192–1194), *Jīlèi biān* 雞肋編 [[KR3l0068]] (Zhuāng Chuò 莊綽, c. 1133–1139), *Mòjì* 默記 [[KR3l0061]] (Wáng Zhì 王銍), *Guòtíng lù* 過庭錄 [[KR3l0058]] (Fàn Gōngchēng 范公偁), *Gāozhāi mànlù* 高齋漫錄 [[KR3l0060]] (Zēng Zào 曾慥), *Bùlǐ kètán* 步里客談 [[KR3l0071]] (Chén Chángfāng 陳長方), and *Běichuāng zhìguǒ lù* 北牕炙輠錄 [[KR3l0070]] (Shī Décāo 施德操) collectively document the trauma of the *Jìngkāng* 靖康 catastrophe and the southern reconstitution.

**The Southern Sòng anecdotal masters.** *Wáng Míngqīng's* 王明清 (b. 1127) trilogy — *Huīchén lù* 揮塵錄 [[KR3l0062]], *Yùzhào xīnzhì* 玉照新志 [[KR3l0063]], *Tóuxiá lù* 投轄錄 [[KR3l0064]] — is the most comprehensive single private record of the Southern Sòng court and is heavily mined by the *Sòngshǐ* compilers. *Tīngshǐ* 桯史 [[KR3l0072]] (Yuè Kē 岳珂, c. 1200–1214), grandson of Yuè Fēi 岳飛, supplies the family-perspective record of the Shàoxīng peace and after. *Dúxǐng zázhì* 獨醒雜志 [[KR3l0073]] (Zēng Mǐnxíng 曾敏行), *Sìcháo wénjiàn lù* 四朝聞見錄 [[KR3l0075]] (Yè Shàowēng 葉紹翁, c. 1225–1235, a key source on the Qìngyuán *dǎngjìn* against Zhū Xī), and *Qíjiù xùwén* 耆舊續聞 [[KR3l0074]] (Chén Hú 陳鵠) round out the High and Late Southern Sòng. *Guǐxīn zázhì* 癸辛雜識 [[KR3l0076]] (Zhōu Mì 周密, c. 1280–1298) and *Suíyǐn mànlù* 隨隱漫錄 [[KR3l0077]] (Chén Shìchóng 陳世崇) are the major Sòng-loyalist records of the Yuán transition. The two great Southern-Sòng *zhìguài* collections — *Hóng Mài's* 洪邁 *Yíjiān zhì jiǎ* 夷堅志甲 [[KR3l0122]] (the surviving *jiǎ* portion of an originally encyclopaedic project) and *Kuíchē zhì* 睽車志 [[KR3l0121]] (Guō Tuàn 郭彖) — extend the Tang *zhìguài* line into the twelfth century, with *Yíjiān zhì* in particular providing some of the richest material on vernacular language change for the period (Wilkinson §6.7, citing *HD* corrections).

**The Yuán cluster.** *Guīqián zhì* 歸潛志 [[KR3l0079]] (Liú Qí 劉祁, 1235–1241) is the canonical record of the JīnYuán transition by an ex-Jīn official; it is widely cited in *Jīnshǐ* compilation. *Shānjū xīnhuà* 山居新話 [[KR3l0081]] (Yáng Yú 楊瑀), *Suìchāng zálù* 遂昌雜錄 [[KR3l0082]] (Zhèng Yuányòu 鄭元祐), *Lèjiāo sīyǔ* 樂郊私語 [[KR3l0083]], *Shānfáng suíbǐ* 山房隨筆 [[KR3l0080]], and *Dōngnán jìwén* 東南紀聞 [[KR3l0078]] survey late-Yuán southern society. *Chuògēng lù* 輟耕錄 [[KR3l0084]] (Táo Zōngyí 陶宗儀, c. 1366–1368, 30 *juǎn*) is the single most influential late-Yuán *bǐjì* and Wilkinson's main example (§53.1, Box 265) of the irreducibly mixed character of the genre: nine subject categories ranging from history (247 entries) and biography (157) to folklore (42) and the supernatural (97).

**The Míng cluster.** *Shuǐdōng rìjì* 水東日記 [[KR3l0085]] (Yè Shèng 葉盛, 1460–1474), *Shūyuán zájì* 菽園雜記 [[KR3l0086]] (Lù Róng 陸容, 1480–1494), *Xiānjìn yífēng* 先進遺風 [[KR3l0087]] (Gěng Dìngxiàng 耿定向 / Máo Zài 毛在), *Gū bù gū lù* 觚不觚錄 [[KR3l0088]] (Wáng Shìzhēn 王世貞, 1580–1590, on the breakdown of Míng institutional culture under Wànlì), and *Yǔlín* 語林 [[KR3l0089]] (Hé Liángjùn 何良俊, 1540–1551, the Míng *shìshuō tǐ* anchor) carry the genre through the dynasty.

**Imperially commissioned *biji xiǎoshuō* and *lèishū*-aligned *zhìguài*.** *Tàipíng guǎngjì* 太平廣記 [[KR3l0118]] (Lǐ Fǎng 李昉 et al., 977–978, 500 *juǎn*) is the foundational imperial compendium of Six-Dynasties-and-Táng *xiǎoshuō*, classified here rather than as a *lèishū* despite its 150-plus subject categories; it preserves c. 7,000 stories drawn from over 500 source-titles, c. 250 of which survive nowhere else (Wilkinson §53.3.1). *Fēnmén gǔjīn lèishì* 分門古今類事 [[KR3l0119]] is its modest twelfth-century successor on a *Tàipíng guǎngjì* topical model.

**The early-Sòng *zhìguài* / *yìrén* corpus.** *Jīshén lù* 稽神錄 [[KR3l0114]] (Xú Xuàn 徐鉉, 916–991, written before he came over to the Sòng from the Southern Táng), *Jiānghuái yìrén lù* 江淮異人錄 [[KR3l0115]] (Wú Shū 吳淑, 947–1002), *Máotíng kèhuà* 茅亭客話 [[KR3l0116]] (Huáng Xiūfù 黃休復), *Kuòyì zhì* 括異志 [[KR3l0117]] (Zhāng Shīzhèng 張師正), *Qīngyì lù* 清異錄 [[KR3l0127]] (Táo Gǔ 陶穀), *Tao-zhū xīnlù* 陶朱新錄 [[KR3l0120]] (Mǎ Chún 馬純), and *NánTáng jìnshì* 南唐近事 [[KR3l0022]] (Zhèng Wénbǎo 鄭文寶, 977) extend the Táng *zhìguài* line directly into the early Sòng. *Běimèng suǒyán* 北夢瑣言 [[KR3l0023]] (Sūn Guāngxiàn 孫光憲, d. 968) and *Nánbù xīnshū* 南部新書* [[KR3l0026]] (Qián Yì 錢易, 1008–1016) preserve substantial Late-Táng and Five-Dynasties anecdote from a tenth-century perspective.

**The Lǔ Xùn *jíyì* recovery cluster.** Entries [[KR3l0131]]–[[KR3l0169]] (the second layer beyond the WYG *Sìkù*) are fragment-reconstructions, mostly drawn from Lǔ Xùn's *Gǔ xiǎoshuō gōu chén* 古小說鉤沈 (1909–11) and continued in modern compilations: *Yān Dānzǐ* 燕丹子 [[KR3l0131]] (reconstructed from *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* by Sūn Xīngyǎn 孫星衍, 1788); *Míngxiáng jì* 冥祥記 [[KR3l0132]] (Wáng Yǎn 王琰, late-fifth-century Buddhist *zhìguài*); *Gǔjīn shànyán* 古今善言 [[KR3l0133]] (Fàn Tài 范泰); *Lièyì zhuàn* 列異傳 [[KR3l0137]] (attributed to Cáo Pī 曹丕 or Zhāng Huá); *Péizǐ yǔlín* 裴子語林 [[KR3l0135]]; *Guō zǐ* 郭子 [[KR3l0155]] (Guō Chéngzhī 郭澄之); *Sú shuō* 俗說 [[KR3l0157]] (Shěn Yuē 沈約); *Xiǎoshuō* 小說 [[KR3l0158]] (Yīn Yún 殷芸, c. 519–529, the first Chinese work explicitly titled "*xiǎoshuō*"); *Qíxié jì* 齊諧記 [[KR3l0168]] (Dōngyáng Wúyí 東陽無疑, LiúSòng); *Yōumíng lù* 幽明錄 [[KR3l0169]] (Liú Yìqìng — the second of his three works in this division); *Xuānyàn jì* 宣驗記 [[KR3l0151]] (also Liú Yìqìng); *Jí líng jì* 集靈記 [[KR3l0167]] (Yán Zhītuī, paired with his *Huányuān jì*); and a long tail of single-fragment *zhìguài* reconstructions ([[KR3l0136]]–[[KR3l0166]]). These entries are *not* WYG-derived and should be read with the understanding that the surviving fragments are typically a few dozen to a few hundred lines, not full books.

**The *Shìér xiǎomíng lù* trilogy.** [[KR3l0170]]–[[KR3l0172]] — *Shìér xiǎomíng lù* (Hóng Shì 洪适 / Wáng Zhì 王銍, attribution disputed), *Shìér xiǎomíng lù shíyí* 侍兒小名錄拾遺 (Zhāng Bāngjī 張邦基), and *Xù bǔ Shìér xiǎomíng lù* 續補侍兒小名錄 (Wáng Zhì) — are a Sòng-period micro-genre of records of the personal names of palace-women and serving-maids, an unusual onomastic supplement to the conventional historical record.

**The terminal point: *Liáozhāi zhìyì*.** *Liáozhāi zhìyì* 聊齋誌異 [[KR3l0130]] (Pú Sōnglíng 蒲松齡, c. 1670–1715) is the great *Qīng* literary-Chinese *zhìguài* / *chuánqí* collection, the work that retrospectively recasts the entire pre-modern *zhìguài* tradition into a single literary genealogy. It sits at the chronological terminus of the division and is the most translated of all KR3l texts.

## Topics

1. **The instability of *xiǎoshuō* as a category.** The division is the bibliographical residue of a classification problem that pre-modern Chinese taxonomy never solved: a single *bǐjì* routinely mixes anecdote, scholarly note, geographic observation, biographical sketch, and *zhìguài*. Wilkinson §53 frames this as the principal *interpretive* challenge for the reader of any work here. *Chuògēng lù* [[KR3l0084]] is the standard example.

2. **The factual / fictional boundary.** Until the May-Fourth re-purposing of *xiǎoshuō* to mean "fiction" in the European sense, no Chinese editorial tradition required the distinction. The division therefore includes works that are now read as fiction (*Liáozhāi zhìyì* [[KR3l0130]]), works that are now read as history (*Sùshuǐ jìwén* [[KR3l0030]], the Wáng Míngqīng trilogy [[KR3l0062]]–[[KR3l0064]]), and works that resist either reading (the great majority).

3. **The pseudepigraphic Hàn cluster.** *Shényì jīng* [[KR3l0093]], *Hǎinèi shízhōu jì* [[KR3l0094]], *Hàn Wǔ gùshì* [[KR3l0095]], *Hàn Wǔdì nèizhuàn* [[KR3l0096]], *Dòngmíng jì* [[KR3l0097]], and the disputed *Xījīng zájì* [[KR3l0001]] (ascribed to Liú Xīn 劉歆, transmitted by Gě Hóng 葛洪, but a post-Liáng compilation by the criterion of internal evidence) constitute a coherent body of Six-Dynasties pseudepigraphic reimaginings of the Western Hàn world, structured around the *fāngshì* and early Daoist mythography that took shape between the second and the sixth centuries.

4. **The *zhìguài* / *chuánqí* relationship.** The division flattens the modern literary-historical distinction: many Táng works classed as *xiǎoshuō* by the *Sìkù* (the *Jíyì jì* line, [[KR3l0104]]–[[KR3l0114]]) are read in modern literary history as the founding documents of the *chuánqí* genre.

5. **The *shìshuō tǐ* persistence.** The character-appraisal anecdotal mode initiated by *Shìshuō xīnyǔ* [[KR3l0002]] continues to generate works for more than a millennium — *Táng yǔlín* [[KR3l0055]], *Yǔlín* [[KR3l0089]], the lost *Péizǐ yǔlín* [[KR3l0135]] — and Wilkinson (§60.3, citing Qián Nánxiù 2001) counts c. thirty *Shìshuō*-style works between the Táng and the early twentieth century, including two Qīng works titled *Nǚ shìshuō* 女世說.

6. **The *lèishū*-mediated survival of the pre-Táng corpus.** A very large share of the works that would otherwise belong in this division survive only as quotation-fragments in *Tàipíng guǎngjì* [[KR3l0118]], *Yìwén lèijù*, *Tàipíng yùlǎn*, and *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn*. The Lǔ Xùn *jíyì* recovery cluster ([[KR3l0131]]–[[KR3l0169]]) makes this survival mechanism explicit.

7. **The *bǐjì* as a corrective to the Standard Histories.** Wilkinson §53 makes the historiographic case explicitly: "*Biji* often relate anecdotes, gossip, and rumor, all subjects considered unfit for more formal works, but not infrequently essential for social history. They are therefore a useful corrective to the *Histories* and other works of Confucian historiography." The Táng anecdotal cluster ([[KR3l0003]]–[[KR3l0017]]) and the entire Sòng *bǐjì* mainstream are mined heavily by modern social, economic, and institutional historians for precisely this reason.

8. **Vernacular language change.** Several works in the division — *Shìshuō xīnyǔ* [[KR3l0002]] (Six-Dynasties spoken language), *Yǒuyáng zázǔ* [[KR3l0125]] (Mid-Táng colloquial), *Yíjiān zhì jiǎ* [[KR3l0122]] (Southern-Sòng vernacular) — are principal sources for the diachronic study of spoken Chinese, far in excess of what their classification under *xiǎoshuō* would suggest.
