# Sānyuán lièshè rùxiù qùjí jí 三垣列舍入宿去極集
 *Compendium of the Three Enclosures, the Sequence of Asterisms, their Right Ascensions and Declinations*

## About the work

This Míng anonymous compendium in one *juǎn* is a tabular star-catalog. The title decomposes into the four organising principles of the catalog:

- *Sānyuán* 三垣 — the Three Enclosures (*Zǐwēi*, *Tàiwēi*, *Tiānshì*) of the circumpolar and zenithal sky;
- *Lièshè* 列舍 — the sequence (*liè* 列) of asterism "lodgings" (*shè* 舍) across the sky;
- *Rùxiù* 入宿 — the *rùxiù dù* 入宿度, the right-ascension coordinate giving how far a star lies in degrees within its lunar lodge (*xiù* 宿);
- *Qùjí* 去極 — the *qùjí dù* 去極度, the polar-distance coordinate giving the declination of each star from the celestial pole.

The catalog is in tabular (*biǎo* 表) form. Each entry gives the asterism name, the star number within the asterism, the *rùxiù* and *qùjí* coordinates, and (where relevant) the astrological assignment. It is the working tool of the Míng-bureau astronomer, complementary to the verse mnemonic of [[王希明]]'s *Bùtiān gē* ([[KR3fa005]]) and to the diagrammatic *túshuō* of [[KR3fa014]] – [[KR3fa016]].

### Abstract

The work is anonymous (*yìmíng* 佚名) and undated. The coordinate system used — *rùxiù* + *qùjí* — is the standard pre-Jesuit equatorial-and-polar-distance system, decisively superseded by the Jesuit-introduced *huángjīng* + *huángwěi* 黃經、黃緯 (ecliptic longitude and latitude) after the *Chóngzhēn lìshū* of 1635. This places the work firmly in the late-Míng pre-Jesuit window. The composition bracket adopted here (1368–1644) is the Míng dynasty; the work most likely belongs to the late Míng (the 1500–1635 sub-window), but no internal evidence narrows it further.

The coordinates given are calibrated for a specific epoch (the *lìyuán* 曆元) which is not securely identifiable from the surviving text; analogy with other Míng catalogs suggests the *Dàtǒng lì* 1384 epoch. Pan Nai (1989) treats the work in his comprehensive survey of pre-modern Chinese stellar coordinates and notes that its tabular discipline anticipates the much larger *Jesuit-collaboration* star tables of the [[KR3fa021]] *Línɡtái yíxiàng zhì* of [[南懷仁]] Verbiest (1674).

The work survives in the *Sìkù wèishōu shū jíkān* and is reprinted in the *Zhōngguó kēxué jìshù diǎnjí tōnghuì* (refid `KX03-07-016`).
