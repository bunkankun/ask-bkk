# Lǎnkū cí 孏窟詞
 *Lyrics of the Lazy-Den*
by [[侯寘]] (撰)

## About the work

The *Lǎnkū cí* 孏窟詞 is the one-*juǎn* Sìkù *cí* collection of Hóu Zhì [[侯寘]] (*fl. Shàoxīng* through *Qiándào* / *Chúnxī*, c. 1141–1180; *zì* Yànzhōu 彥周, *hào* Lǎnkū jūshì 孏窟居士), of Dōngwǔ 東武 (modern Zhūchéng, Shāndōng). Chén Zhènsūn [[陳振孫]]'s *Shūlù jiětí* records him as having served under *Shàoxīng* as *Zhí xuéshì* Prefect of Jiànkāng, but the *Tíyào* shows from internal-evidence that Hóu lived on through *Qiándào* and *Chúnxī*: the *Rénwǔ yuándàn* one *cí* must be from *Rénwǔ* / *Shàoxīng* 32 / 1162 (the year before *Lóngxīng* / *Xiàozōng*'s reign-name change); his *cí* for Zhāng Jìngfū 張敬夫 (Zhāng Shì 張栻) and the Mid-Autumn *cí* to Liú Gōngfù 劉恭甫 both refer to Xiào-zōng-era figures. Hóu was the nephew of Cháo Bǔzhī [[晁補之]]; the family had preserved the literary heritage of the *Yuányòu* old houses, and the volume's social network — its *chángchóu* with high-tier Southern-Sòng officials — reflects that pedigree. The *Tíyào* notes a *Yǎn ér méi · Xiào Yìān tǐ* 眼兒媚·效易安體 — one of the earliest documented self-conscious imitations of the Yìāntǐ 易安體 of Lǐ Qīngzhào [[李清照]] in the *cí* tradition.

### Tiyao

*Lǎnkū cí*, one *juǎn*, by Hóu Zhì of the Sòng. Per Chén Zhènsūn's *Shūlù jiětí*: Zhì, *zì* Yànzhōu, a man of Dōngwǔ; in *Shàoxīng*, as *Zhí xuéshì* he governed Jiànkāng. The collection has *Xì yòng Hè Fānghuí (Hè Zhù) yùn jiànbié Zhū Shǎozhāng* — so the man is early-Southern-Sòng. The *Yǎn ér méi · Xiào Yìān tǐ* — *Yìān* is Lǐ Qīngzhào's *hào*, also an early-*Shàoxīng* person; Hóu already calls it "imitating" — somewhat like the *Xiào Shěn Xiàxián tǐ* of Dù Mù and Lǐ Shāngyǐn. He has also a *Shòu cí* for *Zhí gé* Zhāng Jìngfū, a *Mid-Autumn* *cí* to *Shèrén* Liú Gōngfù — both Xiào-zōng-era figures; and a *Rénwǔ yuándàn* *cí* — *Rénwǔ* / *Shàoxīng* 32 / 1162, the year before Xiàozōng's accession — so Hóu was still alive between *Qiándào* and *Chúnxī*. Chén Zhènsūn merely names his official-year. Hóu was Cháo's nephew, retaining the *Yuányòu* old-family currents; hence his associates are all leading men, and his *cí* is gracefully refined, no wine-shop-and-music-hall *zānxì lángjí* manner. Though his name is not loud, among Southern-Sòng houses he must be counted a *zuòzhě*. The *Wénxiàn tōngkǎo* records one *juǎn*, agreeing with the present. Máo Jìn [[毛晉]] cut it among the *Liùshí jiā cí*, with very loose proof-reading. The worst: *Qínlóu yuè* is *Yì Qíné* — from Lǐ Bái's *cí* line *Qíné mèng duàn Qínlóu yuè*, later it was renamed; the form is properly two-stanza (*shuāngdiào*); Jìn's cut drops a *dí* character from the end of the front-half and joins it to the back-half, making the tune appear to have a variant form — a clear error. Other instances of word-confusion (*Shuǐdiào gētóu*'s *qīng* should not be flat; *Qīngyù àn*'s *mù* repeats rhyme with the prior stanza; *rǎnrǎn nián yuán* — *yuán* makes no sense): also miscut. The *Yáo tiān fèng cuìhuá yǐn* one *cí* especially full of errors, almost unreadable. No other text to collate against: corrections where possible, others left as is. — Compiled, *Qiánlóng* 44 / 1779, 10th month.

### Abstract

The transmitted *Lǎnkū cí* descends through Máo Jìn's cutting. Modern editions: the *Quán Sòng cí* of Táng Guīzhāng [[唐圭璋]] preserves around 91 *cí*. Hóu Zhì's biographical record is fragmentary: nephew of Cháo Bǔzhī (matrilineal Cháo connection); *Shàoxīng* prefectship of Jiànkāng; the *chángchóu* network includes Zhāng Shì [[張栻]] (*zì* Jìngfū), Liú Gōngfù 劉恭甫, and Zhū Shǎozhāng. The collection is best read as a useful mid-Southern-Sòng witness — refined private-friendship *cí* in a register continuous with the late-Northern-Sòng *Yuányòu* tradition the Cháo family preserved. The self-conscious *Yǎn ér méi · Xiào Yìān tǐ* is historically valuable as one of the earliest *cí* explicitly invoking Lǐ Qīngzhào's manner as a category.

## Other points of interest

The early formal-imitation of *Yìān tǐ* documented in this collection is one of the foundational moments of Lǐ Qīngzhào's canonization within Sòng literary culture: the *xiào* 效 (imitate) gesture was reserved for forms recognized as having their own distinctive prosodic-rhetorical identity, and that Hóu Zhì applied it to Lǐ within a generation of her death testifies to the speed of her recognition.
