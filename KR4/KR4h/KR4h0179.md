# Quán Jìn Wén 全晉文
*Complete Prose Writings of the Jin Dynasty*
compiled by [[嚴可均]] 嚴可均 (編)

## About the work

This file contains the *Quán Jìn Wén* 全晉文 section of [[嚴可均]]'s *Quán shànggǔ sāndài Qín Hàn Sānguó Liùcháo wén* 全上古三代秦漢三國六朝文 ([[KR4h0176]]), spanning 178 *juàn* of prose attributed to writers of the Jin dynasty — the largest dynastic section within the anthology. The file opens with writings attributed to Xuāndì 宣帝, the posthumous imperial title of Sīmǎ Yì 司馬懿 (179–251), the Wei general and statesman whose descendants founded the Jin dynasty, and proceeds through the Western Jin (265–316) and Eastern Jin (317–420) periods. Primary sources cited throughout include the *Jìn shū* 晉書, the *Wén xuǎn* 文選 and its commentary by Lǐ Shàn 李善, the *Yìwén lèijù* 藝文類聚, the *Tàipíng yùlǎn* 太平御覽, and the *Shuǐjīng zhù* 水經注. Buddhist and Daoist collectanea are also heavily drawn upon for the Eastern Jin sections.

The Jin dynasty was a period of extraordinary intellectual and cultural productivity alongside political fragmentation. Among the many writers represented are: Lù Jī 陸機 (261–303; essays, *Wén fù* 文賦), Pān Yuè 潘岳 (247–300; memorial prose, elegies), Zuǒ Sī 左思 (ca. 250–305; prefaces), Zhāng Huá 張華 (232–300; bibliographic prose), Jiǎng Tǒng 江統 (?–310; political memorials), and Gé Hóng 葛洪 (283–343; philosophical and Daoist prose). The Eastern Jin sections contain foundational Daoist and Buddhist texts: prose by Wáng Xīzhī 王羲之 (303–361), prefaces by Sūn Chó 孫綽 and Xǔ Xún 許詢, and Buddhist writings by Huì Yuǎn 慧遠 (334–416), including his celebrated *Lúshān zhū dàorén yóu shímén shī xù* 廬山諸道人遊石門詩序. The section thus covers the emergence of landscape writing (shānshuǐ 山水), the philosophical "pure talk" (*qīngtán* 清談) tradition, and early Chinese Buddhist literary culture.

For the structure of the broader anthology, see [[KR4h0176]]. The adjacent dynastic sections are [[KR4h0178]] (Three Kingdoms) and [[KR4h0172]] (Liu Song).

### Abstract

The Jin dynasty (265–420) produced the richest body of prose in the Six Dynasties era, and the *Quán Jìn Wén* is accordingly the largest single dynastic section in Yán Kějūn's anthology, running to 178 *juàn*. Yán drew on the *Jìn shū* 晉書 (compiled by Fáng Xuánlíng 房玄齡 et al., 648) as the primary historical source, supplementing it with Tang literary encyclopedias, the *Wén xuǎn* commentary tradition, Buddhist collectanea, and the *Shuǐjīng zhù* for inscriptions and geographical prose. The breadth of coverage reflects the Jin dynasty's particular place in the history of Chinese writing: the Western Jin consolidated the literary and philosophical advances of the Wei period, while the Eastern Jin presided over the first great diffusion of Buddhism among the educated elite and the development of a distinctive landscape aesthetic.

Key prose genres represented include: imperial edicts and rescripts (*zhào* 詔, *chì* 敕); political memorials (*biǎo* 表, *shū* 書); philosophical essays in the *xuánxué* 玄學 tradition (Wang Bi's intellectual heirs); literary prefaces, including the famous prefaces to *Lán tíng* 蘭亭 gatherings; rhapsodies (*fù* 賦) by Lù Jī and Zuǒ Sī; Buddhist correspondence, treatises, and prefaces by Huì Yuǎn; and Daoist biographical and practical prose by Gé Hóng. The section ends with texts from the final Eastern Jin decades, bridging into the Liu Song material in [[KR4h0172]]. For full compilation history and scholarly significance of the parent anthology, see [[KR4h0176]].
