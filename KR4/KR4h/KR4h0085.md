# Yùshān míngshèng jí 玉山名勝集
 *Famed Scenes of Jade Mountain (Collection)*
by [[顧瑛]]

## About the work

A **8-*juǎn* + 1 *juǎn wàijí*** topographical-and-occasional anthology, edited by **Gù Yīng** ([[顧瑛]], 1310–1369, *zì* Zhòngyíng 仲瑛, *hào* **Yùshān zhǔrén** 玉山主人), the famous late-Yuán Kūnshān 崑山 patron of literature. The work organises into **named-spots** the prose, verse, *jì* and *xù* composed by the dozens of contemporary literary visitors who frequented Gù's **Yùshān cǎotáng** 玉山草堂 estate, with each section headed by the personal name(s) of the person(s) who composed the spot's inscription (*tíé*) and ordered chronologically thereafter. The spots include: **玉山堂** *Yùshāntáng*, **玉山佳處** *Yùshān jiāchù*, **種玉亭** *Zhòngyùtíng*, **小蓬萊** *Xiǎo Pénglái*, **碧梧翠竹堂** *Bìwú cuìzhútáng*, **湖光山色樓** *Húguāng shānsèlóu*, **讀書舍** *Dúshūshè*, **可詩齋** *Kěshīzhāi*, **醉雪齋** *Zuìxuězhāi*, **白雲海** *Báiyúnhǎi*, **來龜軒** *Láiguīxuān*, **雪巢** *Xuěcháo*, **春草池** *Chūncǎochí*, **絳雪亭** *Jiàngxuětíng*, **浣華館** *Huànhuáguǎn*, **柳塘春** *Liǔtángchūn*, **漁莊** *Yúzhuāng*, **書畫舫** *Shūhuàfǎng*, **春暉樓** *Chūnhuīlóu*, **秋華亭** *Qiūhuátíng*, **淡香亭** *Dànxiāngtíng*, **君子亭** *Jūnzǐtíng*, **釣月軒** *Diàoyuèxuān*, **拜石壇** *Bàishítán*, **寒翠所** *Háncuìsuǒ*, **芝雲堂** *Zhīyúntáng*, **金粟影** *Jīnsùyǐng* (etc.).

### Tiyao

Your servants respectfully submit: the *Yùshān míngshèng jí* in 8 *juǎn* + 1 *wàijí juǎn* — the Yuán **Gù Yīng** edited it. Yīng has the *Yùshān púgǎo* 玉山璞稿 ([[KR4d0574]]), already catalogued. The grandness of his estate's ponds and pavilions was unrivaled in the southeast; one age's prevailing talents mostly came to visit and feast. He thus gathered the resulting poetry and prose into this collection, each section keyed to a topographical name (*dìmíng*). [Categories then listed: as above.]

For each spot, the editor places first the inscription's author (*tíé zhī rén*), then Gù Yīng's own commemorative verse, with the prefaces, records, poems and *cí*-pieces appended thereafter. Of the late Yuán's renowned literati, **eight or nine in ten** appear in this volume.

If we consider: the convivial assembly of *Jīngǔ* 金谷 and *Lántíng* 蘭亭 began the tradition of feast-and-poetry; the topographical multitude of *Wǎngchuān* 輞川 and *Yúnxī* 雲谿 began the tradition of poetic topography. Yet for sheer number of guests and abundance of compositions, **nothing surpasses the present collection**. Although it appears in a declining age — with men using literary refuge as an escape — the *wéncǎi fēngliú* (literary brilliance and refined manner) illumines the whole epoch; even centuries later, one seems still to behold them. To preserve this book is one of the eternal *jiāhuà* (fine talks) of the Garden of Letters.

Reverently submitted, fourth month of Qiánlóng 46 (1781). Editor-in-Chief Jǐ Yún (note: SKQS source typesetter's slip — 紀的 in the source for 紀昀), Lù Xīxióng, Sūn Shìyì. General Collator Lù Fèichí.

### Abstract

**Date.** The estate flourished from **Zhìzhèng** 1 (1341), when Gù Yīng (then 32 *suì*) retired from worldly affairs to devote himself to the **Yùshān cǎotáng** project. Most of the recorded gatherings cluster in **Zhìzhèng 8–16 (1348–1356)**; after the **Long-Spear** rebellions of Zhāng Shìchéng disrupted Wúzhōng in 1356 and especially after the **Hóngwǔ** relocation to Línháo 臨濠 in 1370, the gatherings ended. The compilation must have been substantially completed in the late 1340s–early 1350s; the printing window is c. 1350s–1369 (Gù's death).

**The compiler.** [[顧瑛]] was the central late-Yuán *jiāngnán* literary patron; his guest-circle included **Yáng Wéizhēn** 楊維楨 (the most prominent late-Yuán poet), **Ní Zàn** 倪瓚 (painter and recluse), **Zhāng Yǔ** 張雨 (Daoist poet-priest), **Kē Jiǔsī** 柯九思, **Huáng Jìn** 黃溍, [[周砥]] Zhōu Dǐ, **Zhèng Yuányòu** 鄭元祐 ([[KR4h0087]]), and many others. Gù's wealth (he had handed his entire patrimony to his son **Yīngchén** 元臣 at age 40) and his refusal of office made him the late Yuán's exemplary *yǐnjūn* (recluse-host).

**Significance.** (1) The work is the **principal documentary record of the Yùshān cǎotáng literary circle** — the most active literary salon of the late Yuán southeast and the model for subsequent Míng-Qīng *yǐnjūn* literary patronage. (2) Composition-by-topographical-spot is an unusual organizational principle; comparable models are the Táng *Wǎngchuān jí* 輞川集 (of Wáng Wéi 王維 and Péi Dí 裴迪 — the standard precedent the prefaces invoke). (3) The companion volume **[[KR4h0086]]** *Cǎotáng yǎjí* 草堂雅集 (13 *juǎn*) covers the same circle's compositions but organised **by author**; the two anthologies are deliberately complementary. (4) The work is a major documentary witness to **late-Yuán Wúzhōng visual-and-literary culture**: many of the named spots have surviving paintings by Ní Zàn, Wáng Méng 王蒙, etc.

## Other points of interest

The work's organizational principle — *anthology-by-place* — was distinctive enough that the Qīng SKQS editors flagged it explicitly. The model anticipates the Míng-Qīng tradition of garden-anthologies (cf. **[[KR4h0104]]** *Hǎidài huìjí* — a 16th-c. Shāndōng garden-circle anthology with similar structure). The work and its companion *Cǎotáng yǎjí* together constitute the single most concentrated documentary witness to late-Yuán Wúzhōng literary culture — comparable in scope to the Sòng *Liùyī jūshì jí* 六一居士集 for Sòng salon culture or Táng *Yuánshū* 元書 for Táng *jiēshè* (poetry-society) culture.
