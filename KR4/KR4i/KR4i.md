## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR4i 詩文評類 (*Shī wén píng lèi*, "Critiques of Poetry and Prose") is the last of the nine sub-categories of *Jíbù* 集部 (KR4, Belles-Lettres) in the *Sìkù quánshū* 四庫全書 taxonomy. It collects the canon of pre-modern Chinese literary criticism: works that *judge* writing rather than *contain* it. The tradition begins with two Liáng-dynasty 梁 (early 6th c.) monuments — Liú Xié's 劉勰 *Wénxīn diāolóng* 文心雕龍 and Zhōng Róng's 鍾嶸 *Shīpǐn* 詩品 — whose ambition to provide a comprehensive theory of writing was not matched again until the late Míng and Qīng. From the eleventh century onward, criticism became overwhelmingly informal and pointillistic: Ōuyáng Xiū 歐陽修 founded the *shīhuà* 詩話 ("remarks on poetry") in 1071–72, a loose, anecdotal form that Wilkinson (§30.5) describes as "a specialized subgenre of *biji*" 筆記. Some forty Sòng-Yuán-Míng-Qīng *shīhuà*, together with parallel-prose criticism (*sìliù huà* 四六話), epitaph-and-inscription manuals (*jīnshí lì* 金石例 etc.), and examination-essay primers (*zuò yì yào jué* 作義要訣), make up the rest of the division.

The works span roughly thirteen centuries — c. 496 to c. 1778 — and divide cleanly along genre lines: lyric poetry (*shī* 詩), parallel and ornamental prose (*pián* 駢, *sìliù* 四六), examination and stelae prose, and broad surveys of "literature" (*wén* 文) as such. The chronological centre of gravity sits squarely in the Sòng 宋, when the new examination culture and printed-book economy produced the dense network of *shīhuà* whose internal cross-quotation effectively constitutes the canon of Táng and Sòng poetic taste. Cross-references between texts in this division are exceptionally dense: nearly every Southern Sòng *shīhuà* engages with the Jiāngxī 江西 school of Huáng Tíngjiān 黃庭堅 (KR4d), with the *Sūshì* 蘇軾 tradition, and ultimately with the Late-Sòng synthesis of Yán Yǔ's 嚴羽 *Cānglàng shīhuà* 滄浪詩話. Wilkinson's chronological list of literary-critical landmarks (§30.5) overlaps with the KR4i contents but omits the bulk of the Southern Sòng *shīhuà* preserved here.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The two Liáng foundations.** *Wénxīn diāolóng* 文心雕龍 [[KR4i0001]] (Liú Xié 劉勰, c. 496–502) is the first comprehensive Chinese treatise on literature, surveying over thirty genres and laying out a theory of style and composition. *Shīpǐn* 詩品 [[KR4i0003]] (Zhōng Róng 鍾嶸, c. 513–517) grades 122 poets from the Hàn through the Liáng into three classes, traces their stylistic genealogies, and is the earliest comprehensive history of *shī* 詩. *Wénzhāng yuánqǐ* 文章緣起 [[KR4i0004]] (attributed to Rén Fǎng 任昉; received recension Táng–Qīng), traces the origins of every literary genre to a "first" exemplar.

**The Táng anecdote-criticism precursor.** *Běnshì shī* 本事詩 [[KR4i0005]] (Mèng Qǐ 孟棨, 886) records the biographical occasions for famous Táng poems and is the immediate generic ancestor of the Sòng *shīhuà*.

**The Northern Sòng founding of *shīhuà*.** Ōuyáng Xiū's *Liùyī shīhuà* 六一詩話 [[KR4i0006]] (1071–72) is the earliest work to bear the term *shīhuà* in its title; its sequels and imitators form a tight cluster: *Xù shīhuà* 續詩話 [[KR4i0007]] (Sīmǎ Guāng 司馬光), *Zhōngshān shīhuà* 中山詩話 [[KR4i0008]] (Liú Bān 劉攽), *Hòushān shīhuà* 後山詩話 [[KR4i0009]] (attributed to Chén Shīdào 陳師道), *Línhàn yǐnjū shīhuà* 臨漢隱居詩話 [[KR4i0010]] (Wèi Tài 魏泰), *Yōugǔtáng shīhuà* 優古堂詩話 [[KR4i0011]] (Wú Jiān 吳幵), and *Shílín shīhuà* 石林詩話 [[KR4i0018]] (Yè Mèngdé 葉夢得).

**The Southern Sòng compendia.** Three large works gather and organize the proliferating shorter *shīhuà*: *Shīhuà zǒngguī* 詩話總龜 [[KR4i0012]] (Ruǎn Yuè 阮閱, c. 1123), *Tiáoxī yúyǐn cónghuà* 漁隱叢話 [[KR4i0029]] (Hú Zǐ 胡仔, c. 1148–67), and *Shīrén yùxiè* 詩人玉屑 [[KR4i0036]] (Wèi Qìngzhī 魏慶之, c. 1240–44). Together they preserve the bulk of the lost Northern Sòng critical literature.

**Anecdote-criticism on poetry as historical record.** *Táng shī jì shì* 唐詩紀事 [[KR4i0025]] (Jì Yǒugōng 計有功, c. 1145) and its Qīng successor *Sòng shī jìshì* 宋詩紀事 [[KR4i0062]] (Lì È 厲鶚, c. 1740) extend Mèng Qǐ's *běnshì* model into vast period-anthologies built around historical context.

**Late-Sòng theoretical synthesis.** *Cānglàng shīhuà* 滄浪詩話 [[KR4i0035]] (Yán Yǔ 嚴羽, c. 1230–45) periodizes Táng poetry, canonizes the High Táng of Dù Fǔ 杜甫 and Lǐ Bái 李白 as the model, and attacks the Jiāngxī school via the famous Chán-inflected metaphor of "marvellous awakening" (*miào wù* 妙悟). It is the single most influential work of pre-modern Chinese poetic theory and shapes every later orthodoxy down to Wáng Shìzhēn 王士禎.

**Parallel-prose criticism.** *Sìliù huà* 四六話 [[KR4i0016]] (Wáng Zhì 王銍) and *Sìliù tán zhǔ* 四六談麈 [[KR4i0027]] (Xiè Jí 謝伋) are the only sustained Sòng treatises on the parallel-prose (*sìliù*) tradition that dominated official documentary writing.

**Prose-theory manuals.** *Wén zé* 文則 [[KR4i0031]] (Chén Kuí 陳騤, c. 1170s–80s) is the first systematic Chinese rhetoric of prose. *Wén shuō* 文說 [[KR4i0046]] (Chén Yìzēng 陳繹曾) and *Xiūcí jiàn héng* 修辭鑑衡 [[KR4i0047]] (Wáng Gòu 王構) extend the project into the Yuán.

**Inscription and examination manuals.** *Jīnshí lì* 金石例 [[KR4i0048]] (Pān Mǎoxiāo 潘昴霄), *Mùmíng jǔlì* 墓銘舉例 [[KR4i0050]] (Wáng Xíng 王行), and *Jīnshí yàolì* 金石要例 [[KR4i0057]] (Huáng Zōngxī 黃宗羲) form a self-contained cluster of technical works on epitaph and stele composition. *Zuò yì yào jué* 作義要訣 [[KR4i0049]] (Ní Shìyì 倪士毅) is a Yuán manual for the examination essay.

**Míng–Qīng *shīhuà* and the *Wénxīn diāolóng* revival.** *Huáilùtáng shīhuà* 懷麓堂詩話 [[KR4i0051]] (Lǐ Dōngyáng 李東陽), *Tángyīn guǐ qiān* 唐音癸籤 [[KR4i0055]] (Hú Zhènhēng 胡震亨), and Wáng Shìzhēn's *Shīyǒu shīzhuàn lù* 師友詩傳錄 [[KR4i0059]] anchor the post-Yán-Yǔ orthodoxies; Zhào Zhíxìn's 趙執信 paired *Shēngdiào pǔ* 聲調譜 [[KR4i0060]] and *Tánlóng lù* 談龍錄 [[KR4i0061]] are the principal critique of Wáng Shìzhēn's *shényùn* 神韻 aesthetic. Huáng Shūlín's 黃叔琳 *Wénxīn diāolóng jí zhù* 文心雕龍輯註 [[KR4i0002]] (1738) is the foundational Qīng critical edition that opened the modern field of *Lóngxué* 龍學.

**An outlier: the earliest extant *cí* anthology.** *Huājiān jí* 花閒集 [[KR4i0013]] (Zhào Chóngzuò 趙崇祚, 940) is anomalous in this division — it is not literary criticism but the founding *cí* 詞 anthology of the Five Dynasties; the SKQS editors classified it here for the absence of a *cí* category.

## Important persons

- [[劉勰]] — author of *Wénxīn diāolóng*, the inaugural and unsurpassed Chinese theoretical treatise on writing.
- [[鍾嶸]] — author of *Shīpǐn*, the first ranked-history of poets, whose three-class taxonomy shaped a millennium of poetic evaluation.
- [[歐陽修]] — Northern Sòng eminence who founded the *shīhuà* genre with *Liùyī shīhuà*.
- [[司馬光]] — historian (*Zīzhì tōngjiàn*) whose *Xù shīhuà* sealed the genre's legitimacy among the highest tier of Sòng officials.
- [[葉夢得]] — Northern–Southern Sòng official whose *Shílín shīhuà* is the most analytically sophisticated of the early *shīhuà*.
- [[胡仔]] — compiler of *Tiáoxī yúyǐn cónghuà*, the principal preserver of lost Northern Sòng *shīhuà*.
- [[計有功]] — compiler of *Táng shī jì shì*, the indispensable Sòng repository of Táng poetic anecdote.
- [[嚴羽]] — author of *Cānglàng shīhuà*, the doctrinal pivot of pre-modern Chinese poetic theory.
- [[魏慶之]] — compiler of *Shīrén yùxiè*, the most systematic Sòng topical organization of *shīhuà* material.
- [[楊萬里]] — Southern Sòng poet whose *Chéngzhāi shīhuà* is the only major *shīhuà* by a first-rank practising poet.
- [[劉克莊]] — late-Sòng *Hòucūn shīhuà* author, important for the canonization of late Táng and Sòng poets.
- [[陳騤]] — author of *Wén zé*, the first systematic rhetoric of Chinese prose.
- [[黃宗羲]] — Qīng polymath whose *Jīnshí yàolì* completes the epitaph-manual tradition.
- [[李東陽]] — leading Míng official-poet whose *Huáilùtáng shīhuà* set the agenda for early Míng poetic orthodoxy.
- [[楊愼]] — Míng polymath whose *Shīhuà bǔyí* supplements his lost *Shēng'ān shīhuà*.
- [[胡震亨]] — Míng compiler of *Tángyīn guǐqiān*, the most ambitious pre-Qīng study of Táng poetry.
- [[王士禎]] — Qīng arbiter of taste whose *shényùn* doctrine, represented here by *Shīyǒu shīzhuàn lù* and (uncatalogued) *Yúyáng shīhuà*, dominated early-Qīng poetic theory.
- [[趙執信]] — author of *Shēngdiào pǔ* and *Tánlóng lù*, the most articulate Qīng dissenter from *shényùn* orthodoxy.
- [[厲鶚]] — Qīng compiler of *Sòng shī jì shì*, the standard premodern compendium of Sòng-dynasty poetic anecdote.
- [[黃叔琳]] — Qīng editor whose *Wénxīn diāolóng jí zhù* founded the modern philology of Liú Xié's text.

## Topics

- **The birth of the *shīhuà* genre.** Ōuyáng Xiū's 1071–72 experiment with informal, anecdotal poetic commentary became, within a generation, the dominant form of Chinese literary criticism, organizing thirty-plus Sòng texts in this division.
- **Genre theory.** From Liú Xié's thirty-genre taxonomy through Rén Fǎng's *yuánqǐ* etymologies, the question of how to classify writing is the persistent theoretical question of the division.
- **Periodization and the canonization of the High Táng.** Yán Yǔ's argument that the High Táng (Dù Fǔ, Lǐ Bái, Wáng Wéi) is the unsurpassable model creates the dominant orthodoxy of late-Sòng through Qīng poetics, against which every later school (Jiāngxī, *shényùn*, Tóngchéng) defines itself.
- **The Jiāngxī school polemic.** Late Northern and Southern Sòng *shīhuà* are dominated by attacks on or defenses of Huáng Tíngjiān's compositional rules — the central inner-Sòng critical fault-line.
- **Ranking and grading.** From Zhōng Róng's three-class *Shīpǐn* through Sòng *shīhuà* assessments to Qīng *zhèngzōng* 正宗 lists, the impulse to rank poets is one of the division's structural habits.
- **Anecdote vs. theory.** The division contains two distinct modes: comprehensive treatises (Liú Xié, Yán Yǔ, Hú Zhènhēng) and *běnshì* / *jìshì* / *cónghuà* anecdote-anthologies. The relation between the two — whether anecdote *grounds* theory or merely *illustrates* it — is the implicit methodological question of the genre.
- **Parallel-prose criticism.** A small but distinct cluster (*Sìliù huà*, *Sìliù tán zhǔ*) preserves theoretical reflection on the *sìliù* style that dominated Sòng official prose.
- **Technical manuals for stelae and examinations.** The division includes a separate stream of utilitarian compositional manuals (*Jīnshí lì*, *Mùmíng jǔlì*, *Jīnshí yàolì*, *Zuò yì yào jué*) bridging literary criticism and the world of official writing.
- **Tonal prosody and *shényùn*.** Zhào Zhíxìn's *Shēngdiào pǔ* and *Tánlóng lù* together constitute the principal Qīng challenge to Wáng Shìzhēn's *shényùn* aesthetic and a sustained empirical study of regulated verse prosody.
- **Editorial recovery of *Wénxīn diāolóng*.** Huáng Shūlín's 1738 critical edition begins the modern recovery of Liú Xié's text, prefiguring the twentieth-century rise of *Lóngxué*.
