# Jiāng Zǒng Jí 江總集
*Collected Works of Jiang Zong*
by [[江總]] (撰)

## About the work

The *Jiāng Zǒng Jí* 江總集 is a three-*juàn* jíyìběn 輯佚本 (reconstructed anthology) assembled by Zhāng Pǔ 張溥 (1602–1641) as part of his *Hànwèi liùcháo bǎisānjiā jí* 漢魏六朝百三家集. The text is an extra-catalog reconstruction not included in the *Sìkù quánshū* 四庫全書, and accordingly has no *tíyào* 提要. The collection gathers poems and song-texts attributed to Jiāng Zǒng 江總 (519–594 CE) from a wide range of encyclopedic and anthological sources, primarily the *Wényuàn yīnghuá* 文苑英華, the *Yuèfǔ shījí* 樂府詩集, and the *Shījì* 詩紀 (fascicle 104).

The three *juàn* are organized thematically. *Juàn* 1 opens with a rich sequence of yuefu 樂府 ballads — titles include 〈雉子斑〉, 〈隴頭水〉, 〈折楊柳〉, 〈關山月〉, 〈梅花落〉, 〈烏棲曲〉 and other standard yuèfǔ topics — and continues with court-entertainment verse and occasional poems composed at imperial banquets (*yàn*). The later *juàn* extend into regulated verse, miscellaneous poems, and fragments preserved only in later encyclopedias such as the *Tàipíng huányǔ jì* 太平寰宇記 and the *Cǎotáng shījiān* 草堂詩箋. Citations from the *Wénguǎn cílín* 文館詞林, fascicle 158, label some pieces "Suí Jiāng Zǒng" 隋江總, reflecting his survival into the Suí court after the fall of Chén (589 CE).

### Abstract

Jiāng Zǒng 江總 (519–594 CE) was the most prominent poet-official of the late Chén 陳 dynasty. Born in Jìyáng Kǎochéng 濟陽考城 (modern Mínquán county, Hénan), he descended from a distinguished southern aristocratic lineage. He passed the imperial examination in 536 and rose through successive offices under the Liáng and then the Chén dynasties, eventually serving as 尚書令 (Director of the Imperial Secretariat) — the highest civil post — under the last Chén ruler, the notoriously pleasure-loving Chén Hòuzhǔ 陳後主. Jiāng Zǒng was closely identified with the palace-style (*gōngtǐ* 宮體) verse fashionable at the late Chén court, composing ornate, sensuous lyrics that later attracted the censure of moralistic historians. His biography appears in *Chénshū* 陳書 27 and *Nánshǐ* 南史 66.

When the Suí general Hán Qín 韓擒 took Jiànkāng in 589, Jiāng Zǒng was captured and brought to the Suí capital, where he lived out his remaining years at the Suí court. He died in 594. The *Wénguǎn cílín* labels some of his compositions "Suí Jiāng Zǒng," and the *Oxford Handbook of Classical Chinese Literature* notes that Jiāng Zǒng was engaged in active literary exchange with contemporaries including Xú Líng 徐陵 (507–583) and Yáo Chá 姚察 (533–606), once writing a poem of one hundred lines that elicited numerous matching compositions from peers.

Zhāng Pǔ's reconstruction draws chiefly on encyclopedic anthologies (*lèishū* 類書) and the *Shījì* 詩紀 compiled by Féng Wéinà 馮惟訥 (1512–1572). The jíyìběn tradition for this author supplements the *Jiāng Zǒng bié jí* 江總別集 cited in the *Suíshū·Jīngjí zhì* 隋書·經籍志, which records an original collection no longer extant as a whole. The three-*juàn* structure of the present reconstruction is Zhang Pu's editorial organization, not that of any transmitted manuscript.

For the significance of Jiāng Zǒng's palace-style verse within the Six Dynasties literary tradition, see the *Oxford Handbook of Classical Chinese Literature* (Denecke et al. 2017), pp. 226, 401.
