# Xiǎoshuō lèi 小說類 — Fiction and Narrative Prose

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR4k sits within `KR4` 集部 (*jíbù*, Collected Writings) as the supplementary fiction and narrative prose division. Unlike the traditional *Sìkù quánshū* classification which places *xiǎoshuō jiā* 小說家 under *zǐbù* 子部 (KR3l in the Kanripo system), the Kanripo project collects here a far wider compass of Chinese narrative writing — from Hàn historical anecdotes and Táng *bǐjì* 筆記 through *chuánqí* 傳奇 classical tales, Dūnhuáng *biànwén* 變文, Sòng-Yuán *pínghuà* 平話, Yuán *zájù* 雜劇 drama, Míng *huàběn* 話本 collections, the great Míng and Qīng vernacular novels, late-Qīng exposé fiction, and Republican-era popular fiction. All texts carry `source: krp-titles` and are supplementary to the WYG-based *Sìkù* catalog — making KR4k the Kanripo project's primary vehicle for the full sweep of Chinese vernacular and classical narrative.

The chronological span runs from the Eastern Hàn (ca. 189 CE) through the post-Qīng Republic (ca. 1940 CE), with a dense tail of reconstructed pre-Táng historiography (KR4k0341–0419) appended as extra-catalog *jíyìběn* 輯佚本 fragments. The division contains 331 cataloged entries plus approximately 88 extra-catalog entries — a total of some 419 per-text notes — and is among the largest single divisions in the knowledgebase.

The principal English-language reference for the genres represented is Endymion Wilkinson, *Chinese History: A New Manual*. The *bǐjì* and *bǐjì xiǎoshuō* traditions are treated at §53 (Development of the Genre, Databases and Studies, *Bǐjì xiǎoshuō*), with Táng *bǐjì* further covered period by period (Chapters 60–66); vernacular fiction, *huàběn*, and the *Sānyán Èrpāi* 三言二拍 are addressed at §31.1–31.2, including the canonical novels at §31.2.1 and late-Qīng fiction at §31.2.3; popular history, *pínghuà*, *yǎnyì* 演義, and *yěshǐ* 野史 are covered at §54; the Dūnhuáng *biànwén* corpus is referenced at §31.1. For the *chuánqí* classical tale in particular, no dedicated Wilkinson sub-section exists — the form is discussed within the *bǐjì xiǎoshuō* framework (§53.1, §53.3.1) — and the reader is directed to Nienhauser (2010, *Tang Dynasty Tales*) and the *Columbia History of Traditional Chinese Literature* for dedicated Anglophone treatment.

## Important texts and text clusters

The division resolves into roughly a dozen clusters, each representing a major genre or moment in the history of Chinese narrative. The clusters are presented broadly chronologically below.

**Early historical anecdotes and fragments (Hàn–Jìn).** The earliest stratum comprises fragmentary historical texts from the Eastern Hàn through the Six Dynasties, most surviving only in reconstructed form. *Xiàndì Chūnqiū* 獻帝春秋 [[KR4k0001]] preserves scattered late-Hàn court anecdotes; *Jiǔzhōu Chūnqiū* 九州春秋 [[KR4k0003]] by [[司馬彪]] records late-Hàn warlord conflicts; *Shíliùguó Chūnqiū Biéběn* 十六國春秋別本 [[KR4k0004]] by [[崔鴻]] is a Northern Wèi-era survival of the principal Sixteen Kingdoms chronicle. The extra-catalog tail ([[KR4k0341]]–[[KR4k0419]]) extends this stratum with dozens of *jíyìběn* reconstructions: Jìn dynastic histories by [[陸機]] ([[KR4k0348]]), [[習鑿齒]] ([[KR4k0346]]), and [[裴松之]] ([[KR4k0341]]), Sixteen Kingdoms record fragments ([[KR4k0373]]–[[KR4k0388]]), court diaries (*qǐjūzhù* 起居注, [[KR4k0356]]–[[KR4k0361]]), and multiple versions of *Xiàozǐ Zhuàn* 孝子傳 ([[KR4k0392]]–[[KR4k0400]]).

**Táng bǐjì and anecdotal collections.** The Táng *bǐjì* tradition is represented by a cluster of foundational eighth- and ninth-century miscellanies, all essential sources for Táng social, political, and cultural history. *Cháoyě Qiānzǎi* 朝野僉載 [[KR4k0006]] by [[張鷟]] preserves irreverent anecdotes about Wǔ Zétiān's court and is the subject of Rothschild's 2023 annotated translation. *Dàtáng Xīnyǔ* 大唐新語 [[KR4k0008]] by [[劉肅]] models itself on Liú Yìqìng's *Shìshuō Xīnyǔ* and provides one of the most frequently cited *bǐjì* sources for Táng institutional history. *Suí-Táng Jiāhuà* 隋唐嘉話 [[KR4k0012]] by [[劉餗]] and *Táng Guóshǐ Bǔ* 唐國史補 [[KR4k0013]] by [[李肇]] round out the "big three" Táng anecdote collections, the latter notable for its programmatic exclusion of supernatural material. *Mínghuáng Zálù* 明皇雜錄 [[KR4k0010]] by [[鄭處誨]] and *Dàtáng Chuàngyè Qǐjūzhù* 大唐創業起居注 [[KR4k0007]] by [[溫大雅]] — the earliest surviving court diary in Chinese history, an eyewitness account of the Táng founding — complete the core Táng cluster.

**Táng chuánqí classical tales.** The two most studied *chuánqí* 傳奇 narratives in the division are *Huò Xiǎoyù Zhuàn* 霍小玉傳 [[KR4k0009]] by [[蔣防]] — the canonical tale of romantic betrayal and ghostly revenge, translated with full apparatus by Nienhauser (2010) — and *Yóuxiān Kū* 游仙窟 [[KR4k0016]] by [[張文成]] (Zhāng Zhuó again, writing under an alternate name), the earliest surviving prose vernacular experiment in Chinese literature, lost in China but preserved in Japan and recovered in the nineteenth century.

**Dūnhuáng and Sòng vernacular narrative.** The Dūnhuáng *biànwén* corpus is gathered in *Dūnhuáng Biànwénjí Xīnshū* 敦煌變文集新書 [[KR4k0017]], the modern critical edition of the prosimetric transformation texts discovered at Mògāo Caves in 1900 — foundational documents for the rise of Chinese vernacular literature (Mair 1988; Waley 1960). *Dàtáng Sānzàng Qǔjīng Shīhuà* 大唐三藏取經詩話 [[KR4k0021]] is the earliest known narrative treatment of the Xīyóu 西遊 story cycle, bridging the *biànwén* and *pínghuà* traditions. *Wǔdàishǐ Pínghuà* 五代史平話 [[KR4k0015]] is the principal surviving example of the Sòng vernacular historical-narrative genre that directly precedes the Míng *yǎnyì* novel.

**Yuán drama.** *Yuánkān Zájiù Sānshízhǒng* 元刊雜劇三十種 [[KR4k0022]] preserves the earliest printed editions of thirty Yuán *zájù* scripts — the sole extant Yuán-era print witness to the northern dramatic tradition, without the Míng editorial interventions that transformed later recensions. Individual masterworks include *Dòu É Yuān* 竇娥冤 [[KR4k0037]] by [[關漢卿]] and *Xīxiāng Jì* 西廂記 [[KR4k0039]] by [[王實甫]], the latter the most celebrated romantic drama in the Chinese tradition.

**Míng huàběn collections.** The *Sānyán* 三言 of [[馮夢龍]] — *Yùshì Míngyán* 喻世明言 [[KR4k0080]] (1620), *Jǐngshì Tōngyán* 警世通言 [[KR4k0054]] (1624), and *Xǐngshì Héngyán* 醒世恆言 [[KR4k0072]] (1627) — together with the *Èrpāi* 二拍 of [[淩濛初]] — *Chūkè Pāi'àn Jīngqí* 初刻拍案驚奇 [[KR4k0043]] (1628) and *Èrkè Pāi'àn Jīngqí* 二刻拍案驚奇 [[KR4k0048]] (1632) — form the canonical Míng vernacular short-story corpus (Wilkinson §31.1, lines 27658–27670). *Jīngǔ Qíguān* 今古奇觀 [[KR4k0055]], a widely circulated selection from the *Sānyán Èrpāi*, and *Qīngpíngshāntáng Huàběn* 清平山堂話本 [[KR4k0060]], the earliest surviving Míng *huàběn* collection (Jiājìng era), extend the picture of early- to mid-Míng short fiction. *Huānxǐ Yuānjiā* 歡喜冤家 [[KR4k0050]], *Dòupéng Xiánhuà* 豆棚閒話 [[KR4k0101]], and *Shí'èr Lóu* 十二樓 [[KR4k0223]] by [[李漁]] represent further sub-genres of the Míng-Qīng vernacular story.

**Míng novels — the canonical six.** The six canonical Míng-Qīng vernacular novels are well represented. *Sānguó Yǎnyì* 三國演義 [[KR4k0063]] (late 14th c.) by [[羅貫中]] — the foundational *yǎnyì* 演義 historical novel, one of the most widely read works in Chinese literary history. *Shuǐhǔ Quánzhuàn* 水滸全傳 [[KR4k0066]] (mid-16th c.) attributed to [[施耐庵]] and [[羅貫中]] — the outlaw epic that spawned a vast sequel tradition. *Xīyóu Jì* 西遊記 [[KR4k0074]] (1592, Shìdétáng 世德堂 edition) attributed to [[呉承恩]] — the great comic-allegorical pilgrimage novel (Wilkinson §31.2.1). *Jīn Píng Méi* 金瓶梅 [[KR4k0216]] (c. 1590–1617) — the first major Chinese novel of domestic realism, anonymous but attributed to [[蘭陵笑笑生]]. *Fēngshén Yǎnyì* 封神演義 [[KR4k0049]] (Lóngqìng–Wànlì) attributed to [[許仲琳]] — the mythologized Shāng-Zhōu transition narrative. *Hónglóu Mèng* 紅樓夢 [[KR4k0131]] (c. 1740–1792) attributed to [[曹雪芹]] — the summit of the Chinese novel tradition, transmitted in a complex manuscript-and-print recension, with numerous sequels in the division ([[KR4k0133]]–[[KR4k0140]]; Wilkinson §31.2.1).

**Qīng novels and narrative experiments.** *Rúlín Wàishǐ* 儒林外史 [[KR4k0212]] (c. 1740–1750) by [[吳敬梓]] — the great Qīng satirical novel of scholar-official life. *Jìnghuā Yuán* 鏡花緣 [[KR4k0160]] (c. 1819–1827) by [[李汝珍]] — a hundred-chapter fantasy of reversed gender roles and encyclopedic display. *Lǜyě Xiānzōng* 綠野仙蹤 [[KR4k0282]] (1753–1762) by [[李百川]] and *Qílù Dēng* 歧路燈 [[KR4k0197]] (1777–1790) by [[李綠園]] represent mid-Qīng long-form narrative experiments. *Érnǚ Yīngxióng Zhuàn* 兒女英雄傳 [[KR4k0105]] (1842–1851) by [[文康(清小說家)|文康]] — the *cáizǐ jiārén* 才子佳人 / martial heroism hybrid that closes the pre-modern novel tradition.

**Qīng *cáizǐ jiārén* romance.** *Yùjiāo Lí* 玉嬌梨 [[KR4k0280]] (c. 1640–1680) and *Píngshān Lěngyàn* 平山冷燕 [[KR4k0194]] (c. 1660–1680) — the two most famous examples of the *cáizǐ jiārén* 才子佳人 (scholar-beauty) novel, widely translated into European languages in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. *Hǎoqiú Zhuàn* 好逑傳 [[KR4k0126]] (c. 1683–1750) — the "Fortunate Union," the first Chinese novel to appear in English translation (1761, via a German intermediary).

**Late-Qīng exposé fiction.** The late-Qīng social novel is one of the most concentrated clusters in the division. *Guānchǎng Xiànxíng Jì* 官場現形記 [[KR4k0119]] (1901–1905) by [[李寶嘉]] — the most sustained satire of late-Qīng bureaucratic corruption, and the namesake of the "exposé" (*qiǎnzé* 譴責) sub-genre. *Èrshí Nián Mùdǔ Zhī Guài Xiànzhuàng* 二十年目睹之怪現狀 [[KR4k0107]] (1903–1910) by [[吳沃堯]] — a first-person picaresque through the collapsing Qīng social order. *Lǎocán Yóujì* 老殘遊記 [[KR4k0170]] (1903–1906) by [[劉鶚]] — the most artful of the exposé novels, combining social critique with lyrical travel writing (Wilkinson §31.2.3). *Nièhǎi Huā* 孽海花 [[KR4k0188]] (1904–1930) by [[曾樸]] — a roman à clef of late-Qīng intellectual and diplomatic life centered on the courtesan Sài Jīnhuā 賽金花. *Wénmíng Xiǎoshǐ* 文明小史 [[KR4k0241]] (1903–1905) by [[李寶嘉]] and *Fùpù Xiántán* 負曝閑談 [[KR4k0115]] (c. 1895–1905) complete the core exposé cluster.

**Wǔxiá and gōng'àn fiction.** *Qīxiá Wǔyì* 七俠五義 [[KR4k0210]] (1879–1889), a Yuè 樾 recension of the shuōchàng-derived *Sānxiá Wǔyì* 三俠五義 by [[石玉昆]] — the canonical late-Qīng martial-arts and crime-solving novel, spawning *Xiǎo Wǔyì* 小五義 [[KR4k0257]] and a vast sequel tradition. *Pénggōng Àn* 彭公案 [[KR4k0192]] (c. 1892–1899) — the representative *gōng'àn* 公案 casebook novel. *Yōngzhèng Jiànxiá Tú* 雍正劍俠圖 [[KR4k0328]] (1928–1949) extends the tradition into the Republican period.

**Qīng court anecdote and supernatural collections.** *Yuèwēi Cǎotáng Bǐjì* 閱微草堂筆記 [[KR4k0284]] (1789–1798) by [[紀昀]] — the chief compiler of the *Sìkù quánshū*'s own *bǐjì* collection, the most artful Qīng contribution to the *zhìguài* 志怪 tradition. *Hǎishàng Huā Lièzhuàn* 海上花列傳 [[KR4k0124]] (1894) by [[韓邦慶]] — the first major Chinese novel written entirely in Wú 吳 dialect, later translated by Eileen Chang (Wilkinson §31.2.3).

**Republican-era popular fiction.** The Republican stratum includes the palace-intrigue series of [[許嘯天]] (the Gōngwéi Yànshǐ 宮闈豔史 series covering Hàn through Qīng courts, [[KR4k0314]], [[KR4k0321]], [[KR4k0322]], [[KR4k0329]], [[KR4k0310]]) and *Cíxī Hòu Sīshēnghuó Shílù* 慈禧後私生活實錄 [[KR4k0300]] by [[德齡]] (Princess Der Ling), an insider account of the Cíxī court.

**Popular history and sequels.** *Dōngzhōu Lièguó Zhì* 東周列國志 [[KR4k0097]] (c. 1795–1830), *Suí-Táng Yǎnyì* 隋唐演義 [[KR4k0233]] (c. 1675–1695) by [[褚人獲]], and *Shuō Yuè Quánzhuàn* 說岳全傳 [[KR4k0229]] (c. 1740–1744) represent the historical-romance extension of the *yǎnyì* tradition.

## Important persons

- [[張鷟]] — Táng official and writer (658–730), author of the *bǐjì* cornerstone [[KR4k0006]] and the *chuánqí* proto-vernacular tale [[KR4k0016]]; the earliest single-author figure with more than one entry in the division.
- [[蔣防]] — Táng *jìnshì* and *chuánqí* author; creator of *Huò Xiǎoyù Zhuàn* [[KR4k0009]], one of the most frequently translated Táng tales.
- [[劉肅]] — Táng official and author of *Dàtáng Xīnyǔ* [[KR4k0008]], a *Shìshuō*-modeled anecdotal collection essential for Táng institutional history.
- [[李肇]] — Táng *hànlín* academician and *bǐjì* compiler; author of *Táng Guóshǐ Bǔ* [[KR4k0013]], distinguished by its methodological rejection of supernatural material.
- [[王實甫]] — Yuán dramatist; creator of *Xīxiāng Jì* [[KR4k0039]], the canonical Chinese romantic drama.
- [[關漢卿]] — Yuán dramatist; attributed author of *Dòu É Yuān* [[KR4k0037]] and the most celebrated figure of the northern *zájù* stage.
- [[羅貫中]] — Míng novelist active in the late Yuán–early Míng; attributed author of *Sānguó Yǎnyì* [[KR4k0063]] and co-attributed author of *Shuǐhǔ Zhuàn* [[KR4k0066]] — the foundation of the Chinese novel tradition.
- [[施耐庵]] — Yuán-Míng figure; co-attributed author with [[羅貫中]] of *Shuǐhǔ Zhuàn* [[KR4k0066]].
- [[馮夢龍]] — Míng compiler and editor (1574–1646); the single most prolific figure in the division, responsible for the *Sānyán* 三言 *huàběn* collections ([[KR4k0054]], [[KR4k0072]], [[KR4k0080]]) that defined the Míng vernacular short-story canon.
- [[淩濛初]] — Míng writer (1580–1644); compiler of the *Èrpāi* 二拍 collections ([[KR4k0043]], [[KR4k0048]]), the companion corpus to the *Sānyán*.
- [[呉承恩]] — Míng writer (c. 1500–1582); traditionally attributed author of *Xīyóu Jì* [[KR4k0074]].
- [[李漁]] — Míng-Qīng playwright, novelist, and essayist (1611–1680); author of *Shí'èr Lóu* [[KR4k0223]] and multiple other works in the division, a central figure in the late-Míng cult of originality (*qiǎo* 巧).
- [[曹雪芹]] — Qīng author (c. 1715–1763); attributed author of the first eighty chapters of *Hónglóu Mèng* [[KR4k0131]], the most consequential work in the division and in the entire Chinese prose fiction tradition.
- [[吳敬梓]] — Qīng novelist (1701–1754); author of *Rúlín Wàishǐ* [[KR4k0212]], the great Qīng satire of examination culture.
- [[李汝珍]] — Qīng novelist (c. 1763–1830); author of *Jìnghuā Yuán* [[KR4k0160]], the encyclopedic fantasy of reversed social roles.
- [[李寶嘉]] — Qīng novelist and journalist (1867–1906); author of *Guānchǎng Xiànxíng Jì* [[KR4k0119]] and *Wénmíng Xiǎoshǐ* [[KR4k0241]], the defining works of the late-Qīng exposé novel.
- [[吳沃堯]] — Qīng novelist (1866–1910); author of *Èrshí Nián Mùdǔ Zhī Guài Xiànzhuàng* [[KR4k0107]], the picaresque masterwork of late-Qīng fiction.
- [[劉鶚]] — Qīng writer and oracle-bone scholar (1857–1909); author of *Lǎocán Yóujì* [[KR4k0170]].
- [[曾樸]] — Qīng-Republican novelist (1872–1935); author of *Nièhǎi Huā* [[KR4k0188]], revised over three decades across the dynastic divide.
- [[石玉昆]] — Qīng *shuōchàng* 說唱 performer and storyteller (c. 1810–1871); source-author of the *Sānxiá Wǔyì* / *Qīxiá Wǔyì* 七俠五義 [[KR4k0210]] complex and *Xiǎo Wǔyì* [[KR4k0257]].
- [[紀昀]] — Qīng scholar-official and chief *Sìkù* compiler (1724–1805); author of *Yuèwēi Cǎotáng Bǐjì* [[KR4k0284]], the division's principal Qīng *zhìguài* collection.
- [[韓邦慶]] — Qīng novelist (1856–1894); author of *Hǎishàng Huā Lièzhuàn* [[KR4k0124]], the landmark Wú-dialect novel.
- [[許嘯天]] — Republican popular writer (1886–1946); author of the Gōngwéi Yànshǐ 宮闈豔史 palace-intrigue series spanning multiple dynasties.
- Early-medieval historians (tail fragments): [[陸機]] ([[KR4k0348]]), [[習鑿齒]] ([[KR4k0346]]), [[裴松之]] ([[KR4k0341]], [[KR4k0366]]), [[虞預]] ([[KR4k0359]]), [[何法盛]] ([[KR4k0361]]), [[檀道鸞]] ([[KR4k0342]]), [[孔衍]] ([[KR4k0356]]), [[束皙]] ([[KR4k0354]]) — a roll-call of Jìn and LiúSòng historians whose lost works survive only through the *jíyìběn* reconstructions gathered in the tail of the division.

## Topics

- **The four-part division and the place of fiction.** The traditional *Sìkù* taxonomy placed *xiǎoshuō jiā* 小說家 under *zǐbù* 子部 (KR3l), classifying it as a minor philosophical school — a category defined by its exclusion from serious discourse. The Kanripo project's placement of a greatly expanded *xiǎoshuō* corpus under *jíbù* 集部 reverses that judgment: fiction and narrative prose are treated here as *literature* rather than as rejected philosophy. The consequence is a division that assembles, for the first time in the knowledgebase, the full diachronic arc of Chinese narrative — from Hàn fragments through Republican serials — under a single bibliographic roof. The reader should understand that this is an editorial decision, not a reflection of pre-modern Chinese bibliographic practice.

- **Vernacularization and the oral-written interface.** The division is built around a single deep question: how did written Chinese narrative emerge from and differentiate itself from oral performance? The sequence *biànwén* [[KR4k0017]] → *pínghuà* [[KR4k0015]] → *huàběn* [[KR4k0060]], [[KR4k0054]] → *yǎnyì* novel [[KR4k0063]] → full-length authored novel [[KR4k0131]] is the canonical answer, and every term in it is represented in KR4k. The *Sānyán Èrpāi* collections of [[馮夢龍]] and [[淩濛初]] mark the moment when the "storyteller's prompt-book" (*huàběn*) becomes the "simulated-storyteller authored story" (*nǐ huàběn* 擬話本) — the oral frame is retained as a literary convention after the performance context has disappeared.

- **Genre formation, genre boundaries, and the *bǐjì* problem.** The Táng *bǐjì* cluster ([[KR4k0006]], [[KR4k0008]], [[KR4k0010]], [[KR4k0012]], [[KR4k0013]]) exposes a classificatory difficulty that runs through the entire division. Are these texts history, fiction, or something in between? [[李肇]]'s programmatic exclusion of the supernatural from [[KR4k0013]] is an early articulation of the boundary, while [[紀昀]]'s self-conscious return to the *zhìguài* tradition in [[KR4k0284]] eight centuries later demonstrates its persistence as a live question. The *bǐjì* form — unstructured, accretive, claiming factual recording while frequently slipping into the anecdotal and the marvelous — is the most taxonomically resistant genre in the division, and the one that most clearly reveals the inadequacy of the fact/fiction binary for pre-modern Chinese prose.

- **Sequel culture and the commercial novel.** The division documents a pattern that accelerates from the late Míng onward: the successful narrative that generates a chain of sequels, continuations, and rewritings. *Hónglóu Mèng* [[KR4k0131]] alone spawns at least nine sequel texts in the division ([[KR4k0133]]–[[KR4k0140]]); *Shuǐhǔ Zhuàn* produces *Shuǐhǔ Hòuzhuàn* 水滸後傳 and others; *Qīxiá Wǔyì* generates *Xiǎo Wǔyì* [[KR4k0257]] and the sprawling late-Qīng wǔxiá-*gōng'àn* complex. The Republican Gōngwéi Yànshǐ dynasty-by-dynasty series by [[許嘯天]] represents the commodification of this pattern in the print-capitalist publishing market of early twentieth-century Shànghǎi.

- **Translation and cross-cultural reception.** Several texts in the division were among the earliest Chinese literary works to reach European readers in translation. *Hǎoqiú Zhuàn* [[KR4k0126]] appeared in English as early as 1761 (via German); *Yùjiāo Lí* [[KR4k0280]] and *Píngshān Lěngyàn* [[KR4k0194]] were widely translated in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. *Yóuxiān Kū* [[KR4k0016]], lost in China, was preserved in Japanese manuscript transmission and recovered only in the modern period. These cross-cultural trajectories mean that the division is also, in part, a record of the early European encounter with Chinese fiction — and the formative role that *cáizǐ jiārén* romance in particular played in shaping eighteenth-century European images of Chinese literature.

- **The Táng-Sòng transition in prose narrative.** The shift from the literary *chuánqí* tale ([[KR4k0009]], [[KR4k0016]]) to the vernacular *pínghuà* and *huàběn* ([[KR4k0015]], [[KR4k0021]], [[KR4k0060]]) is the prose counterpart of the better-known Táng-Sòng transition in poetry. The division captures the intermediate forms — the *biànwén* prosimetric texts at [[KR4k0017]], the *shīhuà* 詩話 form at [[KR4k0021]] — that make the transition legible as a process rather than a rupture.

- **Jíyìběn reconstruction and the limits of transmission.** The tail of the division ([[KR4k0341]]–[[KR4k0419]]) is a concentrated object lesson in textual loss and Jìn-Qīng scholarly reconstruction. Every text in this stratum is a *jíyìběn* 輯佚本 — a work whose original was lost, probably before the Táng, and whose surviving fragments were reassembled from *lèishū* 類書 quotation, standard-history citation, and Sòng-Yuán encyclopedic excerpts by Qīng and modern scholars. The reader of these entries is never handling a transmitted book but a scholarly artifact — a secondary witness as much to Qīng *kǎozhèng* 考證 methodology as to the early-medieval texts it nominally records.

## Timeline

- **c. 189–280 — *Xiàndì Chūnqiū* 獻帝春秋 [[KR4k0001]]** — fragmentary late-Hàn anecdotal history; the division's chronological floor.
- **c. 280–300 — Jìn dynastic histories (tail fragments): [[陸機]] [[KR4k0348]], [[習鑿齒]] [[KR4k0346]], [[裴松之]] [[KR4k0341]], etc.** — lost Jìn annals, reconstructed from quotation.
- **c. 494–525 — *Shíliùguó Chūnqiū Biéběn* 十六國春秋別本 [[KR4k0004]]** — the received abbreviation of [[崔鴻]]'s Sixteen Kingdoms chronicle.
- **c. 617–618 — *Dàtáng Chuàngyè Qǐjūzhù* 大唐創業起居注 [[KR4k0007]]** — [[溫大雅]]'s eyewitness diary of the Táng founding.
- **c. 713–730 — *Cháoyě Qiānzǎi* 朝野僉載 [[KR4k0006]]** — [[張鷟]]'s miscellany of Wǔ Zétiān-era court and country.
- **c. 760–780 — *Táng Guóshǐ Bǔ* 唐國史補 [[KR4k0013]]** — [[李肇]]'s supplement to the official histories, with programmatic exclusion of the supernatural.
- **c. 800–830 — *Huò Xiǎoyù Zhuàn* 霍小玉傳 [[KR4k0009]]** — [[蔣防]]'s canonical *chuánqí* tale.
- **c. 807 — *Dàtáng Xīnyǔ* 大唐新語 [[KR4k0008]]** — [[劉肅]]'s *Shìshuō*-modeled Táng anecdote collection.
- **c. 850–1000 — Dūnhuáng *biànwén* corpus [[KR4k0017]]** — the prosimetric transformation texts; earliest substantial body of vernacular Chinese narrative.
- **c. 1000–1200 — *Dàtáng Sānzàng Qǔjīng Shīhuà* 大唐三藏取經詩話 [[KR4k0021]]** — earliest known narrative treatment of the Xīyóu cycle.
- **c. 1150–1300 — *Wǔdàishǐ Pínghuà* 五代史平話 [[KR4k0015]]** — the principal surviving Sòng *pínghuà* historical narrative.
- **c. 1271–1368 — *Yuánkān Zájiù Sānshízhǒng* 元刊雜劇三十種 [[KR4k0022]]** — only Yuán-era print witness to the northern *zájù* tradition.
- **c. 1280–1330 — *Xīxiāng Jì* 西廂記 [[KR4k0039]]** — [[王實甫]]'s romantic *zájù* masterwork.
- **c. 1380–1400 — *Sānguó Yǎnyì* 三國演義 [[KR4k0063]]** — [[羅貫中]]'s foundational historical novel.
- **c. 1522–1566 — *Qīngpíngshāntáng Huàběn* 清平山堂話本 [[KR4k0060]]** — earliest surviving Míng *huàběn* collection.
- **c. 1567–1619 — *Fēngshén Yǎnyì* 封神演義 [[KR4k0049]]** — Lóngqìng-Wànlì mythologized Shāng-Zhōu narrative.
- **1592 — *Xīyóu Jì* 西遊記 [[KR4k0074]] (Shìdétáng edition)** — the great comic-allegorical pilgrimage novel, attributed to [[呉承恩]].
- **c. 1590–1617 — *Jīn Píng Méi* 金瓶梅 [[KR4k0216]]** — first major Chinese novel of domestic realism.
- **1620–1627 — [[馮夢龍]]'s *Sānyán* 三言** — *Yùshì Míngyán* [[KR4k0080]], *Jǐngshì Tōngyán* [[KR4k0054]], *Xǐngshì Héngyán* [[KR4k0072]].
- **1628–1632 — [[淩濛初]]'s *Èrpāi* 二拍** — *Chūkè Pāi'àn Jīngqí* [[KR4k0043]], *Èrkè Pāi'àn Jīngqí* [[KR4k0048]].
- **c. 1640–1680 — *Yùjiāo Lí* 玉嬌梨 [[KR4k0280]], *Píngshān Lěngyàn* 平山冷燕 [[KR4k0194]]** — defining *cáizǐ jiārén* romances.
- **c. 1550–1589 — *Shuǐhǔ Quánzhuàn* 水滸全傳 [[KR4k0066]]** — the received 120-chapter outlaw epic text.
- **c. 1658–1665 — *Shí'èr Lóu* 十二樓 [[KR4k0223]]** — [[李漁]]'s vernacular story collection.
- **c. 1683–1750 — *Hǎoqiú Zhuàn* 好逑傳 [[KR4k0126]]** — first Chinese novel in English translation (1761).
- **c. 1740–1750 — *Rúlín Wàishǐ* 儒林外史 [[KR4k0212]]** — [[吳敬梓]]'s satire of the examination culture.
- **c. 1740–1792 — *Hónglóu Mèng* 紅樓夢 [[KR4k0131]]** — attributed to [[曹雪芹]]; the summit of the Chinese novel tradition.
- **c. 1789–1798 — *Yuèwēi Cǎotáng Bǐjì* 閱微草堂筆記 [[KR4k0284]]** — [[紀昀]]'s Qīng *zhìguài* masterwork.
- **c. 1819–1827 — *Jìnghuā Yuán* 鏡花緣 [[KR4k0160]]** — [[李汝珍]]'s hundred-chapter fantasy.
- **c. 1842–1851 — *Érnǚ Yīngxióng Zhuàn* 兒女英雄傳 [[KR4k0105]]** — the *cáizǐ jiārén*-martial hybrid novel.
- **c. 1861–1877 — *Hónglóu Mèng Yǐng* 紅樓夢影 [[KR4k0137]]** — a principal Qīng sequel to *Hónglóu Mèng*.
- **c. 1879–1889 — *Qīxiá Wǔyì* 七俠五義 [[KR4k0210]]** — the canonical late-Qīng wǔxiá-gōng'àn novel.
- **1894 — *Hǎishàng Huā Lièzhuàn* 海上花列傳 [[KR4k0124]]** — [[韓邦慶]]'s Wú-dialect novel.
- **1901–1905 — *Guānchǎng Xiànxíng Jì* 官場現形記 [[KR4k0119]]** — [[李寶嘉]]'s satirical exposé of bureaucratic corruption.
- **1903–1906 — *Lǎocán Yóujì* 老殘遊記 [[KR4k0170]]** — [[劉鶚]]'s lyrical social critique.
- **1903–1910 — *Èrshí Nián Mùdǔ Zhī Guài Xiànzhuàng* 二十年目睹之怪現狀 [[KR4k0107]]** — [[吳沃堯]]'s picaresque masterwork.
- **1904–1930 — *Nièhǎi Huā* 孽海花 [[KR4k0188]]** — [[曾樸]]'s roman à clef, revised across the dynastic divide.
- **c. 1912–1940 — Gōngwéi Yànshǐ 宮闈豔史 series** — [[許嘯天]]'s Republican palace-intrigue fiction (Hàn, Suí, Táng, Yuán, Qīng courts).
- **c. 1928–1949 — *Yōngzhèng Jiànxiá Tú* 雍正劍俠圖 [[KR4k0328]]** — Republican wǔxiá novel; the division's chronological ceiling.
