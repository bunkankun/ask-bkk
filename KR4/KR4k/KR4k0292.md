# Zhù Chūn Yuán Xiǎo Shǐ 駐春園小史
*A Minor History of the Garden Where Spring Stays*
by [[吳航野客]] (撰)

## About the work

*Zhù Chūn Yuán Xiǎo Shǐ* 駐春園小史 is a 24-chapter (*huí* 回) Qīng-dynasty *cáizǐ jiārén* 才子佳人 romance novel. It is attributed to [[吳航野客]] (*Wú Háng Yě Kè*, "Rustic Guest of Wúháng"), a pseudonym suggesting connections to the Fuzhou 福州 area of Fujian (Wúháng 吳航 being an old poetic name for Changle 長樂 county near Fuzhou). The work carries a dated preface (Qiánlóng *rényín* 壬寅 year, ninth month, upper decad — i.e., late autumn 1782) written by a commentator using the pen name Shuǐruò Sǎnrén 水箬散人, who records that the novel had long circulated in manuscript before being prepared for printing at the urging of a friend. The story centers on the scholar Huáng Jiè 黃玠 (styled Yùshǐ 玉史) and the talented young woman Zēng Huànxuě 曾浣雪 (literary name Yún'é 雲娥), whose romance unfolds across the garden pavilion of the title.

## About the work

The narrative sets its action in the Míng dynasty. Zēng Huànxuě's father, Zēng Qīng 曾卿 (styled Yòuqīng 又青), is a Jiājìng-era *jìnshì* of high rank; the heroine herself is a refined literary talent who studies calligraphy and composes poetry from childhood. The romantic plot proceeds through the conventional stages of the genre: first sight across adjacent gardens (the titular *Zhùchūnyuán* adjoins the building where the heroine resides), clandestine correspondence facilitated by the witty maidservant Àiyuè 愛月, misunderstanding and separation, the hero's journey to Jinling, a period of disguise, and eventual reunion. The preface by Shuǐruò Sǎnrén (written at the Kǎixiāng Zhāi 楷香齋 studio) praises the work's elegant prose style, comparing it favorably to *Píng Shān Lěng Yàn* 平山冷燕, *Yùjiāolí* 玉嬌梨, and *Hǎoqiú Zhuàn* 好逑傳. The preface also explicitly contrasts the moral integrity of the female lead (守義 "fidelity to principle") with more licentious romances.

### Abstract

The preface dated to the Qiánlóng *rényín* year (1782) provides a firm *terminus ante quem* for the text's completion and likely dates the first print edition to that same year. The pen name 吳航野客 has not been identified with any named author. The novel belongs to the mature phase of Qīng *cáizǐ jiārén* fiction and shows the influence of several canonical works of the genre named explicitly in the preface. The maidservant figure Àiyuè is praised in the preface as superior even to the famous Hóngniáng 紅娘 of *Xī Xiāng Jì* 西廂記. The embedded commentary — attributed to Shuǐruò Sǎnrén — follows the interlinear comment tradition associated with Jīn Shèngtàn 金聖歎 and his successors. The text is available in a digitized edition through Project Gutenberg (Wuhang Yeke).

The novel circulated widely enough to survive to the present in at least one print edition and has been digitized for modern readers. No critical scholarly edition is known.
