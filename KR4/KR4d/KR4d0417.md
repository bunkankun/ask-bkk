# Fǔshuǐ jí 滏水集
*The Fǔ-shuǐ Collection*
by [[趙秉文]] (撰)

## About the work

The collected works in twenty *juàn* of Zhào Bǐngwén 趙秉文 (CBDB 28846, 1159–1232), *zì* Zhōuchén 周臣, *hào* Xiánxián lǎorén 閑閑老人, native of Cízhōu 磁州 — the foremost literary master of the middle and late Jīn dynasty, his stature equivalent in Jīn north to Yuán Hǎowèn [[KR4d0420]] in the late-Jīn / Yuán transition. Tested for the *jìnshì* in Dàdìng 25 (1185); served the four Jīn reigns Shìzōng, Zhāngzōng, Wèiwáng, Xuānzōng, and Āizōng, rising to *Lǐbù shàngshū jiān shìdú xuéshì tóngxiū guóshǐ zhī jíxiányuàn shì* (Minister of Rites, Concurrent Reader-in-Waiting, Co-Compiler of the National History, Knower of the Academy of Worthies' Affairs). His Jīnshǐ biography (j. 110) — preserved in the SBCK frontmatter as the *Běnzhuàn* of this collection — gives the principal historical record: passed the *jìnshì* by special distinction; demoted in Míngchāng 6 (1195) for a memorial criticizing Chancellor Xū Chíguó 胥持國; re-promoted in Chéngān 5 (1200) by Zhāngzōng's intervention; in Dàān 1 (1209) presented strategy on the Mongol advance, advising a flanking attack from Línhuáng — rejected, with the predicted defeat at Xuāndé following; held the appointment as *Hànlín shìdú xuéshì* through Xuānzōng's reign; in Xìngdìng (1217–) named *Lǐbù shàngshū jiān shìdú xuéshì tóngxiū guóshǐ*; oversaw the 1218 examination at which he selected the controversially formal-irregular Lǐ Xiànnéng 李獻能 as top *jǔrén*; presented the *Wúyì zhíjiě* and *Zhēnguān zhèngyào shēnjiàn* (*Direct Exposition of "Wúyì"*; *Mirror-of-Forgiveness on "Essentials of Zhēnguān Government"*) to Āizōng for moral instruction; in Zhèngdà 9 (1232 first month) composed Āizōng's surrender amnesty proclamation; died Zhèngdà 9, fifth month, *rénchén* day, aged 74. The collection embraces *fù*, *gǔshī*, *lǜshī*, *juéjù*, *zátǐ*, *biǎo* / *jiǎn* / *qǐ*, *bēi*, *jì*, *lùn*, *yǐn*, *sòng*, *zhēn*, *zàn*, *jìwén*, *shūqǐ*, *tíbá* — the most comprehensive surviving genre-range of any Jīn-period literary collection. The preface by **Yáng Yúnyì** 楊雲翼 (1170–1228) of Yuánguāng 2 (1223), praising Zhào as the *zhǔméng* (presiding master) of Jīn letters and the contemporary cultural arbiter, is preserved here.

### Tiyao

*No tíyào in the Kanripo source* (the source files reproduce the SBCK base, which preserves the *Jīnshǐ* biography of Zhào Bǐngwén and Yáng Yúnyì's 1223 preface, but not the Qiánlóng *Sìkù* tíyào). The *Sìkù quánshū zǒngmù tíyào* records the *Fǔshuǐ jí* in twenty *juàn* under "Jīn *biéjí lèi*," classing Zhào as one of the three principal Jīn-dynasty *biéjí* authors (with Wáng Ruòxū [[KR4d0418]] and Wáng Jì [[KR4d0416]]) whose collections survive intact. The editors emphasize the breadth of Zhào's literary range, his role as *zhǔméng* of late-Jīn letters across four reigns, and the historiographical importance of his collection as a primary source for the literary culture of the Jīn imperial court and Hànlín bureau under Shìzōng through Āizōng.

### Abstract

Zhào Bǐngwén (CBDB 28846, 1159–1232) is the senior figure of late-Jīn literary culture, the academic-literary lineage-master of Yuán Hǎowèn (whose 1223 *bá* to this collection is preserved in some recensions), and the principal cultural architect of the Xuānzōng / Āizōng courts (1213–1234) — the final two decades of the Jīn dynasty. The collection — in twenty *juàn* — preserves his entire major prose corpus: court documents (*biǎo*, *jiǎn*, *qǐ*, *jìwén*, *zhì*, *bēi*, *shìyì*, *āicè*, *gào*); literary criticism and historical *lùn* (Western Hàn, Eastern Hàn, Wèi-Jìn *zhèngmíng*, Shǔ-Hàn *zhèngmíng*, Táng, *Zhīrén*, *Qiāndū*, *Hóushǒu*, *Zhí*); foundation-text introductions (*yǐn*) to the *Zhúxī xiānshēng wénjí*, the *Fǎyán wēizhǐ*, the *Dàoxué fǎyuán*, his own *Jiān Tàixuán zàn yǐn* and *Zhōngshuō lèijiě yǐn*; the eight surviving *fù* of *juàn* 2 (the *Dàchūn fù*, *Qīxiá fù*, *Cóngtái fù*, *Jiě cháoxǐng fù*, *Hǎiqīng fù*, *Fǎn Xiǎoshān fù*, *Lángshān fù*, *Huáshān gǎngǔ fù*, *Lìpéng fù*, *Yóu Xuánquán fù*, *Wújìnzàng fù*, *Zhuōxuān fù*, *Yóu Xīyuán fù*, *Xīnjìng tiāndì zhī jiàn fù*); voluminous landscape, occasional, and presentation verse; nine pieces of religious paratext (*qīngcí*, *jìwén*, *zhēn*); and twenty-three pieces of title-and-postface scholarship on Sū Shì, Mǐ Fú, Wén Tóng, Sū Zhé, Sū Xún, Huáng Tíngjiān, Yáng Níngshì, Wáng Tíngjūn — a major archive of late-Jīn calligraphic-aesthetic discourse. Zhào's other principal surviving works are his *Dàodé zhēnjīng jíjiě* [[KR5c0080]] (Daoist scholarship), the *Tàixuán jiānzàn*, *Zhōngshuō lèijiě*, and *Nánhuá lüèshì*. CBDB 28846 firmly establishes 1159–1232; the *Jīnshǐ* biography fully confirms. Wilkinson treats Zhào extensively in the Jīn-dynasty literary culture (§29).

## Other points of interest

The *Jīnshǐ* biography preserved in the frontmatter notes that Zhào "in late life rather sullied [his name] with Chán [Buddhist] sayings," registering the Confucian historiographical reservation about Zhào's late-life involvement in Chán Buddhist and Quánzhēn Daoist circles — the same syncretism that produced his *Dàodé zhēnjīng jíjiě*. The reservation is itself historiographically interesting as a marker of the Yuán-period (Tuōtuō *Jīnshǐ*) editorial stance on the late-Jīn Three-Teachings synthesis. The 1218 examination affair — in which Zhào's selection of Lǐ Xiànnéng as top *jǔrén*, despite Lǐ's irregular *fù* form, provoked a scandal — is one of the few late-Jīn literary controversies preserved in detail.
