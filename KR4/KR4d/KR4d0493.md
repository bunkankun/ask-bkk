# Dàoyuán xuégǔ lù 道園學古錄
*The Dào-yuán (Dào-Garden) Studying-the-Ancients Records*
by [[虞集]] (撰)

## About the work

The 50-*juàn* monumental collection of **Yú Jí** 虞集 (CBDB 10801, 1272–1348), *zì* **Bóshēng** 伯生**, *hào* **Dàoyuán** 道園 ("Dào-Garden"). Senior Yuán Hànlín scholar-statesman, posthumous *shì* **Wénjìng** 文靖. Native of **Shǔjùn** 蜀郡 (Sìchuān). The foundational mid-Yuán Hànlín literary patriarch; with **Yáng Zài** 楊載 [[KR4d0495]], **Fàn Pèng** 范梈 [[KR4d0496]], and **Jiē Xīsī** 揭傒斯 [[KR4d0497]] forming the canonical **mid-Yuán four-master quartet** (*Yú-Yáng-Fàn-Jiē* 虞楊范揭). Yú Jí's collection was edited by his disciple **Lǐ Běn** 李本 from over 10,000 surviving pieces.

The SBCK base preserves the standard transmitted text via several Yuán-era cuts: (1) **Liú Jī** 劉基 (Liú Bówēn, the early-Míng founding-figure) prepared a large-character cut; (2) the *Wūkèzhāng* 烏克章 *Jiànníng* cut. Later editors added a *Dàoyuán lèigǎo* compilation; the relations between *Lèigǎo* and *Xuégǔ lù* show partial overlap.

**Opening preface** (the *Yōng-Yú gōng wén xù*, by an anonymous mid-Yuán Hànlín colleague — likely a senior contemporary): Yú Jí was repeatedly identified by Hànlín colleagues as one with whom to entrust the future of *sī wén* (this *wén* tradition). At the **Zhìzhì** (1321–1323) and **Tiānlì** (1328–1330) eras, "[the] Yōng Yúgōng [Yú Jí] held official office [in the Hànlín] — *wén* also was [in his] luxuriance for a time. The state-temple and imperial-court's *diǎncè* (canonical-investiture documents), the *gōngqīng dàfū* (senior officials')'s *bēibǎn* (stele-and-tablet inscriptions) — all came-forth from the gentleman's hand. *Cuìrán zì chéng yī jiā zhī yán* (purely-and-completely self-completing one-house's words). The mountain-forest's men, *fěngyè zhī shì* (cottage-living scholars), obtaining his presentation-words — [they take it as if] obtaining *gōngbì* (folded-jade emblems). The gentleman's encountering-the-text — *suí shì chóuzuò* (according-to-affairs reciprocating-composing) — *zàocì tiānchéng* (sudden-as-coming, heaven-completed). Originally without *yīháo shàngrén zhī xīn* (one-iota of striving-to-surpass-others mind); also without *jūjū rán bùqū gǔrén zhī yì* (constrained-walking-of-the-ancients meaning). *Jīyòng zìshú, jìngqù zìshēng, zuǒyòu féngyuán* (mechanism-application self-mature, atmosphere-interest self-arising, left-right encountering-source) — each recognizing its appropriate-office."

### Abstract

The literary monument of **Yú Jí** (1272–1348), the singular mid-Yuán Hànlín literary patriarch and the principal *Yùtáng* (Hànlín) state-paper composer of the **Zhìzhì** and **Tiānlì** eras (1321–1330). Yú's career as state-paper composer made him the principal author of the Yuán imperial *diǎncè* (canonical documents) and of the *bēibǎn* (steles-and-tablets) for the senior Mongol-aristocratic and Hàn-Confucian elite. Sū Tiānjué's *Yuán wénlèi* canonized Yú alongside [[馬祖常]], [[袁桷]], and Wáng Gòu 王構 as the **Dàdé / Yányòu** mid-Yuán literary-imperial quartet — with Yú Jí as the senior member.

**Family origins.** Maternal grandfather **Yáng Wénzhòng** 楊文仲, Sòng *Héngyáng* magistrate; the maternal-uncle's-great-grandson **Yáng Chūn** 楊椿 of Méishān composed the preface for the *Dàoyuán yígǎo* [[KR4d0494]] in Zhìzhèng *jǐhài* (1359).

**Collection structure** (per the SBCK base):
- 22 *fù* in *juàn* 1 — antiquarian and architectural rhapsodies including the *Dǎoyóu fù*, *Yúnlín fù*, *Xuányúnshí fù*, *Yùguīzhàng fù*, *Tóngbǎiguàn fù* (the Daoist-establishment rhapsodies), *Lèshuǐtú fù*, *Yújiànzhāi fù*, *Xīzhāi fù*, *Níngyúnshí fù*, *Jiǔhuátái fù*, *Mòzhú fù*, *Sùxuān fù*, *Dōngwō fù*, *Róngxuān fù*, *Fùān fù*, *Nǐ Shàntíng fù*, *Yǐnjūtú fù* — the major Yuán antiquarian / architectural-poetic corpus
- *sāo / cí* in *juàn* 2 — including the *Mǐnshì*, *Guǎngzhāo*, *Qīguān*, *Yuèlùtú cí*, *Yōurángé cí*, *Jīntí cí*, *Shítián shānfáng cí*, *Xuějiāngtú cí*, *Chuílúntíng cí*, *Jùfēng cí*, *Yúnzhōng cí*
- (subsequent *juàn*): substantial poetic and prose corpus

**Imperial state-paper function.** Per the opening preface: "*Cháotíng zhī diǎncè, gōngqīng dàfū zhī bēibǎn — xián chū gōng shǒu*" (the court's canonical-investiture documents, the senior officials' stele-and-tablet inscriptions — all came out of the gentleman's hand). This places Yú Jí as the senior composer of Yuán imperial state-papers and aristocratic memorial inscriptions in the Zhì-zhì-and-Tiān-lì period.

**Position in Yuán literary history.** Yú Jí is the principal Yuán literary-imperial figure of the mid-Yuán cultural climax. The Sìkù editors elsewhere characterize him as the *Wénzhèng zhī jiānzhě* (cultivator of *wénzhèng* — refined-correct) — i.e. the canonical Yuán literary patriarch. CBDB 10801 (1272–1348). *Yuánshǐ* j. 181 (Yú Jí biography). The Wilkinson manual treats Yú Jí extensively in §28, §35, §42.

Composition window: from Yú Jí's earliest preserved compositions (after his 1286 recruitment to the Yuán court by Chéng Jùfū [[程鉅夫]] [[KR4d0466]], when Yú was 14) through his death in 1348.

## Other points of interest

The mid-Yuán **four-master quartet** (Yú Jí, Yáng Zài, Fàn Pèng, Jiē Xīsī) is the canonical formulation of mid-Yuán literary culture in subsequent Yuán-Míng literary historiography. Yú Jí as the senior member established the mid-Yuán Hàn-lín-imperial-Confucian literary standard. The 50-*juàn* *Dàoyuán xuégǔ lù* + 6-*juàn* *Dàoyuán yígǎo* [[KR4d0494]] together preserve Yú's complete surviving corpus.
