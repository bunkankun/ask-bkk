# Báiyún jí 白雲集
*The Bái-yún (White-Cloud) Collection*
by [[許謙]] (撰), [[李伸]] (編)

## About the work

The literary collection of **Xǔ Qiān** 許謙 (1270–1337) — *Báiyún xiānshēng* 白雲先生, posthumous *shì* **Wényì** 文懿 — the fourth and last of the **Běi Shān sì xiānshēng** 北山四先生 (Hé Jī 何基 → Wáng Bǎi 王柏 → Jīn Lǚxiáng 金履祥 → Xǔ Qiān): the Jīnhuá / Wùzhōu 金華 / 婺州 Yuán transmission of Sòng Zhū Xué orthodoxy. The Sìkù base derives from the SBCK reproduction of the standard transmitted Wú-region recension. The collection was compiled and printed by the Míng-period scholar-official **Chén Xiāng** 陳相 of Jīnhuá in Chénghuà 2 (1466) — Chén's preface (preserved in the volume) records that he obtained a manuscript in 4 *juàn* from the *Fāngbó* (Provincial-Governor) **Zhānggōng** (of Jiāngpǔ 江浦) while serving on imperial commission to East-Guǎng. Chén notes that the *Wù*-region (Jīnhuá) Sòng-Yuán Neo-Confucian lineage — Lǚ Zǔqiān (the Sòng *Dōnglái Lǚ chénggōng*) → Wáng Wénxiàn → Hé Wéndìng → the Yuán **Jīn Wénān** 金文安 (Jīn Lǚxiáng) → **Báiyún Xǔ Wényì** — derives canonically from the Zhū-Xī-via-Huáng Miánzhāi 黃勉齋 transmission. The collection's prefaces serially link Xǔ Qiān into the trans-dynastic Confucian *dàotǒng* canon.

### Prefaces

The SBCK base preserves several Míng-period prefaces:

1. **Chén Xiāng's** preface (Chénghuà 2 = 1466), recording the manuscript discovery and the local Jīnhuá Neo-Confucian lineage.
2. A second preface (also Míng-period) tracing the *Dào* lineage from Yáo-Shùn-Yǔ-Tāng → Confucius-Mencius → through the Hàn-Táng Confucians (Jiǎ Yì, Dǒng Zhòngshū, Hán Yù) → to the Sòng patriarchs Zhōu / Chéng / Zhāng / Zhū → through Zhēn Xīshān (Zhēn Déxiù), Cài Jiǔfēng, Hú Wéndìng → to the Yuán worthies **Xǔ Lǔzhāi** [[許衡]], **Liú Jìngxiū** [[劉因]], **Wú Cǎolú** [[吳澄]] — and finally to Xǔ Qiān as receiving the *zhèngchuán* (direct-orthodox transmission) of Zhū Xī.

The collection itself contains *shī*, *cí*, *fù*, prose, *xù*, *jì*, *bēimíng*, *zàn*, *míng* in 4 *juàn*. The principal contents are exhortations to study, *jì* (records) of Confucian academies and shrines, philosophical letters, and *bēizhì* (epitaphs) for fellow Jīn-huá-school members.

### Abstract

The collected works of Xǔ Qiān (1270–1337), the fourth-generation patriarch of the **Běi Shān** 北山 (North-Mountain) Yuán Zhū Xué transmission centered at Jīnhuá in Wùzhōu (modern Zhèjiāng). For full biographical detail see the person note. Xǔ refused all imperial office and lived as the most famous Yuán-period Confucian teacher of his generation — students reportedly came from across the Yuán empire. *Yuánshǐ Rúlín zhuàn* gives the principal biographical record; **Huáng Jìn** [[黃溍]] composed the epitaph (preserved in *Jīnhuá Huáng xiānshēng wénjí*).

The *Báiyún jí* itself is principally a record of Xǔ's pedagogical and *dàoxué*-lineage activities — academy-establishment records, exhortations to students, ritual-and-shrine inscriptions — rather than independent literary composition. The *Sìkù*-canonized **Xǔ Qiān** corpus consists of four works: his *Shàngshū* commentary [[KR1b0028]] *Dúshū cóng shuō* 讀書叢說; his *Shī jí zhuàn míng wù chāo* 詩集傳名物鈔; his *Sì shū cóng shuō* 四書叢說; and the present *Báiyún jí* — making him one of the more comprehensively-transmitted Yuán Neo-Confucians.

The Míng compiler **Lǐ Shēn** 李伸 (an early/mid-Míng scholar) is named in the catalog as editor, but the editorial work is mostly attributable to Chén Xiāng's Chénghuà 1466 print, with Lǐ Shēn's earlier compilation underlying.

Composition window: from Xǔ Qiān's adult literary activity (after his 1290 entry into Jīn Lǚxiáng's school, when Xǔ was 21) through his death in 1337.

## Other points of interest

The Chén Xiāng 1466 print preface — preserving the *Wù*-region Zhū Xī *dàotǒng* genealogy — is an important Míng-period statement of the Yuán-Jīnhuá school's self-understanding of its place in the Sòng-Míng *Lǐxué* succession. The two prefaces together place Xǔ Qiān into the canonical *dàotǒng* sequence ending with the Míng founder-scholars **Sòng Lián** 宋濂 (景濂) and **Wáng Zǐchōng** 王子充 (王禕) — both Jīnhuá natives — making Xǔ the proximate Yuán-source of Míng-founding Confucian institutionality.
