# Shítián wénjí 石田文集
*The Shí-tián (Stone-Field) Collection*
by [[馬祖常]] (撰)

## About the work

The 15-*juàn* collection (5 *juàn* of poetry and *fù*, 10 *juàn* of prose) of **Mǎ Zǔcháng** 馬祖常 (CBDB 24523, 1279–1338), *zì* **Bóyōng** 伯庸**, *hào* (collection-name) **Shítián** 石田 (from his **Shítián shānfáng** 石田山房 residence). Posthumous *shì* **Wénzhēn** 文貞. *Yuánshǐ* j. 143 gives the substantial biography.

**Ethnic-political background.** Mǎ Zǔcháng was a **Yōnggǔ** 雍古 (Öngüt) tribesman by descent — i.e. a member of the Önggüt (a Nestorian-Christian Inner-Mongolian tribe; sometimes called *White Tatars*). The family settled at **Jìngzhōu** 靖州 (Tiānshān region, modern Inner Mongolia). The high-grandfather **Xīlǐjìsū** 錫里濟蘇 (Sìkù-era corrected transliteration; originally written *Xīlǐjísī* 錫里吉思) held end-Jīn office as **Fèngxiáng bīngmǎ pànguān**. Descendants — following the Mongol-Yuán practice of using the ancestral office's *first character* as surname — adopted the surname **Mǎ** (from *Bīngmǎ pànguān*). The great-grandfather **Yǎhā** 雅哈 (Sìkù-corrected; originally *Yuèhé* 月合) accompanied Khubilai south and settled at Biàn (Kāifēng); later relocated to **Guāngzhōu** 光州.

**Career.** In Yányòu 1 (1314), at the restoration of the imperial examination system, Mǎ Zǔcháng came first (*dìyī*) in both the *xiānggòng* (provincial) and *huìshì* (metropolitan) examinations, and second (*dìèr*) at the imperial *tíngshì* (court) examination — the singular **non-Hàn examinee** to perform at such a high level in the restored Yányòu examinations. Awarded *Yìngfèng Hànlín wénzì*; promoted to *Yánchá yùshǐ* (Salt-Inspection Censor); successfully impeached the Grand Councillor **Tèméndéěr** 特們德爾 (Sìkù-corrected; originally *Tiěběndiéér* 鐵本迭兒). After Tèméndéěr returned to power and demoted Mǎ to *Kāipíngxiàn yǐn* (county-magistrate), Mǎ withdrew on grounds of avoiding troubles. After Tèméndéěr's death, Mǎ was appointed *Hànlín dàizhì*; rose to *Lǐbù shàngshū*; soon *Cānyì Zhōngshūshěng shì*; in Yuántǒng 1 (1333) appointed *Yùshǐ zhōngchéng*; transferred to *Shūmì fùshǐ*; requested return; died Zhìzhèng 4 (1344).

The Sìkù editors evaluate the collection: "Prose [is] *jīngshàn hónglì* (refined-and-rich, vast-and-magnificent) — entirely washing-out the *róumàn bēirǒng zhī xí* (soft-and-weak, low-and-redundant practice). Poetry's talent-and-strength [is] *fùjiàn* (rich-and-firm) — like the *Dōumén zhuàngyóu* (Capital-Gate Vigorous-Travel) various pieces — long *piān* great compositions — *jiǒng bóbēn téng* (vast-and-overlapping, expansive-and-leaping) — possessing not-receiving *jīlè* (bridle-restraint) *qì*."

**Position in Yuán literary history.** Sū Tiānjué's compilation of the *Yuán wénlèi* records 20 of Mǎ's poems and 20 of his prose pieces — more than any other author. Sū secured imperial approval to cut the *Shítián jí* and personally composed the preface, calling Mǎ as one who "*jiē wǔ Suí-Táng, shàng zhuī Hàn-Wèi*" (takes-up the lineage of Suí-Táng, ascending to track Hàn-Wèi); his "*wénzhāng wèi zhī yī biàn*" (literary art was, by him, one-transformation). With **Yuán Juè** [[袁桷]] (of Kuàijī), **Yú Jí** [[虞集]] (of Shǔjùn), and **Wáng Gòu** 王搆 (of Dōngpíng) in *gēngdié chànghé* (alternating-exchange-composition) like *jīnshí xiāng xuān* (metals-and-stones mutually-sounding) — yet "the *wén* increased in marvel". The Dàdé and Yányòu eras' literary culmination — and Mǎ Zǔcháng was among the few *jùbǎ* (giant-thumbs).

### Abstract

The collected works of **Mǎ Zǔcháng** (CBDB 24523, 1279–1338), one of the principal Yuán-era *non-Hàn* literary figures and an institutional foundational figure of the post-1314 Yuán imperial-examination Confucian establishment. As the **Yōnggǔ** (Önggüt / Nestorian-Mongolian) descendant who placed first in both provincial and metropolitan examinations and second at the court examination in the restored Yányòu *jìnshì* system (1314), Mǎ established the principle of literary-meritocratic non-Hàn participation in Yuán imperial Confucian governance.

The Sìkù editors place Mǎ among the *jùbǎ* (giant-thumbs) of the Dàdé / Yányòu cultural climax, alongside **Yuán Juè** of Kuàijī (Níngbō), **Yú Jí** of Shǔjùn (Sìchuān), and **Wáng Gòu** of Dōngpíng (Shāndōng) — the geographic-cultural fourfold core of mid-Yuán imperial-literary culture. Sū Tiānjué's *Yuán wénlèi* canonized Mǎ with the largest single corpus of any author (20 poems + 20 prose pieces).

The collection's principal contents:
- **5 *juàn* of *fù* and *shī*** — including the *Dōumén zhuàngyóu* (Capital-Gate Vigorous-Travel) long-poem sequence, characterized as *jiǒng bóbēn téng* (vast-and-overlapping, expansive-and-leaping) — the foundational mid-Yuán Hànlín pictorial-poetic work
- **10 *juàn* of prose** — Hànlín state-papers, *bēimíng*, *mùzhì* — characterized as *jīngshàn hónglì* (refined-rich, vast-magnificent)

**Political career.** As Censor, successfully impeached the Grand Councillor **Tèméndéěr** 特們德爾 (= *Temüder*) — the senior Mongol prime-minister whose corruption marred the Khubilai-and-successor regime. Mǎ's career exemplified censor-officials' constitutional role against imperial corruption. Subsequently rose through *Lǐbù shàngshū*, *Cānyì Zhōngshūshěng shì*, *Yùshǐ zhōngchéng*, *Shūmì fùshǐ* — the senior Yuán imperial offices for a non-Hàn Confucian.

Composition window: from Mǎ's earliest preserved Hànlín pieces (after 1314) through 1338.

## Other points of interest

The Yányòu 1 (1314) imperial-examination restoration — and Mǎ Zǔcháng's place as principal-ranked non-Hàn examinee — is one of the most-consequential moments in Yuán-imperial-Confucian institutional history. The Yuán court's deliberate decision to admit non-Hàn (Önggüt, Khangli, Tangut, etc.) candidates into the *jìnshì* system — and the success of figures like Mǎ Zǔcháng — established a permanent precedent for multi-ethnic Confucian-imperial participation that influenced later dynasties.
