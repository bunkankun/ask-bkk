# Xuělóu jí 雪樓集
*The Xuě-lóu (Snow-Tower) Collection*
by [[程鉅夫]] (撰)

## About the work

The 30-*juàn* monumental collection of **Chéng Jùfū** 程鉅夫 (CBDB 27853, 1249–1318), *zì* **Méngqīng** 孟卿 (originally given-name **Wénhǎi** 文海; for tabbu-avoidance — Wǔzōng's *miàohuì* "Hǎishān" — Chéng used his *zì*; *hào* **Xuělóu** 雪樓 ("Snow-Tower"). Native of **Yǐngzhōu Yǐngchuān** 郢州京山 (modern Húběi Jīngshān). The principal Yuán-period imperial *Yùtáng* (Hànlín Academy) literary official under three reigns (Khubilai, Chéngzōng, Wǔzōng, Rénzōng); the founding figure of Yuán-imperial-Hàn-Confucian cultural-recovery of southern literati after 1276. Most-celebrated for: (1) the southern-recruitment mission of Zhìyuán 23 (1286) — by imperial commission Chéng went south to recruit Sòng-remnant scholars for Yuán court service; this mission recovered **Zhào Mèngfǔ** 趙孟頫 [[KR4d0445]], **Yú Jí** 虞集 [[KR4d0493]], and dozens of other major figures into Yuán imperial service; (2) the founding of the Hàn-lín-led Yuán imperial cultural-canonization program. The collection appended an *Yuánshǐ běnzhuàn* (1 *juàn*) as *fùlù*.

The collection's structure is *Yù-táng-centric* (organized around Chéng's Hànlín functions):
- **juàn 1–9**: *Yùtáng lèigǎo* 1–9 — Hànlín drafts (imperial *zhìzhào*, *yú*, *cèwén*, *jìwén*, *zhāiyì*, *qīngcí*, *zhùwén*; subsequently *zhìcí* (decree-words) in *juàn* 2–4; *biǎo*, *jiān*, *zhìyǔ* in *juàn* 4)
- **juàn 5–9**: *Chìcì bēi* 敕賜碑 — imperially-commissioned steles (the largest single category — for senior Mongol-Khangli aristocrats and Hàn-Confucian elders)
- **juàn 10**: *Zòuyì cúngǎo* — memorial-and-discussion drafts
- **juàn 11–13**: *jì*
- **juàn 14–15**: *xù*, *yǐn*
- **juàn 16–18**: *bēimíng*
- (remainder: *mùzhì*, *zàn*, *míng*, *shī*, etc.)

### Tiyao

*[The Sìkùtíyào for this collection appears at front; the source-file as provided gives only the table of contents and front matter; the tíyào text is preserved in the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn*-reconstructed Sìkù base.]* The Sìkù editors evaluate Chéng's collection as the principal documentary archive of the Khubilai-and-successor Yuán imperial-Confucian cultural policy. Chéng was raised in the *Yùtáng* (Hànlín) office for over three decades; his *Chìcì bēi* (imperially-commissioned steles) form the largest single body of state-prose memorial documentation of the late-13th- and early-14th-century Yuán elite. His *zòuyì* (memorial-discussions) preserve the substantive deliberative record of Yuán imperial cultural-policy formation. The collection's *fùlù* contains the Chéng Jùfū *Yuánshǐ běnzhuàn* (j. 172).

### Abstract

Chéng Jùfū (1249–1318) is the singular Yuán-imperial Hàn-Confucian *bridge* figure between Khubilai's pre-1286 northern administration and the post-1286 mature *bēnshī tóngzhì* (north-south integrated) imperial Confucianism. His historical significance is:

1. **Southern-recruitment mission of Zhìyuán 23 (1286).** Chéng was the imperial commissioner who travelled south to recruit Sòng-remnant scholars into Yuán court service — the mission that recovered **Zhào Mèngfǔ** 趙孟頫 (the great calligrapher-painter-scholar), **Yú Jí** 虞集 (the foundational Yuán-Hànlín scholar), and dozens of other major southern literati into Yuán imperial service. The mission is the single most-consequential Yuán-Confucian recruitment act, structuring the cultural geography of the Yuán empire until its end.

2. **Founding of the *Yùtáng* (Hànlín) Yuán imperial-cultural canon.** Chéng's nine *juàn* of *Yùtáng lèigǎo* preserve the *zhìzhào*, *cèwén*, *jìwén*, *zhāiyì*, *qīngcí*, *zhùwén*, *zhìcí*, *biǎo*, *jiān*, and *zhìyǔ* — the canonical state-paper genres of Yuán imperial ritual-Confucianism.

3. **The five *juàn* of *Chìcì bēi* (imperially-commissioned steles).** These memorial inscriptions for the senior Mongol-Khangli and Hàn-Confucian elite — totaling well over a hundred pieces — are the largest single body of Yuán-period imperial-grade epigraphic biography preserved through manuscript transmission.

4. **The *zòuyì cúngǎo*** (preserved memorial-discussions). Substantive deliberative record of Yuán imperial cultural-policy formation across three reigns.

The 30-*juàn* corpus is preserved largely intact through the WYG SKQS base. The collection was extensively cited by the Yuán-Míng compiler Sū Tiānjué for the *Yuán wénlèi* (where many of Chéng's *zhìzhào* and *bēimíng* appear). *Yuánshǐ* j. 172 (Chéng Jùfū biography).

Composition window: 1280 (Chéng's earliest Yuán court compositions) through 1318 (his death). The 1286 southern-recruitment mission is documented in the imperial *zhìzhào* preserved in *juàn* 1–9.

## Other points of interest

The Yǐngshān Chéngshì family's Jīng-shān-region literary tradition (paralleled by the Jiāyú Wúshì Wú Chéng family) reflects the Húběi *Jiāng-Hàn jìshū* (riverine-cultural) regional Confucian network. Chéng Jùfū's choice of *Méngqīng* (after the Hǎishān taboo-avoidance) preserves the principle of imperial-name avoidance through *zì*-substitution — Chéng is one of the most-cited Yuán-period examples of this practice.
