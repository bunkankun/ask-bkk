# Dàyúnshānfáng wéngǎo chūjí 大雲山房文稿初集
*Prose Drafts from the Great Cloud Mountain Studio, Initial Collection*
by [[惲敬]] (撰)

## About the work

The initial-collection (*chūjí* 初集) of the collected prose of [[惲敬]] Yùn Jìng (1757–1817, *zì* Zǐjū 子居, *hào* Jiǎntāng 簡堂, native of Yánghú 陽湖, Chángzhōu, Jiāngsū), 4 juan, the principal *biéjí* representing the Yánghú 陽湖 school of *gǔwén* prose. Yánghú is the second of the two great early-Jiāqìng *gǔwén* schools, sister-and-rival of Tóngchéng: founded by Yùn Jìng in partnership with [[張惠言]] Zhāng Huìyán [[KR4f0066]], it diverges from Tóngchéng by integrating *Hàn-Wèi piánsàn* (Han-Wei parallel-and-prose blend) into the *gǔwén* line, and by drawing more freely on the *zǐ* (philosophical-masters) literature alongside the canonical *jīng* and *shǐ*. The *chūjí* is organized into *zázhù* 雜著 (philosophical-essay, modeled on the *zǐjiā* tradition), *xùjì* 序記 (prefaces and records), *bēizhì* 碑志 (stelae and epitaphs), *chuán* 傳 (biographies), *dàzhuàn* 大傳 (major biographies), *xiǎochuán* 小傳 / *wàichuán* 外傳, *yíshì* 遺事 / *shù* 述, and *mùbiǎo* 墓表 / *bìjì* 壁記. The companion volumes — *Dàyúnshānfáng wéngǎo èrjí* (second collection) and *Dàyúnshānfáng wéngǎo yánshì* 言事 (memorials and administrative writings) — are bibliographically distinct sub-collections.

### Prefaces

Following the *mùlù* (table of contents), the *chūjí* contains an unusually elaborate *Tōnglì* 通例 ("General Conventions") explaining Yùn Jìng's editorial principles in over twenty articles, each addressing one bibliographic-formal question (how to refer to oneself in different genres; how to record the names of contemporaries; how to handle stele-vs.-biography conventions for an ancestor's name; how to format the title-line for *bēizhì* listing rank, office, fief, and posthumous title; how Manchu and Mongol names should be treated; how mourning-place inscriptions differ from common stele-inscriptions; etc.). The *Tōnglì* is itself a primary document of late-Qiánlóng / early-Jiāqìng bibliographic-genre theory: Yùn writes that *zázhù wén* (miscellaneous philosophical essays) belong to the *zhūzǐjiā zhī liú* 諸子家之流 (lineage of the masters-school), citing the *Wáng Zǐyuān* method; that *xùjì wén* uses the Sòng-style *bǐ rén yuē xián, zìchēng yuē yú* (others called "worthy," self "unworthy") convention; that *bēizhì wén* follows Hàn-Wèi tradition by omitting the composer's name in the inscription itself, but recording it in the collection (per Hán Yù's method); that *chuán wén* following the biographies appends a *lùn yuē* (commentator's note) in the manner of Bān Gù 班固. The treatise distinguishes *dàzhuàn* (major biography, treated as proto-historiographical and so omitted from the collection per Hán Yù's example with Lù Zhì and Yáng Chéng) from *xiǎochuán* / *wàichuán* (lesser biographies, retained), and provides systematic conventions for recording *zì* and *hào*.

### Abstract

Yùn Jìng was a *jǔrén* of Qiánlóng 48 (1783) but never advanced to *jìnshì*. He served as a sub-prefect (*tóngzhī* 同知) and then as a county magistrate in Jiāngxī (in Wǔníng 武寧 and Nánfēng 南豐), gaining a strong administrative reputation. His mature literary partnership with [[張惠言]] Zhāng Huìyán — both natives of Yánghú, both *jǔrén* of 1783, both members of the same Chángzhōu *cífù* circle — produced the *Yánghú* prose school as a programmatic alternative to Tóngchéng. The crucial point of departure is the role of *pián* (parallel-prose) within *gǔwén*: Tóngchéng (especially [[姚鼐]]) had treated *pián* as fundamentally distinct from *gǔwén*; Yánghú reintegrates them, on the philological-historical ground that Hàn-Wèi prose itself was *piánsàn jiānxíng* 駢散兼行 (parallel-and-prose intermixed). The Yánghú position is closely allied with [[阮元]] Ruǎn Yuán's *Wényán shuō* 文言說 (in [[KR4f0062]]) and supplied the early-nineteenth-century theoretical basis for the *piánwén* revival that culminated in [[袁枚]] Yuán Méi's followers and the late-Qīng Chángzhōu *cí pài*.

The *chūjí*'s philosophical-essay (*zázhù*) juan contain Yùn Jìng's most ambitious theoretical writings, notably the *Sān dài yīnyáng wǔxíng shuō* 三代陰陽五行說 (a *kǎozhèng* reconstruction of pre-Qín cosmology, arguing against the orthodox Sòng integrated *yīnyáng wǔxíng* system); the *Shàngshū gǔjīn wén shuō* 尚書古今文說 (on the Old-vs-New-Text *Shàng shū*); essays on *Lǎo Zhuāng* and the *Ǎn yǐ rán shēngfēng* (sceptical-Daoist) tradition; and the *Sān guó zhì zhū rén shū hòu* 三國志諸人書後 (postfaces to *Sān guó zhì* biographies). The *xùjì* and *bēizhì* juan contain his prose for the Yánghú / Chángzhōu network, including biographies of Zhāng Huìyán and the early Chángzhōu *cí*-school masters.

Composition window: c. 1785 (Yùn's earliest mature prose, in his late twenties) through 1817 (his death in Chángzhōu). The *chūjí* is the first of the three SBCK sub-collections; the *èrjí* and *yánshì* extend the recension. The *editio princeps* is the Dàoguāng-era imprint by Yùn's son and disciples.

## Other points of interest

The studio name *Dàyúnshānfáng* 大雲山房 ("Great Cloud Mountain Studio") refers to the Dàyún Mountains south of Chángzhōu, near Yùn Jìng's ancestral village. The *Tōnglì* — Yùn's bibliographic-formal manual — is one of the most systematic statements of premodern Chinese *biéjí* compilation theory in any author's collected works; it is virtually a literary-bibliographic treatise in miniature. Scholars of Qing book-historical convention regularly cite it as documentation of the *gǔwén* school's self-conscious formalization in the early nineteenth century.
