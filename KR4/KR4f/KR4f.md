# KR4f 別集類(清) — Biéjí lèi (Qīng)
*Belles-Lettres — Individual Literary Collections of the Qīng Dynasty*

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR4f sits within KR4 集部 *Jíbù* (the fourth of the four 四部 divisions of the SKQS classification — *Jīng, Shǐ, Zǐ, Jí*) and gathers individual literary collections (*biéjí* 別集 — a single author's collected prose, poetry, and lyric, as distinct from the *zǒngjí* 總集 anthologies of multiple authors) of the Qīng dynasty (清, 1644–1911). All 69 catalog entries in KR4f are Qīng works, including a handful of Míng-Qīng-transition *yímín* 遺民 (Ming-loyalist) figures conventionally classified under their Qīng-dynasty productive years. The Qīng *biéjí* corpus is the largest in Chinese bibliographic history: Wilkinson §66.4.9 records that Ke Yuchun's 2002 *Qīngrén shīwénjí zǒngmù tíyào* lists some 40,000 collected works of over 19,700 Qīng literati, of which only a fraction were printed and a still smaller fraction transmitted to modern collections. The 69 works in KR4f therefore represent a doubly-filtered canon.

Two distinct curatorial filters shape the selection. The *Sìkù quánshū* 文淵閣 selection (1773–1782) admitted Qīng authors approved by the imperial editorial board — Hānlín officials, high-provincial figures, Cheng-Zhu orthodox writers, plus the KāngxīYōngzhèngQiánlóng imperial *yùzhì* 御製 collections — and systematically excluded Ming-loyalist authors on political grounds, so that [[顧炎武]], [[黃宗羲]], [[王夫之]], and [[錢謙益]] are absent from WYG. The *Sìbù cóngkān* 四部叢刊 selection (1919–1936) restored these *yímín* collections and added important later figures — [[姚鼐]], [[全祖望]], [[戴震]], [[錢大昕]], [[阮元]], [[張惠言]], [[龔自珍]], [[曾國藩]] — whose *biéjí* circulated in the Qīng only in family imprints or Tóng-zhì-era post-Tài-píng restoration reprints. Together the two filters preserve roughly complementary canons of Qīng literature.

Three principal Qīng literary-theoretical traditions structure the division: the *Shényùn* 神韻 school of poetic indirection centered on [[王士禛]]; the Tóngchéng 桐城 school of *gǔwén* 古文 prose, descending from [[方苞]] through [[劉大櫆]] to [[姚鼐]] (the Yánghú 陽湖 offshoot of [[張惠言]] and [[惲敬]] partly broke with this synthesis — Wilkinson, Box 173); and the *Chángzhōu* 常州 and *Zhèxī* 浙西 *cí* 詞 schools of allusive lyric, descending from [[陳維崧]] and [[朱彝尊]] in the early Qīng through [[厲鶚]] to [[張惠言]]'s late-imperial *Cí xuǎn* 詞選 program. Cross-cutting these is the great *kǎozhèng* 考證 (evidential research) movement of the late Qiánlóng / early Jiāqìng generation — [[戴震]], [[錢大昕]], [[盧文弨]], [[孫星衍]], [[洪亮吉]], [[汪中]], [[阮元]] — whose *biéjí* preserve the polemical, biographical, and methodological apparatus surrounding the technical monographs catalogued elsewhere in KR1–KR3. The late-Qīng *Gōngyáng* 公羊 New-Text Confucian tradition that runs from [[張惠言]] through [[龔自珍]] to the Hundred-Days Reform generation supplies the diagnostic vocabulary of dynastic decline that closes the division.

## Important texts and text clusters

**Early-Qīng *yímín* prose and verse.** The three great early-Qīng Confucians — [[顧炎武]] in [[KR4f0006]] *Tínglín shījí* 亭林詩集 and [[KR4f0007]] *Tínglín yújí* 亭林餘集; [[黃宗羲]] in [[KR4f0008]] *Nánléi wénàn* 南雷文案; [[王夫之]] in [[KR4f0009]] *Jiāngzhāi shīwénjí* 薑齋詩文集 — were excluded by the *Sìkù* as politically inadmissible and survive in pre-modern print only via the SBCK. To these belong [[KR4f0010]] / [[KR4f0011]] *Mùzhāi chūxué jí* / *Yǒuxué jí* 牧齋初學集 / 牧齋有學集 of [[錢謙益]] (the surrendered late-Míng grand secretary whose poetics shaped the *Yúshān* 虞山 tradition); [[KR4f0021]] *Jūyìtáng jí* 居易堂集 of [[徐枋]] (a recluse who refused all Qīng patronage); and [[KR4f0069]] *Míngzhāi jí* 茗齋集 of [[彭孫貽]].

**Imperial *yùzhì* collections.** [[KR4f0001]] *Shèngzǔ rén huángdì yùzhì wénjí* 聖祖仁皇帝御製文集 (the Kāngxī emperor, 176 juan); [[KR4f0002]] *Shìzōng xiàn huángdì yùzhì wénjí* 世宗憲皇帝御製文集 (the Yōngzhèng emperor, 30 juan); [[KR4f0003]] *Yùzhì lèshàntáng quánjí dìngběn* 御製樂善堂全集定本 (the Qiánlóng emperor's prince-period writings, re-edited under his reign); [[KR4f0004]] *Yùzhì wénjí* 御製文集 and [[KR4f0005]] *Yùzhì shījí* 御製詩集 (Qiánlóng's reign-era prose and 454-juan poetry — the single largest text in KR4f). These four imperial corpora are the principal documentary record of high-Qīng court intellectual culture and of the imperial editorial program that produced the *Sìkù quánshū* itself.

**Early-Qīng Hànlín literary circle.** [[KR4f0012]] / [[KR4f0013]] *Méicūn jí* / *Méicūn jiāzànggǎo* 梅村集 / 梅村家藏藁 of [[吳偉業]] Wú Wěiyè (Ming-loyalist who later took Qīng office); [[KR4f0019]] *Jīng huá lù* 精華錄 of [[王士禛]] Wáng Shìzhēn (founder of *Shényùn*); [[KR4f0020]] *Yáofēng wénchāo* 堯峯文鈔 of [[汪琬]]; [[KR4f0022]] *Wǔtíng wénbiān* 午亭文編 of [[陳廷敬]]; [[KR4f0025]] *Pùshūtíng jí* 曝書亭集 of [[朱彝尊]] Zhū Yízūn (founder of the *Zhèxī* *cí* school); [[KR4f0029]] *Wénduān jí* 文端集 of [[張英]]; [[KR4f0030]] *Xīhé jí* 西河集 of [[毛奇齡]] Máo Qílíng (the most prolific early-Qīng polemicist, 189 juan — the largest single-author *biéjí* in the division); [[KR4f0031]] *Chén Jiǎntǎo sìliù* 陳檢討四六 and [[KR4f0032]] *Chén Jiālíng wénjí* 陳迦陵文集 of [[陳維崧]] (founder of the *Yángxiàn* *cí* school).

**Cheng-Zhu Lǐxué officials.** [[KR4f0014]] *Tāngzǐ yíshū* 湯子遺書 of [[湯斌]]; [[KR4f0015]] *Jiānjìtáng wénjí* 兼濟堂文集 of [[魏裔介]]; [[KR4f0039]] *Róngcūn jí* 榕村集 of [[李光地]] (Kāngxī's principal Sòng-school adviser); [[KR4f0040]] *Sānyútáng wénjí* 三魚堂文集 of [[陸隴其]]; [[KR4f0043]] *Èrxītáng wénjí* 二希堂文集 of [[蔡世遠]]. The documentary record of the Qīng *Lǐxué* establishment that anchored the dynasty's Confucian-orthodox ideological program.

**Tóngchéng prose tradition.** [[KR4f0045]] *Wàngxī jí* 望溪集 of [[方苞]] Fāng Bāo (the founder) and [[KR4f0052]] *Xībàoxuān wénjí* 惜抱軒文集 of [[姚鼐]] Yáo Nài (the second-generation synthesizer of *yìlǐ*, *kǎojù*, *cízhāng*) together define Tóngchéng doctrine. Yáo's parallel anthology *Gǔwéncí lèizuǎn* 古文辭類纂 — not in KR4f — became the standard composition textbook of late-imperial China and the Republican period.

**Mid-Qīng *Zhèxī* lyric and Sòng-poetic line.** [[KR4f0044]] *Jìngyètáng shījí* 敬業堂詩集 of [[查慎行]] (the leading early-Qīng Sòng / Lù Yóu line poet); [[KR4f0049]] *Fánxiè shānfáng jí* 樊榭山房集 of [[厲鶚]] (third in the canonical *Zhèxī* *cí* line after Zhū Yízūn, also the foundational mid-Qīng Sòng-poetry historian via his *Sòng shī jì shì* 宋詩紀事).

**The *kǎozhèng* generation.** [[KR4f0053]] *Dài Dōngyuán jí* 戴東原集 of [[戴震]] (the central QiánJiā intellectual figure); [[KR4f0054]] *Jíqítíng jí* 鮚埼亭集 and [[KR4f0055]] *Jíqítíng shījí* 鮚埼亭詩集 of [[全祖望]] (the *Zhèdōng* historiographer who continued Huáng Zōngxī's *SòngYuán xuéàn*); [[KR4f0058]] *Bàojīngtáng wénjí* 抱經堂文集 of [[盧文弨]]; [[KR4f0059]] *Qiányántáng wénjí* 潛研堂文集 of [[錢大昕]] (foremost historical philologist of the generation); [[KR4f0056]] *Hóng Běijiāng shīwénjí* 洪北江詩文集 of [[洪亮吉]]; [[KR4f0057]] *Sūn Yuānrú shīwénjí* 孫淵如詩文集 of [[孫星衍]]; [[KR4f0060]] / [[KR4f0061]] *Shùxué nèipiān* 述學內篇 / *Róngfǔ xiānshēng yíshī* 容甫先生遺詩 of [[汪中]]; [[KR4f0050]] *Guǒtáng jí* 果堂集 of [[沈彤]]; [[KR4f0062]] *Yánjīngshì yījí* 揅經室一集 of [[阮元]] (the institutional architect of late-Qiánlóng *kǎozhèng*, founder of the Gūshān 詁經精舍 and Xuéhǎitáng 學海堂 academies). This is the largest single cluster in the division and supplies the literary-personal complement to the technical *kǎozhèng* monographs scattered through KR1, KR2, and KR3.

**Chángzhōu / Yánghú school and late-Qīng *Gōngyáng*.** [[KR4f0063]] *Dàyún shānfáng wéngǎo* 大雲山房文稿 of [[惲敬]] (Yánghú co-founder); [[KR4f0066]] *Míngkē wén chūbiān* 茗柯文初編 and [[KR4f0067]] *Míngkē wén bǔbiān* 茗柯文補編 of [[張惠言]] (Yánghú co-founder, *Yúshì Yì* 虞氏易 specialist, founder of the Chángzhōu *cí* school via the *Cí xuǎn xù* 詞選序); [[KR4f0064]] *Dìngān wénjí* 定盦文集 and [[KR4f0065]] *Dìngān wénjí bǔbiān* 定盦文集補編 of [[龔自珍]] (the late-Cháng-zhōu *Gōngyáng* prophet of Qīng decline; maternal grandson of [[段玉裁]], student of Liú Fénglù 劉逢祿).

**Late-Qīng restoration.** [[KR4f0068]] *Zēng Wénzhènggōng shījí* 曾文正公詩集 of [[曾國藩]] (Tóngzhì restoration statesman, suppressor of the Tàipíng, principal Self-Strengthening leader, posthumously honored *Wénzhèng* 文正).

## Important persons

- [[聖祖玄燁]] (1654–1722) — the Kāngxī emperor; his *yùzhì wénjí* is the documentary heart of early-high-Qīng imperial-literary culture and supplies the imperial side of debates carried elsewhere by his senior officials.
- [[顧炎武]] (1613–1682) — founder of Qīng *kǎozhèng*; his poetry in KR4f supplies the early-Qīng *yímín* lyric tradition.
- [[黃宗羲]] (1610–1695) — founder of the *Zhèdōng* historical school; his prose carries the political-philosophical kernels later elaborated in the *Míng yí dài fǎng lù*.
- [[王夫之]] (1619–1692) — third of the three great early-Qīng Confucians; *yímín* poet-philosopher of Héngyáng.
- [[錢謙益]] (1582–1664) — late-Míng grand secretary turned Qīng official; his *Mùzhāi* collections and his preface to [[王士禛]]'s *Jīng huá lù* set the late-seventeenth-century poetic field.
- [[吳偉業]] (1609–1672) — Méicūn; the *yímín*-but-served *qīliù tǐ* 七律 (seven-character regulated verse) master of the early Qīng.
- [[王士禛]] (1634–1711) — founder of the *Shényùn* 神韻 poetic school and the dominant Kāngxī-era poet.
- [[朱彝尊]] (1629–1709) — founder of the *Zhèxī* *cí* school; one of the great early-Qīng erudite poet-philologists.
- [[毛奇齡]] (1623–1716) — the most prolific and polemical early-Qīng Classics commentator; his *Xīhé jí* is the largest *biéjí* in KR4f.
- [[陳維崧]] (1625–1682) — founder of the *Yángxiàn* *cí* school; one of the early-Qīng *cí* triumvirate with Zhū Yízūn and Nàlán Xìngdé.
- [[李光地]] (1642–1718) — Kāngxī's chief Cheng-Zhu *Lǐxué* adviser; principal editor of the imperially-commissioned *Zhūzǐ quánshū* and *Xìnglǐ jīngyì*.
- [[查慎行]] (1650–1727) — leading early-Qīng exponent of the Sòng / Lù Yóu poetic line.
- [[方苞]] (1668–1749) — founder of the Tóngchéng *gǔwén* school; the most influential mid-Qīng prose theorist.
- [[厲鶚]] (1692–1752) — leading mid-Qiánlóng *Zhèxī* *cí*-lyricist and Sòng-poetry historian.
- [[姚鼐]] (1732–1815) — second-generation Tóngchéng master; the *Gǔwéncí lèizuǎn* anthology defined late-imperial prose pedagogy.
- [[戴震]] (1724–1777) — the central intellectual figure of QiánJiā *kǎozhèng*; his presence in KR4f is via the *Dài Dōngyuán jí*'s literary-philosophical essays.
- [[錢大昕]] (1728–1804) — foremost historical philologist of the QiánJiā generation; the *Qiányántáng wénjí* is the literary complement to his *Èrshíèrshǐ kǎoyì* and other technical monographs.
- [[全祖望]] (1705–1755) — *Zhèdōng* historiographer; continuator of Huáng Zōngxī's *SòngYuán xuéàn*.
- [[阮元]] (1764–1849) — principal institutional patron and synthesizer of late-Qiánlóng to Dàoguāng *kǎozhèng*; founder of the Gūshān and Xuéhǎitáng academies.
- [[張惠言]] (1761–1802) — Chángzhōu polymath: *Yúshì Yì* specialist, Yánghú prose co-founder, Chángzhōu *cí* school founder.
- [[龔自珍]] (1792–1841) — the late-Cháng-zhōu *Gōngyáng* New-Text prophet; his prose articulates the late-Qīng diagnosis of dynastic decline and supplies the rhetorical model for the Hundred-Days Reform generation.
- [[曾國藩]] (1811–1872) — Tóngzhì restoration statesman; his *biéjí* closes the division at the late-nineteenth-century turning point.

## Topics

- **The *Sìkù* / SBCK divide.** The Qīng *biéjí* corpus is uniquely shaped by two successive curatorial filters separated by 130 years. The *Sìkù quánshū* (1773–1782) excluded Ming-loyalist authors on political grounds; the *Sìbù cóngkān* (1919–1936) restored these and added important later figures whose collections circulated only in family imprints. The textual provenance of each KR4f entry (WYG vs. SBCK) is therefore the key bibliographic signal of its political-and-canonical status.

- **The *Shényùn* / Sòng-poetic divide.** [[王士禛]]'s *Shényùn* school of poetic indirection — derived from Wáng Wéi and Yán Yǔ's *Cānglàng shīhuà* — dominated KāngxīYōngzhèng critical taste; the principal counter-tradition was the Sòng-poetic line (Lù Yóu, Yuán Hàowèn, Chén Shīdào), represented by [[查慎行]] and by the *Zhèxī* *cí*-and-Sòng-poetics school of [[朱彝尊]] and [[厲鶚]]. The *Sìkù* programmatic preference for *Shényùn* shaped the inclusion-and-evaluation language of many tíyào.

- **The Tóngchéng / Yánghú dispute.** The Tóngchéng program of [[方苞]] and [[姚鼐]] sought to unite *yìlǐ* (Sòng moral metaphysics), *kǎojù* (HànQīng evidential philology), and *cízhāng* (literary craft) under a single *gǔwén* method. The Yánghú offshoot of [[張惠言]] and [[惲敬]] broke partly with this synthesis, accommodating *pián* parallel-prose and Chángzhōu *kǎozhèng* in a more eclectic mode (Wilkinson, Box 173). The dispute structures the entire second half of the division.

- **The *Chángzhōu cí* / *Zhèxī cí* succession.** The early-Qīng *cí* revival had two principal regional traditions: the *Yángxiàn* of [[陳維崧]] and the *Zhèxī* of [[朱彝尊]]. The *Zhèxī* tradition descended through [[厲鶚]] to the early-Jiāqìng generation, where [[張惠言]]'s *Cí xuǎn xù* (preserved in [[KR4f0066]] *Míngkē wén chūbiān*) inaugurated a new *Chángzhōu* program emphasizing allegorical depth — the dominant late-imperial *cí* doctrine through 1900.

- **The QiánJiā *kǎozhèng* personal record.** The mid- to late-Qīng *biéjí* serve as the personal-biographical apparatus of the evidential-research movement. Prefaces, friendship-letters, *bēizhì* 碑誌 epitaphs, *zhuàn* 傳 biographies, and *jì* 記 records here document the social networks among [[戴震]], [[錢大昕]], [[盧文弨]], [[孫星衍]], [[洪亮吉]], [[汪中]], [[段玉裁]], and [[阮元]] — while their technical *kǎozhèng* monographs are catalogued separately across KR1, KR2, and KR3.

- **The late-Qīng *Gōngyáng* and dynastic-decline diagnosis.** From Liú Fénglù through [[張惠言]] to [[龔自珍]], the Chángzhōu *Gōngyáng* New-Text Confucian tradition supplied late-Qīng intellectuals with a critical idiom for diagnosing dynastic decline — the *sān shì* 三世 (three-age) and *yī tǒng* 一統 (unified-rule) doctrines, recast as periodization of Qīng crisis. The vocabulary descends to Kāng Yǒuwéi's *Dàtóng shū* and Liáng Qǐchāo's late-Qīng historiography. [[KR4f0064]] *Dìngān wénjí* is the principal documentary node for this transition.

- **The Tàipíng / Tóngzhì restoration filter.** Many late-Qīng *biéjí* in KR4f (the 1869 reprint of [[KR4f0066]] *Míngkē wén* with [[曾國藩]]'s preface; the 1860s Wú Xù imprint of [[KR4f0064]] *Dìngān wénjí*) are explicitly products of the post-Tài-píng restoration program — the Tóng-zhì-era project to recover the Jiāngnán literary record after the destruction of 1850–1864. The prefaces of these editions are themselves a documentary record of the restoration.
