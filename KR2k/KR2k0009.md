# Dà-Qīng yītǒng zhì 大清一統志
*Comprehensive Gazetteer of the Great Qīng*
by [[和珅]] (奉敕撰)

## About the work

The second edition of the Qīng official empire-wide gazetteer, in 424 (originally 500) juan, edited by Hé-shēn 和珅 (Hesĕn, 1750–1799) and others by imperial command. Begun by imperial order in Qiánlóng 29 (1764) on the basis of the first (1743/44, Xú Qiánxué) edition and finally presented to the throne in 1784 (printed 1790), this is the recension preserved in the *Wényuāngé Sìkù quánshū* and the one used here. It expands the first edition's 342 juan to 500 juan to incorporate the post-1755 conquests in Xīnjiāng, the formal reorganisation of Tibet and Qīnghǎi, and a number of newly internalised territories on the southwestern frontier (Wilkinson, *CHNM* §66.4.3.3). It is the largest and most authoritative Qīng comprehensive gazetteer prior to the third edition (the 1842 *Jiāqìng chóngxiū*) and remains the principal pre-Jiāqìng documentary source for Qīng administrative geography. (*The catalog meta records dynasty as 明, evidently a clerical error: this is a Qīng imperial work; corrected here.*)

### Tiyao

By imperial command, Qiánlóng 29 (1764). This book was first edited and completed in Qiánlóng 8 (1743). For each province, a *tǒngbù* 統部 (provincial overview) was first established, prefaced with maps and tables; first *fēnyě* 分野, next *jiànzhì yán'gé* 建置沿革, next *xíngshèng* 形勝, next *zhíguān* 職官, next *hùkǒu* 戶口, next *tiánfù* 田賦, next *míng-huàn* 名宦 — each subsuming the entire province. For its prefectures and *zhílì zhōu* 直隸州, a separate table was set up, with the counties hung beneath; for each, first *fēnyě*, next *jiànzhì yán'gé*, next *xíngshèng*, next *fēngsú* 風俗, next *chéng-chí* 城池, next *xuéxiào* 學校, next *hùkǒu*, next *tiánfù*, next *shānchuān* 山川, next *gǔjī* 古跡, next *guān-ài* 關隘, next *jīn-liáng* 津梁, next *dī-yàn* 堤堰, next *líng-mù* 陵墓, next *sì-guàn* 寺觀, next *míng-huàn*, next *rénwù* 人物, next *liú-yù* 流寓, next *liè-nǚ* 列女, next *xiān-shì* 仙釋, next *tǔchǎn* 土產 — twenty-one categories in all. Comprising 342 juan in total; with foreign vassals and tributary states appended separately.

By Qiánlóng 20 (1755), with imperial majesty thundering forth and Yīlí 伊犁 pacified, the territory expanded by over 20,000 *lǐ* — for which earlier topographies had no precedent — and the merging or subdivision of prefectures, *zhōu*, and counties, and the increases, decreases, and relocations of officials, also frequently differed from the older establishment. So an imperial decree commanded a re-edition, defining this present recension. Then in Qiánlóng 28 (1763), the south-western *Huí* tribes Àiwūhǎn 愛烏罕, Huòhǎn 霍罕, Qǐqíyùsū 啟齊玉蘇, Wū'ěrgēnqí 烏爾根齊, and the Yúnnán-southern tribal chiefdoms of Zhěngqiàn 整欠 and Jǐnghǎi 景海 successively submitted to the realm; in Qiánlóng 40 (1775) the two Jīnchuān 金川 (Greater and Lesser) were further pacified, with garrison-colonies opened and outposts ranged, broadening the imperial frame again. They were therefore likewise included in the work, to display the unprecedented extent of unified rule.

The territorial frame having broadened beyond what came before, the gathering is yet more comprehensive; the categories remain as before, but the format is yet more detailed. At a glance one has the *Nine Provinces* reaching their borders and the *Eight Extremes* in mutual concord, all visible to the palm of the hand. Of old, the Táng divided the empire into ten *dào*, with Lǒngyòu placed sixth; Lǐ Jífǔ's *Yuánhé jùnxiàn zhì* [[KR2k0003]] retreated it to tenth, the territory by then having fallen to Tǔbō. The Sòng's territorial extent was the most contracted; Ōuyáng Mín's *Yúdì guǎngjì* [[KR2k0006]] for those areas it could not hold, set up the rubric *huàwài zhōu*, already ingenious; Zhù Mù's *Fāngyú shènglǎn* [[KR2k0007]] does not even mention Huái-běi by a single character. Where dynasties have been weak, territorial extent has shrunk daily, and so the records have had to shrink daily; in our enlightened age, the imperial frame extends daily wider, and so the editing must accordingly grow yet daily fuller. Today's *Zhì*, dated by only some decades from the last edition, has territory assigned to it that the earlier work could not accommodate; the august virtue is announced afar, and submission flocks like clouds — this is the manifest token. Even the territorial expansion of Yú-Shùn, increasing nine provinces to twelve, can scarcely be set against the brilliance of our age.

### Abstract

Three editions of the *Dà-Qīng yītǒng zhì* were compiled (Wilkinson, *CHNM* §66.4.3.3):

1. **First edition** (the *Xú Qiánxué* edition): proposed by imperial agreement of the Kāngxī emperor in 1672 and completed only in 1743/44, in 342 juan, under the editorship of Xú Qiánxué 徐乾學 (1631–1694) and successors; the eighteen provincial gazetteers on which it was based had to be newly compiled first, accounting for the long delay.
2. **Second edition** (the *Hé-shēn* edition; **the present work**): commissioned by imperial decree in Qiánlóng 29 (1764) on the basis of the post-1755 territorial expansion (the conquest of the Dzungar territories and the pacification of the Hui regions of Xīnjiāng, the formal incorporation of Tibet and Qīnghǎi, and the new southwestern *tūsī* establishments). Final compilation was supervised by Hé-shēn 和珅 (1750–1799); presented to the throne in 1784 and printed in 1790. The work expanded the first edition's 342 juan to 500 juan; the Wényuāngé Sìkù copy was redivided into 424 juan during the *Sìkù* editing process (Qiánlóng 46, 1781).
3. **Third edition** (the *Mùzhāng'ā / Jiāqìng chóngxiū* edition): completed in 1820 (Jiāqìng 25) under Mùzhāng'ā 穆彰阿 et al.; printed in 1842; the *Jiāqìng yītǒng zhì*, in Wilkinson's words, "the largest and most accurate of all the extant empire-wide gazetteers".

The present (second) edition is the one preserved in the *Wényuāngé Sìkù quánshū* under volumes 474–483. The text proceeds, after the *yù-zhì xù* (imperial preface), province by province through the *Liǎng-jīng* (Beijing and Shèngjīng / Mukden), the eighteen Inner Provinces, the four colonised territories (Xīnjiāng, Tibet, Qīnghǎi, Mongolia / *Wài-fān*), and a final block on tributary states (*Cháoxiǎn* 朝鮮, *Liúqiú* 琉球, *Yuènán* 越南, *Xiānluó* 暹羅, *Miǎndiàn* 緬甸, *Sūlù* 蘇祿, etc.). The provincial-level structure follows a fixed 21-category schema (*fēnyě*, *jiànzhì yán'gé*, *xíngshèng*, *fēngsú*, *chéng-chí*, *xuéxiào*, *hùkǒu*, *tiánfù*, *shānchuān*, *gǔjī*, *guān-ài*, *jīn-liáng*, *dī-yàn*, *líng-mù*, *sì-guàn*, *míng-huàn*, *rénwù*, *liú-yù*, *liè-nǚ*, *xiān-shì*, *tǔchǎn*) which is the most elaborate of any imperial Chinese gazetteer.

A note on attribution: the catalog meta names Hé-shēn as *fèngchì-zhuàn* 奉敕撰 (compiler by imperial command), reflecting his nominal headship in the final phase of the project. In practice, the second edition was drafted and edited by a large team of scholars including the principal *zǒngzuǎn-guān* 總纂官 (Editors-in-Chief) of the *Sìkù quánshū* establishment — Jì Yún 紀昀, Lù Xīxióng 陸錫熊, Sūn Shìyì 孫士毅, with general collation by Lù Fèichí 陸費墀. The dating "Qiánlóng 29 (1764)" given in the catalog meta refers to the start of the second edition's commission; the actual presentation date is Qiánlóng 49 (1784), and the printing 1790. The date bracket 1764–1784 used in this entry reflects the actual composition span of the received Wényuāngé text.

## Other points of interest

The Hé-shēn recension is the first official Chinese imperial gazetteer to incorporate Tibet, Qīnghǎi, and Xīnjiāng as integral provinces of the realm rather than as foreign vassal regions, and is for that reason a key documentary basis for late-Qīng imperial geography. It is also the largest Sìkù entry in volumes by *cè* in the Wényuāngé. The 424-juan division of the Wényuāngé text reflects an editorial decision of the *Sìkù* establishment in 1781; the Hé-shēn presentation copy itself was 500 juan.
