# Guìzhōu tōngzhì 貴州通志
*Comprehensive Gazetteer of Guìzhōu*
supervised by [[鄂爾泰]] (È'ěrtài / Ortai, 1677–1745) — *jiānxiū* 監修
compiled by [[靖道謨]] (Jìng Dàomó, fl. early 18th c.) and [[杜恮]] (Dù Quán, fl. 1730s) — *biānzuǎn* 編纂

## About the work

The Yōngzhèng-era *Guìzhōu tōngzhì* in 46 *juan*, compiled in tandem with the *Yúnnán tōngzhì* ([[KR2k0055]]) under the supervision of the Yún-Guì Governor-General Ortai 鄂爾泰. Drafting was directed by Yáo-zhōu *zhīzhōu* Jìng Dàomó 靖道謨 (also principal *biānzuǎn* of the Yúnnán *tōngzhì*) and continued by Rén-huái 仁懷 *zhīxiàn* Dù Quán 杜恮; the woodblocks were not completed until Qiánlóng 6 (1741) — the latest of the Yōngzhèng-cohort gazetteers to be issued. The *jìnbiǎo* was filed by Acting-Governor Zhāng Guǎngsì 張廣泗, then Sìchuān-Yúnnán-Guìzhōu Governor-General. Guìzhōu's prior gazetteer tradition was even thinner than Yúnnán's: the Míng compiled gazetteers under Zhào Zàn 趙瓚, Xiè Dōngshān 謝東山, and Guō Zǐzhāng 郭子章; the early-Qīng Wèi Jì-qí 衛旣齊 added further redactions; Tián Wén 田雯's *Qián-shū* 黔書 contributed elegant prose but limited factual detail.

### Tiyao

We respectfully note: the *Guìzhōu tōngzhì* is supervised by Grand Secretary Ortai 鄂爾泰 of our dynasty, and others. This book and the *Yúnnán tōngzhì* were compiled in the same period and at the same time. The man who took charge of its affairs was likewise the Yáo-zhōu *zhīzhōu* Jìng Dàomó 靖道謨, who was succeeded by the Rén-huái *zhīxiàn* Dù Quán 杜恮. As against the various provincial *tōngzhì* compilations, this one is the latest to reach completion: only by Qiánlóng 6 (1741) were the cuttings finished. The Governor-General concurrently managing the Provincial Governorship, Zhāng Guǎngsì 張廣泗, presented it with a memorial.

Guìzhōu lies remote in the southwest, where Miáo and Mán peoples are interspersed. Under the Míng, a *Dū-zhǐhuī sī* 都指揮司 was first established; later this was reformed into a *Bù-zhèng sī* 布政司, with prefectures and counties separately set up — and was then ranked alongside the various provinces. Yet before the Táng-Sòng era it had only been *jīmí* and not entirely separated; the wild forest had not been fully cleared. Hence its records and registers in antiquity are sparse — the most desolate and rough.

In the Míng, Zhào Zàn 趙瓚 first launched the new gazetteer; thereafter Xiè Dōngshān 謝東山 and Guō Zǐzhāng 郭子章, and in our dynasty Wèi Jì-qí 衛旣齊 and others, each in turn enlarged and revised — gradually acquiring contour. Yet because the documentary remains were still hard to substantiate, omissions still could not be avoided. Only Tián Wén 田雯's *Qián-shū* 黔書 has been called notably bold and elegant in its writing — but its intent lies in adornment of literary refinement, and it does not lay out factual matters in the round. The present book gathers the writings of various authors and compiles them into a single volume. Although it cannot fully reach across antiquity and the present, in the cartography of the Qián province it is far more detailed than the older texts.

### Abstract

The 1741 *Guìzhōu tōngzhì* is the canonical Qīng-era documentary monument of the *gǎi-tǔ guī-liú* reforms in Guìzhōu — a province whose pre-Yuán political history was almost wholly that of *jīmí* tributary loose-rein governance. The work documents the unprecedented Yōngzhèng-era assertion of direct bureaucratic control over the Miáo, Bùyī (Yí), and Dòng populations of the Wǔlǐng-Yúnguì watershed. Its principal *biānzuǎn* Jìng Dàomó (also the lead compiler of the *Yúnnán tōngzhì*) and the continuing *biānzuǎn* Dù Quán (the Rén-huái *zhīxiàn*) both worked in direct administrative contact with the Miáo communities under transformation.

The work succeeds the early-Qīng Kāngxī-era *Guìzhōu tōngzhì* of Wèi Jì-qí (1673), as well as the Míng compilations by Zhào Zàn (Jiājìng-era), Xiè Dōngshān (Wànlì-era), and Guō Zǐzhāng (Wànlì-era). Its principal Míng-era literary precedent is Tián Wén (1635–1704)'s *Qián-shū* 黔書, but the Sìkù tíyào explicitly criticizes that work as ornamental rather than substantive. The 1741 *tōngzhì* in turn was substantially revised by Yáng Wéi-yùn 楊維瀛's *Guìzhōu tōngzhì* of 1815. The *Wényuāngé Sìkù quánshū* version (vols. 571.1–572.1) is the standard text.

The lateness of the work's woodblock issuance (1741, twelve years after the imperial mandate of 1729) reflects the disruption of the Yōngzhèng-era Miáo rebellion of 1735–1736 in the eastern Guìzhōu prefectures of Tái-gǒng 台拱, Qīng-jiāng 清江, and Bā-zhài 八寨; the gazetteer documents the suppression of that rebellion and the subsequent re-imposition of *gǎi-tǔ guī-liú* under Zhāng Guǎngsì's military command.

## Other points of interest

The *Guìzhōu tōngzhì* is the only Yōngzhèng-cohort provincial gazetteer to document an active counter-insurgency campaign in real time — Zhāng Guǎngsì's *Tái-gǒng-Bā-zhài* suppression of 1735–1736. The *Wǔ-bèi* and *Bīng-fáng* sections are correspondingly more developed than in any other provincial *tōngzhì* of the cohort, preserving substantial documentary materials on early-Qīng frontier military logistics, the *tǔ-bīng* (native militia) system, and the post-rebellion settlement of the eastern-Guìzhōu prefectures.
