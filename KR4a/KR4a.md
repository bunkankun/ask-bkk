# Chǔ cí lèi 楚辭類 — Collected Works → Songs of Chu

## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR4a` is the *Chǔcí* 楚辭 (*Songs of Chu* / *Songs of the South*) subcategory of the Sìkù 集部 (Literature) branch. It is by far the smallest of the *jíbù* subcategories, comprising the anthology itself plus the commentaries and pictorial editions that grew around it. The Sìkù compilers placed *Chǔcí* at the very head of the Literature branch (Wilkinson, *Chinese History: A New Manual*, §30.1, Table 37), reflecting its status as the second great anthology of ancient Chinese verse after the *Shī* 詩 ([[KR1h]]) and the founding text of the *cífù* 辭賦 tradition. The poems collected — *Lí sāo* 離騷, *Jiǔ gē* 九歌, *Tiān wèn* 天問, *Jiǔ zhāng* 九章, *Yuǎn yóu* 遠遊, *Jiǔ biàn* 九辯, *Zhāo hún* 招魂, *Dà zhāo* 大招, and the rest — emerged from the late Warring-States and Hàn south, took their received seventeen-section shape under [[王逸]] in the second century CE, and have been continuously annotated since.

The chronological span of the division is exceptional. The earliest dateable layer is the late-fourth-century BCE work conventionally ascribed to [[屈原]]; the latest piece is the 1782–1784 imperial illustrated edition [[KR4a0006]]. Two commentarial currents structure the tradition: the HànSòng philological line of [[王逸]]'s *Zhāng jù* ([[KR4a0002]]) and [[洪興祖]]'s *Bǔ zhù* ([[KR4a0003]]), which preserves and extends the loyal-minister-in-exile reading inherited from Sīmǎ Qiān; and the Southern Sòng Neo-Confucian re-reading of [[朱熹]] ([[KR4a0004]], [[KR4a0008]], [[KR4a0009]]), which restructured the canon, applied the *xìng / bǐ / fù* 興比賦 categories of the *Máo shī* commentary, and severely pruned what it judged shallow. Qīng *kǎozhèng* 考證 work — best represented in this division by [[蔣驥]]'s topographic-biographical commentary ([[KR4a0007]]) — and Qián-lóng-court pictorial scholarship ([[KR4a0006]]) round out the inherited tradition. Wú Rénjié's *Lí sāo cǎo mù shū* ([[KR4a0005]]) opens a third, *bówù* 博物 (broad-learning-of-things) strand parallel to the line of Lù Jī 陸璣 on the *Shī*.

## Important texts and text clusters

- **The base text.** *Chǔ cí* 楚辭 [[KR4a0001]] — the seventeen-section anthology in TLS base, the textual ground on which everything else in the division builds.
- **HànSòng commentarial cluster.** *Chǔ cí zhāng jù* 楚辭章句 [[KR4a0002]] by [[王逸]] is the earliest surviving complete commentary and the source of the seventeen-section canon; *Chǔ cí bǔ zhù* 楚詞補注 [[KR4a0003]] by [[洪興祖]] is the principal medium of its transmission, reproducing Wáng Yì's commentary in full and adding *bǔyuē* 補曰 notes from a long collation history. Read together, these two works are the platform of the philological tradition; modern critical editions still take Hóng's recension as their base.
- **The Zhū Xī trilogy.** *Chǔ cí jí zhù* 楚辭集注 [[KR4a0004]], *Chǔ cí biàn zhèng* 楚辭辯證 [[KR4a0009]], and *Chǔ cí hòu yǔ* 楚辭後語 [[KR4a0008]] — the *Jí zhù* fixes a new eight-juan canon (twenty-five QūYuán pieces as *Lí sāo* core + sixteen later pieces as *Xù lí sāo*), the *Biàn zhèng* is its passage-by-passage polemic against Wáng Yì and Hóng Xīngzǔ, and the *Hòu yǔ* extends the *sāo* lineage forward through Hàn, WèiJìn, Táng, and Northern Sòng. Written in [[朱熹]]'s last decade, partly under the 1196 *dàoxué* 道學 ban, the trilogy became the orthodox interpretive vocabulary of the *Chǔ cí* in Yuán, Míng, Korean, and Japanese reception.
- **Natural history.** *Lí sāo cǎo mù shū* 離騷草木疏 [[KR4a0005]] by [[吳仁傑]] is the principal botanical commentary, modeled on Lù Jī's *Máo shī cǎo mù niǎo shòu chóng yú shū* but methodologically distinctive in taking the *Shān hǎi jīng* 山海經 as its decisive authority for *Lí sāo* flora.
- **Qīng commentary.** *Shāndàigé zhù Chǔ cí* 山帶閣註楚辭 [[KR4a0007]] by [[蔣驥]] integrates biography and topography into the gloss, anchoring every section to a five-map reconstruction of Qū Yuán's exile route; the appended *Yú lùn* 餘論 and *Shuō yùn* 說韻 carry his rhyme-and-disputation work. The Sìkù tíyào treats it as transitional from late-Kāng-xī commentary to mature QiánJiā *kǎojù* 考據.
- **Pictorial.** *Qīndìng bǔhuì Xiāo Yúncóng Lísāo quántú* 欽定補繪蕭雲從離騷全圖 [[KR4a0006]] — the surviving sixty-four illustrations of [[蕭雲從]]'s 1645 *yímín* 遺民 *Lí sāo tú*, supplemented by ninety-one new images executed by Mén Yìngzhào 門應兆 and the court painters in 1782–1784 in conscious imitation of Lǐ Gōnglín 李公麟's *Jiǔ gē tú* — is the unique pictorial entry in the Sìkù *Chǔcí* subsection.

## Important persons

- [[屈原]] (Qū Yuán, conventionally ca. 343–278 BCE; attributed author): the figure around whom the anthology coalesced and to whom orthodox tradition since Sīmǎ Qiān (*Shǐ jì* 84) ascribes *Lí sāo*, *Tiān wèn*, the *Jiǔ zhāng* group, and most of *Jiǔ gē*. Modern scholarship treats this attribution as a Hàn editorial construction; for the present division he is the canonical authorial reference point.
- [[宋玉]] (Sòng Yù, 3rd c. BCE; attributed author): the canonical second voice, to whom [[王逸]] assigned *Jiǔ biàn* and *Zhāo hún*.
- [[王逸]] (Wáng Yì, fl. 89–158): the foundational figure of the division. His *Chǔ cí zhāng jù* ([[KR4a0002]]) fixed the seventeen-section canon, supplied the first complete commentary, and stabilized the loyal-minister-in-exile reading.
- [[洪興祖]] (Hóng Xīngzǔ, 1090–1155): the Sòng commentator whose *Bǔ zhù* ([[KR4a0003]]) is the principal medium through which [[王逸]]'s text has been transmitted from the twelfth century onward. Officially suppressed by Qín Huì's 秦檜 faction but circulated privately.
- [[朱熹]] (Zhū Xī, 1130–1200): the dominant Sòng Neo-Confucian re-reader of the corpus, author of the trilogy [[KR4a0004]] / [[KR4a0008]] / [[KR4a0009]] that restructured the canon along *xìng / bǐ / fù* lines.
- [[吳仁傑]] (Wú Rénjié, b. 1137): Sòng natural historian whose *Lí sāo cǎo mù shū* ([[KR4a0005]]) is the standard botanical commentary; also the author of *Yì*-classics work in [[KR1a]].
- [[蕭雲從]] (Xiāo Yúncóng, 1596–1673): *yímín* painter whose 1645 *Lí sāo tú* survives partially in [[KR4a0006]].
- [[蔣驥]] (Jiǎng Jì, fl. 1707–1727): Qīng author of [[KR4a0007]], whose itinerary-and-topography method is still cited as a reference point in modern *Chǔcí* studies. (Date discrepancy: catalog meta gives the *fl.* window, CBDB suggests b. 1714; see person note.)

## Topics

- **Political allegory vs. ritual/shamanic reading.** The dominant interpretive dichotomy of the entire commentarial tradition. The orthodox reading — Qū Yuán as the loyal slandered minister, the *Lí sāo* as veiled political protest — runs from Sīmǎ Qiān through [[王逸]], [[洪興祖]], and [[朱熹]] and was elaborated through every YuánMíngQīng commentary. Twentieth-century scholarship, building on the figure of the *wū* 巫, has read *Jiǔ gē* and *Zhāo hún* as séance liturgies; Wilkinson §58.6.3.2 notes the absence of any agreement on the form Chǔ shamanism actually took.
- **The construction of "Qū Yuán."** Modern scholarship (Du 2019, Schneider 2020) treats [[王逸]]'s *Zhāng jù* ([[KR4a0002]]) as the principal site at which the figure of Qū Yuán was constructed: Wáng's sectional prefaces did the editorial work that grounded the anthology around a single biographical hero. The *Chǔcí* tradition is therefore inseparable from a long Chinese myth of loyalty and dissent.
- **The *xìng / bǐ / fù* classification.** [[朱熹]]'s decision in [[KR4a0004]] to label every *zhāng* 章 as *xìng* 興, *bǐ* 比, or *fù* 賦 — categories borrowed from the *Máo shī zhuàn* 毛詩傳 ([[KR1h]]) — projects a unified Confucian poetics across the *Shī* and the *Chǔ cí*. The move was polemic: by deleting *Qī jiàn*, *Jiǔ huái*, *Jiǔ tàn*, and *Jiǔ sī* from the seventeen-section canon and admitting Jiǎ Yì's 賈誼 *fù* and Yáng Xióng's 揚雄 *Fǎn sāo* 反騷 (the last as a polemic foil), Zhū tilted the anthology decisively toward the loyalist-allegorical reading.
- **Section-order and the placement of prefaces.** The transmitted *Zhāng jù* is not [[王逸]]'s autograph: as the Sìkù tíyào to [[KR4a0002]] notes, both the section order and the placement of the *xù* 序 prefaces were rearranged in the Sòng, partly by Chén Shuōzhī 陳說之 in 1032 and partly by unknown later hands. The *Gǔwén Chǔcí Shìwén* 古文楚辭釋文 preserved by Lù Démíng 陸德明 attests an alternative order beginning with *Jiǔ biàn*. This Hóng Xīngzǔ–era debate is recovered in the front matter of [[KR4a0002]] and in [[KR4a0003]].
- **Natural-historical commentary.** The *Cǎo mù shū* ([[KR4a0005]]) inaugurates a *bówù* strand within *Chǔcí* scholarship — close in method to Lù Jī's *Máo shī cǎo mù shū* and Luó Yuàn's *Ěr yǎ yì*, controversial for grounding the botanical glosses in the *Shān hǎi jīng*, and prefiguring Qīng natural-historical philology. The *xiāng cǎo* 香草 (fragrant-plant) catalog of the imperial pictorial edition ([[KR4a0006]]) is the visual counterpart.
- **Topography and biographical reconstruction.** [[蔣驥]]'s *Shāndàigé zhù* ([[KR4a0007]]) introduces a systematic itinerary reconstruction: every section is dated and located by reference to a five-map model of Qū Yuán's exile. Even where modern *Chǔcí* scholarship rejects individual datings, the maps are still cited.
- **Ancient rhyme.** The *Zhāng jù* preserves rhymed sequences in its own commentary (the Sìkù tíyào to [[KR4a0002]] singles them out: e.g. *哀憤結縎, 慮煩冤也*); these are useful Eastern-Hàn rhyme evidence that the *gǔyùn* 古韻 tradition from Wú Yù 吳棫 onward never exploited. [[蔣驥]]'s *Shuō yùn* 說韻 ([[KR4a0007]]) is the division's standalone treatise on *Chǔcí* rhyme; its conflation of *zìmǔ* 字母 (Indic-derived initials) with native *shēngyùn* 聲韻 (ancient rhymes) was already a fair QiánJiā philological complaint by the time of the Sìkù tíyào.
- **Pictorial *Chǔcí*.** [[蕭雲從]]'s 1645 *Lí sāo tú*, taken up by the Qiánlóng court as [[KR4a0006]], is one of the division's most political objects: an *yímín* lament for the fallen Míng, "completed" by court painters in 1782–84 and reframed as an imperially-validated reading.

## Timeline

- c. -300 to 158 — *Chǔ cí* 楚辭 [[KR4a0001]] — the anthology, formed in two stages: a first compilation by Liú Xiàng 劉向 (79–8 BCE) from the imperial library, and the present seventeen-section recension edited by [[王逸]] in the Eastern Hàn, dated here to the receipt of the final piece (Wáng Yì's own *Jiǔ sī*).
- 89–158 — *Chǔ cí zhāng jù* 楚辭章句 [[KR4a0002]] — [[王逸]]'s sectional-and-glossary commentary; fixes the seventeen-section canon and the loyal-minister reading.
- 1110–1155 — *Chǔ cí bǔ zhù* 楚詞補注 [[KR4a0003]] — [[洪興祖]]'s long-accumulated supplementary commentary, reproducing [[王逸]] in full and adding *bǔyuē* notes drawn from a wide collation.
- 1190–1199 — *Chǔ cí jí zhù* 楚辭集注 [[KR4a0004]], *Chǔ cí biàn zhèng* 楚辭辯證 [[KR4a0009]], *Chǔ cí hòu yǔ* 楚辭後語 [[KR4a0008]] — [[朱熹]]'s trilogy, intensifying around the death of Zhào Rǔyú 趙汝愚 (1196) and the *dàoxué* ban.
- 1197–1200 — *Lí sāo cǎo mù shū* 離騷草木疏 [[KR4a0005]] — [[吳仁傑]]'s four-juan botanical commentary, restricted (after [[朱熹]]) to the twenty-five-piece *Lí sāo* core.
- 1713–1727 — *Shāndàigé zhù Chǔ cí* 山帶閣註楚辭 [[KR4a0007]] — [[蔣驥]]'s topographic-biographical commentary, plus the appended *Yú lùn* and *Shuō yùn*.
- 1782–1784 — *Qīndìng bǔhuì Xiāo Yúncóng Lísāo quántú* 欽定補繪蕭雲從離騷全圖 [[KR4a0006]] — the Qiánlóng court's three-juan illustrated edition, completing [[蕭雲從]]'s 1645 fragmentary *Lí sāo tú* with 91 court-painter images.
