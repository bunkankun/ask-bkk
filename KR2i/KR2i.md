# KR2i 載記類 — Annals of Non-Legitimate Regimes

## Scope and scholarly tradition

*Zǎijì* 載記 is the fourth-from-last of the fifteen *History* (史部) sub-categories in the *Sìkù quánshū zǒngmù* 四庫全書總目 (see Wilkinson §47 Table 89). The *Sìkù* editors borrowed the term from the *Jìnshū* 晉書, where Fáng Xuán-líng [[房玄齡]] had used it to house the basic annals of the fourteen of the *Wǔ-hú Shíliù-guó* 五胡十六國 (Sixteen Kingdoms of the Five Barbarians) that the Táng court refused to grant *běn-jì* 本紀 status. *Zǎijì* in turn replaces the earlier bibliographic labels *bàshǐ* 霸史 ("histories of hegemons" — used since the *Suí-zhì* 隋志) and the still earlier *wěishǐ* 偽史 ("false histories" — from Ruǎn Xiào-xù's 阮孝緒 *Qī-lù* 七錄). The euphemism softens with each change, but the underlying judgement is constant: these are histories of states the central tradition did *not* recognize as standing in the orthodox dynastic succession (*zhèng-tǒng* 正統). In the *Sìkù*, the rubric covers 23 works (Wilkinson §47, Table 89), and the corpus that follows here is exactly that *Sìkù* set.

The chronological reach of the category is unusually wide. The two earliest works are Later-Hàn narratives of the Spring-and-Autumn-period *Wú* 吳 and *Yuè* 越 ([[KR2i0001]], [[KR2i0002]]); the latest are early-Qīng. In between sit the major regional histories of every era of division — the Sixteen Kingdoms of the 4th–5th centuries ([[KR2i0005]], [[KR2i0006]]), the Sìchuān gazetteer *Huá-yáng Guó-zhì* [[KR2i0003]], the Hòu-Zhào *Yè-zhōng jì* [[KR2i0004]], the *Mán-shū* on Nán-zhào [[KR2i0007]], the dense cluster of Five-Dynasties / Ten-Kingdoms regional histories ([[KR2i0008]]–[[KR2i0019]]), the early-Qīng synthesis of the Ten Kingdoms ([[KR2i0021]]), and finally a *fù-lù* 附錄 of two East-Asian histories (Korea [[KR2i0022]], Vietnam [[KR2i0023]] and the related Vietnamese gazetteer [[KR2i0020]]) — works on Sinographic states beyond the Chinese empire proper, retained by the *Sìkù* editors precisely on the principle that they too document polities outside the legitimate succession. The transmissional history is heavy with loss and reconstruction: a significant share of the corpus survives only through *Sìkù*-editor recovery from the *Yǒng-lè dà-diǎn* 永樂大典 (notably [[KR2i0004]], [[KR2i0007]], [[KR2i0012]]), late-Míng learned re-compilation (the 100-juàn [[KR2i0005]] is a Tú Qiáo-sūn / Xiàng Lín reconstruction of Cuī Hóng's lost original), or single-line Chinese transmission of foreign-origin texts ([[KR2i0023]], lost in Vietnam itself).

## Important texts and text clusters

- *Wú Yuè Chūnqiū* 吳越春秋 [[KR2i0001]] and *Yuè Jué Shū* 越絕書 [[KR2i0002]] — the two great Later-Hàn narratives of the Wú-Yuè rivalry, founding works of the *bàshǐ* / *zǎijì* category and, along with the *Zhànguó-cè*, the earliest non-canonical histories with a clear regional focus.
- *Huá-yáng Guó-zhì* 華陽國志 [[KR2i0003]] — Cháng Qú's *c.* 348–354 gazetteer of Bā-Shǔ-Hàn-zhōng-Nán-zhōng, **the earliest regional history to survive intact** (Wilkinson §60.3) and the source standing behind Lì Dào-yuán's *Shuǐ-jīng zhù* and Lè Shǐ's *Tài-píng huán-yǔ jì* for everything on the southwest.
- *Yè-zhōng jì* 鄴中記 [[KR2i0004]] — the unique pre-Táng witness to Hòu-Zhào Yè under Shí Hǔ; the Tóng-què, Bīng-jǐng, and Jīn-fèng tái complexes are described nowhere else.
- The Sixteen Kingdoms pair, [[KR2i0005]] (100-juàn Wàn-lì recompilation of Cuī Hóng's lost original by Tú Qiáo-sūn 屠喬孫 and Xiàng Lín 項琳) and [[KR2i0006]] (16-juàn *Hàn-Wèi cóngshū* recension) — paired witnesses to a Northern-Wèi original lost in the Northern Sòng; together with the *Jìn-shū Zǎijì* and Tāng Qiú's 湯球 *Shíliù-guó Chūnqiū jí-bǔ* they form the modern evidence-base for the period 304–439.
- *Mán-shū* 蠻書 [[KR2i0007]] — Fán Chuò's 862–864 ethnographic-strategic treatise on Nán-zhào, the principal source behind Sòng Qí's *Nán-mán-zhuàn* in the *Xīn-Táng-shū* and Sī-mǎ Guāng's *Tōng-jiàn* on Yúnnán; preserves the earliest external lexica of Tibeto-Burman *Bái* and *Yí*.
- **The Southern Táng cluster** — [[KR2i0008]] *Diào-jī Lì-tán*, [[KR2i0009]] *Jiāng-nán Yě-shǐ*, [[KR2i0010]] *Jiāng-nán Bié-lù*, [[KR2i0011]] *Jiāng-biǎo Zhì*, [[KR2i0012]] *Jiāng-nán Yú-zǎi*, [[KR2i0017]] *Mǎ-shì Nán-Táng Shū*, [[KR2i0018]] *Lù-shì Nán-Táng Shū*. This is the densest sub-cluster of the division — six privately-compiled early-Sòng witnesses (most by men with personal ties to the conquered court) plus the two great Sòng *Nán-Táng shū* recensions by [[馬令]] (1105) and [[陸游]] (*c.* 1180–1209). Wilkinson §61.8.1 #5 notes that Lù Yóu's recension "corrects and polishes" Mǎ Lìng's but Mǎ Lìng "nevertheless remains the more important of the two."
- **The Shǔ cluster** — [[KR2i0014]] *Jǐn-lǐ Qí-jiù Zhuàn* (970) and [[KR2i0016]] *Shǔ Táo-wù* (Zhāng Táng-yīng, *c.* 1064–1071) — the two principal Sòng-period chronicles of the Former and Latter Shǔ regimes; uniquely preserve the full *Guān-zhēn* 官箴 of [[孟昶]] Mèng Chǎng, from which the four-line *Jiè-shí* 戒石 admonition was drawn.
- *Sān-Chǔ Xīn-lù* 三楚新錄 [[KR2i0013]] — Zhōu Yǔ-chōng's early-Sòng chronicle of the three regional regimes of the Xiāng-Hàn basin (Mǎ-Chǔ, Wǔ-líng, Jīng-nán).
- *Wǔ-guó Gù-shì* 五國故事 [[KR2i0015]] — an anonymous early-Sòng *xiǎo-shuō*-style miscellany on the five southern Five-Dynasties regional regimes.
- *Wú-Yuè Bèi-shǐ* 吳越備史 [[KR2i0019]] — Qián Yǎn's internal Wú-Yuè dynastic history, written under false attribution to the junior officials Fàn Jǐong 范坰 and Lín Yǔ 林禹; the unique narrative source for the *Bǎo-jìng-ān-mín* 保境安民 ideology of the Qián family.
- *Shíguó Chūnqiū* 十國春秋 [[KR2i0021]] — Wú Rèn-chén's 114-juàn early-Qīng synthesis, the **definitive scholarly treatment** of the Ten Kingdoms. Drawing systematically on every Sòng-period regional history in this division (plus dozens of further sources, many lost), it pioneers the *zǐ-zhù* 子注 method of preserving alternative-source readings as sub-commentary. Wilkinson §61.8.1 #7 places it alongside the *Jiǔ-guó zhì* and the *Zī-zhì tōng-jiàn* as the principal references for the period.
- *Ān-nán Zhì-lüè* 安南志略 [[KR2i0020]] — Lí Trắc's 1340 gazetteer of Đại Việt, the **earliest extant systematic Chinese-language description of Vietnam**, written by a Trần-dynasty defector to the Yuán.
- *Cháo-xiān Shǐ-lüè* 朝鮮史略 [[KR2i0022]] and *Yuè-shǐ Lüè* 越史略 [[KR2i0023]] — the *fù-lù* 附錄 pair: a Joseon-period annalistic Korean history that reached China during the Imjin War, and an anonymous Trần-period Vietnamese chronicle (*Đại Việt sử lược* 大越史略) that, lost in Vietnam, survives only through Míng *Yǒng-lè*-era Chinese transmission.

## Important persons

- [[趙燁]] (Zhào Yè, *fl.* 40–80) — Later-Hàn historian of Wú-Yuè; founding figure of the regional-history genre.
- [[袁康]] (Yuán Kāng) and [[吳平]] (Wú Píng) — Later-Hàn Kuàijī co-compilers of the *Yuè Jué Shū*, whose identity is encoded in the riddling colophon of the work itself.
- [[徐天祐]] (Xú Tiān-yòu) — Yuán-period editor whose *yīn-zhù* on the *Wú Yuè Chūnqiū* (1306) is the standard commentary.
- [[常璩]] (Cháng Qú, *c.* 291–361) — *Sǎn-qí cháng-shì* of Chéng-Hàn, author of [[KR2i0003]]; the model for all later regional gazetteers.
- [[陸翽]] (Lù Huì, 4th c.) — Eastern-Jìn *Guó-zǐ zhù-jiào* whose lost two-juàn *Yè-zhōng jì* the *Sìkù* editors reconstructed.
- [[崔鴻]] (Cuī Hóng, 478–525) — Northern-Wèi historian whose 102-juàn *Shíliù-guó Chūnqiū* — long since lost — stands behind the recensions at [[KR2i0005]] and [[KR2i0006]] and the *Jìn-shū Zǎijì*.
- [[屠喬孫]] (Tú Qiáo-sūn) and [[項琳]] (Xiàng Lín) — late-Míng Jiā-xīng reconstructors of Cuī Hóng's *Shíliù-guó Chūnqiū* in the form transmitted as [[KR2i0005]].
- [[樊綽]] (Fán Chuò, *fl.* 860s) — Táng staff officer on the Annam frontier; author of the *Mán-shū* and witness to the Nán-zhào wars.
- [[陳彭年]] (Chén Péng-nián, 961–1017) — Sòng phonologist and *Jiāng-nán Bié-lù* author; his Southern-Táng connections give his account unique authority.
- [[鄭文寶]] (Zhèng Wén-bǎo, 953–1013) — Southern-Táng loyalist and pupil of [[徐鉉]], whose 1010 *Jiāng-biǎo Zhì* is one of the most authoritative Sòng-period Southern-Táng sources.
- [[龍袞]] (Lóng Gǔn) — early-Sòng compiler of the *Jiāng-nán Yě-shǐ*, the *locus classicus* for both the early-Sòng Southern-Táng tradition and for early-Sòng criticism of that tradition.
- [[句延慶]] (Jù Yán-qìng) — Sòng-period Sìchuān magistrate; author of [[KR2i0014]] in 970.
- [[周羽翀]] (Zhōu Yǔ-chōng) — early-Sòng junior official; author of [[KR2i0013]], whose "three Chǔ" framework had no Five-Dynasties precedent.
- [[張唐英]] (Zhāng Táng-yīng, 1029–1071) — Northern-Sòng *Diàn-zhōng shì-yù-shǐ* of the *Xī-níng* era and elder brother of the chancellor [[張商英]]; author of [[KR2i0016]].
- [[馬令]] (Mǎ Lìng, *fl.* 1105) — late-Northern-Sòng historian of Yí-xīng; grandson of [[馬元康]], a Jīn-líng man with deep Southern-Táng connections; author of the earlier of the two *Nán-Táng shū*.
- [[陸游]] (Lù Yóu, 1125–1210) — the great Southern-Sòng poet, whose late-life *Nán-Táng shū* (*c.* 1180–1209) employs *běn-jì* form for the Lǐ rulers in conscious resistance to Liú Zhī-jī's prescriptions — read by the *Sìkù* editors as a Southern-Sòng *piān-ān* 偏安 gesture.
- [[錢儼]] (Qián Yǎn, 937–1003) — Wú-Yuè prince, younger brother of the last ruler [[錢俶]] (929–988); actual author of the *Wú-Yuè Bèi-shǐ* under false attribution.
- [[黎崱]] (Lí Trắc, *fl.* 1300–1339) — Trần-dynasty Vietnamese aristocrat who surrendered to the Yuán with [[陳鍵]] Trần Kiện in 1287 and lived out his life at Hàn-yáng; author of [[KR2i0020]].
- [[吳任臣]] (Wú Rèn-chén, 1628?–1689?) — early-Qīng polymath of Rén-hé, summoned to the *bó-xué hóng-cí* exam of *Kāng-xī* 18 (1679); author of [[KR2i0021]], the great late-imperial synthesis.

## Topics

The conceptual unity of the division is given by the bibliographic-political category *zǎijì* itself — a recognition that the polities recorded here stand outside the *zhèng-tǒng* line and yet require historical treatment. Several recurring problems organize the corpus:

- **Legitimacy and the choice of historiographical form.** Whether a regional ruler receives *běn-jì*, *zhǔ*, or *zǎijì* treatment is the central editorial question. Cuī Hóng's original *Shíliù-guó Chūnqiū* (recorded by Liú Zhī-jī, *Shǐtōng*) dated events by Jìn / Sòng reign-names while writing as a Northern-Wèi official — a choice the *Sìkù* editors track through both the surviving Míng recensions. Lù Yóu's [[KR2i0018]] takes the opposite path, granting *běn-jì* to the Southern-Táng rulers in defiance of Liú Zhī-jī. Mǎ Lìng [[KR2i0017]] takes the middle path of *zhǔ*. The lost recension of [[胡恢]] Hú Huī used *zǎijì*, on the *Jìn-shū* model. Each choice is a thesis about legitimacy.

- **Forgery, recompilation, and *jí-yì* 輯佚 reconstruction.** A high proportion of the corpus survives only through scholarly recovery: [[KR2i0005]] is a learned Wàn-lì-era reconstruction of a Northern-Wèi original lost in the Northern Sòng; [[KR2i0004]] and [[KR2i0007]] were recovered from the *Yǒng-lè dà-diǎn* by the *Sìkù* editors; [[KR2i0012]] was recovered as a witness *to* [[KR2i0011]], which itself is partially mutilated. The category is therefore a sustained case-study in the methods and limits of late-imperial textual recovery.

- **Source-criticism and the *zǐ-zhù* method.** The early-Qīng *Shíguó Chūnqiū* [[KR2i0021]] is the methodological capstone of the category: Wú Rèn-chén embeds alternative-source readings as sub-commentary on the main narrative, anticipating modern critical-edition practice. The Southern-Táng cluster (six private histories that explicitly read and correct each other — [[KR2i0009]] criticized by [[KR2i0012]] criticized by [[KR2i0017]] / [[KR2i0018]]) is itself a model of cumulative source-criticism.

- **Regional ethnography and the *Yú-dì* 輿地 tradition.** Several works are as much regional gazetteer as dynastic history. The *Huá-yáng Guó-zhì* [[KR2i0003]] is the prototype; the *Mán-shū* [[KR2i0007]], the *Yè-zhōng jì* [[KR2i0004]], the early sections of the *Yuè Jué Shū* [[KR2i0002]], the *Ān-nán Zhì-lüè* [[KR2i0020]], and the *Sìchuān* and *Yún-nán* portions of [[KR2i0007]] all preserve ethnographic and linguistic material on non-Hàn populations not available elsewhere — Tibeto-Burman *Bái* and *Yí* lexica (*Mán-shū*), Yuè military-command transcriptions (*Yuè Jué Shū*), and Sìchuān-southwestern *Cóng* / *Pán-hú* / *Bīn* / *Sǒu* peoples (*Huá-yáng Guó-zhì*).

- **External histories and the limits of the Sinographic record.** The two *fù-lù* texts [[KR2i0022]] (Korea) and [[KR2i0023]] (Vietnam), together with the closely-paired [[KR2i0020]], extend the *zǎijì* logic to states that were never inside the empire. The *Sìkù* editors' decision to retain them — explicitly framed in the *tíyào* of [[KR2i0023]] on the analogy of the *Chūnqiū*'s recording of Wú and Chǔ — preserves uniquely Vietnamese era-names and Korean royal genealogies suppressed from the standard *Yuán-shǐ* / *Míng-shǐ* *wài-guó-zhuàn*.

- **The Southern-Táng historiographical tradition.** Seven of the 23 works (≈ 30 % of the division) treat the Southern Táng. The cluster forms the densest case-study in late-imperial Chinese historiography of source-critical evolution: an official history (Xú Xuàn / Tāng Yuè's *Jiāng-nán lù*, lost) generates a corrective tradition of private histories ([[KR2i0008]], [[KR2i0010]], [[KR2i0011]]), an alternative *yě-shǐ* tradition ([[KR2i0009]]), a Northern-Sòng meta-corrective ([[KR2i0012]]), two great Sòng *Nán-Táng shū* ([[KR2i0017]], [[KR2i0018]]), and an early-Qīng synthesis ([[KR2i0021]]).
