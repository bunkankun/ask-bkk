# KR2i 載記類 — Annals of NonLegitimate Regimes

## Scope and scholarly tradition

*Zǎijì* 載記 is the fourth-from-last of the fifteen *History* (史部) sub-categories in the *Sìkù quánshū zǒngmù* 四庫全書總目 (see Wilkinson §47 Table 89). The *Sìkù* editors borrowed the term from the *Jìnshū* 晉書, where Fáng Xuánlíng [[房玄齡]] had used it to house the basic annals of the fourteen of the *Wǔhú Shíliùguó* 五胡十六國 (Sixteen Kingdoms of the Five Barbarians) that the Táng court refused to grant *běnjì* 本紀 status. *Zǎijì* in turn replaces the earlier bibliographic labels *bàshǐ* 霸史 ("histories of hegemons" — used since the *Suízhì* 隋志) and the still earlier *wěishǐ* 偽史 ("false histories" — from Ruǎn Xiàoxù's 阮孝緒 *Qīlù* 七錄). The euphemism softens with each change, but the underlying judgement is constant: these are histories of states the central tradition did *not* recognize as standing in the orthodox dynastic succession (*zhèngtǒng* 正統). In the *Sìkù*, the rubric covers 23 works (Wilkinson §47, Table 89), and the corpus that follows here is exactly that *Sìkù* set.

The chronological reach of the category is unusually wide. The two earliest works are Later-Hàn narratives of the Spring-and-Autumn-period *Wú* 吳 and *Yuè* 越 ([[KR2i0001]], [[KR2i0002]]); the latest are early-Qīng. In between sit the major regional histories of every era of division — the Sixteen Kingdoms of the 4th–5th centuries ([[KR2i0005]], [[KR2i0006]]), the Sìchuān gazetteer *Huáyáng Guózhì* [[KR2i0003]], the HòuZhào *Yèzhōng jì* [[KR2i0004]], the *Mánshū* on Nánzhào [[KR2i0007]], the dense cluster of Five-Dynasties / Ten-Kingdoms regional histories ([[KR2i0008]]–[[KR2i0019]]), the early-Qīng synthesis of the Ten Kingdoms ([[KR2i0021]]), and finally a *fùlù* 附錄 of two East-Asian histories (Korea [[KR2i0022]], Vietnam [[KR2i0023]] and the related Vietnamese gazetteer [[KR2i0020]]) — works on Sinographic states beyond the Chinese empire proper, retained by the *Sìkù* editors precisely on the principle that they too document polities outside the legitimate succession. The transmissional history is heavy with loss and reconstruction: a significant share of the corpus survives only through *Sìkù*-editor recovery from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* 永樂大典 (notably [[KR2i0004]], [[KR2i0007]], [[KR2i0012]]), late-Míng learned re-compilation (the 100-juàn [[KR2i0005]] is a Tú Qiáosūn / Xiàng Lín reconstruction of Cuī Hóng's lost original), or single-line Chinese transmission of foreign-origin texts ([[KR2i0023]], lost in Vietnam itself).

## Important texts and text clusters

- *Wú Yuè Chūnqiū* 吳越春秋 [[KR2i0001]] and *Yuè Jué Shū* 越絕書 [[KR2i0002]] — the two great Later-Hàn narratives of the WúYuè rivalry, founding works of the *bàshǐ* / *zǎijì* category and, along with the *Zhànguócè*, the earliest non-canonical histories with a clear regional focus.
- *Huáyáng Guózhì* 華陽國志 [[KR2i0003]] — Cháng Qú's *c.* 348–354 gazetteer of BāShǔHànzhōngNánzhōng, **the earliest regional history to survive intact** (Wilkinson §60.3) and the source standing behind Lì Dàoyuán's *Shuǐjīng zhù* and Lè Shǐ's *Tàipíng huányǔ jì* for everything on the southwest.
- *Yèzhōng jì* 鄴中記 [[KR2i0004]] — the unique pre-Táng witness to HòuZhào Yè under Shí Hǔ; the Tóngquè, Bīngjǐng, and Jīnfèng tái complexes are described nowhere else.
- The Sixteen Kingdoms pair, [[KR2i0005]] (100-juàn Wànlì recompilation of Cuī Hóng's lost original by Tú Qiáosūn 屠喬孫 and Xiàng Lín 項琳) and [[KR2i0006]] (16-juàn *HànWèi cóngshū* recension) — paired witnesses to a Northern-Wèi original lost in the Northern Sòng; together with the *Jìnshū Zǎijì* and Tāng Qiú's 湯球 *Shíliùguó Chūnqiū jíbǔ* they form the modern evidence-base for the period 304–439.
- *Mánshū* 蠻書 [[KR2i0007]] — Fán Chuò's 862–864 ethnographic-strategic treatise on Nánzhào, the principal source behind Sòng Qí's *Nánmánzhuàn* in the *XīnTángshū* and Sīmǎ Guāng's *Tōngjiàn* on Yúnnán; preserves the earliest external lexica of Tibeto-Burman *Bái* and *Yí*.
- **The Southern Táng cluster** — [[KR2i0008]] *Diàojī Lìtán*, [[KR2i0009]] *Jiāngnán Yěshǐ*, [[KR2i0010]] *Jiāngnán Biélù*, [[KR2i0011]] *Jiāngbiǎo Zhì*, [[KR2i0012]] *Jiāngnán Yúzǎi*, [[KR2i0017]] *Mǎshì NánTáng Shū*, [[KR2i0018]] *Lùshì NánTáng Shū*. This is the densest sub-cluster of the division — six privately-compiled early-Sòng witnesses (most by men with personal ties to the conquered court) plus the two great Sòng *NánTáng shū* recensions by [[馬令]] (1105) and [[陸游]] (*c.* 1180–1209). Wilkinson §61.8.1 #5 notes that Lù Yóu's recension "corrects and polishes" Mǎ Lìng's but Mǎ Lìng "nevertheless remains the more important of the two."
- **The Shǔ cluster** — [[KR2i0014]] *Jǐnlǐ Qíjiù Zhuàn* (970) and [[KR2i0016]] *Shǔ Táowù* (Zhāng Tángyīng, *c.* 1064–1071) — the two principal Sòng-period chronicles of the Former and Latter Shǔ regimes; uniquely preserve the full *Guānzhēn* 官箴 of [[孟昶]] Mèng Chǎng, from which the four-line *Jièshí* 戒石 admonition was drawn.
- *SānChǔ Xīnlù* 三楚新錄 [[KR2i0013]] — Zhōu Yǔchōng's early-Sòng chronicle of the three regional regimes of the XiāngHàn basin (MǎChǔ, Wǔlíng, Jīngnán).
- *Wǔguó Gùshì* 五國故事 [[KR2i0015]] — an anonymous early-Sòng *xiǎoshuō*-style miscellany on the five southern Five-Dynasties regional regimes.
- *WúYuè Bèishǐ* 吳越備史 [[KR2i0019]] — Qián Yǎn's internal WúYuè dynastic history, written under false attribution to the junior officials Fàn Jǐong 范坰 and Lín Yǔ 林禹; the unique narrative source for the *Bǎojìngānmín* 保境安民 ideology of the Qián family.
- *Shíguó Chūnqiū* 十國春秋 [[KR2i0021]] — Wú Rènchén's 114-juàn early-Qīng synthesis, the **definitive scholarly treatment** of the Ten Kingdoms. Drawing systematically on every Sòng-period regional history in this division (plus dozens of further sources, many lost), it pioneers the *zǐzhù* 子注 method of preserving alternative-source readings as sub-commentary. Wilkinson §61.8.1 #7 places it alongside the *Jiǔguó zhì* and the *Zīzhì tōngjiàn* as the principal references for the period.
- *Ānnán Zhìlüè* 安南志略 [[KR2i0020]] — Lí Trắc's 1340 gazetteer of Đại Việt, the **earliest extant systematic Chinese-language description of Vietnam**, written by a Trần-dynasty defector to the Yuán.
- *Cháoxiān Shǐlüè* 朝鮮史略 [[KR2i0022]] and *Yuèshǐ Lüè* 越史略 [[KR2i0023]] — the *fùlù* 附錄 pair: a Joseon-period annalistic Korean history that reached China during the Imjin War, and an anonymous Trần-period Vietnamese chronicle (*Đại Việt sử lược* 大越史略) that, lost in Vietnam, survives only through Míng *Yǒnglè*-era Chinese transmission.

## Important persons

- [[趙燁]] (Zhào Yè, *fl.* 40–80) — Later-Hàn historian of WúYuè; founding figure of the regional-history genre.
- [[袁康]] (Yuán Kāng) and [[吳平]] (Wú Píng) — Later-Hàn Kuàijī co-compilers of the *Yuè Jué Shū*, whose identity is encoded in the riddling colophon of the work itself.
- [[徐天祐]] (Xú Tiānyòu) — Yuán-period editor whose *yīnzhù* on the *Wú Yuè Chūnqiū* (1306) is the standard commentary.
- [[常璩]] (Cháng Qú, *c.* 291–361) — *Sǎnqí chángshì* of ChéngHàn, author of [[KR2i0003]]; the model for all later regional gazetteers.
- [[陸翽]] (Lù Huì, 4th c.) — Eastern-Jìn *Guózǐ zhùjiào* whose lost two-juàn *Yèzhōng jì* the *Sìkù* editors reconstructed.
- [[崔鴻]] (Cuī Hóng, 478–525) — Northern-Wèi historian whose 102-juàn *Shíliùguó Chūnqiū* — long since lost — stands behind the recensions at [[KR2i0005]] and [[KR2i0006]] and the *Jìnshū Zǎijì*.
- [[屠喬孫]] (Tú Qiáosūn) and [[項琳]] (Xiàng Lín) — late-Míng Jiāxīng reconstructors of Cuī Hóng's *Shíliùguó Chūnqiū* in the form transmitted as [[KR2i0005]].
- [[樊綽]] (Fán Chuò, *fl.* 860s) — Táng staff officer on the Annam frontier; author of the *Mánshū* and witness to the Nánzhào wars.
- [[陳彭年]] (Chén Péngnián, 961–1017) — Sòng phonologist and *Jiāngnán Biélù* author; his Southern-Táng connections give his account unique authority.
- [[鄭文寶]] (Zhèng Wénbǎo, 953–1013) — Southern-Táng loyalist and pupil of [[徐鉉]], whose 1010 *Jiāngbiǎo Zhì* is one of the most authoritative Sòng-period Southern-Táng sources.
- [[龍袞]] (Lóng Gǔn) — early-Sòng compiler of the *Jiāngnán Yěshǐ*, the *locus classicus* for both the early-Sòng Southern-Táng tradition and for early-Sòng criticism of that tradition.
- [[句延慶]] (Jù Yánqìng) — Sòng-period Sìchuān magistrate; author of [[KR2i0014]] in 970.
- [[周羽翀]] (Zhōu Yǔchōng) — early-Sòng junior official; author of [[KR2i0013]], whose "three Chǔ" framework had no Five-Dynasties precedent.
- [[張唐英]] (Zhāng Tángyīng, 1029–1071) — Northern-Sòng *Diànzhōng shìyùshǐ* of the *Xīníng* era and elder brother of the chancellor [[張商英]]; author of [[KR2i0016]].
- [[馬令]] (Mǎ Lìng, *fl.* 1105) — late-Northern-Sòng historian of Yíxīng; grandson of [[馬元康]], a Jīnlíng man with deep Southern-Táng connections; author of the earlier of the two *NánTáng shū*.
- [[陸游]] (Lù Yóu, 1125–1210) — the great Southern-Sòng poet, whose late-life *NánTáng shū* (*c.* 1180–1209) employs *běnjì* form for the Lǐ rulers in conscious resistance to Liú Zhījī's prescriptions — read by the *Sìkù* editors as a Southern-Sòng *piānān* 偏安 gesture.
- [[錢儼]] (Qián Yǎn, 937–1003) — WúYuè prince, younger brother of the last ruler [[錢俶]] (929–988); actual author of the *WúYuè Bèishǐ* under false attribution.
- [[黎崱]] (Lí Trắc, *fl.* 1300–1339) — Trần-dynasty Vietnamese aristocrat who surrendered to the Yuán with [[陳鍵]] Trần Kiện in 1287 and lived out his life at Hànyáng; author of [[KR2i0020]].
- [[吳任臣]] (Wú Rènchén, 1628?–1689?) — early-Qīng polymath of Rénhé, summoned to the *bóxué hóngcí* exam of *Kāngxī* 18 (1679); author of [[KR2i0021]], the great late-imperial synthesis.

## Topics

The conceptual unity of the division is given by the bibliographic-political category *zǎijì* itself — a recognition that the polities recorded here stand outside the *zhèngtǒng* line and yet require historical treatment. Several recurring problems organize the corpus:

- **Legitimacy and the choice of historiographical form.** Whether a regional ruler receives *běnjì*, *zhǔ*, or *zǎijì* treatment is the central editorial question. Cuī Hóng's original *Shíliùguó Chūnqiū* (recorded by Liú Zhījī, *Shǐtōng*) dated events by Jìn / Sòng reign-names while writing as a Northern-Wèi official — a choice the *Sìkù* editors track through both the surviving Míng recensions. Lù Yóu's [[KR2i0018]] takes the opposite path, granting *běnjì* to the Southern-Táng rulers in defiance of Liú Zhījī. Mǎ Lìng [[KR2i0017]] takes the middle path of *zhǔ*. The lost recension of [[胡恢]] Hú Huī used *zǎijì*, on the *Jìnshū* model. Each choice is a thesis about legitimacy.

- **Forgery, recompilation, and *jíyì* 輯佚 reconstruction.** A high proportion of the corpus survives only through scholarly recovery: [[KR2i0005]] is a learned Wàn-lì-era reconstruction of a Northern-Wèi original lost in the Northern Sòng; [[KR2i0004]] and [[KR2i0007]] were recovered from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* by the *Sìkù* editors; [[KR2i0012]] was recovered as a witness *to* [[KR2i0011]], which itself is partially mutilated. The category is therefore a sustained case-study in the methods and limits of late-imperial textual recovery.

- **Source-criticism and the *zǐzhù* method.** The early-Qīng *Shíguó Chūnqiū* [[KR2i0021]] is the methodological capstone of the category: Wú Rènchén embeds alternative-source readings as sub-commentary on the main narrative, anticipating modern critical-edition practice. The Southern-Táng cluster (six private histories that explicitly read and correct each other — [[KR2i0009]] criticized by [[KR2i0012]] criticized by [[KR2i0017]] / [[KR2i0018]]) is itself a model of cumulative source-criticism.

- **Regional ethnography and the *Yúdì* 輿地 tradition.** Several works are as much regional gazetteer as dynastic history. The *Huáyáng Guózhì* [[KR2i0003]] is the prototype; the *Mánshū* [[KR2i0007]], the *Yèzhōng jì* [[KR2i0004]], the early sections of the *Yuè Jué Shū* [[KR2i0002]], the *Ānnán Zhìlüè* [[KR2i0020]], and the *Sìchuān* and *Yúnnán* portions of [[KR2i0007]] all preserve ethnographic and linguistic material on non-Hàn populations not available elsewhere — Tibeto-Burman *Bái* and *Yí* lexica (*Mánshū*), Yuè military-command transcriptions (*Yuè Jué Shū*), and Sìchuān-southwestern *Cóng* / *Pánhú* / *Bīn* / *Sǒu* peoples (*Huáyáng Guózhì*).

- **External histories and the limits of the Sinographic record.** The two *fùlù* texts [[KR2i0022]] (Korea) and [[KR2i0023]] (Vietnam), together with the closely-paired [[KR2i0020]], extend the *zǎijì* logic to states that were never inside the empire. The *Sìkù* editors' decision to retain them — explicitly framed in the *tíyào* of [[KR2i0023]] on the analogy of the *Chūnqiū*'s recording of Wú and Chǔ — preserves uniquely Vietnamese era-names and Korean royal genealogies suppressed from the standard *Yuánshǐ* / *Míngshǐ* *wàiguózhuàn*.

- **The Southern-Táng historiographical tradition.** Seven of the 23 works (≈ 30 % of the division) treat the Southern Táng. The cluster forms the densest case-study in late-imperial Chinese historiography of source-critical evolution: an official history (Xú Xuàn / Tāng Yuè's *Jiāngnán lù*, lost) generates a corrective tradition of private histories ([[KR2i0008]], [[KR2i0010]], [[KR2i0011]]), an alternative *yěshǐ* tradition ([[KR2i0009]]), a Northern-Sòng meta-corrective ([[KR2i0012]]), two great Sòng *NánTáng shū* ([[KR2i0017]], [[KR2i0018]]), and an early-Qīng synthesis ([[KR2i0021]]).
