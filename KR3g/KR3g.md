# KR3g 術數類 — Mantic Arts

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3g sits inside the *Sìkù* Masters Branch (子部) as the *Shùshù lèi* 術數類 — the "Numerical-and-Mantic Arts" — and gathers the works the Qiánlóng editors classed as technical divination, cosmological numerology, geomancy, astrology, fate-calculation, physiognomy, and hemerology. Following Wilkinson (§29.3 on *fāngshù* 方術, §33.1 on fengshui, §39.16 on the Five Agents and the calendar, §56.5 on Sòng cosmology, §57.3 and §58.3.1 on divinatory writing and the oracle bones, §73.2.2 on the *sìbù* classification; cf. *Box 348* on the Masters Branch subdivisions), this is the oldest continuously catalogued non-canonical Chinese genre: the *Hànshū Yìwén zhì* "Shùshù lüè" 數術略 already articulated six subdivisions (*tiānwén* 天文, *lìpǔ* 曆譜, *wǔxíng* 五行, *shīguī* 蓍龜, *zázhàn* 雜占, *xíngfǎ* 形法), and the *Sìkù* *Shùshù lèi* preserves substantially the same six-fold map: 數學 (numerological cosmology) — 占候 (celestial-portent astrology) — 相宅相墓 (residence- and tomb-siting / fengshui) — 占卜 (divination proper) — 命書相書 (fate-calculation and physiognomy) — 陰陽五行 (yīnyáng five-phase divination and almanac-making). The 52 works collected here cover all six.

The chronological span runs from the late Western Hàn (Jiāo Yánshòu's *Yìlín* [[KR3g0029]] and Jīng Fáng's *Yìzhuàn* [[KR3g0030]] in the second half of the 1st century BCE, and Yáng Xióng's *Tàixuán jīng* [[KR3g0001]] c. 2–4 CE) to the high Qiánlóng court (*Qīndìng Xiéjì biànfāng shū* [[KR3g0051]], promulgated 1741, and Wáng Zhí's *Huángjí jīngshì shū jiě* [[KR3g0010]], 1721–67). Three transmissional traditions structure the reading. (1) **The *xiàngshù* 象數 *Yìjīng* lineage**, from the Hàn *guàqì* (hexagram-pneuma) school of Jiāo Yánshòu and Jīng Fáng through Yáng Xióng's alternative-cosmology *Tàixuán*, the Sòng cosmological systems of Shào Yōng (*Huángjí jīngshì*) and Sīmǎ Guāng (*Qiánxū* [[KR3g0004]]), and the *Shàoshì* school of Zhāng Xíngchéng [[KR3g0006]]–[[KR3g0008]] and Zhù Mì [[KR3g0009]]. (2) **The geomantic / *kānyú* 堪輿 lineage**, which the *Sìkù* editors treat as Táng-period in its received textual form despite its pseudepigraphic attributions to the Yellow Emperor and Jìn-period Guō Pú, and which crystallises around the Táng-end / Five-Dynasties *xíngshì* 形勢 trilogy ascribed to Yáng Yúnsōng [[KR3g0021]], [[KR3g0023]], [[KR3g0024]]. (3) **The late-imperial *xīngmìng* 星命 / *zǐpíng* 子平 fate-calculation tradition**, whose proximate SòngYuánMíng synthesis is consolidated in Wàn Mínyīng's twin compendia of 1578 — *Xīngxué dàchéng* [[KR3g0041]] and *Sānmìng tōnghuì* [[KR3g0042]] — and which Wilkinson (§39.16 and *Box 354*) singles out as the canonical late-imperial fate-calculation reference set. Across all three the editors' principal hermeneutic problem is the same: how to preserve a technically valuable but doctrinally peripheral body of work without endorsing its *fāngshì* 方士 pretensions, which the *tíyào* address case by case with a characteristic mixture of textual-critical scepticism and documentary respect.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The Hàn alternative-cosmology cluster.** Yáng Xióng's *Tàixuán jīng* 太玄經 [[KR3g0001]] (c. 2–4 CE, with Fàn Wàng's Jìn-period commentary) is the most ambitious systematic Hàn-period alternative to the *Yìjīng*'s 8-fold structure, organising cosmology on a 9-fold (3⁴) scheme keyed to the 729-day periodicity of the *Tàichū* calendar. Yè Zǐqí's *Tàixuán běnzhǐ* 太玄本旨 [[KR3g0002]] (Míng, 1368–90) is the principal late-imperial commentary. The North-Zhōu monk Wèi Yuánsōng's *Yuánbāo jīngzhuàn* 元包經傳 [[KR3g0003]] (c. 560–575, with Táng commentary by Sū Yuánmíng and Lǐ Jiāng and Sòng supplementary apparatus by Zhāng Xíngchéng) carries the alternative-*Yì* cosmology forward through the SuíTáng transition. Sīmǎ Guāng's *Qiánxū* 潛虛 [[KR3g0004]] (c. 1080–86, with Zhāng Dūnshí's *Qiánxū fāwēi lùn* supplement) is the Sòng-imperial-historian's own *Tàixuán*-modelled cosmological-numerical treatise.

**The Shào Yōng cosmological-historical system and its school.** Shào Yōng's *Huángjí jīngshì shū* 皇極經世書 [[KR3g0005]] (c. 1050–77) is, by Wilkinson's measure (§56.5.4), the most ambitious systematic cosmological-historical synthesis in pre-modern Chinese intellectual history — a 129,600-year *yuánhuìyùnshì* 元會運世 nested temporal scheme combining hexagram-based history with the *guānwù* 觀物 ("observing-things") philosophical method, by one of the *Wǔzǐ* 五子 Founders of Northern-Sòng *Lǐxué*. The *Shàoshì* school is represented by Zhāng Xíngchéng's three works — *Huángjí jīngshì suǒyǐn* [[KR3g0006]], *Huángjí jīngshì guānwù wàipiān yǎnyì* [[KR3g0007]], and the massive 40-juan *Yì tōngbiàn* [[KR3g0008]] (all 1132–66); Zhù Mì's *Guānwù piān jiě* [[KR3g0009]] (1235–41) with its appended *Qǐshù jué*; and, on the Qīng side, Wáng Zhí's *Huángjí jīngshì shū jiě* [[KR3g0010]] (1721–67), the standard late-imperial Cheng-Zhu commentary. Wáng Shí's *Yìxué* [[KR3g0011]] (1127–60) is a related Southern-Sòng treatment.

**The SòngYuán cosmological-numerical supplement.** Cài Shěn's *Hóngfàn huángjí nèipiān* 洪範皇極內篇 [[KR3g0012]] (1200–30) extends the *Hóngfàn* 洪範 / *Hétú* / *Luòshū* numerological tradition Wilkinson treats at §39.11; Bào Yúnlóng's *Tiānyuán fāwēi* [[KR3g0013]] (c. 1280–1310), Dīng Yìdōng's *Dàyǎn suǒyǐn* [[KR3g0014]] (c. 1280–1310) on the *dàyǎn* divinatory number, and Zhāng Lǐ's Yuán-period *Yìxiàng túshuō* [[KR3g0015]] (1300–40) round out this layer. Huáng Dàozhōu's late-Míng *Sānyì dòngjī* 三易洞璣 [[KR3g0016]] (1620–46) is the closing major contribution of the *xiàngshù* cluster, by a *Donglin*-adjacent statesman-scholar.

**The Táng celestial-portent corpus.** Yú Jìcái's *Língtái mìyuàn* 靈臺祕苑 [[KR3g0017]] (originally BěiZhōu, c. 560, re-edited by Wáng Ānlǐ for the Sòng court c. 1085) and Qútán Xīdá's *Táng Kāiyuán zhànjīng* 唐開元占經 [[KR3g0018]] (718–29) are the two principal pre-Sòng Chinese astral-divination encyclopaedias. The *Kāiyuán zhànjīng* is, in addition, the principal surviving compilation of HànWèi *chènwěi* 讖緯 quotation-fragments and the conduit for the first Indian *Navagraha*-calendar (*Jiǔzhí lì* 九執曆) reaching the Chinese imperial astronomical bureau — a transmission Wilkinson registers at §39.16.

**The *xiàngzhái xiàngmù* fengshui cluster.** Nine consecutive works form the *Sìkù*'s geomantic subdivision: the residence-siting *Zháijīng* 宅經 [[KR3g0019]] (pseudepigraphic *Huángdì*, Táng received form), the tomb-siting *Zàngshū* 葬書 [[KR3g0020]] (pseudepigraphic Guō Pú, in fact Sòng-period — translated by Zhang Juwen and identified by Wilkinson §33.1 as the key early text linking fengshui to Confucianism), and the TángSòng *xíngshì* 形勢 ("form-and-configuration") trilogy ascribed to Yáng Yúnsōng — *Hànlóng jīng* 撼龍經 [[KR3g0021]], *Qīngnáng àoyǔ* 青囊奧語 [[KR3g0023]], and *Tiānyù jīng* 天玉經 [[KR3g0024]] — supplemented by Zēng Wénshàn's *Qīngnáng xù* 青囊序 [[KR3g0022]]. The *lǐqì* 理氣 ("principle-and-pneuma") counterpart is supplied by Hé Pǔ's *Língchéng jīngyì* 靈城精義 [[KR3g0025]] (Five-Dynasties / SòngYuán recension) and Lài Wénjùn's *Cuīguān piān* 催官篇 [[KR3g0026]] (Sòng, c. 1150–1250). Cài Yuándìng's *Fāwēi lùn* 發微論 [[KR3g0027]] (1170–98) — by the Sòng Cheng-Zhu master who is also the father of Cài Shěn — supplies the philosophically-rigorised Northern-Sòng Cheng-Zhu reading of geomancy. Together this is the canonical Chinese geomantic primary-source set, treated comprehensively in Paton's *Five Classics of Fengshui* (2013).

**The divination-method (*zhānbǔ*) cluster.** The *Língqí jīng* 靈棋經 [[KR3g0028]] (pseudepigraphic Dōngfāng Shuò, in fact Six-Dynasties received form; with Liú Jī's late-Yuán / early-Míng commentary) is the foundational text of *qíbǔ* counter-divination. Jiāo Yánshòu's *Yìlín* 焦氏易林 [[KR3g0029]] (c. 75–50 BCE) and Jīng Fáng's *Yìzhuàn* 京氏易傳 [[KR3g0030]] (c. 50–37 BCE) are the two foundational Western-Hàn *guàqì* *Yìjīng*-divinatory works: 4,096 four-character verses for every hexagram-pair in the first, and the *bāgōng guà* 八宮卦 (eight-palace hexagram) re-organisation of the *Yìjīng* in the second. The Míng *Liùrén dàquán* 六壬大全 [[KR3g0031]] is the principal late-imperial *Liùrén* 六壬 divinatory reference, and Hú Xù's *Bǔfǎ xiángkǎo* 卜法詳考 [[KR3g0032]] (early Qīng) is the principal Cheng-Zhu-loyalist textual-historical examination of plastromantic and shī-yarrow divination, which Wilkinson (§57.3, §58.3.1) treats as the deep historical origin of Chinese writing itself.

**The *xīngmìng* / *zǐpíng* fate-calculation cluster.** The principal pseudepigraphic foundational texts are *Lǐ Xūzhōng mìngshū* 李虛中命書 [[KR3g0033]] (in fact Sòng-period, attributed via Guǐgǔzǐ to the Táng *xīngmìng* practitioner whose practice Hán Yù records) and the *Yùzhào dìngzhēn jīng* [[KR3g0034]] (pseudepigraphic Guō Pú, Sòng received form). *Xīngmìng sùyuán* [[KR3g0035]] is the principal late-Míng anthology. Two Sòng-period commentaries on the foundational *Luòlùzǐ* divinatory verses occupy [[KR3g0036]] (Tánying, 1127) and [[KR3g0037]] (Xú Zǐpíng — *the* foundational figure of the *zǐpíng* 子平 / eight-character system, c. 950–1100). *Sānmìng zhǐmí fù* [[KR3g0038]] and the Liáo-attributed *Xīngmìng zǒngkuò* 星命總括 [[KR3g0039]] (Yēlǜ Chún) complete the early layer. *Yǎnqín tōngzuǎn* 演禽通纂 [[KR3g0040]] introduces the *qínxīng* 禽星 ("bird-star") sub-system. Wàn Mínyīng's two monumental compendia of 1578 — *Xīngxué dàchéng* 星學大成 in 30 juan [[KR3g0041]] and *Sānmìng tōnghuì* 三命通會 in 12 juan [[KR3g0042]] — are, as Wilkinson notes at §39.16, "the most important primary source on fate calculation compiled in the later empire" and the universally-consulted late-imperial popular-divination reference ("almost 'every house has it'", per the *tíyào*).

**The physiognomy (*xiàngshù*) cluster.** *Yuèbō dòngzhōng jì* [[KR3g0043]] (anonymous, Five-Dynasties / Sòng), *Yùguǎn zhàoshén jú* 玉管照神局 [[KR3g0044]] (attributed to the Southern-Tang minister Sòng Qíqiū, c. 940–1000), and *Tàiqīng shénjiàn* 太清神鑑 [[KR3g0045]] (Five-Dynasties / Sòng) form the early physiognomic layer. Zhāng Xíngjiǎn's Jīn-period *Rénlún dàtǒng fù* 人倫大統賦 [[KR3g0046]] (with Xuē Yánnián's Yuán-period annotation) is, as the *tíyào* notes, one of the few surviving substantive Jīn-Yuán-period authored works in the entire division.

**The *yīnyáng wǔxíng* / *Sānshì* / hemerological cluster.** Wáng Xīmíng's Táng-period *Tàiyǐ jīnjìng shìjīng* 太乙金鏡式經 [[KR3g0047]] (720–41) is the principal received reference for the *Tàiyǐ* 太乙 cosmographic divinatory system; Chéng Dàoshēng's late-Míng *Dùnjiǎ yǎnyì* 遁甲演義 [[KR3g0048]] is the principal received reference for the *Dùnjiǎ* 遁甲 system; with the *Liùrén dàquán* [[KR3g0031]] these three form the canonical *Sānshì* 三式 triplet. Chí Běnlǐ's *Qínxīng yìjiàn* 禽星易見 [[KR3g0049]] (late Míng) is the *qínxīng* extension. The two Qīng imperial *xuǎnzé* 選擇 (auspicious-day-selection) compilations close the division: the Kāngxī-era *Yùdìng Xīnglì kǎoyuán* 御定星歷考原 [[KR3g0050]] (1713, by Lǐ Guāngdì) in 6 juan, and the Qián-lóng-era 36-juan *Qīndìng Xiéjì biànfāng shū* 欽定協紀辨方書 [[KR3g0051]] (1739–41, by Yǔnlù), the comprehensive imperial *xuǎnzé* reference of the entire late-imperial period. As an inserted addition the division also holds Xiāo Jí's Suí-period *Wǔxíng dàyì* 五行大義 [[KR3g0052]] (c. 581–614) — preserved by Japanese transmission and the principal pre-Táng surviving compendium of *yīnyáng wǔxíng* correlative cosmology, edited by Marc Kalinowski (EFEO 1991).

- [[庾季才]] — BěiZhōu astronomer-astrologer, original compiler of *Língtái mìyuàn* [[KR3g0017]]; pivotal figure in the institutional continuity of Chinese state astrology across the Northern-Dynasties / Suí transition.
- [[瞿曇悉達]] — Táng court astronomer of Indian-Buddhist descent (Gautama Siddhārtha), compiler of *Táng Kāiyuán zhànjīng* [[KR3g0018]] (718–29); the conduit for the first systematic Indian astronomical transmission into the Chinese imperial astronomical bureau.
- [[王希明]] — Táng court astronomical-divinatory official, author of *Tàiyǐ jīnjìng shìjīng* [[KR3g0047]]; principal received-text source for the *Tàiyǐ* divinatory system.
- [[楊筠松]] — late-Táng / Five-Dynasties geomancer ("Yáng who saves the poor"), traditional author of the *xíngshì* trilogy [[KR3g0021]], [[KR3g0023]], [[KR3g0024]]; the canonical patriarch of the *xíngshì* (form-and-configuration) school of fengshui.
- [[徐子平]] — Sòng *xīngmìng* technician (c. 950–1100), author of *Luòlùzǐ sānmìng xiāoxī fù zhù* [[KR3g0037]]; the eponymous founder of the *zǐpíng* eight-character fate-calculation system that became standard in late-imperial popular practice.
- [[邵雍]] — Northern-Sòng cosmologist (1011–77), one of the *Wǔzǐ* 五子 founders of *Lǐxué*; author of *Huángjí jīngshì shū* [[KR3g0005]] and the originating figure of the *Shàoshì* cosmological-historical school.
- [[司馬光]] — Northern-Sòng historian and statesman (1019–86), author of *Qiánxū* [[KR3g0004]]; the imperial-historian's own *Tàixuán*-modelled cosmological-numerical exercise, signalling the Northern-Sòng *xiàngshù* revival.
- [[張行成]] — Southern-Sòng cosmologist (fl. 1132–66), author of [[KR3g0006]]–[[KR3g0008]] and the *Yuánbāo* supplement; the principal systematiser of Shào Yōng's cosmological apparatus.
- [[蔡元定]] — Southern-Sòng Cheng-Zhu master (1135–98), author of *Fāwēi lùn* [[KR3g0027]]; provides the philosophically-rigorised Cheng-Zhu reading of geomancy.
- [[蔡沈]] — Southern-Sòng cosmologist (1167–1230), son of Cài Yuándìng, author of *Hóngfàn huángjí nèipiān* [[KR3g0012]]; principal late-Sòng synthesist of the *Hóngfàn* / *Hétú* numerological tradition.
- [[黃道周]] — late-Míng statesman and Donglin-adjacent scholar (1585–1646), author of *Sānyì dòngjī* [[KR3g0016]]; the most substantial Míng contribution to the *xiàngshù* tradition, by a famous loyalist martyr.
- [[萬民英]] — Míng *xīngmìng* synthesist (fl. 1550–1600), author of *Xīngxué dàchéng* [[KR3g0041]] and *Sānmìng tōnghuì* [[KR3g0042]]; produced the canonical late-imperial fate-calculation reference set.
- [[程道生]] — late-Míng *yīnyáng wǔxíng* technician, author of *Dùnjiǎ yǎnyì* [[KR3g0048]]; principal received-text source for the *Dùnjiǎ* divinatory system.
- [[李光地]] — Kāngxī-court Cheng-Zhu loyalist (1642–1718), editorial director of *Yùdìng Xīnglì kǎoyuán* [[KR3g0050]]; also editor of foundational Qīng imperial cosmological-mathematical works in [[KR3f]] and of the imperial *Lǐxué* canon in [[KR3a]] — his presence here marks the imperial-rationalisation of *xuǎnzé* practice.
- [[允祿]] — Qián-lóng-period imperial prince (Zhuāng qīnwáng, 1695–1767), editor-in-chief of *Qīndìng Xiéjì biànfāng shū* [[KR3g0051]]; consolidates the imperial *xuǎnzé* reference for the high Qīng.
- [[祝泌]] — late-Sòng cosmologist (fl. c. 1235–1241), author of *Guānwù piān jiě* [[KR3g0009]]; one of the major *Shàoshì*-school transmitters bridging into the Yuán.
- [[郭璞]] — Jìn-period polymath (276–324), pseudepigraphic author of *Zàngshū* [[KR3g0020]] and *Yùzhào dìngzhēn jīng* [[KR3g0034]]; canonical foundational figure of the geomantic tradition despite the textually-late character of the works attributed to him.

## Topics

The principal subjects, debates, and interpretive problems running through the division are these:

1. **The *xiàngshù* 象數 versus *yìlǐ* 義理 *Yìjīng* split.** The Hàn *xiàngshù* school — represented here by Jiāo Yánshòu, Jīng Fáng, and Yáng Xióng's alternative-cosmology *Tàixuán* — was the *Sìkù* editors' principal reason for classifying these texts in the *Shùshù lèi* rather than [[KR1a]] *Yìlèi*. The Sòng cosmological-numerological revival under Shào Yōng and the *Shàoshì* school re-anchored *xiàngshù* within the *Lǐxué* synthesis, and the editors' placement of Shào Yōng's *Huángjí jīngshì* here rather than in the [[KR1a]] *Yì* division is a deliberate judgement, justified in the *tíyào* by Shào Yōng's cosmological-temporal scheme being structurally distinct from canonical *Yìjīng* exegesis. See Smith, Bol, Adler, and Wyatt, *Sung Dynasty Uses of the I Ching* (1990).

2. **The Hàn *chènwěi* 讖緯 inheritance.** The Hàn apocryphal-classical corpus — extensively excerpted in *Tāng Kāiyuán zhànjīng* [[KR3g0018]] and *Wǔxíng dàyì* [[KR3g0052]] — is the deep substratum of the entire division. Many *wěi* texts survive only through such anthological citation, making these two works indispensable witnesses to a Hàn cosmological-political universe that the *Sìkù* editors themselves treat with explicit ambivalence.

3. **Pseudepigraphy and textual-criticism.** The *Sìkù* editors' systematic textual-critical scepticism is at its sharpest in this division: *Zháijīng* [[KR3g0019]] is unmasked as Táng, *Zàngshū* [[KR3g0020]] as Sòng despite its Jìn attribution, *Língqí jīng* [[KR3g0028]] as Six-Dynasties despite its Hàn attribution, *Lǐ Xūzhōng mìngshū* [[KR3g0033]] as Sòng despite its Táng attribution, and the *Hànlóng / Tiānyù / Qīngnáng* trilogy [[KR3g0021]]–[[KR3g0024]] is treated as Five-Dynasties / Sòng in received form despite Yáng Yúnsōng's late-Táng dating. The *tíyào* preserve a remarkably even-handed editorial position: the *fāngshì*' pseudepigraphic deification of their lineage-founders is regularly criticised, while the technical-and-historical value of the resulting texts is regularly affirmed.

4. **The *Sānshì* 三式 (Three-Form) divinatory typology.** The triplet *Tàiyǐ* [[KR3g0047]] — *Liùrén* [[KR3g0031]] — *Dùnjiǎ* [[KR3g0048]] constitutes the high-technical-cosmological apex of late-imperial Chinese divination, drawing on the *jiǔgōng* nine-palace cosmography of the *Yìwěi Qiánzáo dù* and ultimately on the *Hétú* / *Luòshū* magic-square tradition (cf. Swetz, *Legacy of the Luoshu*, 2002).

5. **Fengshui and the *xíngshì* / *lǐqì* divide.** The *xíngshì* 形勢 (form-and-configuration) school of Yáng Yúnsōng and the *lǐqì* 理氣 (principle-and-pneuma) school of Hé Pǔ and Lài Wénjùn are the two principal late-imperial fengshui schools, both fully represented in the division. Wilkinson (§33.1; cf. Brown, *Laws of the Land*, PUP 2023) treats fengshui as one of the longue-durée mantic practices most deeply embedded in late-imperial Chinese state and society, with documented legal-political consequences extending into Qīng case-law.

6. **The *zǐpíng* synthesis and popular fate-calculation.** Xú Zǐpíng's Sòng-period eight-character ([[KR3g0037]] commentary on *Luòlùzǐ*) consolidation of *xīngmìng* practice into the year-month-day-hour bāzì format — the basis of essentially all late-imperial popular fate-calculation — runs continuously to Wàn Mínyīng's monumental 1578 codification in [[KR3g0042]]. Wilkinson (§39.16 and the discussion at *Sanming tonghui*) registers this as the canonical late-imperial reference set, and notes the appearance of detailed horoscopes — including a technically-correct example — in the *Jīn Píng Méi*.

7. **Imperial rationalisation of mantic practice.** The Qīng imperial *xuǎnzé* corpus — *Yùdìng Xīnglì kǎoyuán* [[KR3g0050]] and *Qīndìng Xiéjì biànfāng shū* [[KR3g0051]] — represents the deliberate state-cosmological project of removing later-accreted *fāngshì* superstitions while preserving the core *yīnyáng wǔxíng* technical apparatus, presented as a state-administrative reference rather than as folk-divination. The Qiánlóng preface to [[KR3g0051]] ironically acknowledges that what folk custom has long followed cannot be entirely removed: the imperial project rationalises rather than abolishes.

8. **Japanese transmission and global circulation.** *Wǔxíng dàyì* [[KR3g0052]] survives only because the Japanese *Onmyōryō* 陰陽寮 preserved and re-transmitted it; Smith ("The transnational travels of geomancy in premodern East Asia", 2019) treats the longue-durée transmission of geomancy from China to the rest of East Asia. The division therefore registers an East-Asian cosmological-technical exchange running in both directions across the *kànyú* / *onmyōdō* boundary.
