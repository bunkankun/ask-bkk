## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR3ep` 醫案類 (*Yī'àn lèi*, Medical Case Records) is the sub-division of the Masters → Medicine branch (`KR3e` 醫家類) that gathers the clinical casebook (*yī'àn* 醫案) and clinical-discourse (*yīhuà* 醫話) literature of the late-imperial and early-Republican periods. Where `KR3e` proper preserves the canonical and quasi-canonical mainstream of Chinese medicine from the imperial *Sìkù quánshū* 四庫全書 (`source: WYG`), every text in `KR3ep` instead derives from the modern *Hǎiwài huíliú zhōngyī shànběn gǔjí cóngshū* 海外回流中醫善本古籍叢書 (`source: hxwd`) edited by Zhèng Jīnshēng 鄭金生, the repatriation project that has returned to circulation rare medical *shànběn* 善本 — case-books in particular — preserved in Japanese and Korean libraries after their disappearance from Chinese collections. The division's chronological centre of gravity is therefore the long Qīng (with two foundational Míng anchors and a substantial Republican-period tail), and its generic latitude admits classic single-author casebooks (e.g. [[KR3ep010]] *Línzhèng zhǐnán yī'àn*), great syndrome-organised anthologies ([[KR3ep017]] *Xù míngyī lèi'àn*), Republican standardised case-history reform (Dīng Gānrén [[KR3ep024]]), surgical (*wàikē* 外科) and suppurative-disorder (*yángkē* 瘍科) specialty casebooks ([[KR3ep042]], [[KR3ep043]]), and two Edo-period casebooks of the Japanese *kohōha* 古方派 that the Sìkù editors had never seen ([[KR3ep001]] Kitayama Yūshōshi, [[KR3ep002]] Asada Sōhaku).

The intellectual genealogy of the *yī'àn* genre is laid out in Endymion Wilkinson's *Chinese History: A New Manual* §41.3 (Medicine), with the biographical-clinical *Urtext* at §41.3.5: the *Shǐjì* 史記 *liè zhuàn* 列傳 of Chúnyú Yì 淳於意 (*Shǐjì* 105), which preserves twenty-five Hàn-period case histories and stands as the conventional point of origin for Chinese clinical narrative. The mature genre is, however, a late-Míng phenomenon: as TJ Hinrichs and Linda Barnes show in *Chinese Medicine and Healing* (ch. 5), it was only "around the sixteenth century" that "the medical casebook (*yi'an*)" appeared "as a new genre of medical texts" — entire books devoted to clinical narrative as a free-standing literary form, in dialogue with the *biji* 筆記 and *yǔlù* 語錄 traditions and with the rising prestige of the literati-physician. The two earliest works in this division — [[KR3ep089]] *Shíshān yī'àn* of [[汪機]] Wāng Jī (1463–1539) and [[KR3ep016]] *Sūn Wényuán yī'àn* of [[孫一奎]] Sūn Yīkuí (1522–1619) — sit at this birth moment, alongside the (not preserved in this division) *Yī'àn* of Xuē Jǐ 薛己 as the foundational Xīnān 新安-school witnesses.

## Important texts and text clusters

**Foundational late-Míng casebooks.** The two surviving late-Míng anchors of the genre in this division:
- *Shíshān yī'àn* 石山醫案 [[KR3ep089]] — [[汪機]] Wāng Jī, the founder of the **Xīnān 新安 medical school**; mid-Míng clinical narrative shaped by the *Dānxī* yin-nourishing tradition in dialogue with the *Lǐ Dōngyuán* / *Xuē Jǐ* warming-tonifying line.
- *Sūn Wényuán yī'àn* 孫文垣醫案 [[KR3ep016]] — [[孫一奎]] Sūn Yīkuí (*hào* Shēngshēngzǐ 生生子), the late-Míng theorist of the *mìngmén* 命門 doctrine; one of the earliest substantial standalone casebooks (also titled *Chìshuǐ xuánzhū yī'àn* 赤水玄珠醫案).

**The Yú Jiāyán philosophical-case turn.** *Yùyì cǎo* 寓意草 [[KR3ep012]] — [[喻昌]] Yú Chāng (1585–1664) of Xīchāng (Jiāngxī), the Míng–Qīng transitional master who reshaped the *yī'àn* into a vehicle for sustained theoretical argumentation: each case becomes an essay yoking pulse-theory, *Shānghán* exegesis, and Confucian *gé-zhì-chéng-yì* 格致誠意 doctrine. The most philosophically dense work in the division.

**The Yè Tiānshì 葉桂 (1666–1745) recension complex.** The single most important cluster in `KR3ep`, and the principal documentary record of mature Qīng warm-disease (*wēnbìng* 溫病) clinical practice:
- *Línzhèng zhǐnán yī'àn* 臨證指南醫案 [[KR3ep010]] — the canonical posthumous compilation by Huá Xiùyún 華岫雲 (printed 1766); the foundational document of Yè's clinical synthesis and one of the most influential medical books of the Qīng. (Also catalogued separately as [[KR3e0042]] in the parent division.)
- *Wèikèběn Yèshì yī'àn* 未刻本葉氏醫案 [[KR3ep009]] — the "unprinted manuscript" recension, a less-edited witness to the master's case-notes.
- *Xúpī Yè Tiānshì wǎnnián fāng'àn zhēnběn* 徐批葉天士晚年方案真本 [[KR3ep013]] — Yè's late cases preserved with the marginal commentary of [[徐大椿]] Xú Dàchūn (Língtāi 靈胎, 1693–1771), the great Qīng critic; a key witness to Qīng inter-school polemic.
- *Méishòutáng fāng'àn xuǎncún* 眉壽堂方案選存 [[KR3ep023]] — Yè cases preserved by his disciple Guō Wéijùn 郭維浚.
- *Yè Tiānshì yī'àn jīnghuá* 葉天士醫案精華 [[KR3ep027]] — a Republican-era selection of "essentials"; symptomatic of the late-Qīng / Republican Yè-school simplifying-handbook tradition.

**The Wēnbìng line after Yè.** *Wú Jūtōng yī'àn* 吳鞠通醫案 [[KR3ep003]] — [[吳塘]] Wú Táng (1758–1836), author of the canonical *Wēnbìng tiáobiàn* 溫病條辨 (1798); the posthumous casebook records the *sānjiāo biànzhèng* 三焦辨證 framework and the *yínqiào sǎn* 銀翹散 / *sāngjú yǐn* 桑菊飲 repertoire in clinical action.

**The Sūzhōu / Wúzhōng post-Yè world.** A dense regional cluster of nineteenth-century Sūzhōu-school casebooks transmitting and modifying the Yè synthesis:
- *Sānjiā yī'àn hékè* 三家醫案合刻 [[KR3ep020]] — [[吳金壽]] Wú Jīnshòu's 1831 joint edition of three early-nineteenth-century Sūzhōu physicians.
- *Yīpiáo yī'àn* 一瓢醫案 [[KR3ep103]] — [[薛雪]] Xuē Xuě (Shēngbái, 1681–1770), Yè Tiānshì's great rival; compiled by Wú Jīnshòu.
- *Sǎoyèzhuāng yī'àn* 掃葉莊醫案 [[KR3ep035]] — Xuē Xuě's own polemically-titled casebook ("Sweeping the Leaf Villa", a barbed reference to Yè Guì 葉桂 = "leaf cinnamon").
- *Hé Dànān yī'àn* 何澹安醫案 [[KR3ep005]] (Hé Yóu 何游, mid-19th c.), *Yěshì Shānrén yī'àn* 也是山人醫案 [[KR3ep028]], *Sùpǔ yī'àn* 素圃醫案 [[KR3ep032]] ([[鄭重光]] Zhèng Chóngguāng, 1638–1716).

**The Ménghé 孟河 lineage.** A second nineteenth-century regional school anchored in Jiāngsū's Ménghé village, with a strong reformist current:
- *Wáng Xùgāo línzhèng yī'àn* 王旭高臨證醫案 [[KR3ep011]] and *Huánxī cǎotáng yī'àn* 環溪草堂醫案 [[KR3ep039]] — [[王旭高]] Wáng Xùgāo (1798–1862); the second is the *qǐnggǔ* 請孤-style teaching record of his clinic.
- *Cáo Rénbó yī'àn lùn* 曹仁伯醫案論 [[KR3ep025]] — [[曹存心]] Cáo Cúnxīn (Rénbó 仁伯), Wáng Xùgāo's teacher.
- *Mǎ Péizhī yī'àn* 馬培之醫案 [[KR3ep015]] — [[馬培之]] (1820–1903), the Ménghé physician summoned to the Empress Dowager's court in 1880.
- *Dīng Gānrén yī'àn* 丁甘仁醫案 [[KR3ep024]] — [[丁甘仁]] (1865–1926), the principal Ménghé heir in Shànghǎi and founder of the modern *Shànghǎi Zhōngyī Xuéyuàn* curriculum.

**The Mèngyīng line and Liǔ Bǎoyí's anthology project.** A second major reformist current of the long nineteenth century, organised by [[王士雄]] Wáng Shìxióng (Mèngyīng 孟英, 1808–1868) and his successors:
- *Huíxī yī'àn* 洄溪醫案 [[KR3ep087]] — Wáng Shìxióng's edition of Xú Dàchūn's cases.
- *Liǔxuǎn sìjiā yī'àn* 柳選四家醫案 [[KR3ep021]] and *Zēngbǔ píngzhù Liǔxuǎn yī'àn* 增補評注柳選醫案 [[KR3ep022]] — [[柳寶詒]] Liǔ Bǎoyí's selection of the four masters (the principal late-Qīng curricular anthology of Sūzhōu-school cases).

**The anthological and critical-commentary line.** A second axis of the genre, cutting across regional schools, organised by syndrome rather than by author:
- *Xù míngyī lèi'àn* 續名醫類案 [[KR3ep017]] — [[魏之琇]] Wèi Zhīxiù (1722–1772) of Hāngzhōu; sixty *juǎn* continuing Jiāng Guàn's mid-Míng *Míngyī lèi'àn* 名醫類案 (= [[KR3e0075]]) into the mid-Qīng. The shorter thirty-six-*juǎn* *Sìkù* recension is preserved as [[KR3ep018]].
- *Qízhèng huì* 奇症彙 [[KR3ep008]] — Shěn Yuán 沈源, an eight-*juǎn* anthology of paradoxical clinical phenomena, programmatically demonstrating that "strange symptoms" admit ordinary-principle explanation.
- *Gǔjīn yī'àn àn* 古今醫案按 [[KR3ep071]] — Yú Zhèn 俞震 (1778); the founding text of the *yī'àn àn* (commented-case-record) sub-genre.

**Republican-era continuations and standardisation.** [[KR3ep024]] *Dīng Gānrén yī'àn*, [[KR3ep007]] *Cóngguì cǎotáng yī'àn* (袁焯 Yuán Zhuō, 1900s–1920s), [[KR3ep027]] *Yè Tiānshì yī'àn jīnghuá*, [[KR3ep022]] *Zēngbǔ píngzhù Liǔxuǎn yī'àn*, [[KR3ep046]] *Jiànhuì cǎotáng yī'àn* — the latest stratum of the division; in this period the Chinese case-record was being explicitly reformed (by He Lianchen and others) on the model of Western case histories, a development documented in Hinrichs & Barnes (2013, ch. 8).

**Specialised regional and external-medicine casebooks.** A fringe of works documenting traditions that the mainstream Yè-school casebook overlooks:
- *Chén Xīntián wàikē fāng'àn* 陳莘田外科方案 [[KR3ep042]] and *Yángkē zhǐnán yī'àn* 瘍科指南醫案 [[KR3ep043]] — external-medicine and suppurative-disorder cases.
- *Lóngshā bājiā yī'àn* 龍砂八家醫案 [[KR3ep037]] — anthology of the eight Lóngshā (Jiāngyīn) family-medicine masters of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.
- *Déxīnjí yī'àn* 得心集醫案 [[KR3ep040]] — [[謝星煥]] Xiè Xīnghuàn (Jiāngxī).
- *Huāyùnlóu yī'àn* 花韻樓醫案 [[KR3ep029]] — [[顧蔓雲]] Gù Mànyún, a rare nineteenth-century female-physician casebook.
- *Yīquán chūbiān* 醫權初編 [[KR3ep031]] — [[王三尊]] Wáng Sānzūn (1717–1721); a *yīhuà*-style discursive volume rather than a casebook proper.

**The Japanese *kohōha* 古方派 witnesses.** Two casebooks of the Edo "return-to-antiquity" school, preserved in Japan and repatriated through the hxwd project:
- *Běishān yī'àn* 北山醫案 [[KR3ep001]] — [[北山友松子]] Kitayama Yūshōshi (1648–1719), pupil of the émigré Míng physician Dài Lì 戴笠 (Dúlì Xìngyì 獨立性易); the principal clinical document of *kohōha* in its founding generation, attesting the personal transmission of late-Míng Chinese medicine to Japan through the Ōbaku 黃檗 Buddhist channel.
- *Xiānzhé yīhuà* 先哲醫話 [[KR3ep002]] — [[淺田宗伯]] Asada Sōhaku (1815–1894); a *yīhuà* anthology of thirteen Edo *kohōha* physicians, cut at Huáng Zūnxiàn's 黃遵憲 instigation in Canton in 1879 — a rare instance of Japanese medical scholarship re-entering Qīng circulation in its own day.

## Important persons

- [[葉桂]] (Yè Guì, *zì* Tiānshì 天士, 1666–1745) — central figure of the division. The Sūzhōu master whose clinical practice generated, posthumously, the largest single recension cluster in `KR3ep` ([[KR3ep009]], [[KR3ep010]], [[KR3ep013]], [[KR3ep023]], [[KR3ep027]]) and whose synthesis structures the practice of every Qīng physician in the collection.
- [[喻昌]] (Yú Chāng, Jiāyán 嘉言, 1585–1664) — Jiāngxī master; author of [[KR3ep012]] *Yùyì cǎo*, the most philosophically articulated text in the genre.
- [[孫一奎]] (Sūn Yīkuí, Wényuán 文垣, 1522–1619) — Huīzhōu / Xīnān physician; foundational late-Míng casebook author ([[KR3ep016]]) and *mìngmén*-doctrine theorist.
- [[汪機]] (Wāng Jī, Shíshān 石山, 1463–1539) — founder of the Xīnān medical school; author of the earliest casebook in the division ([[KR3ep089]]).
- [[薛雪]] (Xuē Xuě, Shēngbái 生白, *hào* Yīpiáo 一瓢, 1681–1770) — Sūzhōu physician and Yè Tiānshì's principal contemporary rival; cases preserved in [[KR3ep035]] and [[KR3ep103]].
- [[徐大椿]] (Xú Dàchūn, Língtāi 靈胎, *hào* Huíxī 洄溪, 1693–1771) — the central mid-Qīng critic of post-Sòng medical doctrine; both author ([[KR3ep087]]) and annotator ([[KR3ep013]]) within the division.
- [[魏之琇]] (Wèi Zhīxiù, Liǔzhōu 柳州, 1722–1772) — Hāngzhōu anthologist; compiler of [[KR3ep017]] / [[KR3ep018]] *Xù míngyī lèi'àn*, the great Qīng continuation of the Míng Jiāng Guàn anthology.
- [[吳塘]] (Wú Táng, Jūtōng 鞠通, 1758–1836) — the principal Qīng *wēnbìng* theorist after Yè; clinical record at [[KR3ep003]].
- [[王士雄]] (Wáng Shìxióng, Mèngyīng 孟英, 1808–1868) — late-Qīng Hāngzhōu / Shànghǎi master and editor; compiler-editor of [[KR3ep087]] and a presence in numerous Sūzhōu-school recensions.
- [[王旭高]] (Wáng Xùgāo, 1798–1862) — Ménghé predecessor; cases in [[KR3ep011]] and [[KR3ep039]].
- [[曹存心]] (Cáo Cúnxīn, Rénbó 仁伯, 1767–1834) — Sūzhōu / Jiāngsū teacher of Wáng Xùgāo; cases in [[KR3ep025]].
- [[馬培之]] (Mǎ Péizhī, 1820–1903) — Ménghé physician who treated the Empress Dowager Cíxǐ; cases in [[KR3ep015]].
- [[丁甘仁]] (Dīng Gānrén, 1865–1926) — Republican-era Shànghǎi reformer; founder of modern TCM education; casebook [[KR3ep024]].
- [[柳寶詒]] (Liǔ Bǎoyí, fl. 1880s–1900s) — late-Qīng anthologist of the four-master Sūzhōu canon ([[KR3ep021]], [[KR3ep022]]).
- [[吳金壽]] (Wú Jīnshòu, fl. 1820s–1830s) — early-nineteenth-century Sūzhōu publisher-physician; editor of [[KR3ep020]] and [[KR3ep103]], pivotal in transmitting the early-Qīng Sūzhōu casebook tradition.
- [[北山友松子]] (Kitayama Yūshōshi, 1648–1719) — founding-generation Edo *kohōha* physician; author of [[KR3ep001]].
- [[淺田宗伯]] (Asada Sōhaku, 1815–1894) — late-Edo / Meiji *kohōha* anthologist; author of [[KR3ep002]].

## Topics

**The rise of the *yī'àn* genre.** The principal historical question hanging over the division is the emergence of the standalone case-book in the long sixteenth century. Why entire books of cases now, after fifteen centuries in which clinical narrative had been confined to biography, *yǔlù*-style anecdote, or scattered notation in formularies? Hinrichs and Barnes (2013, ch. 5) connect the rise to the maturation of the literati-physician identity and to the print-culture conditions of the late-Míng Jiāngnán book market; Furth and Hsiu-yueh Wu (in *Thinking with Cases*, ed. Furth/Zeitlin/Hsiung, 2007) treat the *yī'àn* alongside the legal and literary "case" as a shared late-Míng knowledge-practice. The early witnesses in this division — [[KR3ep089]], [[KR3ep016]], and (in [[KR3e]] proper) Jiāng Guàn's *Míngyī lèi'àn* — are the principal evidentiary base.

**Single-author casebook vs syndromic anthology.** A genre-internal division running through the corpus: the single-author casebook (Yè, Xuē, Wú Jūtōng, Wāng Xùgāo, Mǎ Péizhī, Dīng Gānrén) preserves a clinical voice; the syndromic anthology (Wèi Zhīxiù, Shěn Yuán, Yú Zhèn, Liǔ Bǎoyí, Jiāng Chéngzhī's *Lóngshā bājiā*) re-organises cases for pedagogical and encyclopaedic use, often dissolving the original physician's voice into the editor's. The two forms generate quite different epistemic claims.

**Cold damage versus warm disease in clinical practice.** Where the *Shānghán* / *Wēnbìng* polemic in [[KR3e]] proper plays out as doctrinal exegesis, in `KR3ep` it plays out in case practice. The Yè-school casebooks document the routine deployment of *wèiqìyíngxuè biànzhèng* 衛氣營血辨證 and the light-dispersing repertoire; Xú Dàchūn's annotations on Yè ([[KR3ep013]]) and his own casebook [[KR3ep087]] document the *fùgǔ* 復古 critique that wished to return clinical practice to Zhāng Zhòngjǐng's *Shānghán* line; Wú Jūtōng [[KR3ep003]] documents the consolidation of the systematic *Wēnbìng* repertoire after Yè.

**The Yè-Tiānshì textual problem.** No single Qīng-period clinical author generated more recensional confusion. The genuine Yè cases circulated in manuscript among disciples; the printed recensions — including the canonical [[KR3ep010]] — embed substantial editorial commentary by Huá Xiùyún and others; "Yè-school" simplifying handbooks proliferated through the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries; and Xú Dàchūn's critical annotations ([[KR3ep013]]) attest, by their very existence, the contemporary perception that the printed Yè-corpus had become unreliable. A historian of Qīng medicine working in this division must keep the recensional question always in view.

**Regional schools and the geography of late-imperial medicine.** `KR3ep` is the principal documentary base for the regional medical-school historiography that has structured Western-language Chinese-medicine scholarship since the 1980s: Xīnān (Wāng Jī, Sūn Yīkuí, Chéng Wényòu, Huáng Shùníng), Sūzhōu / Wúzhōng (Yè Tiānshì, Xuē Xuě, Wú Jīnshòu, the *Sānjiā*), Ménghé (Cáo Cúnxīn, Wáng Xùgāo, Mǎ Péizhī, Dīng Gānrén; see Scheid 2007), Hāngzhōu (Wèi Zhīxiù, Wáng Shìxióng), Lóngshā / Jiāngyīn (the eight masters of [[KR3ep037]]), and Jiāngxī (Yú Jiāyán, Xiè Xīnghuàn).

**The *yīhuà* 醫話 register.** A related but distinct genre, modelled on the *shīhuà* 詩話 rather than on biography: brief clinical aperçus framed as conversation or *bon-mot* rather than as full case-narrative. Best represented in this division by [[KR3ep002]] *Xiānzhé yīhuà* and (less purely) [[KR3ep031]] *Yīquán chūbiān*; the formal distinction is explicitly thematised by Huáng Zūnxiàn 黃遵憲 in his colophon to [[KR3ep002]].

**Republican-era reform of the case record.** The latest stratum of the division — Dīng Gānrén [[KR3ep024]] and the early-twentieth-century continuations [[KR3ep007]], [[KR3ep027]], [[KR3ep022]] — overlaps with He Lianchen 何廉臣's Republican-era project (treated in Hinrichs & Barnes 2013, ch. 8) to reformat Chinese case records on the rubrics of Western case histories ("patient", "ailment", "cause", treatment, outcome). The reform was both defensive (to legitimate Chinese medicine against the 1909 medical-licensing exams) and substantive: it standardised what until then had been an idiosyncratic literary form.

**The hxwd repatriation as a textual fact.** The whole division is preserved because of a modern editorial project. Many of the works in `KR3ep` — particularly the anonymous late-Qīng manuscripts ([[KR3ep043]]–[[KR3ep047]]) and both Japanese witnesses — vanished from Chinese circulation in the late nineteenth or early twentieth century and were recovered only through the Zhèng Jīnshēng project of the 2000s. Citation conventions for these texts (manuscript date, recension witness, editorial intervention) must therefore be handled with the same care that is given to Dūnhuáng or Hēishuǐchéng recoveries.

## Timeline

- 1500–1539 — *Shíshān yī'àn* 石山醫案 [[KR3ep089]] — [[汪機]] Wāng Jī; the foundational casebook of the Xīnān school and one of the two earliest in the division.
- 1580–1620 — *Sūn Wényuán yī'àn* 孫文垣醫案 [[KR3ep016]] — [[孫一奎]] Sūn Yīkuí; the second of the foundational late-Míng casebooks, vehicle of *mìngmén*-doctrine clinical reasoning.
- 1643–1664 — *Yùyì cǎo* 寓意草 [[KR3ep012]] — [[喻昌]] Yú Chāng; the philosophical-case turn of the Míng–Qīng transition.
- 1680–1716 — *Sùpǔ yī'àn* 素圃醫案 [[KR3ep032]] — [[鄭重光]] Zhèng Chóngguāng; early-Qīng Xīnān-tradition casebook.
- 1690–1719 — *Běishān yī'àn* 北山醫案 [[KR3ep001]] — [[北山友松子]] Kitayama Yūshōshi; the principal clinical document of founding-generation *kohōha* Japan.
- 1700–1760 — *Shěnshì yī'àn* 沈氏醫案 [[KR3ep033]] — Shěn Pán 沈璠; early-to-mid-Qīng Jiāngnán casebook.
- 1717–1721 — *Yīquán chūbiān* 醫權初編 [[KR3ep031]] — [[王三尊]] Wáng Sānzūn; early-Qīng *yīhuà*-style discursive volume.
- 1720–1745 — *Wèikèběn Yèshì yī'àn* 未刻本葉氏醫案 [[KR3ep009]] and *Méishòutáng fāng'àn xuǎncún* 眉壽堂方案選存 [[KR3ep023]] — [[葉桂]] Yè Tiānshì cases preserved in early manuscript recensions.
- 1720–1770 — *Yīpiáo yī'àn* 一瓢醫案 [[KR3ep103]] — [[薛雪]] Xuē Xuě, Yè Tiānshì's principal rival.
- 1720–1855 — *Huíxī yī'àn* 洄溪醫案 [[KR3ep087]] — [[徐大椿]] Xú Dàchūn (cases 1720s–1771; first compiled and printed by [[王士雄]] Wáng Shìxióng in the mid-nineteenth century).
