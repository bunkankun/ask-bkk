# Cáo Rén-bó yī-àn lùn 曹仁伯醫案論
*Discussions on the Medical Cases of Cáo Rén-bó*
by [[曹存心]] Cáo Cún-xīn 曹存心 (*zì* Rén-bó 仁伯, *hào* Yuè-cūn 樂存, 1767–1834), of Cháng-shú 常熟 (Jiāngsū).

## About the work

A single-*juǎn* casebook of the early-nineteenth-century Cháng-shú physician Cáo Cún-xīn — one of the most influential clinicians of the Jiāng-nán post-Yè-Tiān-shì tradition. The work is distinguished from the more straightforward case-records of other casebooks by its *lùn* (*discussion*) format: each case is followed by an extended editorial discussion (Cáo's own or his disciple's) explicating the clinical reasoning at length. This makes it a primary teaching text rather than simply a record. Cáo was deeply influential as a teacher: Wáng Shū-hé 王旭高 ([[王旭高]]) attended his teaching, as did Wú Jīn-shòu [[吳金壽]] and Liǔ Bǎo-yí 柳寶詒 [[柳寶詒]]'s teacher Cài Lěng-ān 蔡冷庵.

The hxwd opening case is a clinical-philosophical *lùn* discussing male sexual physiology and the relationship of bed-environment, bone-and-tendon climatology, and the *bì* 痹 (impediment) syndrome — using *Shāng-hán lùn* and *Zhōng-fēng* doctrine as the diagnostic frame. The discussion form here is fully developed: each case is a vehicle for substantive theoretical exposition.

### Abstract

Cáo Cún-xīn 曹存心 (1767–1834, CBDB 88559) — Cháng-shú (Jiāngsū) physician of the early nineteenth century, conventionally treated as one of the four "Sūzhōu masters" of the post-Yè-Tiān-shì tradition (alongside Yè Tiān-shì's actual descendants and Yáng Yìn 楊瀜 = Yáng Lì-shān 楊立山 and Wáng Jiǔ-fēng 王九峰 [[王九峰]]). His teaching at his *Liú-yīng-táng* 留影堂 studio at Cháng-shú attracted leading pupils across Jiāng-nán and his clinical synthesis — heavy on the *jīng-luò* 經絡 / *jīn-yè* 津液 framework and on the use of light moistening prescriptions — was foundational for the late-Qīng *wēn-bìng* tradition. His other principal works include the *Liú-yīng yī-àn* 留影醫案 (the version of his cases anthologised in [[KR3ep021]]) and [[KR3ep095]] *Yán-líng dì-zǐ jì-yào* 延陵弟子紀要 (a record of his teaching of his disciples).

The composition window 1810–1834 reflects his mature clinical decades up to his death year. The hxwd recension is one of several Cáo casebook recensions and represents the *Lùn* (discussion-style) version of the cases. The casebook was widely reprinted in the late nineteenth century and was used as a clinical-pedagogical text in the early Republican medical schools.
