# Yè Tiān-shì Cáo Rén-bó Hé Yuán-cháng yī-àn 葉天士曹仁伯何元長醫案
*Combined Medical Case Records of Yè Tiān-shì, Cáo Rén-bó, and Hé Yuán-cháng*
by [[葉桂]] Yè Guì (Tiānshì 天士, 1666–1745), [[曹存心]] Cáo Cún-xīn (Rén-bó 仁伯, 1767–1834), and [[何元長]] Hé Yuán-cháng (1752–1806).

## About the work

A three-*juǎn* composite casebook that joins selections from the case material of three of the most important Qīng *wēn-bìng* / *biàn-zhèng* physicians of the Sūzhōu region across more than a century: **Yè Tiān-shì** (founder of the *wēn-bìng* school proper), **Cáo Cún-xīn** (Rén-bó, of Cháng-shú), and **Hé Yuán-cháng** of Sūzhōu. The arrangement is by syndrome (春溫門, 三十三方; 風溫門; etc.).

### Abstract

The text belongs to a Qīng tradition of *sān-jiā / sì-jiā yī-àn* (three-family / four-family casebook) compilations — most famously [[KR3ep020]] *Sān-jiā yī-àn hé-kè* of Wú Jīn-shòu (1830) — that thematically arrange syndrome-grouped selections of cases by major Sūzhōu-school masters for didactic purposes. The present compilation appears to be an anonymous nineteenth-century editorial selection (compiler not identified in the hxwd source) drawing from:

1. **Yè Guì 葉桂** (Yè Tiānshì, 1666–1745) — the founder of the *wēn-bìng* school proper; cases drawn principally from the *Lín-zhèng zhǐ-nán yī-àn* ([[KR3ep010]]) and related Yè-school sources.
2. **Cáo Cún-xīn 曹存心** (Rén-bó, 1767–1834) — the leading Sūzhōu / Cháng-shú physician of the late-Qiánlóng to Jiā-qìng period; cases drawn from [[KR3ep025]] *Cáo Rén-bó yī-àn lùn*, [[KR3ep069]] *Cáo Rén-bó yī-àn*, and related materials.
3. **Hé Yuán-cháng 何元長** (1752–1806) — the leading early-Jiā-qìng Sūzhōu physician of the Hé-family medical lineage; one of the "Hé-family three" of Hé Wǎn-tíng 何琬亭, Hé Yuán-cháng, and Hé Shū-shān 何書山, ancestors of the famous Méng-hé Hé-family clinicians.

The composition window 1720–1880 reflects the combined active dates of the three physicians plus a probable mid-to-late-nineteenth-century editorial compilation. The *sān-jiā* format makes the text a heavily-used didactic resource in Republican-era Chinese-medical schools.
