## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR3eb` 診法 (*Zhěn fǎ*, "Diagnostic Methods") is the second sub-division of `KR3e` 醫家類 (Masters — Medicine) and gathers, with very few exceptions, the entire specialist literature of Chinese clinical diagnostics: pulse-diagnosis (*màizhěn* 脈診), tongue-diagnosis (*shézhěn* 舌診), inspection-diagnosis (*wàngzhěn* 望診), and the integrated *sì zhěn* 四診 ("four examinations" — *wàng* 望, *wén* 聞, *wèn* 問, *qiè* 切). All 56 entries are sourced from the 華夏文獻 (*hxwd*) supplementary database rather than the Wényuāngé *Sìkù quánshū*: the Qiánlóng-era Sìkù compilers absorbed the principal diagnostic *Classics* (*Mài jīng* 脈經, *Mài jué* 脈訣, *Bīnhú màixué* 瀕湖脈學) into other medical sub-categories, but the densely-printed specialist diagnostic literature that proliferated from the Míng *Jiājìng* era through the Republican period was largely excluded; hxwd accordingly preserves the bulk of the genre in this division. Endymion Wilkinson's *Chinese History: A New Manual* discusses pulse-diagnosis under Section 41.3.1, with *Mài jīng* 脈經 [[KR3eb011]] listed as the foundational text and indispensable starting-point for any Western-language work on the topic.

Chronologically the division spans roughly seventeen centuries. The anchor work is [[王叔和]] Wáng Shūhé's *Mài jīng* 脈經 [[KR3eb011]] of c. 280 — the first systematic book-length pulse treatise and the source from which every later pulse manual descends. The bulk of the corpus is, however, late: about three quarters of the texts are Míng or later, and a substantial Qīng-Republican stratum (Zhōu Xuéhǎi 周學海, Sūn Dǐngyí 孫鼎宜, Liú Héngruì 劉恆瑞, Cáo Bǐngzhāng 曹炳章) carries the diagnostic tradition into the *kǎojù* and Chinese-Western integrative phase. The principal commentarial and transmissional traditions a reader must keep in mind are: (i) the Wáng Shūhé *Mài jīng* lineage and the medieval and late-imperial efforts to recover, reform, or refute the *Mài jīng* by way of its Sòng-spuriously-attributed offshoot the *Mài jué* 脈訣 (the "pseudo-Wáng-Shūhé *Mài jué*"); (ii) the four-character mnemonic tradition founded by [[崔嘉彥]] Cuī Jiāyàn (*Zǐxū* 紫虛) in the Southern Sòng; (iii) the Míng *Bīnhú* school of [[李時珍]] Lǐ Shízhēn, whose pulse-poems became the standard mnemonic from 1564 onward; (iv) the YuánMíng tongue-diagnostic tradition starting with the Áo–Dù *Jīnjìng lù* 金鏡錄 of 1341; and (v) the late-Qīng / Republican synthetic period that integrates classical pulse-and-tongue inspection with Western anatomical and pathological knowledge.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The foundational pulse Classic.** One work towers over the entire division.
- *Mài jīng* 脈經 [[KR3eb011]] — [[王叔和]] Wáng Shūhé, c. 280. Ten juan, ninety-eight 篇; the first systematic treatise on pulse-diagnosis, co-ordinating the dispersed pulse-doctrines of the *Sùwèn*, *Língshū*, *Nánjīng*, and the early HànJìn medical traditions into a single twenty-four-pulse repertoire grounded in the *cùnguānchǐ* 寸關尺 wrist topology. Every later pulse manual in the division descends from it.

**The pseudo-*Mài jué* 偽脈訣 and its commentarial industry.** The single most consequential textual phenomenon of pulse-diagnosis literature after Wáng Shūhé is the *Mài jué* 脈訣, a Sòng (probably Northern-Sòng) heptasyllabic mnemonic falsely attributed to Wáng Shūhé. The pseudo-*Mài jué* taught the *qī biǎo bā lǐ jiǔ dào* 七表八裡九道 ("seven exterior, eight interior, nine paths") pulse classification and a left-right *cùnguānchǐ* viscera allocation at variance with the *Mài jīng*. Its enormous popularity from the Sòng onward generated three overlapping strands of literature in this division.
- *Defenders and popularisers*: *Xīn kān Wùtīngzǐ sújiě Mài jué* 新刊勿聽子俗解脈訣 [[KR3eb046]] (Xióng Zōnglì 熊宗立, Míng); *Túzhù Wáng Shūhé Mài jué* 圖註王叔和脈訣 [[KR3eb048]] (Zhāng Shìxián 張世賢, early Míng); *Qiè Wángshì mìchuán Shūhé túzhù shìyì Mài jué* 鍥王氏秘傳叔和圖注釋義脉訣評林捷徑統宗 [[KR3eb045]] (anonymous, late-Wàn-lì commercial); *Xīn biān zhěn mài xūzhī* 新編診脈須知 [[KR3eb047]] (Wú Hóng 吳洪, Míng); *Mài jué rǔhǎi* 脈訣乳海 [[KR3eb016]] (Wáng Bāngfù 王邦傅, 1715).
- *Polemical critics*: *Mài jué kānwù* 脈訣刊誤 [[KR3eb026]] ([[戴起宗]] Dài Qǐzōng, 1341, supplemented by [[汪機]] Wāng Jī, Míng); *Zhěn jiā shūyào* 診家樞要 [[KR3eb023]] ([[滑壽]] Huá Shòu, 1359); *Mài jué kǎozhèng* 脈訣考證 [[KR3eb019]] ([[李時珍]] Lǐ Shízhēn, c. 1564–1593); *Mài jué huìbiàn* 脈訣彙辨 [[KR3eb018]] (Lǐ Yánshì 李延罡, 1664–1666); *Mài lǐ qiúzhēn* 脈理求真 [[KR3eb024]] ([[黃宮繡]] Huáng Gōngxiù, 1769).
- *Late tradition*: by the high-Qīng the polemic was largely closed and the pseudo-*Mài jué* was relegated to popular-market editions; the more sophisticated 18th- and 19th-century pulse manuals (KR3eb013, KR3eb024, KR3eb027, KR3eb028, KR3eb031, KR3eb034, KR3eb036) work directly from the *Mài jīng* and the *Nèijīng* corpus.

**The Cuī Jiāyàn four-character mnemonic tradition.** [[崔嘉彥]] Cuī Jiāyàn (*Zǐxū* 紫虛, *fl.* Southern Sòng, c. 1150–1190) composed a tetrasyllabic mnemonic on pulse-doctrine that became one of the longest-lived pedagogical texts in the genre. Its descendants in this division: *Mài jué* 脈訣 [[KR3eb025]] (the Cuī original — to be distinguished from the pseudo-Wáng-Shūhé *Mài jué*); *Mài xué lèi biān* 脈學類編 [[KR3eb033]] (Cuī's text reclassified by Lǐ Yánwén 李言聞, Lǐ Shízhēn's father, c. 1500–1570); *Yī dēng xùyàn* 醫燈續焰 [[KR3eb001]] (Pān Jí 潘楫, 1652 — Cuī's mnemonic expanded with prose commentary and formularies); *Mài lǐ jíyào* 脈理集要 [[KR3eb032]] (Wáng Huàn 王宦, late Míng, preserving Cuī's *Shànggǔ sānzhěn* 上古三診 doctrine).

**The Bīnhú school of Lǐ Shízhēn.** [[李時珍]] Lǐ Shízhēn (*hào* Bīnhú 瀕湖, 1518–1593) produced the most influential single pulse text after Wáng Shūhé. The triad:
- *Bīnhú màixué* 瀕湖脈學 [[KR3eb014]] (1564) — the standard twenty-seven-pulse taxonomy in heptasyllabic mnemonic verse, the canonical memorisation manual from the late-Míng onward; the satellite entry [[KR3eb015]] 瀕湖脈學脈訣 is its mnemonic-verse extract.
- *Mài jué kǎozhèng* 脈訣考證 [[KR3eb019]] — Lǐ's polemical demolition of the pseudo-*Mài jué*, asserting the *Mài jīng* as the only legitimate ancestor.

**The late-Míng synthetic generation.** Three works of the 1580s–1640s consolidate the genre.
- *Mài yǔ* 脈語 [[KR3eb040]] (Wú Kūn 吳昆, 1584) — dialogue-form pulse manual integrating colour-and-pulse diagnosis.
- *Dìngzhèng Tàisù mài bìjué* 訂正太素脈秘訣 [[KR3eb017]] (Zhāng Tàisù 張太素, late Wànlì) — the principal received version of the divinatory *Tàisù* pulse tradition that reads fate from pulse-form, often denounced by orthodox physicians but historically influential.
- *Zhěn jiā zhèngyǎn* 診家正眼 [[KR3eb020]] ([[李中梓]] Lǐ Zhōngzǐ, 1642; collated by Lǐ Yánshì 1664) — the key late-Míng pulse synthesis, expanding the twenty-seven Bīnhú pulses to twenty-eight (adding *jí* 疾) and identifying the pulse with *shén* 神; the framework on which most early-Qīng manuals build.

**The early-Qīng synthesis line.** A continuous lineage in the high-Kāngxī to Qiánlóng period brings together the *Mài jīng*, Bīnhú, and Lǐ Zhōngzǐ traditions.
- *Zhěn zōng sānmèi* 診宗三昧 [[KR3eb022]] ([[張璐]] Zhāng Lù, 1689) — master-disciple dialogues insisting on integrated pulse-and-forearm-skin (*chǐfū* 尺膚) palpation.
- *Mài guàn* 脈貫 [[KR3eb034]] (Wáng Xián 王賢, 1711) — nine-juan systematic synthesis of the entire pulse-doctrinal heritage.
- *Mài quē* 脈確 [[KR3eb013]] (Huáng Yùnxī 黃韞兮, 1746) — *Nèijīng*-first conservative manual rejecting late-Míng novelties.
- *Mài lǐ qiúzhēn* 脈理求真 [[KR3eb024]] (Huáng Gōngxiù, 1769).
- *Mài xiàng tǒnglèi* 脈象統類 [[KR3eb027]] and *Zhūmài zhǔbìng shī* 諸脈主病詩 [[KR3eb028]] (Shěn Jīn'áo 沈金鰲, 1773) — a paired prose-taxonomy and verse-mnemonic, twenty-eight pulses organised by a six-character rubric matrix.
- *Yī xué mài dēng* 醫學脈燈 [[KR3eb031]] (Cháng Cháoyí 常朝宜, 1749); *Sān zhǐ chán* 三指禪 [[KR3eb021]] (Zhōu Xuétíng 周學霆, 1827); *Zhěn mài sānshíèr biàn* 診脈三十二辨 [[KR3eb043]] (Guǎn Yùhéng 管玉衡, c. 1700–1750); *Yī huì yuányào* 醫會元要 [[KR3eb005]] and *Yī xué zhǐyào* 醫學指要 [[KR3eb006]] (Cài Yíjī 蔡貽績, 1812 / 1823); *Mài jué chǎnwēi* 脈訣闡微 [[KR3eb039]] (Chén Shìduó 陳士鐸, 1687).

**The Zhōu Xuéhǎi 周學海 cycle.** [[周學海]] Zhōu Xuéhǎi (1856–1906) of Jiànglǐng produced the most extensive and analytically sophisticated late-Qīng treatment of pulse-diagnosis in five integrated works.
- *Mài yì jiǎnmó* 脈義簡摩 [[KR3eb036]] (1891) — the most extensive of the cycle (eight juan); introduces the *xíngshì* 形勢 ("form-vs-momentum") doctrine that re-frames the twenty-eight-pulse system as combinations of a small number of dimensions.
- *Mài jiǎn bǔyì* 脈簡補義 [[KR3eb037]] (1893); *Biànmài píngmài zhāngjù* 辨脈平脈章句 [[KR3eb038]] (1893, close commentary defending the *Shānghán lùn*'s pulse chapters); *Chóngdìng zhěn jiā zhíjué* 重訂診家直訣 [[KR3eb007]] (1891–1896, the cycle's revised abridgement); plus the non-pulse-diagnostic *Xíngsè wàizhěn jiǎnmó* 形色外診簡摩 [[KR3eb049]] (1894).

**The late-Qīng to Republican philological reconstructions.** *Mài jīng chāo* 脈經鈔 [[KR3eb029]] (Sūn Dǐngyí 孫鼎宜, 1909) is a *kǎojù* reconstruction of Wáng Shūhé's *Mài jīng*; *Mài jué xīnbiān* 脈訣新編 [[KR3eb030]] (Liú Běnchāng 劉本昌, 1939–1941) defends Wáng's pulse-position allocations against early-Republican critique.

**The four-diagnostics (四診) genre.** Works that re-balance the diagnostic art by re-asserting the *Nèijīng*'s integration of inspection, listening, questioning, and pulse-taking against the centuries-long over-emphasis on pulse alone.
- *Chá bìng zhǐnán* 察病指南 [[KR3eb002]] (Shī Fā 施發, 1241) — the earliest illustrated pulse text and the earliest sustained Southern-Sòng manual to subordinate pulse-feeling to the wider diagnostic art (33 schematic pulse-diagrams).
- *Sì zhěn juéwēi* 四診抉微 [[KR3eb003]] (Lín Zhīhàn 林之瀚, 1723) — the most systematic early-Qīng restoration of the *Nèijīng* hierarchy of four diagnostics.
- *Yī zōng jīnjiàn · Sì zhěn xīnfǎ yàojué* 醫宗金鑑·四診心法要訣 [[KR3eb004]] ([[吳謙]] Wú Qiān et al., 1742) — the Qiánlóng-imperial one-juan four-diagnostics curriculum in heptasyllabic mnemonic verse, the standard training text for the Qīng imperial medical examinations.

**Inspection and non-pulse diagnostics.** *Wàng zhěn zūnjīng* 望診遵經 [[KR3eb050]] (Wāng Hóng 汪宏, 1875) is the most systematic single-author Qīng treatment of inspection-diagnosis; *Xíngsè wàizhěn jiǎnmó* [[KR3eb049]] (Zhōu Xuéhǎi, 1894) supplies the form-and-colour external-inspection complement to the Zhōu pulse cycle.

**The tongue-diagnostic sequence.** A short, internally-connected lineage runs from the mid-fourteenth to the early twentieth century.
- *Áoshì Shānghán Jīnjìng lù* 敖氏傷寒金鏡錄 [[KR3eb051]] ([[杜本]] Dù Běn, 1341, recompiling a lost work of Áo shì 敖氏) — **the earliest surviving Chinese treatise on tongue-diagnosis**: 36 numbered tongue-figures pairing colour-and-coating types with *Shānghán* syndromes and canonical prescriptions; methodological ancestor of every later work in the line.
- *Shānghán shéjiàn* 傷寒舌鑑 [[KR3eb054]] (Zhāng Dēng 張登, 1668) — the high-Qīng synthesis of the ÁoDù tradition: 120 figures organised by tongue-coating colour, the taxonomic standard for the next two centuries.
- *Línzhèng yànshé fǎ* 臨症驗舌法 [[KR3eb052]] (Yáng Yúnfēng 楊雲峰, c. 1830–1873) — extends tongue-diagnosis beyond *Shānghán* to general clinical use; introduces a three-axis (form / texture / colour) analytic.
- *Cháshé biànzhèng xīnfǎ* 察舌辨症新法 [[KR3eb053]] (Liú Héngruì 劉恆瑞, c. 1910–1916) — first Republican-era tongue manual; eight-fold inspection framework integrated with Western anatomical knowledge.
- *Shé zhěn wèndá* 舌診問答 [[KR3eb055]] (Hé Shū 何舒, 1947); *Biàn shé zhǐnán* 辨舌指南 [[KR3eb056]] (Cáo Bǐngzhāng 曹炳章, 1920) — the latter the encyclopaedic six-juan, 144-colour-plate Republican summa of the genre, integrating Western histology with the classical *shézhěn* canon.

## Important persons

- [[王叔和]] (Wáng Shūhé, *zì* Xī 熙, c. 210–285, *Tàiyī lìng* of Western Jìn) — author of the *Mài jīng* [[KR3eb011]]; the founder of the entire pulse-diagnostic genre, and the figure to whom (or *against* whom) every later text in the division positions itself.
- [[崔嘉彥]] (Cuī Jiāyàn, *hào* Zǐxū 紫虛, *fl.* c. 1150–1190, Southern Sòng) — author of the four-character mnemonic *Mài jué* [[KR3eb025]] that founded the principal mnemonic-pedagogy lineage of the genre.
- [[施發]] (Shī Fā, *fl.* c. 1241, Southern Sòng) — author of *Chá bìng zhǐnán* [[KR3eb002]]; the earliest sustained Sòng attempt at illustrated pulse-diagnostics and at re-asserting the wider four-diagnostics.
- [[滑壽]] (Huá Shòu, c. 1304–1386) — author of *Zhěn jiā shūyào* [[KR3eb023]]; the principal YuánMíng critic of the pseudo-*Mài jué*'s seven-eight-nine classification and a key relay back to the *Mài jīng* and the *Nánjīng*.
- [[戴起宗]] (Dài Qǐzōng, *fl.* c. 1300–1341, Yuán) — author of *Mài jué kānwù* [[KR3eb026]]; the first sustained line-by-line refutation of the pseudo-*Mài jué*, repeatedly re-issued through the Míng with [[汪機]] Wāng Jī's supplements.
- [[杜本]] (Dù Běn, *zì* Bóyuán 伯原, *hào* Qīngbì 清碧, 1276–1350) — recompiler of the *Áoshì Shānghán Jīnjìng lù* [[KR3eb051]] of 1341, the foundational text of the tongue-diagnostic tradition.
- [[李時珍]] (Lǐ Shízhēn, *hào* Bīnhú 瀕湖, 1518–1593) — author of *Bīnhú màixué* [[KR3eb014]] and *Mài jué kǎozhèng* [[KR3eb019]]; founder of the late-Míng *Bīnhú* school that produced the standard mnemonic of the imperial era. His father [[李言聞]] Lǐ Yánwén ([[KR3eb033]]) was the immediate transmitter of the Cuī Jiāyàn tradition to the Lǐ household.
- [[吳昆]] (Wú Kūn, *zì* Shānfǔ 山甫, 1552–1620) — author of *Mài yǔ* [[KR3eb040]]; representative late-Míng integrator of colour-and-pulse diagnostics.
- [[李中梓]] (Lǐ Zhōngzǐ, 1588–1655) and his student-collator Lǐ Yánshì 李延罡 (also written 李延是, *fl.* 1660s) — joint authors of the *Zhěn jiā zhèngyǎn* [[KR3eb020]] and the *Mài jué huìbiàn* [[KR3eb018]]; the principal late-Míng / early-Qīng pulse-pedagogues, fixing the twenty-eight-pulse system as standard.
- [[張璐]] (Zhāng Lù, *zì* Lùyù 路玉, 1617–1699) — author of *Zhěn zōng sānmèi* [[KR3eb022]]; the high-Kāngxī master of integrated diagnostic palpation.
- [[張登]] (Zhāng Dēng, *fl.* c. 1660s) — eldest son of Zhāng Lù; author of the *Shānghán shéjiàn* [[KR3eb054]] of 1668, the synthesis that fixed the tongue-diagnostic colour-coating taxonomy for the next two centuries.
- [[吳謙]] (Wú Qiān, c. 1689–1748) — *Tàiyī yuàn* compiler-in-chief of the imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* [[KR3eb004]]; through the *Sì zhěn xīnfǎ yàojué* he fixed the four-diagnostics curriculum of the Qiánlóng-era state medical examinations.
- [[黃宮繡]] (Huáng Gōngxiù, c. 1730–1817) and [[沈金鰲]] (Shěn Jīn'áo, 1717–1776) — mid-Qiánlóng pulse-pedagogues who shifted the genre from polemic against the pseudo-*Mài jué* to constructive synthesis (KR3eb024; KR3eb027–KR3eb028).
- [[周學海]] (Zhōu Xuéhǎi, 1856–1906) — author of the five-work Zhōu pulse-and-external-diagnostic cycle ([[KR3eb007]], [[KR3eb036]]–[[KR3eb038]], [[KR3eb049]]); the most analytically sophisticated late-Qīng diagnostician, originator of the *xíngshì* (form-vs-momentum) re-framing of the twenty-eight-pulse system.
- [[丹波元簡]] (Tanba no Genkan / Taki Mototane, 1755–1810) — Edo-period *kǎojù* synthesiser of the Chinese pulse literature in *Mài xué jíyào* [[KR3eb035]] (1795); the principal Tokugawa contribution to the genre.
- [[孫鼎宜]] (Sūn Dǐngyí, *fl.* 1909) — author of the late-Qīng *Mài jīng chāo* [[KR3eb029]], a philological reconstruction of Wáng Shūhé's *Mài jīng* opening the modern critical-edition phase.
- [[曹炳章]] (Cáo Bǐngzhāng, 1878–1956) — Shàoxīng-based Republican-era physician and bibliographer; author of *Biàn shé zhǐnán* [[KR3eb056]] of 1920, the encyclopaedic closing summa of the tongue-diagnostic tradition.

## Topics

The division is organised around a small number of intersecting debates and genre conventions.

**Authentic *Mài jīng* versus pseudo-*Mài jué*.** The single largest fault-line in pulse-diagnostic literature. The pseudo-*Mài jué*'s *qī biǎo bā lǐ jiǔ dào* classification of the pulses, its falsely-attributed WángShūhé authorship, and its incompatibility with the *Mài jīng*'s twenty-four-pulse system became the central polemical target from Dài Qǐzōng (1341) and Huá Shòu (1359) through Lǐ Shízhēn (1564–1593), Wú Kūn (1584), Lǐ Yánshì (1664–1666), and Huáng Gōngxiù (1769). The polemic is finally closed in the high-Qīng, after which serious pulse manuals work directly from the *Mài jīng* and the *Nèijīng*.

**The pulse-set: 24, 27, 28, or other?** Wáng Shūhé's twenty-four pulses; Cuī Jiāyàn's seven-eight-nine framework; the pseudo-*Mài jué*'s *qī biǎo bā lǐ jiǔ dào*; Lǐ Shízhēn's twenty-seven; Lǐ Zhōngzǐ's twenty-eight (the addition of *jí* 疾); the *Yīzōng jīnjiàn*'s twenty-eight; and Zhōu Xuéhǎi's dimensional reduction to *xíng* + *shì* form a continuous taxonomic debate that runs the length of the division.

**The *cùnguānchǐ* viscera-allocation problem.** Wáng Shūhé's allocation of the *xiǎocháng* and *dàcháng* to the *cùn* position, and of the kidney with the *mìngmén* / triple-burner to the *chǐ* position, departs from a strand of *Nèijīng* doctrine and was disputed across the centuries — by Huá Shòu in the Yuán, by Yú Jiāyán in the Qīng, and again in the *Sì zhěn juéwēi* of 1723 and *Mài lǐ qiúzhēn* of 1769. The debate cuts across the entire division.

**Pulse alone versus *sì zhěn*.** Throughout the imperial era pulse-feeling acquired a near-mystical professional status (epitomised in the *Tàisù mài* tradition [[KR3eb017]] of pulse-as-fate-divination), and almost every period produced a counter-reaction asserting the *Nèijīng* ideal of four-fold inspection. Shī Fā (1241), Lín Zhīhàn (1723), and Wú Qiān et al. (1742) are the principal restorers; Wāng Hóng (1875) and Zhōu Xuéhǎi (1894) push the rebalancing further by producing the first sustained inspection-and-external-diagnostic monographs.

**The tongue-diagnostic tradition.** A relatively young branch of the diagnostic art, dating from the 1341 Áo–Dù *Jīnjìng lù*. Tongue-diagnosis was first developed inside the *Shānghán* tradition for differentiating cold-damage syndromes ([[KR3eb051]] [[KR3eb054]]) and then, in the late-Qīng and Republican era, extended to general clinical use (Yáng Yúnfēng, Liú Héngruì) and integrated with Western anatomical knowledge (Cáo Bǐngzhāng).

**Mnemonic verse, illustration, and the popular market.** From Cuī Jiāyàn's four-character mnemonic and the heptasyllabic pseudo-*Mài jué* through Lǐ Shízhēn's *Bīnhú* poems, Shěn Jīn'áo's *Zhūmài zhǔbìng shī*, and Wú Qiān's imperial *Sì zhěn xīnfǎ yàojué*, the diagnostic genre has been distinctively pedagogical and mnemonic. The pseudo-*Mài jué*'s commercial popularity drove an entire late-Míng publishing genre of illustrated, vernacular-glossed commercial editions ([[KR3eb045]] [[KR3eb046]] [[KR3eb048]]).

**Late-Qīng and Republican integration with Western medicine.** The closing stratum of the division (KR3eb053, KR3eb056) consciously integrates classical Chinese diagnostic categories with Western anatomy, histology, and pathology — paralleling the *Nèijīng*-side work of [[唐宗海]] Táng Zōnghǎi in [[KR3ea044]] within the same broader programme.

**The Daoist–Buddhist contemplative framing.** *Sān zhǐ chán* 三指禪 [[KR3eb021]] (Zhōu Xuétíng, 1827) and *Mài jué chǎnwēi* [[KR3eb039]] (Chén Shìduó, 1687) frame pulse-mastery as a contemplative or revelatory practice — a minor but persistent strand inside an otherwise *kǎojù*-oriented genre.
