# Mài Xiàng Tǒng Lèi 脈象統類
*A Comprehensive Classification of Pulse Images*
by [[沈金鰲]] (Shěn Jīn'áo, *zì* Zhīwēi 芷薇, *hào* Zùnshēng zǐ 尊生子, 1717–1776, 清)

## About the work

A one-juan pulse-classification compendium by the mid-Qiánlóng Wúxī 無錫 physician Shěn Jīn'áo, one component of his very influential *Shěn shì zùnshēng shū* 沈氏尊生書 medical *cóngshū* (1773). The book applies a **six-character organising rubric** — *fúchén chíshù huásè* 浮沉遲數滑澀 (floating-sunken, slow-rapid, slippery-rough) — as a six-fold matrix into which all twenty-eight standard pulse types are sorted. Shěn argues in his programmatic opening that the inherited twenty-eight-pulse repertoire is unwieldy without an organising classification; the six-character matrix gives the practitioner a working clinical taxonomy.

### Abstract

Shěn Jīn'áo 沈金鰲 (1717–1776) was an Wúxī polymath who turned from civil-examination studies to medicine in middle age and produced, in the years 1760–1773, the seventy-juan *Shěn shì zùnshēng shū* — covering pulse, *Shānghán*, paediatrics, women's medicine, formulary, and *bíngjī* pathology — which remained one of the standard Qing medical training compendia. The *Mài xiàng tǒng lèi* is the pulse component of that work; the companion *Zhū mài zhǔ bìng shī* 諸脈主病詩 [[KR3eb028]] supplies the mnemonic verses that accompany it. Shěn's pulse-classification scheme stands within the High-Qing rationalising tradition that includes Huáng Gōngxiù's [[KR3eb024]] *Mài lǐ qiú zhēn* (also of the 1760s); the two works represent contemporary but distinct attempts at the same project.
