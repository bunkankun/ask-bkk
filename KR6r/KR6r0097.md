# Chán-yuàn méng-qiú yáo-lín 禪苑蒙求瑤林
*Introductory Inquiries on the Chán Garden — A Forest of Jade*

original 3-juan compendium by [[志明]] (Bó-hūn / Cuò-ān Zhì-míng, fl. late 12th c., 撰), Jīn-dynasty Cáo-dòng monk of Sōng-shān Shào-lín-sì; with annotation by the Yuán Cáo-dòng master [[德諫]] (Xuě-táng Dé-jiàn, fl. mid-13th c., 注)

## About the work

A 3-juan Chán-school analogue of the secular *méng-qiú* 蒙求 ("introductory inquiry") learning-handbook tradition. The genre originates with **Lǐ Hàn** 李翰's mid-Táng *Méng-qiú* (746) — a paired-couplet compendium of historical references designed for novice-memorisation, in which each couplet pairs two figures or events with characteristically symmetric four-character phrases (e.g., *Kǒng yán suí yuán / Mèng mǔ duàn jī*, "Confucius spoke at Suí-yuán / Mencius's mother cut the cloth"). The form was widely imitated throughout the Sòng for various subject-matter: poetry-history, materia-medica, and (here) the Chán tradition.

### Abstract

[[志明]]'s *Chán-yuàn méng-qiú yáo-lín* extracts from the canonical Chán-historical tradition — primarily [[KR6q0001]] *Jǐng-dé chuán-dēng lù* and successor *dēng-lù* — its principal lineage-figures and pivotal incidents, and arranges them as **paired four-character couplets** for memorisation by Chán novices. Each couplet juxtaposes two figures or events from the Chán lineage tradition with structurally parallel phrasings, designed to fix the principal coordinates of the lineage in the novice's memory.

The original text by [[志明]] survives in the canon together with the **prose annotation** (*zhù* 注) of [[德諫]], a Yuán Cáo-dòng dharma-heir of Wàn-sōng Xíng-xiù 萬松行秀 resident at the Mongol-capital monastery Yān-jīng Dà Wàn-shòu-sì. Dé-jiàn's annotation glosses each couplet with prose elaborations identifying the persons, events, and texts referenced. The composite annotated work is the standard form transmitted in the canon. The bracket of dating reflects the two-stage composition: [[志明]]'s original from the late 12th c. (under the Jīn), Dé-jiàn's annotation from the mid-13th c. (under the early Yuán).

The work is one of the principal **pedagogical** documents of the Cáo-dòng tradition specifically: both [[志明]] and Dé-jiàn were Cáo-dòng masters, the original was composed at Shào-lín-sì (the Cáo-dòng establishment), and the work transmits the Cáo-dòng-school presentation of the Chán lineage. The Manji Xuzangjing (X87 no. 1614) preserves the text on the basis of late-Míng / Qīng prints.

## Other points of interest

The work is one of the principal canonical witnesses to the **Jīn-dynasty Buddhist establishment** of the north — i.e., the Buddhist culture of the Jurchen-ruled territory that was institutionally separated from the Southern Sòng south after 1142. The Jīn-Buddhist Cáo-dòng establishment at Sōng-shān Shào-lín-sì, where [[志明]] was abbot, was the principal northern Cáo-dòng centre and the parent-house of the lineage that produced **Wàn-sōng Xíng-xiù** 萬松行秀 — the great early-Yuán Cáo-dòng master and Dé-jiàn's teacher — in the next generation. The work thus documents a **Jīn–Yuán Cáo-dòng continuity** that the Southern-Sòng-centred *dēng-lù* tradition tends to obscure.
