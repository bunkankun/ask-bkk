### Scope and scholarly tradition

KR6r 史傳部 (*Shǐ-zhuàn bù*) collects the historiographical, biographical, hagiographical, apologetic, and miracle-tale literature of the Chinese Buddhist canon. In Taishō terms the division corresponds primarily to volumes 49–52 (T2026–T2120), supplemented by Xù-zàngjīng / Wàn-zì 卍 *Xuzangjing* materials for late Sòng, Yuán, Míng, and Qīng works that did not enter the Taishō main canon. The matching CANWWW division (`div21.xml`, 史傳部) preserves the same opening sequence — *Zhuǎnjí sānzàng jí zázàng zhuàn* 撰集三藏及雜藏傳 (T49N2026, [[KR6r0001]]) at the head, followed by the Aśoka and Indian-patriarch biographies, the great 高僧傳 sequence, the chronological 統紀 histories, the apologetic 弘明 corpus, the 感應/靈驗 miracle literature, the 往生傳 Pure-Land hagiographies, and the geographical 山志/寺志 monographs — and is the structural reference for the division.

Chronologically the division reaches from late Hàn translation literature (the *Jiāyè jié jīng* 迦葉結經, c. 148–170, [[KR6r0002]]) to late Qīng and early-Republican compilations (the *Xiūxī wénjiàn lù* 修西聞見錄, c. 1860–1880, [[KR6r0083]]; the Japanese-edited *Xuánzàng sānzàng shīzī zhuàn cóngshū* 玄奘三藏師資傳叢書, c. 1900–1916, [[KR6r0048]]). Generically it is the most miscellaneous of the canonical divisions: a single shelf joins translated Indian historical lore (the Aśoka cycle, the four-patriarch biographies, the *Yìbù zōnglún lùn* 異部宗輪論 on the eighteen schools), continuator chains of Chinese-language biography (*Gāosēng zhuàn* 高僧傳 → *Xù gāosēng zhuàn* 續高僧傳 → *Sòng gāosēng zhuàn* 宋高僧傳 → *Dà-Míng gāosēng zhuàn* 大明高僧傳 → *Bǔ-xù gāosēng zhuàn* 補續高僧傳), sectarian lineage charters (Tiāntái 釋門正統, Chán *Chuánfǎ zhèngzōng jì* 傳法正宗記, Pure-Land *Wǎngshēng* 往生 collections, esoteric 兩部 transmissions), polemics for and against the dharma (*Hóngmíng jí* 弘明集 → *Guǎng hóngmíng jí* 廣弘明集 → *Biàn wěi lù* 辯偽錄 → *Sān-jiào píngxīn lùn* 三教平心論), pilgrimage accounts (*Fǎxiǎn zhuàn* 法顯傳, *Dà-Táng Xīyù jì* 大唐西域記, *Nánhǎi jìguī nèifǎ zhuàn* 南海寄歸內法傳), and sacred-site gazetteers (*Luòyáng qiélán jì* 洛陽伽藍記, *Gǔ Qīngliáng zhuàn* 古清涼傳, *Lúshān jì* 廬山記, *Pǔtuó-Luòjiā shān zhuàn* 補陀洛迦山傳). The principal scholarly traditions a reader must keep in view are accordingly four: (1) the philological transmission of translated Indian materials, where pre-*Gāosēng zhuàn* sources and the early translator attributions are often unstable; (2) the Liáng → Táng → Sòng → Míng → Qīng continuator structure of monk biography, where each *gāosēng zhuàn* is read against its predecessor; (3) the Sòng-Yuán project of Buddhist universal history modelled on Sīmǎ Guāng's 資治通鑑 (*Fózǔ tǒngjì* 佛祖統紀, *Fózǔ lìdài tōngzài* 佛祖歷代通載); and (4) the running 三教 (Three Teachings) polemic from Mòuzǐ down to Qīng layman apologetics, the textual record of which is concentrated almost entirely in this division.

### Important texts and text clusters

- **Indian historical lore in translation.** *Āyù wáng zhuàn* 阿育王傳 [[KR6r0031]] (Ān Fǎqīn 安法欽, late 3rd c.) and *Āyù wáng jīng* 阿育王經 [[KR6r0032]] (Sēnghéluó 僧伽婆羅, c. 506–511) are the two principal Chinese recensions of the Aśoka cycle and the source-base for all later Chinese knowledge of Mauryan Buddhist history.
- **Four-patriarch biographies.** *Mǎmíng púsà zhuàn* 馬鳴菩薩傳 [[KR6r0035]] (Aśvaghoṣa), *Lóngshù púsà zhuàn* 龍樹菩薩傳 [[KR6r0036]] (Nāgārjuna), *Típó púsà zhuàn* 提婆菩薩傳 [[KR6r0038]] (Āryadeva) — all translated by Kumārajīva c. 401–413 — and *Pó-sǒu pán-dòu fǎshī zhuàn* 婆藪槃豆法師傳 [[KR6r0039]] (Vasubandhu, by Paramārtha) anchor the Chinese tradition's image of the Indian doctrinal patriarchs.
- **Eighteen-schools cluster.** *Yìbù zōnglún lùn* 異部宗輪論 [[KR6r0007]] (Vasumitra, tr. Xuánzàng 662), Kuījī's commentary *Yìbù zōnglún lùn shù-jì* 異部宗輪論疏述記 [[KR6r0008]], and Paramārtha's parallel translations *Shíbā bù lùn* 十八部論 [[KR6r0009]] and *Bù-zhí yì lùn* 部執異論 [[KR6r0010]] are the canonical Chinese sources on the sectarian history of early Indian Buddhism.
- **The 高僧傳 continuator series.** *Gāosēng zhuàn* 高僧傳 [[KR6r0052]] (Huìjiǎo, 519) inaugurates the genre; *Xù gāosēng zhuàn* 續高僧傳 [[KR6r0053]] (Dàoxuān, 645–667) and *Sòng gāosēng zhuàn* 宋高僧傳 [[KR6r0054]] (Zànníng et al., 982–988) are its core continuations; *Dà-Míng gāosēng zhuàn* 大明高僧傳 [[KR6r0055]] (Rúxīng, 1617) and *Bǔ-xù gāosēng zhuàn* 補續高僧傳 [[KR6r0063]] (Míng-hé 明河) extend the sequence into the Míng. *Bǐqiūní zhuàn* 比丘尼傳 [[KR6r0056]] (Bǎochàng, c. 516–519) is the indispensable parallel for bhikṣuṇī biography.
- **Buddhist universal histories.** *Lìdài sānbǎo jì* 歷代三寶紀 [[KR6r0011]] (Fèi Chángfáng, 597) is the foundational dynasty-by-dynasty register; *Fózǔ tǒngjì* 佛祖統紀 [[KR6r0012]] (Zhìpán, 1258–1269) is the Tiāntái-perspective magnum opus structured on Sīmǎ Guāng's annals model; *Fózǔ lìdài tōngzài* 佛祖歷代通載 [[KR6r0013]] (Niànchāng, 1333–1341) and *Shìshì jīgǔ lüè* 釋氏稽古略 [[KR6r0014]] (Juéàn, 1351–1354) are the Yuán counterparts, with *Shìjiàn jīgǔ lüè xùjí* 釋鑑稽古略續集 [[KR6r0015]] (Huànlún 幻輪, 1576) carrying the chronology forward.
- **Apologetic compilations.** *Hóngmíng jí* 弘明集 [[KR6r0137]] (Sēngyòu, c. 502–518) and its Táng continuation *Guǎng hóngmíng jí* 廣弘明集 [[KR6r0138]] (Dàoxuān, 664) are the foundational anthologies of pro-Buddhist polemic and Three-Teachings debate; *Pò xié lùn* 破邪論 [[KR6r0142]] and *Biàn zhèng lùn* 辯正論 [[KR6r0143]] (Fǎlín, early Táng) and *Jí gǔjīn fó-dào lùn-héng* 集古今佛道論衡 [[KR6r0139]] (Dàoxuān, 661) document the high-Táng phase of the contest; the Sòng-Yuán phase is carried by Qìsōng's *Hùfǎ lùn* 護法論 [[KR6r0147]] and *Tánjīn wénjí* 鐔津文集 [[KR6r0148]], by Yuán *Biàn wěi lù* 辯偽錄 [[KR6r0149]] (against the Daoist 化胡 charge), and by *Sān-jiào píngxīn lùn* 三教平心論 [[KR6r0150]] and *Zhé yí lùn* 折疑論 [[KR6r0151]].
- **Chán transmission charters.** *Fù fǎzàng yīnyuán zhuàn* 付法藏因緣傳 [[KR6r0051]] (Jíjiāyè/Tánjyào, 472) supplies the Indian half of the transmission line; *Chuánfǎ zhèngzōng jì* 傳法正宗記 [[KR6r0100]], *Chuánfǎ zhèngzōng dìng-zǔ tú* 傳法正宗定祖圖 [[KR6r0101]], and *Chuánfǎ zhèngzōng lùn* 傳法正宗論 [[KR6r0102]] (Qìsōng, 1061–1064) consolidate the Sòng-Chán version. *Cáoxī dàshī biézhuàn* 曹溪大師別傳 [[KR6r0108]] is the early independent life of Huìnéng.
- **Pilgrimage and ethnography.** *Gāosēng Fǎxiǎn zhuàn* 高僧法顯傳 [[KR6r0119]] (Fǎxiǎn, c. 414–422), *Dà-Táng Xīyù jì* 大唐西域記 [[KR6r0121]] (Xuánzàng, dictated to Biànjī, 646), *Dà-Táng Xīyù qiúfǎ gāosēng zhuàn* 大唐西域求法高僧傳 [[KR6r0059]] and *Nánhǎi jìguī nèifǎ zhuàn* 南海寄歸內法傳 [[KR6r0126]] (Yìjìng, 691), together with *Běi-Wèi sēng Huìshēng shǐ Xīyù jì* 北魏僧惠生使西域記 [[KR6r0120]] and the *Yóu-fāng jì chāo* 遊方記抄 [[KR6r0123]] (Wùkōng et al.), constitute the world's principal medieval corpus on Indian and Central Asian Buddhism.
- **Xuánzàng-cycle hagiography.** *Dà-Táng gù sānzàng Xuánzàng fǎshī xíng-zhuàng* 大唐故三藏玄奘法師行狀 [[KR6r0042]] and the monumental *Dà-Táng Dà-Cí'ēn-sì sānzàng fǎshī zhuàn* 大唐大慈恩寺三藏法師傳 [[KR6r0043]] (Huìlì base text, 彥悰 Yàncóng's redaction) are the primary biographical sources for Xuánzàng; the 玄奘上表記 [[KR6r0154]] preserves his memorials.
- **Esoteric (mìzōng) lineage records.** *Xuánzōng cháo fānjīng sānzàng Shàn-wú-wèi zèng Hónglú qīng xíng-zhuàng* 玄宗朝翻經三藏善無畏贈鴻臚卿行狀 [[KR6r0045]] (Śubhakarasiṃha), *Dà-Táng gù dàdé zèng sī-kōng Dà-biànzhèng-guǎngzhì sānzàng xíng-zhuàng* 大唐故大德贈司空大辨正廣智不空三藏行狀 [[KR6r0046]] (Amoghavajra), the *Huìguǒ héshàng xíng-zhuàng* 惠果和尚行狀 [[KR6r0049]], and *Liǎng-bù dàfǎ xiāng-chéng shīzī fùfǎ jì* 兩部大法相承師資付法記 [[KR6r0115]] record the eighth-century esoteric school that anchored Japanese Shingon.
- **Buddha-biography sequence.** *Shìjiā pǔ* 釋迦譜 [[KR6r0025]] (Sēngyòu, early 6th c.), *Shìjiāshì pǔ* 釋迦氏譜 [[KR6r0026]] (Dàoxuān, 665), the verse *Shìjiā rúlái chéng-dào jì* 釋迦如來成道記 [[KR6r0027]] and its commentary [[KR6r0028]], the Koryŏ *Shìjiā rúlái xíng-jì sòng* 釋迦如來行蹟頌 [[KR6r0029]] (Ūnmuk 雲默, 14th c.), and *Shìjiā rúlái yìnghuà lù* 釋迦如來應化錄 [[KR6r0030]] form a continuous Chinese-language tradition of Śākyamuni-life literature.
- **Miracle and stimulus-response literature.** Sūtra-specific 感應/靈驗 collections include the *Hóng-zàn Fǎhuá zhuàn* 弘贊法華傳 [[KR6r0066]] and *Fǎhuá zhuàn-jì* 法華傳記 [[KR6r0067]] for the *Lotus*; *Huáyán jīng zhuànjì* 華嚴經傳記 [[KR6r0084]] and the Huáyán 感應 cluster [[KR6r0085]] [[KR6r0087]] [[KR6r0088]] [[KR6r0089]]; and a substantial Diamond-sūtra group [[KR6r0174]] [[KR6r0175]] [[KR6r0176]] [[KR6r0177]] [[KR6r0178]] [[KR6r0179]] [[KR6r0180]] [[KR6r0181]]. General-purpose miracle collections include *Míngbào jì* 冥報記 [[KR6r0116]] (Táng Lín 唐臨), *Sān-bǎo gǎnyīng yàolüè lù* 三寶感應要略錄 [[KR6r0118]] (Liáo), *Jí Shénzhōu sānbǎo gǎntōng lù* 集神州三寶感通錄 [[KR6r0152]] (Dàoxuān), and the Qīng *Jiànwén lù* 見聞錄 [[KR6r0163]] and *Xiàn-guǒ suíǐ-lù* 現果隨錄 [[KR6r0164]].
- **Pure-Land 往生傳 cluster.** *Wǎngshēng xīfāng jìngtǔ ruì-yìng zhuàn* 往生西方淨土瑞應傳 [[KR6r0074]] (Shàokāng 少康/Wénshěn 文諗, 805), *Jìngtǔ wǎngshēng zhuàn* 淨土往生傳 [[KR6r0075]] (Jièzhū 戒珠, 1064), *Xīn-xiū wǎngshēng zhuàn* 新修往生傳 [[KR6r0078]] (Wáng Gǔ 王古, late 11th c.), *Wǎngshēng jí* 往生集 [[KR6r0076]] (Yúnqī Zhūhóng 雲棲祩宏, 1584), and the great Qīng compilations *Jìngtǔ shèngxián lù* 淨土聖賢錄 [[KR6r0080]] and its continuation [[KR6r0081]] form the standard Pure-Land hagiographical sequence.
- **Sacred-geography monographs.** *Luòyáng qiélán jì* 洛陽伽藍記 [[KR6r0127]] (Yáng Xuànzhī, 547) is the foundational urban-monastic gazetteer; the Wǔtái cluster — *Gǔ Qīngliáng zhuàn* 古清涼傳 [[KR6r0133]], *Guǎng Qīngliáng zhuàn* 廣清涼傳 [[KR6r0134]], *Xù Qīngliáng zhuàn* 續清涼傳 [[KR6r0135]] — covers the Mañjuśrī mountain; *Lúshān jì* 廬山記 [[KR6r0130]] (Chén Shùn-yǔ 陳舜俞, 1071–1075), *Tiāntái-shān jì* 天台山記 [[KR6r0131]], *Nán-Yuè zǒng-shèng jí* 南嶽總勝集 [[KR6r0132]], *Bǔtuó-Luòjiā shān zhuàn* 補陀洛迦山傳 [[KR6r0136]], and *Sì-tǎ jì* 寺塔記 [[KR6r0128]] (Duàn Chéng-shì 段成式) round out the sacred-site corpus.
- **Korean and Japanese works.** The Korean *Sāmguk yusa* 三國遺事 [[KR6r0016]] (Iryŏn, 1281–1283) and *Hǎidōng gāosēng zhuàn* 海東高僧傳 [[KR6r0058]] (Kakhun, 1215), the *Cháoxiǎn Chán-Jiào kǎo* 朝鮮禪教考 [[KR6r0114]] and *Dōngguó sēngní lù* 東國僧尼錄 [[KR6r0024]], and Choi Chiwŏn's *Táng Dà-Jiànfú-sì gù sì-zhǔ fānjīng dàdé Fǎzàng héshàng zhuàn* 唐大薦福寺故寺主翻經大德法藏和尚傳 [[KR6r0044]] preserve the Korean tradition; the Japanese-edited *Xuánzàng sānzàng shīzī zhuàn cóngshū* 玄奘三藏師資傳叢書 [[KR6r0048]] and *Míngbào jì jí-shū* 冥報記輯書 [[KR6r0168]] supply early-20th-century compilations.
- **Late-imperial layman biography.** *Jūshì zhuàn* 居士傳 [[KR6r0185]] and *Shàn-nǚrén zhuàn* 善女人傳 [[KR6r0186]] (both Péng Jìqīng 彭際清, 1770s) are the standard Qīng hagiographies of lay men and women practitioners.

### Important persons

- [[安世高]] — Parthian translator at the Later-Hàn capital and the earliest translator represented in the division (*Jiāyè jié jīng* 迦葉結經, c. 148–170, [[KR6r0002]]).
- [[鳩摩羅什]] — Kumārajīva, around 401–413 the translator who fixes the Chinese image of the four Indian patriarchs ([[KR6r0035]]–[[KR6r0038]]).
- [[真諦]] — Paramārtha, mid-6th c., translator of *Pó-sǒu pán-dòu fǎshī zhuàn* 婆藪槃豆法師傳 [[KR6r0039]], *Shíbā bù lùn* [[KR6r0009]], and *Bù-zhí yì lùn* [[KR6r0010]] — the chief alternative to Xuánzàng on the eighteen schools.
- [[僧祐]] — Sēngyòu (445–518), Liáng-era cataloguer-historian, author of *Hóngmíng jí* 弘明集 [[KR6r0137]] and *Shìjiā pǔ* 釋迦譜 [[KR6r0025]]; founder of the apologetic-anthology and Buddha-biography genres.
- [[慧皎]] — Huìjiǎo (497–554), author of *Gāosēng zhuàn* 高僧傳 [[KR6r0052]]; defines the genre that runs through the division.
- [[寶唱]] — Bǎochàng (6th c.), Liáng court compiler responsible for *Bǐqiūní zhuàn* 比丘尼傳 [[KR6r0056]] and the surviving abstract of his lost *Míng-sēng zhuàn* 名僧傳 [[KR6r0062]].
- [[楊衒之]] — Yáng Xuànzhī, author of *Luòyáng qiélán jì* 洛陽伽藍記 [[KR6r0127]] (547) — the central source for Northern-dynasties Buddhist urbanism.
- [[費長房]] — Fèi Chángfáng, Suí compiler of *Lìdài sānbǎo jì* 歷代三寶紀 [[KR6r0011]] (597), the prototypical dynasty-by-dynasty Buddhist chronicle.
- [[法顯]] — Fǎxiǎn, late-4th / early-5th c. pilgrim, author of *Gāosēng Fǎxiǎn zhuàn* 高僧法顯傳 [[KR6r0119]].
- [[玄奘]] — Xuánzàng (602–664), pilgrim, translator, and dictator of *Dà-Táng Xīyù jì* 大唐西域記 [[KR6r0121]]; the principal biographical subject of [[KR6r0042]] [[KR6r0043]] [[KR6r0048]] [[KR6r0154]] and translator of *Yìbù zōnglún lùn* [[KR6r0007]] and *Fǎ-zhù jì* [[KR6r0005]].
- [[辯機]] — Biànjī, redactor of Xuánzàng's *Xīyù jì* [[KR6r0121]].
- [[道宣]] — Dàoxuān (596–667), Vinaya patriarch and the division's single most prolific author: *Xù gāosēng zhuàn* [[KR6r0053]], *Guǎng hóngmíng jí* [[KR6r0138]], *Jí gǔjīn fó-dào lùn-héng* [[KR6r0139]], *Jí Shénzhōu sānbǎo gǎntōng lù* [[KR6r0152]], *Dàoxuān lǜshī gǎntōng lù* [[KR6r0153]], *Shìjiāshì pǔ* [[KR6r0026]], *Shìjiā fāng-zhì* [[KR6r0122]], *Jí shāmén bù yīng bài-sú děng shì* [[KR6r0141]].
- [[慧立]] — Huìlì (615–c. 675), base author of *Dà-Cí'ēn-sì sānzàng fǎshī zhuàn* [[KR6r0043]].
- [[彥悰]] — Yàncóng, redactor and annotator of Huìlì's Xuánzàng biography [[KR6r0043]] and author of *Táng hùfǎ shāmén Fǎlín biézhuàn* 唐護法沙門法琳別傳 [[KR6r0041]].
- [[窺基]] — Kuījī (632–682), Xuánzàng's principal disciple and commentator on the eighteen-schools treatise (*Yìbù zōnglún lùn shù-jì* [[KR6r0008]]).
- [[法琳]] — Fǎlín (572–640), early-Táng polemicist, author of *Pò xié lùn* [[KR6r0142]] and *Biàn zhèng lùn* [[KR6r0143]].
- [[義淨]] — Yìjìng (635–713), India pilgrim and Vinaya specialist, author of *Dà-Táng Xīyù qiúfǎ gāosēng zhuàn* [[KR6r0059]] and *Nánhǎi jìguī nèifǎ zhuàn* [[KR6r0126]].
- [[贊寧]] — Zànníng (919–1001), Sòng-court Buddhist historian; chief compiler of *Sòng gāosēng zhuàn* [[KR6r0054]] and *Dà-Sòng sēng-shǐ lüè* 大宋僧史略 [[KR6r0060]].
- [[契嵩]] — Qìsōng (1007–1072), Yún-mén Chán apologist, author of *Chuánfǎ zhèngzōng jì* / *Dìng-zǔ tú* / *Lùn* [[KR6r0100]] [[KR6r0101]] [[KR6r0102]], *Hùfǎ lùn* [[KR6r0147]], and *Tánjīn wénjí* [[KR6r0148]].
- [[慧洪]] — Juéfàn Huìhóng 覺範慧洪 (1071–1128), author of *Lín-jiān lù* 林間錄 [[KR6r0157]] and its sequel [[KR6r0158]]; the central early-Sòng witness for Chán anecdote.
- [[志磐]] — Zhìpán (fl. mid-13th c.), Tiāntái historian, author of *Fózǔ tǒngjì* [[KR6r0012]].
- [[念常]] — Niànchāng (1282–c. 1341), Yuán Chán historian, author of *Fózǔ lìdài tōngzài* [[KR6r0013]].
- [[覺岸]] — Juéàn (1286–?), Yuán Tiāntái-leaning chronicler, author of *Shìshì jīgǔ lüè* [[KR6r0014]].
- [[一然]] — Iryŏn (1206–1289), Koryŏ Sŏn monk, author of *Sāmguk yusa* [[KR6r0016]].
- [[覺訓]] — Kakhun (fl. early 13th c.), author of *Hǎidōng gāosēng zhuàn* [[KR6r0058]].
- [[崔致遠]] — Choi Chiwŏn (857–c. 910), Silla literatus and author of the *Fǎzàng héshàng zhuàn* [[KR6r0044]].
- [[祩宏]] — Yúnqī Zhūhóng (1535–1615), late-Míng Pure-Land patriarch, author of *Wǎngshēng jí* [[KR6r0076]] and *Zī-mén chóng-xíng lù* 緇門崇行錄 [[KR6r0160]].
- [[彭際清]] — Péng Jìqīng (Péng Shàoshēng 彭紹升, 1740–1796), Qīng layman, author of *Jūshì zhuàn* [[KR6r0185]] and *Shàn-nǚrén zhuàn* [[KR6r0186]]; signatory editor of *Jìngtǔ shèngxián lù* [[KR6r0080]] through his nephew Péng Xīsù 彭希涑.

### Topics

- **Buddhist historiography as a parallel canon.** The *Fózǔ tǒngjì* [[KR6r0012]], *Fózǔ lìdài tōngzài* [[KR6r0013]], *Shìshì jīgǔ lüè* [[KR6r0014]], *Lóngxīng biānnián tōnglùn* [[KR6r0017]], *Shìshì tōngjiàn* [[KR6r0020]], *Lìcháo Shìshì zī-jiàn* [[KR6r0021]], and *Zōngtǒng biānnián* [[KR6r0105]] together constitute a self-conscious imitation of the secular dynastic and tōng-jiàn forms, deliberately reading the Buddhist past against the Confucian historiographical template.
- **Continuator structure and the politics of inclusion.** The *Gāosēng zhuàn* → *Xù* → *Sòng* → *Míng* → *Bǔ-xù* sequence and its bhikṣuṇī, Pure-Land, and Chán parallels turn biography into the principal arena where the canon adjusts the boundary of who counts as orthodox at each generational turn.
- **Lineage construction (傳燈/付法/相承).** The Chán *Chuánfǎ zhèngzōng* programme of Qìsōng (1060s, [[KR6r0100]]–[[KR6r0102]]), the Tiāntái *Shìmén zhèngtǒng* [[KR6r0018]] and *Tiāntái jiǔzǔ zhuàn* [[KR6r0068]], the esoteric *Liǎng-bù dàfǎ xiāng-chéng* [[KR6r0115]], and the Yuán-Míng *Chán-dēng shì-pǔ* [[KR6r0110]] / *Fózǔ zōngpài shì-pǔ* [[KR6r0111]] / *Zī-mén shì-pǔ* [[KR6r0112]] are competing answers to the same problem: how to legitimate a school through documented descent.
- **The 三教 (Three Teachings) controversy.** Almost the entire apologetic record of the Confucian-Buddhist-Daoist contest sits in this division — Sēngyòu's *Hóngmíng jí* [[KR6r0137]] and Dàoxuān's *Guǎng hóngmíng jí* [[KR6r0138]] for the medieval phase; Fǎlín's *Pò xié* / *Biàn zhèng* [[KR6r0142]] [[KR6r0143]] for the early-Táng phase; the 拜君親 (bowing to ruler/parents) dispute in *Jí shāmén bù yīng bài-sú děng shì* [[KR6r0141]]; the Sòng-Yuán *Hùfǎ lùn* [[KR6r0147]], *Sān-jiào píngxīn lùn* [[KR6r0150]], *Zhé yí lùn* [[KR6r0151]]; and the Yuán *Biàn wěi lù* [[KR6r0149]] against the Daoist 化胡 polemic.
- **Pilgrimage and ethnography.** The Fǎxiǎn–Xuánzàng–Yìjìng triad ([[KR6r0119]] [[KR6r0121]] [[KR6r0126]]) and the lesser pilgrimage records ([[KR6r0120]] [[KR6r0059]] [[KR6r0123]]) are the principal Chinese-language record of medieval Indian and Central Asian Buddhism and the foundation of all subsequent Chinese geographical knowledge of South Asia.
- **Stimulus-response (感應) and miracle as evidential genre.** The sūtra-specific miracle collections (Lotus, Huáyán, Diamond, Guānyīn, Kṣitigarbha) and the general *Míngbào jì* / *Gǎntōng lù* tradition treat the miraculous as a quasi-empirical demonstration of doctrine, in tension with both the *zhuàn* genre's documentary norms and the apologetic literature's argumentative norms.
- **Pure-Land hagiography and the canon of *wǎng-shēng*.** The 805 *Ruì-yìng zhuàn* [[KR6r0074]] inaugurates a continuator sequence ([[KR6r0075]] [[KR6r0078]] [[KR6r0076]] [[KR6r0080]] [[KR6r0081]] [[KR6r0082]] [[KR6r0083]]) that ends with full-scale Qīng compendia and is the chief literary vehicle of Pure-Land identity formation.
- **Sacred geography.** The urban-monastic gazetteer (*Luòyáng qiélán jì*, *Sì-tǎ jì*) and the mountain gazetteer (Wǔtái, Lúshān, Tiāntái, Nán-Yuè, Pǔtuó) together build the canonical map of Chinese Buddhist sacred space.
- **Lay Buddhist subjectivity in late imperial China.** *Míng-gōng fǎ-xǐ zhì* [[KR6r0169]], *Jiànwén lù* [[KR6r0163]], *Jūshì zhuàn* [[KR6r0185]], and *Shàn-nǚrén zhuàn* [[KR6r0186]] mark the emergence of layman-centred hagiography as a distinct mode in the Míng-Qīng.
- **Korean and inter-East-Asian transmission.** *Sāmguk yusa* [[KR6r0016]], *Hǎidōng gāosēng zhuàn* [[KR6r0058]], *Cháoxiǎn Chán-Jiào kǎo* [[KR6r0114]], and the Silla *Fǎzàng héshàng zhuàn* [[KR6r0044]] situate Korean Buddhism inside the Chinese-canonical record; the late Japanese inclusions ([[KR6r0048]] [[KR6r0168]] [[KR6r0179]]) document the same continuing pan-East-Asian editorial project.
