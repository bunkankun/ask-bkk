# Kèchén yīhuà 客塵醫話
*"Dust-of-the-Guest" Medical Discourses*
by [[計楠]] Jì Nán (*hào* Shòuqiáo 壽喬, *fl.* late Qiánlóng — Jiāqìng era), a Qīng physician of the Wújiāng 吳江 (Jiāngsū) cultural milieu, associated with the West-Lake 西冷 / Hángzhōu medical circuit and a friend of the lyric poet Shěn Tāo 沈翿.

## About the work

A short one-*juǎn* compilation of medical discourse essays by Jì Nán (Shòuqiáo), prepared in the early Jiāqìng era while the author was retiring from a clinical practice based at the *Géxiānlǐng* 葛仙嶺 in Hángzhōu and on the eve of taking up a posting as district *guǎngwén* 廣文 (assistant-instructor of education). The *Kèchén* 客塵 ("dust-of-the-guest") titular metaphor — drawn from a *Chángēzì* 長安子 Buddhist trope of the impermanent settling of dust on a wandering guest — frames the essays as informal jottings written for friends rather than formal doctrinal treatises. The contents are predominantly *yīhuà*-style aphorisms: clinical-theoretical reflections on internal-medicine practice, brief case-record fragments, and tonal reflections on the physician's vocation in a Suzhou-Hangzhou *wénrén* 文人 (literatus) milieu.

### Prefaces

The hxwd `_000.txt` preserves a postface (跋, *bá*) signed *Jiāqìng jiǔnián zōuyuè èrshí yǒu liù rì, Wújiāng tóngyán dì Shěn Tāo bàishǒu bá* 嘉慶九年陬月二十有六日吳江同研弟沈翿拜手跋 (= 26th of the first month of Jiāqìng 9 = February 1804). Shěn Tāo's postface — itself a small piece of late-Qián-Jiā literary prose — describes a twenty-year friendship with Jì Nán, records the West-Lake 西冷 setting of Jì's practice "beneath the *Géxiānlǐng* 葛仙嶺" (the famous Daoist hill associated with Gě Hóng 葛洪 = *Bàopǔzǐ* 抱朴子) and the recent death of mutual friends ("Xuěquán 雪泉, Shífān 石帆, Lígǔ 梨谷 — all have become different beings"), and supplies the dating of Jì's anticipated departure for the *guǎngwén* posting in the second month of Jiāqìng 9 — providing the work's principal external date-anchor (the *yīhuà* must have been completed shortly before this 1804 postface).

### Abstract

Jì Nán 計楠 (*hào* Shòuqiáo 壽喬), a Suzhou-Hangzhou physician of the late Qiánlóng — early Jiāqìng era, is one of the better-documented members of the *wénrén yīshēng* "literatus-physician" milieu in which the *Kèchén yīhuà* takes shape. The catalog meta dates him conventionally to the Qīng; internal evidence (Shěn Tāo's twenty-year friendship dating to *c.* 1780s, the 1804 postface, the Géxiānlǐng practice, the imminent *guǎngwén* posting) is consistent with a working life *fl.* 1780s–1820s.

The work's interest is principally as **a late-Qián-Jiā physician's self-presentation in the literatus mode**: where the contemporary [[KR3eq001]] *Téngshì yītán* (1802) takes a polemical position in the Edo-Japanese *kohō / gosei* debate and [[KR3eq004]] *Àitáng yīhuà* (early Dàoguāng) advances a doctrinal thesis about Liver-qì pathology, Jì Nán's *Kèchén yīhuà* is a more discursive, friendship-mediated *bǐjì* product — the *yīhuà* genre at its informal, pre-canonical stage before the Wáng Shìxióng [[王士雄]]-Asada Sōhaku 淺田宗伯 1850s–1870s consolidation. The composition window 1800–1804 reflects the implied late-Qián-Jiā composition of the essays and the Shěn Tāo postface terminus. The work entered modern Chinese circulation through the hxwd repatriation series.
