# Lǜ-zōng wèn-dá 律宗問答
*Vinaya-School Questions and Answers*
by [[俊芿]] (Shunjō, 問); answered by [[了然]] (Liǎorán, 答), [[智瑞]] (Zhì-ruì, 答), and [[妙音]] (Miào-yīn, 答)

## About the work

A two-fascicle (上下) Sino-Japanese Vinaya disputation of *Jiā-dìng* 嘉定 2 (1209), assembled from the questions submitted by the Japanese pilgrim **Shunjō** 俊芿 ([[俊芿]], 1166–1227) — at that point in his twelve-year sojourn in Sòng China and shortly to return to Japan to found *Sennyū-ji* 泉涌寺 — together with the line-by-line answers separately supplied by **three Sòng Vinaya masters**: *Lín-ān-fǔ Bù-kōng jiào-yuàn* 臨安府不空教院 [[了然]] *Zhì-yǒng Liǎo-rán* (1077–1141 — but see note below), *Huì-jī jùn Yáo-jiāng Jí-lè-zuò-zhǔ* 會稽郡姚江極樂座主 [[智瑞]] *Zhì-ruì*, and *Zhī-yán Jìng-huái Jìng-fàn* 芝嵓淨懷淨梵 [[妙音]] *Miào-yīn*. The catalog meta gives the principal author's name as *Jùn-nǎi* 俊艿; the source signatures and DILA A000811 alike give 俊芿 (modern Japanese *Shunjō*).

### Abstract

The *Lǜ-zōng wèn-dá* preserves **the most substantial early-thirteenth-century Sino-Japanese exchange on Vinaya doctrine**: thirty questions across two fascicles, treating the foundational doctrinal frame of the *Nán-shān* Vinaya tradition. The questions are organized around discrete *疑* (*yí*, "doubts" / topics of inquiry); the surviving topic-headings include: *Sān-guān shǐ-chū yí* 三觀始出疑 (the *Nán-shān* threefold-contemplation programme: its origin and inheritance from the *Chán* master *Dào-zhěng* 道整); *Sān-guān-jiāo míng-yì yí* 三觀名義疑 (terminology of the three contemplations); *Sān-guān shōu-jiào yí* 三觀收教疑 (which doctrinal-classification scheme the three contemplations subsume); *Nán-shān lì-zōng wéi-shī yí* 南山立宗違師疑 (the apparent contradiction whereby Dào-xuān, who inherited from *Zhì-shǒu* 智首 a "*cause-and-effect*" *zōng* 因果為宗, established a "*precept-as-zōng*" 戒為宗 of his own); and *Chāo shí-mén shè-shǔ zhū-piān yí* 鈔十門攝屬諸篇疑 (how the ten-gate scheme of the *Shì-chāo* relates to its other chapters).

The respondents are arranged in three parallel columns: each *yí* receives an answer from each of the three Sòng masters. The variation between answers is itself the source's principal scholarly value — providing a three-way controlled comparison of late-Sòng Vinaya pedagogy:
- **Liǎo-rán** answers from a *Tiāntái–Vinaya* integrative perspective, with frequent reference to his own *Tōng-zhēn jì* ([[KR6k0253]]).
- **Zhì-ruì** answers in a more strictly *Nán-shān*-traditional vein, conservative on procedural matters but doctrinally engaged.
- **Miào-yīn**, of the *Líng-zhī* line, gives the post-*Yuán-zhào* reading.

The opening answer to *Sān-guān shǐ-chū yí* — Liǎo-rán's — points the reader directly to his *Tōng-zhēn jì* for the standard exposition; Zhì-ruì's letter-preface (15th day of the 8th month of *Jiā-dìng* 2 = September 15, 1209), preserved entire, frames the entire exchange as a Sino-Japanese intellectual courtesy ("*so that the schools of the two countries might together see to the bottom of the Vinaya sea*"). The colophon to *juàn xià* terminates the work without an editorial postface; it is unclear whether Shunjō or one of his Japanese disciples edited the three response-sets into the surviving combined two-fascicle form.

### Note on Liǎo-rán's lifedates

DILA A000035 records *Zhì-yǒng Liǎo-rán* (1077–1141), and the present work is dated 1209 — which is incompatible with that *Liǎo-rán*'s death in 1141. The respondent of *Lǜ-zōng wèn-dá* must therefore either be a later Vinaya master also named Liǎo-rán (homonym), or the existing person note's death-date is incorrect. The *Sennyū-ji* records of Shunjō's Sòng-China study list a *Liǎo-rán* of *Lín-ān-fǔ Bù-kōng-jiào-yuàn* among Shunjō's preceptors during his second decade in China (1199–1211), suggesting the two are distinct individuals; the *Wèn-dá*'s respondent is more likely a different, otherwise-undocumented *Liǎo-rán* active in the early thirteenth century. The single person note for [[了然]] is retained for both, with this discrepancy flagged.

## Translations and research

- *Jingakuin Shunjō zenshū* 浄学院俊芿全集 (Tokyo: Sanshōsha, 1968) — edition of Shunjō's collected works including the *Lǜ-zōng wèn-dá* materials.
- Daikobō Sennyū-ji henshū 大菩坊泉涌寺編輯, *Sennyū-ji-shi* 泉涌寺史 (Kyoto: Hōzōkan, 1984) — the principal Japanese-language history of Shunjō's foundation, with substantial discussion of the Vinaya curriculum he brought back from Sòng China.
- For the broader Kamakura-period *Hokkyō-ritsu* / *Nankyō-ritsu* division, see Paul Groner, *Saicho: The Establishment of the Japanese Tendai School* (Honolulu: University of Hawaiʻi Press, 2000) and the more recent *Buddhism and Medieval Society* literature.

## Other points of interest

- Shunjō's pilgrimage and the *Lǜ-zōng wèn-dá* form the textual foundation for the **Northern Vinaya** (*Hokkyō-ritsu* 北京律) reform movement in early-Kamakura Japan, contemporary with — and theologically distinct from — *Eison*'s 叡尊 (1201–1290) Saidai-ji-based *Nankyō-ritsu* 南京律 line.
- The work is one of the few surviving *full* records of a Japanese pilgrim's doctrinal questioning of contemporary Sòng Buddhist authorities; comparable in genre to *Eisai*'s 榮西 *Kōzen gokoku-ron* but more narrowly Vinaya-focussed.
- The catalog meta variant *Jùn-nǎi* 俊艿 reflects a graphic confusion of 芿 (rare) with 艿; the source itself preserves 芿.
