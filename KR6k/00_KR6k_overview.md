# KR6k — Vinaya / Monastic Discipline (律部 *Lǜbù*)

## Scope

The KR6k division of the Kanripo catalog comprises **263 texts** of **Buddhist Vinaya** (律 *lǜ*; Skt. *vinaya*; Pali *vinaya*) — the literature of monastic discipline, ordination procedure, *prātimokṣa* recitation, *karmavācanā* legal forms, and the broader Mahāyāna **bodhisattva-precept** (菩薩戒 *púsà-jiè*) tradition that complements the Hīnayāna *prātimokṣa* in East Asian Buddhism.

The division's textual scope covers:

1. **The four canonical Indian Vinayas in Chinese translation** (5th–7th centuries): the **Mahīśāsaka** *Wǔfēn lǜ* (KR6k0001), the **Mahāsāṃghika** *Móhē-sēngqí lǜ* (KR6k0006), the **Dharmaguptaka** *Sìfēn lǜ* (KR6k0009), and the **Sarvāstivāda** *Shísòng lǜ* (KR6k0016) — together with their derivative *prātimokṣa* and *karmavācanā* extracts.

2. **The Mūlasarvāstivāda corpus** of **Yìjìng** ([[義淨]]) (early 8th century, KR6k0023–0040): the most extensive single-author Vinaya translation project in East Asian Buddhist history (eighteen texts, c. 200 fascicles).

3. **Indian Vinaya commentaries** in Chinese translation: the *Sàpóduō pínì pípóshā* (KR6k0021, anon.); the *Sàpóduō-bù pínì módélèjiā* (KR6k0022, Saṃghavarman); the *Lǜ èr-shí-èr míng-liǎo lùn* (KR6k0042, the only Sammitīya text in any language); the *Shàn-jiàn lǜ pípóshā* (KR6k0043, Buddhaghosa's *Samantapāsādikā*, the only Theravāda commentary in Chinese); the *Pínì-mǔ jīng* (KR6k0044, anon.).

4. **Hīnayāna *prātimokṣa* extracts** of the **Kāśyapīya** (KR6k0041) and **early Vibhajyavāda** (KR6k0045) traditions — the only extant witnesses to certain lost schools.

5. **Mahāyāna bodhisattva-precept literature**: the foundational **Fànwǎng jīng** 梵網經 (Brahmajāla Sūtra, KR6k0076) and its many commentaries (KR6k0078–0104), Korean Silla scholastic exegesis (Wǒnhyo, Ŭijŏk, Taehyŏn), Sòng and Míng-Qīng commentaries; the **Pú-sà yīngluò běn-yè jīng** (KR6k0105) and its commentaries; the *Yogācārabhūmi*-derived **Pú-sà-jiè-běn** translations of Dharmakṣema and Xuánzàng (KR6k0121–0123); and lay-precept literature.

6. **The Tang Nán-shān Sìfēn-Vinaya tradition of Dàoxuān** ([[道宣]]): the *Three Great Works* (南山三大部) — the *Sìfēn lǜ shānbǔ suíjī jiémó* (KR6k0046), the *Sìfēn lǜ shānfán bǔquē xíngshì chāo* (KR6k0128), and the *Sìfēn lǜ bǐqiū hán-zhù jièběn* (KR6k0131) — together with the rival Tang traditions of the **Dōng-tǎ** school (Huáisù, KR6k0047–0048) and the **Xiāng-bù** school (Dìng Bīn, KR6k0132).

7. **Sòng-period Nán-shān commentaries**: principally those of **Yuán-zhào** ([[元照]]) and **Yǔn Kān** ([[允堪]]), constituting the standard medieval Chinese reception of the Tang tradition.

8. **Late-Míng / early-Qīng Vinaya revival** literature, including the works of **Zhūhóng** ([[袾宏]]), **Zhìxù** ([[智旭]]), **Hóngzàn** ([[弘贊]]), **Sānmèi Jì-guāng** ([[寂光]]), and the **Bǎohuá-shān** institutional tradition.

9. **Dunhuang Vinaya manuscript fragments** (KR6k0104, KR6k0106, KR6k0133–0142) preserving lost Tang exegetical traditions.

10. **Auxiliary disciplinary literature**: confessional texts (*chàn-fǎ*), monastic conduct manuals (*wēi-yí*), *śrāmaṇera/śrāmaṇerikā* novice rules, lay precepts, casuistic Q&A literature (the *Mùlián wǔ-bǎi-wèn* tradition), Sòng imperial Yìjīng-yuàn translations.

## Doctrinal Framework

East Asian Vinaya operates on a **dual-track** institutional foundation:

1. **The Dharmaguptaka *Sìfēn lǜ*** (KR6k0009) as the canonical *prātimokṣa* basis for monastic *upasaṃpadā* (full ordination) — imposed by imperial decree of Tang Zhōngzōng in **709 CE** (景龍三年) and observed continuously throughout China, Korea, Japan (until Saichō's *endon-kai* 圓頓戒 reform in early Heian Japan), and Vietnam to the present day.

2. **The Mahāyāna bodhisattva-precepts** — primarily from the **Fànwǎng jīng** (KR6k0076) but supplemented by the *Yogācārabhūmi*-derived bodhisattva-precept tradition (KR6k0121–0123) — taken as a Mahāyāna supplement (and in Japanese Tendai, as a substitute) for the *prātimokṣa*.

This **dual-Vinaya** framework — *prātimokṣa* + bodhisattva-precepts — was systematised by **Dàoxuān** 道宣 ([[道宣]], 596–667) of the **Nán-shān** 南山 school in the Tang and remains the operative model of East Asian monastic discipline. The synthesis was made possible by Dàoxuān's distinctive interpretation of the Dharmaguptaka tradition as having a unique Mahāyāna affinity, which permitted Chinese Mahāyāna Buddhists to embrace a Hīnayāna *prātimokṣa* without theological compromise.

## Major Lineages and Schools

- **The Tang Sìfēn schools (three rival traditions, 7th–8th c.)**:
  - **Nán-shān** 南山 (Dàoxuān, [[道宣]]) — the dominant tradition, eventually universal.
  - **Dōng-tǎ** 東塔 (Huáisù, [[懷素]]) — strict-textualist, eclipsed in late Tang.
  - **Xiāng-bù** 相部 (Fǎlì, Dìng Bīn, [[定賓]]) — eclipsed in late Tang.
- **Sòng Nán-shān recovery**: Yǔn Kān ([[允堪]]) and Yuán-zhào ([[元照]]) — the medieval Chinese transmission.
- **Late-Míng Vinaya revival**: Zhūhóng ([[袾宏]]), Zhìxù ([[智旭]]), Hóngzàn ([[弘贊]]), Yuán-xián ([[元賢]]) — the integration of Vinaya with late-Míng Pure Land, Chán, and Tiāntái synthesis.
- **Bǎohuá-shān institutional tradition** (寶華山, late Míng to 20th century): Sānmèi Jì-guāng ([[寂光]]) → Jiànyuè Dú-tǐ 見月讀體 (1602–1679) → continuing line — the dominant institutional Vinaya-ordination centre of late-imperial Chinese Buddhism.
- **Korean Silla Vinaya scholarship**: Wǒnhyo ([[元曉]]), Ŭijŏk ([[義寂]]), Taehyŏn ([[太賢]]), Sŭngjang ([[勝莊]]) — major commentaries on the *Fànwǎng* and bodhisattva-precept tradition.
- **Japanese Risshū** (律宗): transmitted by Jiànzhēn 鑑真 (Ganjin) in 754; later Japanese contributions include Gyōnen ([[凝然]])'s massive *Bommō kaihon-sho nichijū-shō* and the Kamakura Vinaya revival of Eison.

## Key Persons (anchor figures)

- **Buddhajīva** ([[佛陀什]]), **Buddhabhadra** ([[佛陀跋陀羅]] / [[覺賢]]), **Buddhayaśas** ([[佛陀耶舍]]), **Puṇyatara** ([[弗若多羅]]), **Kumārajīva** ([[鳩摩羅什]]), **Saṃghabhadra** ([[僧伽跋陀羅]]) — the foundational 5th-century translators of the four Vinayas.
- **Yìjìng** ([[義淨]], 635–713) — Mūlasarvāstivāda transmission.
- **Guṇavarman** ([[求那跋摩]], 367–431) — early Mahāyāna bodhisattva-precept transmission.
- **Dàoxuān** ([[道宣]], 596–667) — founder of the Nán-shān school; the single most influential figure.
- **Yuán-zhào** ([[元照]], 1048–1116) — Sòng Nán-shān recovery.
- **Zhūhóng** ([[袾宏]], 1535–1615), **Zhìxù** ([[智旭]], 1599–1655) — late-Míng Vinaya revival.

## Status of cataloging

This entry covers **150** of the division's 263 texts (KR6k0001–KR6k0150), processed in this run. Subsequent entries (KR6k0151–KR6k0263) — covering the further Sòng / Míng / Qīng *Sìfēn* commentaries, Dàoxuān's auxiliary works (the *Pínì zuò-chí xù-shì*, the *Guān-zhōng jiè-tán tú-jīng*, the *Jìng-xīn jiè-guān fǎ*, the *Shì-mén zhāng-fú yí*, etc.), and the Tibetan-derived Yuán-period Mūlasarvāstivāda procedural texts of **Bahasiba** 拔合思巴 (KR6k0191–0192) — remain to be cataloged in future runs.

## Notes on canonical placement

The *Sìfēn lǜ* canonical primacy (709 CE imperial decree) determined the practical-disciplinary basis of all East Asian Mahāyāna Buddhism. The Mūlasarvāstivāda translations of Yìjìng arrived too late to displace this established framework but became the *de facto* Vinaya of Tibetan Buddhism, where the *Sìfēn lǜ* was never received. The *Fànwǎng* bodhisattva-precept tradition functioned as the Mahāyāna doctrinal-spiritual supplement throughout the East Asian transmission. The Theravāda Vinaya commentary preserved at KR6k0043 represents the only direct Pāli-tradition transmission to China — an isolated but historically significant connection.
