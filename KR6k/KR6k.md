## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR6k` 律部 (*Lǜ bù*, "Vinaya section") is the Kanripo mirror of the Vinaya division of the Chinese Buddhist canon — the canonical *Vinaya-piṭaka* together with its Indic and East-Asian exegetical and ritual-procedural literature. In CANWWW  the material is distributed across three divisions: `div13` 律部 holds the Vinayas proper and their *prātimokṣa-*, *karman-* and *paripṛcchā-*satellites (T22 No. 1421 — T24 No. 1504); `div14` 律疏部 holds the principal Suí–Tang exegetical layer (T40 Nos. 1804–1815, plus T45 Nos. 1892–1910); `div27` 續律疏部 holds the later Sòng–Míng–Qīng Chinese sub-commentaries together with the Japanese scholastic apparatus. Kanripo `KR6k` integrates these three CANWWW divisions and supplements them with a large Wàn xùzàng 卍續藏 (X) commentarial tail, so that 263 works are catalogued here in total — roughly the same order of magnitude as the *Prajñāpāramitā* division [[KR6c]] but with a quite different shape: where the *Bōrě bù* is anchored by a small handful of recurrently retranslated Indic sūtras, the Vinaya division is anchored by five Indic disciplinary corpora belonging to five different *nikāya* schools, all surviving only in their Chinese form, together with a much larger East-Asian scholastic and ritual-procedural superstructure.

The corpus falls into six historically and doctrinally distinct strata. (1) The **early-fifth-century translation wave** (404–431) at Chángān and Jiànyè brought into Chinese the full disciplinary literature of four Indic *nikāya* schools — the *Shísòng lǜ* 十誦律 of the Sarvāstivāda, the *Sìfēn lǜ* 四分律 of the Dharmaguptaka, the *Móhēsēngqí lǜ* 摩訶僧祇律 of the Mahāsāṃghika, and the *Wǔfēn lǜ* 五分律 of the Mahīśāsaka — together with supplementary texts of the Kāśyapīya and Sammitīya. The Mahīśāsaka, Mahāsāṃghika, and Kāśyapīya Vinayas survive in no other language, so this stratum is the principal evidentiary base for *all* modern comparative-Vinaya scholarship (Clarke 2015; *cf.* Wilkinson §29.5.7.6). (2) The **Tang *Sìfēnlǜ* scholastic synthesis** at Chángān (620–697) saw the *Sìfēn lǜ* gradually displace the *Shísòng lǜ* as the practical Vinaya of Chinese monasticism, under three competing exegetical schools — Fǎlì's 法礪 ([[法礪]]) Xiāngbù 相部, Dàoxuān's 道宣 ([[道宣]]) Nánshān 南山, and Huáisù's 懷素 ([[懷素]]) Dōngtǎ 東塔 — of which the Nánshān school's victory under the imperial decree of Dàizōng (777) made Dàoxuān's *San dàbù* 三大部 the canonical Chinese Vinaya curriculum thereafter. (3) The **Yìjìng Mūlasarvāstivāda programme** (700–714) translated the disciplinary corpus of the late-Indic Mūlasarvāstivāda — by then the dominant Vinaya school of Indian Buddhism and (still later) the canonical Vinaya of the Tibetan, Mongolian, and Bhutanese traditions — into Chinese as a single eighteen-text *vastu*-by-*vastu* programme, but this Vinaya never displaced the *Sìfēn* in Chinese practice. (4) The **Mahāyāna *bodhisattva-vinaya* stream** is anchored by the apocryphal *Fànwǎng jīng* 梵網經 ([[KR6k0076]], attributed to Kumārajīva but Chinese in origin) and runs in parallel to the school-Vinaya tradition, generating its own large commentarial corpus (Zhìyǐ 智顗, Fǎzàng 法藏, Wǒnhyo 元曉, Tàixián 太賢, Yúnqī Zhūhóng 雲棲袾宏, Ǒuyì Zhìxù 蕅益智旭); the alternative *Yogācāra*-school *bodhisattva-precepts* (Maitreya / Asaṅga, translated by Dharmakṣema and re-translated by Xuánzàng) provided the Mahāyāna stream with a second textual base. (5) The **Sòng *Língzhī*-school revival** (Yǔnkān 允堪 1005–1061; Yuánzhào 元照 1048–1116; Shǒuyī 守一 / Miàolián 妙蓮 in the Southern Sòng) produced an extensive sub-commentarial apparatus on Dàoxuān's *San dàbù*, recovered the doctrine of the precept-body (戒體 *jiètǐ*), and re-anchored Vinaya practice in the Tiāntái doctrinal system. (6) The **late-Míng / Qīng Vinaya revival** (Yúnqī Zhūhóng, Zhìxù, Dúté 讀體 1601–1679 at Bǎohuáshān 寶華山, Hóngzàn 弘贊 1611–1685 at Dǐnghú-shān 鼎湖山, the *Sānfēng* line of Fǎzàng 法藏 1573–1635) re-standardised Chinese ordination practice through the new *sāntán* 三壇 (three-platform) ordination system, generated the daily-use Vinaya manual that has remained in use to the present (*Píní rìyòng qièyào* [[KR6k0224]]), and produced the most thorough Qīng-period commentaries on every layer of the disciplinary tradition.

## Important texts and text clusters

### The four (then five) school-Vinayas

- *Sìfēn lǜ* 四分律 [[KR6k0009]] — the **Dharmaguptaka** Vinaya translated 410–412 by [[佛陀耶舍]] (Buddhayaśas) and [[竺佛念]] (Zhú Fóniàn). After the Tang imperial decree of 777 the canonical Chinese Vinaya and the operational basis of Chinese, Korean, Japanese, and Vietnamese monasticism to the present. Its *bhikṣu-prātimokṣa* is [[KR6k0010]] / [[KR6k0011]]; the *bhikṣuṇī-prātimokṣa* is [[KR6k0012]]; the early *karmavācanā* compendia [[KR6k0013]] [[KR6k0014]] [[KR6k0015]].
- *Shísòng lǜ* 十誦律 [[KR6k0016]] — the **Sarvāstivāda** Vinaya translated 404–409 by [[弗若多羅]] (Puṇyatāra) and [[鳩摩羅什]] (Kumārajīva). The first complete Indian Vinaya translated into Chinese and the dominant Vinaya of southern-Chinese monasticism through the Liú-Sòng and Qí–Liáng. Its *prātimokṣa* is [[KR6k0017]] / [[KR6k0018]]; the *karmavācanā* manuals [[KR6k0019]] [[KR6k0020]]; the principal Indic commentary [[KR6k0021]] *Sàpóduō pínì pípóshā* and the *mātṛkā* [[KR6k0022]].
- *Móhēsēngqí lǜ* 摩訶僧祇律 [[KR6k0006]] — the **Mahāsāṃghika** Vinaya translated 416–418 by [[法顯]] (Fǎxiǎn) and [[佛陀跋陀羅]] (Buddhabhadra), based on the manuscript Fǎxiǎn brought back from Pāṭaliputra. Surviving in no other language; the principal evidentiary witness to the disciplinary tradition of the *Mahāsāṃghika* — the lineage whose schism in the second post-Nirvāṇa century is conventionally regarded as the institutional ancestry of Mahāyāna Buddhism. Its *prātimokṣa* is [[KR6k0007]] / [[KR6k0008]].
- *Wǔfēn lǜ* 五分律 [[KR6k0001]] — the **Mahīśāsaka** Vinaya translated 422–423 by [[佛陀什]] (Buddhajīva) and [[竺道生]] (Zhú Dàoshēng). Surviving in no other language; the only complete Mahīśāsaka disciplinary text in any tradition. Its *prātimokṣa* (in two recensions [[KR6k0002]] [[KR6k0003]]), *bhikṣuṇī-prātimokṣa* [[KR6k0004]], and *karmavācanā* [[KR6k0005]].
- *Gēnběn shuōyīqièyǒubù pínàiyé* 根本說一切有部毘奈耶 [[KR6k0023]] — the **Mūlasarvāstivāda** Vinaya, translated 700–714 by [[義淨]] (Yìjìng) after his Indian sojourn 671–695. Translated as a single eighteen-text *vastu*-by-*vastu* programme: the *Pravrajyā-vastu* [[KR6k0025]], *Varṣā-vastu* [[KR6k0026]], *Pravāraṇā-vastu* [[KR6k0027]], *Carma-vastu* [[KR6k0028]], *Bhaiṣajya-vastu* [[KR6k0029]], *Kaṭhina-vastu* [[KR6k0030]], *Saṃghabhedavastu* [[KR6k0031]], *Kṣudraka-vastu* [[KR6k0032]], *Nidāna-mātṛkā* [[KR6k0033]], the *prātimokṣas* [[KR6k0035]] / [[KR6k0036]], the *karmavācanā* [[KR6k0034]], the *uddāna* summaries [[KR6k0037]] [[KR6k0038]], the *Vinaya-saṃgraha* digest [[KR6k0039]], and Viśākhā's versified Vinaya [[KR6k0040]]. Although this is the Vinaya of the Indo-Tibetan tradition to the present, it never displaced the *Sìfēn* in Chinese practice.
- The minor-school witnesses: *Jiětuō jièjīng* 解脫戒經 [[KR6k0041]] (Kāśyopadyā translated by [[般若流支]] in 543); *Lǜ èrshíèr míngliǎo lùn* 律二十二明了論 [[KR6k0042]] (the only extant Sammitīya Vinaya in any language, translated by [[真諦]] Paramārtha in 568); *Shànjiàn lǜ pípóshā* 善見律毘婆沙 [[KR6k0043]] (the *Samantapāsādikā*, Buddhaghosa's Pāli Vinaya commentary, translated by [[僧伽跋陀羅]] in 489 — the only Theravāda Vinaya document in Chinese); *Pínìmǔ jīng* 毘尼母經 [[KR6k0044]] (an unattributed *mātṛkā*-style Vinaya commentary, 4th–5th c.); and *Bínàiyē* 鼻奈耶 [[KR6k0045]] ([[竺佛念]], 383 — the **earliest extant Chinese Vinaya translation**, predating the four-school programme by twenty-one years).

### Dàoxuān's *Three Great Works* of the Nánshān school

The institutional triad of the Tang *Sìfēnlǜ* curriculum, codified at Zhōngnánshān 終南山 by Dàoxuān between 626 and 667:

- *Sìfēn lǜ shānfán bǔquē xíngshì chāo* 四分律刪繁補闕行事鈔 [[KR6k0128]] (T1804) — the procedural manual of monastic practice; the most-studied Vinaya book in pre-modern East Asia.
- *Sìfēn lǜ shānbǔ suíjī jiémó* 四分律刪補隨機羯磨 [[KR6k0046]] (T1808) — the codified *karmavācanā* compendium for all monastic legal acts.
- *Sìfēn lǜ bǐqiū hánzhù jièběn* 四分律比丘含注戒本 [[KR6k0131]] (T1806) — Dàoxuān's annotated *prātimokṣa* recension; with the parallel *Sìfēn bǐqiūnī chāo* 四分比丘尼鈔 [[KR6k0155]] for the women's saṅgha.

Around the *San dàbù* runs the principal Tang *Nánshān* sub-commentarial cluster — Dàoxuān's own *Yìchāo* 義鈔 [[KR6k0176]], the Five-Dynasties *Jiǎnzhèng jì* [[KR6k0167]] of Jǐng Xiāo 景霄, and the eighth-century *Sōuxuán lù* [[KR6k0162]] of Zhì Hóng 志鴻; the Tang Xiāngbù tradition is represented by Fǎlì's *Shū* [[KR6k0161]] with Dìngbīn's *Shìzōng yìjì* [[KR6k0163]] and Dìngbīn's *Bǐqiū jièběn shū* [[KR6k0132]]; the Tang Dōngtǎ tradition by Huáisù's *Kāizōng jì* [[KR6k0165]] and his alternative *karmavācanā* recensions [[KR6k0047]] [[KR6k0048]].

The principal Sòng *jì* — running line-by-line sub-commentaries on Dàoxuān's *San dàbù* by Yǔnkān 允堪 and Yuánzhào 元照, the two pillars of the *Língzhī* 靈芝 revival — fill out the Vinaya scholastic apparatus: Yuánzhào's *Zīchí jì* [[KR6k0129]] on the *Xíngshì chāo*; his *Xíngzōng jì* [[KR6k0146]] on the *Hánzhù jièběn shū*; his *Jìyuán jì* [[KR6k0158]] on the *Suíjī jiémó shū*; Yǔnkān's *Fāhuī jì* [[KR6k0144]] on the same; the *Fǔyào jì* [[KR6k0177]] on the *Yìchāo*; the Japanese *Zīxíng chāo* [[KR6k0130]] by Shōen 照遠 (14th c., Tō-ji Vinaya scholar). Yuánzhào's wider Vinaya prose — the *Zhīyuán jí* [[KR6k0217]], *Bǔxù Zhīyuán jí* [[KR6k0218]], *Zhīyuán yíbiān* [[KR6k0216]] — preserves the doctrinal foundations of the *Língzhī* revival.

### Dàoxuān's wider Vinaya treatises

A distinct cluster of nine Dàoxuān works on institutional and ritual aspects of monastic discipline that fall outside the *San dàbù* proper:

- *Jiètán tújīng* 戒壇圖經 [[KR6k0178]] — the canonical Tang manual on the architectural form of the ordination platform; the foundational document for every East-Asian Vinaya-school ordination hall.
- *Jìngxīn jièguān fǎ* 淨心戒觀法 [[KR6k0179]] — Vinaya observance integrated with meditative practice.
- *Zhāngfú yí* 釋門章服儀 [[KR6k0180]] — the canonical regulation of monastic vestments.
- *Liàngchù qīngzhòng yí* 量處輕重儀 [[KR6k0181]] — the disposition of a deceased monk's property.
- *Guījìng yí* 釋門歸敬儀 [[KR6k0182]] — refuge-taking.
- *Xínghù lǜyí* 教誡新學比丘行護律儀 [[KR6k0183]] — novice instruction.
- *Lǜxiàng gǎntōng zhuàn* 律相感通傳 [[KR6k0184]] — the hagiographic Wèituó 韋陀 tradition.
- *Qíhuánsì tújīng* 中天竺舍衛國祇洹寺圖經 [[KR6k0185]] — the cosmographic blueprint of the standard Buddhist monastery.

The cluster's Sòng running commentaries — Yuánzhào and Liángxìn's *Yìngfǎ jì* [[KR6k0214]] on the *Zhāngfú yí*; Yuánzhào's *Liùwù tú* [[KR6k0186]] (the *Pratītyasamutpāda* of robes and bowl); Miàoshēng's *Sānyī xiǎnzhèng tú* [[KR6k0254]] and *Liùwù tú biàné* [[KR6k0187]] — together with the Tiāntái-line subcommentary *Tōngzhēn jì* [[KR6k0253]] and *Hùfǎ jì* [[KR6k0252]] on the *Guījìng yí* — preserve the most extensive Sòng-period institutional-Vinaya scholarship.

### The *Fànwǎng jīng* + Mahāyāna *bodhisattva-vinaya* cluster

The Mahāyāna disciplinary stream is anchored by the *Brahma-net Sūtra*, the foundational scripture of East-Asian *bodhisattva-precepts*, generating one of the densest commentarial clusters in the entire Buddhist canon:

- *Fànwǎng jīng* 梵網經 [[KR6k0076]] (T1484) — canonically ascribed to Kumārajīva but widely recognised as a Chinese apocryphon composed c. 440–480; the canonical scripture of the Mahāyāna ten-major-precepts and forty-eight-minor-precepts system. With Kūkai's [[空海]] Shingon *Fànwǎngjīng kāití* [[KR6k0077]].
- The **Tang scholastic commentarial cluster** spans every major doctrinal school. Tiāntái: Zhìyǐ–Guàndǐng *Púsàjiè yìshū* [[KR6k0078]] and its Tang revision by Míngkuàng [[KR6k0079]] and Guàndǐng's independent *Yìshū* [[KR6k0084]]. Huáyán: Fǎzàng *Púsà jièběn shū* [[KR6k0080]]. The Silla/Korean cluster: Wǒnhyo *Sījì* [[KR6k0090]] (the Korean scholastic foundation), Ŭijŏk *Púsà jièběn shū* [[KR6k0082]] (Silla Yogācāra), Tàixián *Gǔjì jì* [[KR6k0083]], Sŭngjang *Shùjì* [[KR6k0091]], and the Cí'ēn Yogācāra readings of Zhī Zhōu [[KR6k0092]] and Fǎ Xiǎn [[KR6k0093]]. The Japanese sub-commentary stratum is anchored by Gyōnen's 凝然 *Rìzhū chāo* 梵網戒本疏日珠鈔 [[KR6k0081]] (1276–1321) — the largest single commentary on the *Fànwǎng jīng* in any language.
- The **late-Míng commentarial flowering** under Yúnqī Zhūhóng and his successors: Zhūhóng's three-part synthesis [[KR6k0086]] *Yìshū fāyǐn* with its *Shìyì* [[KR6k0087]] and *Wènbiàn* [[KR6k0088]]; Zhìxù's *Xuányì* [[KR6k0096]] and *Hézhù* [[KR6k0097]], the principal Tiāntái-school late-imperial commentary; Hóngzàn's *Lüèshū* [[KR6k0098]] and *Bànyuè sòng púsà jiè yíshì zhù* [[KR6k0099]]; Jìguāng's *Zhíjiě* [[KR6k0100]] with its *Shìyì* [[KR6k0101]], the principal Bǎohuáshān commentary; Déyù's red-ink *Shùnzhū* [[KR6k0102]]; Shūyù's *Chūjīn* [[KR6k0103]]. Dunhuang fragments [[KR6k0104]] preserve the lost middle-Tang commentarial layer.
- The **Yogācāra alternative**: the bodhisattva-precept section of the *Yogācārabhūmi* translated twice by [[曇無讖]] Dharmakṣema as [[KR6k0122]] *Púsà jièběn* (414–421) and re-translated by [[玄奘]] Xuánzàng as [[KR6k0123]] (645–664); the procedural *Jiémówén* [[KR6k0121]] of Xuánzàng, with Zhìxù's late-Míng commentary [[KR6k0195]] *Jiānyào* and [[KR6k0196]] *Jiémówén shì*.
- The **bodhisattva-precept conferral manuals**: the Tang Tiāntái *Shòu púsà jiè yí* by Zhànrán [[KR6k0249]], the doubtfully-attributed parallel work by Huìsī [[KR6k0248]], the combined-rite *Lüè shòu sānguī wǔbājiè bìng púsàjiè* [[KR6k0250]] by Chéngzhào, Yánshòu's *Shòu púsà jiè fǎ* [[KR6k0251]], and the foundational late-Míng Chán-school *Hóngjiè fǎyí* [[KR6k0246]] of Sānfēng Fǎzàng with its successor *San-tán* manual [[KR6k0247]].
- The **bodhisattva-vinaya sūtra cluster** in T24: *Yīngluò běnyè jīng* [[KR6k0105]] (a Chinese apocryphon, with Wǒnhyo's commentary [[KR6k0107]] and a Dunhuang fragment [[KR6k0106]]); *Shòu shíshànjiè jīng* [[KR6k0108]]; *Yōupósè jièjīng* [[KR6k0110]] (Dharmakṣema's lay-bodhisattva precept *sūtra*); *Qīngjìng pínì fāngguǎng jīng* [[KR6k0111]] (Kumārajīva, c. 405); *Cíngjìng pínì jīng* [[KR6k0112]]; the Sòng late-Indic *Dàshèng jiè jīng* [[KR6k0119]] and *Bāzhǒng chángyǎng gōngdé jīng* [[KR6k0120]]; the Sòng *Qīngjìng pínàiyé zuìshàng dàshèng jīng* [[KR6k0127]]; together with the apocryphal-historical *Shèlìfú wèn jīng* [[KR6k0053]] and the *paripṛcchā*-genre satellites [[KR6k0054]] [[KR6k0057]].

### Casuistic, lay-precept, and novice-conduct literature

- The *Mùlián wǔbǎiwèn* 目連五百問 cluster — the Buddhist Vinaya casuistic catechism: [[KR6k0072]] [[KR6k0073]] (Buddhist apocrypha, c. 350–600) with their late-Míng commentaries [[KR6k0074]] (Xìngqí) and [[KR6k0075]] (Yǒnghǎi).
- The lay-precept tradition: *Yōupósè wǔjièxiāng jīng* [[KR6k0065]] (Guṇavarman 431, the principal Chinese exposition of the lay five precepts) with its companion *Yōupósè wǔjiè wēiyí jīng* [[KR6k0125]]; the *aṣṭāṅga-uposatha* tradition [[KR6k0120]] and Zhìxù's *Bāzhāi jīng kēzhù* [[KR6k0241]]; Hóngzàn's *Guījiè yàojí* [[KR6k0233]] and *Bāguān zhāifǎ* [[KR6k0234]]; the early-19th-century *Zàijiā lǜyào guǎngjí* [[KR6k0243]] compiled by Yí Rùn and Chén Xīyuàn from Zhìxù's materials — the principal late-imperial lay-Vinaya compendium.
- The novice-conduct literature: *Dà bǐqiū sānqiān wēiyí* [[KR6k0059]] (the *3000 decorums*, ascribed to An Shìgāo); the *Shāmíshíjièfǎ bìng wēiyí* [[KR6k0060]] and *Shāmí wēiyí* [[KR6k0061]] (Guṇavarman); their *bhikṣuṇī* counterparts [[KR6k0063]] [[KR6k0064]]; the Sòng *Shāmí shíjiè yízé jīng* [[KR6k0062]] (Dānapāla); Hóngzàn's *Shìchāmónánī jièběn* [[KR6k0051]] for the probationary nun; Yúnqī Zhūhóng's *Shāmí lǜyí yàolüè* (the canonical post-1600 novice manual; preserved here in its commentaries [[KR6k0228]] *Shùyì* by Shūyù, [[KR6k0232]] *Zēngzhù* by Hóngzàn) and the parallel late-Míng Tiāntái redaction [[KR6k0242]] by Zhìxù; Hóngzàn's versified *Shāmí xuéjiè yíguǐ sòngzhù* [[KR6k0237]] and *Shāmén rìyòng* [[KR6k0231]].

### Repentance (懺) liturgies

- *Cíbēi dàochǎng chànfǎ* 慈悲道場懺法 [[KR6k0198]] (T1909) — the *Liánghuáng bǎochàn* 梁皇寶懺, codified at the Liáng court 502–549 under imperial sponsorship; the principal court-Buddhist repentance liturgy of medieval China and the foundational document of all subsequent East-Asian *chànfǎ*. Still performed continuously to the present.
- *Cíbēi shuǐchàn fǎ* 慈悲水懺法 [[KR6k0199]] (T1910) — Zhìxuán's 知玄 *Water Repentance* (850–883). With the late-Míng / Qīng commentaries [[KR6k0202]] (Zhìzhèng, Tiāntái) and [[KR6k0203]] (Xīzōng).
- The *Shuǐlù* 水陸 mass — the most elaborate East-Asian Buddhist ritual programme: the foundational Sòng liturgy *Fǎjiè shèngfán shuǐlù shènghuì xiūzhāi yíguǐ* [[KR6k0204]] of Zhìpán 志磐, in the Míng redaction of Zhūhóng; the late-Qīng doctrinal synthesis *Xìngxiàng tōnglùn* [[KR6k0205]] and the new *Fǎlún bǎochàn* [[KR6k0206]] of Zhǐguàn 咫觀 (1850–1880), the first systematic adaptation of the *Shuǐlù* under the late-Qīng *Lóngzàng* canon.
- The *zànyí* tradition: Sòng patriarch-veneration verses for Dàoxuān [[KR6k0207]] (Zhìyuán) [[KR6k0208]] (Rényuè) [[KR6k0209]] (Yǔnkān) and for Yuánzhào [[KR6k0210]] (Zé'ān) — the foundational lineage-veneration texts of the *Nánshānlǜ* school.
- The late-imperial repentance cluster: *Fànwǎng jīng chànhuǐ xíngfǎ* [[KR6k0245]] (Zhìxù); *Wúzhōng shífó xiànghǎo chànyí* [[KR6k0200]] (Chuándēng); *Shèlì chànfǎ* [[KR6k0201]] (Jìsēng, 1718); Hóngzàn's three-part liturgical sequence [[KR6k0238]] [[KR6k0239]] [[KR6k0240]] for relic-veneration, buddha-veneration, and the *gòngtiān* 供天 deva-offering.

### The Sòng *Língzhī*-school revival

Beyond the *jì* on Dàoxuān's *San dàbù*, the Sòng *Língzhī* school produced a substantial body of independent Vinaya scholarship: Yǔnkān's *Yībō míngyì zhāng* [[KR6k0212]] (the canonical Sòng pedagogical handbook on robes and bowl), his *Xīnshòujiè bǐqiū liùniàn wǔguān fǎ* [[KR6k0213]] (the *liùniàn / wǔguān* daily reflections), his *Jìngxīn jièguān fǎ fāzhēn chāo* [[KR6k0211]] (sub-commentary on Dàoxuān's *Jìngxīn jièguān fǎ*); Yuánzhào's *Dàojù fù* [[KR6k0215]] (the *rhapsody on monastic equipment*); the Southern Sòng compendia of Shǒuyī and Xíngzhī — *Zhōngnán jiāyè* [[KR6k0219]] (the principal Southern-Sòng *Nánshānlǜ* doctrinal compendium) and *Lǜzōng huìyuán* [[KR6k0220]]; Miàolián's two polemics *Péngzhé zhíbiàn* [[KR6k0221]] and *Péngzhé zhēn* [[KR6k0222]] defending the *Nánshān* doctrine; Huáixiǎn's terminological dictionary *Lǜzōng xīnxué míngjù* [[KR6k0255]]; the early-Kamakura Sino-Japanese exchange *Lǜzōng wèndá* [[KR6k0256]] (1209, Shunjō et al.); and Shěngwù's Yuán-period monastic code *Lǜyuàn shìguī* [[KR6k0257]] — the only Yuán-dynasty Vinaya monastic code.

### Late-Míng / Qīng Vinaya revival

The two dominant late-imperial Vinaya centres — Bǎohuáshān 寶華山 and Dǐnghú-shān 鼎湖山 — together with the related Yúnqī, Tiāntái and Sānfēng lineages produced the working Vinaya literature of the late Míng and the entire Qīng:

- The **Bǎohuáshān corpus** of Dúté (1601–1679) and his successor Shūyù 書玉: *Píní zhǐchí huìjí* [[KR6k0223]] (the foundational study handbook), *Píní rìyòng qièyào* [[KR6k0224]] (the daily-use Vinaya manual still in continuous monastic use), *Shāmíní lǜyí yàolüè* [[KR6k0225]] (the women's novitiate manual), *Chuánjiè zhèngfàn* [[KR6k0226]] (the canonical Chinese ordination handbook that codified the *sāntán* 三壇 system), and *Píní zuòchí xùshì* [[KR6k0160]] for the karman procedures. Shūyù's supplementary apparatus: *Xiāngrǔ jì* [[KR6k0227]] (commentary on the *Píní rìyòng*), *Shùyì* [[KR6k0228]], *Èrbù sēng shòujiè yíshì* [[KR6k0229]], *Jiémó yíshì* [[KR6k0230]]. The *Sìfēn lǜ míngyì biāoshì* [[KR6k0173]] of Hóngzàn is the indispensable late-imperial Vinaya lexicon for this body of work.
- The **Dǐnghú-shān corpus** of Hóngzàn (1611–1685): *Sìfēn jièběn rúshì* [[KR6k0149]] (the principal late-Míng commentary on the *Sìfēn-prātimokṣa*), the lay manuals [[KR6k0233]] [[KR6k0234]], the *Bǐqiū shòujiè lù* [[KR6k0235]] / *Bǐqiūnī shòujiè lù* [[KR6k0236]] (historical-procedural witnesses to the *bhikṣuṇī*-ordination question), and the comprehensive *Míyú biāoshì* glossary [[KR6k0173]]. Hóngzàn's *Píní rìyòng* alternative *Shāmén rìyòng* [[KR6k0231]] was the principal early-Qīng competitor to Dúté's manual.
- **Cáodòng-Chán Vinaya synthesis**: Yuánxián 元賢 (1578–1657)'s *Sìfēn jièběn yuēyì* [[KR6k0150]], *Lǜxué fārèn* [[KR6k0260]] (the late-Míng pedagogical introduction to Vinaya study); Hánshì 函昰 and the Lǐngnán Cáodòng school; Jīnshì 今釋's *Púsà jièshū suíjiàn lù* [[KR6k0095]].
- **Tiāntái-Chán synthesis under Ǒuyì Zhìxù** (1599–1655): *Píní jíyào* [[KR6k0151]] (Zhìxù's restructured *Sìfēn* digest); the *Píní zhēnjìng lù* [[KR6k0143]] anthology; the *bodhisattva-precept* trio [[KR6k0195]] [[KR6k0196]] [[KR6k0245]]; the lay-precept compendium [[KR6k0244]]; the *Sìfēn lǜ chíjiè jiēdù lüèshì* [[KR6k0174]]; the *Shāmí shíjiè wēiyí lùyào* [[KR6k0242]].
- **The Tang/Sòng Tiāntái-line subcommentaries on Dàoxuān's wider works** — *Tōngzhēn jì* [[KR6k0253]] (Liǎorán); *Hùfǎ jì* [[KR6k0252]] (Yànqǐ).
- **The Sānfēng line of Fǎzàng** 法藏 (1573–1635): the early-imperial Chán-school ordination manual *Hóngjiè fǎyí* [[KR6k0246]] and its full *sāntán* successor [[KR6k0247]] — texts whose later imperial suppression (the *Sānfēng* faction was banned under the Yōngzhèng emperor in 1733) makes them important historical witnesses.
- **Yuán imperial Mongolian Vinaya manuals**: ʼPhags-pa's 拔合思巴 *Mūlasarvāstivāda* karman manual [[KR6k0191]] and *bhikṣu* training manual [[KR6k0192]] — Beijing court productions for the Mongol-Tibetan Buddhist community.

### Dunhuang Vinaya fragments

T85 preserves a distinctive Tang-period Vinaya manuscript-fragment cluster recovered from the Mogao library cave: working *Sìfēn* commentary fragments [[KR6k0104]] [[KR6k0133]] [[KR6k0134]] [[KR6k0135]] [[KR6k0106]], a working selection [[KR6k0136]], a *wēiyí* decorum manual [[KR6k0137]], a *Pínìxīn* doctrinal distillation [[KR6k0138]], comparative Vinaya selections [[KR6k0139]] [[KR6k0141]], a working anthology [[KR6k0140]], and the *shǒujué* oral-pedagogical record [[KR6k0142]]. The cluster is the principal evidentiary witness to the operational, practical Vinaya scholarship of Dunhuang monasticism — distinct from the Chángān scholastic tradition preserved in the canonical Suí-Tang commentaries.

## Topics

- **The four Chinese Vinayas in conflict and synthesis.** The five-school disciplinary corpora translated 383–714 made China the first culture in which all Indic Vinaya traditions were systematically available in a single language. The fifth-century period saw active competition between the Sarvāstivāda (*Shísòng*, southern preference) and Dharmaguptaka (*Sìfēn*, northern preference), eventually resolved by the imperial decree of Dàizōng (777) mandating exclusive use of the *Sìfēn lǜ* — the decision that shaped all subsequent East-Asian monastic discipline.
- **The Sìfēnlǜ scholastic schools.** The Tang produced three competing schools of *Sìfēnlǜ* exegesis — the *Xiāngbù* of Fǎlì at Yè, the *Nánshān* of Dàoxuān at Zhōngnánshān, and the *Dōngtǎ* of Huáisù at the Xīmíngsì. Each developed its own karman-formulary [[KR6k0046]] vs. [[KR6k0047]] and its own *prātimokṣa* recension [[KR6k0131]] vs. [[KR6k0132]] vs. [[KR6k0049]]; the *Nánshān* eventually displaced both rivals after the eighth century.
- **The Mahāyāna *bodhisattva-vinaya* problem.** The presence in the Mahāyāna canon of two parallel disciplinary traditions — the *Fànwǎng jīng* tradition (Chinese-apocryphal, ten major + forty-eight minor precepts, conferred on monks and laity alike) and the Yogācāra tradition (Indian-canonical, derived from the *Yogācārabhūmi*, framed as supplementary to the school-Vinaya) — generated one of the central exegetical debates of East-Asian Buddhism. The Tiāntái and Huáyán schools generally favoured the *Fànwǎng* reading; the Cí'ēn / Fǎxiàng school favoured the Yogācāra reading; the Silla scholastics produced a distinctive synthetic interpretation.
- **The precept-body (戒體 jiètǐ).** The doctrinal-metaphysical question: what is the ontological status of the discipline conferred by ordination? Is it a physical *rūpa*-dharma (the Sarvāstivāda position), a *citta-viprayukta* unconditioned dharma (the early *Sìfēn* / *Dharmaguptaka* position), or a *citta-saṃprayukta* mental state (the Mahāyāna / *Língzhī*-school position)? Yuánzhào's *jiètǐ* theology in his *Zhīyuán jí* [[KR6k0217]] is the canonical Sòng synthesis, integrating Vinaya, Tiāntái doctrinal classification, and Huáyán *fǎjiè* metaphysics.
- **The dual-saṅgha (二部僧) ordination of nuns.** The canonical requirement that *bhikṣuṇī* ordination be conferred by both the male and female saṅghas — and the historical question of when, and through what lineage, this had first been achieved in China — runs from Guṇavarman's foundational 433 ordination texts [[KR6k0015]] [[KR6k0052]] through Dúté's *Chuánjiè zhèngfàn* [[KR6k0226]] and Hóngzàn's *Bǐqiūní shòujiè lù* [[KR6k0236]]. The historical accuracy of Hóngzàn's narrative was disputed in 17th-century Qīng scholarship and remains a live issue in contemporary East-Asian Buddhism.
- **The ordination platform and the architecture of monastic discipline.** Dàoxuān's *Jiètán tújīng* [[KR6k0178]] and *Qíhuánsì tújīng* [[KR6k0185]] are the foundational documents for the institutional vision in which the architecture of the monastery itself enacts the discipline. The *sāntán* 三壇 (three-platform) ordination of the late-Míng Bǎohuáshān revival [[KR6k0226]] is the operative codification of this vision for the late-imperial and modern periods.
- **Vinaya casuistry.** The casuistic genre — Q&A on points of discipline — runs from the early *paripṛcchā* sūtras (*Shèlìfú wèn jīng* [[KR6k0053]], *Yōubālí wèn fó jīng* [[KR6k0054]], *Mùlián suǒwèn jīng* [[KR6k0057]]) through the apocryphal *Mùlián wǔbǎiwèn* [[KR6k0072]] [[KR6k0073]] and its late-Míng commentaries [[KR6k0074]] [[KR6k0075]], down to the popular *Jièshā sìshíbā wèn* [[KR6k0262]] of 1700–1739. The genre is the principal vehicle for practical-pastoral monastic instruction.
- **Repentance liturgy.** The *chànfǎ* corpus — anchored by the Liáng-court *Liánghuáng bǎochàn* [[KR6k0198]] (502–549) — is the principal liturgical interface between Vinaya and lay-Buddhist devotion. The *Shuǐlù* mass [[KR6k0204]] and the *Cíbēi shuǐchàn* [[KR6k0199]] generated the most elaborate East-Asian Buddhist ritual programmes; the late-Qīng adaptations [[KR6k0205]] [[KR6k0206]] document the continuing imperial-court adaptation of the genre.
- **The Vinaya as institutional history.** Dàoxuān's hagiographic *Lǜxiàng gǎntōng zhuàn* [[KR6k0184]] and the lineage-veneration *zànyí* tradition for the Nánshān masters [[KR6k0207]]–[[KR6k0210]] document the way the Vinaya tradition wrote itself as an institutional history of patriarchal succession — a function paralleling the lineage-claims of the Chán school but anchored in scriptural-procedural rather than mind-to-mind transmission.
- **East-Asian transmission.** The transmission of the *Sìfēn lǜ* to Korea (foundational for the Silla scholastic tradition documented by Wǒnhyo, Ŭijŏk, and Tàixián), to Japan (where Ganjin's 鑑真 establishment of the Tang Vinaya at Nara — outside KR6k proper — drew on this body of literature, and where the Kamakura *Lǜzōng wèndá* [[KR6k0256]] documents the Sino-Japanese exchange), and to Vietnam are the principal continuing transmission histories of the division.
- **The late-Indian Mūlasarvāstivāda detour.** Yìjìng's ambitious 700–714 programme to make the late-Indian Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya available in Chinese, even as the Chinese practice itself was decisively shifting to the Dharmaguptaka *Sìfēn*, is one of the great translational paradoxes of Tang Buddhism. The Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya as preserved here remains the most thorough Chinese-language witness to a Vinaya tradition that has nowhere been the operational discipline of Chinese monasticism — but is the canonical Vinaya of the entire Tibetan Buddhist world.
