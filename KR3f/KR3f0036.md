# Wǔ-cáo suàn-jīng 五曹算經
*The Five Bureaus' Mathematical Classic*
author unknown; annotated by [[甄鸞]] (Zhēn Luán, Northern Zhōu) and [[李淳風]] (Lǐ Chúnfēng, Tang)

## About the work

A 5-juan elementary mathematical handbook organized topically around the practical computational concerns of the *wǔ-cáo* 五曹 — the "Five Bureaus" of imperial-administrative practice — one bureau per chapter:

- **Juàn 1** *Tián-cáo* 田曹 (Field Bureau) — agricultural land mensuration
- **Juàn 2** *Bīng-cáo* 兵曹 (Military Bureau) — military supply-and-deployment computations
- **Juàn 3** *Jí-cáo* 集曹 (Collection Bureau) — taxation collection-and-distribution
- **Juàn 4** *Cāng-cáo* 倉曹 (Granary Bureau) — grain-storage volume computations
- **Juàn 5** *Jīn-cáo* 金曹 (Treasury Bureau) — currency-and-trade exchange computations

The work belongs to the same elementary-mathematical-handbook genre as the [[KR3f0033]] *Sūn-zǐ suàn-jīng* — providing topical problem-with-solution exercises at a more elementary level than the *Jiǔ-zhāng*. The Tang *Suàn-xué guǎn* curriculum paired the *Wǔ-cáo* with the *Sūn-zǐ* as the elementary-introductory pair, with a one-year combined study period.

The work's authorship is unknown. The Sui *Jīng-jí zhì* lists a *Jiǔ-zhāng-Liù-cáo suàn-jīng* 九章六曹算經 1 *juàn* (Nine-Chapters-and-Six-Bureaus, with 6 not 5 bureau-chapters); the Tang *Yì-wén zhì* first lists a separately-titled *Wǔ-cáo suàn-jīng* in 5 *juàn* with annotations by Zhēn Luán (Northern Zhōu) and Hán Yán 韓延 (also a Northern Dynasties annotator) and the imperial annotation by Lǐ Chúnfēng. The Sìkù 提要 follows Zhēn Luán's annotation as the *terminus ante quem*: since Zhēn Luán annotated the work, it must have existed before his death (c. 575 CE). The 5-bureau (vs. Sui-recorded 6-bureau) form may indicate a different recension from the *Jiǔ-zhāng-Liù-cáo* one.

The Sìkù recension is recovered from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn*; the Zhēn-Luán, Hán-Yán, and Lǐ-Chúnfēng annotations have been lost in the transmission, but the main text is preserved completely.

### Tiyao

Respectfully examined: The Suí *Jīng-jí zhì* has *Jiǔ-zhāng-Liù-cáo suàn-jīng* 1 *juàn* — but [it] has no *Wǔ-cáo* title; its 6-*cáo* chapter-titles are also not transmitted. The Táng *Yì-wén zhì* first has Zhēn Luán's *Wǔ-cáo suàn-jīng* 5 *juàn*, Hán Yán's *Wǔ-cáo suàn-jīng* 5 *juàn*, [and] Lǐ Chúnfēng's annotation [of the] *Wǔ-cáo Sūn-zǐ et al. suàn-jīng* 20 *juàn*. The Mán-Hán-and-Zhēn two houses are both annotators of this book. As for the [original] composer, [we] do not know who.

Examining the Hàn-shū *Méi Fú zhuàn*: [Méi] Fú memorialized saying: "*[I] heard [that] in Qí Huán-[gōng]'s time, there was [the man] of the* Jiǔ-jiǔ *(Nine-Nine [tables])*". Yán Shīgǔ's annotation: "*the* Jiǔ-jiǔ *arithmetic-method [is] like the present-day* Jiǔ-zhāng *and* Wǔ-cáo *and so on*". For arithmetic-learning, although many, [it] does not exceed multiplication-and-division; multiplication-and-division [are] no more than [taken] from 1-to-9 in turn the *jiǔ-zhī shù* (nine-of-them numbers). Therefore [Méi Fú] raised the *Jiǔ-jiǔ* in his speech. And Shīgǔ accordingly with the [name of] the books at his time present — the *Jiǔ-zhāng* and *Wǔ-cáo* and so on — fits-them-into [Yán's commentary]; not [meaning] that Méi Fú's time had this book.

Zhū Yízūn's *Pù-shū-tíng jí* has the *Wǔ-cáo suàn-jīng bá* (postface) saying: "*tradition has it that the method comes from Sūn Wǔ. However, [Zhū] Yízūn merely says 'tradition has it', [without] cited evidence — indeed [this is] insufficient to depend [on]*". Examining the Táng-shū *Xuǎn-jǔ zhì*: it calls "*[for the] Sūn-zǐ [and] Wǔ-cáo [together,] one-year period [of study]*". Since [it] says "*together-period*", then [the] *Wǔ-cáo* does not come from *Sūn-zǐ* — it is clear.

Tentatively determining [it] by Zhēn Luán's annotation: then this book is certainly before the Northern Qí period.

From the Yuán and Míng onwards, [there has been] long [no] printed recension; book-collecting houses' transmitted-copies have transcription-errors, almost incommunicable. Now [it is] dispersed-and-seen within the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn*: Zhēn Luán's, Hán Yán's, [and] Lǐ Chúnfēng's annotations, although also dispersed-and-lost — but the classic-text [is] section-by-section completely-good. Now [we have] cross-referenced and collated, restoring the old appearance — accordingly [it has] become a *jué-wú jǐn-yǒu* (singular and rare) good edition.

Examining: the *Xià-hóu Yáng suàn-jīng* cites *Tián-cáo* and *Cāng-cáo* twice, [and] *Jīn-cáo* once; but in this book all there is no [such] text. However, this book's beginning-and-end clearly-divided, [its] threads-and-veins comprehensively-running, [it] also does not seem to have anything lost-or-missing. [We] suspect the Suí *zhì*'s *Jiǔ-zhāng-Liù-cáo* — its headings perhaps [are] also similar to what [Xià-hóu] Yáng cited as *Tián-cáo, Cāng-cáo, Jīn-cáo*, etc., names — these [are] then *that* book's text, therefore [we] do not dare to depend [on this] in supplement-inserting them, [for fear of] confusing its truth.

Respectfully collated, *Qián-lóng* 47, fourth month [May 1782].

### Abstract

Composition window: c. 400–570 CE. The 提要's *terminus ante quem* of Zhēn Luán's death (c. 575) is firm; the *terminus post quem* is less clear but Northern-Dynasties period is the conventional placement. The work belongs to the same broad genre and period as the [[KR3f0033]] *Sūn-zǐ suàn-jīng*.

The work's significance is principally institutional-and-pedagogical rather than mathematically-innovative: as one of the *Suàn-jīng shí-shū*, it shaped the Tang Imperial Academy curriculum at the elementary level, supplying topical problem-with-solution exercises organized around the practical concerns of imperial administration. Its 5-bureau organization (field, military, collection, granary, treasury) reflects the standard Tang-period fiscal-administrative division of imperial responsibilities.

For the parallel elementary-handbook, see [[KR3f0033]] *Sūn-zǐ suàn-jīng*. For other *Suàn-jīng shí-shū* members, see [[KR3f0032]] *Jiǔ-zhāng*, [[KR3f0035]] *Hǎi-dǎo*, [[KR3f0037]] *Wǔ-jīng suàn-shù*. For the principal annotators, see [[甄鸞]] and [[李淳風]].
