### Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR3f` 天文算法類 is the sixth category of the *Sìkù quánshū* 子部, gathering works on observational and computational astronomy, calendrical mathematics (*lìfǎ* 曆法), instrumentation, and pure mathematics (*suànshù* 算術). Following the Sui-Tang library convention, the *Sìkù* editors kept astronomy and mathematics together as a single division on the principle that calendar-making was the practical end of mathematics: Wilkinson (§37.1) notes that astronomy and mathematics formed two of the four main scientific traditions of pre-modern China, each with its own paradigm, classical text(s) and learned community, and that astronomy before the telescope was "largely computational" — hence inseparable from arithmetic, geometry and algebra. Within KR3 子部, the division sits between medicine (`KR3e`) and the mantic arts (`KR3g`), reflecting its status as a useful, government-regulated technical knowledge.

Chronologically the division spans more than two thousand years, from the *Hàn* mathematical classics (the *Zhōubì* 周髀 and *Jiǔzhāng* 九章 traditions, Wilkinson §38.17.2) through the medieval *Suànjīng shíshū* 算經十書 corpus, the Sòng-Yuán algebraic peak, the dramatic late-Míng / early-Qīng absorption of Jesuit science, and the Kāngxī–Qiánlóng court compilations that synthesized the Western and Chinese traditions. Two organizing principles structure the corpus: in mathematics, the *Suànjīng shíshū* tradition recovered by Dài Zhèn 戴震 from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* 永樂大典 (eight of the ten were entered into the *Sìkù*); in astronomy, the cycle of dynastic calendar reforms (*Dàtǒng lì* 大統曆, *Shíxiàn lì* 時憲曆) and the cosmological debate between the *gàitiān* 蓋天, *húntiān* 渾天 and *xuānyè* 宣夜 theories (Wilkinson §39.1.1).

### Important texts and text clusters

- **The Ten Mathematical Classics (算經十書).** The Sui-Tang state curriculum at the *Suànxué guǎn* 算學館, recovered for the *Sìkù* by Dài Zhèn from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn*: *Zhōubì suàn-jīng* 周髀算經 [[KR3f0001]], *Jiǔzhāng suàn-shù* 九章算術 [[KR3f0032]], *Sūnzǐ suàn-jīng* 孫子算經 [[KR3f0033]], *Hǎidǎo suàn-jīng* 海島算經 [[KR3f0035]], *Wǔcáo suàn-jīng* 五曹算經 [[KR3f0036]], *Wǔjīng suàn-shù* 五經算術 [[KR3f0037]], *Xiàhóu Yáng suàn-jīng* 夏侯陽算經 [[KR3f0038]], *Zhāng Qiūjiàn suàn-jīng* 張邱建算經 [[KR3f0039]], *Jígǔ suàn-jīng* 緝古算經 [[KR3f0040]], and the auxiliary *Shù-shù jì-yí* 數術記遺 [[KR3f0034]]. The *Jiǔzhāng* with Liú Huī's 劉徽 commentary remains the most influential Chinese mathematical work in history; the *Zhōubì* is also the principal source for *gàitiān* cosmology (Wilkinson §38.17.2, §39.1.1).
- **Sòng-Yuán algebra.** The high-water mark of indigenous Chinese mathematics, centered on the *tiānyuán shù* 天元術 (counting-rod algebra). Qín Jiǔsháo's 秦九韶 *Shù-xué jiǔ-zhāng* 數學九章 [[KR3f0041]] (1247) gives the first explicit statement of the Chinese Remainder Theorem; Lǐ Yě's 李冶 *Cè-yuán hǎi-jìng* 測圓海鏡 [[KR3f0042]] (1248) and *Yì-gǔ yǎn-duàn* 益古演段 [[KR3f0044]] (1259) develop the *tiānyuán* method systematically; Gù Yìngxiáng's 顧應祥 *Cè-yuán hǎi-jìng fēn-lèi shì-shù* 測圓海鏡分類釋術 [[KR3f0043]] is the Míng restatement (without rod-algebra).
- **Sòng instrument and classical-astronomy texts.** Sū Sòng's 蘇頌 *Xīn yí-xiàng fǎ-yào* 新儀象法要 [[KR3f0002]] (1094), the illustrated treatise on the great water-powered armillary clock-tower at Kāifēng, and Wáng Yīnglín's 王應麟 *Liù-jīng tiānwén biān* 六經天文編 [[KR3f0003]], an early-philological compendium of celestial references in the Classics.
- **Yuán cosmological synthesis.** Zhào Yǒuqīn's 趙友欽 *Géxiàng xīnshū* 革象新書 [[KR3f0005]] (with Wáng Yī's 王禕 Míng abridgement [[KR3f0004]]) consolidates *húntiān* cosmology with quantitative methods.
- **Míng calendar crisis and reform.** Zhū Zǎiyù's 朱載堉 *Shèng-shòu wàn-nián lì* 聖壽萬年曆 [[KR3f0007]] (1595) and Xíng Yúnlù's 邢雲路 *Gǔ-jīn lǜ-lì kǎo* 古今律歷考 [[KR3f0008]] (1596–1611) attempt to restore the failing *Dàtǒng* calendar; Bèi Lín's 貝琳 *Qī-zhèng tuī-bù* 七政推步 [[KR3f0006]] (1477) transmits the Islamic *Huíhuí lì* 回回曆 for planetary computation.
- **The Jesuit corpus (1607–1640s).** The first wave of European mathematics and astronomy in Chinese: *Jǐhé yuánběn* 幾何原本 [[KR3f0047]] (Euclid's first six books, Ricci–Xú Guāngqǐ 1607), *Tóng-wén suàn-zhǐ* 同文算指 [[KR3f0046]] (European arithmetic, Ricci–Lǐ Zhīzǎo 1614), *Cè-liáng fǎ-yì* 測量法義 [[KR3f0014]] (practical geometry), *Qián-kūn tǐ-yì* 乾坤體義 [[KR3f0009]] (cosmology), *Yuán-róng jiào-yì* 圜容較義 [[KR3f0016]] (isoperimetric problems), *Hún-gài tōng-xiàn tú-shuō* 渾蓋通憲圖說 [[KR3f0015]] (astrolabe), *Biǎo dù shuō* 表度說 [[KR3f0010]] and *Jiǎn-píng yí shuō* 簡平儀說 [[KR3f0011]] (Xióng Sānbá / Sabatino de Ursis), *Tiān-wèn lüè* 天問畧 [[KR3f0012]] (Manuel Dias on telescopic astronomy), and culminating in the *Xīnfǎ suàn-shū* 新法算書 [[KR3f0013]] — the recompiled *Chóngzhēn lìshū* 崇禎曆書, the technical foundation of the *Shíxiàn* calendar.
- **The Kāngxī–Qiánlóng imperial compendia.** The four pillars of Qīng court mathematical-astronomical scholarship, all part of the *Lǜlì yuānyuán* 律曆淵源 project and its sequels: *Yù-zhì lì-xiàng kǎo-chéng* 御製歷象考成 [[KR3f0018]], its *Hòu-biān* 後編 [[KR3f0019]] (incorporating Keplerian elliptical orbits), *Qīn-dìng yí-xiàng kǎo-chéng* 欽定儀象考成 [[KR3f0020]] (the great star catalog), and *Yù-zhì Shù-lǐ jīng-yùn* 御製數理精蘊 [[KR3f0048]] (the encyclopedic mathematical companion).
- **The Méi Wéndǐng school.** Méi Wéndǐng 梅文鼎 (1633–1721), the dominant Qīng mathematician-astronomer, is represented by his collected *Lì-suàn quán-shū* 歷算全書 [[KR3f0026]], the catalog *Wù'ān lì-suàn shū-jì* 勿菴歷算書記 [[KR3f0028]], and *Dà-tǒng lì-zhì* 大統歷志 [[KR3f0027]]; his younger brother Méi Wénmì 梅文鼏 contributes *Zhōng-Xī jīng-xīng tóng-yì kǎo* 中西經星同異考 [[KR3f0029]]. Allied works include Wáng Xīchǎn's 王錫闡 independent *Xiǎo'ān xīn-fǎ* 曉菴新法 [[KR3f0021]] (1663), Jiāng Yǒng's 江永 *Shù-xué* 數學 [[KR3f0031]], and the secondary tradition of Dù Zhīgēng's 杜知耕 *Shù-xué yào* 數學鑰 [[KR3f0050]], Chén Xū's 陳訏 *Gōu-gǔ yǐn-méng* 句股引蒙 [[KR3f0052]], Chén Shìrén's 陳世仁 *Shǎo-guǎng bǔ-yí* 少廣補遺 [[KR3f0054]], Zhuāng Hēngyáng's 莊亨陽 *Zhuāng-shì suàn-xué* 莊氏算學 [[KR3f0055]], and Tú Wényī's 屠文漪 *Jiǔ-zhāng lù-yào* 九章錄要 [[KR3f0056]].
- **Mukinge / Polish-Jesuit alternative.** Mù Níjié / Jan Mikołaj Smogulecki 穆尼閣 and Xuē Fèngzuò's 薛鳳祚 *Tiān-bù zhēn-yuán* 天步眞原 [[KR3f0024]] and *Tiān-xué huì-tōng* 天學會通 [[KR3f0025]] preserve a logarithmic and Copernican-leaning channel of Western astronomy independent of the Beijing Jesuit mission.
- **Japanese supplement.** *Fó-guó lì-xiàng biān* 佛國暦象編 [[KR3f0057]] (Enzū 圓通, c. 1810) is a late Edo Buddhist defense of Indian/Buddhist cosmology against Jesuit astronomy.
- **Pre-Táng cosmological fragments.** The Liù-cháo fragmentary corpus collected under `KR3f` from `krp-titles` includes *Yùfú ruìtú* 玉符瑞圖 [[KR3f0058]] (auspicious-image fragments), Yáo Xìn's 姚信 *Xīntiān lùn* 昕天論 [[KR3f0059]], Xú Zhěng's 徐整 *Cháng-lì* 長曆 [[KR3f0060]], Dù Yù's 杜預 *Nián-lì* 年曆 [[KR3f0061]], Yú Sǒng's 虞聳 *Qióng-tiān lùn* 穹天論 [[KR3f0062]], Yú Xǐ's 虞喜 *Ān-tiān lùn* 安天論 [[KR3f0063]], and the *Lòu-kè jīng* 漏刻經 [[KR3f0064]] (clepsydra manual). These are the chief surviving evidence for the *xuānyè* and supplementary cosmologies displaced by *húntiān* orthodoxy.

### Important persons

- [[劉徽]] — late-3rd-c. commentator on the *Jiǔzhāng suàn-shù*, author of the *Hǎidǎo suàn-jīng*, and the most original Chinese mathematician of the early-medieval period; his commentary defines the canonical reading of the *Jiǔzhāng*.
- [[李淳風]] — Táng court astronomer; annotator and editor of the *Suànjīng shíshū* curriculum (commentaries on KR3f0001, 0032, 0035, 0036, 0037, 0039), the single figure who fixed the canonical Táng form of the medieval mathematical classics.
- [[甄鸞]] — Northern-Zhōu mathematician, author of *Wǔjīng suàn-shù* and annotator of multiple *Suànjīng* texts; the principal pre-Táng commentator.
- [[王孝通]] — early-Táng author of the *Jígǔ suàn-jīng*, containing the world's first solution of cubic equations.
- [[蘇頌]] — Northern-Sòng minister and polymath; engineer of the Kāifēng water-clock and astronomical tower described in [[KR3f0002]].
- [[秦九韶]] — Southern-Sòng mathematician whose *Shù-xué jiǔ-zhāng* (1247) [[KR3f0041]] presents the Chinese Remainder Theorem; one of the two leading representatives of Sòng-Yuán algebra.
- [[李冶]] — Jīn-Yuán mathematician, author of [[KR3f0042]] and [[KR3f0044]]; consolidator of the *tiānyuán* algebraic method.
- [[趙友欽]] — late-Yuán cosmographer; *Géxiàng xīnshū* [[KR3f0005]] is the most thoroughly quantitative *húntiān* synthesis before the Jesuit arrival.
- [[朱載堉]] — Míng prince, mathematician of equal-temperament fame; *Shèng-shòu wàn-nián lì* [[KR3f0007]] applies his rigorous numerical methods to the calendar.
- [[邢雲路]] — late-Míng astronomer whose *Gǔ-jīn lǜ-lì kǎo* [[KR3f0008]] documented the *Dàtǒng* calendar's accumulating error and set the stage for reform.
- [[利瑪竇]] — Matteo Ricci; with Xú Guāngqǐ and Lǐ Zhīzǎo, the founder of the Chinese mathematical-astronomical Jesuit corpus ([[KR3f0009]], [[KR3f0046]], [[KR3f0047]]).
- [[徐光啟]] — co-translator of *Jǐhé yuánběn* and architect of the *Chóngzhēn lìshū* reform that became *Xīnfǎ suàn-shū* [[KR3f0013]].
- [[李之藻]] — Ricci's principal Chinese collaborator after Xú; translator/expounder of [[KR3f0015]], [[KR3f0016]], and [[KR3f0046]].
- [[湯若望]] — Johann Adam Schall von Bell; chief editor of [[KR3f0013]] in its 1645 imperial recompilation and architect of the *Shíxiàn lì* under the early Qīng.
- [[戴進賢]] — Ignaz Kögler; chief Jesuit astronomer at the Kāngxī–Qiánlóng court, principal compiler of [[KR3f0019]] and [[KR3f0020]].
- [[王錫闡]] — early-Qīng independent astronomer; *Xiǎo'ān xīn-fǎ* [[KR3f0021]] is the most original native synthesis of Chinese and Western methods.
- [[梅文鼎]] — the foundational Qīng mathematician; his *Lì-suàn quán-shū* [[KR3f0026]] and ancillary works define the dominant 17th–18th-c. school and the thesis that Western mathematics is "originally Chinese" (*Xīxué Zhōng-yuán* 西學中源).
- [[江永]] — mid-Qīng polymath; [[KR3f0031]] applies the Méi school's methods to surveying and calendrics.
- [[薛鳳祚]] — translator and partner of Smogulecki; brought logarithms and a Copernican-friendly alternative to Beijing-Jesuit orthodoxy ([[KR3f0024]], [[KR3f0025]]).
- [[聖祖玄燁]] — the Kāngxī Emperor; commissioning patron of [[KR3f0048]] and the broader *Lǜlì yuānyuán* programme.
- [[圓通]] (Enzū) — Edo-period Japanese monk-astronomer whose [[KR3f0057]] is the principal late East-Asian Buddhist response to Western astronomy.

### Topics

- **The *Suànjīng shíshū* and the state curriculum.** The Táng *Guózǐ jiàn* set the canon (Wilkinson §38.17), and Dài Zhèn's recovery from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* in the 1770s — the immediate prelude to the *Sìkù* — is the textual scaffolding on which the entire mathematical half of `KR3f` is built. Most of the medieval titles in this division are essentially Dài Zhèn editions.
- **Counting-rod algebra (*tiānyuán shù* 天元術).** The Sòng-Yuán method of representing one unknown by its position on the counting board, peaking in Qín Jiǔsháo and Lǐ Yě. The technique was largely forgotten by mid-Míng (Gù Yìngxiáng could no longer follow Lǐ Yě's [[KR3f0042]] and had to "explicate" it in [[KR3f0043]]), and was only restored to Chinese mathematical consciousness when Jesuit-era and Qīng scholars (especially Méi Wéndǐng) began re-reading the Sòng-Yuán texts.
- **The three cosmologies: *gàitiān* 蓋天, *húntiān* 渾天, *xuānyè* 宣夜.** The *Zhōubì* [[KR3f0001]] is the locus classicus of *gàitiān*; the Sòng-Yuán-Míng tradition (Sū Sòng [[KR3f0002]], Zhào Yǒuqīn [[KR3f0005]]) is *húntiān*; the fragmentary *Xīntiān lùn* [[KR3f0059]], *Qióng-tiān lùn* [[KR3f0062]] and *Ān-tiān lùn* [[KR3f0063]] are the principal evidence for *xuānyè* and its variants. The *húntiān* model became orthodox after the 6th century (Wilkinson §39.1.1).
- **Calendar reform as political crisis.** Every major dynastic transition in the division (Yuán→Míng, Míng→Qīng) coincides with a calendrical reform: the *Dàtǒng lì* inherits the *Shòushí lì* 授時曆 (1280); Xíng Yúnlù [[KR3f0008]] and Zhū Zǎiyù [[KR3f0007]] document its breakdown; the *Chóngzhēn lìshū* / *Xīnfǎ suàn-shū* [[KR3f0013]] is the Jesuit-led replacement, instituted as the *Shíxiàn lì* under the Qīng in 1645; the *Lì-xiàng kǎo-chéng* and its sequel [[KR3f0018]], [[KR3f0019]] are its Kāngxī–Qiánlóng refinements.
- **The Jesuit transmission and its limits.** The corpus from [[KR3f0009]] through [[KR3f0019]] documents the Ricci-Xú-Lǐ collaboration (Euclid, arithmetic, geometry, astrolabe, telescope) and its institutional outcome in the Imperial Astronomical Bureau. Crucially, the Jesuits transmitted Ptolemaic and Tychonic, not Copernican, planetary models; full Keplerian astronomy enters only in [[KR3f0019]] (1742, by Kögler). The Smogulecki/Xuē Fèngzuò line [[KR3f0024]]–[[KR3f0025]] is a partial exception.
- **The *Lǜlì yuānyuán* programme.** The Kāngxī emperor's three-part synthesis ([[KR3f0018]] *Lì-xiàng kǎo-chéng*; the lost *Lǜ-lǚ zhèng-yì* on harmonics; [[KR3f0048]] *Shù-lǐ jīng-yùn* on mathematics) is the single most consequential state intervention in this division, producing the authoritative Qīng textbooks of astronomy and mathematics. Méi Wéndǐng's [[KR3f0026]] and the entire Méi-school corpus are in dialogue with it.
- **"Western methods are originally Chinese" (*Xīxué Zhōng-yuán* 西學中源).** The Qīng accommodationist thesis, developed by Méi Wéndǐng and ratified by the Kāngxī court, that European mathematics and astronomy ultimately derive from the lost learning of the *Zhōubì* and the *Jiǔzhāng*. The thesis underpins the synthetic ambition of [[KR3f0026]] and [[KR3f0048]] and is the philosophical horizon against which most 17th–18th-c. texts in this division should be read.
- **Mathematical traditions outside the *Suànjīng* canon.** Practical computation manuals (Tú Wényī [[KR3f0056]], Chén Xū [[KR3f0052]]), specialist tracts on circle-arc geometry (Gù Yìngxiáng [[KR3f0045]]), the *shǎoguǎng* 少廣 root-extraction tradition (Chén Shìrén [[KR3f0054]]), and Méi-school surveying ([[KR3f0031]]).

### Timeline

- c. 100 BCE – 200 CE — *Zhōubì suàn-jīng* 周髀算經 [[KR3f0001]] — the *gàitiān* cosmological-mathematical classic; one of the two oldest extant Chinese mathematical works.
- c. 100 BCE – 263 CE — *Jiǔzhāng suàn-shù* 九章算術 [[KR3f0032]] — Hàn-era compendium with Liú Huī's 3rd-c. commentary; the foundational text of imperial Chinese mathematics.
- 220–589 — *Yùfú ruìtú* 玉符瑞圖 [[KR3f0058]] — Liù-cháo auspicious-image fragments preserved in Táng-Sòng leishu.
- c. 240–280 — *Xīntiān lùn* 昕天論 [[KR3f0059]] (Yáo Xìn 姚信) — Wú-period cosmological treatise, fragment.
- c. 230–260 — *Cháng-lì* 長曆 [[KR3f0060]] (Xú Zhěng 徐整) — Wú-period long chronology, fragments.
- 263–280 — *Hǎidǎo suàn-jīng* 海島算經 [[KR3f0035]] (Liú Huī) — surveying problems originally appended to the *Jiǔzhāng* commentary.
- c. 260–284 — *Nián-lì* 年曆 [[KR3f0061]] (Dù Yù 杜預) — early-Jìn calendrical reckoning, fragments.
- c. 270–310 — *Qióng-tiān lùn* 穹天論 [[KR3f0062]] (Yú Sǒng 虞聳) — Jìn-period cosmological fragment.
- c. 320–356 — *Ān-tiān lùn* 安天論 [[KR3f0063]] (Yú Xǐ 虞喜) — Eastern-Jìn cosmological fragment; Yú Xǐ is the discoverer of the precession of the equinoxes.
- 100–500 — *Sūnzǐ suàn-jīng* 孫子算經 [[KR3f0033]] — first extant account of counting-rod arithmetic; origin of the "Sūnzǐ" remainder problem.
- c. 400–570 — *Wǔcáo suàn-jīng* 五曹算經 [[KR3f0036]] — administrative-mathematics textbook for the five government bureaus.
- 466–489 — *Zhāng Qiūjiàn suàn-jīng* 張邱建算經 [[KR3f0039]] — Northern-Wèi mathematical classic; famous for the "Hundred Fowls" indeterminate problem.
- 560–575 — *Wǔjīng suàn-shù* 五經算術 [[KR3f0037]] (Zhēn Luán) — mathematical exegesis of the Confucian *Classics*.
- 560–1300 — *Lòu-kè jīng* 漏刻經 [[KR3f0064]] — clepsydra manual, attributed pre-Táng but containing Sòng anachronisms.
- 580–618 — *Xiàhóu Yáng suàn-jīng* 夏侯陽算經 [[KR3f0038]] — Suí mathematical compendium.
- 600–700 — *Shù-shù jì-yí* 數術記遺 [[KR3f0034]] — attributed to Xú Yuè 徐岳, with Zhēn Luán commentary; the canonical text on archaic counting devices.
- 618–626 — *Jígǔ suàn-jīng* 緝古算經 [[KR3f0040]] (Wáng Xiàotōng 王孝通) — first solution of cubic equations; mainstay of the Táng *Suànxué guǎn* curriculum.
- 1094 — *Xīn yí-xiàng fǎ-yào* 新儀象法要 [[KR3f0002]] (Sū Sòng) — the Kāifēng water-driven armillary clock-tower described and illustrated.
- 1241–1296 — *Liù-jīng tiānwén biān* 六經天文編 [[KR3f0003]] (Wáng Yīnglín) — philological compendium of celestial passages in the *Six Classics*.
- 1247 — *Shù-xué jiǔ-zhāng* 數學九章 [[KR3f0041]] (Qín Jiǔsháo) — Chinese Remainder Theorem and high-water mark of Sòng algebra.
- 1248 — *Cè-yuán hǎi-jìng* 測圓海鏡 [[KR3f0042]] (Lǐ Yě) — systematic treatise on inscribed circles using *tiānyuán* algebra.
- 1259 — *Yì-gǔ yǎn-duàn* 益古演段 [[KR3f0044]] (Lǐ Yě) — pedagogical sequel introducing *tiānyuán* to beginners.
- 1281–1335 — *(Yuánběn) Géxiàng xīnshū* 革象新書 [[KR3f0005]] (Zhào Yǒuqīn) — full *húntiān* cosmographic-mathematical synthesis.
- 1368–1373 — *(Chóngxiū) Géxiàng xīnshū* 革象新書 [[KR3f0004]] — Wáng Yī's early-Míng abridgement.
- 1477 — *Qī-zhèng tuī-bù* 七政推步 [[KR3f0006]] (Bèi Lín) — Chinese transmission of the Islamic *Huíhuí* planetary method.
- 1540–1565 — *Cè-yuán hǎi-jìng fēn-lèi shì-shù* 測圓海鏡分類釋術 [[KR3f0043]] (Gù Yìngxiáng) — Míng restatement of Lǐ Yě, without *tiānyuán*.
- 1552 — *Hú-shǐ suàn-shù* 弧矢算術 [[KR3f0045]] (Gù Yìngxiáng) — Míng monograph on circle-arc and sagitta computations.
- 1595 — *Shèng-shòu wàn-nián lì* 聖壽萬年曆 [[KR3f0007]] (Zhū Zǎiyù) — Wàn-lì reform proposal, mathematically the most rigorous of the late-Míng calendars.
- 1596–1611 — *Gǔ-jīn lǜ-lì kǎo* 古今律歷考 [[KR3f0008]] (Xíng Yúnlù) — vast late-Míng survey of calendrical history that diagnosed the *Dàtǒng* error.
- 1605–1610 — *Qián-kūn tǐ-yì* 乾坤體義 [[KR3f0009]] (Ricci) — first Chinese exposition of Aristotelian-Ptolemaic cosmography.
- 1606–1607 — *Jǐhé yuánběn* 幾何原本 [[KR3f0047]] (Ricci, Xú Guāngqǐ) — first six books of Euclid in Chinese.
- 1607 — *Hún-gài tōng-xiàn tú-shuō* 渾蓋通憲圖說 [[KR3f0015]] (Lǐ Zhīzǎo) — the astrolabe in Chinese.
