# Zhāng Qiūjiàn suàn-jīng 張邱建算經
*Zhāng Qiūjiàn's Mathematical Classic*
by [[張邱建]] (Zhāng Qiūjiàn, 5th cent., 隋, *zhuàn* 撰); annotated by [[甄鸞]] (Zhēn Luán, Northern Zhōu, *zhù* 注); imperially commissioned re-annotation by [[李淳風]] (Lǐ Chúnfēng, Tang, *fèng-chì zhù-shì* 奉敕注釋); detailed procedures (*xì-cǎo* 細草) by Liú Xiàosūn 劉孝孫 (Tang)

## About the work

A 3-juan systematic mathematical classic in 100 problems-and-solutions, composed by Zhāng Qiūjiàn of Qīng-hé in the late 5th or early 6th century. Structurally it follows the *Jiǔ-zhāng* problem-and-solution format but covers considerably more advanced material, with topics including:

- *Juàn shàng* (Upper): self-multiplication-and-division (*chéng-chú*); right-triangle measurement (*gōu-gǔ cè-wàng*) — problems 12-15 specifically on remote-sensing geometry; sum-and-difference relations of *gōu-gǔ*; redoubled-and-inverted *gōu-gǔ* configurations.
- *Juàn zhōng* (Middle): proportional distribution (*chā-fēn*) and combined-with average-distribution (*jūn-shū*); area-and-volume problems (*fāng-yuán mì-jī*); construction calculations (*shāng-gōng*); price-and-tax differences (*guì-jiàn chā-fēn*); arithmetic-progression problems; areal *fāng-tián* fractional methods.
- *Juàn xià* (Lower): the famous *Hundred Fowls problem* (*bǎi-jī wèn-tí*); indeterminate-equation problems; deer-corral and granary three-volume problems with diagrams; *hú-shǐ* (arc-and-arrow) circular-segment problems.

The work's most famous contribution is the *Hundred Fowls problem*: a chicken costs 5 *qián*, a hen costs 3 *qián*, three chicks cost 1 *qián*; with 100 *qián* one purchases 100 fowls. How many of each? This is a system of two equations in three unknowns with positive-integer constraints — i.e., a problem in *indeterminate analysis*. The solution-procedure given is the foundation of pre-modern Chinese indeterminate-equation analysis and the historical predecessor of what would become the European *Diophantine analysis* tradition (Diophantus c. 250 CE worked on similar problems, but his work was lost to medieval Europe and only rediscovered in the Renaissance; the Chinese tradition's continuous engagement with such problems from Zhāng Qiūjiàn through Qín Jiǔsháo's 13th-century elaboration is independent of any Hellenistic transmission).

The Sìkù recension descends from Máo Jìn's 毛晉 (1599-1659) *Jí-gǔ-gé* 汲古閣 transcription of a Sòng manuscript fragment. The *Sòng* edition is the Northern Sòng imperial-secretariat collation by Zhào Yànruò 趙彦若, with the Zhēn Luán *shù yuē* 術曰 (procedure-says) annotations, the Liú Xiàosūn *cǎo yuē* 草曰 (procedure-elaboration-says) detailed working, and the Lǐ Chúnfēng *chén ... jǐn àn* 臣...謹按 imperial-annotated comments preserved together. The Sìkù editors collated the Máo Jìn transcription against the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* fragments and supplied missing diagrams (problems 12-15 of the *gōu-gǔ* section, the missing arc-and-arrow problem in the middle *juàn*, the deer-corral and three-volume problems in the lower *juàn*).

The *Sìkù* 提要 explicitly commends the work for its connection to ancient *Jiǔ-zhāng* methodology: "*[the work's] questions-and-answers cross-collate and clarify; in total 100 conditions, condensed-and-ancient in style, rather like the* Jiǔ-zhāng *and unlike the recent methods. But its threads-and-organization [are] refined-and-dense, truly able to deeply investigate the ancients' purport*". The Tang Imperial Academy thus made it a specialist text for advanced study.

For the broader *Suàn-jīng shí-shū* context, see [[KR3f0032]], [[KR3f0033]], [[KR3f0035]], [[KR3f0036]], [[KR3f0037]], [[KR3f0038]]. For the principal author, see [[張邱建]]. For later Chinese indeterminate-analysis development, see Qín Jiǔsháo's *Shù-shū jiǔ-zhāng* (=[[KR3f0041]] *Shù-xué jiǔ-zhāng*).
