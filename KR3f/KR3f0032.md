# Jiǔ-zhāng suàn-shù 九章算術
*Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art*
attributed (by tradition) to [[張蒼]] (Zhāng Cāng, early Hàn) and [[耿壽昌]] (Gěng Shòuchāng, mid-Hàn) for compilation; with the foundational commentary by [[劉徽]] (Liú Huī, 魏-Jìn, *zhù* 注, *Jǐng-yuán* 4 = 263 CE); imperial annotation by [[李淳風]] (Lǐ Chúnfēng, 唐, *zhù-shì* 注釋); and phonetic-and-meaning gloss by [[李籍]] (Lǐ Jí, 唐, *yīn-yì* 音義, 1 appended *juàn*)

## About the work

The foundational mathematical classic of the Chinese tradition, in 9 *juàn* containing 246 problems with their solutions, organized into nine topical chapters covering the principal branches of pre-modern Chinese applied mathematics:

- **(I) *Fāng-tián* 方田 (Square Fields, 38 problems)** — area calculations for rectangular, triangular, trapezoidal, circular, and other plane figures; including the foundational treatment of fractions and π (taken as 3 in the original but corrected by Liú Huī to 3.14 and by Zǔ Chōngzhī to 3.1415926).
- **(II) *Sù-mǐ* 粟米 (Grain-Rice, 46 problems)** — proportional exchange of commodities (the *Rule of Three* in elementary arithmetic).
- **(III) *Cuī-fēn* 衰分 (Distribution by Proportion, 20 problems)** — proportional distribution problems.
- **(IV) *Shǎo-guǎng* 少廣 (Less-Width, 24 problems)** — areas-and-roots: extraction of square-and-cube roots, particularly through area-decomposition methods.
- **(V) *Shāng-gōng* 商功 (Construction Computations, 28 problems)** — volume calculations for prisms, pyramids, cones, frustra, and spheres.
- **(VI) *Jūn-shū* 均輸 (Equitable Transport, 28 problems)** — taxation, transport, and labor-distribution calculations involving complex weighted averages.
- **(VII) *Yíng bù-zú* 盈不足 (Surplus-and-Deficit, 20 problems)** — the *Rule of False Position* method for solving linear equations.
- **(VIII) *Fāng-chéng* 方程 (Rectangular Arrays, 18 problems)** — systematic solution of systems of linear equations by what is in effect Gaussian elimination performed on counting-rod arrangements; the chapter introduces *negative numbers* as a standard mathematical concept (the first known systematic treatment in any tradition).
- **(IX) *Gōu-gǔ* 勾股 (Right-Triangle, 24 problems)** — the Pythagorean theorem and its applications, including remote-sensing techniques and the famous "broken-bamboo" and "leaning-ladder" problems.

Plus a 10th *Yīn-yì* 音義 (Phonetic-and-Meaning) appended *juàn* by [[李籍]] (Lǐ Jí, Tang).

The work is the principal foundational classic of pre-modern Chinese mathematics, comparable in importance to Euclid's *Elements* in the Greek tradition. Its received recension reflects multiple layers of compilation-and-annotation:

- **Original compilation (early Hàn)**: traditionally attributed to Zhāng Cāng 張蒼 and Gěng Shòuchāng 耿壽昌, both early-Hàn officials with mathematical interests. Internal evidence (the work's references to *Cháng-ān Shàng-lín* 長安上林, the imperial park established under Wǔ-dì in the 130s BCE) shows that the received text cannot be earlier than the mid-Western-Hàn (c. 100 BCE).
- **The Liú Huī commentary (魏-Jìn, 263+ CE)**: Liú Huī of the Wèi-Jìn period composed the foundational commentary, supplying systematic geometric proofs for the procedures stated baldly in the original. His preface (preserved in the SBCK recension) traces the work back to Zhōu Gōng's *Six Arts* curriculum and acknowledges the early-Hàn redaction by Zhāng Cāng and Gěng Shòuchāng. Liú Huī also supplied the *Chóng-chā* 重差 (Double-Differences) supplementary chapter, which would later be detached as the [[KR3f0035]] *Hǎi-dǎo suàn-jīng* under the Tang.
- **The Lǐ Chúnfēng imperial annotation (唐, 656 CE)**: Lǐ Chúnfēng under Táng Gāo-zōng's commission compiled-and-annotated the *Suàn-jīng shí-shū* 算經十書 (Ten Mathematical Classics), of which the *Jiǔ-zhāng suàn-shù* is the principal text. His annotations clarify the Liú Huī commentary at points where it had become obscure.
- **The Lǐ Jí phonetic-and-meaning gloss (唐, late 9th century)**: Lǐ Jí supplied the *Yīn-yì* appended *juàn*, providing phonetic readings and meaning-glosses for difficult terms.
- **Recovery from *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* (Sìkù period, 18th century)**: the work had nearly disappeared from circulation by the late Míng; the *Sìkù* editors recovered most of it from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn*. Some chapters had to be supplemented from other sources.

The work's substantive significance for Chinese mathematics is comprehensive. It establishes the basic problem-types and computational procedures that would shape Chinese mathematical practice for two millennia. Its *Fāng-chéng* chapter's introduction of negative numbers — and its systematic Gaussian-elimination procedure for systems of linear equations — predates the comparable European developments by over a millennium. Its *Gōu-gǔ* chapter's treatment of right-triangle problems is one of the earliest systematic geometric treatises in any tradition.

### Abstract

Composition window: c. 100 BCE – 263 CE. The base-text was compiled in the mid-to-late Western Hàn (the *Cháng-ān Shàng-lín* reference fixes a *terminus post quem* of approximately 110 BCE); the Liú Huī commentary added to the received recension was composed beginning in 263 CE and continued into the Western-Jìn period after 265.

The work's significance:

(a) **Foundational classic of Chinese mathematics**: alongside the [[KR3f0001]] *Zhōubì suàn-jīng*, the *Jiǔ-zhāng suàn-shù* is one of the two foundational pre-modern Chinese mathematical works. Through the *Jiǔ-zhāng*, the Chinese tradition's distinctive applied-mathematical heritage — proportional exchange, area-and-volume calculation, root extraction, system-of-equations solution, right-triangle problems — entered the canonical record. The 246 problems would shape the form of Chinese mathematical exposition (problem-with-solution) for two millennia.

(b) **The Liú Huī commentary**: Liú Huī's contribution transformed the received compilation from a procedural collection into a theoretically-rigorous work. His foundational accomplishments include (i) the *gē-yuán* 割圓 (circle-cutting) method for π, computing π = 3.1416 via 192-gon (extended to 3072-gon for higher precision in the surviving fragments); (ii) systematic geometric proofs (*chū-rù hùxiāng-bǔ* 出入互相補, "outward-and-inward mutual-compensation") for the area-and-volume formulas of the *Fāng-tián* and *Shāng-gōng* chapters; (iii) the explicit articulation of negative-number arithmetic in the *Fāng-chéng* chapter; (iv) the *Chóng-chā* 重差 supplementary chapter on remote-sensing techniques (later detached as the [[KR3f0035]] *Hǎi-dǎo suàn-jīng*).

(c) **The Lǐ Chúnfēng (Tang) editorial-and-pedagogical canon**: Lǐ Chúnfēng's 656 imperial annotation made the *Jiǔ-zhāng* the *first* text of the *Suàn-jīng shí-shū* (Ten Mathematical Classics), the official curriculum of the Tang Imperial Academy's *Suàn-xué guǎn* 算學館 (Mathematics School). Through this institutional canonization, the *Jiǔ-zhāng* shaped East-Asian mathematical pedagogy for centuries (the same canonization led to the work's transmission to Korea and Japan as part of the standard mathematical curriculum).

(d) **The negative-number breakthrough**: the *Fāng-chéng* chapter's systematic treatment of negative numbers — represented in the original as red-and-black counting-rod color-conventions — is the first known systematic mathematical use of negative numbers in any culture. The European mathematical tradition would not systematically embrace negative numbers until the 16th-17th centuries.

(e) **The Sìkù recovery**: the work's near-disappearance by the late Míng and its Yǒnglè dàdiǎn-based recovery in the 18th century is one of the major *Sìkù*-period textual restorations. The Sìkù-recension's collation against pre-Sòng manuscript fragments and Tang-period epigraphic citations established the canonical text used by modern scholarship.

For other Tang-period *Suàn-jīng shí-shū* members, see [[KR3f0033]] *Sūn-zǐ suàn-jīng*, [[KR3f0034]] *Shù-shù jì-yí*, [[KR3f0036]] *Wǔ-cáo suàn-jīng*, [[KR3f0037]] *Wǔ-jīng suàn-shù*. ## Other points of interest

The 提要's identification of internal-textual evidence (the *Cháng-ān Shàng-lín* reference) for dating the work's redaction post-Wǔ-dì is one of the more refined pieces of late-imperial Chinese textual-historical analysis in the *Sìkù tíyào* corpus. The *Sìkù* editors' willingness to use anachronistic-reference methodology to establish dating of received texts — quite rare in late-imperial Chinese textual scholarship — anticipates modern philological methods.

Liú Huī's preface (preserved in the SBCK recension) — with its tracing of mathematical knowledge back through Zhōu Gōng's *Six Arts* to the legendary *Bāo-xī shì* (Fú-xī)'s eight-trigrams — articulates a distinctive Chinese mathematical-historical theory in which mathematics is a central component of the canonical Confucian curriculum, alongside ritual, music, archery, charioteering, and writing. This framing places mathematics within the *liù-yì* (Six Arts) tradition rather than as a separate technical specialty, an intellectual positioning that would shape Chinese mathematical practice through the late imperial period.
