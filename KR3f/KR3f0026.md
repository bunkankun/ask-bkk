# Lì-suàn quán-shū 歷算全書
*Complete Book of Calendrical Computation*
by [[梅文鼎]] (Méi Wéndǐng, 1633–1721, 清, *zhuàn* 撰); posthumous compilation by Wèi Lìtóng 魏荔彤 and Yáng Zuōméi 楊作枚, with additions

## About the work

The canonical anthology of Méi Wéndǐng's mathematical-and-astronomical writings, in 60 *juàn* containing 29 separate sub-treatises (*shí-èr-shí-jiǔ zhǒng* 二十九種). Méi Wéndǐng composed the works over five decades (c. 1670–1721, his entire mature career); the anthology was assembled posthumously by Wèi Lìtóng (a wealthy patron-of-scholarship from Bǎi-mén 柏門) who obtained Méi's papers and entrusted them to Yáng Zuōméi of Wú-xī for editing-and-supplementation. Yáng added his own commentary and supplied missing material before publication c. 1726. The Sìkù-preserved recension is reorganized by the Sìkù editors (the original Wèi-Yáng arrangement was held by the editors to be "*sequentially confused, not having grasped the principal threads*"), with calendrical-astronomical works placed first and mathematical works following.

The reorganized contents (per the 提要) are:

**Calendrical-astronomical (前 / first half):**
- *Lì-xué yí-wèn* 歷學疑問 (3 *juàn*) — the foundational philosophical-historical work; presented to Kāngxī in 1702 with imperial approbation; opens the anthology
- *Lì-xué yí-wèn bǔ* 歷學疑問補 — supplementary calendrical theory
- *Lì-xué dá-wèn* 歷學答問 — letters of calendrical exchange with the *gōng-qīng-dài-fū* (high officials)
- *Hú sān-jiǎo jǔ-yào* 弧三角舉要 — spherical trigonometry, with sphere-instrument diagrams
- *Huán-zhōng shǔ-chǐ* 環中黍尺 — the spherical-trigonometric "angle-substituting-for-computation" method
- *Suì-zhōu dì-dù hé-kǎo* 嵗周地度合考 — apsidal-and-yearly mensuration; comparison of Western years-months-and-degree-units
- *Píng-lì dìng sān-chā shuō* 平立定三差説 — explanation of the seven-regulators' surplus-and-shrinkage
- *Dōng-zhì kǎo* 冬至考 — winter-solstice examination using *Tǒng-tiān*, *Dà-míng*, *Shòu-shí* methods
- *Zhū-fāng rì-guǐ* 諸方日軌 — solar-altitude tables for latitudes 20°–42°
- *Wǔ-xīng jì-yào* 五星紀要 — planetary motion overview
- *Huǒ-xīng běn-fǎ* 火星本法 — Mars-specific (Tycho's reform was originally for Mars)
- *Qī-zhèng xì-cǎo* 七政細草 — practical computation procedures
- *Kuí-rì hòu-xīng jì-yào* 揆日候星紀要 — Zhílì, Jiāng-nán, Hé-nán, Shǎn-xī shadow-and-stellar-position tables
- *Èr-míng bǔ-zhù* 二銘補註 — annotations on the *Yǎng-yí míng* and *Jiǎn-yí míng* (Guō Shǒujìng-period instrument-inscriptions)
- *Lì-xué pián-zhī* 歷學駢枝 — annotated *Dà-tǒng* calendrical-method
- *Jiāo-huì guǎn-jiàn* 交會管見 — eclipse-position reform from north-south-east-west to up-down-left-right
- *Jiāo-shí méng-qiú* 交食蒙求 — eclipse-computation procedures

**Mathematical (後 / second half):**
- *Gǔ suàn yǎn-lüè* 古算衍略 — extension of ancient mathematics
- *Chóu suàn* 籌算, *Bǐ suàn* 茟算, *Dù suàn shì-lì* 度算釋例 — three computational-instrument primers (rod-counting, Napier's-bones [imported via Jesuits], proportional compass)
- *Fāng-chéng lùn* 方程論 — discussion of equation systems
- *Gōu-gǔ chǎn-wēi* 勾股闡微 — right-triangle theorems
- *Sān-jiǎo-fǎ jǔ-yào* 三角法舉要 — plane-trigonometry essentials
- *Jiě gē-yuán zhī gēn* 解割圜之根 — circle-section root-explanation
- *Fāng-yuán mì-jī* 方圓冪積 — square-and-circle areas
- *Jǐ-hé bǔ-biān* 幾何補編 — Euclid supplement
- *Shǎo-guǎng shí-yí* 少廣拾遺 — *Shǎo-guǎng* completion (one of the *Nine Chapters*'s technical sections)
- *Qiàn-dǔ cè-liàng* 塹堵測量 — solid-geometry measurement (with the *qiàn-dǔ* prismatic-figure)

The 提要's enthusiastic verdict (the most fulsome any *Sìkù* tíyào* gives a private scholar in the *Tiānwén suànfǎ* category): "*from Guō Shǒujìng onward, [his] equal has rarely been seen*"; "*self-receiving the Sage Son-of-Heaven's special-distinguishing recognition was indeed not by chance*".

### Tiyao

[Sub-classification: 子部, *Tiānwén suànfǎ* class 1, *tuī-bù* sub-category. Edition: WYG.]

Respectfully [examined]: *Lì-suàn quán-shū*, 60 *juàn*, by Méi Wéndǐng of Our Dynasty. Wéndǐng's *zì* was Dìngjiǔ 定九, [a man of] Xuān-chéng. *Dǔ-zhì shì-gǔ* (firmly-purposeful, fond-of-the-ancient), [he was] especially refined in the calendrical-and-arithmetic learning. In Kāngxī 41 [1702], Grand Secretary Lǐ Guāngdì once presented his *Lì-xué yí-wèn* to the throne. It happened that the *Sage Ancestor Benevolent Imperial Ancestor* [Kāngxī] was on a southern progress at Dé-zhōu, and summoned [Méi] for an audience. The imperially-written *Jī-xué cān-wēi* 積學參微 ("accumulated learning, threading the subtle") in four characters was bestowed on him; on account of [his] old age [he was] sent home. Subsequently, the imperial decree to compile the *Lǜ-lǚ lì-suàn shū* descended to the Jiāng-nán Governor-General; [the imperial command] summoned his grandson [Méi] Juéchéng [Méi Juéchéng 梅㲄成] to enter palace service. After the *Lǜ-lǚ zhèng-yì* book was completed, [Méi Wéndǐng] further received the imperial command via post-station to verify-and-collate. Years later, at over 90 years old, [he] died at home. The *Sage [Kāngxī]* specifically commanded the [Imperial] Textile Bondservant Cáo Fǔ to manage the funeral arrangements — to this day [this] is transmitted as the supreme honor of *examining the ancient*.

The various calendrical-arithmetic books he composed: Lǐ Guāngdì once printed seven titles. The remainder, mostly compositions of his late years, [are either] already revised-into-volumes or roughly-having-the-draft. Wèi Lìtóng obtained their copies and entrusted [them] to Yáng Zuōméi of Wú-xī for collation-and-correction. [Yáng] Zuōméi accordingly appended his own theories; [together] for [Méi] supplementing what was incomplete, and printed [it] for circulation. In total 29 titles, named the *Lì-suàn quán-shū*. However, the sequential-arrangement was confused, not grasping the principal threads.

Respectfully heavy-edited [we have re-organized]: those who are calendrical-establishers placed in front, those who are calculation-discussers placed behind.

[The 提要 lists each of the 29 titles with brief content notes — see the section above for the reorganized list.]

In general, [the work treats] the explication of arithmetic methods, either expanding what the *Nine Chapters* had not completed, or noting the surface-figures of the present method, or discussing the variations of Chinese-and-Western body-shapes, or explaining the proportions of arc-and-sine and right-triangle and eight-lines [trigonometry]. Indeed [with this], the technique of calendrical-arithmetic at this point is greatly complete.

Our State, having cultivated-clear the calendrical-numbers, probed-the-deep and sought-the-hidden, gathered the great-completion of a thousand-ancient. [Méi] Wéndǐng, as a *cǎo-yě shū-shēng* (rural village book-student), was able to cover-thoroughly the investigation, deeply-knowing the source-flow. What he discussed and composed are all sufficient to *penetrate the purport of Chinese-and-Western and bend [it] to the middle of present-and-ancient*. From Guō Shǒujìng onward, [his] equal has rarely been seen. His receiving the *Sage Son-of-Heaven*'s special-distinguishing recognition was indeed not by chance.

Respectfully collated, *Qián-lóng* 46, tenth month [November 1781].

#### Lǐ Guāngdì's *Gōng-jì* 恭記 (Reverently-Recorded, 1704)

In *rén-wǔ* [1702], 10th month, [I] accompanied the imperial chariot on the southern progress, halting at Dé-zhōu. There was an imperial command to fetch the books that had been engraved [under my supervision]. [I] reported that, because of haste, [I] had not brought them; furthermore, the books mostly were classics-and-examination-time-essays, response-school-tutoring's needs — not sufficient to dust the imperial gaze. There was Mr. Méi Wéndǐng of Xuān-chéng, *Lì-xué yí-wèn* in 3 *juàn*, that [I] had revised-and-engraved. [I] respectfully submitted [it], seeking the *Sage Instruction*.

[Imperial] decree said: "*The Imperial Self has paid attention to calendrical-arithmetic for many years; in this matter the Self can decide its right-or-wrong. Will leave the book for inspection. After two days, [the bearer will] receive a face-to-face [audience]*".

The Above said: "*The book submitted yesterday is very fine-hearted; furthermore the discussion is also impartial. This man has used [his] effort deeply. The Self [will] take [it] back to the palace and inspect-and-read in detail*".

I therefore requested that *the August Above* personally add imperial brush refutation-and-revision-and-fixing — so that the rural-and-village scholar would have something to take-and-cut. I myself also obtained to share-in-hearing one or two — fortunate beyond endurance.

The *Above* assented to [this]. Crossing into the next year's spring, the imperial chariot again [made the] southern progress, and accordingly, at the imperial residence, sent back the original book.

Face-to-face spoken: "*The Self has already detail-by-detail read [it]. Within, the circles, dots, smearings, and the slip-attached comments — all are by the Imperial brush*". Subjectively I again asked where this book had defects. The *Above* said: "*No defects, but the computational methods are not yet complete*" — for the Méi book originally was not finished.

The *Sage Verdict* accordingly reached this. Privately I hold: from antiquity, scholars of *Way-and-enterprise* who, having received the imperial decree, had something composed — not to mention them. As for the private-house storage-records, [these are] mostly buried-in-dust and *bù-fù* (jar-coverings). As for the calendrical-imagery and celestial-officer's depths, [these are] especially what the Confucians of the world call *zhuān-mén jué-xué* (specialist abstruse learning). For [those of] the *fond-of-the-strange and indulging-in-the-rare* type, [they] often cannot finish a chapter and leave-off — how could [they] reach to trouble the *Yǐ-yè* (second-watch)'s viewing, line-by-line discussion and character-by-character debate, mutually responding like teacher-and-disciple? Master Méi's encounter may be called *thousand-loads in a moment* (a once-in-a-millennium occurrence).

Now the *Imperial Hand-writing* circulates throughout the realm; only there has not yet been a book *cut-by-the-Sage-hand* (personally edited by the imperial hand) stored among men. How is this only the *Luòxià is-or-is-not is firmly fixed*? Even *Zǐ Yún*'s bequeathed compilations — what is called *encountering an age's ruler, surpassing all the masters* — also need not [wait] for Huán Tán's repeated sigh!

Having returned the book to Master Méi, [I] write its time-month, accordingly raise [it] [for the record], so Méi may treasure-and-receive it. *Jiǎ-shēn* [1704], 5th month, *rén-xū*-day, (subject) Lǐ Guāngdì respectfully records.

### Abstract

Composition window: c. 1670 (Méi Wéndǐng's first surviving mathematical writings; he was approximately 37) – 1721 (his death; the latest works in the anthology). The composition span is therefore over fifty years, with most of the major works completed in the 1680s-1700s. Posthumous assembly: 1721–1726 (Wèi Lìtóng obtains the papers, Yáng Zuōméi edits-and-supplements). First Sìkù-recension reorganization: 1781.

The work's significance:

(a) **The foundational early-Qīng synthesis of Chinese-and-Western mathematical-astronomical learning**: Méi Wéndǐng's project — the systematic comparative engagement of the Chinese mathematical tradition (the *Jiǔ-zhāng*, the *Zhōubì*, the *Shòu-shí*) with the European mathematical tradition (Euclidean geometry, spherical trigonometry, Tychonic-Keplerian astronomy as transmitted by the Jesuits) — is the foundational achievement of high-Qīng mathematical historiography. Through the *Lì-suàn quán-shū* and through Méi Juéchéng's incorporation of Méi Wéndǐng's work into the imperial [[KR3f0018]] *Lì-xiàng kǎo-chéng* and [[KR3f0019]] *Hòu-biān*, the Méi synthesis defined the Chinese mathematical curriculum for the eighteenth century.

(b) **The *Xī-fǎ Zhōng-yuán* (Western methods originate from the East) framing**: Méi Wéndǐng's general historiographical position — that Western mathematical-astronomical superiority is real but represents the *recovery* and *refinement* of methods that had originally been Chinese (the *Zhōubì*'s implicit cosmology, the *Jiǔ-zhāng*'s implicit algebra, the *Shòu-shí*'s implicit precession-correction) — would become the standard high-Qīng historiographical position, repeated throughout the *Sìkù tíyào* (cf. [[KR3f0001]], [[KR3f0014]], [[KR3f0015]], [[KR3f0019]]).

(c) **The 1702 imperial encounter**: Lǐ Guāngdì's *Gōng-jì* preface preserves one of the most vivid first-hand accounts of Kāngxī-period imperial-scholarly interaction. The Kāngxī emperor's personal reading of Méi's *Lì-xué yí-wèn*, his marginalia-and-comments, his face-to-face dialogue with Lǐ Guāngdì about the work's merits-and-defects, and his ultimate verdict ("*no defects, but the computational methods are not yet complete*") together constitute a unique documentary moment in late-imperial Chinese intellectual history. The episode — and the *Jī-xué cān-wēi* imperial calligraphy bestowed on Méi — established Méi's reputation and made possible the broader institutional reception of his work through his grandson Méi Juéchéng.

(d) **The 29-title structure as Chinese mathematical curriculum**: the 29 sub-titles of the *Lì-suàn quán-shū* — covering everything from elementary calendar-theory to advanced spherical trigonometry, from ancient Chinese problems (the *Nine Chapters*' *fāng-tián*, *shǎo-guǎng*, *gōu-gǔ*) to introduced European techniques (Euclidean geometry, Napier's-bones, spherical trigonometry, eclipse-prediction) — together constitute a comprehensive Chinese mathematical curriculum. The Kāngxī-Yōngzhèng imperial mathematical academy's *Shù-lǐ jīng-yùn* (1722) and the broader Qīng mathematical-pedagogical tradition all draw heavily on this curricular structure.

The 提要's verdict — *zì Guō Shǒujìng yǐlái hǎn jiàn qí bǐ* "*from Guō Shǒujìng onward, [his] equal has rarely been seen*" — places Méi Wéndǐng in direct succession with the Yuán-period architect of the *Shòu-shí lì*, leapfrogging the entire Míng dynasty's mathematical-astronomical production. This is one of the most laudatory editorial assessments in the entire *Sìkù tíyào* corpus.

For the related Méi-family works, see [[KR3f0027]] *Dà-tǒng lì-zhì*, [[KR3f0028]] *Wù'ān lì-suàn shū-jì*, and [[KR3f0029]] *Zhōng-Xī jīng-xīng tóng-yì kǎo* (by his brother Méi Wénmǐng). For the imperial works that absorbed his mathematical contributions, see [[KR3f0018]] *Lì-xiàng kǎo-chéng* and [[KR3f0019]] *Hòu-biān*. For the principal author, see [[梅文鼎]].

## Other points of interest

The Cáo Fǔ 曹頫 connection — the Kāngxī emperor's specific commission of the imperial textile bondservant of the famous Cáo family of Sūzhōu (the family later famous as the model for Cáo Xuěqín's *Hóng-lóu mèng* of c. 1750) to manage Méi Wéndǐng's funeral arrangements — is one of the most personally-affecting moments in the late-imperial Chinese imperial-patronage record. The episode places Méi Wéndǐng directly in the social-and-cultural network that would shortly produce the greatest novel of the Qīng dynasty.

The 提要's framing of Méi Wéndǐng as a *cǎo-yě shū-shēng* (rural village book-student) who nonetheless achieved imperial recognition — and surpassed even Guō Shǒujìng — is a paradigm of the high-Qīng meritocratic-of-intellect ideology. The implicit contrast with the institutionally-positioned Western Jesuits (who came as Bureau Directors) is part of the editorial framing: Méi Wéndǐng demonstrates that *Chinese* private scholarship can match-and-exceed the institutionally-supported foreign expertise.
