# Cíyòu xīnshū 慈幼新書
*A New Book of Compassionate Childcare*
by [[程雲鵬]] (撰); preface by [[張希良]]

## About the work

A fourteen-*juǎn* late-Míng / early-Qīng paediatric compendium by **Chéng Yúnpéng** 程雲鵬 (*zì* Fèngchú 鳳雛), a Hú-běi physician and *wén-zhāng* essayist of the *Shùn-zhì* — early *Kāng-xī* generation. The catalog meta records the dynasty as 明; internal evidence (the recorded close association with the eminent Qīng scholar Wèi Xǐ 魏禧, 1624–1681) confirms the work as in fact early Qīng (c. 1650–1700). The work covers paediatric medicine *guǎng-bó jīng-wēi* 廣博精微 (broadly and minutely) — from *tāi-chǎn* 胎產 (obstetrics) through *dòu-zhěn* 痘疹 (smallpox-and-eruptive-fevers), *jīng-xián* 驚癇 (convulsions and epilepsy), *hán-rè* 寒熱 (cold-and-heat disorders), down to *ěr-mù hóu-chǐ* 耳目喉齒 (ear-eye-throat-teeth) and *chuāng-jiē jiè-xuǎn* 瘡癤疥癬 (sores and skin disorders).

### Prefaces

**Preface** by **Zhāng Xī-liáng** 張希良 of Huáng-ān 黃安 (the present *_001.txt* file front-matter; the *_000.txt* file is empty for this text). Zhāng frames the work in terms of Confucian *shì jūn-zǐ* 士君子 social-ethical obligation: a gentleman serves the world in any capacity, *suī qióng ér zài-xià* 雖窮而在下 (even if poor and in a lowly position); medicine is one such service. Zhāng records his prior knowledge of Chéng's reputation as a *wén-zhāng jù-zǐ* (literary giant) of the Chǔ region through Jiǎng Shèn-zhāi's recommendation during Zhāng's tenure as *sī-duó* 司鐸 (Provincial Education Officer) of Jiāng-xià 江夏. He then records the famous Wèi-Xǐ-on-Chéng's-medicine anecdote (Wèi's *Wǔ-hóu yòng-bīng* praise, Chéng's *Dèng Yǔ wèi-cháng wàng-shā yī-rén* deflection) and Chéng's parallel inner-alchemy training with the Yán-líng and Huá-shān adepts. Zhāng closes with a self-deprecating contrast: as a former *Mù-tiān* (Hanlin) academic with administrative responsibility over the Zhèjiāng *shì-xí* 士習 (scholarly customs), he had been frustrated by his inability to alleviate the *chē-xià chuāng-yí* 車下創痍 (the wounds of the people beside the road); Chéng, by contrast, can through his book *jiē* (raise) the people *yú rén-shòu zhī yù* 於仁壽之域 (into the region of humaneness and longevity).

### Abstract

The *Cíyòu xīnshū* is one of the more clinically grounded early-Qīng paediatric works, distinguished by its synthesis of mainstream paediatric *zàng-fǔ* doctrine with the *Wǔ-hóu yòng-bīng* (Zhū-gě Liàng-as-strategist) metaphor of clinical reasoning — i.e. an emphasis on case-specific tactical adaptation against the prescription-formulary norm. The work circulated in the Hú-běi region in manuscript and early print, with an early-Qīng imprint preserved in the *hxwd* recension. Its fourteen *juǎn* (ctext.org preserves *juǎn* 4 *dòu-chuāng* 痘瘡 as a representative chapter) cover the standard paediatric disease range; the section structure follows the conventional Wàn-family / Xuē-family Míng paediatric template rather than the Chén-Fù-zhèng *jīng / chù* polemical re-classification. The work is among the principal Hú-běi-regional paediatric works of the seventeenth century, complementing the better-known *Yòu-yòu jí-chéng* (Lǐng-nán, 1750) and *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn yòu-kē xīn-fǎ* (imperial, 1742).

## Other points of interest

The catalog meta's dynasty attribution to *Míng* is incorrect. The Zhāng Xī-liáng preface's reference to Wèi Xǐ (1624–1681) makes the work early Qīng beyond doubt; a reasonable composition window is c. 1650–1700. The case-driven *Wǔ-hóu yòng-bīng* metaphor of clinical reasoning is one of the most articulated statements of the case-specific-tactical position in early-Qīng paediatric writing.
