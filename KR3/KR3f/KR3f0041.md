# Shùxué jiǔzhāng 數學九章
(originally *Shùshū jiǔzhāng* 數書九章 — the Sìkù transmitted title appears to be corrupted)
*Mathematical Treatise in Nine Chapters*
by [[秦九韶]] (Qín Jiǔsháo, 1208–1261, 宋, *zhuàn* 撰), composed Chúnyòu 7 (1247)

## About the work

Qín Jiǔsháo's monumental 18-juan mathematical treatise in 9 topical chapters containing 81 problems-with-solutions, completed in Chúnyòu 7 (1247) — universally recognized as the supreme mathematical work of the Sòng-Yuán period and one of the great mathematical works of any pre-modern tradition. The 9-chapter organization parallels the *Jiǔzhāng suànshù* ([[KR3f0032]]) in chapter-count but the topical content is entirely different, and the level of mathematical sophistication is dramatically higher.

The 9 chapters:

**(I) *Dàyǎn* 大衍 (Great Expansion)** — Chinese Remainder Theorem in systematic algorithmic form, the *Dàyǎn qiúyī shù* 大衍求一術 (Great-Expansion seeking-one method). This generalizes the [[KR3f0033]] *Sūnzǐ suànjīng* problem-26's specific procedure into a complete algorithm for solving any system of linear congruences with arbitrary moduli (including the difficult case where the moduli share common factors). The chapter is the foundational document of pre-modern Chinese number theory and one of the great algorithmic achievements of pre-modern mathematics. The 提要 specifically commends the chapter as the only place in the entire *Suànjīng* tradition that addresses the *meaning* (*lìfǎ zhī yì* 立法之意) underlying the procedure — i.e., gives a methodological-meta-statement about *why* the algorithm works.

**(II) *Tiānshí* 天時 (Heavenly Times)** — astronomical-calendrical computation: solar-and-lunar motion, planetary positions, gnomon shadows, the *Wèishù* (Star-Stem-Procedure) for planetary calculation. The 提要 criticizes some specific technical errors (particularly the conflation of *Wèishù* with the *fāngchéng* method) but acknowledges the chapter's substantive astronomical content.

**(III) *Tiányù* 田域 (Field Domains)** — area calculations including triangular, trapezoidal, and curvilinear figures.

**(IV) *Cèwàng* 測望 (Surveying)** — height-and-distance measurement (extending the [[KR3f0035]] *Hǎidǎo* tradition).

**(V) *Fùyì* 賦役 (Taxation and Service)** — administrative computation of taxes and labor.

**(VI) *Qiángǔ* 錢榖 (Currency and Grain)** — financial calculations.

**(VII) *Yíngjiàn* 營建 (Civil Engineering)** — earthwork volumes for hydraulic and construction projects.

**(VIII) *Jūnlǚ* 軍旅 (Military Affairs)** — formations and supply calculations.

**(IX) *Shìyì* 市易 (Trade)** — commercial calculations.

The work's most consequential individual technical contribution is the **systematic *lì tiānyuányī shù*** 立天元一術 ("setting up the heavenly-element-one method"), the foundational Chinese algebra system that uses counting-rod arrangements to symbolically represent and manipulate polynomial equations. The 提要's important observation: "*the European new method [renamed it]* jiègēnfāng *and used it for the Nine Chapters and the Eight Lines [trigonometric tables] — its source actually opens from* [Qín] Jiǔsháo*; this can also be said to be a contribution to mathematics*". This is the classic *Xīfǎ Zhōngyuán* 西法中源 framing of European algebra: the European *jiègēnfāng* (introduced via Antoine Thomas, S.J., into the Kāngxī-period [[KR3f0018]] *Lìxiàng kǎochéng*) is the rediscovery of Qín Jiǔsháo's *tiānyuányī*.

The *lì tiānyuányī* method was used by Guō Shǒujìng 郭守敬 in the *Shòushí lì*'s arc-and-arrow (*húshǐ*) computations, and by Lǐ Yě 李冶 ([[KR3f0042]] *Cèyuán hǎijìng* / [[KR3f0043]] *Yìgǔ yǎnduàn*) for right-triangle and circle problems. Through this lineage, Qín Jiǔsháo's algebra became the foundation of the Sòng-Yuán mathematical tradition.

The 提要 acknowledges some specific weaknesses (the *Shi-guà fāwēi* part of the *Dàyǎn* chapter is criticized as misapplying *Dàyǎn* methodology to *Yìjīng* yarrow-stalk computation; the *Tiānshí* chapter's astronomical computations use averaged rather than fixed seasonal-nodes and so are less accurate than post-Tycho European methods). But the verdict overall is highly favorable: the work has "*both flaws-and-merits visible alongside, but the refined-and-correct passages are the majority*"; Qín Jiǔsháo "*during the Sòng's late period alone rose-up and clarified the lost-learning*".

The Sìkù recension is recovered from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn*; the editors corrected obvious errors, supplemented missing material, reordered displaced sections, and added explanatory *ànyǔ* 案語 (editorial notes) under each problem.

For Qín Jiǔsháo's biography, see [[秦九韶]]. For the related Sòng-Yuán mathematicians, see [[KR3f0042]] *Cèyuán hǎijìng* by Lǐ Yě and [[KR3f0046]] *Yáng Huī suànfǎ* by Yáng Huī. For the foundational source the *Dàyǎn* generalizes, see [[KR3f0033]] *Sūnzǐ suànjīng*.

## Other points of interest

The 提要's characterization of the Sòng-period intellectual climate — "*esteemed empty-discussion and despised practical-use; mathematics, although one of the Sage-Gate Six Arts, was likewise scorned and not spoken of... only Shěn Kuò exhausted his heart on this matter, and outside the* Mèngxī bǐtán *there was no completed book*" — is a strikingly negative late-imperial Chinese assessment of Sòng intellectual culture's relationship to mathematics. The framing isolates Qín Jiǔsháo (and the related Sòng-Yuán mathematicians) as solitary figures in an otherwise mathematically-impoverished period.

The 提要's *Xīfǎ Zhōngyuán* identification of Qín Jiǔsháo's *tiānyuányī* with European *jiègēnfāng* algebra is the most influential statement of the *Sòng-Yuán recovered* thesis in the *Sìkù* corpus. Through this framing, the 18th-century Chinese mathematical community could position the imported Western algebra as the rediscovery of native Chinese mathematics, rather than as a foreign import. Modern scholarship has tempered this claim (the European algebra developed independently and exceeded the Sòng-Yuán in symbolic generality), but the underlying observation that the two traditions converged on equivalent symbolic-algebraic methods for polynomial equations is correct.
