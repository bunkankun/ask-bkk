# Qióngtiān lùn 穹天論
*Treatise on the Vaulted Heaven*
by [[虞聳]] (Yú Sǒng, Western Jìn cosmologist, *Héjiān xiāng* 河間相, late 3rd – early 4th c., 晉, *zhuàn* 撰)

## About the work

A very short cosmological-and-astronomical treatise from the Western Jìn period, surviving only as a single substantive paragraph preserved in the *Jìn shū tiānwén zhì* 晉書·天文志 (compiled by Lǐ Chúnfēng 李淳風 *et al.* in the early Táng). The *qióngtiān* ("vaulted heaven") model proposed in the treatise became one of the four canonical post-Hàn alternative cosmologies, alongside Yáo Xìn's *xīntiān* 昕天 ([[KR3f0059]]) and Yú Xǐ's *āntiān* 安天 ([[KR3f0063]]). The Kanripo edition reproduces the *Jìn shū* fragment with an appended *fùlù* 附錄 of related cosmological-historical material from the same chapter.

### Abstract

Composition window: c. 270–310, during Yú Sǒng's mature service at the Western Jìn court as *Héjiān xiāng* 河間相. The exact composition date is not preserved. The *Jìn shū tiānwén zhì* — the principal source — provides the work's text as part of its historiographical survey of cosmological theories from the *gàitiān* and *húntiān* schools onward.

The text begins with the signature image: *"tiān xíng qiónglóng rú jīzǐ, mù qí jì zhōu jiē sìhǎi zhī biǎo, fú yú yuánqì zhī shàng, pì rú fù lián yǐ yì shuǐ, ér bù mò zhě, qì chōng qí zhōng gù yě"* 天形穹窿如雞子，幕其際周接四海之表，浮於元氣之上，譬如覆奩以抑水，而不沒者，氣充其中故也 ("the form of the heavens is vaulted, like a chicken's egg; its canopy at the rim circumferentially abuts the four seas, and floats atop primordial *qì* — as when one inverts a small box (*lián* 奩) onto water, and it does not sink, because *qì* fills its interior"). The model thus retains the *húntiān* image of the celestial sphere but adds a physical-mechanical account of how the vault stays in place — by the buoyant interior *qì* — and explicitly limits the cosmic-vault image to the upper hemisphere ("the heaven extends down to the four seas," not all the way around as in the strict *hún* model).

The text then makes precise quantitative claims: the celestial pole (*chénjí* 辰極) lies thirty degrees below the geometrical north on the earth-disc, tilted at the *mǎo*–*yǒu* 卯酉 (east-west) axis. Human observers are positioned c. 100,000 *lǐ* south of the *mǎoyǒu* axis, so that the Dipper's pole is not above the geographical centre of the earth but inclines toward the human standpoint. The sun's path along the ecliptic (黃道) at winter solstice extends 115° north of the ecliptic-pole and at summer solstice 67° south, generating seasonal variation by the angle of the ecliptic at the two solstices.

The *fùlù* preserves a parallel *Jìn shū tiānwén zhì* passage summarising the four-model cosmography: of the three traditional schools (*húntiān*, *gàitiān*, *xuānyè*), *xuānyè* has been transmitted "by hearsay only" and is no longer extant as a coherent theory; later Yú Bǐng 虞昺 (corrected here for the source's reading; the *Jìn shū* gives Yú Sǒng) composed *Qióngtiān lùn*, Yáo Xìn composed *Xīntiān lùn*, and Yú Xǐ composed *Āntiān lùn* (*"hòu yǒu Yú Bǐng zuò Qióngtiān lùn, Yáo Xìn zuò Xīntiān lùn, Yú Xǐ zuò Āntiān lùn, zhòng xíng shū xiàng cāncī qí jiān"* 後有虞昺作穹天論，姚信作昕天論，虞喜作安天論，眾形殊象參差其間). The variant *Yú Bǐng* 虞昺 (a sibling of Yú Xǐ on some accounts) for *Yú Sǒng* in the Kanripo fragment's secondary attribution is a known textual confusion: the *Jìn shū* main text in its primary attribution clearly reads *Yú Sǒng* 虞聳 as *zúzǔ* of Yú Xǐ, but variant transmissions assign the *Qióngtiān lùn* to Yú Bǐng. The Kanripo krp-titles register and the surviving fragment-quotation chain (via the *Jìn shū* and *Tàipíng yùlǎn*) both follow the Yú Sǒng attribution, which is followed here.

The *gàitiān* school is further sub-divided in the *fùlù* into three variants: (a) the heaven moves like a chariot-canopy among the eight cardinal directions (*"tiān rú jūgài, yóu hū bā jí zhī zhōng"* 天如車蓋，遊乎八極之中); (b) the heaven is shaped like a hat with the centre high and the edges low; (c) the heaven is like a slanted chariot-canopy, high in the south and low in the north. These three *gàitiān* sub-variants, alongside the four named treatises (*hún* / *xīn* / *qióng* / *ān*), constitute the standard Táng-period historical inventory of pre-Sòng Chinese cosmology.

For Yú Sǒng's biographical context, see [[虞聳]]; for the related cosmological treatises, see [[KR3f0059]] (*Xīntiān lùn*) and [[KR3f0063]] (*Āntiān lùn*).

## Other points of interest

The *Qióngtiān* model — vaulted heaven floating on *qì*, with a physical-mechanical explanation for stability — is one of the earliest extant Chinese applications of buoyancy reasoning to a cosmological problem. The analogy of the inverted box held up by interior *qì* against water (*"fù lián yǐ yì shuǐ"* 覆奩以抑水) anticipates the Sòng-period interest in pneumatic-and-fluid-mechanical analogies in natural philosophy (e.g. Shěn Kuò 沈括's *Mèngxī bǐtán* 夢溪筆談 reflections on atmospheric pressure and on buoyant solids).
