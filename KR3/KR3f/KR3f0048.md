# Yùzhì Shùlǐ jīngyùn 御製數理精蘊
*Imperially Composed Essential Principles of Mathematics*
by [[聖祖玄燁]] (the Kāngxī emperor, 1654–1722, 清, *chìzhuàn* 敕撰 — by imperial decree composed); editorial direction by [[允祿]] Yǔnlù et al., with [[何國宗]] Hé Guózōng, Méi Juéchéng 梅㲄成, and others as principal mathematical staff. Chartered Kāngxī 52 (1713); printed Yōngzhèng 2 (1724).

## About the work

The mathematical core of the great Kāngxī-Yōngzhèng-period imperial synthesis *Lǜlì yuānyuán* 律歷淵源 (Foundations of Music and Astronomy, 100 *juàn* total). In 53 *juàn*, the work systematically expounds the entire mathematical curriculum then accessible — combining the foundational Chinese mathematical tradition (the *Hétú*, *Luòshū*, *Zhōubì jīng*, *Jiǔzhāng*, *suànfǎ*) with the European mathematics transmitted through the Jesuit channel (Euclidean geometry, trigonometry, logarithms, algebra). The work is the foundational systematic mathematical curriculum of the high Qīng and was the working reference for Chinese mathematical education through the late-Qīng period.

Structure (per the 提要):

**Upper Edition (上編 *shàngbiān*) — 5 *juàn* — *Lìgāng míngtǐ* 立綱明體 (Establishing the Outline, Clarifying the Body)**:
- *Shùlǐ běnyuán* 數理本源 (Source-and-Foundation of Numerical Principle)
- *Hétú* 河圖 (Yellow-River Diagram)
- *Luòshū* 洛書 (Luò-River Writing)
- *Zhōubì jīng jiě* 周髀經解 (Explanation of the *Zhōubì*)
- *Jǐhé yuánběn* 幾何原本 (Euclid's *Elements* — re-exposition)
- *Suànfǎ yuánběn* 算法原本 (Original-Foundations of Calculation Methods)

**Lower Edition (下編 *xiàbiān*) — 40 *juàn* — *Fēntiáo zhìyòng* 分條致用 (Categorical Practical Application)**:
- *Shǒubù* 首部 (Head Section — basic operations)
- *Xiànbù* 線部 (Line Section — linear and proportional procedures)
- *Miànbù* 面部 (Surface Section — area calculations)
- *Tǐbù* 體部 (Body Section — solid-volume calculations)
- *Mòbù* 末部 (End Section — including *jiègēnfāng* 借根方 algebra)

**Tables (表 *biǎo*) — 8 *juàn***:
- *Bāxiàn biǎo* 八線表 (Eight-Lines Table — trigonometric values: *sin*, *cos*, *tan*, *cot*, *sec*, *csc*, *vers*, *covers*)
- *Duìshù chǎnwēi biǎo* 對數闡微表 (Subtle-Exposition Logarithm Table)
- *Duìshù biǎo* 對數表 (Logarithm Table)
- *Bāxiàn duìshù biǎo* 八線對數表 (Eight-Lines-Logarithm Table)

The 提要's substantive comparative-mathematical observations:

(a) **The reform of *fāngchéng***: the old Chinese *fāngchéng* (system-of-equations) tradition had treated the 2-color, 3-color, 4-color, 5-color cases as separate methods, with substantial methodological errors in the positive-and-negative-number rules. The *Shùlǐ jīngyùn* unifies all cases into four general patterns (*héshù* 和數 — sum-numbers; *jiàoshù* 較數 — difference-numbers; *héjiào jiānyòng* — combined sum-and-difference; *héjiào jiāobiàn* — sum-and-difference exchange-variation), simplifying the apparatus and correcting the errors. This is the foundational systematization of Gaussian-style elimination in the Chinese tradition.

(b) **The π refinement**: the work computes π by both Zǔ Chōngzhī's 6-sided-inscribed-polygon method and Zhào Yǒuqīn's 4-sided-inscribed-polygon method ([[KR3f0005]]), showing that both yield the same value 3.14159625, equivalent to the Western value. The work then reforms the *húshǐ* (arc-and-arrow) procedures to use this refined value, replacing the inadequate older Chinese *jìngyī wéisān* 徑一圍三 (3:1 ratio) approximation.

(c) **The trigonometric tables and the eight-lines**: the work introduces the *bāxiàn* 八線 (eight lines) — the eight trigonometric functions sin, cos, tan, cot, sec, csc, versin, coversin — at intervals of 1 *cùn* / 10 *miǎo* across one *xiàngxiàn* (quadrant), tabulating to high precision.

(d) **Extension of Euclidean solid geometry**: the work supplements the 6-book Ricci-Xú [[KR3f0047]] *Jǐhé yuánběn* with the Euclidean material on regular solids (treated in the original *Elements* books XIII and XV but never translated by Ricci-Xú). The *Shùlǐ jīngyùn* computes volumes of inscribed-and-circumscribed regular polyhedra up to 12-and-20-sided, using the *lǐfēnzhōngmò xiàn* 理分中末線 (the *golden ratio* — *median-and-extreme proportion* of Euclid's book VI definition 3).

(e) **Algebra (*jiègēnfāng* 借根方)**: the work introduces the European algebra received through the Jesuit channel. The 提要 explicitly identifies *jiègēnfāng* as the recovered *lì tiānyuányī* 立天元一 of Lǐ Yě ([[KR3f0042]]) and Qín Jiǔsháo ([[KR3f0041]]) — the *Xīfǎ Zhōngyuán* 西法中源 framing.

(f) **Logarithms and proportional compass**: the work introduces logarithms (received via [[薛鳳祚]] Xuē Fèngzuò's [[KR3f0024]] [[KR3f0025]] Smogulecki transmission) and proportional-compass methodology (*bǐlìguī jiě* 比例規解), framing both as "*calculation by quantity, replacing computation by computation*".

The 提要's verdict — for the entire work and for the Kāngxī project as a whole — is exceptional: "*[the work is] indeed an unprecedented book; even specialist named-houses cannot peep at one part-in-ten-thousand of its height-and-depth*". This is one of the most enthusiastic editorial verdicts in the entire *Sìkù tíyào* corpus.

For the parallel *Lǜlì yuānyuán* parts, see [[KR3f0018]] *Lìxiàng kǎochéng*. For the principal author, see [[聖祖玄燁]]. For the principal Chinese-mathematical-staff figures, see [[允祿]], [[何國宗]], [[梅文鼎]], [[李冶]] (recovered through [[KR3f0042]]), [[秦九韶]] (recovered through [[KR3f0041]]). For the European mathematical sources, see [[利瑪竇]] (Ricci) and the various Jesuit collaborators of the Kāngxī mathematical academy.

## Other points of interest

The Kāngxī mathematical academy (the *Méngyǎng zhāi* 蒙養齋, established Kāngxī 52 / 1713 in the inner palace) is among the most institutionally important developments of the high Qīng. Through the *Méngyǎng zhāi* the Kāngxī emperor created a permanent imperial mathematical-and-astronomical research-and-teaching establishment, descendant of which (through the Yōngzhèng-Qián-lóng-period continuations) would constitute the institutional thread of imperial Chinese mathematical research from the late Míng to the late Qīng.

The 提要's *Xīfǎ Zhōngyuán* identification of *jiègēnfāng* (algebra) with *lì tiānyuányī* — anchored in Méi Juéchéng's *Chìshuǐ yízhēn* and articulated authoritatively in this 提要 — is the foundational late-imperial Chinese statement of the *Sòng-Yuán recovered* thesis. Through this framing, the 18th-century Chinese mathematical community could integrate the imported Western algebra into the Chinese mathematical canon as the rediscovery of native methodology, rather than as a foreign import.
