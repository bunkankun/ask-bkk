# KR3fc 算法 — Suànfǎ (Mathematical Methods)

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3fc 算法 (*Suànfǎ*) is the pure-mathematics sub-division of KR3f 天文算法類 (*Tiānwén suànfǎ lèi*), parallel to the asterism-and-observational [[KR3fa]] 天文 and the calendrical-method [[KR3fb]] 曆法. Where the Sìkù 算書之屬 sub-category is represented inside the imperial canon by the *Suànjīng shíshū* 算經十書 corpus and the Kāngxī–Qiánlóng compendia at [[KR3f0032]]–[[KR3f0056]], KR3fc is the Kyoto-University supplementary collection (refid prefixes `KX03-07-057` through `KX03-07-124`, plus `KXDLJ003*` items) drawn from the *Zhōngguó kēxué jìshù diǎnjí tōnghuì* 中國科學技術典籍通匯 and cognate cóngshū reprints, gathering the indigenous Chinese mathematical literature in its full historical depth — including the Sòng-Yuán algebraic peak whose tradition the *Sìkù* could only partially document, and the dense Qīng *kǎozhèng* 考證 recovery effort that returned that tradition to active use after a four-century hiatus. Wilkinson §38.17 frames this corpus as one of the four principal scientific traditions of pre-modern China; the present sub-division is its most direct documentary witness.

The chronological span runs from the Hàn-Wèi-Jìn *Jiǔzhāng* 九章 redaction with [[劉徽]] Liú Huī's *Jǐngyuán* 4 (263) commentary [[KR3fc001]] through the Suí-Táng curricular consolidation under [[李淳風]] Lǐ Chúnfēng (656), the Sòng-Yuán algebraic flowering of [[秦九韶]] Qín Jiǔsháo (1247), [[李冶]] Lǐ Yě (1248), [[楊輝]] Yáng Huī (1261–75), and [[朱世傑]] Zhū Shìjié (1299–1303), the Yuán-Míng popular-arithmetic codification culminating in [[程大位]] Chéng Dàwèi's *Suànfǎ tǒngzōng* 算法統宗 (1592, in the WYG line at [[KR3f0045]]; the cognate [[KR3fc027]] is the Kyoto recension), the early-Jesuit *Gōugǔ* 勾股 / *jǐhé* 幾何 encounter under [[徐光啟]] Xú Guāngqǐ (1606–33) and [[陳藎謨]] Chén Jìnmó (c. 1640), the long Qīng *kǎozhèng* recovery of the indigenous tradition centered on [[戴震]] Dài Zhèn's reconstitution of the *Suànjīng shíshū* from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* and the Yángzhōu–Sūzhōu mathematical school of [[焦循]] Jiāo Xún, [[汪萊]] Wāng Lái, and [[李銳]] Lǐ Ruì (c. 1795–1820), and culminating in the late-Qīng synthesis of [[李善蘭]] Lǐ Shànlán (1845–67), where the Sòng-Yuán *tiānyuán* tradition meets the differential calculus on the verge of full Westernization. The Wilkinson §38.17.2 list of "the ten" — *Zhōubì*, *Jiǔzhāng*, *Sūnzǐ*, *Hǎidǎo*, *Wǔcáo*, *Wǔjīng suànshù*, *Xiàhóu Yáng*, *Zhāng Qiūjiàn*, *Jígǔ*, with *Shùshù jìyí* as auxiliary — is the indispensable framing concept; nearly half the works in KR3fc are commentaries, reconstructions, or extensions of texts in this list.

The intellectual posture of the division as a whole is captured by the studio names of its principal Qīng authors: [[戴震]] Dài Zhèn's *Wù'ān* 勿菴 (in his Anhui school), [[焦循]] Jiāo Xún's *Lǐtáng* 里堂 (whence [[KR3fc042]] *Lǐtáng xuésuàn jì* 里堂學算記), [[汪萊]] Wāng Lái's *Héngzhāi* 衡齋 ([[KR3fc049]] *Héngzhāi suànxué*), and above all [[李善蘭]] Lǐ Shànlán's *Zégǔxī zhāi* 則古昔齋 ([[KR3fc078]]) — "the studio for following the ancients". The studio names declare the *kǎozhèng* programme: to recover the lost Chinese mathematical tradition (Sòng-Yuán algebra; the *Suànjīng shíshū*; the ancient calendrical systems) on its own terms, not as a tributary of European mathematics but as a coequal achievement to be brought back into productive scholarly use.

## Important texts and text clusters

The 78 cataloged works (the sequence runs `KR3fc001`–`KR3fc080` with `KR3fc049a` and `KR3fc077` absent in the present corpus) divide into nine readily recognisable clusters.

**Suànjīng shíshū textual cluster.** The Kyoto recensions of the ancient mathematical classics, complementing the WYG / *Sìkù* line of [[KR3f0032]]–[[KR3f0040]].
- *Jiǔzhāng suànjīng* 九章筭經 [[KR3fc001]] — the Liú Huī-and-Lǐ Chúnfēng *Jiǔzhāng* in its five-*juàn* Táng-canonical recension under the older orthography *suàn* 筭; the head of the *Suànjīng shíshū* curriculum.
- *Zhāng Qiūjiàn suànjīng* 張丘建算經 [[KR3fc004]] — the Northern-Wèi text with [[李淳風]]'s *zhùshì*, principal source for indeterminate problems (the "hundred fowls" problem).
- *Jígǔ suànjīng* — three Qīng commentaries on Wáng Xiàotōng's 王孝通 Táng text: [[李潢]] Lǐ Huáng's *Kǎozhù* [[KR3fc005]], [[張敦仁]] Zhāng Dūnrén's *Xìcǎo* [[KR3fc006]], and [[劉衡]] Liú Héng's *Bǔzhù* [[KR3fc007]] — a textbook example of the dense Qīng commentarial work on a single short Táng classic.

**Lǐ Huáng's recovery program.** [[李潢]] Lǐ Huáng's (?–1812) magisterial xìcǎo túshuō series, posthumously completed and edited by [[沈欽裴]] Shěn Qīnpèi, supplies the standard early-19th-century *kǎozhèng* readings for three of the canonical Ten.
- *Jiǔzhāng suànshù xìcǎo túshuō* 九章算術細草圖說 [[KR3fc002]] — the now-standard reading edition of the *Jiǔzhāng*.
- *Hǎidǎo suànjīng xìcǎo túshuō* 海島算經細草圖說 [[KR3fc003]] — the parallel reading edition of Liú Huī's *Hǎidǎo* 海島.
- *Jígǔ suànjīng kǎozhù* 緝古算經考注 [[KR3fc005]] — the parallel reading edition of Wáng Xiàotōng's *Jígǔ*.

**Sòng-Yuán algebraic peak.** The four monuments of medieval Chinese algebra, all preserved in the present sub-division.
- *Shùshū jiǔzhāng* 數書九章 [[KR3fc008]] (1247) — [[秦九韶]] Qín Jiǔsháo's 18-*juàn* / 81-problem treatise. Contains the first complete algorithmic statement of the Chinese Remainder Theorem (*Dàyǎn qiúyī shù* 大衍求一術), systematic *tiānyuán* algebraic equations, and a Horner-type method for polynomial evaluation. The Kyoto edition preserves the correct title against the *Sìkù*'s corrupted *Shùxué jiǔzhāng* at [[KR3f0041]].
- *Cèyuán hǎijìng xìcǎo* 測圓海鏡細草 [[KR3fc015]] (1248) — [[李冶]] Lǐ Yě's 16-*juàn* treatise systematizing the *tiānyuányī* 立天元一 method for setting up and solving polynomial equations in a single unknown, framed around the inscribed-circle problems of the right triangle. The parallel WYG entry is at [[KR3f0042]].
- *Suànxué qǐméng* 算學啟蒙 [[KR3fc016]] (1299) and *Sìyuán yùjiàn* 四元玉鑑 [[KR3fc017]] (1303) — [[朱世傑]] Zhū Shìjié's two principal works. The *Qǐméng* is the elementary primer that consolidated the entire Sòng-Yuán curriculum in 259 problems; the *Sìyuán yùjiàn* extends *tiānyuányī* to systems of up to four unknowns (*tiān, dì, rén, wù*) via a rod-numeral two-dimensional notation, with the *xiāofǎ* 消法 elimination procedure (anticipating Bézout's resultant by four centuries) and the *zhāochā* 招差 finite-difference interpolation (anticipating Newton). The *Qǐméng* was lost in China after the Míng, recovered in 1839 from a Korean reprint and re-edited as [[KR3fc020]]; the *Sìyuán yùjiàn* itself was preserved continuously but became unintelligible by 1700 and was re-elucidated in the 1830s as [[KR3fc018]]–[[KR3fc019]].
- *Yáng Huī suànfǎ* 楊輝算法 [[KR3fc010]] and the cognate *Xiángjiě jiǔzhāng suànfǎ* 詳解九章算法 [[KR3fc011]] and *Suànfǎ* 算法 [[KR3fc021]] — [[楊輝]] Yáng Huī's (fl. 1261–75) collected work. The *Xiángjiě* preserves [[賈憲]] Jiǎ Xiàn's eleventh-century method for *n*-th-root extraction (the "Yáng Huī triangle", anticipating Pascal by six centuries) and Liú Yì's lost *Yìgǔ gēnyuán* 議古根源 polynomial techniques.

**The post-Sòng-Yuán recovery of *tiānyuán* and *sìyuán*.** The works that took the lost algebraic tradition of the previous cluster and reconstructed it for nineteenth-century use.
- *Suànxué yuánliú* 算學源流 [[KR3fc009]] — an anonymous Sòng-Yuán manuscript on the history and method of mathematics, transmitted through the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn*.
- *Xiángjiě jiǔzhāng suànfǎ zhájì* 詳解九章算法札記 [[KR3fc012]] — [[宋景昌]] Sòng Jǐngchāng's c. 1840 *zhájì* notes that supply the running textual-critical apparatus for the Yíjiātáng reprint of Yáng Huī.
- *Xīnbiān sìyuán yùjiàn* 新編四元玉鑒 [[KR3fc018]], *Sìyuán yùjiàn xìcǎo* 四元玉鑑細草 [[KR3fc019]], *Xīnbiān suànxué qǐméng / Zǒngkuò* 新編算學啟蒙/總括 [[KR3fc020]] — [[沈欽裴]] Shěn Qīnpèi and [[羅士琳]] Luō Shìlín's 1820s–1830s editorial reconstructions of Zhū Shìjié, the works through which Sòng-Yuán algebra re-entered nineteenth-century Chinese mathematics.

**Yuán-Míng popular-arithmetic codification.** The corpus of practitioner manuals that transmitted basic computation from the abacus revolution through to the late Míng.
- *Dīng Jù suànfǎ* 丁巨算法 [[KR3fc013]] (1355) — early Yuán practitioner's handbook.
- *Xiángmíng suànfǎ* 詳明算法 [[KR3fc014]] (1373) — [[安止齋]] Ān Zhǐzhāi and [[何平子]] Hé Píngzǐ's standard primer of the Yuán-Míng transition.
- *Suànfǎ quánnéng jí* 算法全能集 [[KR3fc022]] — [[賈亨]] Jiǎ Hēng's late-fourteenth-century compendium.
- *Jiǔzhāng xiángzhù bǐlèi suànfǎ dàquán* 九章詳註比類算法大全 [[KR3fc023]] (1450) — [[吳敬]] Wú Jìng's massive Míng compendium, the dominant *Jiǔzhāng*-derivative textbook before Chéng Dàwèi.
- *Xīnbiān zhízhǐ suànfǎ tǒngzōng* 新編直指算法統宗 [[KR3fc027]] (1592) — [[程大位]] Chéng Dàwèi's *Suànfǎ tǒngzōng*, the work that codified abacus computation for two centuries of Míng-Qīng practice and that circulated throughout East Asia (the parallel WYG entry is at [[KR3f0045]]).
- *Shéndào dàbiān lìzōng suànhuì* 神道大編歷宗算會 [[KR3fc024]] — [[周述學]] Zhōu Shùxué's encyclopedic mid-Míng mathematical-and-calendrical compendium.
- *Gōugǔ suànshù* 勾股算術 [[KR3fc025]] — [[顧應祥]] Gù Yīngxiáng's mid-Míng right-triangle textbook (also represented at [[KR3f0049]]); the same Gù Yīngxiáng whose loss of comprehension of the *tiānyuán* notation in his Cèyuán hǎijìng restatement [[KR3f0043]] dates the disappearance of Sòng-Yuán algebra in the mainstream Míng tradition.

**Early-Jesuit *gōugǔ* / *jǐhé* encounter.** The first Chinese mathematical works produced under the impact of Ricci and his successors. The *Sìkù* shelves the Jesuit translations themselves at [[KR3f0046]]–[[KR3f0047]] in KR3f; KR3fc preserves the *Chinese* responses.
- *Gōugǔ yì* 勾股義 [[KR3fc026]] (1606–08) — [[徐光啟]] Xú Guāngqǐ's reframing of right-triangle theory in Euclidean-deductive form after the *Jǐhé yuánběn* 幾何原本 translation.
- *Dìngfǎ píngfāng suànshù* 定法平方算術 [[KR3fc029]] — Xú Guāngqǐ on systematic square-root extraction.
- *Dùcè* 度測 [[KR3fc030]], *Dùsuàn jiě* 度算解 [[KR3fc031]], *Kāipíngfāng shuō* 開平方說 [[KR3fc032]], *Kāilìfāng shuō* 開立方說 [[KR3fc033]] — [[陳藎謨]] Chén Jìnmó's quartet of late-Míng didactic treatises on surveying, sector-rod computation, and root extraction, the principal vehicle for the Jesuit *settore* (proportional compass) in Chinese mathematics.
- *Jiāliàng suànjīng* 嘉量算經 [[KR3fc028]] — [[朱載堉]] Zhū Zǎiyù's late-Míng applied-mathematics treatise on the imperial standard measures (the same prince-mathematician whose calendrical and acoustical work appears at [[KR3f0007]] and [[KR4d0035]]).

**Méi Wéndǐng and the early-Qīng synthesis.** The dominant early-Qīng mathematical school. The principal Méi Wéndǐng works are in the WYG line at [[KR3f0026]]–[[KR3f0028]]; the present sub-division preserves the Anhui-school adjacents and Méi's auxiliary works.
- *Suànhǎi xiángshuō* 算海詳說 [[KR3fc034]] — [[李長茂]] Lǐ Chángmào's late-Kāngxī mathematical compendium.
- *Gǔsuàn yǎnlüè* 古算衍略 [[KR3fc035]] and *Chóusuàn* 籌算 [[KR3fc036]] — [[梅文鼎]] Méi Wéndǐng's historical-mathematical essay and his treatise on Napier's rods, the principal Chinese channel for European logarithmic computation.
- *Gēyuán mìlǜ jiéfǎ* 割圈密率捷法 [[KR3fc037]] — [[安圖]] Āntú (Minggatu, c. 1692–1763), the Mongol mathematician of the *Qīntiānjiān*, on trigonometric power-series expansions for sine, cosine and arc-functions, with [[陳際新]] Chén Jìxīn's posthumous completion. The single most original eighteenth-century Chinese mathematical work.
- *Gōugǔ gēyuán jì* 勾股割圓記 [[KR3fc038]] and the four-work compendium *Zhǔnwàng jiǎnfǎ / Gēyuán húshǐ bǔlùn / Gōugǔ gēyuán quányì tú / Fāngyuán bǐlì shùbiǎo* [[KR3fc039]] — [[戴震]] Dài Zhèn's mathematical writings; Dài is also the figure who in the *Sìkù* project (concurrent with these) recovered the *Suànjīng shíshū* from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* and so re-established the Hàn-Táng mathematical tradition as a primary text-base.
- *Tóngdù jì* 同度記 [[KR3fc040]] — [[孔繼涵]] Kǒng Jìhán's treatise; Kǒng Jìhán was Dài Zhèn's son-in-law and the publisher of the foundational *Suànjīng shíshū* edition.

**The Yángzhōu–Sūzhōu school (high Qīng kǎozhèng mathematics).** The four-person Qián-Jiā high school: [[焦循]], [[汪萊]], [[李銳]], and [[張敦仁]]. The largest single cluster in the division.
- *Lǐtáng xuésuàn jì* 里堂學算記 [[KR3fc042]] and its constituent treatises *Jiājiǎn chéngchú shì* 加減乘除釋 [[KR3fc043]], *Tiānyuányī shì* 天元一釋 [[KR3fc044]], *Shìhú* 釋弧 [[KR3fc045]], *Shìlún* 釋輪 [[KR3fc046]], *Shìtuǒ* 釋橢 [[KR3fc047]], *Kāifāng tōngshì* 開方通釋 [[KR3fc048]] — [[焦循]] Jiāo Xún's expository series, with the *Tiānyuányī shì* the central document of the early-nineteenth-century recovery of Sòng-Yuán algebra.
- *Héngzhāi suànxué* 衡齋算學 [[KR3fc049]] — [[汪萊]] Wāng Lái's 1796–1813 collected treatises on indeterminate equations, the theory of polynomial roots (Wāng anticipated Descartes' rule of signs in the Chinese context), and the algebraic logic of *kāifāng*.
- *Qiúyī suànshù* 求一算術 [[KR3fc041]] — [[張敦仁]] Zhāng Dūnrén's full restatement of the *Dàyǎn qiúyī shù* (Chinese Remainder Theorem) procedure of Qín Jiǔsháo.
- The Lǐ Ruì calendar-and-equation series [[KR3fc050]]–[[KR3fc060]] — [[李銳]] Lǐ Ruì's eleven treatises, the most prolific output of any single author in the division. The six "Hàn / Sòng calendrical reconstructions" — *Zhàogào rìmíng kǎo* [[KR3fc050]], *Hàn Sāntǒng shù* [[KR3fc051]], *Hàn Sìfēn shù* [[KR3fc052]], *Hàn Qiánxiàng shù* [[KR3fc053]], *Bǔxiū Sòng Fèngyuán shù* [[KR3fc054]], *Bǔxiū Sòng Zhàntiān shù* [[KR3fc055]] — return the ancient calendrical algorithms to fully computable form from the bare descriptions in the Standard-History *lìzhì* 曆志; the parallel mathematical works *Rìfǎ shuòyú qiángruò kǎo* [[KR3fc056]], *Fāngchéng xīnshù cǎo* [[KR3fc057]], *Gōugǔ suànshù xìcǎo* [[KR3fc058]], *Húshǐ suànshù xìcǎo* [[KR3fc059]], and the foundational *Kāifāng shuō* 開方說 [[KR3fc060]] (the high-water mark of pre-modern Chinese equation theory, treating the multiplicity of polynomial roots and the case-by-case behavior of higher-degree equations).

**Late-Qīng infinite-series and approach to Westernization.** The mid-to-late nineteenth-century tradition, where indigenous *gēyuán* 割圓 (cyclotomic) series and *gōugǔ* methods, the late-Míng *jiègēnfāng* 借根方 (algebraic-equation) absorption of European symbolic algebra, and the imminent full Western synthesis run in parallel.
- *Rújī yǐnméng* 如積引蒙 [[KR3fc061]] — [[汪曰楨]] Wāng Yuēzhēn's introduction to the *rújī* algebraic-equation method.
- [[劉衡]] Liú Héng's six-work *Liùjiǔ xuān* compendium [[KR3fc062]]–[[KR3fc067]] together with the *Yìyóu lù* 藝游錄 [[KR3fc080]] of [[駱騰鳳]] Luò Téngfèng — early-to-mid-nineteenth-century practitioners' digests covering surveying, root extraction, *jiègēnfāng*, and the four-term proportion.
- *Gēyuán liánbǐlì shù tújiě* 割圈連比例術圖解 [[KR3fc068]] — [[董佑誠]] Dǒng Yòuchéng's illustrated treatment of the cyclotomic continued-proportion (infinite-series) method.
- *Xiàngshù yīyuán* 象數一原 [[KR3fc069]], *Gōugǔ liùshù* [[KR3fc070]], *Sānjiǎo héjiào shù* [[KR3fc071]], *Kāi zhūchéngfāng jiéshù* [[KR3fc072]] — [[項名達]] Xiàng Míngdá's mathematical work, with [[戴煦]] Dài Xù's *Qiúbiǎo jiéshù* 求表捷術 [[KR3fc073]]: together the principal Chinese statement, on the eve of full Westernization, of an indigenous calculus of infinite series (sine, cosine, arctan, logarithm expansions, all derived inside the cyclotomic tradition).
- *Bǐsuàn shuōlüè* 筆算說略 [[KR3fc074]] — [[鄭復光]] Zhèng Fùguāng's exposition of European brush-and-pen notational arithmetic, the first dedicated Chinese textbook of Western algorithmic notation.
- *Cèyuán mìlǜ* 測圓密率 [[KR3fc075]] — [[徐有壬]] Xú Yǒurén's precision-ratio circle methods.
- *Zhìqū shù / Zhìqū tújiě* 致曲術/致曲圖解 [[KR3fc076]] — [[夏鸞翔]] Xià Luánxiáng's late treatise on curves.
- *Zégǔxī zhāi suànxué shísān zhǒng* 則古昔齋算學十三種 [[KR3fc078]] (1845–67) — [[李善蘭]] Lǐ Shànlán's collected thirteen treatises, the climax of indigenous-tradition Chinese mathematics: the *Duǒjī bǐlèi* 垛積比類 (finite differences), the *Fāngyuán chǎn yōu* 方圓闡幽 (cyclotomic series), and *Húshǐ qǐ mì* 弧矢啓秘 (arc-sagitta series) all anticipate, in their own *gēyuán* idiom, the central results of European real analysis. Lǐ's subsequent translation work at the Mòhǎi Shūguǎn (Newton, De Morgan, Loomis) marks the boundary at which the indigenous mathematical tradition becomes the modern Chinese mathematical tradition.
- *Qiúyī shù tōngjiě* 求一術通解 [[KR3fc079]] — [[黃遵憲]] Huáng Zūnxiàn's late-nineteenth-century general-exposition rewrite of the *Dàyǎn qiúyī shù*, the work with which the long Qīng commentarial cycle on Qín Jiǔsháo's Chinese Remainder Theorem effectively closes.

## Important persons

The persons listed here are those whose careers shaped KR3fc specifically; many figure also in [[KR3fa]] (asterism / observational astronomy) and [[KR3fb]] (calendrical method) with distinct roles there.

- [[劉徽]] — Wèi-Jìn (mid-third-century) mathematician; the *Jiǔzhāng* commentator whose *Jǐngyuán* 4 (263) preface and exegesis is the foundational document of Chinese mathematical reasoning and the chief witness to the inferential-justificatory style of Hàn-Wèi mathematics ([[KR3fc001]]; also at [[KR3f0032]]).
- [[李淳風]] — Táng (602–670); director of the *Tàishǐjú* 太史局, editor of the *Suànjīng shíshū* and author of the *zhùshì* sub-commentary attached to the *Jiǔzhāng*, *Zhāng Qiūjiàn*, *Jígǔ*, and other classics ([[KR3fc001]], [[KR3fc004]]).
- [[張邱建]] — Northern-Wèi (c. fifth century); compiler of the *Zhāng Qiūjiàn suànjīng* ([[KR3fc004]]), the principal early Chinese text on indeterminate problems.
- [[秦九韶]] — Sòng (1208–1268); author of *Shùshū jiǔzhāng* (1247), the first complete algorithmic statement of the Chinese Remainder Theorem ([[KR3fc008]]; also at [[KR3f0041]]).
- [[楊輝]] — Sòng (fl. 1261–1275); the principal redactor of Liú Yì's lost polynomial-equation work and the transmitter of Jiǎ Xiàn's binomial-coefficient method ([[KR3fc010]]–[[KR3fc011]], [[KR3fc021]]).
- [[李冶]] — Jīn-Yuán (1192–1279); systematizer of the *tiānyuányī* method via the *Cèyuán hǎijìng* ([[KR3fc015]]; also at [[KR3f0042]]–[[KR3f0044]]).
- [[朱世傑]] — Yuán (c. 1249–1314); author of the *Suànxué qǐméng* (1299) and *Sìyuán yùjiàn* (1303), the peak of pre-modern Chinese algebra ([[KR3fc016]]–[[KR3fc020]]).
- [[吳敬]] — Míng (fl. mid-fifteenth century); compiler of the *Jiǔzhāng xiángzhù bǐlèi suànfǎ dàquán* (1450), the bridge from Yuán algebra to the Míng popular tradition ([[KR3fc023]]).
- [[程大位]] — Míng (1533–1606); author of the *Suànfǎ tǒngzōng* (1592), the abacus-computation textbook that defined late-Míng and early-Qīng practical mathematics ([[KR3fc027]]; principal entry at [[KR3f0045]]).
- [[徐光啟]] — Míng (1562–1633); the principal Chinese collaborator with Matteo Ricci; co-translator of the *Jǐhé yuánběn* 幾何原本 and architect of the *Chóngzhēn lìshū* reform ([[KR3fc026]], [[KR3fc029]]; principal entries at [[KR3f0047]] and [[KR3f0013]]).
- [[陳藎謨]] — Míng (early seventeenth century); leading transmitter of the proportional-compass / Napier's-rods computational apparatus ([[KR3fc030]]–[[KR3fc033]]).
- [[朱載堉]] — Míng (1536–1611); prince-mathematician of the Zhèngfǔ 鄭府 cadet branch, author of the *Jiāliàng suànjīng* applied-measurement treatise ([[KR3fc028]]).
- [[梅文鼎]] — Qīng (1633–1721); the dominant figure of the early-Qīng synthesis ([[KR3fc035]]–[[KR3fc036]]; principal entries at [[KR3f0026]]–[[KR3f0028]]).
- [[安圖]] — Qīng (Minggatu, c. 1692–1763); the Mongol *Qīntiānjiān* mathematician; the *Gēyuán mìlǜ jiéfǎ* ([[KR3fc037]]) is the most original eighteenth-century Chinese mathematical work, deriving sin/cos/arc-functions in power-series form.
- [[戴震]] — Qīng (1724–1777); chief recover of the *Suànjīng shíshū* from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* in the *Sìkù* project; author of the *Gōugǔ gēyuán jì* and its companions ([[KR3fc038]]–[[KR3fc039]]). The historical pivot of the entire Qīng *kǎozhèng* mathematics programme.
- [[孔繼涵]] — Qīng (1739–1783); Dài Zhèn's son-in-law and publisher of the *Suànjīng shíshū* ([[KR3fc040]]).
- [[李潢]] — Qīng (?–1812); author of the standard *xìcǎo túshuō* reading editions of the *Jiǔzhāng*, *Hǎidǎo*, and *Jígǔ* ([[KR3fc002]]–[[KR3fc003]], [[KR3fc005]]).
- [[沈欽裴]] — Qīng (fl. early nineteenth century); Lǐ Huáng's editorial executor and the redactor of [[KR3fc019]] *Sìyuán yùjiàn xìcǎo*.
- [[張敦仁]] — Qīng (1754–1834); author of *Jígǔ suànjīng xìcǎo* [[KR3fc006]] and the *Qiúyī suànshù* restatement of Qín Jiǔsháo [[KR3fc041]].
- [[焦循]] — Qīng (1763–1820); the Lǐtáng author whose *Tiānyuányī shì* recovered Sòng-Yuán algebra for the nineteenth century ([[KR3fc042]]–[[KR3fc048]]).
- [[汪萊]] — Qīng (1768–1813); the *Héngzhāi suànxué* theorist of polynomial roots and indeterminate equations ([[KR3fc049]]).
- [[李銳]] — Qīng (1769–1817); the most prolific mathematician of the high-Qīng school, eleven treatises in the present division ([[KR3fc050]]–[[KR3fc060]]); reconstructor of the lost Hàn-Sòng calendrical algorithms.
- [[羅士琳]] — Qīng (1789–1853); the Yángzhōu editor of Zhū Shìjié's *Sìyuán yùjiàn* and *Suànxué qǐméng* ([[KR3fc018]], [[KR3fc020]]); the figure through whom Sòng-Yuán algebra is most directly transmitted to the late-Qīng generation.

- [[劉衡]] — Qīng (1776–1841); the Húběi practitioner whose seven treatises [[KR3fc007]] and [[KR3fc062]]–[[KR3fc067]] are the most prolific late-Qīng applied-mathematics output in the division.

## Topics

The principal subjects, debates, and interpretive problems running through KR3fc:

- **The Sòng-Yuán algebraic peak and its disappearance.** The *tiānyuányī* method of [[李冶]] Lǐ Yě, the *sìyuán shù* (four-unknown method) of [[朱世傑]] Zhū Shìjié, and the *Dàyǎn qiúyī shù* (Chinese Remainder Theorem) of [[秦九韶]] Qín Jiǔsháo were already incomprehensible to mainstream Míng mathematicians by 1500 (the most famous evidence: [[顧應祥]] Gù Yīngxiáng's openly puzzled *Cèyuán hǎijìng* restatement [[KR3f0043]], which simply omits the rod-algebra of Lǐ Yě's procedures). The question *how a tradition this sophisticated was forgotten within two generations of the Yuán-Míng transition* is the central problem of Chinese mathematical historiography, and the Qīng *kǎozhèng* programme — culminating in [[羅士琳]] Luō Shìlín's reconstructions and [[焦循]] Jiāo Xún's *Tiānyuányī shì* — is the long answer.

- **The Suànjīng shíshū recovery.** [[戴震]] Dài Zhèn's recovery of eight of the Ten from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* during the *Sìkù* project, [[孔繼涵]] Kǒng Jìhán's foundational edition, and [[李潢]] Lǐ Huáng's *xìcǎo túshuō* reading editions ([[KR3fc002]]–[[KR3fc005]]) together constitute the textual-philological foundation of all modern study of pre-Sòng Chinese mathematics.

- **Gōugǔ 勾股 — the right triangle as organizing concept.** From the *Zhōubì* through the *Jiǔzhāng* chapter 9, [[徐光啟]]'s *Gōugǔ yì* [[KR3fc026]] (the Euclidean re-presentation), Méi Wéndǐng's Anhui-school treatments, Dài Zhèn's *Gōugǔ gēyuán jì* [[KR3fc038]], the cluster of "gōugǔ" titles in the Liú Héng and Xiàng Míngdá series [[KR3fc058]], [[KR3fc064]], [[KR3fc070]], and Lǐ Shànlán's [[KR3fc078]] — the right triangle is the single most recurrent figure of Chinese mathematical exposition across two thousand years.

- **Kāifāng 開方 — root extraction and the theory of equations.** The Hàn-Wèi square-and-cube-root algorithms in [[KR3fc001]], the Sòng generalization to *n*-th roots in [[楊輝]]'s *Xiángjiě* [[KR3fc011]] preserving Jiǎ Xiàn's binomial-coefficient triangle, the Qín-Jiǔsháo Horner-style polynomial evaluation in [[KR3fc008]], the Míng didactic reductions in [[KR3fc032]]–[[KR3fc033]] and Méi Wéndǐng's *Píngfāng* tradition, [[焦循]]'s *Kāifāng tōngshì* [[KR3fc048]], and culminating in [[李銳]]'s *Kāifāng shuō* [[KR3fc060]] — the most sophisticated pre-modern Chinese treatment of the theory of equations, including a systematic analysis of multiple roots.

- **The Dàyǎn qiúyī shù 大衍求一術 (Chinese Remainder Theorem).** From the *Sūnzǐ suànjīng* problem 26 ([[KR3f0033]]) through Qín Jiǔsháo's full algorithm [[KR3fc008]], [[張敦仁]]'s *Qiúyī suànshù* [[KR3fc041]] modernization, and [[黃遵憲]]'s late-Qīng *Qiúyī shù tōngjiě* [[KR3fc079]] — the principal Chinese contribution to number theory.

- **Cyclotomic / infinite-series methods (gēyuánshù 割圓術).** The medieval origin in Liú Huī's circle-cutting computation of π in [[KR3fc001]], the eighteenth-century *Gēyuán mìlǜ jiéfǎ* power-series of [[安圖]] Āntú / Minggatu [[KR3fc037]], the *Gōugǔ gēyuán jì* of [[戴震]] [[KR3fc038]], the [[項名達]] / [[戴煦]] series [[KR3fc069]]–[[KR3fc073]], and the *Fāngyuán chǎn yōu* in Lǐ Shànlán's *Zégǔxī zhāi* [[KR3fc078]] — the indigenous Chinese form of analytic-series computation, developed largely independently of European calculus from the 1730s through the 1860s.

- **The Jesuit encounter and the jiègēnfāng 借根方.** The translation of European algebraic notation into Chinese as the *jiègēnfāng* ("borrowed-root method") in the *Shùlǐ jīngyùn* ([[KR3f0048]]) and the subsequent reckoning, by [[焦循]] in [[KR3fc044]] and successors, of the relationship between *jiègēnfāng* (European) and *tiānyuányī* (Chinese). Settling this equivalence is the conceptual hinge between indigenous and Westernized Chinese mathematics; the late-Qīng Liú Héng didactic series [[KR3fc066]]–[[KR3fc067]] and Wāng Yuēzhēn's [[KR3fc061]] are practical fruits of the equivalence.

- **The kǎozhèng reconstruction of ancient calendrical algorithms.** The series of [[李銳]] Lǐ Ruì reconstructions [[KR3fc051]]–[[KR3fc055]] — *Sāntǒng*, *Sìfēn*, *Qiánxiàng*, *Fèngyuán*, *Zhàntiān* — converts the bare descriptions in the Standard-History *lìzhì* into running computational procedures, recovering the technical content of Hàn-Sòng mathematical astronomy through pure textual scholarship. The parallel project on the calendrical side is in [[KR3fb]].

- **The transition to modern mathematics.** [[鄭復光]] Zhèng Fùguāng's *Bǐsuàn shuōlüè* [[KR3fc074]] (Western notational arithmetic), [[李善蘭]] Lǐ Shànlán's *Zégǔxī zhāi* [[KR3fc078]] as the closing monument of indigenous mathematics on the eve of Lǐ's Mòhǎi Shūguǎn translations of Newton and De Morgan — these works trace the actual moment of transition. The division ends, chronologically, at the boundary of the modern Chinese mathematical tradition.
