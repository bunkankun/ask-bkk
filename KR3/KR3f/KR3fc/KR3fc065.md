# Chóubiǎo kāi zhūchéngfāng jiéfǎ 籌表開諸乘方捷法
 *A Rapid Tabular Method for the Extraction of n-th Roots*
by [[劉衡]] (撰)

## About the work

[[劉衡]] Liú Héng's (1776–1841) treatise in 4 *juàn* on a tabular *jiéfǎ* 捷法 ("rapid method") for the extraction of arbitrary *n*-th roots (*zhūchéngfāng* 諸乘方, literally "various multiplications-of-squares," the indigenous Chinese label for higher-degree powers). The method uses precomputed *chóubiǎo* 籌表 ("rod-tables") — printed tables of selected powers — to reduce the root-extraction procedure to a sequence of table look-ups.

### Abstract

The classical Chinese root-extraction procedure is the *zēngchéng kāifāng* 增乘開方 algorithm of the Sòng-Yuán tradition — the Horner-style synthetic-division method developed by [[賈憲]] Jiǎ Xiàn and [[秦九韶]] Qín Jiǔsháo and preserved in the *Shùshū jiǔzhāng* ([[KR3fc008]]). The procedure is fully general (it works for any-degree polynomial root extraction) but computationally laborious: each digit of the root requires a full pass through the coefficient sequence.

Liú Héng's *Chóubiǎo jiéfǎ* aims to reduce this labour for the special case of pure *n*-th root extraction (*x*ⁿ = *N* for given *N* and *n*). The procedure precomputes a table of *n*-th powers of carefully chosen numbers and reduces the extraction to: (1) bracketing *N* between consecutive tabulated powers, (2) interpolating to obtain the leading digits of the root, (3) applying a correction procedure for the remaining digits. The 4-*juàn* presentation lays out the tables, the procedure, and an extensive collection of worked examples covering *n* from 2 (ordinary square roots) up to *n* = 10 or thereabouts.

The work prefigures the late-Qīng / Tóngzhì interest in computational tabulation that would culminate in [[戴煦]] Dài Xù's [[KR3fc073]] *Qiúbiǎo jiéshù* 求表捷術 (rapid methods for table-construction, especially trigonometric and logarithmic tables) and [[李善蘭]] Lǐ Shànlán's translation work on logarithms. Liú Héng's tabular root-extraction is the indigenous-tradition precursor of these mid-nineteenth-century tabular projects.

Dating: Liú Héng lived 1776–1841. *notBefore* 1800; *notAfter* 1841.
