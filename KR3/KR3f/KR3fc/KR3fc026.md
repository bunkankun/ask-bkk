# Gōugǔ yì 勾股義
 *Meaning of the Right Triangle*
by [[徐光啟]] (撰)

## About the work

A brief late-Míng treatise on the right triangle in 1 *juàn* by [[徐光啟]] Xú Guāngqǐ (1562–1633), composed in the immediate aftermath of his collaboration with Matteo Ricci on the partial translation of Euclid's *Elements* as *Jǐhé yuánběn* 幾何原本 (1607). The catalog records "extent: 1562-1633" — interpretable as Xú Guāngqǐ's lifedates being inadvertently entered as the *extent* field. CBDB id 30598 confirms the dates.

### Abstract

The *Gōugǔ yì* belongs to Xú Guāngqǐ's project of placing the indigenous Chinese mathematical tradition into dialogue with the newly-translated Euclidean geometry. Where [[顧應祥]] Gù Yìngxiáng's [[KR3fc025]] *Gōugǔ suànshù* had treated the right triangle as a stand-alone procedural-mathematical topic to be worked through problem-by-problem, Xú Guāngqǐ's *Gōugǔ yì* — composed within months of the *Jǐhé yuánběn*'s publication — treats the right triangle as a topic to be *demonstrated* (the *yì* 義 of the title — "meaning" or "principle" — corresponds to the demonstrative-deductive Euclidean *λόγος* / *ratio*).

The work systematizes the metric relations of the right triangle by deriving them from Euclidean propositions translated in the *Jǐhé yuánběn* (notably the propositions of Books I and VI on similar triangles and the Pythagorean theorem itself, Book I proposition 47). Xú Guāngqǐ presents fifteen propositions on the right triangle, each accompanied by a Euclidean-style demonstration referring back to the *Jǐhé yuánběn* by proposition-number. The result is the first systematic application of the deductive-demonstrative method to the indigenous Chinese *gōugǔ* tradition — an explicit programmatic intervention in the indigenous tradition by the methodology of the newly-imported Euclidean geometry.

The work is one of the most consequential mathematical documents of the late Míng. It establishes a methodological pattern — deriving indigenous results from Euclidean foundations — that would shape Chinese mathematical writing through the QiánJiā era ([[梅文鼎]] Méi Wéndǐng's [[KR3f0049]] *Gōugǔ jǔyú* and Lǐ Shànlán's mid-19th-century mathematical synthesis are direct descendants of Xú Guāngqǐ's approach). The work also exemplifies the *bǔrú* 補儒 ("supplementing Confucianism") rhetoric of late-Míng Jesuit-collaborator scholars: where Gù Yìngxiáng's *Cèyuán hǎijìng* exposition had been forced to omit the lost *tiānyuányī* method that gave the work its substantive coherence, Xú Guāngqǐ's *Gōugǔ yì* offers Euclidean demonstration as a substitute for the lost indigenous foundation.

Dating: the work is conventionally dated 1606–1608 in the immediate aftermath of the *Jǐhé yuánběn* (1607) — *notBefore* 1606, allowing for early-stage drafting concurrent with the *Jǐhé* translation; *notAfter* 1608, by which time the work was in circulation.
