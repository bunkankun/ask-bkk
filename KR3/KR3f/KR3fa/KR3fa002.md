# Gǔ Zhōubì suànjīng 古周髀算經
 *The Ancient Gnomon-Classic of the Zhou*
by [[趙爽]] (注)

## About the work

The *Gǔ Zhōubì suànjīng* 古周髀算經 is the catalog's name for the long, expanded reception of the *Zhōubì suànjīng* 周髀算經 ([[KR3f0001]]) — the foundational Hàn-era text on the *gāitiān* 蓋天 ("canopied-sky") cosmology and the gnomon-and-shadow geometry that underpinned the early Chinese state's astronomical practice. The label *Gǔ* 古 ("ancient") distinguishes this five-*juǎn* received compound — the main classic *plus* the Hòu Hàn *zhù* 注 of Zhào Shuǎng 趙爽, the *yīntú* 隂圖 ("dim diagrams") that Zhào appended, and additional later commentary layers — from the two-*juàn* core text alone ([[KR3f0001]]). The five-juan figure matches the Sòng-imperial reprint and the *Sìkù quánshū* listing, which presents the same composite: the *jīng* 經 plus Zhào's *zhù* plus the Bèi Yīn 甄鸞 (sic, 6th-c.) and the Táng [[李淳風]] commentary.

### Abstract

The *Zhōubì* is one of the most-studied and most-debated texts in the early Chinese scientific corpus. Cullen (1996) has shown convincingly that the layered text known today coalesced in the first centuries BCE / CE around an older nucleus of practical gnomonic procedures attached to a Western-Hàn cosmographical thought-experiment in which the heavens are a parallel disc above a parallel flat earth, both 81,000 *lǐ* across and 80,000 *lǐ* apart — the *gāitiān* model. Wilkinson summarises the position in his discussion of *gāitiān*: "The main source for *gaitian* theory is the *Zhoubi suanjing*" (Wilkinson, *Chinese History: A New Manual*, §gaitian). The text's most-cited content is the Pythagorean-style demonstration (*gōugǔ* 句股) attributed to Shāng Gāo 商高 in dialogue with the Duke of Zhōu 周公, which gives the right-triangle relation *gōu²+gǔ²=xián²* with the canonical (3,4,5) numerical example.

The catalog meta dates the work to the Hàn dynasty; the actual composition window of the received recension extends from the late Western Hàn through the Hòu Hàn into the Three-Kingdoms period when [[趙爽]] Zhào Shuǎng of Wú wrote the *zhù* and the *Gōugǔ yuánfāng túshuō* 句股圓方圖說 essay that is normally appended. The bracket 100 BCE – 250 CE adopted here covers the nucleus through Zhào Shuǎng. The further Northern-Zhōu glosses by 甄鸞 Zhēn Luán (6th c.) and the Táng-court commentary by [[李淳風]] (656) are textual accretions, not part of the *Gǔ Zhōubì* proper. The five-*juǎn* division of the modern catalog reflects the Sòng-Yuán transmission and Bào Huànzhī 鮑澣之's 1213 printing. Duan Yucai 段玉裁 in the Qīng proposed adding the *Zhōubì* to the Confucian *Classics*, recognising the cultural status of the work (Wilkinson §1.1).

The *Zhōubì* was lost in independent transmission and recovered for the *Sìkù quánshū* by Dài Zhèn 戴震 from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* 永樂大典; the catalog's separate *Zhōubì suànjīng jiàokān jì* [[KR3fa003]] records Dài's collation work. The relation between the early *gāitiān* of the *Zhōubì* and the later *hùntiān* 渾天 ("encircled-sky" / armillary) cosmology displaced it as the orthodox model after the 1st c. CE is discussed at length in Cullen's monograph.

## Other points of interest

The *Zhōubì*'s *gāitiān* model is one of the very few cases in early Chinese science where a quantitative cosmological theory survives with its underlying gnomonic measurements and a geometrical demonstration of its key relation. The pedagogical dialogue between Duke of Zhōu and Shāng Gāo on the *gōugǔ* relation is one of the most-quoted passages in the entire mathematical canon. Joseph Needham was repeatedly drawn to the question of whether this constitutes an "independent" Chinese Pythagorean theorem; Cullen has provided the most careful modern treatment.
