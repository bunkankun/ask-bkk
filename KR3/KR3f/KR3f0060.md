# Xú Zhěng chánglì 徐整長曆
*Xú Zhěng's Long Chronology*
by [[徐整]] (Xú Zhěng, Sūn-Wú 孫吳 court historian, mid-3rd century, 三國吳, *zhuàn* 撰)

## About the work

A cosmogonic-and-chronological fragment compilation transmitted under Xú Zhěng's name, surviving only as quotations in the Táng–Sòng *lèishū* (encyclopaedias). The text is sister to his more famous *Sānwǔ lìjì* 三五歷紀, and on the surviving evidence the two works overlap substantially — both contain versions of the Pángǔ 盤古 cosmogony, both work outward from cosmogony into a primordial chronology of the Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors. The Kanripo edition is a modern (probably Qing) one-juan reconstruction from leishu fragments under the *Chánglì* title.

### Abstract

Composition window: c. 230–260, during Xú Zhěng's mature service at the Sūn-Wú 孫吳 court of Sūn Quán 孫權 (r. 222–252) and his successors. The exact composition date is not preserved. The *Suí shū jīngjí zhì* lists the *Chánglì* as a 1-*juàn* work distinct from the 2-*juàn* *Sānwǔ lìjì*, but the close substantive overlap in the surviving fragments suggests they may be sibling redactions or excerpts of a single larger Xú-Zhěng cosmological-historical compilation.

The surviving fragments collected in this Kanripo reconstruction include:

(1) **Cosmographic-astronomical measurements**: the sun has a diameter of 1,000 *lǐ* 里 with a circumference of 3,000 *lǐ* and a height of 7,000 *lǐ* below the heaven; the moon similarly 1,000 *lǐ* in diameter, 3,000 *lǐ* in circumference, 10,000 *lǐ* below the heaven; the great stars have a diameter of 100 *lǐ*, medium 50, small 30; the seven stars of the Northern Dipper (北斗) are separated by 9,000 *lǐ*, all of them positioned below the sun and moon. These dimensions, retained from the *gàitiān* 蓋天 ("umbrella heaven") tradition codified in the *Zhōubì suànjīng* [[KR3f0001]] / [[KR3fa002]], anchor Xú Zhěng's cosmography in the earlier orthodox tradition rather than the *húntiān* model.

(2) **Cosmological etymology**: stars are *"yuánqì zhī yīng, shuǐ zhī jīng yě"* 元氣之英，水之精也 ("the flower of primordial *qì* and the essence of water"). The Dipper, anchored at Mount Kūnlún 崑崙, "channels *qì* downward to the world," producing rain and dew in spring-and-summer, frost and snow in autumn-and-winter.

(3) **The Pángǔ cosmogony** (the most famous fragment, in close-to-identical wording to the *Sānwǔ lìjì* version preserved in Lǐ Fǎng's *Tàipíng yùlǎn* *juàn* 2 and Ouyang Xún's 歐陽詢 *Yìwén lèijù* *juàn* 1): the primordial chaos resembling a chicken's egg, the gestation of Pángǔ for eighteen thousand years, the separation of *yángqīng* 陽清 (clear *yang*) to form the sky and *yīnzhuó* 陰濁 (heavy *yin*) to form the earth, the simultaneous one-*zhàng*-per-day growth of sky, earth, and Pángǔ. The fragment closes with the cosmological gloss *"shù qǐ yú yī, lì yú sān, chéng yú yī, shèng yú qī, chù yú jiǔ, gù qù dì jiǔwàn lǐ"* 數起於一，立於三，成於一，盛於七，處於九，故去地九萬里 — number arises from one, is established at three, completed at one [reading variant 五], reaches its plenum at seven, comes to rest at nine; hence the distance of the heaven from the earth is ninety thousand *lǐ*.

(4) **The Yellow-Thearch golden-age gloss**: *"Huángdì shí fēng bù míng tiáo, yǔ bù pò kuài yě"* 黃帝時風不鳴條，雨不破塊也 ("in the time of the Yellow Thearch the wind did not whistle through the branches and the rain did not break the clods") — the formulaic *zhìshì* 治世 (well-governed age) trope, repeatedly recycled in later Hàn–Six Dynasties literature.

(5) **The pre-history of the Three Sovereigns**: the *Tiānhuáng* 天皇 ("Heavenly Sovereign") and the unfolding of the cosmic-political timeline from the cosmogenetic moment through to the historicised Three-Sovereigns / Five-Emperors sequence. The phrase *"míngxìng shǐ yá, ménghóng zī méng, suì qǐ shètí, yuánqì zhào qǐ"* 溟涬始牙，濛鴻滋萌，歲起攝提，元氣肇啟 ("the primordial vapours begin to bud, the dense mists nourish the sprout, the year begins at Shètí [the *jiǎyín* 甲寅 epoch], primordial *qì* is just opening") is one of the canonical early-medieval Chinese cosmogonic incipits.

Xú Zhěng's work is foundational for the Chinese cosmogonic mythological canon: prior to him the Pángǔ figure is not attested in any surviving text, and after him every later *huìtú* (illustrated compendium), every *tōngjiàn xùbiān* (continuation of the *Comprehensive Mirror*) reaching back to mythological time, and every Qing-period *Lìdài jìyuán* (Era-name chronology) treatise begins its cosmic-historical narrative with the Pángǔ creation. Endymion Wilkinson's *Chinese History: A New Manual* (§56.4) accordingly identifies Pángǔ as the conventional first figure of the Chinese mythological time-stream, and notes that the Yigǔpài 疑古派 (Gu Jiegang 顧頡剛 and others) made the historicity of Pángǔ one of their primary targets of skepticism (Wilkinson §56.6).

The Kanripo placement of this work under *Zǐbù tiānwén suànfǎ* — rather than under the historical or mythological divisions — reflects the work's cosmographic-and-chronological orientation: it is a *chánglì* 長曆 ("long chronology"), an astronomically-grounded chronology of the deep mythological past.

## Other points of interest

The likely provenance of the Pángǔ cosmogony from Indo-Buddhist contact has been argued since the early-20th-century work of Lǐ Sì-chún 李思純 and reiterated by Yuán Kē — the formal closeness to the *Puruṣa-sūkta* of *Ṛg-veda* X.90 and to the Bodhi-tree-as-cosmic-pillar imagery of the Mahāyāna cosmographic literature is striking, and Xú Zhěng's southern-Wú milieu (in which Indian-monk Kāng Sēng-huì 康僧會 was active in Yáng-zhōu in the 240s–250s) is a plausible point of entry. The hypothesis is rejected by some Chinese scholarship (which prefers an autochthonous southern-minority origin for the figure), and remains contested. The Kanripo placement of Xú Zhěng's work within the *Zǐ-bù tiānwén* division — rather than within Daoist or Buddhist divisions — is editorially neutral on this provenance question.
