# KR3j — Zájiā lèi 雜家類 (Masters → Miscellaneous)

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3j is the *Zájiā lèi* 雜家類 of the *Sìkù quánshū* — the residual, syncretist, and notebook-form division of the Masters branch (子部, *Zǐbù*). The category has two roots. The first is the Hàn bibliographic class *Zájiā* 雜家 (rendered by Wilkinson as "Syncretists" in his table of the *Hànshū* *Yìwén zhì*'s ten Masters subclasses; cf. Wilkinson § 73.2 and Box 348), originally containing twenty works in which Confucian, Daoist, Mohist, Legalist, agriculturalist, and yīnyáng / Five-Phase teachings were deliberately combined — the *Lǚshì chūnqiū* [[KR3j0009]] and the *Huáinánzǐ* [[KR3j0010]] being the paradigmatic examples. The second root is the *bǐjì* 筆記 ("brush notes") of the post-Táng period, a genre Wilkinson devotes a full chapter to (§ 53): informal scholarly notebooks whose contents range across classical exegesis, history, geography, philology, anecdote, and connoisseurship, and which the *Sìkù* editors found impossible to file under any of the more specialised Masters subclasses. The *Sìkù* therefore enlarged the old Hàn *Zájiā* into a six-fold class — *zá xué* 雜學 (syncretist philosophy), *zá kǎo* 雜考 (evidential investigation), *zá shuō* 雜說 (miscellaneous discourse), *zá pǐn* 雜品 (connoisseurship), *zá zuǎn* 雜纂 (anthological compilation), *zá biān* 雜編 (multi-work compendia) — and used it to absorb the bulk of the *bǐjì* corpus. As Wilkinson notes (§ 53.1), *bǐjì* "often started as reading notes" and ranged across "scholastic points of history or philology … the flux of city life … local customs and practical guidebook-style advice"; their classification in the *Sìkù* was therefore split between *Zájiā* in the Masters branch and *záshǐ* 雜史 in the History branch, with *Zájiā* receiving the more reflective and philological writings while *záshǐ* received the more event-driven ones. Until the late nineteenth century, *cóngshū* 叢書 (multi-work compendia) had no separate place in the four-branch scheme and were filed inside *Zájiā*'s sixth subclass *zá biān* (Wilkinson § 73.2.3); the four KR3j *cóngshū* on disk are survivors of that older arrangement.

The 201 works catalogued in KR3j cover an unbroken span from the late Warring States to the early eighteenth century — *Mòzǐ* [[KR3j0002]], the *Hèguānzǐ* [[KR3j0006]], the *Gōngsūn Lóngzǐ* [[KR3j0007]] and other survivors of the "Hundred Schools" at one end; the great Qīng *kǎozhèng* notebooks of [[顧炎武]], [[閻若璩]], [[何焯]], and [[王士禛]] at the other. KR3j is therefore not a single tradition but a holding-tank for several: the Hàn syncretist *zǐ*-book, the Six-Dynasties character-and-mores literature ([[KR3j0011]] *Rénwù zhì*, [[KR3j0014]] *Yánshì jiāxùn*), the Sòng evidential *bǐjì* (the works of [[洪邁]], [[王應麟]], [[程大昌]]), the Sòng-Yuán-Míng connoisseurship treatises ([[KR3j0167]] *Dòngtiān qīnglù*, [[KR3j0170]] *Gégǔ yàolùn*, [[KR3j0174]] *Cháng wù zhì*), the late-Míng polymathic miscellanies ([[KR3j0066]] *Tōngyǎ*, [[KR3j0190]] *Shǎoshì shānfáng bǐcóng*, [[KR3j0160]] *Wùlǐ xiǎozhì*), and finally the Qīng evidential apex anchored by [[KR3j0070]] *Rì zhī lù*. Across this span the division acts as the *Sìkù*'s reservoir for serious intellectual work that did not fit the Masters branch's narrower philosophical, technical, or arts categories.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The pre-Qín masters cluster.** Six short books at the head of the division preserve the names — and contested texts — of pre-Qín thinkers who fell outside the four orthodox schools, and whose surviving recensions are mostly Hàn–Sòng reconstitutions: [[KR3j0001]] *Yùzǐ* 鬻子 (attributed to the Zhōu-era [[鬻熊]] but transmitted with a Táng commentary by [[逢行珪]]), [[KR3j0002]] *Mòzǐ* 墨子 (the principal surviving Mohist scripture), [[KR3j0004]] *Yǐnwénzǐ* 尹文子 (the *Míngjiā* 名家 / School of Names), [[KR3j0005]] *Shènzǐ* 慎子 (proto-Legalist), [[KR3j0006]] *Hèguānzǐ* 鶡冠子 (a Huáng Lǎo syncretist), [[KR3j0007]] *Gōngsūn Lóngzǐ* 公孫龍子 (the second of the two surviving *Míngjiā* texts), [[KR3j0008]] *Guǐgǔzǐ* 鬼谷子 (the *Zòngheng jiā* 縱橫家 / Strategists' manual), and [[KR3j0194]] *Shīzǐ* 尸子 (a Qīng *jíyì* reconstitution). The *Sìkù* editors filed them in *Zájiā* rather than in *Rújiā* or *Dàojiā* precisely because their schools had become extinct.

**The Hàn-syncretist hinge.** [[KR3j0009]] *Lǚshì chūnqiū* 呂氏春秋 (239 BCE) and [[KR3j0010]] *Huáinán hónglièjiě* 淮南鴻烈解 (the *Huáinánzǐ*, c. 139 BCE) are the locus classicus of Hàn-era *Zájiā* thought as Wilkinson uses the term in § 73.2 / Box 348. They self-consciously assemble Confucian, Daoist, Mohist, and yīnyáng material into a coherent imperial-state programme. [[KR3j0192]] *Xīnlùn* 新論 ([[桓譚]]), [[KR3j0080]] *Lùnhéng* 論衡 ([[王充]]), [[KR3j0023]] *Báihǔ tōngyì* 白虎通義 (attributed to [[班固]]), and [[KR3j0081]] *Fēngsú tōngyì* 風俗通義 ([[應劭]]) extend the syncretist *zǐ*-book idiom across the Eastern Hàn.

**Six Dynasties and Suí-Táng character / *jiāxùn* / governance literature.** [[KR3j0011]] *Rénwù zhì* 人物志 ([[劉卲]]), the foundation text for Wèi-Jìn character-theory and physiognomic recruitment; [[KR3j0012]] *Jīnlóuzǐ* 金樓子 (Liáng [[蕭繹]]); [[KR3j0013]] *Liúzǐ* 劉子 ([[劉晝]]); [[KR3j0014]] *Yánshì jiāxùn* 顏氏家訓 ([[顏之推]]), the most influential of all family-precepts books; [[KR3j0015]] *Cháng duǎn jīng* 長短經 (Táng [[趙蕤]], 716), a syncretist statecraft and stratagem manual; [[KR3j0017]] *Huà shū* 化書 (the Daoist-physiological treatise of [[譚峭]]).

**The Sòng evidential *bǐjì*.** The most consequential single cluster in the division. [[KR3j0038]] *Róngzhāi suíbǐ* 容齋隨筆 ([[洪邁]], begun 1162), [[KR3j0055]] *Kùnxué jìwén* 困學紀聞 ([[王應麟]], composed after 1276), [[KR3j0039]] *Kǎogǔ biān* 攷古編 and [[KR3j0040]] *Yǎnfán lù* 演繁露 (both [[程大昌]]), [[KR3j0034]] *Nénggǎizhāi mànlù* 能改齋漫錄 ([[吳曾]]), [[KR3j0037]] *Xuélín* 學林 ([[王觀國]]), [[KR3j0041]] *Wěi lüè* 緯略 ([[高似孫]]), [[KR3j0045]] *Yěkè cóngshū* 野客叢書 ([[王楙]]) and the rest of the long Sòng cluster from [[KR3j0033]] to [[KR3j0057]] collectively establish *kǎozhèng* 考證 as a recognisable scholarly mode three centuries before the Qīng apex; the Qīng editors of the *Sìkù* preserve them as the direct ancestors of [[KR3j0070]] *Rì zhī lù*.

**The polymath Sòng *bǐjì*.** Distinct from the strictly philological cluster but overlapping it. [[KR3j0092]] *Mèngxī bǐtán* 夢溪筆談 ([[沈括]], c. 1086–1095), the single most famous Chinese pre-modern technical-encyclopedic notebook (the *Bì Shēng* movable-type entry is its most-cited passage); [[KR3j0093]] *Chóuchí bǐjì* 仇池筆記 and [[KR3j0094]] *Dōngpō zhì lín* 東坡志林 ([[蘇軾]]); [[KR3j0105]] *Shílín yànyǔ* 石林燕語, [[KR3j0106]] *Bìshǔ lùhuà* 避暑錄話, [[KR3j0107]] *Yánxià fàngyán* 巖下放言 ([[葉夢得]]); [[KR3j0123]] *Lǎo xué ān bǐjì* 老學庵筆記 ([[陸游]]); [[KR3j0134]] *Qí dōng yě yǔ* 齊東野語 ([[周密]]).

**Sòng–Yuán *cóngshū*-style anthological compilations** (*zá zuǎn* and *zá biān*): [[KR3j0082]] *Qún shū zhì yào* 群書治要 (Táng [[魏徵]], 631 — the ancestor of the genre); [[KR3j0178]] *Yìlín* 意林 (Táng [[馬總]]); [[KR3j0179]] *Gàn zhū jí* 紺珠集 and [[KR3j0180]] *Lèi shuō* 類說 ([[曾慥]], 1136); [[KR3j0181]] *Shì shí lèi yuàn* 事實類苑; [[KR3j0185]] *Shuō fú* 說孚 ([[陶宗儀]]).

**The Míng polymathic peak.** [[KR3j0059]] *Dān qiān yú lù* 丹鉛餘錄 ([[楊愼]]); [[KR3j0066]] *Tōngyǎ* 通雅 ([[方以智]], c. 1642), the most systematic Míng philological compendium and the proximate ancestor of Qīng *kǎozhèng* on the linguistic side; [[KR3j0160]] *Wùlǐ xiǎozhì* 物理小識 (also [[方以智]]), the parallel natural-philosophical compendium; [[KR3j0190]] *Shǎoshì shānfáng bǐcóng* 少室山房筆叢 ([[胡應麟]], 1602), the most ambitious Míng *bǐjì*-as-cóngshū; [[KR3j0149]] *Cǎo mù zǐ* 草木子 ([[葉子奇]]).

**The Qīng evidential apex.** [[KR3j0070]] *Rì zhī lù* 日知錄 ([[顧炎武]], 1670s–82) is the single most influential intellectual work in the division and the foundation stone of Qīng *Hàn xué* 漢學; [[KR3j0072]] *Qiánqiū zhájì* 潛邱劄記 ([[閻若璩]]); [[KR3j0073]] *Zhànyuán zhájì* 湛園札記 ([[姜宸英]]); [[KR3j0076]] *Yìmén dú shū jì* 義門讀書記 ([[何焯]]); [[KR3j0074]] *Bái tián zá zhù* 白田雜著 ([[王懋竑]]); [[KR3j0069]] *Yìfǔ* 義府 ([[黃生]]) — collectively the canonical kǎozhèng cluster the *Sìkù* editors place beside Wáng Yīnglín as proof of the genre's continuity. The five-volume late-Qīng notebooks of [[王士禛]] ([[KR3j0162]] *Jūyì lù* 居易錄, [[KR3j0163]] *Chí běi ǒu tán* 池北偶談, [[KR3j0164]] *Xiāng zǔ bǐjì* 香祖筆記, [[KR3j0165]] *Fēngān yúhuà* 分甘餘話, [[KR3j0166]] *Gǔfūyútíng zálù* 古夫於亭雜錄) close the division's chronological line at the turn of the eighteenth century.

**The connoisseurship cluster** (*zá pǐn*). [[KR3j0167]] *Dòngtiān qīnglù* 洞天清錄 ([[趙希鵠]], Sòng — antiques and the literati object); [[KR3j0169]] *Yún yān guò yǎn lù* 雲烟過眼錄 ([[周密]]); [[KR3j0170]] *Gégǔ yàolùn* 格古要論 ([[曹昭]], 1388); [[KR3j0172]] *Zūn shēng bā jiān* 遵生八牋 ([[高濂]], 1591); [[KR3j0174]] *Cháng wù zhì* 長物志 ([[文震亨]]), the canonical late-Míng manual of literati taste in furniture, gardens, and the things of the studio.

## Important persons

- [[呂不韋]] (Lǚ Bùwéi, d. 235 BCE) — convener of the *Lǚshì chūnqiū* salon; the founding figure of imperial-state syncretism in this division.
- [[劉安]] (Liú Ān, c. 179–122 BCE) — Prince of Huáinán, convener of the *Huáinánzǐ* circle and the second canonical *Zájiā* syncretist.
- [[王充]] (Wáng Chōng, 27–c. 100) — Eastern Hàn rationalist polemicist; *Lùnhéng* is the most sustained early prose argument against portent-mongering and excess in the division.
- [[顏之推]] (Yán Zhītuī, 531–c. 591) — author of the *Yánshì jiāxùn*, the model of the Six-Dynasties / Suí *jiāxùn* tradition and a major source for the social history of the period.
- [[沈括]] (Shěn Kuò, 1031–1095) — Northern Sòng polymath; the *Mèngxī bǐtán* is the single most influential pre-modern Chinese technical-scientific *bǐjì*.
- [[蘇軾]] (Sū Shì, 1037–1101) — model of the literati personal notebook ([[KR3j0093]], [[KR3j0094]]).
- [[葉夢得]] (Yè Mèngdé, 1077–1148) — author of the *Shílín* trilogy ([[KR3j0105]]–[[KR3j0107]]), a Northern–Southern Sòng witness on court life and classical scholarship.
- [[洪邁]] (Hóng Mài, 1123–1202) — author of *Róngzhāi suíbǐ*, the most consequential Sòng evidential *bǐjì* before Wáng Yīnglín and the work that did most to fix the form.
- [[程大昌]] (Chéng Dàchāng, 1123–1195) — twin author of *Kǎogǔ biān* and *Yǎnfán lù*; a key Sòng *kǎozhèng* witness on ritual and institutions.
- [[王應麟]] (Wáng Yīnglín, 1223–1296) — Sòng-loyalist polymath; the *Kùnxué jìwén* is the most important single *kǎozhèng bǐjì* of the Sòng and a direct model for Qīng *Hàn xué*.
- [[周密]] (Zhōu Mì, 1232–1298) — Sòng-Yuán *yímín* whose work is split across two sub-genres in the division: *zá shuō* in *Qí dōng yě yǔ* and *zá pǐn* in *Yún yān guò yǎn lù*.
- [[陸游]] (Lù Yóu, 1125–1210) — author of the *Lǎo xué ān bǐjì*, one of the most-cited Sòng anecdotal-historical notebooks.
- [[楊愼]] (Yáng Shèn, 1488–1559) — the most prolific Míng *bǐjì* author and the principal target of subsequent Míng-Qīng rebuttals (e.g. [[KR3j0062]] *Zhèng Yáng* 正楊).
- [[胡應麟]] (Hú Yīnglín, 1551–1602) — late-Míng bibliographer and polymath whose *Shǎoshì shānfáng bǐcóng* is the great Míng *bǐjì*-as-cóngshū.
- [[方以智]] (Fāng Yǐzhì, 1611–1671) — late-Míng / early-Qīng polymath; author of both *Tōngyǎ* (philology) and *Wùlǐ xiǎozhì* (natural philosophy) — the most ambitious bridge between Míng polymathy and Qīng evidential learning.
- [[顧炎武]] (Gù Yánwǔ, 1613–1682) — Sòng-loyalist refusenik whose *Rì zhī lù* defines the Qīng evidential tradition in this division.
- [[閻若璩]] (Yán Ruòqú, 1636–1704) — Qīng evidential master whose *Qiánqiū zhájì* — together with his annotations on Wáng Yīnglín — institutionalised the Sòng-to-Qīng *kǎozhèng* lineage.
- [[何焯]] (Hé Zhuō, 1661–1722) — early-Qīng evidential editor whose *Yìmén dú shū jì* and joint annotations to *Kùnxué jìwén* fix the Qīng reception of Wáng Yīnglín.
- [[王士禛]] (Wáng Shìzhēn, 1634–1711) — the leading literary arbiter of the Kāngxī court whose five separate notebooks ([[KR3j0162]]–[[KR3j0166]]) are the most thoroughly preserved late-Qīng literati *bǐjì* in the division.
- [[文震亨]] (Wén Zhènhēng, 1585–1645) — author of *Cháng wù zhì*, the canonical late-Míng manual of literati connoisseurship.

## Topics

- **The category as residual class.** *Zájiā* is, in both its Hàn and *Sìkù* incarnations, the bibliographer's category of last resort: works that do not belong in the four orthodox Masters subclasses (Confucians, Daoists, Legalists, Logicians) or in the more specialised technical subclasses (Military, Astronomy, Mantic Arts, Medicine, Arts, Connoisseurship in the narrow sense, *Lèishū*, Fiction) end up here. Reading the division therefore requires keeping the *Sìkù*'s six internal subclasses in view (see Scope, above).
- **The *bǐjì* genre.** Most of the division — roughly [[KR3j0026]] through [[KR3j0166]] plus most of the connoisseurship cluster — consists of *bǐjì* (Wilkinson § 53). The genre's range and unevenness, its mingling of factual and anecdotal entries, its frequent reading-notes form, and its synonyms (*bǐtán*, *cóngtán*, *suíbǐ*, *zájì*, *zázhì*, *zhájì*; Wilkinson Box 266) are all directly relevant. Wilkinson § 53.1 explicitly remarks that the *bǐjì* form was usually filed in *Zájiā* in the Masters branch — the division on disk is therefore one of the largest single homes of the genre in the *Sìkù*.
- **Pseudepigraphy and recension.** Most of the pre-Qín *zǐ* in the head of the division ([[KR3j0001]], [[KR3j0004]], [[KR3j0005]], [[KR3j0006]]) are partially or wholly transmitted in post-Hàn recensions; some (the *Yùzǐ* in particular) are securely Táng. The *Sìkù* editors flag this consistently in their *tíyào*. Compositions dates in KR3j frontmatter therefore follow the project's policy (CLAUDE.md § Per-text procedure) of dating the **received recension**, not the attributed author's lifedates — so e.g. [[KR3j0001]] is dated 600–653 (Táng) and [[KR3j0003]] is dated to its Sòng forger's lifetime.
- **The growth of evidential learning.** The division is the principal site at which the trajectory from Sòng *kǎozhèng bǐjì* (Hóng Mài, Wáng Yīnglín, Chéng Dàchāng) through Míng polymathy (Yáng Shèn, Hú Yīnglín, Fāng Yǐzhì) to Qīng *Hàn xué* (Gù Yánwǔ, Yán Ruòqú, Hé Zhuō) can be read continuously off the shelf. The *Sìkù* editors' selection deliberately preserves that lineage.
- **The connoisseurship sub-genre and literati material culture.** The twelve *zá pǐn* texts collect the canonical Sòng-to-Qīng manuals on antiques, calligraphy, painting, gardens, food, dress, and the literati object — a body of evidence indispensable for the social and material history of the late imperial elite.
- **The boundary with *xiǎoshuō* 小說 and with *zá shǐ* 雜史.** Many of the more anecdotal *bǐjì* in the division ([[KR3j0083]] *Fēngshì wén jiàn jì*, [[KR3j0100]] *Lěngzhāi yè huà*, [[KR3j0127]] *Hè lín yù lù*) shade towards what later catalogers would call *xiǎoshuō*; the *Sìkù* keeps them in *Zájiā* on the strength of their factual content, but Wilkinson § 53.3 and § 52.1 make clear that the line was always negotiable, and many of the same titles are re-classified elsewhere in modern editions.
- **The *cóngshū* problem.** The four *zá biān* compendia ([[KR3j0188]] *Yǎnshān wàijí*, [[KR3j0189]] *Gǔjīn shuōhǎi*, [[KR3j0190]] *Shǎoshì shānfáng bǐcóng*, [[KR3j0191]] *Dùnyín zálù*) preserve the older arrangement under which multi-work compendia were filed in *Zájiā* rather than constituting their own branch; the late nineteenth-century rise of *cóngshū* as a fifth branch (Wilkinson § 73.2.3) effectively drains this subclass.
- **Reconstituted pre-Táng fragments.** The closing krp-titles entries ([[KR3j0196]] *Shǐ xué piān*, [[KR3j0197]] *Lù shì yào lǎn*, [[KR3j0198]] *Wùlǐ lùn*, [[KR3j0199]] *Guǎng zhì*, [[KR3j0200]] *Fā méng jì*) are Qīng *jíyì* 輯佚 reconstitutions of lost Three Kingdoms / Jìn works whose fragments survive in *lèishū* — a final demonstration of the division's role as the *Sìkù*'s home for genre-hybrid Masters material.
