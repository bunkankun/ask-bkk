## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR3ee` 鍼灸 (*Zhēnjiǔ*, "Acupuncture and Moxibustion") is the fifth sub-division of `KR3e` 醫家類 (Masters — Medicine) and gathers the principal Chinese specialist literature of needling, cauterization, channel-and-collateral (*jīngluò* 經絡) doctrine, acupoint (*shūxué* 腧穴) cataloging, and the closely-affiliated late-imperial *tuīná* 推拿 / *ànmó* 按摩 manipulation arts. All 57 entries are drawn from the 漢學文典 (*hxwd*) supplementary database rather than the Wényuāngé *Sìkù quánshū*: the Sìkù compilers absorbed the foundational classical acumoxa works (the *Huángdì nèijīng Língshū* corpus, *Zhēnjiǔ jiǎyǐ jīng* 針灸甲乙經, and the Sòng *Tóngrén shūxué zhēnjiǔ tújīng* 銅人腧穴鍼灸圖經) into the medical division proper or into 子部·醫家 broadly, but the densely-printed specialist literature of the Míng-Qīng period — much of it commercial-market didactic verse, illustrated bronze-man manuals, and chronoacupuncture handbooks — was excluded as technical and was preserved largely by hxwd. Endymion Wilkinson (*Chinese History: A New Manual*, Section 41.3.1, "General Works") lists [[皇甫謐]] Huángfǔ Mì's *Zhēnjiǔ jiǎyǐ jīng* (here as [[KR3ee005]]) as the foundational classic of the genre and the indispensable starting-point for any Western-language work on acupuncture, a framing this overview adopts.

Chronologically the division spans almost seventeen centuries. The anchor work is the Wèi-Jìn *Zhēnjiǔ jiǎyǐ jīng* [[KR3ee005]] (c. 256–282), the earliest comprehensive systematization of acumoxa doctrine and the 349-acupoint catalog from which every later acupoint manual descends. A Táng moxibustion stratum is preserved in the *Huángdì míngtáng jiǔjīng* [[KR3ee018]] and its Yuán parallel [[KR3ee023]]. The decisive turn comes in the Northern Sòng: [[王惟一]] Wáng Wéiyī's imperially-commissioned *Tóngrén shūxué zhēnjiǔ tújīng* 銅人腧穴鍼灸圖經 [[KR3ee056]] of 1026–1027, accompanied by two life-size bronze acupuncture mannequins and two stone-stele inscriptions, fixed the standard acupoint catalog for nine centuries; the Jīn 1186 augmented recension [[KR3ee057]] and a parallel transmission [[KR3ee055]] continue the same line. Jīn-Yuán innovation then produced two further foundations: [[閻明廣]] Yán Míngguǎng's Jīn-1153 *Zǐwǔ liúzhù zhēn jīng* 子午流注針經 [[KR3ee045]], establishing the stem-branch chronoacupuncture system, and [[滑壽]] Huá Shòu's Yuán *Shísì jīng fāhuī* 十四經發揮 [[KR3ee026]] of 1341–1364, establishing the fourteen-channel pedagogical framework (the twelve regular channels plus *Rèn* 任 and *Dū* 督) that remains universally standard. The Míng-Qīng tradition is dominated by three commanding syntheses: [[徐鳳]] Xú Fèng's *Zhēnjiǔ dàquán* 針灸大全 [[KR3ee002]] of 1439, operationalizing 子午流注 and 靈龜八法; [[楊繼洲]] Yáng Jìzhōu's *Zhēnjiǔ dàchéng* 針灸大成 [[KR3ee027]] of 1601, the single most influential post-classical acupuncture text in East-Asian medical history; and [[吳謙]] Wú Qiān et al.'s imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn · Cìjiǔ xīnfǎ yàojué* 醫宗金鑑·刺灸心法要訣 [[KR3ee015]] of 1742, the Qiánlóng-era standard curriculum. The division closes with the late-Qīng Húnán pedagogical cluster around [[廖潤鴻]] Liào Rùnhóng (1874), the Sino-Western synthesis of [[劉鍾衡]] Liú Zhōnghéng (1894), the late-Qīng moxibustion-specific monographs of [[吳亦鼎]] Wú Yìdǐng (1851) and the *Jiǔfǎ mìchuán* (1883), the Edo–Meiji Japanese stratum ([[杉山和一]] Sugiyama Wa'ichi, [[原昌克]] Hara Masakatsu, [[小阪營升]] Kosaka Genyū, [[管周桂]] Sugahara Shūkei, [[佐藤利信]] Satō Toshinobu), and the Republican-era pediatric-massage works of [[張筱衫]] Zhāng Xiǎoshān and [[塗蔚生]] Tú Wèishēng.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The foundational classical works.** Three early-imperial and Táng works stand behind the entire later tradition.
- *Zhēnjiǔ jiǎyǐ jīng* 針灸甲乙經 [[KR3ee005]] — [[皇甫謐]] Huángfǔ Mì, c. 256–282. Twelve juan; the oldest extant comprehensive acumoxa synthesis, preserving lost *Míngtáng* 明堂 material and organizing the canonical doctrine for all later imperial revisions. The first systematic 349-acupoint catalog.
- *Huángdì míngtáng jiǔjīng* 黃帝明堂灸經 [[KR3ee018]] — anonymous Táng (c. 700–1100). Principal surviving moxibustion-only catalog preserving the otherwise-lost 明堂 acupoint tradition that lies behind the *Jiǎyǐ jīng*.
- *Xīfāngzǐ míngtáng jiǔjīng* 西方子明堂灸經 [[KR3ee023]] — anonymous Yuán (c. 1295–1330). Extending and refining the Táng *Huángdì míngtáng jiǔjīng*; one of three principal surviving witnesses to the lost 明堂 corpus.

**The Northern-Sòng imperial standardization and the 銅人 ("bronze-man") tradition.** The single most consequential post-Táng moment in acumoxa history is the Northern-Sòng court's standardization of acupoint location and indication under [[王惟一]] Wáng Wéiyī.
- *Tóngrén shūxué zhēnjiǔ tújīng* 銅人腧穴鍼灸圖經 [[KR3ee056]] — Wáng Wéiyī, 1026–1027 (imperial commission of Sòng Rénzōng). Three juan, accompanied by two life-size hollow-bronze acupuncture mannequins (cast 1027) and a stone-stele inscription erected at the *Tàiyī jú* 太醫局. The textual basis for nine centuries of acupoint pedagogy; every later acupoint atlas in the division derives from it.
- *Tóngrén zhēnjiǔ jīng* 銅人鍼灸經 [[KR3ee055]] — anonymous Sòng. Seven-juan parallel hxwd transmission of substantively the same material as the Wáng Wéiyī compendium.
- *Xīnkān bǔzhù Tóngrén shūxué zhēnjiǔ tújīng* 新刊補註銅人腧穴鍼灸圖經 [[KR3ee057]] — Jīn 1186 augmented recension by Bìxiéguìsǒu 閉邪瞶叟, adding needling-technique apparatus and disease-indexed acupoint tables; the principal post-Sòng witness to the *Tóngrén* tradition and the most important Jīn-period medical work.

**Other Sòng acumoxa works.** Outside the imperial *Tóngrén* line, the Sòng produced a small but doctrinally important set of further works.
- *Zhēnjiǔ shénshū* 針灸神書 [[KR3ee004]] — attributed [[瓊瑤真人]] Qióngyáo zhēnrén (Sòng, c. 1186–1200). Principal Sòng witness to the non-court manipulation tradition (the 刮戰搖按攝彈搓搜 method and the *fùbù pánpán* 腹部盤盤 technique) otherwise absent from the imperial canon.
- *Zhēnjiǔ zīshēng jīng* 針灸資生經 [[KR3ee020]] — [[王執中]] Wáng Zhízhōng, 1209–1220. Seven juan; the most influential Southern-Sòng acumoxa treatise, defining the post-Sòng *bìngduìxué* 病對穴 ("syndrome-to-point") pedagogy and integrating the formulary tradition with acupuncture practice.
- *Jiǔ gāohuāng shùxué fǎ* 灸膏肓腧穴法 [[KR3ee046]] — [[莊綽]] Zhuāng Chuò, 1128–1148. Southern-Sòng one-juan monograph on the single acupoint *gāohuāng* 膏肓, with precise bone-landmark localization; the standard source for *gāohuāng* moxibustion in all subsequent Chinese, Korean, and Japanese practice.
- *Sòngběn bèijí jiǔfǎ* 宋本備急灸法 [[KR3ee049]] — printed by Sūn Jùqīng 孫炬卿, 1226–1250. Southern-Sòng emergency-treatment moxibustion manual surviving via Japanese transmission; the principal witness to the popular-medicine *qízhúmǎ jiǔfǎ* 騎竹馬灸法 ("riding-the-bamboo-horse" moxibustion) method.

**The 子午流注 (chronoacupuncture) tradition.** Stem-branch hourly needling — *zǐwǔ liúzhù* 子午流注 — is the principal Jīn-Yuán doctrinal innovation in the division.
- *Zǐwǔ liúzhù zhēn jīng* 子午流注針經 [[KR3ee045]] — [[閻明廣]] Yán Míngguǎng, Jīn, 1153. Three juan; the foundational text of *zǐwǔ liúzhù*, governing the assignment of acupoints to heavenly-stem hours and basis for all subsequent East-Asian chronotherapy.
- *Zǐwǔ liúzhù shuōnán* 子午流注說難 [[KR3ee048]] — [[閻明廣]] Yán Míngguǎng, Jīn, 1153. Companion technical exposition establishing the *shènsānjiāo* 腎–三焦 hour-exchange doctrine that resolves the apparent ten-stem-versus-twelve-channel asymmetry.
- The system is systematized in Míng by Xú Fèng's *Zhēnjiǔ dàquán* [[KR3ee002]] and operationalized in [[王國瑞]] Wáng Guóruì's *Yùlóng jīng* [[KR3ee008]]; it is applied in a martial-arts context by [[楊成博]] Yáng Chéngbó [[KR3ee051]].

**Yuán channel-and-acupoint synthesis.** The Yuán produced the framework on which all subsequent acupuncture pedagogy is built.
- *Shísì jīng fāhuī* 十四經發揮 [[KR3ee026]] — [[滑壽]] Huá Shòu, 1341–1364. Three juan; the most influential Yuán acupuncture treatise, establishing the **fourteen-channel** framework (the twelve regular channels plus the *Rèn* 任 and *Dū* 督 governing-and-conception vessels) as the standard pedagogical organisation, against the pre-Yuán twelve-channel scheme. Decisive for every later Míng-Qīng manual.
- *Biǎnquè shényìng zhēnjiǔ Yùlóng jīng* 扁鵲神應針灸玉龍經 [[KR3ee008]] — [[王國瑞]] Wáng Guóruì, 1329. First Yuán systematization of the entire clinical curriculum as 120-acupoint mnemonic verse, pioneering the *xuéfǎ xiāngyìng* 穴法相應 point-pairing schema foundational to modern pedagogy.
- *Zhēn jīng zhǐnán* 針經指南 [[KR3ee017]] — [[竇傑]] Dòu Jié (*zì* Hànqīng 漢卿), Sòng-Yuán transition, composition c. 1180–1241, first printed 1295. The principal Sòng-Yuán source for the **Biāoyōu fù** 標幽賦 — the most-memorized acupuncture rhapsody in the entire tradition — and for the foundational *liúzhù bāxué* 流注八穴 doctrine of eight extraordinary-vessel confluent points.
- *Zhēn jīng jiéyào* 針經節要 [[KR3ee019]] — [[杜思敬]] Dù Sījìng, Yuán, c. 1315–1320. Yuán portable vade mecum distilling *Sùwèn* / *Língshū* / *Nànjīng* into clinical-decision-ready form.

**Míng comprehensive syntheses.** The Míng is the great age of acumoxa publication, and four works tower over the rest.
- *Pǔjì fāng · Zhēnjiǔ mén* 普濟方·針灸門 [[KR3ee013]] — [[朱橚]] Zhū Sù (Zhōu Dìngwáng 周定王), 1406. The acupuncture section (~40 juan) of the colossal early-Míng imperial formulary; the largest pre-modern acupuncture synthesis, absorbing the entire earlier corpus into symptom-organized reference and preserving otherwise-lost Yuán material.
- *Shényìng jīng* 神應經 [[KR3ee029]] — [[陳會]] Chén Huì, with [[劉瑾]] Liú Jǐn's supplementation, 1425. The most clinically utilitarian early-Míng acupuncture handbook with syndrome-first organisation; the principal channel through which the Sōngjiāng acupuncture lineage entered the Míng imperial curriculum, and (via 1474 Korean transmission) the first documented Chinese acupuncture text absorbed in Japan.
- *Zhēnjiǔ dàquán* 針灸大全 [[KR3ee002]] — [[徐鳳]] Xú Fèng, 1439. Six juan; the first systematic Míng operationalisation of *zǐwǔ liúzhù* and of the *Língguī bāfǎ* 靈龜八法 eight-confluent-point chronoacupuncture method; foundational for all post-Míng chronoacupuncture practice.
- *Zhēnjiǔ dàchéng* 針灸大成 [[KR3ee027]] — [[楊繼洲]] Yáng Jìzhōu, 1601. Ten juan; the **single most influential acupuncture work in Chinese medical history**, absorbing the entire preceding Sòng-Yuán-Míng corpus under Yáng's editorial apparatus and clinical cases. The foundation of all modern East-Asian acupuncture pedagogy and the principal channel into early-modern Japanese and Korean acupuncture transmission.

**Gāo Wǔ's philological trilogy and Míng polemic against post-classical mnemonics.** [[高武]] Gāo Wǔ (Míng *jǔrén*, *fl.* 1519–1537) produced three coordinated works that opened the philological-critical reception of the canon in the acumoxa genre.
- *Zhēnjiǔ jiéyào* 針灸節要 [[KR3ee028]] — Gāo Wǔ, 1519. Source-text abridgement and topical re-organisation of the canon — the foundation on which the later two works build.
- *Zhēnjiǔ jùyīng* 針灸聚英 [[KR3ee014]] — Gāo Wǔ, 1529. The most philologically rigorous Míng acupuncture compendium, comparing the post-classical didactic verses (賦, 歌訣) against the canonical *Sùwèn* / *Língshū* / *Nànjīng* sources; the principal source for Yáng Jìzhōu's critical apparatus in the *Dàchéng* [[KR3ee027]].
- *Zhēnjiǔ Sù-Nàn yàozhǐ* 針灸素難要旨 [[KR3ee021]] — Gāo Wǔ, 1537. The most back-to-source of the trilogy: a topical re-organisation of *Sùwèn* and *Nànjīng* acumoxa material stripped of later commentaries.
- The Gāo Wǔ programme is complemented by [[汪機]] Wāng Jī's catechistic *Zhēnjiǔ wènduì* 針灸問對 [[KR3ee016]] of 1530 — the most theoretically explicit Míng acumoxa treatise, subjecting the inherited mnemonic tradition to *Sù-Nàn* canonical critique in polemical dialogue form.

**Other Míng works.** Three further Míng works deserve separate mention.
- *Língshū jīngmài yì* 靈樞經脈翼 [[KR3ee024]] — [[夏英]] Xià Yīng, 1456–1500. Early-Míng accessible exposition of the *Língshū* channel doctrine through versification and prose paraphrase, mediating between Huá Shòu's terse [[KR3ee026]] and clinical application.
- *Qí jīng bā mài kǎo* 奇經八脈考 [[KR3ee001]] — [[李時珍]] Lǐ Shízhēn, c. 1572–1578. The foundational Míng monograph on the eight extraordinary vessels (*qíjīng bāmài* 奇經八脈), integrating Sòng-Yuán acumoxa channel doctrine with Daoist inner-alchemical theory; the standard reference for *qíjīng* doctrine ever since.
- *Yáng Jìngzhāi zhēnjiǔ quánshū* 楊敬齋針灸全書 [[KR3ee009]] — attributed Chén Yán 陳言, 1591 (attribution conjectural). Late-Míng didactic-verse anthology positioned textually between Xú Fèng's *Dàquán* and Yáng Jìzhōu's *Dàchéng*.
- *Zhēnfāng liùjí* 針方六集 [[KR3ee041]] — [[吳昆]] Wú Kūn, 1618. Six-juan late-Míng acupuncture-and-formulary compendium contemporary with — but independent of — Yáng Jìzhōu's *Dàchéng*; an integration of acupuncture with Wú Kūn's wider medical-philological corpus (see also his *Sùwèn Wú zhù* [[KR3ea014]]).
- *Lèijīng túyì* 類經圖翼 [[KR3ee031]] — [[張介賓]] Zhāng Jièbīn, 1624. Eleven-juan illustrated acupuncture-doctrinal companion to the monumental *Lèijīng* [[KR3ea036]]; the principal late-Míng illustrated reference, bridging Yáng Jìzhōu's *Dàchéng* and the Qīng imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* and integrating the warming-tonifying-school doctrinal perspective.

**The Míng *jīngluò* 經絡 (channel-doctrine) monographs.** A distinctive late-Míng genre re-centres clinical reasoning on channel-collateral theory, against formulary-dominated practice.
- *Jīngluò quánshū* 經絡全書 [[KR3ee011]] — [[沈子祿]] Shěn Zǐlù, completed by [[徐師曾]] Xú Shīzēng, 1576. The most influential late-Míng channel-doctrine synthesis, integrating philological Confucian methodology with medical pedagogy; foundational for Yáng Jìzhōu's *Dàchéng*.
- *Jīngluò huìbiān* 經絡彙編 [[KR3ee010]] — [[翟良]] Zhái Liáng, 1628–1644. Late-Míng polemical treatise re-centring all clinical reasoning on channel-collateral doctrine.
- *Jīngluò kǎo* 經絡考 [[KR3ee025]] — [[張三錫]] Zhāng Sānxī, 1573–1620. Channel-doctrine monograph integrating the Jīn-Yuán four-master tradition with the Míng emphasis on channel-tropism (*guījīng* 歸經) pharmacotherapy.

**The Qīng imperial curriculum and the post-curriculum responses.** [[吳謙]] Wú Qiān's *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* 醫宗金鑑 (1742) absorbs the acumoxa tradition into a single state-mandated textbook.
- *Yīzōng jīnjiàn · Cìjiǔ xīnfǎ yàojué* 醫宗金鑑·刺灸心法要訣 [[KR3ee015]] — Wú Qiān et al., 1742. The eight-juan acupuncture section of the imperial textbook; the institutional Qīng acupuncture curriculum standardizing the imperial examinations until the late 19th century, and the principal channel through which Sòng-Míng doctrine entered Republican-era TCM.
- *Zhēnjiǔ féngyuán* 針灸逢源 [[KR3ee030]] — [[李學川]] Lǐ Xuéchuān, 1815. The most theoretically rigorous Qīng acupuncture compendium, presented as a corrective to the *Yīzōng jīnjiàn*-dominated curriculum and aiming to restore the canonical *Sù-Nàn* foundation.
- *Zhèngzhì zhēn jīng* 證治針經 [[KR3ee038]] — [[郭誠勳]] Guō Chéngxūn, 1823. Mid-Qīng acupuncture-pharmacology compendium in the *zhézhōng* 折衷 ("balanced eclecticism") manner, refusing sectarian commitment in favour of canonical-source *kǎozhèng* 考正 method.
- *Zhēnjiǔ yìxué* 針灸易學 [[KR3ee012]] — [[李守先]] Lǐ Shǒuxiān, 1798–1847. The most popular Qīng beginner primer, pairing didactic verses with worked clinical examples.
- *Shēnjīng tōngkǎo* 身經通考 [[KR3ee044]] — [[李瀠]] Lǐ Yíng, 1645–1720. Early-Qīng channel-doctrine catechistic compendium in eclectic style.
- *Màidù yùnxíng kǎo* 脈度運行考 [[KR3ee039]] — [[李彰五]] Lǐ Zhāngwǔ, 1898. Rigorous late-Qīng technical-philological treatise subjecting the *Língshū* *yíngqì* 營氣 circulation doctrine to mathematical scrutiny, discarding the popular twelve-hour twelve-channel mnemonic for precise *cùncùnfēn* tabulation.

**The late-Qīng Húnán pedagogical cluster.** [[廖潤鴻]] Liào Rùnhóng (active 1874) produced the dominant late-Qīng textbook line, fixing the verse-mnemonic + atlas + clinical-syndrome structure later inherited by post-1949 TCM textbooks.
- *Zhēnjiǔ jíchéng* 針灸集成 [[KR3ee007]] — Liào Rùnhóng, 1874. Influential late-Qīng portable handbook recasting the imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* curriculum as five-character verse with reduced-scale 銅人 diagrams for field practice.
- *Kǎozhèng zhōushēn xuéfǎ gē* 考正周身穴法歌 [[KR3ee032]] — Liào Rùnhóng, 1874. The most-copied late-Qīng acupuncture verse, replacing the older *Zhōushēn jīngxué fù* as the standard memorisation material in late-Qīng schools.
- *Miǎnxuétáng zhēnjiǔ jíchéng* 勉學堂針灸集成 [[KR3ee037]] — Liào Rùnhóng, 1874. Expanded version of [[KR3ee007]]; the most-reproduced late-Qīng pedagogical model.
- *Jīngmài túkǎo* 經脈圖考 [[KR3ee036]] — [[陳惠疇]] Chén Huìchóu, 1878. The principal late-Qīng illustrated channel-and-acupoint reference, re-drawing the 銅人 diagrams in lithographic style with precise *cùn*-by-*cùn* measurements.

**The moxibustion-specific late-Qīng works.** Moxibustion-only monographs, distinct from the unified acumoxa handbooks, form a small but coherent cluster.
- *Shénjiǔ jīnglún* 神灸經綸 [[KR3ee050]] — [[吳亦鼎]] Wú Yìdǐng, 1851. Four-juan late-Qīng moxibustion-specific treatise restoring moxibustion to canonical *Língshū* status against the dominant Qīng pharmacotherapy bias; covers the *Tàiyǐ shénzhēn* 太乙神針 and *qízhúmǎ jiǔfǎ* methods.
- *Jiǔfǎ mìchuán* 灸法秘傳 [[KR3ee047]] — Sìchuān monastic source, edited by Léi Fēng 雷豐 and printed by [[劉國光]] Liú Guóguāng, 1883. The principal late-Qīng moxibustion-technique manual; the major witness to the *Tàiyǐ shénzhēn* compound-moxa tradition.

**The Sino-Western late-Qīng synthesis.** *Zhōngxī huìcān tóngrén túshuō* 中西匯參銅人圖說 [[KR3ee034]] — [[劉鍾衡]] Liú Zhōnghéng, 1894. The major late-Qīng *huìcān* 匯參 work updating the *Tóngrén* atlas with Western anatomical reference while retaining the channel-acupoint apparatus; conceptually paralleling [[唐宗海]]'s *Zhōngxī huìtōng yī jīng jīngyì* [[KR3ea044]] of 1892 in the *Nèijīng* division and preparing the ground for Republican TCM modernization.

**The Republican-era works.** Three works carry the imperial-era tradition into the 1920s and 1930s.
- *Jīnzhēn mìchuán* 金針秘傳 [[KR3ee006]] — [[方慎庵]] Fāng Shènān, 1928–1937. Sūzhōu late-Qīng family-transmission monograph defending the "genuine acupuncture" against Republican biomedical marginalization; foundation for post-1949 TCM reconstitution.
- *Zhēnjiǔ wèndá* 針灸問答 [[KR3ee035]] — [[譚志光]] Tán Zhìguāng, 1927. Húnán Republican catechistic manual transmitting the Liào Rùnhóng late-Qīng pedagogical line into the Republican TCM curriculum.

**The Edo and Meiji Japanese stratum.** Five works document the Japanese reception and back-transmission of the Chinese acumoxa tradition.
- *Xuǎnzhēn sānyào jí* 選針三要集 [[KR3ee052]] — [[杉山和一]] Sugiyama Wa'ichi, 1670–1694. Kanbun acupuncture monograph by Edo's most famous blind acupuncturist and founder of the *Sugiyama-ryū* 杉山流; transmits the Sòng-Yuán-Míng Chinese canon into Edo practice and establishes the *bǔxiè* 補瀉 + *qǔxué* 取穴 + *xūshí* 虛實 pedagogical framework that became the basis of the Edo blind-acupuncturist guild tradition.
- *Zhēnjiǔxué gāngyào* 針灸學綱要 [[KR3ee040]] — [[管周桂]] Sugahara Shūkei 菅周桂, 1766. Edo *kogaku* 古学 ("ancient-learning") acupuncture-revivalist treatise advocating 70 essential acupoints against the elaborate post-Míng didactic tradition; the Edo philological parallel to the Chinese back-to-canon polemic of Gāo Wǔ and Wāng Jī.
- *Jīngxué huìjiě* 經穴匯解 [[KR3ee022]] — [[原昌克]] Hara Masakatsu 原昌克 (Yoshitanechi), 1803. Eight-juan Edo philological treatise systematically comparing Chinese and Japanese acupoint catalogs with full critical apparatus; the major Edo back-transmission text, circulating into late-Qīng China.
- *Jīngxué zuǎnyào* 經穴纂要 [[KR3ee043]] — [[小阪營升]] Kosaka Genyū 小阪營升, 1810–1830. Edo philological acupoint catalog cited by Hara Masakatsu; represents the five-generation Mito-domain Kosaka transmission.
- *Zhēnxué tōnglùn* 針學通論 [[KR3ee042]] — [[佐藤利信]] Satō Toshinobu, 1850–1900. Bakumatsu / Meiji-period reform-pedagogy treatise integrating Western anatomical vocabulary with the *Sugiyama-ryū* blind-acupuncturist tradition.

**The *tuīná* 推拿 / *ànmó* 按摩 tail.** Two late entries integrate the closely-related manipulation-medicine specialty.
- *Lízhèng ànmó yàoshù* 釐正按摩要術 [[KR3ee054]] — [[張筱衫]] Zhāng Xiǎoshān, 1888. The most successful late-Qīng pediatric-massage monograph; preserves the lost Wàn-lì-era Zhōu Yúfán 周於蕃 manuscript and makes a sustained diagnostic argument for *biànzhèng* 變證-disciplined (not folk) *tuīná* practice, with comprehensive 銅人 charts.
- *Tuīná juéwēi* 推拿抉微 [[KR3ee053]] — [[塗蔚生]] Tú Wèishēng, 1928. The principal Republican-era pediatric-massage monograph, integrating the late-Qīng technical core with full classical diagnostic and pharmacotherapeutic apparatus.

**Outlying works.** *Língmén chuánshòu tóngrén zhǐxué* 凌門傳授銅人指穴 [[KR3ee003]] — anonymous Qīng family compendium, c. 1644–1850, a portable bedside vade mecum preserving the Míng didactic-verse apparatus. *Zīshēng jí* 資生集 [[KR3ee033]] — [[董熿]] Dǒng Huáng (compiler), with [[張萬選]] Zhāng Wànxuǎn's editing, 1644–1722, a Qīng gynaecology-with-acupuncture monograph. *Yáng Chéngbó xiānshēng yíliú xuédào mìshū* 楊成博先生遺留穴道秘書 [[KR3ee051]] — [[楊成博]] Yáng Chéngbó, Míng-Qīng, a rare manuscript witness to the *diǎnxué* 點穴 (point-striking) and *jiēgǔ* 接骨 (bone-setting) underground lineage tradition, applying *zǐwǔ liúzhù* hourly-pathway doctrine to combat-strike calculation and rescue.

## Topics

The division is organised around a small number of long-running debates and methodological problems.

**Acupoint standardization and the 銅人 problem.** The single defining moment in post-Táng acumoxa history is Wáng Wéiyī's 1027 imperial bronze-man standardization [[KR3ee056]] [[KR3ee057]] [[KR3ee055]]. The standardization of acupoint location, depth, and indication produced both the disciplinary stability that allowed the late-imperial acupuncture textbook tradition to flourish and the perennial controversies over points whose Sòng-court placement deviated from the *Língshū*, the *Jiǎyǐ jīng*, or the *Míngtáng* tradition. The whole of the Míng-Qīng acupoint-catalog literature ([[KR3ee014]] [[KR3ee021]] [[KR3ee022]] [[KR3ee032]] [[KR3ee036]] [[KR3ee043]] [[KR3ee056]]–[[KR3ee057]]) can be read as the ongoing negotiation of this standard against the canonical sources.

**The fourteen-channel framework.** Huá Shòu's Yuán innovation [[KR3ee026]] of adding the *Rèn* 任 and *Dū* 督 vessels to the twelve regular channels — yielding the *shísì jīng* 十四經 — became the universally standard pedagogical organisation from the Yuán onward, replacing the pre-Yuán twelve-channel scheme. The framework structures every later channel-doctrine monograph in the division ([[KR3ee010]] [[KR3ee011]] [[KR3ee024]] [[KR3ee025]] [[KR3ee036]] [[KR3ee044]]) and the entire Míng-Qīng didactic-verse genre.

**The 子午流注 (chronoacupuncture) tradition.** Stem-branch hourly needling [[KR3ee045]] [[KR3ee048]] was the principal Jīn-Yuán doctrinal innovation in the division. The system was systematized in Míng by Xú Fèng [[KR3ee002]] and incorporated into the standard didactic curriculum from Wáng Guóruì's *Yùlóng jīng* [[KR3ee008]] onward. The internal coherence problem — reconciling the ten heavenly stems with the twelve channels — is addressed by Yán Míngguǎng's *shènsānjiāo* hour-exchange doctrine [[KR3ee048]] and remains the principal technical difficulty in the system.

**The eight extraordinary vessels (奇經八脈).** Treated systematically in [[李時珍]] Lǐ Shízhēn's monograph [[KR3ee001]] and underlying the *liúzhù bāxué* eight-confluent-point system of [[竇傑]] [[KR3ee017]] and Xú Fèng [[KR3ee002]]. The integration of *qíjīng* doctrine with Daoist inner-alchemical theory is a distinctive Lǐ Shízhēn contribution that flows downstream into both medical and self-cultivation traditions.

**Moxibustion as an independent specialty.** The Táng *Míngtáng jiǔjīng* [[KR3ee018]] and its Yuán parallel [[KR3ee023]] preserve a moxibustion-only canon that is otherwise absorbed into the unified acumoxa handbooks. The late-Qīng moxibustion-specific revival of Wú Yìdǐng [[KR3ee050]] and the *Jiǔfǎ mìchuán* [[KR3ee047]] — both polemical defences of moxibustion against the dominant Qīng pharmacotherapy bias — return moxibustion to the canonical *Língshū* status it occupied in the Hàn-Jìn period.

**Mnemonic verse and the late-imperial commercial-print market.** From [[竇傑]]'s *Biāoyōu fù* through the Yuán-Míng *Yùlóng* mnemonic, Xú Fèng's *Língguī* method, Yáng Jìzhōu's *Dàchéng* compilation of the inherited rhapsodies, [[吳謙]]'s imperial seven-character verses, and Liào Rùnhóng's late-Qīng five-character textbook, the acumoxa genre has been distinctively pedagogical-mnemonic. The commercial late-Míng market drove a parallel industry of illustrated vernacular-glossed bronze-man manuals ([[KR3ee003]] [[KR3ee009]] [[KR3ee029]]). [[高武]] Gāo Wǔ's trilogy [[KR3ee014]] [[KR3ee021]] [[KR3ee028]] and [[汪機]] Wāng Jī's catechism [[KR3ee016]] are the principal Míng polemical responses, subjecting the post-classical verse-tradition to *Sù-Nàn* canonical critique.

**The 病對穴 (syndrome-to-point) versus canonical-doctrine tension.** From [[王執中]] Wáng Zhízhōng's Southern-Sòng [[KR3ee020]] onward, the dominant late-imperial pedagogy organises acupuncture knowledge as a mapping from clinical syndrome to therapeutic point — a clinically-utility-first ordering that pulls against the canonical Língshū-Jiǎyǐ-Tóngrén organisation by channel and acupoint. The opposing currents are visible across nearly every Míng-Qīng handbook: the syndrome-first early-Míng *Shényìng jīng* [[KR3ee029]] and the colossal *Pǔjì fāng · Zhēnjiǔ mén* [[KR3ee013]] versus the doctrine-first late-Míng *Jīngluò quánshū* [[KR3ee011]] and the early-Qīng *Zhēnjiǔ féngyuán* [[KR3ee030]].

**Sino-Western anatomical integration.** Liú Zhōnghéng's *Zhōngxī huìcān tóngrén túshuō* [[KR3ee034]] of 1894 is the first systematic attempt to read the *Tóngrén* atlas alongside late-Qīng Western anatomical knowledge — the acumoxa-side analogue of [[唐宗海]]'s [[KR3ea044]] in the *Nèijīng* division. Although later regarded as transitional, it is the historical starting-point of the *zhōngxī yīhuìtōng* 中西醫匯通 programme applied to acupuncture and prepares the ground for Republican-era reform pedagogy.

**Edo Japanese reception and back-transmission.** Five Japanese works in the division ([[KR3ee022]] [[KR3ee040]] [[KR3ee042]] [[KR3ee043]] [[KR3ee052]]) document the Edo and Meiji absorption, philological critique, and partial back-transmission of the Chinese acumoxa canon. The Edo *kogaku* movement (Sugahara Shūkei) parallels the Chinese back-to-canon polemics of Gāo Wǔ; the Edo philological tradition (Hara Masakatsu, Kosaka Genyū) parallels the Tanba *Nèijīng* / *Nànjīng* commentaries of [[KR3ea010]] [[KR3ea012]] [[KR3ea026]] [[KR3ea062]]; and the blind-acupuncturist guild tradition (Sugiyama Wa'ichi, Satō Toshinobu) is a distinctive Japanese contribution with no Chinese counterpart.

**The *tuīná* 推拿 / *ànmó* 按摩 affiliation.** The pediatric massage works at the tail of the division [[KR3ee053]] [[KR3ee054]] reflect the post-Míng disciplinary boundary at which manual manipulation — historically a sub-specialty of the acumoxa physician — became a separate technical literature, most heavily developed for paediatric application.
