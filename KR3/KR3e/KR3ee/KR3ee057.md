# Xīnkān bǔzhù Tóngrén shūxué zhēnjiǔ tújīng 新刊補註銅人腧穴鍼灸圖經
*Newly Printed and Supplemented Illustrated Canon of Acupoints for Acupuncture and Moxibustion on the Bronze Mannequin*
originally by [[王惟一]] Wáng Wéiyī (原著, Northern Sòng, 1027); newly cut and supplemented by [[閉邪瞶叟]] Bìxiéguìsǒu (增補, Jīn, 1186)

## About the work
The five-juan **Jīn-period augmented and annotated recension** of Wáng Wéiyī's [[KR3ee056]] *Tóngrén shūxué zhēnjiǔ tújīng* — the principal post-Sòng witness to the bronze-man acupuncture tradition and one of the most important transmitted forms of the Sòng standardization. The augmented edition was cut and printed in *Jīn Dàdìng* 26 = 1186 by an editor who signs himself **Bìxiéguìsǒu** 閉邪瞶叟 ("The Closed-Off-Evil Blind Old Man") — a transparent self-deprecating pen-name whose civil identity is not preserved. The hxwd source file for KR3ee057 is placeholder-only (only the header `#+TITLE: 新刊補註銅人腧穴鍼灸圖經`), so the present note draws on the well-attested external record of the work and its later reception.

The five-juan structure of the Jīn augmented edition adds two new juan to the original three: juan 1–3 reproduce Wáng Wéiyī's twelve-channel + *rèn*/*dū* + body-region original; juan 4 supplies a **needling-technique manual** with depth-and-angle tables, moxa-count tables, and prohibited-point lists; juan 5 supplies a **disease-and-symptom indexed point-list** giving the standardized point prescriptions for the principal clinical syndromes. The augmentations are the editor's own additions, drawing on the Jīn-period acupuncture-pedagogical tradition centred on the Imperial Medical College at Yānjīng 燕京 (modern Beijing).

### Abstract
The 1186 Jīn-augmented *Xīnkān bǔzhù* is one of the two principal pre-modern recensions of the *Tóngrén* (alongside the SKQS three-juan original [[KR3ee056]] / seven-juan subdivided [[KR3e0017]] / [[KR3ee055]]) and is the form in which the *Tóngrén* tradition continued to circulate in North China after the 1126 fall of Kāifēng and the loss of one of the original bronze mannequins to Jīn plunder. Its significance is threefold: (1) it is the **principal Jīn-period transmission** of the Sòng acupuncture standardization, demonstrating the continuity of the imperial medical canon across the Jurchen conquest; (2) it adds **technical needling-instruction apparatus** absent from the 1027 original, making the work usable as a complete acupuncture textbook rather than only a point-location reference; (3) it preserves the **woodblock-printed line illustrations** of the bronze mannequin and the channel diagrams that became canonical in the Yuán-Míng tradition — the 1909 *Liúshì Xuántǒng jìyuán* 劉氏宣統紀元 reproduction of the 1186 Jīn edition (held at the Needham Research Institute, Cambridge) is the principal modern reproduction (reproduced in Hinrichs & Barnes 2013, fig. 4.2).

The editor's pseudonym **Bìxiéguìsǒu** 閉邪瞶叟 has resisted positive identification. *Bìxié* 閉邪 ("close-off-the-evil") is a *Yìjīng* *Wényán* 文言 commentary phrase (閉邪存其誠 — "close off the evil and preserve one's sincerity"), and *guìsǒu* 瞶叟 ("blind old man") is a topos of self-deprecation used by Jīn scholars under the dynasty. The pseudonym is consistent with an Imperial Medical College physician of the Jīn court who chose to conceal his name when reissuing the canonical Sòng work under the new dynasty — a not-uncommon Jurchen-period editorial practice. The work is registered in the *Jīnshǐ Yìwén zhì* and in subsequent bibliographies.

The 1909 *Xuántǒng* photo-lithographic reproduction (Hinrichs & Barnes 2013, 105; *Wang Weiyi's 1023 work … reproduction of 1186 C.E. Jin Dynasty republication … Guichi: Liushi Xuantong jiyuan, 1909*) is the standard modern access form; collated by Mǎ Jìxīng against the SKQS recension and the recovered *Dàxiàngguó* stele fragments. The Jīn augmented edition's diagrammatic conventions — front-view, back-view, and side-view of the body with point-marks at the canonical 354-point inventory — became the visual standard for all subsequent acupuncture pedagogy and are the direct ancestor of the modern acupuncture chart.

## Other points of interest
The Jīn 1186 augmented edition is one of the few extant medical works that bear a Jīn-period imprint with full editorial apparatus; the standard *Jīnshǐ Yìwén zhì* records very few free-standing medical printings under the Jurchens, and the *Xīnkān bǔzhù* is the chief surviving witness to the imperial-medical-college book culture of the Jīn court at Yānjīng. Its survival into the modern period via Qīng-Republican reproduction is therefore one of the most important pieces of evidence for continuity of medical canon-formation across the Jīn-Yuán transition.
