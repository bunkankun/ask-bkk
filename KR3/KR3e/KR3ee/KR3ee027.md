# Zhēnjiǔ dàchéng 針灸大成
*Great Compendium of Acupuncture and Moxibustion*
by [[楊繼洲]] Yáng Jìzhōu (撰)

## About the work
The single most influential acupuncture work in the Chinese medical tradition: a ten-juan late-Míng synthesis compiled by the imperial physician [[楊繼洲]] Yáng Jìzhōu (1522–1620, of Sānqú 三衢, Zhèjiāng) on the foundation of his family's hereditary *Wèishēng zhēnjiǔ xuánjī mìyào* 衛生針灸玄機秘要 and printed in Wànlì 29 (1601). The opening *Zhēndào yuánliú* 針道源流 ("The genealogical source of the way of needling") chapter gives a long historical survey: the *Sùwèn* 12-juan canon, traditionally ascribed to Yellow-Emperor / Qíbó dialogue but identified by [[劉向]] Liú Xiàng with "the various sons of the Hán family" and by [[程子]] Chéngzǐ with "the end of the Warring States" — analogous in transmission to the *Lǐjì*, gathered by Hàn Confucians; the *Nànjīng* 13-juan attributed to [[扁鵲]] Qín Yuèrén; the lost commentaries of [[呂博望]] Lǚ Bówàng, [[王惟一]] Wáng Wéiyī, [[虞氏]] Yúshì, [[紀齊卿]] Jì Qíqīng, [[楊玄操]] Yáng Xuáncāo, [[呂廣]] Lǚ Guǎng, [[王宗正]] Wáng Zōngzhèng, [[周仲立]] Zhōu Zhònglì, [[李子野]] Lǐ Zǐyě; the *Jiǎyǐ jīng* of [[皇甫謐]] Huángfǔ Mì ([[KR3ee005]]); the *Tàisù jīng* of [[楊上善]] Yáng Shàngshàn; the *Tóngrén tújīng* of Wáng Wéiyī ([[KR3ee056]]); the *Yùlóng jīng* of [[王國瑞]] Wáng Guóruì ([[KR3ee008]]); the *Zīshēng jīng* of [[王執中]] Wáng Zhízhōng ([[KR3ee020]]); the *Zhēnjiǔ dàquán* of [[徐鳳]] Xú Fèng ([[KR3ee002]]); the *Jùyīng* of [[高武]] Gāo Wǔ ([[KR3ee014]]); etc. The body of the *Dàchéng* then incorporates virtually the entire pre-existing acupuncture corpus, with Yáng's editorial annotations and his own clinical cases.

### Tiyao
The *Sìkù quánshū zǒngmù tíyào* (*Zǐbù · Yījiā lèi*) carries a tiyao for the *Dàchéng* and praises its comprehensiveness; it remarks that Yáng's work "fully encompasses everything from the Yellow Emperor and Qíbó to the present day, omitting nothing essential" (上自岐黃下迄今日，要無遺漏).

### Abstract
The *Zhēnjiǔ dàchéng* is the single most influential acupuncture text in East Asian history. Yáng Jìzhōu's editorial method is encyclopedic: virtually the entire pre-existing Sòng-Yuán-Míng acupuncture corpus is absorbed, with Yáng's *àn* 案 ("editorial note") interpolations marking his own clinical experience and his critical comparisons. The work is divided into ten juan: juan 1 the source-tradition essays and the canonical didactic verses; juan 2 the *Biāoyōu fù* ([[KR3ee017]]) with Yáng's annotated commentary; juan 3 the *Yùlóng gē* (after [[KR3ee008]]) and the chronoacupuncture verses; juan 4 the channel-trajectories with diagrams; juan 5 the head-to-foot acupoint catalog; juan 6–7 the clinical-syndrome chapters with point-selection; juan 8 the special-needling and moxibustion techniques (the *Sòng'ér jiāchuán zhēnfǎ* 宋兒家傳針法 etc.); juan 9 paediatric needling; juan 10 Yáng's own clinical case-records. The work was reprinted dozens of times in the late Míng and Qīng, translated and adapted into Japanese and Korean acupuncture curricula, and is the principal source for almost all modern Chinese-medicine acupuncture pedagogy. The first edition (1601) was a Píngyáng commercial print; the SKQS recension is from a Qīng *Yītǒng zhèngmài* 醫統正脈 reprint.

## Other points of interest
The *Zhēnjiǔ dàchéng* is the single most-translated Chinese medical text into modern European languages and the principal source-text of all modern East-Asian acupuncture practice.
