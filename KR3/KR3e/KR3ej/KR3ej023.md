# Lúxìn jīng 顱顖經
*Classic of the Cranium and Fontanelle*
anonymous (attributed traditionally to a *Shīwū* 師巫 / [[衛沈]] Wèi Shěn lineage)

## About the work

A two-*juǎn* short paediatric *jīng* 經 (canonical classic) of uncertain authorship, conventionally dated to the late Táng — early Sòng period (c. 600–1100). The work's own preface traces a legendary transmission: from the *Huángdì nèizhuàn* 黃帝內傳 of the Yellow Emperor, kept in the *jīnguì* 金匱 (golden coffer) and revealed only to King Mù of Zhōu's *xiánshì shīwū* 賢士師巫 (worthy-scholar shaman-priests) on Mt Kōngtóng 崆峒. The actual textual history is obscure: [[柯逢時]] Kē Féngshí's 1910 collation note to the *Xiǎo'ér wèishēng zǒngwēi lùnfāng* ([[KR3ej017]]) identifies the *Lúxìn* as "肇於東漢衛沈，而成於宋人錢仲陽" (originated with [[衛沈]] Wèi Shěn of the Eastern Hàn and brought to completion by [[錢乙]] Qián Zhòngyáng [Qián Yǐ] of the Sòng) — a transmission story that combines legendary authentication with the historical role of Qián Yǐ as the principal paediatric tradition-shaper. The work is among the earliest *xiǎoér* monographs preserved in the canon and is one of the principal sources for the early Chinese paediatric doctrines of *biànzhèng* 變蒸 (developmental crises), *jīngxián* 驚癇, *gān* 疳, and *dòuzhěn* 痘疹.

### Prefaces

The front-matter contains the work's own preface (*yuánxù* 原序). The preface frames the work as derived from the Yellow-Emperor canon: *fú lúxìn zhě, wèi tiāndì yīnyáng huà gǎn lúxìn, gù shòu míng yě* 夫顱顖者，謂天地陰陽化感顱囟，故受名也 (the *lúxìn* — cranium-and-fontanelle — takes its name from the heaven-earth yin-yang transformation that affects the infant skull and fontanelle). It then recounts the *Huángdì nèizhuàn* / Mt Kōngtóng legendary transmission, the *zhēnyīn cuòzá* 真陰錯雜 doctrine of paediatric pathology (true *yīn* disordered, leading to congenital constitutional anomalies), and frames the work's purpose as supplying the *zhēnpíng biànzhèng, nǎi dìng sǐshēng* 真憑辨證，乃定死生 (genuinely grounded *biànzhèng* to determine life-or-death). The preface contains the doctrine that maternal pulse during pregnancy reveals fetal sex (*yángshèng fā yīn* 陽盛發陰, *dāng rèn nán* 當妊男 — *if yang predominates and yin is brought out, [a] male [is being] gestated*).

### Abstract

The *Lúxìn jīng* is one of the earliest extant Chinese paediatric texts, surviving through citation in the Sòng *Tàipíng shènghuì fāng* 太平聖惠方 and *Yòuyòu xīnshū* 幼幼新書 ([[KR3ej010]]) and as a standalone two-*juǎn* work in the *Sìkù quánshū* 四庫全書 recension (where it is classified under *zǐbù yījiā lèi* 子部醫家類). The work covers: *biànzhèng* developmental cycles, *jīngfēng* 驚風 nosology in an early form, *gān* chronic-malnutrition syndromes, *dòuzhěn* eruptive fevers, the *qífēng* 臍風 (umbilical wind, neonatal tetanus) emergency, and the standard miscellaneous paediatric disorders. Its principal historical significance is as the earliest extant systematic statement of the Chinese paediatric *zàngfǔ* and *biànzhèng* doctrines, which Qián Yǐ subsequently refined and rebuilt into the standard post-Sòng paediatric synthesis. Date bracket: the lower bound (c. 600) is conservative for the legendary Hàn-Táng layer; the upper bound (c. 1100) is the *terminus ante quem* set by Qián Yǐ's citation of the work and the early Sòng *Tàipíng shènghuì fāng* incorporation. The catalog meta's *Sòng* dynasty attribution reflects the standard bibliographic placement; the work itself is best understood as a *yǐnxíng* (hidden-author) Táng-Sòng compilation incorporating older Hàn-Táng paediatric material.

## Other points of interest

The work's title contains a graphical-vocabulary curiosity: the character 顖 (*xìn*) is a Sòng-era variant of the more common 囟 (*xìn*, fontanelle); some recensions of the work title use 顱囟經 with the simpler form. The textual history of *Lúxìn jīng* is intertwined with that of the early-Sòng *xiǎoér* paediatric corpus, and several modern philological studies have argued that the received text incorporates several distinct chronological layers (a Táng core, a Sòng editorial expansion, possibly a late-Sòng *biànzhèng* addition).
