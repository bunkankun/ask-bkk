# Nèifǔ mìchuán jīngyàn nǚkē 內府秘傳經驗女科
*Inner-Palace Secretly-Transmitted Clinical Experience in Gynecology*
by [[龔廷賢]] (Gōng Tíngxián = Gōng Zǐcái = Yúnlín Shānrén, 1522–1619, late Míng)

## About the work

A three-*juǎn* gynecology compendium attributed to the major late-Míng physician-publisher Gōng Tíngxián 龔廷賢 of Jīnxī 金谿 (Jiāngxī). The work is structured as a numbered series of *lùn* 論 (case-treatises) on common gynecological conditions, beginning with *yuèjīng qiánqī lùn* 月經前期論 (1), *yuèjīng hòuqī lùn* 月經後期論 (2), and proceeding through menstrual disorders, *bēnglòu* 崩漏, *dàixià* 帶下, *qiúzǐ* 求子, *tāiqián* 胎前, *chǎnhòu* 產後, and *fùrén zábìng* 婦人雜病. Each entry combines a brief symptomatic description with an étiological gloss and one or two prescribed formulas given with full drug compositions and dosages. The title *Nèifǔ mìchuán jīngyàn* — "Inner-Palace secretly-transmitted clinical experience" — reflects Gōng Tíngxián's brief tenure at the *Tàiyīyuàn* 太醫院 as *lìmù* 吏目 (clerk) during the Wànlì reign and his strategic literary use of imperial-court medical-knowledge prestige.

### Prefaces

The KR `hxwd` recension carries no separable preface; the text begins directly with the *Yuèjīng qiánqī lùn* heading. The "*Nèifǔ mìchuán*" framing is internal to the work's title.

### Abstract

Gōng Tíngxián (1522–1619, see [[龔廷賢]]) was the most prolific late-Míng physician-publisher and the principal vehicle for the dissemination of Míng clinical doctrine in popular print. His major works — the [[萬病回春]] *Wànbìng huíchūn* (1587) and [[壽世保元]] *Shòushì bǎoyuán* (1615) — were among the most reprinted Chinese medical texts of the late Míng. The *Nèifǔ mìchuán jīngyàn nǚkē* is one of his series of specialised compilations targeting specific medical sub-fields (gynecology, paediatrics, *shānghán*, etc.) issued under his Yùnlínshānrén 雲林山人 imprint. The dating: Gōng's medical-publishing career began in the 1580s and continued to his death (traditionally 1619); the gynecology compilation is best bracketed within this range, with `notBefore` 1580 / `notAfter` 1619.

Doctrinally the work applies the standard late-Míng [[李東垣]] Lǐ Dōngyuán *Bǔzhōng yìqì tāng* spleen-stomach framework combined with the [[朱丹溪]] Zhū Dānxī *Xiāoyáo sǎn* / *Sìwù tāng* liver-blood framework. The *Tiáojīng wán* 調經丸 (Menstrual-Regulating Pill), *Zǐjīn wán* 紫金丸 (Purple-Gold Pill), and *Huángqín sǎn* 黃芩散 are prescribed as central formulas. The work's formulary closely overlaps that of Gōng's *Shòushì bǎoyuán* gynecology section, suggesting the *Nèifǔ mìchuán nǚkē* was extracted, reorganised, and re-published from his larger encyclopedias for specialised circulation. This kind of editorial *re-presentation* of his own material under different titles was a signature feature of Gōng's commercial publishing strategy.

The text circulated in late Míng and Qīng popular medical book markets in multiple imprints and survives in numerous editions; the `hxwd` recension is one of the Qīng popular reprints preserved in overseas Chinese-medicine library holdings.

## Other points of interest

The "*Nèifǔ mìchuán*" ("Secretly transmitted from the inner [imperial] court") framing is itself a piece of late-Míng commercial publishing strategy: it claims privileged imperial-medical access for a work whose contents in fact draw on a broader Sòng-Yuán-Míng synthesis. Comparable Gōng titles include *Tàiyī shēnxiào shíèr fēngbàngbēi yīxué* and various other works styled with imperial-medical prestige markers. The phenomenon is a key example of late-Míng commercial medical publishing's use of court-prestige branding.
