# Tāichǎn zhǐnán 胎產指南
*A Compass for Pregnancy and Childbirth*
by [[單南山]] (Shàn Nánshān, *fl.* mid–late 18th c.)

## About the work

An eight-*juǎn* obstetrical manual of the late Qiánlóng / Xiánfēng eras, organised as a practical handbook covering all the standard categories of pre-partum (*tāiqián*), parturient (*línchǎn*), and post-partum (*chǎnhòu*) gynecology and obstetrics. The work's therapeutic axis is explicitly stated in its prefatory matter — "for pre-partum disorders one takes the Dānxī *Āntāi yǐn* 安胎飲 as the principal formula; for post-partum disorders one takes the *Shēnghuà tāng* 生化湯 as the principal formula" — placing it squarely in the late-Míng / Qīng *Shēnghuà tāng* obstetrical orthodoxy descending from Fù Qīngzhǔ. Its appeal in popular practice rested on its brevity and the directness of its case-and-prescription format, allowing even a non-pulsologist to "look up the symptom, check the formula, follow the procedure" (對症檢方，依法調治). Its title alludes to the legendary "south-pointing chariot" (*zhǐnán chē* 指南車) presented by the Duke of Zhōu to the Yuècháng emissaries — a compass for the lost in a region of obstetrical danger.

### Abstract

Per the 1856 (Xiánfēng *bǐngchén*) preface of [[周丹忱]] Zhōu Dānchén of Sìmíng 四明 (Níngbō), the work originated as a manuscript circulated under the name of one Shàn Nánshān 單南山, "whose native place and personal name can no longer be ascertained." It first came to wide use through [[錢升穟]] Qián Shēngsuì of Yuèjùn 越郡 (Shàoxīng), who, having obtained the secret manuscript, gained province-wide fame as an obstetrician. In *Qiánlóng guǐsì* (1773) the manuscript was acquired in Níngbō by [[伊學曾]] Yī Xuézēng (originally from Sìmíng), who in turn became famous for obstetrics in his native district. After Yī Xuézēng's death the manuscript passed to his nephew [[蔡益亭]] Cài Yìtíng (*míngjīng* graduate), who circulated copies; his protégé [[歐孔章]] Ōu Kǒngzhāng of Hùxī 滬西 ultimately commissioned the wood-block edition, with [[陳彩盅]] Chén Cǎizhōng of Tiāntái 天台 as its editor. The blocks were cut at the Ōu family Zhōngxiàn dì 中憲第 mansion in 1856. The edition added Yī Xuézēng's clinical case-notes *Nǚkē yīàn* 女科醫案 (7 *juǎn*) as appended companion text.

The composition window for the underlying *Zhǐnán* manuscript itself is therefore best taken as the mid-18th century at latest — before Qián Shēngsuì's adoption (Shàn Nánshān must precede Qián, who is documented active in Yuèjùn before 1773). The 1856 print is its first surviving recension, and `notBefore`/`notAfter` are bracketed to cover the period from c. 1750 through to first publication. Shàn Nánshān is also credited with the companion work *Pánzhū jí tāichǎn zhèngzhì* 盤珠集胎產症治 ([[KR3ei049]]) — the two works circulate as a paired set in the Hǎiwài huíguī series.

The catalog meta gives no person beyond Shàn Nánshān; the editorial figures Zhōu Dānchén, Chén Cǎizhōng, Yī Xuézēng, Cài Yìtíng, and Ōu Kǒngzhāng are mentioned in the prose body and linked but not in the work-note frontmatter, in conformity with the project rule.
