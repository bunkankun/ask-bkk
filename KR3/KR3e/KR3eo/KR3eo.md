## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR3eo` 養生類 (*Yǎngshēng lèi*, Nourishing Life) is the sub-division of the Masters → Medicine branch (`KR3e` 醫家類) that gathers manuals on the prolongation of life, dietary therapy (*shíliáo* 食療 / *shízhì* 食治), guided-gymnastic and breathing exercises (*dǎoyǐn* 導引, *xíngqì* 行氣), massage (*ànmó* 按摩), inner-alchemical *xìngmìng* 性命 cultivation, and the care of the aged (*yǎnglǎo* 養老). Where `KR3e` proper preserves the clinical-medical mainstream from the *Sìkù quánshū* 四庫全書 imperial library (`source: WYG`), every text in `KR3eo` instead derives from the *Hǎiwài huíliú zhōngyī shànběn gǔjí cóngshū* 海外回流中醫善本古籍叢書 (`source: 漢學文典`), the modern repatriation series edited by [[鄭金生]] Zhèng Jīnshēng that has gathered Chinese medical *shànběn* preserved abroad (especially in Japan and Korea) and returned to circulation rare editions that the Qiánlóng-era Sìkù editors had either rejected or never seen. The division therefore exhibits two distinguishing features: (i) a chronological centre of gravity shifted later than the `KR3e` clinical mainstream — Míng and Qīng witnesses dominate, with a strong Wànlì-era nucleus around the publisher–anthologist [[胡文煥]] Hú Wénhuàn; and (ii) a generic latitude that admits non-canonical genres (illustrated *dǎoyǐn* charts, cosmetic manuals, dietary almanacs, monthly almanacs, *xìngmìng* alchemical primers) that the Sìkù editors had relegated to 道家 or excluded altogether.

The genre's intellectual centre of gravity lies in Endymion Wilkinson's *Chinese History: A New Manual* Section 41.4 (Self-cultivation), particularly §41.4.2 (Longevity Techniques) and §41.4.3 (Fitness), with substantial overlap into §36.1 (Food). The transmission tradition runs from the Hàn–Wèi *xíngqì* and *fángzhōng* 房中 fragments preserved in the Mǎwángduī silks and the Zhāngjiāshān *Yǐn shū* 引書 (sources outside this division), through the Suí–Táng synthesis attempted by [[孫思邈]] Sūn Sīmiǎo, the Liǎng-Sòng anthological *yǎngshēng* of [[周守忠]] Zhōu Shǒuzhōng and [[陳直]] Chén Zhí, the Yuán imperial dietetics of [[忽思慧]] Hū Sīhuì, the Míng *cóngshū* programme of Hú Wénhuàn and the synthetic *yīxué* projects of [[萬全]] Wàn Quán, down to the Qīng dietary critique ([[王士雄]] Wáng Shìxióng, [[章穆]] Zhāng Mù), external therapy ([[吳師機]] Wú Shījī), and the inner-alchemical popularisation that runs from [[馬齊]] Mǎ Qí to [[汪東亭]] Wāng Dōngtíng. The Bodhidharma-attributed *Yìjīn jīng* 易筋經 / *Xǐsuǐ jīng* 洗髓經 corpus, late-Qīng to early-Republican in its received form, sits at the boundary with martial-arts literature (Wilkinson §26.15.1).

## Important texts and text clusters

**Foundational Táng witnesses.** Two extracts from Sūn Sīmiǎo's *Qiānjīn* corpus stand at the head of the division:
- *Shèyǎng zhěnzhōng fāng* 攝養枕中方 [[KR3eo029]] — Sūn Sīmiǎo's "pillow-side" longevity manual, the foundational late-Táng synthesis of self-cultivation, breath-regulation, and pharmacology in a *yǎngshēng* register.
- *Qiānjīn shízhì* 千金食治 [[KR3eo038]] — the food-therapy chapter of the *Bèijí qiānjīn yàofāng*, the earliest systematic statement of the *yīshí tóngyuán* 醫食同源 principle that governs subsequent *shíliáo* literature.

**The geriatric (養老) tradition of Chén Zhí 陳直.** The single most coherent cluster in the division, transmitted in four overlapping recensions:
- *Yǎnglǎo fèngqīn shū* 養老奉親書 [[KR3eo001]] — Chén Zhí's original Northern-Sòng monograph (1078–1085), the earliest extant Chinese geriatric work.
- *Shòuqīn yǎnglǎo shū* 壽親養老書 [[KR3eo016]] — Chén Zhí's four-juan original recension.
- *Shòuqīn yǎnglǎo xīn shū* 壽親養老新書 [[KR3eo031]] — Zōu Xuàn's Yuán expansion (c. 1297–1307) adding three further juan; the same text is preserved at [[KR3e0021]] in the WYG.
- *Ānlǎo huáiyòu shū* 安老懷幼書 [[KR3eo032]] — a composite Sòng–Yuán–Míng recension supplemented by Huáng Yìngzǐ 黃應紫 with paediatric materials.

**Sòng anthological *yǎngshēng*.** Zhōu Shǒuzhōng's two foundational compendia, both compiled in the Jiādìng 嘉定 era (1208 onward):
- *Yǎngshēng lèizuǎn* 養生類纂 [[KR3eo022]] — the great Southern-Sòng topical anthology in 22 juan, re-issued by Hú Wénhuàn.
- *Yǎngshēng yuèlǎn* 養生月覽 [[KR3eo024]] — the standard month-by-month *yǎngshēng* almanac.

**Yuán imperial dietetics and dietary literature.** The greatest single Mongol-Yuán contribution to the genre is the imperial dietary office's pair:
- *Yǐnshàn zhèngyào* 飲膳正要 [[KR3eo040]] — Hū Sīhuì's 1330 imperial dietary manual presented to Wénzōng 文宗, with Yú Jí 虞集's preface; the most important pre-modern Chinese cookbook and the source from which the bulk of Mongol-Yuán culinary practice can be reconstructed.
- *Shíliáo fāng* 食療方 [[KR3eo039]] — Hū's companion volume on food therapy proper.
- *Yǐnshí xūzhī* 飲食須知 [[KR3eo044]] — Jiǎ Míng's late-Yuán manual of dietary avoidances and incompatibilities; together with the *Cháoshì bìngyuán bǔyǎng xuāndǎo fǎ* 巢氏病源補養宣導法 [[KR3eo033]] and the *Sānyuán cānzàn yánshòu shū* 三元參贊延壽書 [[KR3eo012]] of Lǐ Péngfēi, it forms the Yuán *yǎngshēng* corpus proper.

**Míng synthetic *yǎngshēng*.** Two clusters dominate the Míng:
- The *qì*-monist literatus tradition: *Shèshēng yàoyì* 攝生要義 [[KR3eo015]] by Wáng Tíngxiāng 王廷相 (writing as Hébīn zhàngrén 河濱丈人, c. 1530–1544); the early-Míng *Hòushēng xùnzuǎn* 厚生訓纂 [[KR3eo009]] (Zhōu Chén 周臣, 1549); and the *Yǎngshēng lèiyào* 養生類要 [[KR3eo004]] (Wú Zhènglún 吳正倫, 1564).
- The Wàn Quán 萬全 synthesis (Húběi family physician, 1549–1582): *Yǎngshēng sìyào* 養生四要 [[KR3eo025]] and the monumental versified clinical compendium *Bǎomìng gēkuò* 保命歌括 [[KR3eo026]] (35 juan), the largest Míng *yǎngshēng*-clinical synthesis in the division.

**The Hú Wénhuàn 胡文煥 publishing programme (Wànlì, c. 1590–1602).** Hú Wénhuàn — Hángzhōu literatus, *Wénhuìtáng* 文會堂 publisher, and the most prolific Wànlì-era anthologist — is responsible for an unusually large share of the division (eleven titles), preserving short *yǎngshēng* and esoteric medical manuals that would otherwise be lost: the inner-cultivation *Lèixiū yàojué* 類修要訣 [[KR3eo011]] and *Shèshēng jílǎn* 攝生集覽 [[KR3eo014]]; the dietary *Yǎngshēng shíjì* 養生食忌 [[KR3eo042]]; the cosmetic-and-female-health *Xiānglián rùnsè* 香奩潤色 [[KR3eo020]]; the *Tàisù* pulse-prognostication pair *Tàisù màijué mìshū* 太素脈訣秘書 [[KR3eo018]] and *Tàisù xīnyào* 太素心要 [[KR3eo019]]; the medical-introductory *Yīxué quányú* 醫學權輿 [[KR3eo027]]; and the two principal *dǎoyǐn* manuals in the division, *Yǎngshēng dǎoyǐn mìjí* 養生導引秘籍 [[KR3eo034]] and *Yǎngshēng dǎoyǐn fǎ* 養生導引法 [[KR3eo036]]. Hú's programme is the principal mechanism by which Sòng-Yuán *yǎngshēng* short-form literature survived into the modern period.

**Illustrated *dǎoyǐn* and the body of the immortal.** *Bǎoshēng xīnjiàn* 保生心鑑 [[KR3eo041]] ([[鐵峰居士]] Tiěfēng Jūshì, 1505–1506), with 32 illustrated *chénzhī* figures, is the principal Míng illustrated *dǎoyǐn* manual in the division. *Jǐnshēn jīyào* 錦身機要 [[KR3eo010]] ([[混沌子]] Hùndùnzǐ) and the *Xīnyìn gànzhū jīng* 心印紺珠經 [[KR3eo021]] ([[李湯卿]] Lǐ Tāngqīng, Yuán) complete the metaphysically inflected branch of the tradition. *Xiūzhēn mìyào* 修真秘要 [[KR3eo023]] ([[王蔡]] Wáng Cài, 1513–1515) and *Fúshòu dānshū* 福壽丹書 [[KR3eo030]] ([[龔居中]] Gōng Jūzhōng, 1610–1632) carry the inner-alchemical *xìngmìng* programme into the late Míng.

**Qīng dietary and external therapy.** A late-imperial cluster of analytical, clinical-dietary, and external-therapy works:
- *Suíxījū yǐnshí pǔ* 隨息居飲食譜 ([[KR3eo043]]) (Wáng Shìxióng, 1861) — the most influential nineteenth-century critical dietary manual, by the [[溫熱經緯]] *Wēnrè jīngwěi* author.
- *Tiáojí yǐnshí biàn* 調疾飲食辯 ([[KR3eo045]]) (Zhāng Mù) and *Shíwù jíyào* 食物輯要 ([[KR3eo046]]) (Mù Shìxī) — the discriminating-dietary scholarship.
- *Jíjiù guǎngshēng jí* 急救廣生集 ([[KR3eo047]]) (Chéng Péngchéng, c. 1800–1830) and *Lǐyuè piánwén* 理瀹駢文 ([[KR3eo048]]) (Wú Shījī, 1864–1870) — the founding texts of the modern *wàizhìfǎ* 外治法 (external-therapy) sub-discipline; the latter is the canonical theoretical statement of plaster-and-poultice therapy.

**Qīng inner-alchemical popularisation and longevity manuals.** *Yǎngshēng mìzhǐ* 養生秘旨 [[KR3eo005]] (Mǎ Qí, Kāngxī Manchu Grand Secretary), *Shòushì qīngbiān* 壽世青編 [[KR3eo006]] ([[尤乘]] Yóu Chéng, 1666–1690), *Shòushì chuánzhēn* 壽世傳真 [[KR3eo002]] ([[徐文弼]] Xú Wénbì, 1768–1771), *Fèngshí zhǐyào* 奉時旨要 [[KR3eo008]] ([[江涵]] Jiāng Hán), *Shòushì biān* 壽世編 [[KR3eo017]] (Qīngpǔ Zhū jūnzǐ 青浦諸君子), *Xiū Kūnlún zhèngyàn* 修崑崙證驗 [[KR3eo003]] ([[天休子]] Tiānxiūzǐ, 1846–1847), and *Xìngmìng yàozhǐ* 性命要旨 [[KR3eo007]] (Wāng Dōngtíng, 1880s) — late-Qīng workshop on the inner-alchemy / *xìngmìng* programme as transmitted to literate non-specialists.

**Massage and the Bodhidharma exercises.** *Dònggōng ànmó mìjué* 動功按摩秘訣 [[KR3eo035]] (the Wāng brothers Qǐshèng 啟聖 and Qǐxián 啟賢, eighteenth century) is the principal Qīng massage manual. *Dámó xǐsuǐ yìjīn jīng* 達摩洗髓易筋經 [[KR3eo037]] preserves, in early-twentieth-century editorial form, the late-imperial Bodhidharma-attributed *Yìjīn jīng* / *Xǐsuǐ jīng* corpus that links *yǎngshēng* with the martial-arts tradition of the Shàolínsì 少林寺.

## Important persons

- [[孫思邈]] (Sūn Sīmiǎo, c. 581–682) — the Táng polymath whose *Qiānjīn* corpus supplies both the foundational *yǎngshēng* methodology ([[KR3eo029]]) and the foundational dietary-therapy programme ([[KR3eo038]]).
- [[巢元方]] (Cháo Yuánfāng, fl. 605–615) — the Suí imperial physician whose disease-aetiology classic furnished the source-base for the Sòng-compiled *Cháoshì bìngyuán bǔyǎng xuāndǎo fǎ* [[KR3eo033]].
- [[陳直]] (Chén Zhí, fl. 1078–1085) — Northern-Sòng magistrate of Xīnghuàxiàn, the founder of Chinese geriatric medicine and the central author of the division's *yǎnglǎo* cluster ([[KR3eo001]] [[KR3eo016]] [[KR3eo031]] [[KR3eo032]]).
- [[鄒鉉]] (Zōu Xuàn, fl. 1297–1307) — Yuán expander of Chén Zhí's geriatric corpus, responsible for the form in which it became canonical ([[KR3eo031]] [[KR3eo032]]).
- [[周守忠]] (Zhōu Shǒuzhōng, fl. 1208–1224) — Southern-Sòng anthologist whose two *yǎngshēng* compendia ([[KR3eo022]] [[KR3eo024]]) supply the topical and calendrical templates for all later *yǎngshēng* literature.
- [[李鵬飛]] (Lǐ Péngfēi, fl. 1288–1291) — Yuán author of the *Sānyuán cānzàn yánshòu shū* [[KR3eo012]], the principal Yuán synthetic *yǎngshēng* treatise.
- [[忽思慧]] (Hū Sīhuì, fl. 1325–1330) — Mongol-Yuán imperial dietary officer (*yǐnshàn tàiyī* 飲膳太醫); author of [[KR3eo039]] [[KR3eo040]], the foundational pre-modern Chinese dietetic manuals.
- [[賈銘]] (Jiǎ Míng, c. 1269–1374) — Yuán-Míng centenarian, author of *Yǐnshí xūzhī* [[KR3eo044]], the standard manual of dietary incompatibilities.
- [[王廷相]] (Wáng Tíngxiāng, 1474–1544) — Míng Minister of War and *qì*-monist philosopher; author of [[KR3eo015]] under the sobriquet Hébīn zhàngrén, the one work in the division that integrates *yǎngshēng* into a coherent philosophical position.
- [[萬全]] (Wàn Quán, c. 1499–1582) — Húběi family physician; the most prolific Míng *yǎngshēng*-clinical synthesiser ([[KR3eo025]] [[KR3eo026]]).
- [[胡文煥]] (Hú Wénhuàn, c. 1551–1607) — Hángzhōu literatus and Wénhuìtáng publisher; responsible for editing, printing, or re-issuing eleven titles in the division and for the survival of much of its short-form Sòng-Yuán content ([[KR3eo011]] [[KR3eo014]] [[KR3eo018]]–[[KR3eo020]] [[KR3eo022]] [[KR3eo027]] [[KR3eo034]] [[KR3eo036]] [[KR3eo042]]).
- [[鐵峰居士]] (Tiěfēng Jūshì, fl. 1505–1506) — the most consequential Míng illustrated *dǎoyǐn* author ([[KR3eo041]]).
- [[龔居中]] (Gōng Jūzhōng, fl. 1610–1632) — late-Míng inner-alchemical and clinical compiler; *Fúshòu dānshū* [[KR3eo030]] is his principal *yǎngshēng* work.
- [[馬齊]] (Mǎ Qí, 1652–1739) — Kāngxī era Manchu Grand Secretary; his compiled *Yǎngshēng mìzhǐ* [[KR3eo005]] is a high-Qīng court-elite document of *yǎngshēng* practice.
- [[尤乘]] (Yóu Chéng, c. 1620–1690) — Sūzhōu physician; author of *Shòushì qīngbiān* [[KR3eo006]], a much-reprinted Kāngxī *yǎngshēng* manual.
- [[徐文弼]] (Xú Wénbì, fl. 1768–1771) — author of *Shòushì chuánzhēn* [[KR3eo002]], the most popular Qiánlóng-era *yǎngshēng* compendium.
- [[王士雄]] (Wáng Shìxióng, 1808–1868) — author of *Suíxījū yǐnshí pǔ* [[KR3eo043]] and the principal dietetic critic of the high-Qīng *Wēnbìng* school.
- [[章穆]] (Zhāng Mù, fl. 1810–1830) — dietary discrimination ([[KR3eo045]]).
- [[程鵬程]] (Chéng Péngchéng, fl. 1800–1830) — author of *Jíjiù guǎngshēng jí* [[KR3eo047]], a pioneering external-therapy compendium.
- [[吳師機]] (Wú Shījī, 1806–1886) — founder of the modern *wàizhì* (external-therapy) sub-discipline ([[KR3eo048]]).
- [[汪東亭]] (Wāng Dōngtíng, fl. 1880s) — late-Qīng inner-alchemical popularisation ([[KR3eo007]]).

## Topics

The division clusters around a small number of recurring problems and debates.

**Dietary therapy and *yīshí tóngyuán* 醫食同源.** The single most continuous thematic strand. From Sūn Sīmiǎo's *Qiānjīn shízhì* [[KR3eo038]] through the Yuán imperial *Yǐnshàn zhèngyào* [[KR3eo040]] / *Shíliáo fāng* [[KR3eo039]] and Jiǎ Míng's *Yǐnshí xūzhī* [[KR3eo044]], to the Míng *Yǎngshēng shíjì* [[KR3eo042]] and *Shíwù jíyào* [[KR3eo046]], and culminating in the Qīng analytical works of Wáng Shìxióng ([[KR3eo043]]) and Zhāng Mù ([[KR3eo045]]). The progression is from compiled list to discriminating critique. Wilkinson §36.1 #6 and §41.4.2 are the principal Western entry points.

**Guided gymnastics (導引) and breath-cultivation (行氣).** From the Sòng compilation of Cháo Yuánfāng's *dǎoyǐn* materials ([[KR3eo033]]), through the illustrated Míng manuals ([[KR3eo041]]), the Hú Wénhuàn *dǎoyǐn* programme ([[KR3eo034]] [[KR3eo036]]), the *ànmó* manual of the Wāng brothers ([[KR3eo035]]), down to the Bodhidharma corpus ([[KR3eo037]]). Wilkinson §41.4.3 is the standard scholarly orientation; the modern translations of Livia Kohn and Vivienne Lo are the principal entry points for the Mǎwángduī–Zhāngjiāshān excavated background.

**Care of the elderly (養老).** The Chén Zhí–Zōu Xuàn lineage ([[KR3eo001]] [[KR3eo016]] [[KR3eo031]] [[KR3eo032]]) is the principal Chinese geriatric tradition before the modern era; its thesis that "food therapy surpasses pharmacotherapy for the aged" defines the field.

**Inner alchemy (內丹) and *xìngmìng* 性命 cultivation.** The non-clinical pole of the division — works that read *yǎngshēng* as a programme of inner-alchemical refinement rather than as preventive medicine. *Xīnyìn gànzhū jīng* [[KR3eo021]], *Jǐnshēn jīyào* [[KR3eo010]], *Xiūzhēn mìyào* [[KR3eo023]], *Fúshòu dānshū* [[KR3eo030]], *Xiū Kūnlún zhèngyàn* [[KR3eo003]], *Xìngmìng yàozhǐ* [[KR3eo007]]. The boundary with the Dàojiā 道家 sub-division is porous and inheritances run in both directions.

**Monthly and seasonal regimens.** The almanac branch — Zhōu Shǒuzhōng's *Yǎngshēng yuèlǎn* [[KR3eo024]] is the prototype; Jiāng Hán's *Fèngshí zhǐyào* [[KR3eo008]] is the major Qīng exemplar. The cosmological premise is that *qì* flows differently through each lunar month and each of the twenty-four *jiéqì* solar nodes, and that diet, conduct, and exercise must vary accordingly.

**The literatus *yǎngshēng* tradition.** A distinctive strand running from Sū Shì 蘇軾 (whose *Sū-Shěn liángfāng* [[KR3e0020]] sits in `KR3e` proper) through Wáng Tíngxiāng ([[KR3eo015]]) to the Qīng scholar-officials Mǎ Qí ([[KR3eo005]]) and the *Qīngpǔ Zhū jūnzǐ* circle ([[KR3eo017]]). These works are characterised by philosophical self-consciousness and prose elegance rather than by clinical-medical authority.

**External therapy (外治法).** The late-Qīng *wàizhìfǎ* programme — plaster, poultice, fumigation, acupressure as substitutes for internal pharmacotherapy. Chéng Péngchéng ([[KR3eo047]]) and Wú Shījī ([[KR3eo048]]) are the founding figures. Wú's *Lǐyuè piánwén*, written entirely in parallel-prose, is the theoretical statement; it has acquired renewed interest in twentieth-century *zhōngyī* practice.

**The *Tàisù* pulse tradition (太素脈).** A heterodox Míng prognostic-pulse tradition that read fortune, character, and lifespan from pulse-pattern, preserved here in Hú Wénhuàn's pair ([[KR3eo018]] [[KR3eo019]]) — the principal extant Wànlì-era witnesses to a tradition that the orthodox Sìkù *yījiā* sub-category explicitly excluded.

**Repatriation philology (海外回流).** A specifically modern topic: the division as a whole is the product of the Zhèng Jīnshēng-led *漢學文典* publishing programme, which has returned to circulation Míng-Qīng *yǎngshēng* editions long preserved only in Japanese (Naikaku Bunko, Sonkeikaku) and Korean (Kyujanggak) collections. The textual recensions transmitted in `KR3eo` are accordingly often earlier or fuller than the WYG / `KR3e` parallels — most strikingly for the Chén Zhí corpus ([[KR3eo001]] vs. [[KR3e0021]]).
