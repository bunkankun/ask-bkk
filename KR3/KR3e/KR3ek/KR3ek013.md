# Yángyī dàquán 瘍醫大全
*Complete Compendium of Ulcer Medicine*
by [[顧世澄]] (Gù Shìchéng, also Gù Chéng 顧澄; *zì* Liànjiāng 練江; *hào* Jìngzhāi 靜齋, *fl.* Qiánlóng, 清) — third-generation hereditary physician of Wúhú 蕪湖 (Ānhuī), practising in Yángzhōu 揚州.

## About the work

A forty-*juǎn* surgical encyclopedia, the most comprehensive single Qīng *wàikē* reference and the largest pre-modern Chinese surgical compendium overall. Compilation completed in Qiánlóng 25 (1760), additional prefaces added in 1762 and 1773 (the printing date). Gù was the third generation of a hereditary medical family, preserving his grandfather Nínghuá 寧華's and father Qīngyán 青岩's secret prescriptions alongside his own and an exhaustive citation of the prior surgical literature. The work was conceived as a *wàikē* counterpart to the imperial [[KR3ek009]] *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* (1742) and was financially sponsored by fellow Huái-region physicians.

### Abstract

Three prefaces frame the work in the Kanripo digitisation: that of Wāng Lìdé [[汪立德]] (1773), the author's own *zìxù* dated *Qiánlóng yǐmào* (1760), and that of Qiáo Guāngliè [[喬光烈]] (1762), plus a fifteen-item *fánlì* 凡例. Gù explicitly describes the work as inspired by the imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* compilation effort and conceived as a defining *wàikē* synthesis bridging *nèijīng* doctrinal foundations, pulse-doctrine, channel topography, and topographically organised surgical cases.

The forty *juǎn* divide as follows: *juǎn* 1–7, classical theory, pulses, channels, principles of inner pattern; *juǎn* 8–35, topographically ordered ailments from crown to sole, each with woodcut illustration, classical formula, master commentary, and family secret recipes; *juǎn* 36–40, paediatric and obstetric surgery, including a smallpox section drawn from Niè Jiǔwú [[聶九吾]] and Zhū Chúnhè [[朱純嘏]], trauma (knife / fire / falls), animal bites and toxic ingestions, with a closing *Hán mén* 寒門 (cold-damage gateway) capsule for emergency reference. Every lesion is illustrated, making the work the largest single-author illustrated Chinese surgical compendium.

The methodological centre of the work is its systematic completion of the *nèiwài jiānzhì* programme inaugurated by Chén Zìmíng's [[KR3ek012]] *Wàikē jīngyào* (1263), codified by Chén Shígōng's [[KR3ek014]] *Wàikē zhèngzōng* (1617), and given imperial sanction in the *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* (1742). Where Chén Shígōng pulled the *wàikē* tradition toward operative surgery and Wáng Hóngxù's [[KR3ek017]] *Quánshēng jí* (1740) pulled it sharply toward internal tonification, Gù Shìchéng's synthesis sits comprehensively in the middle. The *Yángyī dàquán* remained the standard Qīng *wàikē* desk reference into the Republican period.

Gù's lifedates are not documented; he speaks of forty-plus years of practice in his 1760 preface, suggesting birth in the early 18th century.

## Other points of interest

The work has been valued by historians of Chinese surgical iconography for its over one thousand woodcut lesion illustrations — among the largest single illustrated corpora in pre-modern Chinese medicine.
