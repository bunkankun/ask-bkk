## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR3es` 醫史類 (*Yī shǐ lèi*, Medical History, Biography, and Bibliography) is the sub-division of `KR3e` 醫家類 (Masters → Medicine) that gathers the principal meta-medical works of the East-Asian tradition — texts whose subject is not the practice of medicine but the **history, prosopography, bibliography, and doxography** of medicine. As with the other lettered sub-divisions of `KR3e`, every text in `KR3es` derives from the *Hǎiwài huíliú zhōngyī shànběn gǔjí cóngshū* 海外回流中醫善本古籍叢書 (`source: hxwd`), the modern repatriation series edited by Zhèng Jīnshēng 鄭金生 et al. (Běijīng: Rénmín wèishēng chūbǎnshè, 2003), which has restored to Chinese-language circulation rare *shànběn* witnesses preserved in Japanese, Korean, and European holdings. The division is small (six titles) but unusually international: three of the six are works in literary Chinese composed by late-Edo Japanese physicians ([[KR3es003]], [[KR3es004]], [[KR3es006]]), reflecting the fact that the systematic philological and prosopographical study of Chinese-medical history was carried out in Edo Japan more intensively than in late-imperial China, and that the *Hǎiwài huíliú* programme was designed precisely to bring this Edo-philological inheritance back into Chinese-language scholarship.

The genre's frame in Endymion Wilkinson's *Chinese History: A New Manual* is Section 41.7 (Medical History: Research), in particular §41.7.1 (Overviews), §41.7.5 (Twentieth-Century China — the institutional struggle in which [[KR3es005]] intervenes), and §41.7.8 (Bibliographies — where the *Zhōngguó yījí kǎo* [[KR3es003]] is the foundational pre-modern resource). Hinrichs & Barnes, *Chinese Medicine and Healing: An Illustrated History* (Harvard 2013), provides the principal English-language survey of the institutional and intellectual contexts traced by the works in this division. The chronological span (c. 1186 to c. 1925) brackets the foundational Jīn-period *qǐyuán* doctrinal-historical treatise of the Yìshuǐ school against the Republican-era *zhōngyī* historiographical manifesto of the Shanghai Mènghé current; the centre of gravity, however, lies in the bakumatsu Tamba / Asada / Imamura circle of the *Igaku-kan* 醫學館, which produced three of the six titles within a fifteen-year span (1850–1865).

## Important texts and text clusters

**The *qǐyuán* / *yuánliú* "tracing-to-origins" genre.** Three of the six works construct themselves as origin-narratives, in two distinct but related senses:
- *Yīxué qǐyuán* 醫學啟源 [[KR3es001]] (Zhāng Yuánsù, c. 1186) — the foundational doctrinal treatise of the Yìshuǐ 易水 school and the title-template for the genre. Although not historiographical in the strict modern sense, the work's programmatic claim to re-found medicine on the *Nèijīng* and the *wǔyùn liùqì* doctrine and its polemical insistence that "*gǔfāng* must not be applied to *xīnbìng* unaltered" make it the doctrinal-historical pivot on which the JīnYuán reform of medicine turned, and it is included here in that capacity.
- *Yīshì qǐyuán* 醫事啟源 [[KR3es004]] (Imamura Ryōan, 1861) — a polemical exercise in comparative historiography that traces every supposedly novel Western surgical or pharmacological technique (anatomy, catheterisation, blister therapy, leech blood-letting, smallpox inoculation) to a Chinese textual antecedent in the *Sùwèn*, *Língshū*, and *Qiānjīn fāng*. The work explicitly invokes Zhāng Yuánsù's *qǐyuán* title-template, claiming the same mantle for its own late-Edo *kanpō* defence against *rangaku*.
- *Zhōngguó yīxué yuánliú lùn* 中國醫學源流論 [[KR3es005]] (Xiè Guān, 1924–1925) — the Republican-era historian's manifesto, surveying "the source streams (源) of each tradition and the branches (流) into which they divided" across four thousand years. Volker Scheid (2007) calls it "the foundational Republican-era history of Chinese medicine."

**Bakumatsu Tamba / Asada / Imamura *kanpō* philology.** The three Edo-Japanese works in `KR3es` were produced within a single intellectual circle around the shogunal *Igaku-kan* 醫學館 in the 1810s–1860s and define the late-Edo *kǎojù igaku* 考証醫學 tradition:
- *Zhōngguó yījí kǎo* 中國醫籍考 / *Yījí kǎo* 醫籍考 [[KR3es003]] (Taki Motoin 多紀元胤, substantially completed 1819, printed 1831/1832) — an eighty-juan annotated bibliography of the entire Chinese medical corpus, organised in seven categories (*yījīng*, *běncǎo*, *shízhì*, *cángxiàng*, *zhěnfǎ*, *míngtáng jīngmài*, *fānglùn*), each title accompanied by transmission status and a chronologically arranged anthology of bibliographic-historical notices. The single most exhaustive pre-modern bibliography of Chinese medicine and the foundation of all subsequent medical-bibliographic scholarship.
- *Huángguó míngyī zhuàn* 皇國名醫傳 / *Kōkoku meii den* [[KR3es006]] (Asada Sōhaku 淺田惟常, *qiánbiān* 3 juan 1850–51 + *Kyōrin zawa* 杏林雜話 appendix 1865) — the principal late-Edo prosopographical history of Japanese medicine, covering physicians from mythological antiquity through the bakumatsu *kǎoshèng* 考証 schools. The prosopographical pendant to Motoin's bibliographical *Yījí kǎo*.
- *Yīshì qǐyuán* [[KR3es004]] (already noted above) — Asada wrote the *bá* 跋 colophon to this work, marking the close personal collaboration between Imamura and Asada.

**Medical doxography.** *Gǔjīn míngyī huìcuì* 古今名醫彙粹 [[KR3es002]] (Luó Měi, 1675) is the principal early-Qīng florilegium of medical doctrine and case practice, drawn from physicians of the Hàn through the early Qīng. Organised in three parts (*Lùn jí* 論集 / *Mài jí* 脈集 / *Bìngnéng jí* 病能集) totalling eight juan, it opens with [[張介賓]] Zhāng Jièbīn's *Dà bǎo lùn* 大寶論 and is doctrinally aligned with the late-Míng *wēnbǔ* / *mìngmén* programme; its companion *Gǔjīn míngyī fānglùn* 古今名醫方論 (later partially absorbed into Wú Qiān's *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* of 1742, see [[KR3e0090]]) was the most popular pre-modern Chinese medical anthology.

## Important persons

- [[張元素]] (Zhāng Yuánsù, *zì* Jiégǔ 潔古, c. 1151–1234) — founder of the Yìshuǐ 易水 school of JīnYuán medicine, author of [[KR3es001]] *Yīxué qǐyuán*. His doctrines of *yǐnjīng yào* 引經藥 (channel-guiding drugs) and *qìwèi hòubó* 氣味厚薄 / *shēngjiàng fúchén* 升降浮沉 became the basis of the Yìshuǐ-school materia medica transmitted through [[李杲]] Lǐ Gǎo and [[王好古]] Wáng Hǎogǔ to [[KR3e0057]] *Tāngyè běncǎo*.
- [[羅美]] (Luó Měi, *zì* Dànshēng 澹生, *fl.* Kāngxī) — Xīn'ān 新安 (Huīzhōu) physician, compiler of the *Gǔjīn míngyī huìcuì* [[KR3es002]] and its more famous formulary companion *Gǔjīn míngyī fānglùn*.
- [[多紀元簡]] (Taki Mototane, 1755–1810) — shogunal *oku-i* 奧醫師, founding director of the *Igaku-kan*, and the initiator of the *Yījí kǎo* project completed by his son.
- [[丹波元胤]] (Taki Motoin, 1789–1827) — Mototane's son, principal compiler of the [[KR3es003]] *Zhōngguó yījí kǎo*. The pivotal figure in the bakumatsu *kǎojù igaku* tradition.
- [[丹波元堅]] (Taki Motokata, 1795–1857) — Motoin's younger brother, supervised the posthumous 1831/1832 printing of the *Yījí kǎo*.
- [[今邨亮]] (Imamura Ryōan 今村了庵, 1814–1890) — late-Tokugawa shogunal *oku-i* and *kanpō* philologist, author of [[KR3es004]] *Yīshì qǐyuán*. Subsequently retained as imperial physician after the 1868 Restoration.
- [[淺田惟常]] (Asada Sōhaku 浅田宗伯, *imina* Korehisa 惟常, *hào* Ritsuen 慄園, 1815–1894) — the foremost *kanpō* physician of the bakumatsu and Meiji periods, author of [[KR3es006]] *Kōkoku meii den* and of more than eighty other medical titles. Also imperial physician after 1868.
- [[謝觀]] (Xiè Guān, *zì* Lìhéng 利恆, 1880–1950) — founding principal of the Shanghai College of Traditional Chinese Medicine, chief author of the 1921 *Zhōngguó yīxué dà cídiǎn* 中國醫學大辭典, and lead actor in the November 1929 protest against the *Fèizhǐ jiùyī àn* 廢止舊醫案. Author of [[KR3es005]] *Zhōngguó yīxué yuánliú lùn*.
- [[秦伯未]] (Qín Bówèi, 1901–1970) and [[張贊臣]] (Zhāng Zànchén, 1904–1993) — Xiè Guān's chief disciples, compilers of the *Xiè Lìhéng yīxué cóngshū* 謝利恆醫學叢書 (1935) in which the *Yuánliú lùn* first appeared in print, and the next generation's leadership of Shanghai TCM institutions.
- [[張介賓]] (Zhāng Jièbīn, 1563–1640) — *Jǐngyuè* 景岳, the late-Míng *wēnbǔ* thinker whose *Dà bǎo lùn* opens Luó Měi's *Huìcuì* [[KR3es002]] and whose doctrinal programme dominates its selection (see also [[KR3e0085]]–[[KR3e0086]] for his major works).
- [[薛己]] (Xuē Jǐ, 1487–1559) — Míng imperial physician, the original articulator of the *wēnbǔ* / *mìngmén* doctrine reproduced in the gynaecology section of [[KR3es002]].

## Topics

The division is organised around several distinctive themes:

**Medical historiography as a genre.** The works gathered here document the late-imperial East-Asian invention of medical history as a self-conscious genre. The earliest model, Zhāng Yuánsù's *qǐyuán* [[KR3es001]], frames historiography as **doctrinal genealogy**: tracing the present discipline back to a foundational classical source (the *Nèijīng*–*Sùwèn* corpus) and rejecting the formulary medicine of the Sòng pharmacy bureaus as a degeneration from it. Later works generalise: Luó Měi's [[KR3es002]] becomes systematic doxography, Motoin's [[KR3es003]] systematic bibliography, Asada's [[KR3es006]] systematic prosopography, Imamura's [[KR3es004]] systematic comparative origin-tracing, and Xiè Guān's [[KR3es005]] systematic narrative history. The *qǐyuán* / *yuánliú* template runs through all six.

**The bakumatsu *kǎojù igaku* programme.** The single most concentrated cluster in `KR3es` is the bakumatsu Tamba / Asada / Imamura circle of the shogunal *Igaku-kan* in the 1810s–1860s. Their method — combining Qing-dynasty Chinese *kǎojù* textual philology with the Tamba family's hereditary medical archive and Japanese access to titles long since lost in mainland China — produced what is still today the most exhaustive corpus of pre-modern medical-historical scholarship. The three works in `KR3es` ([[KR3es003]], [[KR3es004]], [[KR3es006]]) define the genre.

**Pre-Restoration *kanpō* self-defence against *rangaku*.** Imamura's [[KR3es004]] (1861) was composed in the year the bakufu elevated the Itō Genboku vaccination clinic into the official *Seiyō Igakusho* 西洋醫學所 (the precursor of the Tokyo University medical faculty), and Asada's [[KR3es006]] *Kyōrin zawa* prefaces (1865, 1872) defend the choice to write in *hanbun* on the principle that "all learning derives from the Chinese land" 學術皆原乎漢土. Both works are documentary samples of the late-Edo *kanpō* tradition's intellectual self-positioning against the rising *rangaku* establishment, and their rhetorical strategy — assimilating European technique to native classical antecedents — is identical in form to the mid-Meiji arguments mounted in the 1880s in defence of *kanpō* against state abolition.

**Republican *zhōngyī* self-definition.** Xiè Guān's [[KR3es005]] (1924–1925) is the Chinese-side pendant to the bakumatsu *kanpō* self-defence: written from inside the institutional struggle for the survival of Chinese medicine, against the 1929 *Fèizhǐ jiùyī àn* and the universalising medical ideology of the League of Nations health office. Its periodisation — *Xīzhōu*–Hàn / *WèiJìn*–Táng / Sòng–Míng / Qīng-onward — became, through Xiè's disciples [[秦伯未]] and [[張贊臣]], the implicit frame of mid-twentieth-century mainland Chinese medical historiography.

**The *Wēnbǔ* / *Mìngmén* doctrinal alignment.** Luó Měi's [[KR3es002]] is the principal early-Qīng demonstration that medical doxography can be sectarian: although Luó reproduces the cooling positions of Liú Wánsù and Zhū Zhènhēng in full, his structural decision to open the *Lùn jí* with Zhāng Jièbīn's *Dà bǎo lùn* and to lead the gynaecology section with Xuē Jǐ's *jīngbìng* discussion makes the *wēnbǔ* alignment unambiguous. Read against [[KR3e0086]] Zhāng's *Jǐngyuè quánshū* and [[KR3er001]] Zhào Xiànkě's *Yīguàn*, [[KR3es002]] is the historiographical articulation of the same doctrinal programme.

**The *Hǎiwài huíliú* repatriation programme.** The hxwd series of which `KR3es` is part was assembled specifically to recover materials of this kind — Edo-period Japanese medical-historical scholarship long preserved in Japanese libraries but lost or inaccessible in China. The fact that three of the six titles in this division are bakumatsu Japanese, and that the principal Chinese-medical bibliography of the past two centuries was compiled in Edo rather than in Beijing, is itself the principal historiographical lesson of `KR3es`.

## Timeline

- 1186–1234 — *Yīxué qǐyuán* 醫學啟源 [[KR3es001]] — Zhāng Yuánsù's foundational Yìshuǐ-school doctrinal-historical treatise, the *qǐyuán* title-template for the genre.
- 1675 — *Gǔjīn míngyī huìcuì* 古今名醫彙粹 [[KR3es002]] — Luó Měi's early-Qīng eight-juan medical doxography aligned with the *wēnbǔ* programme; first printed only in 1801 after 126 years of manuscript circulation.
- 1799–1819 — *Zhōngguó yījí kǎo* 中國醫籍考 [[KR3es003]] — Taki Mototane's and Taki Motoin's bakumatsu eighty-juan annotated bibliography of the Chinese medical corpus; printed posthumously by Taki Motokata in 1831/1832.
- 1850–1865 — *Huángguó míngyī zhuàn* 皇國名醫傳 [[KR3es006]] — Asada Sōhaku's bakumatsu prosopographical history of Japanese medicine, *qiánbiān* (3 juan, 1851) plus *Kyōrin zawa* appendix (1865 / 1872 prefaces).
- 1861 — *Yīshì qǐyuán* 醫事啟源 [[KR3es004]] — Imamura Ryōan's bakumatsu twenty-essay polemic tracing European medical technique to Chinese classical antecedents; *bá* by Asada Sōhaku, marking the close *kanpō*-philological collaboration of the Tamba / Asada / Imamura circle.
- 1924–1925 — *Zhōngguó yīxué yuánliú lùn* 中國醫學源流論 [[KR3es005]] — Xiè Guān's Republican-era historical-doctrinal manifesto for the survival of *zhōngyī*; first integrally printed 1935 in the *Xiè Lìhéng yīxué cóngshū*.
