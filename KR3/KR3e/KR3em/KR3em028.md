# Hóukē jíyè 喉科集腋
*Gleanings on Laryngology*
by [[沈青芝]] Shěn Qīngzhī (late-Qīng physician of Lìyáng 溧陽).

## About the work

A two-*juǎn* late-Qīng laryngology compendium — *shàngjuǎn* covering theory, needling technique, and the diagnostic typology of severe and acute throat conditions, *xiàjuǎn* covering the prescriptions. The author's stated aim is the practical preservation of life in the face of epidemic *báihóu fēng* 白喉風 outbreaks, supplemented with a comprehensive review of the inherited laryngology corpus. The text combines orthodox doctrinal teaching (the *yānhóu* anatomical division, the *zhēnfǎ* 針法 at the standard hand- and face-acupoints — *shǎoshāng* 少商, *shāngyáng* 商陽, *zhōngchōng* 中衝, *guānchōng* 關衝, *shǎochōng* 少衝, *jiáchē* 頰車, *qūchí* 曲池, *hégǔ* 合谷 — with a *zhúrì xuèshén* 逐日血神 daily-prohibition calendar restricting the use of each acupoint), the typology of severe conditions (*hóubì* 喉痹, *hóufēng* 喉風, *chánhóu fēng* 纏喉風, *báihóu fēng* 白喉風, *lànhóu shā* 爛喉痧, *yīnxū hóulóng* 陰虛喉嚨), and a detailed discussion of the 19th-century *báihóu* epidemics.

### Prefaces

The work is prefaced by a *xiǎoxù* 小序 by [[方燕昭]] Fāng Yànzhāo (*zì* Bóróng 伯融, of Dìngyuǎn 定遠), the author's friend of ten years' standing, dated *Guāngxù* 7 *xīnsì* 辛巳, first month of autumn (1881), written "at the Wēncuì xuān 溫翠軒 of his temporary residence at Hánshàng" 邗上 (Yángzhōu 揚州). Fāng records that he had repeatedly asked Shěn for his laryngology methods without success until Shěn, declaring himself aged, finally entrusted him with the volume saying "the heart's-blood of my entire life is in this." A subsequent self-preface (*zì xù* 自序) by Shěn Qīngzhī himself, dated *Guāngxù* *gēngyín* 庚寅 (1890) mid-winter, signed "from the Jújǐng Shānfáng 橘井山房 at Guǎnglíng 廣陵", recounts the *Qiánlóng* 54 (1789) outbreak of *báihóu fēng* in the north, the *Xiánfēng* 6 (1856) outbreak that swept both north and south, and the catastrophic *Guāngxù* *gēngyín* (1890) outbreak in the Yángzhōu region in which "of every ten or twenty cases, only two or three survived." Shěn reports having declined an offer of payment from Fāng for the publication of the work, electing to release it freely to the world.

### Abstract

*Hóukē jíyè* is one of the major late-Qīng laryngology compendia, and — alongside [[KR3em027]] *Báihóu quánshēng jí* of [[李紀方]] (1882) — one of the principal indigenous-medical responses to the diphtheria epidemics that swept south China in the 1880s and 1890s. Shěn Qīngzhī's etiological theory is distinctive in late-imperial laryngology: he argues that *báihóu fēng* arises when the "lung — which belongs to metal and whose colour is white — falls under conditions of summer drought combined with secondary yīn-damp infection, like dry wood that grows mushrooms after long time"; hence "wind-drying drugs do not save, only cooling-moistening prescriptions can preserve life." He explicitly criticizes the use of *shēngtí xīnwēn fēngzào suānsè* 升提辛溫風燥酸澀 (uplifting/pungent-warm/wind-drying/sour-astringent) classes of drug in *báihóu*, and grounds his own prescribing in the transformations of the *lóngdǎn xiègān tāng* 龍膽瀉肝湯, *báihǔ tāng* 白虎湯, *pǔjì xiāodú yǐn* 普濟消毒飲, *xījiǎo dìhuáng tāng* 犀角地黃湯, and *huánglián jiědú tāng* 黃連解毒湯, modified to exclude exactly those four prohibited drug-classes.

Shěn Qīngzhī is not recorded in CBDB and his life-dates are not securely fixed. He was a senior physician of Lìyáng 溧陽 (Jiāngsū) practising in the Zhènjiāng / Yángzhōu region; his self-preface implies a working life of "several decades" reaching from the 1850s through the 1890 epidemic. The composition window is therefore set at 1860 (a defensible *terminus a quo* for sustained adult practice) – 1890 (the date of his self-preface, the latest stratum in the received text). The preface by Fāng Yànzhāo gives a *terminus ante quem* of 1881 for an earlier draft of the work, but the integrated *shàngxià* 上下 two-*juǎn* recension carrying the 1890 *gēngyín* preface is what survives in the present witness.

## Other points of interest

The text's "*shínán* 十難 [ten difficulties]" discussion — listing ten clinical problems in differential diagnosis of *báihóu* — is cited approvingly as "a method that may stand as the model for later students." Shěn's pointed dating of the three major *báihóu* epidemics he had heard of or lived through (1789, 1856, 1890) is among the more precise epidemic-chronicling passages in the late-Qīng medical corpus.
