# Zábìng guǎngyào 雜病廣要
*Comprehensive Essentials of Miscellaneous Diseases*
by [[丹波元堅]] (Tamba no Motokata 丹波元堅 / Taki Genken, *zì* Yìróu 亦柔, *hào* Chítíng 茝庭, 1795–1857)

## About the work

The principal philological compendium of clinical syndrome literature compiled in the late-Edo Bakufu medical academy (*Igakukan* 醫學館). Tamba Motokata draws on more than three hundred pre-Qing Chinese medical texts — close to 800,000 *zì* in total — and organises them under five rubrics: *wàiyīn* 外因 (external causes), *nèiyīn* 內因 (internal causes), *qìxuè* 氣血, *zàngfǔ* 臟腑, *shēntǐ* 身體, each subdivided into a total of seventy-three *mén* 門 across forty *juǎn*. For each disease the work provides terminology, etiology, pathomechanism, pulse and symptom signs, differential diagnosis, treatment principles and methods, prescriptions, prevention, and convalescent regulation. The work is the *zábìng* 雜病 companion to Motokata's earlier *Shānghán guǎngyào* 傷寒廣要 (12 *juǎn*).

### Abstract

Tamba Motokata, the youngest son of [[丹波元簡]] (Tamba no Mototane / Taki Mototane) and the senior figure in the Tamba philological circle after his elder brother's early death, was the principal exponent of the late-Edo *kōshōgaku* 考證學 movement in Chinese-medical philology. He served the Tokugawa shōgunate as *oku-ishi* 奥醫師 and was elevated to the rank of *hōin* 法印 in 1841.

The composition history is fully recorded in Motokata's preface, dated the 18th day, third month of *Ansei* 3 = 1856 (note: the user-prompt date of 1853 is too early; 1856 is correct), written at the *Cùnchéng yàoshī* 寸誠藥肆 in Edo. He began drafting in *Wùshēn* 戊申 = 1848, after the completion of the *Shānghán guǎngyào*, with an original plan covering nine rubrics — adding *qīqiào* 七竅 (seven orifices), *wàikē* 外科 (external medicine), *fùkē* 婦科 (women's medicine), and *xiǎoérkē* 小兒科 (paediatrics) to the five-rubric scheme — but by *Bǐngchén* 丙辰 = 1856 he had reached only the *shēntǐ* 身體 rubric, with forty *juǎn* and seventy-three *mén* completed. The text as we have it is therefore the substantial completed half of an unfinished larger project.

The *Zábìng guǎngyào* is the apex of Edo *kōshōgaku* medicine: an exhaustive-philological, source-comparative, lightly-annotated digest in which Tamba's editorial voice is mostly suppressed in favour of the original sources, while the selection-and-arrangement quietly enacts the school's preference for early sources over late, for *zhèngzhì* 證治 (syndrome-and-treatment) literature over speculative cosmology, and for the Han-Tang formula tradition as the doctrinal anchor. The companion to [[真柳誠]] Mayanagi Makoto's modern critical-bibliographical work on the Tamba philological corpus.

## Other points of interest

The 1856 preface explicitly acknowledges the work's incompleteness, and the modern Chinese reception has occasionally treated the absence of the surgical, gynaecological and paediatric volumes as a major loss; Mayanagi has argued that drafts of these may have been lost in the chaos of the Bakumatsu years. The work was rapidly received in Qīng-period Sūzhōu and Hángzhōu medical circles where its philological method offered an alternative to the *wēnbǔ* / *wēnbìng* doctrinal disputes that dominated late-Qīng Chinese medical writing.
