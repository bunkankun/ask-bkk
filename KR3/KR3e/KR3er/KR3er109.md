# Shěnshì zūnshēng shū 沈氏尊生書
*Master Shěn's Book on Honouring Life*
by [[沈金鰲]] Shěn Jīnáo (*zì* Qínyuán 芹源, *hào* Zūnshēng lǎorén 尊生老人 / Rètiān cǎotáng 熱天草堂, 1717–1776, Wúxī 無錫, Chángzhōu prefecture, Jiāngsū).

## About the work

A 72-*juǎn* comprehensive medical compendium — actually a *cóngshū* 叢書 (collected works) of eight separate but interrelated medical treatises — composed by the polymath Wúxī physician Shěn Jīnáo over decades and conventionally dated to *Qiánlóng* 38 / 1773 (the date of the publication preface). The eight constituent works, organised as a unified curriculum, are:

1. ***Màixiàng tǒnglèi*** 脈象統類 (1 *juǎn*, the opening *juǎnshǒu* 卷首 of the present hxwd recension): Shěn's pulse-classification monograph, organising the 28 (or 30, in some recensions) classical pulse-categories under the six grand rubrics *fúchénchíshùhuásè* 浮沉遲數滑澀 — the basis of Shěn's diagnostic-system reform.

2. ***Zhū mài zhǔbìng shītú*** 諸脈主病詩圖 (1 *juǎn*): the verse mnemonic supplement to the *Màixiàng*.

3. ***Zábìng yuánliú xīzhú*** 雜病源流犀燭 (30 *juǎn*): the great miscellany of internal-medicine pathomechanism — Shěn's principal doctrinal-and-clinical work, organised by disease-category with detailed *yuánliú* 源流 (etiology-and-development) discussion.

4. ***Shānghán lùn gāngmù*** 傷寒論綱目 (16 *juǎn*): Shěn's systematic re-organisation of Zhāng Zhòngjǐng's *Shānghán lùn* under the *gāngmù* (outline-and-items) format, with a heavy assimilation of Wú Jūtōng's *Wēnbìng tiáobiàn* protocols.

5. ***Yàoyào fēnjì*** 藥要分劑 (10 *juǎn*): Shěn's materia-medica, organised by the *qīfāng shíjì* 七方十劑 (seven-formula-types, ten-formulary-functions) classification of the Sòng tradition.

6. ***Fùkē yùrěn*** 婦科玉尺 (6 *juǎn*): obstetrics-and-gynaecology — Shěn's principal women's-medicine work, widely cited in subsequent Qīng gynaecological literature.

7. ***Yòukē shìjīngbǎo lù*** 幼科釋謎 (6 *juǎn*): paediatrics.

8. ***Yàofāng dàilěi*** 藥方代累 (2 *juǎn*): a closing formulary appendix.

(The *Sìkù quánshū* committee characterised the *Zūnshēng shū* as one of the most ambitious individually-authored medical compendia of the high Qīng.)

The work's title — *zūnshēng* 尊生 ("honouring life") — invokes the *Zhuāngzǐ* 莊子 *Shànshēng* 善生 ("benefiting life") tradition and the early-Qīng *yǎngshēng* 養生 literature, but Shěn's emphasis is firmly clinical rather than longevity-philosophical: the doctrinal preliminaries, the pulse-diagnosis, and the formulary together constitute a complete clinical-pedagogical curriculum.

Shěn's clinical-doctrinal position is an eclectic synthesis: he draws on the late-Míng / early-Qīng *Mìngmén* / *xiāntiān* tradition (Xuē Jǐ → Zhào Xiànkě → Zhāng Jièbīn) for the constitutional-fortifying framework; on the *Sì dàjiā* for the differentiation of clinical patterns; on Yè Tiānshì and Wú Jūtōng for the *wēnbìng* protocols; and on his own clinical experience for the specific drug-and-formula recommendations. The eclecticism is characteristic of the high-Qīng (Qián-lóng-era) mainstream medical curriculum.

### Abstract

The 1773 *editio princeps* dating of the unified *Zūnshēng shū* is the consensus modern figure; individual constituent works were composed over a longer period (the *Zábìng yuánliú xīzhú* and *Màixiàng* are documentable to the 1760s). Shěn was a *jǔrén* (provincial-examination graduate) of *Qiánlóng* 9 / 1744 but never advanced further in the examination track; he practised medicine in Wúxī and acquired major patronage from the Qián-lóng-era Chángzhōu cultural establishment.

CBDB has Shěn Jīnáo at c_personid 0099034.

The work was reprinted multiple times in the late Qīng and early Republican periods and remains a standard reference in Chinese-medical curricula today.

## Translations and research

*No full European-language translation of the* Shěn-shì zūn-shēng shū *located*. Particular chapters of the *Fù-kē yù-rěn* have been studied: Charlotte Furth, *A Flourishing Yin: Gender in China's Medical History 960–1665* (California, 1999), and Yi-Li Wu, *Reproducing Women: Medicine, Metaphor, and Childbirth in Late Imperial China* (California, 2010). For Shěn's place in high-Qīng Jiāng-nán medicine see Marta Hanson, *Speaking of Epidemics* (Routledge, 2011), and Volker Scheid, *Currents of Tradition* (Eastland, 2007).

## Other points of interest

The work's 72-*juǎn* scope and integrated curricular organisation make it one of the half-dozen most-ambitious individually-authored Chinese medical compendia (alongside the *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng* 證治準繩 of Wáng Kěntáng, the *Jǐngyuè quánshū* of Zhāng Jièbīn, the *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* 醫宗金鑑 imperial compilation, and the *Féngshì jǐnnáng mìlù* of Féng Zhàozhāng).
