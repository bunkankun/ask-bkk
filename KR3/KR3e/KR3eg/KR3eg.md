## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3eg is the **warm-disease** (*wēnbìng* 溫病) and **epidemic-disease** (*wēnyì* 瘟疫) subdivision within the Kanripo medicine class (KR3e 醫家類, *yījiā lèi*, "Masters → Medicine"). All 50 texts in the division are drawn from the jicheng.tw 漢學文典 digital collection that compiles the wēnbìng canon together with adjacent epidemic-medical literature. The texts collected here address those acute, externally-contracted febrile conditions which the late-imperial Chinese-medical tradition came to recognise as etiologically distinct from the cold-injury (*Shānghán* 傷寒) framework systematised by Zhāng Jī 張機 in the late Hàn — a recognition first crystallised in the late Míng with [[吳有性]]'s *Wēnyì lùn* ([[KR3eg004]], 1642) and elaborated across the Qīng into a doctrinal apparatus parallel to, and ultimately co-equal with, the *Shānghán lùn* tradition. Adjacent disease categories included in the same subdivision are summer-heat (*shǔbìng* 暑病), miasma-malaria (*zhàngnüè* 瘴瘧), *shā* 痧 distension disorders, cholera-like *huòluàn* 霍亂, bubonic plague (*shǔyì* 鼠疫), smallpox (*dòukē* 痘科), dysentery (*lìjí* 痢疾), and assorted folk-medical pestilential categories.

The chronological span runs from the late Míng (鄭全望's 1609 *Zhàngnüè zhǐnán*, 張鳳逵's 1623 *Shāngshǔ quánshū*, and Wú Yǒuxìng's 1642 *Wēnyì lùn*) through the entire Qīng — during which the wēnbìng synthesis matures in the Sūzhōu-Hángzhōu axis (Yè–Xuē–Wú Jūtōng–Wáng Mèngyīng) and is then transmitted into a contested Republican-era doctrinal field — to the late Republican period (王德宣's 1935 *Wēnbìng zhèngzōng*). The division includes four Edo / Bakumatsu-period Japanese *kanpō* 漢方 contributions (KR3eg022, 025, 045, 046), all transmitted back to China via 湯本求真 Tāngběn Qiúzhēn's *Huáng Hàn yīxué cóngshū* 皇漢醫學叢書 (Shanghai, 1936), and several late-Qīng / Republican texts preserved through 裘慶元 Qiū Qìngyún's *Sānsān yīshū* 三三醫書 (Hángzhōu, 1924). Wilkinson §41.7.4 (late-imperial medicine) and especially §41.7.6 (epidemics) frame the secondary literature; Marta Hanson's *Speaking of Epidemics in Chinese Medicine* (Routledge, 2011) is the standard English-language monograph and is the principal reference for almost every per-text note in this division. T. J. Hinrichs and Linda L. Barnes (eds.), *Chinese Medicine and Healing* (Harvard Belknap, 2013), pp. 156, 203–207 provides the standard concise English overview.

## Important texts and text clusters

**The canonical four Qīng *wēnbìng* monographs.** The mid-Qīng synthesis crystallises around four foundational texts, all preserved here together with their principal commentaries:
- *Wēnrè lùn* 溫熱論 ([[KR3eg001]]) of [[葉桂]] Yè Tiānshì — the kernel-document of the *wèiqìyíngxuè* 衛氣營血 four-fen staging doctrine. Critically annotated by Chén Guāngsōng in *Wēnrè lùn jiānzhèng* ([[KR3eg016]]).
- *Shīrè tiáobiàn* 濕熱條辨 ([[KR3eg020]]) of [[薛雪]] Xuē Shēngbái — foundational treatise on damp-warm pathology; versified by [[王旭高]] Wáng Xùgāo in *Xuē shì shīrè lùn gējué* ([[KR3eg015]]).
- *Wēnyì lùn* 溫疫論 ([[KR3eg004]]) of [[吳有性]] Wú Yǒuxìng — the late-Míng pestilential-qi (*lìqì* 戾氣) doctrine, the source-document of the entire wēnyì current.
- *Wàigǎn wēnbìng piān* 外感溫病篇 ([[KR3eg009]]) of [[陳平伯]] Chén Píngbó — the canonical treatment of *fēngwēn* 風溫 (wind-warm) and the locus classicus of cool-releasing therapy.

**The mid-Qīng *summa* texts.** *Wēnbìng tiáobiàn* 溫病條辨 ([[KR3eg010]]; alternate recension [[KR3eg011]]) of [[吳塘]] Wú Jūtōng establishes the *sānjiāo* 三焦 (three-burner) staging doctrine and the prescription apparatus (*Yínqiào sǎn* 銀翹散, *Sāngjú yǐn* 桑菊飲, *Āngōng niúhuáng wán* 安宮牛黃丸) that remains operational in modern TCM. *Wēnrè jīngwěi* 溫熱經緯 ([[KR3eg008]]) of [[王士雄]] Wáng Mèngyīng is the mid-19th-century compendium that transmitted Yè, Xuē, Chén, and Yú Lín into the late-Qīng curriculum and remains the principal modern textbook entry-point to the canonical four.

**The Wú–Dài–Liú line of *wēnyì* extension.** Three generations adapt Wú Yǒuxìng's pestilential-qi doctrine to successive regional and clinical contexts: [[戴天章]] Dài Tiānzhāng's *Guǎng wēnyì lùn* ([[KR3eg023]], 1675, Jiāngníng / Sūzhōu) supplies the *wǔjiān shíjiá* 五兼十夾 syndrome schema and the working clinical apparatus; [[劉奎]] Liú Kuí's *Sōngfēng shuō yì* ([[KR3eg039]], 1785, Shāndōng) extends the framework to northern epidemic conditions; the late-Qīng / Republican re-edition by [[陸懋修]] Lù Màoxiū and [[何廉臣]] Hé Liánchén appears as *Chóngdìng Guǎng wēnrè lùn* ([[KR3eg006]]). The Sūzhōu physician [[周揚俊]] Zhōu Yángjùn's earlier *Wēnrè shǔyì quánshū* ([[KR3eg032]], 1679) provides the conceptual bridge between Wú and the mature Yè-Tiānshì synthesis. [[余霖]] Yú Lín's *Yìzhěn yīdé* ([[KR3eg031]], 1785) supplies the gypsum-centred *Qīngwēn bàidú yǐn* 清瘟敗毒飲 prescription, the parallel and partial alternative to Wú's *dàhuáng* purgative protocol.

**The summer-heat / *shǔbìng* cluster.** [[張鳳逵]] Zhāng Fèngkuí's *Zēngdìng Yèpíng Shāngshǔ quánshū* ([[KR3eg005]], base text 1623) is the first Chinese monograph dedicated specifically to summer-heat as a distinct disease category, with later Yè Tiān-shì-attributed annotations and a Dào-guāng-era expansion. [[李識候]] Lǐ Shíhòu's *Shǔzhèng fāyuán* ([[KR3eg047]]) traces the *Nèijīng* doctrinal sources.

**The latent-pathogen (*fúqì* / *fúxié*) cluster.** A late-Qīng / Republican doctrinal current concentrating on warm-disease conditions caused by pathogens dormant within the body before clinical manifestation: [[柳寶詒]] Liǔ Bǎoyí's *Wēnrè féngyuán* ([[KR3eg003]]), [[劉吉人]] Liú Jírén's *Fúxié xīnshū* ([[KR3eg028]]), and [[蔣樹杞]] Jiǎng Shùqǐ's *Fúwēn zhèngzhì shíyàn tán* ([[KR3eg027]]) — the three are transmitted together through Qiū Qìngyún's *Sānsān yīshū* (1924).

**The cholera-response cluster.** Three mid-to-late-19th-century Chinese-medical responses to the Asian cholera pandemics that reached China in the 1820s: [[王士雄]] Wáng Mèngyīng's *Suíxījū chóngdìng huòluàn lùn* ([[KR3eg040]], 1838 / 1862) — the heat-aetiology argument, the most influential of the three; [[田宗漢]] Tián Zōnghàn's *Yījì fúyīn lùn* ([[KR3eg014]], 1854) — the *fúyīn* analytical category; and [[許志]] Xǔ Zhì's *Huòluàn ránxī shuō* ([[KR3eg042]]).

**The southern-frontier / miasma-malaria cluster.** [[鄭全望]] Zhèng Quánwàng's *Zhàngnüè zhǐnán* ([[KR3eg038]], 1609) is the principal late-Míng monograph on miasma-malaria, continuing the Sòng *Zhàngnüè wèishēng fāng* tradition and introducing climatic-historical reasoning about the northward expansion of the *zhàng* zone. [[郭志邃]] Guō Zhìsuì's *Shāzhàng yùhéng* ([[KR3eg037]], 1675) is the canonical reference for the *shā* 痧 disease category. [[宋兆淇]] Sòng Zhàoqí's *Nánbìng biéjiàn* ([[KR3eg029]], 1878) makes explicit the geographic-particularist argument that southern Jiāngnán climate produces a clinical picture requiring its own doctrinal framework.

**Plague.** [[羅汝蘭]] Luó Rǔlán's *Shǔyì yuēbiān* ([[KR3eg041]], 1891–1894) is the principal Chinese-medical monograph composed in immediate response to the 1890–1894 Guǎngdōng outbreak that, transmitted to Hong Kong in May 1894, initiated the Third Plague Pandemic.

**The Edo / Bakumatsu Japanese *kanpō* cluster.** Four Japanese contributions transmitted to China through 湯本求真 Yumoto Kyūshin (1876-194)'s *Huáng Hàn yīxué cóngshū* (Shanghai, 1936): [[秋吉質]] Akiyoshi Tadashi's *Wēnyì lùn shīpíng* ([[KR3eg022]]), an Edo commentary on Wú Yǒuxìng; [[源元凱]] Minamoto Genkai's *Wēnbìng zhī yánjiū* ([[KR3eg025]], 1788–1810), incorporating the Tenmei 8 (1788) Kyoto epidemic and the original rigid / soft pathogen (*gō-ja* / *jū-ja* 剛邪 / 柔邪) doctrinal innovation; [[高島久貫]] Takashima Hisanuki's *Xièyì xīnlùn* ([[KR3eg045]]) on the Bunsei 5 (1822) and Ansei 5 (1858) cholera waves; [[池田瑞仙]] Ikeda Zuisen's *Dòukē biànyào* ([[KR3eg046]], 1780–1816), the principal Edo treatise on smallpox, by the Bakufu Medical Academy specialist.

**The pedagogical / mnemonic cluster.** Three *gējué* / *gēkuò* versifications of the canonical wēnbìng monographs by [[王旭高]] Wáng Xùgāo: *Xuē shì shīrè lùn gējué* ([[KR3eg015]]) of *Shīrè tiáobiàn*; *Wú Yòukě wēnyì lùn gēkuò* ([[KR3eg024]]) of Wú Yǒuxìng; *Wēnyì míngbiàn gējué* ([[KR3eg033]]) of Dài Tiānzhāng's *Guǎng wēnyì lùn* (under the pirated-edition title). Plus the late-Qīng pedagogical primers [[婁傑]] Lóu Jié's *Wēnbìng zhǐnán* ([[KR3eg007]], 1903), [[馬宗元]] Mǎ Zōngyuán's *Wēnbìng biànzhèng* ([[KR3eg019]]), and [[周魁]] Zhōu Kuí's *Wēnzhèng zhǐguī* ([[KR3eg030]]).

**The late-Qīng / Republican critique and Sino-Western integration cluster.** Three sharp internal critiques of the Wú-Jūtōng-Wáng-Mèngyīng *wēnbìng* mainstream from the *zūnjīngpài* 尊經派 (revere-the-classics) and *Shānghán*-ist positions: [[周岩]] Zhōu Yán's *Liùqì gǎnzhèng yàoyì* ([[KR3eg018]], 1880–1905); [[王德宣]] Wáng Déxuān's *Wēnbìng zhèngzōng* ([[KR3eg012]], 1935); [[沈麟]] Shěn Lín's *Chóngdìng wēnrè jīngjiě* ([[KR3eg013]], Republican). Plus Sino-Western integrationist treatises: [[吳瑞甫]] Wú Ruìfǔ's *Zhōngxī wēnrè chuànjiě* ([[KR3eg021]], 1920) and [[劉裁吾]] Liú Cáiwú's *Jìngbìng yǔ nǎomóyán quánshū* ([[KR3eg044]]).

## Important persons

- [[葉桂]] Yè Tiānshì (1666–1745) — the foundational mid-Qīng Sūzhōu physician of the *wēnbìng* tradition; *Wēnrè lùn* ([[KR3eg001]]) is the doctrinal kernel of the entire later canon.
- [[薛雪]] Xuē Shēngbái (1681–1770) — Yè's contemporary in Sūzhōu; the foundational treatise on damp-warm ([[KR3eg020]]).
- [[吳有性]] Wú Yǒuxìng (1582–1652) — the late-Míng author of the pestilential-qi doctrine ([[KR3eg004]]); the most-commented-upon source-text in the division.
- [[吳塘]] Wú Jūtōng (1758–1836) — the *summa*-author of the southern *wēnbìng* school, *Wēnbìng tiáobiàn* ([[KR3eg010]]); his prescription apparatus is the operational core of modern TCM warm-disease therapy.
- [[王士雄]] Wáng Mèngyīng (1808–1868) — the mid-19th-century editor and theorist who consolidated the canonical four into the standard *Wēnrè jīngwěi* ([[KR3eg008]]) and produced the principal Chinese-medical treatise on cholera ([[KR3eg040]]).
- [[戴天章]] Dài Tiānzhāng (1644–1722) — the principal early-Qīng extender of Wú Yǒuxìng's framework into a working clinical apparatus ([[KR3eg023]]).
- [[陳平伯]] Chén Píngbó (late Qiánlóng / Jiāqìng) — author of the canonical *fēngwēn* monograph ([[KR3eg009]]).
- [[余霖]] Yú Lín (1723–1795) — author of the gypsum-centred *Yìzhěn yīdé* ([[KR3eg031]]); his *Qīngwēn bàidú yǐn* prescription remains in active TCM use today.
- [[周揚俊]] Zhōu Yángjùn (late 17th c.) — the early-Qīng Sūzhōu transitional figure between Wú Yǒuxìng and Yè Tiānshì ([[KR3eg032]]).
- [[郭志邃]] Guō Zhìsuì (mid-17th c.) — the Jiāxīng physician who established the *shā* category as a doctrinally rigorous clinical entity ([[KR3eg037]]).
- [[雷豐]] Léi Fēng (1833–1888) — the Qúzhōu physician of the late-Qīng Zhèjiāng wing of the *wēnbìng* tradition ([[KR3eg002]]).
- [[柳寶詒]] Liǔ Bǎoyí (1842–1901) — the principal late-Qīng theorist of latent-qi (*fúqì*) warm disease ([[KR3eg003]]).
- [[王旭高]] Wáng Xùgāo (1798–1862) — the Wúxī physician whose *gējué* / *gēkuò* mnemonic versifications ([[KR3eg015]], [[KR3eg024]], [[KR3eg033]]) defined the standard mid-Qīng pedagogical idiom.
- [[何廉臣]] Hé Liánchén (1861–1929) — the leading Shàoxīng-school (*Yuèpài* 越派) editor of the late-Qīng / Republican period; principal contributor to [[KR3eg006]] and [[KR3eg034]].
- [[羅汝蘭]] Luó Rǔlán (late Qīng) — the Liánjiāng physician whose *Shǔyì yuēbiān* ([[KR3eg041]]) documents the 1890–1894 Guǎngdōng plague onset.
- [[鄭全望]] Zhèng Quánwàng (late Míng) — the Jiāngxī physician who composed the principal late-Míng miasma-malaria monograph ([[KR3eg038]]).
- [[源元凱]] Minamoto Genkai (Edo) — the principal Edo-period *kanpō* warm-disease theorist; his rigid / soft pathogen distinction ([[KR3eg025]]) is the most substantial Japanese doctrinal contribution in the division.
- [[池田瑞仙]] Ikeda Zuisen (1734–1816) — the Bakufu Medical Academy smallpox specialist whose *Dòukē biànyào* ([[KR3eg046]]) is one of the principal Edo *kanpō* texts on smallpox.
- [[王德宣]] Wáng Déxuān (民國) — the Húnán State Medical College professor whose *Wēnbìng zhèngzōng* ([[KR3eg012]], 1935) is the sharpest internal Republican-era critique of the Qīng *wēnbìng* canon.
- [[吳瑞甫]] Wú Ruìfǔ (1872–1952) — the Xiàmén physician whose *Zhōngxī wēnrè chuànjiě* ([[KR3eg021]], 1920) is the principal early-Republican Sino-Western integrationist warm-disease treatise; principal warm-disease text of the Mínnán medical tradition.

## Topics

- **The *Shānghán* / *wēnbìng* relation.** The defining doctrinal question of the entire division: is warm-disease etiologically distinct from cold-injury (the position of Wú Yǒuxìng, Yè Tiānshì, Wú Jūtōng, and the southern *wēnbìng* mainstream), or is it a sub-category within the *Shānghán* framework (the position of the *zūnjīngpài* in the late Qīng and of the Republican-era *Shānghán*-ists Zhōu Yán, Wáng Déxuān, and Shěn Lín)? Wáng Mèngyīng's *Wēnrè jīngwěi* ([[KR3eg008]]) attempts to retire the polemic by treating both as valid frameworks for different etiological situations; [[陸子賢]] Lù Zǐxián's *Liùyīn tiáobiàn* ([[KR3eg017]]) is the principal late-Qīng cold-warm integrationist statement.
- **Pestilential-qi (*lìqì* 戾氣 / *záqì* 雜氣) doctrine.** Wú Yǒuxìng's claim that epidemic warm disease is caused by a distinct pestilential agent — entering through mouth and nose, lodging in the *móyuán* 膜原 — is the foundational theoretical innovation of the late-Míng / early-Qīng *wēnyì* tradition. Dài Tiānzhāng ([[KR3eg023]]) supplies the working clinical apparatus; Liú Kuí ([[KR3eg039]]) extends it northward; Minamoto Genkai ([[KR3eg025]]) refines and partially refutes it from a Japanese clinical position.
- **The four-fen versus three-burner staging.** Yè Tiānshì's *wèiqìyíngxuè* 衛氣營血 staging (in [[KR3eg001]]) and Wú Jūtōng's *sānjiāo* 三焦 staging (in [[KR3eg010]]) constitute the parallel staging systems of the mature Qīng *wēnbìng* doctrinal apparatus. They are not in competition but provide complementary clinical frames.
- **Latent-qi versus newly-contracted (*fúqì* 伏氣 vs *xīngǎn* 新感) pathogens.** The principal interpretive divide running through the late-Qīng *wēnbìng* current: the southern Sūzhōu line (Yè–Wú Jūtōng–Wáng Mèngyīng) emphasises newly-contracted pathogens; the latent-pathogen line (Liǔ Bǎoyí [[KR3eg003]], Liú Jírén [[KR3eg028]], Léi Fēng [[KR3eg002]], Jiǎng Shùqǐ [[KR3eg027]]) emphasises pathogens dormant within the body and emerging seasonally.
- **Geographic and climatic particularism.** The argument that southern Jiāngnán climate produces clinical pictures requiring distinct doctrinal frameworks — implicit in Yè–Xuē–Wú Jūtōng, made explicit in [[宋兆淇]] Sòng Zhàoqí's *Nánbìng biéjiàn* ([[KR3eg029]]). The geographical argument extends to the *zhàng* / miasma category as the disease-imagination of the southern frontier (Zhèng Quánwàng [[KR3eg038]]; cf. Yang 2010, Baldanza 2022 in Wilkinson §41.7.6) and to the long-term southward shift of climatic boundaries (Lee et al. 2017).
- **Doctrinal response to novel epidemics.** The successive arrival of novel disease categories — cholera (1820s), bubonic plague (1890s) — provided clinical tests of the Qīng *wēnbìng* apparatus. Wáng Mèngyīng's heat-aetiology argument for cholera ([[KR3eg040]]) and Luó Rǔlán's wēnbìng-framed response to plague ([[KR3eg041]]) are the principal cases. Hanson 2011 reads the entire division as a study in the *geographic imagination* through which a doctrinal tradition expanded its categorical reach.
- **Cool-acrid release and gypsum-centred therapy.** The signature therapeutic principles of the wēnbìng school: cool-acrid (rather than hot-acrid) surface-release (locus classicus in Chén Píngbó [[KR3eg009]]); heavy-gypsum *qì*-level heat-clearing (locus classicus in Yú Lín [[KR3eg031]]); *Yínqiào sǎn* 銀翹散 and *Sāngjú yǐn* 桑菊飲 as the standard surface-cooling prescriptions (Wú Jūtōng [[KR3eg010]]).
- **Editorial transmission, pseudepigraphy, and pirated editions.** The late-Qīng wēnbìng canon is constituted as much by editorial transmission as by primary composition. Wáng Mèngyīng's flagging of doubtful Chén / Xuē attribution ([[KR3eg009]], [[KR3eg020]]) is one of the locus classicus *kǎojù* 考據 cases in Chinese medical philology; the Dài Tiānzhāng / *Wēnyì míngbiàn* false-attribution-and-correction story ([[KR3eg023]], [[KR3eg033]]) is another. The successive editorial layers in *Chóngdìng Guǎng wēnrè lùn* ([[KR3eg006]]) — Dài 1675, Lù Màoxiū 1864, Hé Liánchén 1909 — exemplify the late-Qīng practice of continuous re-edition.
- **Sino-Japanese exchange.** Edo and Bakumatsu Japanese *kanpō* texts ([[KR3eg022]], [[KR3eg025]], [[KR3eg045]], [[KR3eg046]]) are transmitted to Republican-era China through Tāngběn Qiúzhēn's 1936 *Huáng Hàn yīxué cóngshū*; the round-trip is one of the more striking East-Asian medical-bibliographic patterns of the early 20th century.
- **Sino-Western integration in the Republican era.** The wēnbìng tradition's encounter with bacteriological epidemiology and Western internal medicine: [[吳瑞甫]] Wú Ruìfǔ ([[KR3eg021]]) on warm-disease, [[劉裁吾]] Liú Cáiwú ([[KR3eg044]]) on meningitis as *jìngbìng* 痙病, [[何廉臣]] Hé Liánchén ([[KR3eg034]]) on damp-warm seasonal epidemics. The polemical-defensive counterpoint is in [[蔣樹杞]] Jiǎng Shùqǐ ([[KR3eg027]]) and [[沈麟]] Shěn Lín ([[KR3eg013]]).

## Timeline

- **1609** — *Zhàngnüè zhǐnán* 瘴瘧指南 [[KR3eg038]] — late-Míng monograph on miasma-malaria, occasioned by the 1602 epidemic; opens the division chronologically.
- **1623–1854** — *Zēngdìng Yèpíng Shāngshǔ quánshū* 增訂葉評傷暑全書 [[KR3eg005]] — Zhāng Fèngkuí's first Chinese monograph on summer-heat as a distinct disease category, with later Yè-attributed annotations and a Dàoguāng expansion.
- **1642** — *Wēnyì lùn* 溫疫論 [[KR3eg004]] — Wú Yǒuxìng's response to the 1641 Chóngzhēn epidemic; foundation of the entire later wēnbìng / wēnyì tradition.
- **1675** — *Guǎng wēnyì lùn* 廣瘟疫論 [[KR3eg023]] — Dài Tiānzhāng's clinicisation of Wú Yǒuxìng; the *wǔjiān shíjiá* schema.
- **1675–1678** — *Shāzhàng yùhéng* 痧脹玉衡 [[KR3eg037]] — Guō Zhìsuì establishes the *shā* category as a doctrinally rigorous clinical entity.
- **1675–1909** — *Chóngdìng Guǎng wēnrè lùn* 重訂廣溫熱論 [[KR3eg006]] — Dài Tiānzhāng's text in successive Lù-Màoxiū / Hé-Liánchén editorial layers.
- **1679** — *Wēnrè shǔyì quánshū* 溫熱暑疫全書 [[KR3eg032]] — Zhōu Yángjùn's early-Qīng Sūzhōu treatise; the conceptual bridge between Wú Yǒuxìng and Yè Tiānshì.
- **1740–1770** — *Shīrè tiáobiàn* 濕熱條辨 [[KR3eg020]] — Xuē Shēngbái's foundational treatise on damp-warm pathology.
- **1746–1792** — *Wēnrè lùn* 溫熱論 [[KR3eg001]] — Yè Tiānshì's foundational text; the kernel of the four-fen staging doctrine.
- **1750–1850** — *Wēnyì lùn shīpíng* 溫疫論私評 [[KR3eg022]] — Akiyoshi Tadashi's Edo-period *kanpō* commentary on Wú Yǒuxìng.
- **1780–1805** — *Biànyì suǒyán* 辨疫瑣言 [[KR3eg035]] — Lǐ Bǐng's mid-Qīng critique of Wú Yǒuxìng's *Dáyuán yǐn* prescription; the most philologically rigorous Qīng critique of Wú.
- **1780–1816** — *Dòukē biànyào* 痘科辨要 [[KR3eg046]] — Ikeda Zuisen's Edo-period treatise on smallpox; recompiled by his son in 1807.
- **1780–1820** — *Wàigǎn wēnbìng piān* 外感溫病篇 [[KR3eg009]] — Chén Píngbó's canonical statement of cool-releasing therapy in *fēngwēn* pathology.
