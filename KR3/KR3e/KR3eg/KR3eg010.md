# Wēnbìng tiáobiàn 溫病條辨
*Detailed Analysis of Warm Diseases*
by [[吳塘]] (Wú Táng, *hào* Jūtōng 鞠通, 1758–1836)

## About the work

The doctrinal *summa* of the southern *wēnbìng* school in 6 *juǎn*, drafted by Wú Jūtōng ca. Jiāqìng 3 (1798) and printed Jiāqìng 18 (1813). Together with [[葉桂]] Yè Tiānshì's *Wēnrè lùn* ([[KR3eg001]]), [[王士雄]] Wáng Mèngyīng's *Wēnrè jīngwěi* ([[KR3eg008]]), and [[吳有性]] Wú Yǒuxìng's *Wēnyì lùn* ([[KR3eg004]]), the *Wēnbìng tiáobiàn* constitutes the **canonical four** of the Qīng warm-disease tradition; modern Chinese medicine further counts it among the **Four Great Classics** of TCM alongside the *Nèijīng*, *Shānghán lùn*, and *Jīnguì yàoluè*.

Wú's central doctrinal contribution is the **three-burner** (*sānjiāo* 三焦) staging of warm-disease pathology — proceeding from upper to lower, from shallow to deep — a parallel to (and clinical complement of) Yè Tiānshì's *wèiqìyíngxuè* (four-fen) staging. The two schemata together provide the modern *wēnbìng* clinical apparatus.

### Abstract

The composition history is well documented from the prefaces. Wú had a complete draft by Jiāqìng 3 (1798); he showed it to his friend [[徵保]] Zhèngbǎo in Jiāqìng 9 (1804); after another decade of revision, the book was printed in Jiāqìng 18 (1813). The motivation, given in Wú's own preface, was the catastrophic experience of the 1793 Beijing epidemic, during which Wú had worked through the outbreak applying the southern *wēnbìng* doctrinal frame to a northern epidemic context — an experience that demanded a doctrinally rigorous synthesis.

The six *juǎn*: (juǎn 1) wind-warm, warm-toxin, warm-epidemic, winter-warm, *fēngwēn 風溫* and varieties of upper-burner warm disease; (juǎn 2) middle-burner; (juǎn 3) lower-burner; (juǎn 4) miscellaneous addenda on relevant clinical scenarios; (juǎn 5) the prescription apparatus; (juǎn 6) supplementary appendix on women and children.

The prescription apparatus is the book's enduring contribution: ***Yínqiào sǎn*** 銀翹散, ***Sāngjú yǐn*** 桑菊飲, ***Huòxiāng zhèngqì sǎn*** 藿香正氣散, ***Qīngyíng tāng*** 清營湯, ***Qīnggōng tāng*** 清宮湯, ***Xījiǎo dìhuáng tāng*** 犀角地黃湯, ***Āngōng niúhuáng wán*** 安宮牛黃丸 — all of these remain the operational core of modern TCM warm-disease therapy and were prominently deployed in 2003 (SARS) and 2020 (COVID-19) TCM treatment protocols.

Doctrinally, the work establishes the *wēnbìng* tradition as an autonomous medical framework standing alongside *Shānghán*, no longer subordinate to it. The work has generated an immense commentary tradition; the standard Mínguó-era critical edition is by [[雷豐]] Léi Shàoyì's son or pupil — see [[KR3eg011]] for the duplicate KR catalog entry on a different recension.

## Other points of interest

The Wú Jūtōng prescription apparatus has the most enduring clinical relevance of any Qīng medical text — *Yínqiào sǎn* remains the standard TCM "common cold and influenza" prescription in modern Chinese pharmacies, and *Āngōng niúhuáng wán* remains the principal TCM ICU stroke prescription. The book is also notable for Wú's explicit polemic against the *Shānghán*-extremist position that warm disease is merely a subtype of cold damage — a position that, by 1813, was untenable.
