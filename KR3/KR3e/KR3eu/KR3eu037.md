# Yīzōng jīnjiàn · Zhènggǔ xīnfǎ yàozhǐ 醫宗金鑑·正骨心法要旨
 *Heart-Method Essentials for Bone-Setting*
by [[吳謙]] (奉敕撰) and [[劉裕鐸]] (奉敕撰), under imperial commission

## About the work

The *Zhènggǔ xīnfǎ yàozhǐ* 正骨心法要旨 is the bone-setting / orthopaedic chapter of the *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* compendium ([[KR3e0090]] / KR3eu016) — 5 *juàn* (also transmitted as 4) and the seventh and last structural division of the compendium. The Sìkù *tíyào* (cited in [[KR3e0090]]) explicitly notes that the imperial editors added this chapter because "in antiquity this art existed, but apart from Xuē Jǐ's *Zhèngtǐ lèiyào* there has been no specialist book — we therefore supplement the lacuna."

### Abstract

The chapter codifies the bone-setting practice of the Qīng imperial *Tàiyīyuàn* and the imperially-employed Mongol *zhènggǔ* 正骨 specialists of the Shàngsìyuàn 上駟院 (the imperial stables service, which traditionally trained bone-setters because of the high incidence of equestrian injuries among Manchu bannermen). It is the first imperial-Chinese illustrated bone-setting manual to incorporate explicit Mongol-tradition manipulation techniques — Qián-lóng-era Mongol bone-setters served under imperial patronage as specialists at the Shàngsìyuàn — and the chapter records both Chinese literate-physician (Xuē-school) and Mongol manipulative-craft traditions. Topics covered include the *bāfǎ* 八法 (eight manipulation techniques: *mō* 摸 palpation, *jiē* 接 connection, *duān* 端 holding, *tí* 提 lifting, *àn* 按 pressing, *mó* 摩 rubbing, *tuī* 推 pushing, *ná* 拿 grasping), the *bādà jiàn* 八大件 (eight standard splinting/supporting devices), and lesion-by-lesion practice for skull fractures, dislocations of every major joint, vertebral injuries, etc., with extensive illustrations of splints, devices, and manipulation techniques.

The chapter is one of the most important Qīng medical-policy innovations: by promoting bone-setting from a marginal artisan craft to an official imperial-academy specialty, the *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* established the institutional foundation of modern Chinese *zhōngyī gǔshāngkē* 中醫骨傷科. The verse-mnemonic format and detailed illustrations made the chapter the standard Qīng-and-Republican bone-setting reference until well into the 20th century. Composition window 1742–1749.

The chapter's principal upstream source is [[薛己]] Xuē Jǐ's *Zhèngtǐ lèi yào* (KR3eu006), the only specialist literate-physician bone-setting monograph that preceded it.

## Other points of interest

The Mongol-tradition contribution to Qīng imperial orthopaedics — visible in this chapter's bāfǎ 八法 manipulation taxonomy — is one of the more interesting cases of Inner-Asian medical-craft influence on the literate-Chinese imperial-medical institution; the *Shàngsìyuàn* 上駟院 bone-setting service was an explicitly Manchu-Mongol institutional carrier.
