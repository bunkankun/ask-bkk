# Jiàn-zhì fàn zàn 犍稚梵讚
*Sanskrit Hymn on the Gaṇḍī (Wooden Bell)*
by [[法賢]] (Fǎxián / Tiānxīzāi, 譯)

## About the work

A one-*juǎn* Northern-Sòng translation of an Indian Buddhist *stotra* on the *gaṇḍī* — the wooden monastic call-bell used to summon the *saṃgha* to ritual and to assembly. The Sanskrit title is *Gaṇḍīstotragāthā*. The Chinese phonetic 犍稚 (or 揵稚 in the Taishō) renders the Sanskrit *gaṇḍī*. Translated by [[法賢]] Fǎxián (originally Tiānxīzāi).

### Abstract

The *Gaṇḍīstotragāthā* is one of the more curious of the canonical Indian Buddhist *stotras* — a hymn addressed not to a deity or to the Buddha but to the wooden monastic bell that summons the *saṃgha*. The genre reflects the broader Indian and Buddhist tendency to ritualize devotion to liturgical objects. Sanskrit fragments of the work are preserved; the Tibetan translation also survives.

Fǎxián's translation is bracketed by his Institute career, 982–1000. The Taishō uses 金臧廣勝寺本 as base. Note that the catalog title gives the second character as 稚 *zhì*, but the Taishō text uses 揵稚 (with a *zhì* substituted for the standard *jiàn*-*zhī* form); this is a known variant orthography.

## Other points of interest

The *Gaṇḍī*-hymn is a small but valuable witness to the everyday Indian monastic life: the wooden call-bell was the principal instrument of monastic timekeeping, and a hymn in its praise reflects the devotional valuation of the most ordinary instruments of monastic discipline.
