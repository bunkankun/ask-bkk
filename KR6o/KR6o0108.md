# Dàshèng qǐxìn lùn nèiyì lüètàn jì 大乘起信論內義略探記
*Brief Record of an Inquiry into the Inner Meaning of the Awakening of Faith*
by [[太賢]] (Tàixián / Daehyeon, 作)

## About the work

A one-*juǎn* topical commentary on the *Awakening of Faith* by the Silla Yogācāra master [[太賢]] (Tàixián / Daehyeon, fl. mid-8th century). Tàixián was the leading transmitter of the [[玄奘]] Xuánzàng / Cí'ēn 慈恩 Yogācāra tradition in Korea — known there as *Hǎidōng Yúgā zǔ* 海東瑜伽祖 ("the patriarch of *yoga* east of the sea") — and his approach to the *Qǐxìn lùn* therefore reads it through a Yogācāra lens, in contrast to the Huáyán reading developed by [[法藏]].

### Abstract

The text is signed simply 太賢作 ("composed by Tàixián"), without a place-name. It opens with a statement of method: "In commenting on this treatise I roughly open two gates: first, stating the great meaning of the treatise; second, probing the meanings within the treatise" (粗開二門。一述論大意。二探論中義). The "great meaning" section sets out the Mahāyāna context: "since the true source is silent and serene, it cannot be measured by consciousness; since the dharma-nature is empty and condensed, it cannot be expounded in words…" — but the text then turns to detailed glosses on specific Yogācāra-relevant topoi: the eight consciousnesses, the *ālayavijñāna*, the *Tathāgatagarbha* / *ālaya* identification, the *xūn-xí* 熏習 (*vāsanā*) doctrine.

Tàixián's reading is significant historically because it represents the Yogācāra-school engagement with a text that the Huáyán school had largely claimed for its own. He treats the *Qǐ-xìn lùn* as a sub-stratum of the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra* and *Cheng-wei-shi lun* tradition rather than as the foundational document of *Tathāgatagarbha-pratītyasamutpāda*. Composition is bracketed by Tàixián's documented activity period c. 740–770 (he conducted royal merit ceremonies in 753).

The Taishō establishes the text from the 高麗 base collated against 原 (a Tokugawa Japanese printing).

## Other points of interest

Tàixián's inclusion in the Taishō Vol. 44 alongside the Huáyán-school commentaries reflects the modern editorial decision to gather all *Qǐxìn lùn* commentaries together regardless of doctrinal tradition. In its original Silla and Tang context, however, this work would have circulated in Yogācāra-school networks separate from the Huáyán transmission of [[法藏]] and [[元曉]].
