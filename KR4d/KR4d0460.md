# Bái-yún jí 白雲集
*The Bái-yún (White-Cloud) Collection*
by [[許謙]] (撰), [[李伸]] (編)

## About the work

The literary collection of **Xǔ Qiān** 許謙 (1270–1337) — *Bái-yún xiān-shēng* 白雲先生, posthumous *shì* **Wén-yì** 文懿 — the fourth and last of the **Běi-Shān sì xiān-shēng** 北山四先生 (Hé Jī 何基 → Wáng Bǎi 王柏 → Jīn Lǚ-xiáng 金履祥 → Xǔ Qiān): the Jīn-huá / Wù-zhōu 金華 / 婺州 Yuán transmission of Sòng Zhū-Xué orthodoxy. The Sìkù base derives from the SBCK reproduction of the standard transmitted Wú-region recension. The collection was compiled and printed by the Míng-period scholar-official **Chén Xiāng** 陳相 of Jīn-huá in Chéng-huà 2 (1466) — Chén's preface (preserved in the volume) records that he obtained a manuscript in 4 *juàn* from the *Fāng-bó* (Provincial-Governor) **Zhāng-gōng** (of Jiāng-pǔ 江浦) while serving on imperial commission to East-Guǎng. Chén notes that the *Wù*-region (Jīn-huá) Sòng-Yuán Neo-Confucian lineage — Lǚ Zǔ-qiān (the Sòng *Dōng-lái Lǚ chéng-gōng*) → Wáng Wén-xiàn → Hé Wén-dìng → the Yuán **Jīn Wén-ān** 金文安 (Jīn Lǚ-xiáng) → **Bái-yún Xǔ Wén-yì** — derives canonically from the Zhū-Xī-via-Huáng Mián-zhāi 黃勉齋 transmission. The collection's prefaces serially link Xǔ Qiān into the trans-dynastic Confucian *dào-tǒng* canon.

### Prefaces

The SBCK base preserves several Míng-period prefaces:

1. **Chén Xiāng's** preface (Chéng-huà 2 = 1466), recording the manuscript discovery and the local Jīn-huá Neo-Confucian lineage.
2. A second preface (also Míng-period) tracing the *Dào* lineage from Yáo-Shùn-Yǔ-Tāng → Confucius-Mencius → through the Hàn-Táng Confucians (Jiǎ Yì, Dǒng Zhòng-shū, Hán Yù) → to the Sòng patriarchs Zhōu / Chéng / Zhāng / Zhū → through Zhēn Xī-shān (Zhēn Dé-xiù), Cài Jiǔ-fēng, Hú Wén-dìng → to the Yuán worthies **Xǔ Lǔ-zhāi** [[許衡]], **Liú Jìng-xiū** [[劉因]], **Wú Cǎo-lú** [[吳澄]] — and finally to Xǔ Qiān as receiving the *zhèng-chuán* (direct-orthodox transmission) of Zhū Xī.

The collection itself contains *shī*, *cí*, *fù*, prose, *xù*, *jì*, *bēi-míng*, *zàn*, *míng* in 4 *juàn*. The principal contents are exhortations to study, *jì* (records) of Confucian academies and shrines, philosophical letters, and *bēi-zhì* (epitaphs) for fellow Jīn-huá-school members.

### Abstract

The collected works of Xǔ Qiān (1270–1337), the fourth-generation patriarch of the **Běi-Shān** 北山 (North-Mountain) Yuán Zhū-Xué transmission centered at Jīn-huá in Wù-zhōu (modern Zhè-jiāng). For full biographical detail see the person note. Xǔ refused all imperial office and lived as the most famous Yuán-period Confucian teacher of his generation — students reportedly came from across the Yuán empire. *Yuán-shǐ Rú-lín zhuàn* gives the principal biographical record; **Huáng Jìn** [[黃溍]] composed the epitaph (preserved in *Jīn-huá Huáng xiān-shēng wén-jí*).

The *Bái-yún jí* itself is principally a record of Xǔ's pedagogical and *dào-xué*-lineage activities — academy-establishment records, exhortations to students, ritual-and-shrine inscriptions — rather than independent literary composition. The *Sìkù*-canonized **Xǔ Qiān** corpus consists of four works: his *Shàng-shū* commentary [[KR1b0028]] *Dú-shū cóng shuō* 讀書叢說; his *Shī jí zhuàn míng wù chāo* 詩集傳名物鈔; his *Sì shū cóng shuō* 四書叢說; and the present *Bái-yún jí* — making him one of the more comprehensively-transmitted Yuán Neo-Confucians.

The Míng compiler **Lǐ Shēn** 李伸 (an early/mid-Míng scholar) is named in the catalog as editor, but the editorial work is mostly attributable to Chén Xiāng's Chéng-huà 1466 print, with Lǐ Shēn's earlier compilation underlying.

Composition window: from Xǔ Qiān's adult literary activity (after his 1290 entry into Jīn Lǚ-xiáng's school, when Xǔ was 21) through his death in 1337.

## Other points of interest

The Chén Xiāng 1466 print preface — preserving the *Wù*-region Zhū-Xī *dào-tǒng* genealogy — is an important Míng-period statement of the Yuán-Jīn-huá school's self-understanding of its place in the Sòng-Míng *Lǐ-xué* succession. The two prefaces together place Xǔ Qiān into the canonical *dào-tǒng* sequence ending with the Míng founder-scholars **Sòng Lián** 宋濂 (景濂) and **Wáng Zǐ-chōng** 王子充 (王禕) — both Jīn-huá natives — making Xǔ the proximate Yuán-source of Míng-founding Confucian institutionality.
