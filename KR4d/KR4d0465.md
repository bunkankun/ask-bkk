# Mù-ān jí 牧庵集
*The Mù-ān (Herding-Hut) Collection*
by [[姚燧]] (撰), [[劉致]] (撰附年譜)

## About the work

The literary monument of **Yáo Suì** 姚燧 (CBDB 35141, 1238–1313) — *zì* **Duān-fǔ** 端甫, *hào* **Mù-ān** 牧庵 ("Herding-Hut"), native of **Yíng-zhōu Liǔ-chéng** 營州柳城 (modern Liáo-níng). Mid-Yuán Hàn-Confucian senior official; the Sìkù editors identify him as the singular Yuán-period *yī-dài zōng-gōng* (one-generation patriarchal master) of *gǔ-wén* prose — the inheritance of the Hàn–Sòng prose tradition (Sī-mǎ Qiān, Yáng Xióng, Bān Gù, Hán Yù, Liǔ Zōng-yuán, Ōu-yáng Xiū, Sū Shì) bearing on the founding Yuán imperial enterprise. The SBCK base derives from the Zhì-shùn 至順 *rén-shēn* (1332) cut prepared by Yáo's disciple **Liú Zhì** 劉致 (*Hàn-lín dài-zhì*) — sourced from the Jiāng-zhè *jùn-xiàn shàn-xué yú qián* (county-school surplus funds) and cut at the Hàng-zhōu *Rú-xué tí-jǔ-sī*, supervised by the *Pí-yáng* (Pó-yáng) man **Wú Shàn** 吳善, with the textual collation done by **Yè Jǐng-xiū** 葉景修 (a Qián-táng / Háng-zhōu scholar). The 50-*juàn* (per *Yuán-shǐ*) original is preserved by the SBCK base; some recensions number 36 *juàn* (the catalog meta), reflecting different early counts.

The Sìkù catalog's *fù-lù* (appendix) entry records that the *nián-pǔ* (chronological biography) of Yáo Suì by Liú Zhì is appended to the collection (1 *juàn*) — the principal contemporary biographical witness.

### Prefaces

The SBCK base preserves two foundational prefaces:

1. **Wú Shàn's preface** (Zhì-shùn *zhāo-yáng* zuò-è = 1332): Wú, then *Jiāng-zhè děng-chù rú-xué tí-jǔ*, supervised the printing. The preface frames Yáo Suì as the singular Yuán-period equivalent of:
   - Hàn (400 years): Sī-mǎ Qiān, Yáng Xióng, Bān Gù — 4 persons
   - Two Jìn-Wèi-Sui: no one of comparable stature
   - Táng (300 years): Hán Yù, Liǔ Zōng-yuán — 2 persons
   - Sòng (300 years): Ōu-yáng Xiū, Sū Shì — 2 persons
   - **Yuán** (the imperial dynasty): **Yáo Suì alone**
   
   Wú records the Zhì-dà *wù-shēn* (1308) period when Yáo was *Hàn-lín chéng-zhǐ* and Wú first met him; the subsequent Yáo *gào-jié* (resignation-southern-journey) which prevented further compilation; the Yáo-disciple Liú Zhì's commission in Zhì-shùn *rén-shēn* (1332); the final collation results — 3 *gǔ-fù*, 222 poems, 38 *xù*, 53 *jì*, 140 *bēi-míng-mù-zhì*, 58 *zhì-gào*, 2 *zhuàn*, 15 *zàn*, 11 *shuō*, 10 *zhù-cè*, 13 *zá-zhù*, 124 *yuè-fǔ* — total 689 pieces in 50 *juàn*.

2. **Yuán-period original preface** (by Yáo Suì's *mén-rén*, name partly illegible — likely Hú Zhī-yù or another senior contemporary): characterizes Yáo as the *cháng-jué jiāng-shuài* (great-decisive General) of Yuán prose — like Hán Yù and Lǐ Yōng of Táng, like Ōu and Sū of Sòng — a singular consolidating figure of the empire-founding generation. Records that Yáo composed many imperial-grade *bēi-míng* for the senior Mongol-aristocratic families; that the Tài-dìng-era post-Yáo collection-gathering was initiated through Jiǎ Huàn Huá-fǔ in Pó-yáng.

### Abstract

The literary monument of the singular Yuán-period *gǔ-wén* master, characterized by the imperial-Yuán editors as the Yuán-dynastic equivalent of Hán Yù, Liǔ Zōng-yuán, Ōu-yáng Xiū, and Sū Shì in their respective dynasties. Yáo Suì (1238–1313) rose to *Hàn-lín xué-shì chéng-zhǐ* 翰林學士承旨 — the senior Hàn-lín literary office — under Khubilai's successors; his prose became the canonical model for Yuán-period state-papers, *bēi-míng* (steles), and *mù-zhì* (epitaphs) for the senior Mongol-aristocratic and Hàn-Confucian elite.

The 689-piece (per Wú Shàn's 1332 collation) corpus comprises:
- 3 *gǔ-fù* — major rhapsodies in the Hàn tradition
- 222 *shī* — poetry across all forms
- 38 *xù* — prefaces (collections of contemporary literary figures)
- 53 *jì* — records (memorial-and-foundation pieces)
- **140 *bēi-míng* and *mù-zhì*** — the largest single category, including the foundational steles for senior Mongol-Khangli families and Hàn-Confucian elder statesmen (Téng Ān-shàng's *mù-jié míng* [[KR4d0459]] is among these)
- 58 *zhì-gào* — imperial commission decrees drafted by Yáo
- 2 *zhuàn* — biographies
- 15 *zàn*, 11 *shuō*, 10 *zhù-cè*, 13 *zá-zhù*
- 124 *yuè-fǔ* (*cí*-form)

The collection's pivotal historical importance is the *bēi-míng* / *mù-zhì* category: Yáo Suì was the senior Yuán composer of imperial-grade memorial inscriptions for the founding-and-second-generation elite. These 140 pieces are the largest single body of Yuán-period epigraphic biography preserved through manuscript transmission rather than stone survival.

The preservation chain: Yáo's death 1313 → Tài-dìng-era partial gathering (in Pó-yáng) → Jiāng-zhè provincial supervision in Zhì-shùn *rén-shēn* (1332) under Liú Zhì's commission, Wú Shàn's collation, Yè Jǐng-xiū's textual editing → SBCK reproduction. Composition window: 1260 (Yáo's earliest official compositions) through 1313.

## Other points of interest

The Wú Shàn 1332 preface's tabulation of Yáo Suì's relative status — the Yuán equivalent of Hán Yù, Sū Shì — is one of the foundational Yuán-period imperial self-characterizations of cultural-political continuity with Hàn-Táng-Sòng. The fact that this canonization happened only 19 years after Yáo's death (and at a moment of Yuán imperial cultural consolidation under Wén-zōng) makes it a particularly authoritative contemporary judgment.
