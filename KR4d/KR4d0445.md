# Sōng-xuě-zhāi jí 松雪齋集
*The Sōng-xuě (Pine-Snow Studio) Collection*
by [[趙孟頫]] (撰)

## About the work

The collected works in ten *juàn* of Zhào Mèng-fǔ 趙孟頫 (CBDB 17690, 1254–1322), *zì* Zǐ-áng 子昂, *hào* Sōng-xuě dào-rén 松雪道人 ("Pine-Snow Daoist"), posthumous *shì* Wén-mǐn 文敏, posthumously enfeoffed Wèi-guó-gōng 魏國公 ("Duke of Wèi") — the singular Yuán-period Sinitic-cultural arbiter: tenth-generation descendant of Sòng Tài-zǔ through Tài-zǔ's fourth son; resident of Wú-xīng 吳興 (Hú-zhōu, Zhè-jiāng); the dominant Yuán-period calligrapher (restoring the Wáng Xī-zhī orthodoxy), the dominant Yuán-period painter (literati-painting tradition), the dominant Yuán-period bureaucratic-Confucian elder. Originally a Sòng *zōng-shì* (imperial descendant) who never held office under the Southern Sòng; in 1286 (Zhì-yuán 23) recruited by Kublai Khan via the Censorate-recommendation policy — "world-emperor first selection among the Sòng remnant talent"; entered Yuán court service and rose through the *Bīng-bù láng-zhōng*, *Cháo-liè dài-fū*, *Tài-shǐ-yuàn shǐ*, *Jí-xián xué-shì*, ultimately *Hàn-lín xué-shì chéng-zhǐ* (the highest Hàn-lín literary office); concurrent *Róng-lù dài-fū* and *Zhī-zhì-gào jiān xiū guó-shǐ*. Posthumously promoted *Róng-lù dài-fū Jiāng-zhè děng-chù xíng zhōng-shū-shěng píng-zhāng zhèng-shì*, posthumously enfeoffed Wèi-guó-gōng, *shì* Wén-mǐn (Zhì-shùn 3 = 1332 imperial edict, preserved in the SBCK frontmatter). The principal contemporary biographical document — the **Sòng Lián**-supplied posthumous-canonization-discussion text dated Zhì-shùn 3 (1332) — is preserved at the front of the SBCK base. The collection's editorial history: assembled and edited by Zhào's son **Zhào Yōng** 趙雍 (Zhòng-mù 仲穆); first published in Zhì-yuán *jǐ-mǎo* (1339) by the Huā-xī Shěn-shì Bó-yù 沈氏伯玉 at family-school; preserved in the SBCK base. Foundational prefaces by **Hé Zhēn-lì** 何貞立 (Cháng-shā, 1339) and **Dài Biǎo-yuán** [[KR4d0431]] (Dà-dé *wù-xū* = 1298) — Dài's preface is the principal contemporary literary evaluation, ranking Zhào's *gǔ-fù* alongside the Chǔ-Hàn masters, his *gǔ-shī* alongside Bào Zhào and Xiè Líng-yùn, his other compositions still surpassing Gāo Shì and Lǐ Áo. The collection contains: *gǔ-fù* (5), *gǔ-shī* (juàn 2–3), *lǜ-shī* 5- and 7-syllable (juàn 4–5), *jué-jù* 5/6/7-syllable, *zá-zhù* (essays), *xù*, *jì*, *bēi-míng*, *zhì-míng*, *zhì*, *zàn*, *míng*, *tí-bá*, *yuè-fǔ* (*cí* — including the famous *Wú-xīng fù* in *juàn* 1 — the great Wú-xīng-topography *fù* equal to Sòng Yù's *Gāo-táng fù*; and works for Zhào's Quán-zhēn-Daoist religious associates including Wú Quán-jié 吳全節, Zhāng Bó-yǔ 張伯雨, Zhāng Yǔ-cái 張與材, the Long-chūn-gōng Sūn-zhēn-rén). Composition window: from Zhào's first official compositions (*c.* 1286 Yuán induction) through 1322.

### Tiyao

*No standard Sìkù tíyào in the Kanripo source* (the SBCK base preserves the *Shì wén* — posthumous-canonization-discussion text; the *Fēng-zèng* — Zhì-shùn 3 [1332] imperial edict of posthumous-promotion; Hé Zhēn-lì's preface of Zhì-yuán *jǐ-mǎo* [1339]; Dài Biǎo-yuán's preface of Dà-dé *wù-xū* [1298]; plus the Huā-xī Shěn-shì Bó-yù *jiā-shú* colophon. No Qián-lóng *Sìkù tí-yào* is preserved in this source). The standard Sìkù tíyào outside the source places the *Sōng-xuě-zhāi jí* at the head of "Yuán *bié-jí lèi* 5"; characterizes Zhào as "Yuán-dynasty *yī-rén*" (singular-individual) of literary, calligraphic, painting, and ritual-Confucian arts; emphasizes the *biàn jiāo-sì pèi-wèi*, *dìng Guāng-tiān-mén biǎn-míng* (debate on the *Jiāo* / suburban sacrifice configurations and the fixing of the *Guāng-tiān-mén* tablet-name) as the most consequential of Zhào's institutional contributions to the Yuán state ritual.

### Abstract

Zhào Mèng-fǔ (CBDB 17690, 1254–1322) is the singular Yuán-period Sinitic-cultural arbiter. Tenth-generation descendant of Sòng Tài-zǔ (the founding Sòng emperor) through his fourth son; originally a Sòng *zōng-shì* (imperial-clan member) who, despite the family's Sòng-imperial blood, never held office under the Southern Sòng. In 1286 (Zhì-yuán 23) recruited by Kublai Khan via the Censorate-recommendation policy specifically targeting late-Sòng remnant talent — recorded as "*shǒu yīng shì xuǎn*" (first to answer the selection). Rose through the Yuán official-academic system to the highest available offices for Hàn-Confucian elites — *Hàn-lín xué-shì chéng-zhǐ jiān Róng-lù dài-fū*, the senior literary-and-historical office of the Yuán state. The collection preserves: (1) the great Wú-xīng-topographical *fù* (the *Wú-xīng fù* of *juàn* 1, the Yuán-period *Gāo-táng fù* equivalent); (2) extensive correspondence and presentation poetry with Yuán-court literary figures (Yáo Suì 姚燧, Hú Zhī-yù [[胡祗遹]], Wáng Yún 王惲, Yuán Hǎo-wèn-style elder Yán Fù 閻復, the senior Mongol-Confucian Bù-hū-mù 不忽木 Kāng-lǐ Bù-hū-mù, Yē-lǜ Yǒng-jiē 也先帖木兒, the Cí-zhōu Zhào Bǐng-wén tradition); (3) substantial Daoist religious-friendship pieces (with the Quán-zhēn lineage: Wú Quán-jié, Sūn-zhēn-rén of Cháng-chūn-gōng, Dù Dào-jiān 杜道堅, Zhāng Bó-yǔ, Zhāng Yǔ-cái — these document Zhào's role as the principal Sòng-Yuán-transition mediator with Daoism); (4) the great *bēi-míng* / *mù-zhì-míng* corpus including the *Quán-dìng-gōng shén-dào bēi-míng* (the canonical Yuán-imperial-foundational stele on the founding general Mongolian Quán-dìng-gōng), the *Wèi-guó-gōng Kāng-lǐ Bù-hū-mù shén-dào bēi* (for the senior Mongol-Confucian advisor Bù-hū-mù), the *Wǔ-wēi-jūn Jiǎ-fǔ wàng-zēng bēi*, the Tài-yīn gōng / Lóng-xìng-sì foundational steles; (5) the Yuán-period imperial *zhì-zhào* drafts; (6) the *Sōng-xuě-zhāi shī*, *cí* and *yuè-fǔ* including the great *Xuán-zhōu shí-yǒng* and the *Tiān-guān-shān tí-yǒng* cycles. Composition window: 1286 through 1322 — the entire post-1286 Yuán court service. CBDB 17690 firmly establishes 1254–1322; *Yuán-shǐ* j. 172 gives the standard biography. Wilkinson treats Zhào extensively (§38, §35, §28.1, §42).

## Other points of interest

The Dài Biǎo-yuán 1298 preface preserved in the SBCK base — Dài's elegant *xī-lè* (lake-pleasure) analogy comparing scholarly literary judgment to local connoisseurship — is one of the finest single Yuán-period literary-critical paratexts. The Hé Zhēn-lì 1339 postscript locates Zhào's posthumous reception (within 17 years of his death) in the early *Yuán-tǒng* / Zhì-yuán era, when Zhào's calligraphic style was already canonized but his literary substance was about to be eclipsed. The Cháng-shā Hé family's preservation of the Huā-xī Shěn-shì 1339 first edition through to the SBCK reproduction is one of the better-documented Yuán-Míng-Qīng *bié-jí* transmission lineages.
