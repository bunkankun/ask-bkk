# Fǔ-shuǐ jí 滏水集
*The Fǔ-shuǐ Collection*
by [[趙秉文]] (撰)

## About the work

The collected works in twenty *juàn* of Zhào Bǐng-wén 趙秉文 (CBDB 28846, 1159–1232), *zì* Zhōu-chén 周臣, *hào* Xián-xián lǎo-rén 閑閑老人, native of Cí-zhōu 磁州 — the foremost literary master of the middle and late Jīn dynasty, his stature equivalent in Jīn north to Yuán Hǎo-wèn [[KR4d0420]] in the late-Jīn / Yuán transition. Tested for the *jìnshì* in Dà-dìng 25 (1185); served the four Jīn reigns Shì-zōng, Zhāng-zōng, Wèi-wáng, Xuān-zōng, and Āi-zōng, rising to *Lǐ-bù shàng-shū jiān shì-dú xué-shì tóng-xiū guó-shǐ zhī jí-xián-yuàn shì* (Minister of Rites, Concurrent Reader-in-Waiting, Co-Compiler of the National History, Knower of the Academy of Worthies' Affairs). His Jīn-shǐ biography (j. 110) — preserved in the SBCK frontmatter as the *Běn-zhuàn* of this collection — gives the principal historical record: passed the *jìnshì* by special distinction; demoted in Míng-chāng 6 (1195) for a memorial criticizing Chancellor Xū Chí-guó 胥持國; re-promoted in Chéng-ān 5 (1200) by Zhāng-zōng's intervention; in Dà-ān 1 (1209) presented strategy on the Mongol advance, advising a flanking attack from Lín-huáng — rejected, with the predicted defeat at Xuān-dé following; held the appointment as *Hàn-lín shì-dú xué-shì* through Xuān-zōng's reign; in Xìng-dìng (1217–) named *Lǐ-bù shàng-shū jiān shì-dú xué-shì tóng-xiū guó-shǐ*; oversaw the 1218 examination at which he selected the controversially formal-irregular Lǐ Xiàn-néng 李獻能 as top *jǔ-rén*; presented the *Wú-yì zhí-jiě* and *Zhēn-guān zhèng-yào shēn-jiàn* (*Direct Exposition of "Wú-yì"*; *Mirror-of-Forgiveness on "Essentials of Zhēn-guān Government"*) to Āi-zōng for moral instruction; in Zhèng-dà 9 (1232 first month) composed Āi-zōng's surrender amnesty proclamation; died Zhèng-dà 9, fifth month, *rén-chén* day, aged 74. The collection embraces *fù*, *gǔ-shī*, *lǜ-shī*, *jué-jù*, *zá-tǐ*, *biǎo* / *jiǎn* / *qǐ*, *bēi*, *jì*, *lùn*, *yǐn*, *sòng*, *zhēn*, *zàn*, *jì-wén*, *shū-qǐ*, *tí-bá* — the most comprehensive surviving genre-range of any Jīn-period literary collection. The preface by **Yáng Yún-yì** 楊雲翼 (1170–1228) of Yuán-guāng 2 (1223), praising Zhào as the *zhǔ-méng* (presiding master) of Jīn letters and the contemporary cultural arbiter, is preserved here.

### Tiyao

*No tíyào in the Kanripo source* (the source files reproduce the SBCK base, which preserves the *Jīn-shǐ* biography of Zhào Bǐng-wén and Yáng Yún-yì's 1223 preface, but not the Qián-lóng *Sìkù* tíyào). The *Sìkù quán-shū zǒng-mù tí-yào* records the *Fǔ-shuǐ jí* in twenty *juàn* under "Jīn *bié-jí lèi*," classing Zhào as one of the three principal Jīn-dynasty *bié-jí* authors (with Wáng Ruò-xū [[KR4d0418]] and Wáng Jì [[KR4d0416]]) whose collections survive intact. The editors emphasize the breadth of Zhào's literary range, his role as *zhǔ-méng* of late-Jīn letters across four reigns, and the historiographical importance of his collection as a primary source for the literary culture of the Jīn imperial court and Hàn-lín bureau under Shì-zōng through Āi-zōng.

### Abstract

Zhào Bǐng-wén (CBDB 28846, 1159–1232) is the senior figure of late-Jīn literary culture, the academic-literary lineage-master of Yuán Hǎo-wèn (whose 1223 *bá* to this collection is preserved in some recensions), and the principal cultural architect of the Xuān-zōng / Āi-zōng courts (1213–1234) — the final two decades of the Jīn dynasty. The collection — in twenty *juàn* — preserves his entire major prose corpus: court documents (*biǎo*, *jiǎn*, *qǐ*, *jì-wén*, *zhì*, *bēi*, *shì-yì*, *āi-cè*, *gào*); literary criticism and historical *lùn* (Western Hàn, Eastern Hàn, Wèi-Jìn *zhèng-míng*, Shǔ-Hàn *zhèng-míng*, Táng, *Zhī-rén*, *Qiān-dū*, *Hóu-shǒu*, *Zhí*); foundation-text introductions (*yǐn*) to the *Zhú-xī xiān-shēng wén-jí*, the *Fǎ-yán wēi-zhǐ*, the *Dào-xué fǎ-yuán*, his own *Jiān Tài-xuán zàn yǐn* and *Zhōng-shuō lèi-jiě yǐn*; the eight surviving *fù* of *juàn* 2 (the *Dà-chūn fù*, *Qī-xiá fù*, *Cóng-tái fù*, *Jiě cháo-xǐng fù*, *Hǎi-qīng fù*, *Fǎn Xiǎo-shān fù*, *Láng-shān fù*, *Huá-shān gǎn-gǔ fù*, *Lì-péng fù*, *Yóu Xuán-quán fù*, *Wú-jìn-zàng fù*, *Zhuō-xuān fù*, *Yóu Xī-yuán fù*, *Xīn-jìng tiān-dì zhī jiàn fù*); voluminous landscape, occasional, and presentation verse; nine pieces of religious paratext (*qīng-cí*, *jì-wén*, *zhēn*); and twenty-three pieces of title-and-postface scholarship on Sū Shì, Mǐ Fú, Wén Tóng, Sū Zhé, Sū Xún, Huáng Tíng-jiān, Yáng Níng-shì, Wáng Tíng-jūn — a major archive of late-Jīn calligraphic-aesthetic discourse. Zhào's other principal surviving works are his *Dào-dé zhēn-jīng jí-jiě* [[KR5c0080]] (Daoist scholarship), the *Tài-xuán jiān-zàn*, *Zhōng-shuō lèi-jiě*, and *Nán-huá lüè-shì*. CBDB 28846 firmly establishes 1159–1232; the *Jīn-shǐ* biography fully confirms. Wilkinson treats Zhào extensively in the Jīn-dynasty literary culture (§29).

## Other points of interest

The *Jīn-shǐ* biography preserved in the frontmatter notes that Zhào "in late life rather sullied [his name] with Chán [Buddhist] sayings," registering the Confucian historiographical reservation about Zhào's late-life involvement in Chán Buddhist and Quán-zhēn Daoist circles — the same syncretism that produced his *Dào-dé zhēn-jīng jí-jiě*. The reservation is itself historiographically interesting as a marker of the Yuán-period (Tuō-tuō *Jīn-shǐ*) editorial stance on the late-Jīn Three-Teachings synthesis. The 1218 examination affair — in which Zhào's selection of Lǐ Xiàn-néng as top *jǔ-rén*, despite Lǐ's irregular *fù* form, provoked a scandal — is one of the few late-Jīn literary controversies preserved in detail.
