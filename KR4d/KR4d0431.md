# Shàn-yuán wén-jí 剡源文集
*The Shàn-yuán Collection*
by [[戴表元]] (撰)

## About the work

The thirty-*juàn* collected works of Dài Biǎo-yuán 戴表元 (CBDB 28306, 1244–1310), *zì* Shuài-chū 帥初, alternate *zì* Zēng-bó 曾伯, *hào* Shàn-yuán 剡源 (after his retirement site at Yú-lín 榆林 in Shàn-yuán 剡源, Fèng-huà 奉化, Míng-zhōu) and Zhì-yě wēng 質野翁 / Chōng-ān lǎo-rén 充安老人 in old age; native of Fèng-huà-zhōu 奉化州 (modern Níng-bō region, Zhè-jiāng). Studied under the foundational late-Sòng masters **Wáng Yīng-lín** 王應麟 [[KR2g0030 et al.]] (of Sì-míng) and **Shū Yuè-xiáng** 舒岳祥 (of Tiān-tái) and was inducted into both lineages of late-Sòng literary and historical learning. Sòng Xián-chún 10 (1274) *jìnshì* by the *sān-shè-fǎ* internal-promotion (at age 31); appointed *jiào-shòu* of Jiàn-níng-fǔ; declined re-appointment to Lín-ān *jiào-shòu* and *Hù-bù zhǎng-gù* posts. After 1276 retired to Yú-lín in his native Fèng-huà. In Dà-dé 8 (1304, age 60+) accepted Yuán recruitment as *jiào-shòu* of Xìn-zhōu 信州; re-appointed at Wū-zhōu 婺州 but declined on illness. Considered by his contemporaries — Yuán Hàn-lín *xiū-zhuàn-bó-shì* called him "the foremost figure of Jiāng-nán *wén-zhāng* (literary art) in the Zhì-yuán / Dà-dé era," and through his disciple **Yuán Jué** 袁桷 (1266–1327) Dài's literary lineage passed to the *Yuán sì dà jiā* (Four Great Masters of Yuán). His student **Rèn Shì-lín** 任士林 [[KR4d0444]] is another Yuán-period figure. The collection was first compiled and prefaced by **Sòng Lián** 宋濂 in 1371 (Hóng-wǔ 4 / *Hàn-lín shì-zhì* / National Historiography Office); cut by Sòng during his *Sī-yè* tenure at the Imperial University; re-cut at the *Nán-tài-xué* in Wàn-lì *xīn-sì* (1581) by Dài's eight-generation descendant Dài Xún 戴洵 (then *Sī-chéng* / Imperial University Chancellor); supplemented by **Zhōu Yí** 周儀 (*Hùa-dōng* official, 1573 — preface dated *guǐ-yǒu* — compiling additional pieces over 15–16 years until 1572 in his Sì-chuān posting) and re-cut at Chongqing (Bā-yú); the present base reflects the Zhōu Yí–Dài Xún Wàn-lì recension. The collection is the largest and most substantial early-Yuán Hàn-Confucian *bié-jí* of the Jiāng-nán region after Yuán Hǎo-wèn (whose Yuán-arc was already in the Hé-běi context) and Wú Chéng [[KR4d0446]]: 30 *juàn* — 6 of *jì* (records), 11 of *xù* (prefaces), 2 of *mù-zhì* (epitaphs), 1 of *biǎo-bēi* (steles), 2 of *tí* (colophons), 1 of *bá-míng-zhēn-zàn* (miscellaneous epigraphs), 1 of *fù*, 1 of *shǐ-lùn* (historical essays), 1 of *zá-zhù* (essays), 1 of *jì-wén* (eulogies), 1 of *shū-qǐ* / *shū-shū* / *zhuàn* / *zhá-zǐ* / *shuō* / *yù* / *shū* (correspondence and misc.), 2 of *jiǎng-yì* (classical exegesis on *Lǐ-jì* and *Lúnyǔ*), 4 of poetry (*gǔ-shī* / *pái-lǜ* / *gǔ-shī* 七言 / *lǜ-shī* / *jué-jù*).

### Tiyao

*No Sìkù tíyào in the Kanripo source* (the SBCK base preserves instead five foundational Míng prefaces: by Sòng Lián 宋濂 (1371, Hóng-wǔ 4); by Dài Xún 戴洵 (1581); by Zhōu Yí 周儀 / Yǔ-kě-fǔ 羽可甫 (1573); by Zhōu Rǔ-lì 周汝礪 (1581); plus the *Yuán-shǐ* biography excerpt for the front matter). The standard Sìkù tíyào outside the source places the work under "Yuán *bié-jí lèi*" and characterizes Dài as the principal Jiāng-nán Yuán-period literary master of the Zhì-yuán / Dà-dé era — heir to Wáng Yīng-lín and Shū Yuè-xiáng, teacher of Yuán Jué, transitional figure between the late-Sòng and high-Yuán literary cultures of the Sì-míng region.

### Abstract

Dài Biǎo-yuán (CBDB 28306, 1244–1310) is one of the foundational early-Yuán Hàn-Confucian literary masters of Jiāng-nán. His career — Sòng 1274 *jìnshì*, brief Jiàn-níng *jiào-shòu*, retirement after 1276 to Yú-lín, late-life acceptance of Yuán Xìn-zhōu *jiào-shòu* recruitment in 1304 — embodies the late-life accommodation to Yuán service that the *yí-mín* generation eventually accepted under economic pressure (Sòng Lián's preface emphasizes that "[Dài's] family was poor, the destruction-and-burning [aftermath of 1276] meant clothing and food were doubly scarce; he then turned his whole heart to reading and selling-prose-to-feed-his-old-and-young"). His most consequential historiographical role is as the link between his teachers Wáng Yīng-lín / Shū Yuè-xiáng and his student Yuán Jué (1266–1327, whose Yuán-period official career and literary work established the *Jīn-huá / Sì-míng* literary line that fed into the *Yuán sì dà jiā* of high-Yuán literature: Yú Jí, Yáng Zài, Fàn Hēng, Jiē Xī-sī). Sòng Lián's 1371 preface, written for the original print at the Míng *Nán-tài-xué*, anchors Dài in the post-Sòng Confucian literary recovery; Dài Xún's 1581 re-cutting at the same institution (as the eighth-generation descendant and then *Sī-chéng* of the *Nán-tài-xué*) renews the institutional transmission. Composition window: from Dài's 1274 *jìnshì* and youthful Jiàn-níng *jiào-shòu* service through his 1310 death, with the bulk in the post-1276 retirement at Yú-lín. CBDB 28306 firmly establishes 1244–1310. Wilkinson treats Dài extensively (§28.1, §35).

## Other points of interest

The Míng print history — original Sòng Lián 1371 print at the *Nán-tài-xué*; Wàn-lì 1581 re-cut at the same institution by Dài's eight-generation descendant Dài Xún as then-Chancellor — is one of the rare cases where a Yuán *bié-jí*'s Míng-period institutional preservation passes through the same lineal-descendant-as-administrator arrangement that operated for many Yuán-and-Míng family-collected-works compilations. The prose's clear-and-balanced "*pǐng-shēn yǎ-jié huà chén-fǔ wèi shén-qí*" (clear-and-elegant, transforming the dead-old into the spirit-strange) style was a deliberate Yuán-period reaction against the late-Sòng *jiāng-hú* poetics that Fāng Huí [[方回]]'s *Yíng-kuí lǜ-suǐ* dominantly defined; Yuán Jué's literary inheritance from Dài took this critical posture forward.
