# Shànyuán wénjí 剡源文集
*The Shàn-yuán Collection*
by [[戴表元]] (撰)

## About the work

The thirty-*juàn* collected works of Dài Biǎoyuán 戴表元 (CBDB 28306, 1244–1310), *zì* Shuàichū 帥初, alternate *zì* Zēngbó 曾伯, *hào* Shànyuán 剡源 (after his retirement site at Yúlín 榆林 in Shànyuán 剡源, Fènghuà 奉化, Míngzhōu) and Zhìyě wēng 質野翁 / Chōngān lǎorén 充安老人 in old age; native of Fènghuàzhōu 奉化州 (modern Níngbō region, Zhèjiāng). Studied under the foundational late-Sòng masters **Wáng Yīnglín** 王應麟 [[KR2g0030 et al.]] (of Sìmíng) and **Shū Yuèxiáng** 舒岳祥 (of Tiāntái) and was inducted into both lineages of late-Sòng literary and historical learning. Sòng Xiánchún 10 (1274) *jìnshì* by the *sānshèfǎ* internal-promotion (at age 31); appointed *jiàoshòu* of Jiànníngfǔ; declined re-appointment to Línān *jiàoshòu* and *Hùbù zhǎnggù* posts. After 1276 retired to Yúlín in his native Fènghuà. In Dàdé 8 (1304, age 60+) accepted Yuán recruitment as *jiàoshòu* of Xìnzhōu 信州; re-appointed at Wūzhōu 婺州 but declined on illness. Considered by his contemporaries — Yuán Hànlín *xiūzhuànbóshì* called him "the foremost figure of Jiāngnán *wénzhāng* (literary art) in the Zhìyuán / Dàdé era," and through his disciple **Yuán Jué** 袁桷 (1266–1327) Dài's literary lineage passed to the *Yuán sì dà jiā* (Four Great Masters of Yuán). His student **Rèn Shìlín** 任士林 [[KR4d0444]] is another Yuán-period figure. The collection was first compiled and prefaced by **Sòng Lián** 宋濂 in 1371 (Hóngwǔ 4 / *Hànlín shìzhì* / National Historiography Office); cut by Sòng during his *Sīyè* tenure at the Imperial University; re-cut at the *Nántàixué* in Wànlì *xīnsì* (1581) by Dài's eight-generation descendant Dài Xún 戴洵 (then *Sīchéng* / Imperial University Chancellor); supplemented by **Zhōu Yí** 周儀 (*Hùadōng* official, 1573 — preface dated *guǐyǒu* — compiling additional pieces over 15–16 years until 1572 in his Sìchuān posting) and re-cut at Chongqing (Bāyú); the present base reflects the Zhōu Yí–Dài Xún Wànlì recension. The collection is the largest and most substantial early-Yuán Hàn-Confucian *biéjí* of the Jiāngnán region after Yuán Hǎowèn (whose Yuán-arc was already in the Héběi context) and Wú Chéng [[KR4d0446]]: 30 *juàn* — 6 of *jì* (records), 11 of *xù* (prefaces), 2 of *mùzhì* (epitaphs), 1 of *biǎobēi* (steles), 2 of *tí* (colophons), 1 of *bámíngzhēnzàn* (miscellaneous epigraphs), 1 of *fù*, 1 of *shǐlùn* (historical essays), 1 of *zázhù* (essays), 1 of *jìwén* (eulogies), 1 of *shūqǐ* / *shūshū* / *zhuàn* / *zházǐ* / *shuō* / *yù* / *shū* (correspondence and misc.), 2 of *jiǎngyì* (classical exegesis on *Lǐjì* and *Lúnyǔ*), 4 of poetry (*gǔshī* / *páilǜ* / *gǔshī* 七言 / *lǜshī* / *juéjù*).

### Tiyao

*No Sìkù tíyào in the Kanripo source* (the SBCK base preserves instead five foundational Míng prefaces: by Sòng Lián 宋濂 (1371, Hóngwǔ 4); by Dài Xún 戴洵 (1581); by Zhōu Yí 周儀 / Yǔkěfǔ 羽可甫 (1573); by Zhōu Rǔlì 周汝礪 (1581); plus the *Yuánshǐ* biography excerpt for the front matter). The standard Sìkù tíyào outside the source places the work under "Yuán *biéjí lèi*" and characterizes Dài as the principal Jiāngnán Yuán-period literary master of the Zhìyuán / Dàdé era — heir to Wáng Yīnglín and Shū Yuèxiáng, teacher of Yuán Jué, transitional figure between the late-Sòng and high-Yuán literary cultures of the Sìmíng region.

### Abstract

Dài Biǎoyuán (CBDB 28306, 1244–1310) is one of the foundational early-Yuán Hàn-Confucian literary masters of Jiāngnán. His career — Sòng 1274 *jìnshì*, brief Jiànníng *jiàoshòu*, retirement after 1276 to Yúlín, late-life acceptance of Yuán Xìnzhōu *jiàoshòu* recruitment in 1304 — embodies the late-life accommodation to Yuán service that the *yímín* generation eventually accepted under economic pressure (Sòng Lián's preface emphasizes that "[Dài's] family was poor, the destruction-and-burning [aftermath of 1276] meant clothing and food were doubly scarce; he then turned his whole heart to reading and selling-prose-to-feed-his-old-and-young"). His most consequential historiographical role is as the link between his teachers Wáng Yīnglín / Shū Yuèxiáng and his student Yuán Jué (1266–1327, whose Yuán-period official career and literary work established the *Jīnhuá / Sìmíng* literary line that fed into the *Yuán sì dà jiā* of high-Yuán literature: Yú Jí, Yáng Zài, Fàn Hēng, Jiē Xīsī). Sòng Lián's 1371 preface, written for the original print at the Míng *Nántàixué*, anchors Dài in the post-Sòng Confucian literary recovery; Dài Xún's 1581 re-cutting at the same institution (as the eighth-generation descendant and then *Sīchéng* of the *Nántàixué*) renews the institutional transmission. Composition window: from Dài's 1274 *jìnshì* and youthful Jiànníng *jiàoshòu* service through his 1310 death, with the bulk in the post-1276 retirement at Yúlín. CBDB 28306 firmly establishes 1244–1310. Wilkinson treats Dài extensively (§28.1, §35).

## Other points of interest

The Míng print history — original Sòng Lián 1371 print at the *Nántàixué*; Wànlì 1581 re-cut at the same institution by Dài's eight-generation descendant Dài Xún as then-Chancellor — is one of the rare cases where a Yuán *biéjí*'s Míng-period institutional preservation passes through the same lineal-descendant-as-administrator arrangement that operated for many Yuán-and-Míng family-collected-works compilations. The prose's clear-and-balanced "*pǐngshēn yǎjié huà chénfǔ wèi shénqí*" (clear-and-elegant, transforming the dead-old into the spirit-strange) style was a deliberate Yuán-period reaction against the late-Sòng *jiānghú* poetics that Fāng Huí [[方回]]'s *Yíngkuí lǜsuǐ* dominantly defined; Yuán Jué's literary inheritance from Dài took this critical posture forward.
