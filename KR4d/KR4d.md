# KR4d 別集類·宋金元 — Individual Collected Works of the Sòng, Jīn, and Yuán

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR4d sits inside the *Sìkù quánshū jíbù* 集部 *biéjí lèi* 別集類 (Collections → individual collected works), the Sìkù compilers' chronological *biéjí* sequence, immediately after KR4c (Tang and Five Dynasties) and before KR4e (Ming) and KR4f (Qing). It is the **SòngJīnYuán segment**: 415 Sòng, 5 Jīn, and 175 Yuán biéjí, 595 entries in all — the second-largest dynastic block in KR4 after KR4f, and the largest dynastic block in KR4 if Yuán-into-early-Míng cataloging conventions are taken into account. The opening is Xú Xuàn's *Qíshěng jí* 騎省集 ([[KR4d0001]], 991 — a Five-Dynasties → Sòng transition figure whose Sòng dating places him in this division rather than KR4c) and the closing tail is the late-Yuán *Tiěyá* circle and the painter-poet Ní Zàn 倪瓚 ([[KR4d0575]], [[KR4d0576]], death 1374) on the cusp of the Míng.

The division is the **first dynastic *biéjí* block where collected works survive in critical mass**. Wilkinson, *Chinese History: A New Manual* §30.4.3, gives the survival figures: of pre-Táng *biéjí* only nineteen reach the Sìkù; of perhaps a thousand recorded Táng *biéjí* roughly three hundred survive; but for the Sòng about **800 individuals' biéjí survive** (≈ 20–30 % of those printed), and for Liáo / Jīn / Yuán together more than 250 survive. The reason is the printing revolution: from the Northern Sòng forward, *biéjí* are printed during or shortly after their authors' lifetimes by family, disciples, and provincial offices, and are no longer dependent on a single manuscript line. Wilkinson §62.3.10 gives the headline figure that the Sìkù itself contains 355 Sòng *biéjí*; the Kanripo extends slightly past that selection (415 Sòng entries) by including a handful of *Sìkù cúnmù* and post-Sìkù collections. For Yuán, Wilkinson §64.3.8 records about 250 extant *biéjí*, of which 175 are catalogued here. The standard modern reference catalogues are Zhū Shàngshū 祝尚書 *Sòngrén biéjí xùlù* 宋人別集敘錄 (rev. 2020) and Lù Jùnlíng 陸峻嶺 *Yuánrén wénjí piānmù fēnlèi suǒyǐn* 元人文集篇目分類索引 (1979).

Two structural points govern reading the division. First, the *biéjí* form itself is functionally biographical: a Sòng or Yuán *biéjí* contains both the literary output (*shī*, *fù*, *cí*, *jì*, *xù*, *bēi*) and the official documentary residue (*zòuyì*, *gōngdú*, *qǐ*, *zhuàng*, *zhì*, *biǎo*, *jiàoshū*) of a single life — making KR4d simultaneously the documentary archive of the SòngYuán bureaucracy and the literary corpus of the same period (cf. §19.7: ~55 % of Táng-through-Qīng *biéjí* are by *jìnshì* holders). Second, the Sòng innovated the now-standard arrangement of *biéjí* contents — by genre at the top level, then chronologically within each genre — explicitly tied to the parallel Sòng establishment of the *niánpǔ* 年譜 (Wilkinson §30.4.1). This means KR4d collections can be read as ordered biographical sources in a way Táng *biéjí* often cannot. A high proportion of KR4d entries — at least two dozen — survive only through *Sìkù* editorial reconstruction from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* 永樂大典, the most aggressive imperial recovery program of the eighteenth century.

For the period-level periodization Wilkinson is silent on the school-level subdivisions of Sòng poetry (*Xīkūn tǐ* 西崑體, *Jiāngxī shīpài* 江西詩派, *Yǒngjiā sìlíng* 永嘉四靈, *Jiānghúpài* 江湖派), all of which are nevertheless basic to reading the division and for which the supplementary reference is Denecke et al., *Oxford Handbook of Classical Chinese Literature*.

## Important texts and text clusters

- **Early Northern Sòng *gǔwén* and reform-poetry pioneers (c. 990–1060).** [[KR4d0001]] *Qíshěng jí* 騎省集 (Xú Xuàn 徐鉉), [[KR4d0009]] *Xiǎoxù jí* 小畜集 (Wáng Yǔchèn 王禹偁), [[KR4d0012]] *Wǔyí xīnjí* 武夷新集 (Yáng Yì 楊億, the *Xīkūn tǐ* anchor), [[KR4d0013]] *Lín Héjìng jí* 林和靖集 (Lín Bū 林逋), [[KR4d0014]] / [[KR4d0015]] *Mù cānjūn jí* / *Hénán Mùgōng jí* (Mù Xiū 穆修), [[KR4d0025]] *Fàn Wénzhèng jí* 范文正集 (Fàn Zhòngyān), [[KR4d0028]] *Cúlái jí* 徂徠集 (Shí Jiè 石介), [[KR4d0034]] *Sū xuéshì jí* 蘇學士集 (Sū Shùnqīn 蘇舜欽), [[KR4d0056]] *Wǎnlíng jí* 宛陵集 (Méi Yáochén 梅堯臣). Together these document the decisive break from late-Táng / *Xīkūn* aestheticism towards the *Qìnglì* (1041–48) reform-period prose-and-verse program.
- **The Northern Sòng *bā dàjiā* and the Sū circle.** Five of the six Sòng *TángSòng bā dàjiā* are present: Ōuyáng Xiū's principal *biéjí* [[KR4d0068]] *Wénzhōng jí* 文忠集 (153 *juǎn*, edited by Zhōu Bìdà 周必大, with a digest at [[KR4d0069]] *Ōuyáng wéncuì*); [[KR4d0073]] *Línchuān wénjí* 臨川文集 + [[KR4d0074]] *Wáng Jīnggōng shīzhù* (Wáng Ānshí 王安石, with Lǐ Bì 李壁's 1207 commentary); [[KR4d0054]] *Yuánfēng lèigǎo* 元豐類藁 (Zēng Gǒng 曾鞏); [[KR4d0072]] *Jiāyòu jí* 嘉祐集 (Sū Xún 蘇洵); [[KR4d0076]] *Dōngpō quánjí* 東坡全集 (Sū Shì 蘇軾, the post-loss reconstruction by Cài Shìyīng 蔡士英 in 115 *juǎn* under the Kāngxī court) with its four-text Sòng / Yuán / Qīng commentary apparatus [[KR4d0077]]–[[KR4d0080]] and Zhā Shènxíng 查慎行's [[KR4d0081]] *Sūshī bǔzhù* (1702 — the only Qīng entry in the division, retained because it is part of the Sūshī commentarial ecology); [[KR4d0082]] / [[KR4d0083]] *Luánchéng jí* / *Yìngzhào jí* (Sū Zhé 蘇轍).
- **The *Sūmén sì xuéshì* 蘇門四學士 and the *Jiāngxī shīpài*.** [[KR4d0085]] / [[KR4d0086]] *Shāngǔ nèijí shīzhù* / *wàijí shīzhù* (Huáng Tíngjiān 黃庭堅, with Rèn Yuān 任淵 / Shǐ Róng 史容's annotated editions, the founding text of the *Jiāngxī shīpài*); [[KR4d0087]] / [[KR4d0088]] *Hòushān jí* / *Hòushān shīzhù* (Chén Shīdào 陳師道, with Rèn Yuān again); [[KR4d0090]] *Zhāng yòushǐ wénjí* (Zhāng Lěi 張耒); [[KR4d0091]] *Huáihǎi jí* 淮海集 (Qín Guān 秦觀); [[KR4d0105]] *Jīlèi jí* 雞肋集 (Cháo Bǔzhī 晁補之). [[KR4d0195]] *Dōnglái shī jí* (Lǚ Běnzhōng 呂本中) carries the *Jiāngxī shīpài tú* 江西詩派圖 — the canon-list that constituted the school as a literary identity. The principal Northern-Sòng → Southern-Sòng poet-bridge is [[KR4d0153]] *Jiǎnzhāi jí* 簡齋集 (Chén Yǔyì 陳與義).
- **The conservative pole and the great Northern-Sòng official corpora.** [[KR4d0040]] *Chuánjiā jí* 傳家集 (Sīmǎ Guāng 司馬光); [[KR4d0024]] *Ānyáng jí* 安陽集 (Hán Qí 韓琦); [[KR4d0029]] *Duānmíng jí* 端明集 (Cài Xiāng 蔡襄); [[KR4d0035]] *Sū Wèigōng wénjí* (Sū Sòng 蘇頌, the future *Xīnyí xiàng* astronomer); [[KR4d0061]] *Lùgōng wénjí* (Wén Yànbó 文彥博); [[KR4d0044]] *Gōngshì jí* + [[KR4d0045]] *Péngchéng jí* (the Liú 劉敞 / 劉攽 brothers).
- **The early *Dàoxué* and *Lǐxué* literary corpora.** Although the *yǔlù* and treatises live in KR3a / KR3c, the literary collections of the founding cohort are filed here: [[KR4d0062]] *Jīrǎng jí* 擊壤集 (Shào Yōng 邵雍, with its distinctive *xiàngshù*-inflected *yǒngshǐ shī*); [[KR4d0065]] *Zhōu Yuángōng jí* (Zhōu Dūnyí 周敦頤); [[KR4d0136]] / [[KR4d0137]] *Guīshān jí* / *xiānshēng yǔlù* (Yáng Shí 楊時); [[KR4d0182]] *Yùzhāng wénjí* (Luó Cóngyàn 羅從彥, of the *Yánpíng sān xiānshēng*); [[KR4d0177]] *Píngshān jí* (Liú Zǐhuī 劉子翬, one of Zhū Xī's teachers); and the central [[KR4d0226]] *Huìān jí* 晦菴集 (Zhū Xī 朱熹, 1265 Hú Bīngwén edition), which Wilkinson §30.4.1 takes as the paradigmatic late-imperial *biéjí*. The opposing *Xīnxué* pole is [[KR4d0253]] *Xiàngshān jí* 象山集 (Lù Jiǔyuān 陸九淵). The senior late-Sòng *Lǐxué* officialdom: [[KR4d0306]] *Xīshān wénjí* 西山文集 (Zhēn Déxiù 真德秀) and [[KR4d0305]] *Hèshān jí* 鶴山集 (Wèi Liǎowēng 魏了翁).
- **The *Jìngkāng* trauma and Sòng patriotic literature.** A distinctive cluster running from the Jīn invasion through the early Southern-Sòng: [[KR4d0188]] *Shǎoyáng jí* 少陽集 (Chén Dōng 陳東, executed 1127); [[KR4d0135]] *Zōng zhōngjiǎn jí* 宗忠簡集 (Zōng Zé 宗澤); [[KR4d0138]] *Liángxī jí* 梁谿集 (Lǐ Gāng 李綱); [[KR4d0148]] *Zhōngzhèng déwén jí* (Zhào Dǐng 趙鼎); [[KR4d0191]] *Yuè Wǔmù yíwén* 岳武穆遺文 (the surviving prose of Yuè Fēi 岳飛, edited by Xú Jiē 徐階 in the Míng); [[KR4d0196]] *Dànān wénjí* 澹菴文集 (Hú Quán 胡銓, of the famous 1138 anti-peace memorial). These collections are continuous in spirit with the late-Sòng loyalist cluster below and constitute the principal documentary basis for the historiography of the *Jìngkāng* and *Shàoxīng* (1127–1162) reconstitution.
- **The *Zhōngxīng sì dà shīrén* 中興四大詩人.** The four canonical High Southern-Sòng poets: [[KR4d0265]] *Shíhú shījí* (Fàn Chéngdà 范成大), [[KR4d0266]] *Chéngzhāi jí* 誠齋集 (Yáng Wànlǐ 楊萬里), [[KR4d0229]] *Liángxī yígǎo* (Yóu Mào 尤袤), and the largest single-author cluster of the division at [[KR4d0267]]–[[KR4d0270]] (Lù Yóu 陸游's *Jiànnán shīgǎo* 劎南詩槀, *Wèinán wénjí* 渭南文集, *Fàngwēng shīxuǎn*, and *Jiàngǔ jīngxuǎn Lù Fàngwēng*).
- **The *Yǒngjiā* and *Yǒngkāng* utilitarian schools and their Daoxue counterparts.** [[KR4d0264]] *Làngyǔ jí* 浪語集 (Xuē Jìxuān 薛季宣, founder), [[KR4d0233]] *Zhǐzhāi jí* 止齋集 (Chén Fùliáng 陳傅良), [[KR4d0273]] *Shuǐxīn jí* 水心集 (Yè Shì 葉適, the mature *Yǒngjiā gōnglìxué* synthesis), [[KR4d0303]] *Lóngchuān jí* 龍川集 (Chén Liàng 陳亮, the *Yǒngkāng* utilitarian and Zhū Xī's interlocutor in the famous *wángbà* exchange). Their constitutional-historical counterpart is [[KR4d0232]] *Dōnglái jí* (Lǚ Zǔqiān 呂祖謙) and [[KR4d0284]] *Nánxuān jí* (Zhāng Shì 張栻, edited by Zhū Xī).
- **The *Yǒngjiā sìlíng* 永嘉四靈 and the *Jiānghúpài* 江湖派.** The reaction against *Jiāngxī*-school recondite verse: [[KR4d0297]] *Fānglánxuān jí* (Xú Zhào 徐照) and [[KR4d0298]] *Èrwēitíng shījí* (Xú Jǐ 徐璣), two of the four *Sìlíng* (the others — Zhào Shīxiù 趙師秀 and Wēng Juǎn 翁卷 — survive only outside KR4d). The *Jiānghúpài* anchors are [[KR4d0278]] *Shípíng shījí* 石屏詩集 (Dài Fùgǔ 戴復古) and [[KR4d0340]] *Hòucūn jí* 後村集 (Liú Kèzhuāng 劉克莊, also the principal *Jiānghú* anthologist).
- **The Sòng loyalist (*yímín* 遺民) corpus.** [[KR4d0365]] *Wénshān jí* 文山集 + [[KR4d0366]] *Wén Xìnguó jí Dù shī* (Wén Tiānxiáng 文天祥, including the *Yuándū* prison-cell *Jí Dù shī* assembled wholly from Dù Fǔ couplets); [[KR4d0367]] *Diéshān jí* 叠山集 (Xiè Fāngdé 謝枋得); [[KR4d0395]] *Jìshān wénjí* (Lín Jǐngxī 林景熙, of the famous *Mèngmò shī* on the desecration of the Six Sòng tombs); [[KR4d0331]] *Sānshān Zhèng Júshān xiānshēng qīngjùn jí* (attributed to Zhèng Sīxiào 鄭思肖); [[KR4d0382]] *Sìmíng wénxiàn jí* (Wáng Yìnglín 王應麟); [[KR4d0406]] *Bóyá qín* 伯牙琴 (Dèng Mù 鄧牧, anti-totalitarian remnant prose); [[KR4d0407]] *Cúnyǎtáng yígǎo* (Fāng Fèng 方鳳); [[KR4d0374]] *Xūxī jí* 須溪集 (Liú Chénwēng 劉辰翁, also the principal late-Sòng anthologist and commentator on Tang poetry).
- **The Jīn dynasty (5 entries).** [[KR4d0416]] *Zhuōxuān jí* (Wáng Jì 王寂); [[KR4d0417]] *Fǔshuǐ jí* 滏水集 (Zhào Bǐngwén 趙秉文, the head of the late-Jīn *wénrén*); [[KR4d0418]] *Hūnán jí* 滹南集 (Wáng Ruòxū 王若虛); [[KR4d0419]] *Zhuāngjìng jí* (Lǐ Jùnmín 李俊民); and the pivotal Jīn → Yuán figure [[KR4d0420]] *Yíshān jí* 遺山集 (Yuán Hàowèn 元好問, the supreme Jīn poet whose *Zhōngzhōu jí* preserves the rest of Jīn verse).
- **Early-Yuán northern statesmen.** [[KR4d0421]] *Zhànrán jūshì jí* 湛然居士集 (Yēlǜ Chǔcái 耶律楚材, the Khitan Buddhist who served Genghis Khan and Ögedei); [[KR4d0424]] *Língchuān jí* (Hè Jīng 郝經, who refused the southern Sòng seal-bearing mission and was held seventeen years); [[KR4d0464]] *Qiūjiàn jí* (Wáng Yùn 王惲); [[KR4d0465]] *Mùān jí* (Yáo Suì 姚燧, the most distinguished early-Yuán prose stylist); [[KR4d0466]] *Xuělóu jí* (Chéng Jùfū 程鉅夫); [[KR4d0453]] *Lǔzhāi yíshū* (Xǔ Héng 許衡, the northern *Lǐxué* master); [[KR4d0454]] *Jìngxiū xiānshēng wénjí* (Liú Yīn 劉因, the model of non-cooperation).
- **The Sòng → Yuán literary bridge and the southern *Lǐxué*.** [[KR4d0431]] *Shànyuán wénjí* (Dài Biǎoyuán 戴表元, the principal stylistic bridge); [[KR4d0428]] *Tóngjiāng xùjí* (Fāng Huí 方回, surrendered Sòng prefect, compiler of the *Yíngkuí lǜsuǐ* 瀛奎律髓 and the principal Yuán theorist of the *Jiāngxī* tradition); [[KR4d0445]] *Sōngxuězhāi jí* 松雪齋集 (Zhào Mèngfǔ 趙孟頫, calligrapher–painter–poet, the most consequential collaborating Hànrén of the early Yuán); [[KR4d0446]] *Wú Wénzhèng jí* (Wú Chéng 吳澄, the southern *Lǐxué* master).
- **The *Yányòu* generation: the *Yuán shī sì dà jiā* 元詩四大家 and the *Rúlín sìjié* 儒林四傑.** The mature mid-Yuán imperial-academy literary establishment, crystallized after the 1313 restoration of the examinations: [[KR4d0493]] *Dàoyuán xuégǔ lù* + [[KR4d0494]] *Dàoyuán yígǎo* (Yú Jí 虞集), [[KR4d0495]] *Yáng Zhònghóng jí* (Yáng Zǎi 楊載), [[KR4d0496]] *Fàn Déjī shījí* (Fàn Pēng 范梈), [[KR4d0497]] *Wénān jí* (Jiē Xīsī 揭傒斯) — the four together = *Yuán shī sì dà jiā*. Add [[KR4d0502]] / [[KR4d0503]] *Wénxiàn jí* / *Jīnhuá Huáng xiānshēng wénjí* (Huáng Jǐn 黃溍), [[KR4d0504]] *Guīzhāi wénjí* (Ōuyáng Xuán 歐陽玄, chief compiler of the *SòngLiáoJīn shǐ*), [[KR4d0505]] *Dàizhì jí* (Liǔ Guàn 柳貫), [[KR4d0473]] *Qīngróng jūshì jí* (Yuán Juè 袁桷); the four (Yú / Huáng / Liǔ / Ōu) = *Rúlín sìjié*. This is the documentary residue of the Tuōtuō *guóshǐyuàn* compilation enterprise.
- **The *sèmù* literati operating in Chinese.** A genuine Yuán novelty: Khitan, Tangut, Uyghur, and Mongol authors with full *biéjí*. [[KR4d0421]] (Yēlǜ Chǔcái, Khitan Buddhist); [[KR4d0491]] *Shítián wénjí* 石田文集 (Mǎ Zǔcháng 馬祖常, of Önggüd Christian descent, the most distinguished *sèmù* poet of the *Sì dà jiā* generation); [[KR4d0514]] *Yànmén jí* 雁門集 + [[KR4d0519]] *Sà Tiānxī shījí* (Sà Dūlā 薩都拉, of Türkic / Tangut origin, the leading mid-late Yuán *sèmù* poet).
- **The late-Yuán *Tiěyá* circle and the painter-poet bridge into the Míng.** [[KR4d0585]] *Dōngwéizǐ jí* + [[KR4d0586]] *Tiěyá gǔ yuèfǔ* + [[KR4d0587]] *Fùgǔ shījí* + [[KR4d0589]] *Lìzé yíyīn* (Yáng Wéizhēn 楊維楨, the transgressive *yuèfǔ* revival); [[KR4d0575]] *Ní Yúnlín xiānshēng shījí* + [[KR4d0576]] *Qīngbìgé quánjí* (Ní Zàn 倪瓚, painter-poet of the *Yuán sì dà jiā* of painting, *Hóngwǔ*-era recluse); [[KR4d0539]] *Méihuā dàorén yímò* (Wú Zhèn 吳鎮); these mark the chronological tail.
- **The *shīsēng* sub-corpus.** Roughly a dozen monk-poet collections distributed across the division: [[KR4d0031]] *Tánjīn jí* 鐔津集 (Shì Qìsōng 釋契嵩); [[KR4d0093]] *Cānliáozǐ shījí* (Shì Dàoqián 釋道潛, Sū Shì's monastic friend); [[KR4d0095]] *Shímén wénzìchán* 石門文字禪 (Shì Huìhóng 釋惠洪, the foundational *wénzìchán* corpus); [[KR4d0357]] *Běijiàn jí* (Shì Jūjiǎn 釋居簡); [[KR4d0372]] *Qiánshān jí* (Shì Wénxiàng 釋文珦). They are the documentary basis for the late-Sòng / Yuán *Chán* literary participation.

## Important persons

- [[蘇軾]] — central Northern Sòng poet, prose master, calligrapher; the most heavily commentated *biéjí* in the division, with five-text apparatus.
- [[歐陽修]] — leader of the Sòng *gǔwén* movement and de facto Sòng literary-canon arbiter; both his own *Wénzhōng jí* ([[KR4d0068]]) and Zhōu Bìdà's editorial residue shape the entire mid-Northern-Sòng prose ecology.
- [[王安石]] — *Xīnfǎ* reformer; the *Línchuān wénjí* + Lǐ Bì commentary together define the Sòng *zhèngshū* prose tradition.
- [[蘇轍]] — Sū Shì's brother and political voice; chief editor of the lost Sòng *Dōngpō qījí*.
- [[蘇洵]] — eldest of the *sān Sū* 三蘇; *bā dàjiā* essay-foundation.
- [[曾鞏]] — sixth of the *TángSòng bā dàjiā*; the model of disciplined Sòng prose.
- [[黃庭堅]] — founder of the *Jiāngxī shīpài*; the densest Northern-Sòng commentary tradition after Sū.
- [[陳師道]] — *Sūmén liù jūnzǐ*; austere Sòng *gǔshī*.
- [[秦觀]], [[張耒]], [[晁補之]] — completing the *Sūmén sì xuéshì* 蘇門四學士.
- [[司馬光]] — conservative pole, historian; *Chuánjiā jí* is the literary residue of the *Zīzhì tōngjiàn* enterprise.
- [[范仲淹]] — *Qìnglì* reformer; *Yuèyánglóu jì* programmatic prose.
- [[梅堯臣]] and [[蘇舜欽]] — co-founders of the new mid-Sòng *gǔshī* style.
- [[周敦頤]] and [[邵雍]] — Dàoxué progenitors with literary collections in this division.
- [[朱熹]] — central Southern-Sòng *Lǐxué* synthesis; *biéjí* is among the most widely read late-imperial collections.
- [[陸九淵]] — *Xīnxué* pole; the principal philosophical alternative to Zhū Xī.
- [[呂祖謙]] — *Wúxué* polymath; bridge between Zhū Xī and the *Yǒngjiā* utilitarians.
- [[陸游]], [[楊萬里]], [[范成大]], [[尤袤]] — *Zhōngxīng sì dà shīrén*.
- [[陳亮]] and [[葉適]] — *Yǒngkāng* and *Yǒngjiā* utilitarian schools, Zhū Xī's principal interlocutors.
- [[真德秀]] and [[魏了翁]] — senior late-Southern-Sòng *Lǐxué* officialdom and Sòng-loyalist transmitters.
- [[劉克莊]] — *Jiānghúpài* anchor and principal late-Sòng anthologist.
- [[戴復古]] — Itinerant *Jiānghú* poet, the popular face of late-Sòng verse.

## Topics

- **The Sòng *gǔwén* continuation and the *TángSòng bā dàjiā* canon.** The Sòng leg of the *TángSòng bā dàjiā* — Ōuyáng Xiū, Zēng Gǒng, Wáng Ānshí, the three Sū — anchors the Sìkù conception of mature classical prose. The post-Sū Northern-Sòng *gǔwén* legacy then bifurcates into the Sòng *Dàoxué* prose tradition (Zhū Xī's letters and disquisitions) and the Yǒngjiā / Yǒngkāng utilitarian prose (Yè Shì, Chén Liàng); the trajectory continues through the Yuán in the Yú Jí / Huáng Jǐn / Liǔ Guàn / Ōuyáng Xuán generation as the model for state-document prose. KR4d is the principal documentary archive of this arc.
- **The *Xīkūn* → reform-poetry → *Jiāngxī* → *Sìlíng* / *Jiānghú* axis of Sòng poetry.** The early-Sòng Yáng Yì–Liú Yún ornamental *Xīkūn tǐ* is displaced in the 1040s by the Méi Yáochén / Sū Shùnqīn reform program; this matures into the SūHuáng *Dōngpō* / *Shāngǔ* style; the *Jiāngxī shīpài* (Lǚ Běnzhōng's canonical list) carries it through the Northern-Southern Sòng transition; the *Yǒngjiā sìlíng* and the *Jiānghúpài* mount a late-twelfth-century reaction recovering late-Tang plain-style verse; Yán Yǔ's *Cānglàng shīhuà* codifies the resulting periodization. Almost every major *biéjí* in KR4d either belongs to or argues against one of these positions.
- **Cí 詞 versus shī 詩 inside the *biéjí*.** Many KR4d collections contain *cí* alongside their *shī* and *wén* (Sū Shì, Lù Yóu, Yáng Wànlǐ, Yè Mèngdé, Chén Liàng, etc.), and many of the canonical *cí* poets are present here only by their secondary genres ([[KR4d0216]] Zhāng Xiàoxiáng's *Yúhú jí*; cf. *cí*-only collections in adjacent divisions). The *biéjí* form thus preserves *cí* as a part of integrated authorial output rather than as a separate genre — important for the editorial history of the Sòng *cí* canon.
- **Dàoxué and Lǐxué literary practice.** A thread through the entire division: the founding Northern-Sòng cohort (Zhōu Dūnyí, Shào Yōng), the *Yánpíng* line (Yáng Shí, Luó Cóngyàn, Lǐ Tóng), Zhū Xī's massive *biéjí*, the SìLúWèiZhēn senior-officialdom set, and the southern / northern Yuán transmitters (Wú Chéng, Xǔ Héng). The *xīnxué* counter-pole (Lù Jiǔyuān) and the utilitarian *Yǒngjiā* / *Yǒngkāng* alternatives are equally present. KR4d documents what *Dàoxué* looked like as a literary practice, not only as a philosophical school.
- **The *Jìngkāng* trauma and the loyalist tradition.** Two parallel loyalist clusters: the high-Northern-Sòng-into-Southern-Sòng (Chén Dōng → Zōng Zé → Lǐ Gāng → Yuè Fēi → Hú Quán → Lù Yóu), and the late-Sòng-into-early-Yuán (Wén Tiānxiáng → Xiè Fāngdé → Lín Jǐngxī → Zhèng Sīxiào → Dèng Mù → Liú Chénwēng). Each is editorially distinctive: the first largely transmitted by family and by Sòng official offices; the second often surviving only in late-Míng or early-Qīng *jíběn* recovery and in Sìkù *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* extraction. The pair frames KR4d as the principal documentary archive of pre-modern Chinese loyalist literary identity.
- **The Mongol-court Hànrén establishment and the *sèmù* literati.** The integration of Khitan, Jurchen, Tangut, Uyghur, Önggüd, and Mongol authors into the SòngYuán literary canon is a feature unique to KR4d in the entire Kanripo corpus. The Hànrén collaborators (Yēlǜ Chǔcái, Xǔ Héng, Yú Jí, Zhào Mèngfǔ) and the *sèmù* poets (Mǎ Zǔcháng, Sà Dūlā) collectively redefine what a "Chinese" *biéjí* can be: Christian-descent, Tangut-Uyghur, and Mongol authors writing in Chinese on classical themes for a multi-ethnic court audience.
- **The *Yányòu* (1313) restoration of the examinations and the maturation of Yuán prose.** Most of the principal Yuán-period *biéjí* date from after the 1313 *kējǔ* restoration, which produced the *Yuán shī sì dà jiā* and the *Rúlín sìjié*; their collections double as the documentary residue of the Tuōtuō *guóshǐyuàn* compilation effort that produced the *Sòng*, *Liáo*, and *Jīn shǐ*. The corpus is therefore not just a literary archive but a state-historiographical archive.
- **Editorial reconstruction and the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn* recovery.** A high proportion of KR4d entries — including [[KR4d0004]] Pān Láng, [[KR4d0308]] Gě Shàotǐ, [[KR4d0322]], [[KR4d0332]], [[KR4d0342]] Hán Hù, [[KR4d0347]], [[KR4d0351]], [[KR4d0364]], [[KR4d0374]] Liú Chénwēng, [[KR4d0518]], [[KR4d0565]], [[KR4d0581]], [[KR4d0591]] — survive only because the eighteenth-century *Sìkù* editors extracted them from the *Yǒnglè dàdiǎn*. KR4d is therefore also a record of what the Qīng imperial editorial enterprise judged worth recovering. The complementary editorial axis is the dense Sòng / Yuán / Míng / Qīng commentary apparatus around the canonical poets ([[KR4d0076]]–[[KR4d0081]] for Sū Shì, [[KR4d0085]]–[[KR4d0088]] for HuángChén, [[KR4d0073]]–[[KR4d0074]] for Wáng Ānshí), structurally parallel to the KR4c HánLiǔDùLǐ commentarial clusters.
