# Qīng-róng jū-shì jí 清容居士集
*The Qīng-róng (Clear-and-Composed) Recluse-Layman Collection*
by [[袁桷]] (撰)

## About the work

The 50-*juàn* monumental collection of **Yuán Juè** 袁桷 (CBDB 10389, 1266–1327), *zì* **Bó-zhǎng** 伯長, *hào* **Qīng-róng jū-shì** 清容居士 ("Clear-Composed Recluse-Layman"), posthumous *shì* **Wén-qīng** 文清, posthumously enfeoffed **Chén-liú jùn-gōng** 陳留郡公. Native of **Qìng-yuán-lù** 慶元路 (modern Níng-bō 寧波, Zhè-jiāng). Great-grandson of the Southern-Sòng *zhī Shū-mì-yuàn shì* **Yuán Sháo** 袁韶 — the family one of the principal "southeastern old-households of [Sòng-Yuán] transmission of moral-and-cultural inheritance" (*dōng-nán gù-jiā yí-xiàn* 東南故家遺獻). The SBCK base preserves the complete 50-*juàn* corpus from the Yuán-period cut.

The collection's structure is encyclopaedic, reflecting Yuán Juè's senior Hàn-lín career:
- **juàn 1**: 22 *fù* (rhapsodies) — including topographic-architectural pieces (*Yún-lín fù*, *Tóng-bǎi-guàn fù*, *Chūn-táng fù*, *Rén-shòu-táng fù*, *Lè-shuǐ-tú fù*, *Yú-jiān-zhāi fù*, *Xī-zhāi fù*, *Jiǔ-huá-tái fù*, *Mò-zhú fù*, *Sù-xuān fù*, *Dōng-wō fù*, *Róng-xuān fù*, *Fù-ān fù*, *Yǐn-jū-tú fù*) — major Yuán antiquarian-and-architectural documentation
- **juàn 2**: *sāo / cí* — including the *Mǐn-shì*, *Guǎng-zhāo*, *Qī-guān*, *Yuè-lù-tú cí*, *Yōu-rán-gé cí*, *Jīn-tí cí*, *Shí-tián shān-fáng cí*, *Xuě-jiāng-tú cí*, *Chuí-lún-tíng cí*, *Jù-fēng cí*, *Yún-zhōng cí*
- (subsequent *juàn*): substantial poetry of all forms; major prose (*xù*, *jì*, *bēi-míng*, *mù-zhì-míng*, *tí-bá*, *shū*, *zhì-gào*, *yuè-fǔ*); *zòu-yì* memorial-discussions

### Abstract

Yuán Juè (1266–1327) is the principal Yuán-period scholar-statesman of the southeastern Sòng-aristocratic family-tradition. Great-grandson of the Sòng Vice-Censor Yuán Sháo, Yuán Juè inherited the Qìng-yuán (Níng-bō) Yuán-shì family's substantial Sòng-Yuán-transition library and ritual-cultural standing. His career trajectory traversed:

1. **Lì-zé shū-yuàn** 麗澤書院 at Wù-zhōu (modern Jīn-huá) — *shān-zhǎng* of the academy founded in honour of **Lǚ Zǔ-qiān** 呂祖謙 (the great Sòng Wù-zhōu Confucian) — placing Yuán Juè in the direct Wù-zhōu Sòng-orthodoxy lineage
2. **Hàn-lín Guó-shǐ-yuàn** — recommended to the Hàn-lín-Historical Academy as *jiǎn-yuè-guān*; rose through senior offices to *Hàn-lín shì-jiǎng xué-shì* 翰林侍講學士 (Hàn-lín Lecturer-in-Waiting Academician) — the principal late-Yuán imperial-historical-Confucian office
3. Retirement and posthumous promotion: *Cān-zhī zhèng-shì* of the Jiāng-Zhè *xíng-shěng*; enfeoffed *Chén-liú-jùn-gōng*; *shì* Wén-qīng

The collection's significance:
- **The largest single Yuán-period imperial-literary corpus from the southeastern aristocratic perspective**, preserving the Sòng-Yuán transition through to the Yuán mid-period (1290–1327). 50 *juàn*.
- **Foundational mid-Yuán Hàn-lín state-papers**, particularly *bēi-míng* and *mù-zhì-míng* for the Yuán imperial and Sòng-remnant elite — including Yuán Juè's *mù-zhì-míng* for Ān Xī [[安熙]] (preserved in 蘇天爵's *Yuán wén-lèi*)
- **Major antiquarian-and-architectural *fù* corpus** (*juàn* 1)
- **Substantial gazetteer-historiographical context** for Qìng-yuán / Sì-míng — Yuán Juè composed the *Yán-jiā Sì-míng zhì* [[KR2k0025]] in 1320 at the request of *zǒng-guǎn* Mǎ Zé 馬澤

CBDB 10389 (1266–1327 firm). *Yuán-shǐ* j. 172 (Yuán Juè biography). His lost works (per Sū Tiān-jué's *mù-zhì-míng*) include the *Yì shuō* and *Chūn-qiū shuō*.

## Translations and research

- *Yuán-shǐ* j. 172 (Yuán Juè biography) — the standard biography.
- Hok-lam Chan, "Chinese Official Historiography at the Yüan Court" — discusses Yuán Juè's role in Yuán imperial historiography.
- Various Sòng-Yuán transition studies treat Yuán Juè substantially.

## Other points of interest

The Yuán-shì of Qìng-yuán is one of the principal Sòng-Yuán transition family-archives — the family preserved its Sòng-aristocratic ritual-cultural identity through three generations of Yuán imperial service, maintaining the Wù-zhōu Sòng-Confucian library and learning traditions while adapting to Yuán imperial-Confucian institutionality. Yuán Juè's editorial role in *Yán-jiā Sì-míng zhì* (1320) makes him one of the principal Yuán-period gazetteer-compilers.
