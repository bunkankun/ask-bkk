# Zhàn-rán jū-shì jí 湛然居士集
*The Zhàn-rán jū-shì (Lay Master of Quiescence) Collection*
by [[耶律楚材]] (撰)

## About the work

The collected works in fourteen *juàn* (the catalog meta gives 14 *juàn*; the *Sìkù* editors describe the extent as identical) of Yēlǜ Chǔ-cái 耶律楚材 (CBDB 29279, 1190–1243), *zì* Jìn-qīng 晉卿, *hào* Zhàn-rán jū-shì 湛然居士 ("Lay Master of Quiescence"), the senior Sinitic-Khitan administrator at the Mongol court — first under Chinggis Khan on the 1219–1224 Central-Asian campaigns, then under Ögödei (r. 1229–1241) as effective *Zhōng-shū lìng* (Chancellor) responsible for installing Chinese-style fiscal-and-administrative institutions across the conquered Jīn territories. Native of the Jīn capital Yān-jīng (modern Běijīng), descendant of the Khitan-Liáo ruling house. The collection's principal preface — preserved in the SBCK base — is by **Lǐ Wēi 李微** (*hào* Jiǔ-shān jū-shì 九山居士), dated *guǐ-yǐ* (1233, the year after the Jīn fall), praising Yēlǜ's writings as the product of a *hào-rán zhī qì* (overflowing energy) born of administrative responsibility. The work was assembled and re-cut by Sòng Gōng-zhōng-hēng 宗仲亨 of the *Zhōng-shū-shěng dū-shì*. The corpus is dominated by occasional poetry from the Central-Asian campaigns (the *Xī-yóu* 西遊 poetic sequence is the most-anthologized — the only Sinitic-language poetic record of the Mongol western campaigns), administrative court documents, prefaces and dedications to Buddhist and Daoist temples (Yēlǜ was a lay Buddhist of the Lín-jǐ Chán lineage of Wàn-sōng Xíng-xiù 萬松行秀 but engaged broadly with Daoism and Confucianism), and *zàn* / *jì-wén* commemorating his administrative colleagues. Yēlǜ's other major work is the *Xī-yóu lù* 西遊錄 (Record of the Westward Travels) on his Central-Asian itinerary, separately catalogued. The Lǐ Wēi preface in the SBCK base provides the foundational paratext; the *Yuán-shǐ* j. 146 has Yēlǜ's biography.

### Tiyao

*No Sìkù tíyào in the Kanripo source* (the SBCK base preserves instead the substantive **Lǐ Wēi** preface of 1233 — placing Yēlǜ alongside Zhū-gě Liàng and Fàn Zhòng-yān as a literatus-administrator whose literary mastery is the by-product of *hào-rán zhī qì* and meritorious work, not literary ambition). The Sìkù tíyào outside the source places the *Zhàn-rán jū-shì jí* under "Yuán *bié-jí lèi*" and characterizes Yēlǜ as the foundational Sinitic literatus at the Mongol court — whose poetic record of the western campaigns is the principal Sinitic-language source for the Mongol invasions of Khwarazm and Central Asia.

### Abstract

Yēlǜ Chǔ-cái (CBDB 29279, 1190–1243) is the most consequential Khitan literatus to enter Mongol service. Born at Yān-jīng (Běijīng) and educated in the Jīn imperial tradition; after the Mongol capture of Yān-jīng in 1215 he was summoned by Chinggis Khan in 1218 and served thereafter as the senior Sinitic adviser at the Mongol court. Under Chinggis on the western campaigns (1219–1224) he accompanied the army through Khwarazm, Bukhara, Samarkand, and the Hindu-Kush region; under Ögödei (1229–1241) he served as effective *Zhōng-shū lìng*, responsible for installing Chinese-style fiscal-and-administrative institutions in north China — including the household-registration system, the *tóu-xià* enfeoffment compromise, and the *Yāo-bù* tax system that stabilized post-conquest revenues. The collection's poetic record of the western campaigns — particularly the *Hú-zhōu* (Bukhara), *Yěr-mí-shī* (Iram-Sea), *Sā-mǎ-ěr-gàn* (Samarkand) poems — is the only Sinitic-language poetic record of the Mongol western campaigns and a primary source for the historical geography of thirteenth-century Central Asia. The Buddhist *yú-zhā* prose (correspondence with Wàn-sōng Xíng-xiù) and the Daoist *zàn* (commemorating Quán-zhēn temple foundations on the order of Chinggis) document Yēlǜ's role as cultural-religious mediator between the Mongol court and the resident Chinese religious orders. CBDB 29279 firmly establishes 1190–1243; the *Yuán-shǐ* j. 146 *Yēlǜ Chǔ-cái zhuàn* gives the standard biography. Wilkinson treats Yēlǜ extensively as the foundational early-Yuán Sinitic-Khitan administrator (§35).

## Other points of interest

Yēlǜ's account of Qiū Chǔ-jī's 1222 audience with Chinggis Khan — recorded at imperial command and circulated in 1232 as the *Xuán-fēng qìng-huì lù* 玄風慶會錄 [[KR5a0177]] — is a foundational document of Mongol-Quán-zhēn-Daoist relations and the principal source for Chinggis's reception of the Daoist patriarch. The poetic correspondence with Wàn-sōng Xíng-xiù in this collection complements the *Cóng-róng-lù* 從容錄 and *Wàn-sōng yǔ-lù* 萬松語錄 as documentation of Yēlǜ's lay-Buddhist Chán practice.
