# Dào-yuán xué-gǔ lù 道園學古錄
*The Dào-yuán (Dào-Garden) Studying-the-Ancients Records*
by [[虞集]] (撰)

## About the work

The 50-*juàn* monumental collection of **Yú Jí** 虞集 (CBDB 10801, 1272–1348), *zì* **Bó-shēng** 伯生**, *hào* **Dào-yuán** 道園 ("Dào-Garden"). Senior Yuán Hàn-lín scholar-statesman, posthumous *shì* **Wén-jìng** 文靖. Native of **Shǔ-jùn** 蜀郡 (Sì-chuān). The foundational mid-Yuán Hàn-lín literary patriarch; with **Yáng Zài** 楊載 [[KR4d0495]], **Fàn Pèng** 范梈 [[KR4d0496]], and **Jiē Xī-sī** 揭傒斯 [[KR4d0497]] forming the canonical **mid-Yuán four-master quartet** (*Yú-Yáng-Fàn-Jiē* 虞楊范揭). Yú Jí's collection was edited by his disciple **Lǐ Běn** 李本 from over 10,000 surviving pieces.

The SBCK base preserves the standard transmitted text via several Yuán-era cuts: (1) **Liú Jī** 劉基 (Liú Bó-wēn, the early-Míng founding-figure) prepared a large-character cut; (2) the *Wū-kè-zhāng* 烏克章 *Jiàn-níng* cut. Later editors added a *Dào-yuán lèi-gǎo* compilation; the relations between *Lèi-gǎo* and *Xué-gǔ lù* show partial overlap.

**Opening preface** (the *Yōng-Yú gōng wén xù*, by an anonymous mid-Yuán Hàn-lín colleague — likely a senior contemporary): Yú Jí was repeatedly identified by Hàn-lín colleagues as one with whom to entrust the future of *sī wén* (this *wén* tradition). At the **Zhì-zhì** (1321–1323) and **Tiān-lì** (1328–1330) eras, "[the] Yōng Yú-gōng [Yú Jí] held official office [in the Hàn-lín] — *wén* also was [in his] luxuriance for a time. The state-temple and imperial-court's *diǎn-cè* (canonical-investiture documents), the *gōng-qīng dà-fū* (senior officials')'s *bēi-bǎn* (stele-and-tablet inscriptions) — all came-forth from the gentleman's hand. *Cuì-rán zì chéng yī jiā zhī yán* (purely-and-completely self-completing one-house's words). The mountain-forest's men, *fěng-yè zhī shì* (cottage-living scholars), obtaining his presentation-words — [they take it as if] obtaining *gōng-bì* (folded-jade emblems). The gentleman's encountering-the-text — *suí shì chóu-zuò* (according-to-affairs reciprocating-composing) — *zào-cì tiān-chéng* (sudden-as-coming, heaven-completed). Originally without *yī-háo shàng-rén zhī xīn* (one-iota of striving-to-surpass-others mind); also without *jū-jū rán bù-qū gǔ-rén zhī yì* (constrained-walking-of-the-ancients meaning). *Jī-yòng zì-shú, jìng-qù zì-shēng, zuǒ-yòu féng-yuán* (mechanism-application self-mature, atmosphere-interest self-arising, left-right encountering-source) — each recognizing its appropriate-office."

### Abstract

The literary monument of **Yú Jí** (1272–1348), the singular mid-Yuán Hàn-lín literary patriarch and the principal *Yù-táng* (Hàn-lín) state-paper composer of the **Zhì-zhì** and **Tiān-lì** eras (1321–1330). Yú's career as state-paper composer made him the principal author of the Yuán imperial *diǎn-cè* (canonical documents) and of the *bēi-bǎn* (steles-and-tablets) for the senior Mongol-aristocratic and Hàn-Confucian elite. Sū Tiān-jué's *Yuán wén-lèi* canonized Yú alongside [[馬祖常]], [[袁桷]], and Wáng Gòu 王構 as the **Dà-dé / Yán-yòu** mid-Yuán literary-imperial quartet — with Yú Jí as the senior member.

**Family origins.** Maternal grandfather **Yáng Wén-zhòng** 楊文仲, Sòng *Héng-yáng* magistrate; the maternal-uncle's-great-grandson **Yáng Chūn** 楊椿 of Méi-shān composed the preface for the *Dào-yuán yí-gǎo* [[KR4d0494]] in Zhì-zhèng *jǐ-hài* (1359).

**Collection structure** (per the SBCK base):
- 22 *fù* in *juàn* 1 — antiquarian and architectural rhapsodies including the *Dǎo-yóu fù*, *Yún-lín fù*, *Xuán-yún-shí fù*, *Yù-guī-zhàng fù*, *Tóng-bǎi-guàn fù* (the Daoist-establishment rhapsodies), *Lè-shuǐ-tú fù*, *Yú-jiàn-zhāi fù*, *Xī-zhāi fù*, *Níng-yún-shí fù*, *Jiǔ-huá-tái fù*, *Mò-zhú fù*, *Sù-xuān fù*, *Dōng-wō fù*, *Róng-xuān fù*, *Fù-ān fù*, *Nǐ Shàn-tíng fù*, *Yǐn-jū-tú fù* — the major Yuán antiquarian / architectural-poetic corpus
- *sāo / cí* in *juàn* 2 — including the *Mǐn-shì*, *Guǎng-zhāo*, *Qī-guān*, *Yuè-lù-tú cí*, *Yōu-rán-gé cí*, *Jīn-tí cí*, *Shí-tián shān-fáng cí*, *Xuě-jiāng-tú cí*, *Chuí-lún-tíng cí*, *Jù-fēng cí*, *Yún-zhōng cí*
- (subsequent *juàn*): substantial poetic and prose corpus

**Imperial state-paper function.** Per the opening preface: "*Cháo-tíng zhī diǎn-cè, gōng-qīng dà-fū zhī bēi-bǎn — xián chū gōng shǒu*" (the court's canonical-investiture documents, the senior officials' stele-and-tablet inscriptions — all came out of the gentleman's hand). This places Yú Jí as the senior composer of Yuán imperial state-papers and aristocratic memorial inscriptions in the Zhì-zhì-and-Tiān-lì period.

**Position in Yuán literary history.** Yú Jí is the principal Yuán literary-imperial figure of the mid-Yuán cultural climax. The Sìkù editors elsewhere characterize him as the *Wén-zhèng zhī jiān-zhě* (cultivator of *wén-zhèng* — refined-correct) — i.e. the canonical Yuán literary patriarch. CBDB 10801 (1272–1348). *Yuán-shǐ* j. 181 (Yú Jí biography). The Wilkinson manual treats Yú Jí extensively in §28, §35, §42.

Composition window: from Yú Jí's earliest preserved compositions (after his 1286 recruitment to the Yuán court by Chéng Jù-fū [[程鉅夫]] [[KR4d0466]], when Yú was 14) through his death in 1348.

## Other points of interest

The mid-Yuán **four-master quartet** (Yú Jí, Yáng Zài, Fàn Pèng, Jiē Xī-sī) is the canonical formulation of mid-Yuán literary culture in subsequent Yuán-Míng literary historiography. Yú Jí as the senior member established the mid-Yuán Hàn-lín-imperial-Confucian literary standard. The 50-*juàn* *Dào-yuán xué-gǔ lù* + 6-*juàn* *Dào-yuán yí-gǎo* [[KR4d0494]] together preserve Yú's complete surviving corpus.
