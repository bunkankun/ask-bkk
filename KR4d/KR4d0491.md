# Shí-tián wén-jí 石田文集
*The Shí-tián (Stone-Field) Collection*
by [[馬祖常]] (撰)

## About the work

The 15-*juàn* collection (5 *juàn* of poetry and *fù*, 10 *juàn* of prose) of **Mǎ Zǔ-cháng** 馬祖常 (CBDB 24523, 1279–1338), *zì* **Bó-yōng** 伯庸**, *hào* (collection-name) **Shí-tián** 石田 (from his **Shí-tián shān-fáng** 石田山房 residence). Posthumous *shì* **Wén-zhēn** 文貞. *Yuán-shǐ* j. 143 gives the substantial biography.

**Ethnic-political background.** Mǎ Zǔ-cháng was a **Yōng-gǔ** 雍古 (Öngüt) tribesman by descent — i.e. a member of the Önggüt (a Nestorian-Christian Inner-Mongolian tribe; sometimes called *White Tatars*). The family settled at **Jìng-zhōu** 靖州 (Tiān-shān region, modern Inner Mongolia). The high-grandfather **Xī-lǐ-jì-sū** 錫里濟蘇 (Sìkù-era corrected transliteration; originally written *Xī-lǐ-jí-sī* 錫里吉思) held end-Jīn office as **Fèng-xiáng bīng-mǎ pàn-guān**. Descendants — following the Mongol-Yuán practice of using the ancestral office's *first character* as surname — adopted the surname **Mǎ** (from *Bīng-mǎ pàn-guān*). The great-grandfather **Yǎ-hā** 雅哈 (Sìkù-corrected; originally *Yuè-hé* 月合) accompanied Khubilai south and settled at Biàn (Kāi-fēng); later relocated to **Guāng-zhōu** 光州.

**Career.** In Yán-yòu 1 (1314), at the restoration of the imperial examination system, Mǎ Zǔ-cháng came first (*dì-yī*) in both the *xiāng-gòng* (provincial) and *huì-shì* (metropolitan) examinations, and second (*dì-èr*) at the imperial *tíng-shì* (court) examination — the singular **non-Hàn examinee** to perform at such a high level in the restored Yán-yòu examinations. Awarded *Yìng-fèng Hàn-lín wén-zì*; promoted to *Yán-chá yù-shǐ* (Salt-Inspection Censor); successfully impeached the Grand Councillor **Tè-mén-dé-ěr** 特們德爾 (Sìkù-corrected; originally *Tiě-běn-dié-ér* 鐵本迭兒). After Tè-mén-dé-ěr returned to power and demoted Mǎ to *Kāi-píng-xiàn yǐn* (county-magistrate), Mǎ withdrew on grounds of avoiding troubles. After Tè-mén-dé-ěr's death, Mǎ was appointed *Hàn-lín dài-zhì*; rose to *Lǐ-bù shàng-shū*; soon *Cān-yì Zhōng-shū-shěng shì*; in Yuán-tǒng 1 (1333) appointed *Yù-shǐ zhōng-chéng*; transferred to *Shū-mì fù-shǐ*; requested return; died Zhì-zhèng 4 (1344).

The Sìkù editors evaluate the collection: "Prose [is] *jīng-shàn hóng-lì* (refined-and-rich, vast-and-magnificent) — entirely washing-out the *róu-màn bēi-rǒng zhī xí* (soft-and-weak, low-and-redundant practice). Poetry's talent-and-strength [is] *fù-jiàn* (rich-and-firm) — like the *Dōu-mén zhuàng-yóu* (Capital-Gate Vigorous-Travel) various pieces — long *piān* great compositions — *jiǒng bó-bēn téng* (vast-and-overlapping, expansive-and-leaping) — possessing not-receiving *jī-lè* (bridle-restraint) *qì*."

**Position in Yuán literary history.** Sū Tiān-jué's compilation of the *Yuán wén-lèi* records 20 of Mǎ's poems and 20 of his prose pieces — more than any other author. Sū secured imperial approval to cut the *Shí-tián jí* and personally composed the preface, calling Mǎ as one who "*jiē wǔ Suí-Táng, shàng zhuī Hàn-Wèi*" (takes-up the lineage of Suí-Táng, ascending to track Hàn-Wèi); his "*wén-zhāng wèi zhī yī biàn*" (literary art was, by him, one-transformation). With **Yuán Juè** [[袁桷]] (of Kuài-jī), **Yú Jí** [[虞集]] (of Shǔ-jùn), and **Wáng Gòu** 王搆 (of Dōng-píng) in *gēng-dié chàng-hé* (alternating-exchange-composition) like *jīn-shí xiāng xuān* (metals-and-stones mutually-sounding) — yet "the *wén* increased in marvel". The Dà-dé and Yán-yòu eras' literary culmination — and Mǎ Zǔ-cháng was among the few *jù-bǎ* (giant-thumbs).

### Abstract

The collected works of **Mǎ Zǔ-cháng** (CBDB 24523, 1279–1338), one of the principal Yuán-era *non-Hàn* literary figures and an institutional foundational figure of the post-1314 Yuán imperial-examination Confucian establishment. As the **Yōng-gǔ** (Önggüt / Nestorian-Mongolian) descendant who placed first in both provincial and metropolitan examinations and second at the court examination in the restored Yán-yòu *jìn-shì* system (1314), Mǎ established the principle of literary-meritocratic non-Hàn participation in Yuán imperial Confucian governance.

The Sìkù editors place Mǎ among the *jù-bǎ* (giant-thumbs) of the Dà-dé / Yán-yòu cultural climax, alongside **Yuán Juè** of Kuài-jī (Níng-bō), **Yú Jí** of Shǔ-jùn (Sì-chuān), and **Wáng Gòu** of Dōng-píng (Shāndōng) — the geographic-cultural fourfold core of mid-Yuán imperial-literary culture. Sū Tiān-jué's *Yuán wén-lèi* canonized Mǎ with the largest single corpus of any author (20 poems + 20 prose pieces).

The collection's principal contents:
- **5 *juàn* of *fù* and *shī*** — including the *Dōu-mén zhuàng-yóu* (Capital-Gate Vigorous-Travel) long-poem sequence, characterized as *jiǒng bó-bēn téng* (vast-and-overlapping, expansive-and-leaping) — the foundational mid-Yuán Hàn-lín pictorial-poetic work
- **10 *juàn* of prose** — Hàn-lín state-papers, *bēi-míng*, *mù-zhì* — characterized as *jīng-shàn hóng-lì* (refined-rich, vast-magnificent)

**Political career.** As Censor, successfully impeached the Grand Councillor **Tè-mén-dé-ěr** 特們德爾 (= *Temüder*) — the senior Mongol prime-minister whose corruption marred the Khubilai-and-successor regime. Mǎ's career exemplified censor-officials' constitutional role against imperial corruption. Subsequently rose through *Lǐ-bù shàng-shū*, *Cān-yì Zhōng-shū-shěng shì*, *Yù-shǐ zhōng-chéng*, *Shū-mì fù-shǐ* — the senior Yuán imperial offices for a non-Hàn Confucian.

Composition window: from Mǎ's earliest preserved Hàn-lín pieces (after 1314) through 1338.

## Other points of interest

The Yán-yòu 1 (1314) imperial-examination restoration — and Mǎ Zǔ-cháng's place as principal-ranked non-Hàn examinee — is one of the most-consequential moments in Yuán-imperial-Confucian institutional history. The Yuán court's deliberate decision to admit non-Hàn (Önggüt, Khangli, Tangut, etc.) candidates into the *jìn-shì* system — and the success of figures like Mǎ Zǔ-cháng — established a permanent precedent for multi-ethnic Confucian-imperial participation that influenced later dynasties.
