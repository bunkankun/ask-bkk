# Zànlǐ dìzàng púsà chànyuàn yí 讚禮地藏菩薩懺願儀
*The Ritual of Praise, Salutation, Repentance, and Vow before the Bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha*
by [[智旭]] (Zhìxù / Ǒuyì 蕅益, 述)

## About the work

The *Zàn-lǐ dì-zàng púsà chàn-yuàn yí* in 1 fascicle is the late-Ming repentance liturgy (*chàn-yuàn yí* 懺願儀) for the bodhisattva [[地藏菩薩]] Kṣitigarbha, composed by the late-Ming Buddhist master [[智旭]] Zhì-xù (1599–1655) — better known by his sobriquet *Ǒu-yì* 蕅益, the most influential Buddhist scholar of the late Ming. The Xuzangjing print signs the entry "明　菩薩沙彌古吳　智旭　述" — "set forth by Zhì-xù, bodhisattva-novice of *Gǔ-Wú* [Sūzhōu], in the Míng dynasty". The text is structured as a complete *chàn-fǎ* (repentance ritual) liturgy, with rubrics in interlinear notes giving the prescribed actions of the practitioner — purifying body, mouth, and mind; venerating the buddhas; visualising the bodhisattva; reciting confession formulas; and concluding with vows.

### Prefaces

The text opens directly with the liturgical instructions and the prescribed visualisation, without a separate preface.

### Abstract

The *chàn-yuàn yí* is part of the great late-Ming revival of Buddhist liturgical scholarship, a project to which [[智旭]] contributed several major works (the [[KR6f0050|*Wéi-shí xīn yào* 唯識新要]] on Yogācāra, his *Línlóng dà-lǚn* 楞嚴大論 on the *Śūraṅgama*, and his commentaries on the [[KR6h0016|*Dì-zàng běn-yuàn jīng*]] and other Mahāyāna sūtras). Like his contemporaries [[袾宏]] Yún-qī Zhū-hóng and [[德清]] Hān-shān Dé-qīng, [[智旭]] worked to reconstitute properly Buddhist ritual forms after the perceived corruptions of the late-Ming popular tradition; the *Chàn-yuàn yí* is one such reconstruction, prescribing a structured Kṣitigarbha repentance based on the doctrinal framework of the [[KR6h0016|*Běn-yuàn jīng*]].

The dating window 1610–1655 reflects the bracket of [[智旭]]'s active scholarly life, conventionally given as 1610 (the year of his ordination) to 1655 (the year of his death). The work was widely adopted in Chinese, Korean, and Japanese (especially Ōbaku) Buddhist communities and remains in use as the standard Kṣitigarbha repentance liturgy in contemporary East Asian practice.
