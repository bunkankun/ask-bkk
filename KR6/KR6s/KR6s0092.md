# DàZhōu kāndìng zhòngjīng mùlù 大周刊定眾經目錄
*The Great Zhōu Catalog of the Various Sutras as Authoritatively Established*
by [[明佺]] (等撰)

## About the work

A fifteen-juan **imperially-commissioned official Buddhist canonical catalog** of the **Wǔ Zé-tiān** 武則天 *Zhōu* dynasty, compiled in **Tiān-shòu / Wàn-suì-tōng-tiān-zhèng-shèng** 695 CE under the imperial command of **Empress Wǔ Zé-tiān** by a court team led by **Míng-quán** 明佺, **Bo-yán** 波延, and others, of **Fó-shòu-jì-sì** 佛授記寺 (the imperial monastery in Luòyáng renamed for Wǔ Zé-tiān's Buddhist patronage). The byline reads **大唐天后勅佛授記寺沙門明佺等撰** ("Composed by *śramaṇa* Míng-quán et al. of Fó-shòu-jì-sì by command of the Great Tang Heavenly Empress"). Preserved at T55 no. 2153.

### Prefaces

The text opens with the **DàZhōu kāndìng zhòngjīng mùlù xù** 大周刊定眾經目錄序. In paraphrase:

> *I privately consider: the **True-truth** and the **Conventional-truth** rely on writing-and-letter to be manifest. The **Right Dharma** and the **Semblance Dharma** through protection-upholding obtain to preserve. Therefore obtaining the ten-thousand kalpas of circulating-and-distributing, the four births of benefit.*
>
> *Our Great Zhōu Heavenly Strategist, **Golden Wheel Sage Spirit Imperial Sovereign Majesty** [Wǔ Zétiān]: the Way is fixed in the constant kalpas; her position adjacent to the **upper forbearance**. Mounting the original vow she descends-to-be-born; spreading great compassion she broadly saves. The **Golden Wheel** turns ascending; her transformation envelops the four continents. The **precious horse** flies-and-runs; her sound permeates the eight extremities…*

[The preface continues with elaborate panegyric of Wǔ Zétiān as Buddhist sovereign and founding patron of the Zhōu dynasty's Buddhist program.]

### Abstract

Authorship and date: composed in **695 CE** under the imperial command of **Empress Wǔ Zétiān** 武則天 (624–705, r. 690–705 as emperor of the **Zhōu** dynasty), by a court-team led by **Míngquán** 明佺 at **Fóshòujìsì** in Luòyáng. *notBefore* = **695**, *notAfter* = **695**. Catalog dynasty 唐 (here following the Buddhist canonical convention; the actual political dynasty in 695 was Wǔ Zétiān's **Zhōu** 周, established 690 by displacing the Tang).

The work is one of the principal **imperial-Buddhist patronage projects** of the Wǔ Zétiān dynasty — alongside her sponsorship of:

- The new translation of the **Avataṃsaka-sūtra** by **Śikṣānanda** 實叉難陀 (695–699), the basis of the Tang-period 80-juan *Huá-yán-jīng* (cf. [[KR6s0011]] and the **Huì-yuàn yīn-yì** tradition).
- The promotion of **Huáyán** 華嚴 doctrine through her chief Buddhist advisor **Fǎzàng** 法藏 (643–712), the third Huáyán patriarch.
- The renaming of major temples in her honor (the **Fóshòujìsì** name itself comes from her imperial rescript identifying her as a **Buddha-foretold sovereign**).

The 15-juan structure is the **most extensive** of the Suí-Tang catalog tradition before Zhì-shēng's Kāi-yuán-shì-jiào-lù. Its bibliographic significance lies in providing a comprehensive snapshot of the canonical state at the **height of Wǔ Zé-tiān's imperial Buddhist program** (mid-690s) — a period of intensive canonical translation activity (the new 80-juan *Avataṃsaka*, the 28-fascicle *Mahāprajñāpāramitā-saṅgaha-sūtra*, etc.) that substantially expanded the canonical corpus.

The work was subsequently superseded by **Zhìshēng's** *Kāiyuán shìjiào lù* (T2154, 730), but remains a primary witness to the canonical state in the late-7th-century imperial Wǔ Zétiān period.

## Other points of interest

The Wǔ Zétiān dynasty's intensive Buddhist patronage made the brief **Zhōu** 周 dynasty (690–705) one of the principal imperial-Buddhist eras in Chinese history, despite its political brevity. The 695 catalog is one of the most enduring institutional outputs of this program — preserving the canonical state of Buddhism at the moment of imperial peak-engagement, before the Tang restoration of 705.
