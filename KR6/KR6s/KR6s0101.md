# Jǐngyòu xīnxiū fǎbǎo lù 景祐新修法寶錄
*The Newly Revised Catalog of Dharma-Treasures of the Jǐng-yòu Era*
edited by [[呂夷簡]] (等編修)

## About the work

A fourteen-juan Northern Sòng official Buddhist canonical bibliography, compiled in the **Jǐngyòu** 景祐 era (1034–1038) of **Sòng Rénzōng** 仁宗 under the editorial direction of the senior court official **Lǚ Yíjiǎn** 呂夷簡 (979–1044), Chief Councilor (*zǎixiàng* 宰相) of the early-Rén-zōng era. The work updates the earlier Sòng canonical catalogs ([[KR6s0099]] *Tiānshèng zǒnglù*, [[KR6s0100]] *Dàzhōngxiángfú fǎbǎo lù*) with the additional translation activity through the early Jǐngyòu period. Preserved in the Zhàochéng Jīncáng at A112 no. 1502.

### Prefaces

The text opens with the **Jǐngyòu xīnxiū fǎbǎo lù xù** 景祐新修法寶錄序. In paraphrase:

> *Of old the Tathāgata, in the substance complete with **bhūtatathatā**, the Way completed right awakening, lifting up the multitude beings' delusion-and-falsehood, manifesting the **good teaching**'s round-penetration. Mahāyāna and Hīnayāna — following the capacity-and-occasion in their response-and-stimulation. **Right Dharma** and **Semblance Dharma** — pointing to the sudden-and-gradual to open the predecessors. From the **Jade-Hair** [Buddha's *uṣṇīṣa*] sinking its radiance and the **Golden River** showing the extinction, there were the great **Kāśyapa** and **Anāgāmi** [arhats] together stitching the bequeathed text, praising-and-clarifying the meaning-fully-understood. This then is the *quán-yú* (origin-source) of the *jīng* and *lùn*.*
>
> *Subsequently the **white horse** came west [the legendary first arrival of Buddhism to Hàn China in 67 CE]. The **Lántái** [Imperial Library] in the middle was hidden. So the **Cīna people** came to recognize the root of the pure-and-clear, despised the dust of vexations-and-troubles, abandoned the heretical schools, all rushed to the wonderful shade — as if leaving the burning-house and peacefully obtaining the carriage; as if in the middle of the river ferried by means of a raft…*

[The preface continues with extensive doctrinal-historical framing.]

### Abstract

Authorship and date: composed under the editorial direction of **Lǚ Yíjiǎn** 呂夷簡 (CBDB; **979–1044**; *zì* **Tānfū** 坦夫; native of **Láizhōu** 萊州 in modern Shāndōng), the senior court official and three-time Chief Councilor of the early-Rén-zōng era (1029–1033, 1035–1037, 1040–1044). The work was completed in the **Jǐngyòu** era (1034–1038), conventionally dated **1034–1037**. *notBefore* = **1034**, *notAfter* = **1037**. Catalog dynasty 宋.

The work is the **third in the series** of three early-Sòng official canonical bibliographies — [[KR6s0099]] *Tiānshèng* (1023–1031), [[KR6s0100]] *Dàzhōngxiángfú* (1011–1013), and the present *Jǐngyòu* (1034–1037). Together they constitute the principal **early-Sòng imperial-bibliographic series** documenting the consolidation of the Sòng-period Chinese Buddhist canon following the foundational **Kāibǎo canon-printing project** (971–983).

The substantive editorial leadership by **Lǚ Yíjiǎn** — a senior secular Chief Councilor rather than a Buddhist scholar-monk — is significant: it parallels the editorial leadership of [[KR6s0100]] by **Yáng Yì** (a senior court literatus). The pattern reflects the deep **Northern-Sòng literati-elite engagement with imperial Buddhist canonical scholarship**, with senior court figures contributing direct editorial work to canonical-bibliographic projects.

## Other points of interest

The series of three early-Sòng official Buddhist canonical bibliographies under literati-bureaucratic editorial leadership ([[KR6s0099]]–[[KR6s0101]]) exemplifies the **early-Sòng integration of Buddhist canonical scholarship with the senior court bureaucratic establishment** — a pattern that contrasts with the more strictly monastic-scholarly canonical-bibliographic tradition of the Tang (cf. [[KR6s0084]]–[[KR6s0098]]) and reflects the broader Sòng-era restructuring of Buddhism's institutional position within the imperial system.
