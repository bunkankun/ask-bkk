# Chuánjiào dàshī jiānglái Yuèzhōu lù 傳教大師將來越州錄
*The Catalog of [Texts] Brought Back from Yuè-zhōu by the Great Master of Transmission of the Teaching*
by [[最澄]] (撰), = **Saichō** 最澄 (767–822)

## About the work

A single-juan **Japanese Buddhist canonical-importation catalog**, the companion to **Saichō's** [[KR6s0105]] *Tāizhōu lù*. This catalog documents the texts that Saichō received during the **Yuèzhōu** 越州 portion of his 805 return route from Tang China — particularly the **Esoteric** texts and rituals he received at **Lóngxīngsì** 龍興寺 in Yuèzhōu under the master **Shùnxiǎo** 順曉. Preserved at T55 no. 2160. See [[KR6s0105]] for the principal substantive treatment of Saichō and his Tang mission.

### Prefaces

The text has no preserved separate preface; the body provides the catalog of Yuè-zhōu-acquired texts.

### Abstract

Authorship and date: composed by **Saichō** in **805 CE** immediately upon his return to Japan, complementing [[KR6s0105]]. *notBefore* = **805**, *notAfter* = **805**. Catalog dynasty 日本.

The Yuè-zhōu portion of Saichō's mission is significant for the **Esoteric** content: at Lóng-xīng-sì he received Esoteric initiation under **Shùn-xiǎo** 順曉, including ritual transmissions and *dhāraṇī* texts that became the foundational Esoteric component of the **Tendai-Shingon** integration that characterizes the Tendai school's doctrinal-ritual structure (in contrast to the more strictly Esoteric **Shingon** lineage of **Kūkai**, who received his Esoteric transmission directly from **Huì-guǒ** 惠果, the eighth Esoteric patriarch in Cháng-ān).

The two Saichō catalogs ([[KR6s0105]] + [[KR6s0106]]) together document the **complete inventory** of Saichō's 804–805 Tang study mission — totaling some **460 texts** (per the combined counts of the two catalogs).

## Other points of interest

The Yuèzhōu Esoteric component of Saichō's mission is one of the principal pieces of evidence for the **Tendai school's distinctive Esoteric integration** — distinguishing it from the more strictly doctrinal-only Tang Tiāntái lineage and from the more strictly Esoteric Shingon lineage. The Tendai-Esoteric integration (called *Taimitsu* 台密) became one of the defining features of Japanese Tendai Buddhism.
