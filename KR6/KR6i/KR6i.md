### Scope and scholarly tradition

KR6i mirrors the Taishō Tripiṭaka's 經集部 (*Jīngjí bù*, "Sūtrasaṃgraha"), the catch-all division in which the Taishō editors gathered the Mahāyāna sūtras that did not belong to one of the canon's named text-cycles. The principal axis is Taishō nos. 425–847 across Vols. 14–17, corresponding to CANWWW `div11.xml`; the Kanripo arrangement supplements this with Xuzangjing (卍續) commentaries, a handful of Jiāxīng-zàng titles, and — at the tail end (KR6i0589–0596) — a small block of Yogācāra 論釋 that drifted into the *jīng* collection through their attachment to single-sūtra commentary. Texts in the other "named" divisions are explicitly excluded: Prajñāpāramitā (KR6a), Ratnakūṭa (KR6b), Mahāsannipāta (KR6c), Avataṃsaka (KR6d), Nirvāṇa (KR6e), Lotus (KR6f), Bhadrakalpika (KR6g) and the Āgamas (KR6h) are catalogued elsewhere.

Because *Jīngjí bù* is structurally residual, its scholarly profile is not generic. The "leftover" works concentrate, in fact, several of the most consequential sūtras in the entire Chinese Mahāyāna corpus — *Vimalakīrtinirdeśa*, *Śūraṅgamasamādhi*, *Samādhirāja*, *Suvarṇaprabhāsa*, the three Chinese *Laṅkāvatāras*, the *Tathāgatagarbha-sūtra* and the *Anūnatva-apūrṇatva-nirdeśa*, the *Bhaiṣajyaguru* corpus, the Maitreya cycle, and the major Sinitic apocrypha *Yuánjué jīng* 圓覺經, *Zhànchá shàn'è yèbào jīng* 占察善惡業報經, *Yúlánpén jīng* 盂蘭盆經, and the *Shíwáng jīng* 十王經. The transmissional pattern is therefore stratigraphic: an Eastern Hàn–Western Jin layer of single-fascicle sūtras translated by Ān Shìgāo, Zhī Qiān, and above all Dharmarakṣa; a Yáo Qín / Northern Liáng turning-point in which Kumārajīva, Buddhabhadra, and Dharmakṣema fix the literary canon; a sixth- and seventh-century Yogācāra wave (Bodhiruci, Paramārtha, Saṅghapāla, Divākara, Xuánzàng); a Tang aftermath that adds re-translations and the great commentarial schools (Zhìyǐ, Jízàng, Kuījī, Zōngmì); and a Sòng coda of Indian translations from Shīhù 施護, Fǎtiān 法天, and Fǎxián 法賢. The chronological depth is matched by a high commentarial density — the *Vimalakīrti* alone carries some twenty exegetical works in this division.

### Important texts and text clusters

**The *Vimalakīrti* cluster.** *Wéimójié suǒshuō jīng* 維摩詰所說經 [[KR6i0076]] (Kumārajīva, 406) is the canonical Chinese *Vimalakīrti-nirdeśa* and the most influential single text in the division — it shaped Chinese Buddhist literary style and supplied the central image of the bodhisattva-householder. Zhī Qiān's earlier *Fóshuō wéimójié jīng* 佛說維摩詰經 [[KR6i0075]] (Wú, c. 223–253) and Xuánzàng's *Shuō wúgòuchēng jīng* 說無垢稱經 [[KR6i0077]] preserve the parallel recensions. The exegetical apparatus runs from Sui–Tang masters — Huìyuǎn 慧遠 [[KR6i0079]], Zhìyǐ 智顗 [[KR6i0080]]–[[KR6i0081]], Jízàng 吉藏 [[KR6i0083]]–[[KR6i0084]], Kuījī 窺基 [[KR6i0085]] — through Song Tiāntái revival and Ming–Qing commentary, ending with the Qīng *Wéimó jīng wúwǒ shū* [[KR6i0095]] tradition.

**The Maitreya cluster.** A tight block of six canonical sūtras (KR6i0030–0036) covers the lower-birth (*xiàshēng*) and upper-birth (*shàngshēng*) traditions, including *Fóshuō mílè lái shí jīng* 佛說彌勒來時經 [[KR6i0036]], the briefest and most archaic of the canonical Maitreya texts. Tang commentarial elaboration comes via Jízàng [[KR6i0037]], Kuījī [[KR6i0038]], the Korean exegete Wǒnhyo 元曉 [[KR6i0039]], and Gyeongheung 憬興 [[KR6i0040]] — a rare witness to Silla Buddhist scholarship inside the Chinese canon.

**The *Bhaiṣajyaguru* (Medicine Buddha) cluster.** *Yàoshī liúlí guāng rúlái běnyuàn gōngdé jīng* 藥師琉璃光如來本願功德經 [[KR6i0048]] (Xuánzàng, 650) is the canonical East Asian standard, with the seven-Buddha expanded recension *Yàoshī liúlí guāng qī fó běnyuàn gōngdé jīng* [[KR6i0049]] (Yìjìng, 707) and a Tang ritual-manual lineage. Tang Dunhuang exegesis survives in two anonymous *Yàoshī jīng shū* [[KR6i0054]]–[[KR6i0055]] preserved in Taishō Vol. 85, and Ming–Qing devotional commentary revives the cluster with Língyào 靈耀's *Yàoshī jīng zhíjiě* [[KR6i0056]] and the Ming repentance-liturgy tradition (KR6i0057–0058).

**The *Suvarṇaprabhāsa* (Golden Light) cluster.** Dharmakṣema's *Jīnguāngmíng jīng* 金光明經 [[KR6i0301]] (Northern Liáng, c. 414–426) is the earliest complete Chinese witness; Yìjìng's *Jīnguāngmíng zuìshèng wáng jīng* 金光明最勝王經 [[KR6i0303]] (Táng, 703) renders the full Sanskrit recension and supplies the canonical "Right Discourse on the Dharma of Kings" chapter foundational to East Asian state-protective Buddhism. The commentarial chain includes Zhìyǐ–Guàndǐng 灌頂 [[KR6i0304]], Zhīlǐ 知禮 [[KR6i0305]], Jízàng [[KR6i0308]], and a Tang Esoteric layer.

**The *tathāgatagarbha* / Laṅkāvatāra cluster.** The doctrinal core of East Asian Buddha-nature thought sits inside KR6i: Buddhabhadra's *Dàfāngděng rúláizàng jīng* 大方等如來藏經 [[KR6i0323]] (c. 420), Bodhiruci's *Fóshuō bù zēng bù jiǎn jīng* 佛說不增不減經 [[KR6i0325]] (c. 525), the three Chinese *Laṅkāvatāras* — Guṇabhadra's four-fascicle *Léngqié ābáduōluó bǎo jīng* 楞伽阿跋多羅寶經 [[KR6i0327]] (443), Bodhiruci's ten-fascicle *Rù léngqié jīng* 入楞伽經 [[KR6i0328]] (513), and Śikṣānanda's seven-fascicle *Dàshèng rù léngqié jīng* 大乘入楞伽經 [[KR6i0329]] (700–704) — and Divākara's *Dàshèng mìyán jīng* 大乘密嚴經 [[KR6i0359]] (676–688). The Laṅkāvatāra triad is foundational both for East Asian Yogācāra and for early Chán self-identification as the "Laṅkāvatāra School" (楞伽宗).

**The samādhi cluster.** Three central Indian samādhi-sūtras stand together: Kumārajīva's *Shǒulèngyán sānmèi jīng* 佛說首楞嚴三昧經 [[KR6i0279]] (c. 402–409 — to be distinguished sharply from the apocryphal *Lèngyán jīng* T945), his *Sīyì fàntiān suǒwèn jīng* 思益梵天所問經 [[KR6i0218]] (c. 402–405), and Narendrayaśas's *Yuèdēng sānmèi jīng* 月燈三昧經 [[KR6i0276]] (Northern Qí, 557–559), the canonical Chinese witness to the *Samādhirāja-sūtra*.

**The Ullambana / filial-piety cluster.** *Fóshuō yúlán-pén jīng* 佛說盂蘭盆經 [[KR6i0364]] (attrib. Dharmarakṣa, but widely considered a Western Jin Chinese apocryphon) and its short twin *Fóshuō bào'ēn fèng-pén jīng* 佛說報恩奉盆經 [[KR6i0376]] together founded the East Asian *zhōngyuán* / Ghost Festival. Zōngmì's *Yúlán-pén jīng shū* [[KR6i0365]] and the ten or so subsequent Song–Qīng sub-commentaries make the cluster one of the densest commentarial bodies in the division. The related *Fùmǔ ēn nán bào jīng* 佛說父母恩難報經 [[KR6i0363]] (and the apocryphal *Fùmǔ ēnzhòng jīng* tradition) carry the same filial-piety theme into popular Buddhism.

**The *Yuánjué jīng* (Perfect Enlightenment) cluster.** *Dàfāngguǎng yuánjué xiūduōluó liǎoyì jīng* 大方廣圓覺修多羅了義經 [[KR6i0551]] — attributed to Buddhatrāta but securely identified as a Tang Chinese apocryphon (c. 690–750) — together with Zōngmì's massive *Yuánjué jīng dàshū* [[KR6i0555]] and the long Sòng–Míng commentarial chain (KR6i0553–0574) constitute the textual base of the Huáyán–Chán synthesis Zōngmì engineered.

**The Sinitic apocrypha and divinatory / underworld cluster.** Alongside *Yuánjué jīng*, the division contains the *Zhànchá shàn'è yèbào jīng* 占察善惡業報經 [[KR6i0545]] (Sui-period Sinitic apocryphon, foundational to the Kṣitigarbha cult and the unique wooden-block divination liturgy), Zàngchuān 藏川's *Shíwáng jīng* 佛說地藏菩薩發心因緣十王經 [[KR6i0583]] and *Yùxiū shíwáng shēngqī jīng* [[KR6i0584]] (Late Tang, foundational to East Asian ten-kings underworld iconography), and the late *Huàzhū bǎomìng zhēn jīng* [[KR6i0586]] (Míng *zhēn-jīng*-genre fusion of Buddhist form, Daoist neidan imagery, and popular medicine).

**The Mañjuśrī cluster.** Dharmarakṣa's *Wénshū shīlì xiàn bǎozàng jīng* 佛說文殊師利現寶藏經 [[KR6i0062]] (c. 270–308), Saṅghapāla's *Wénshū shīlì wèn jīng* 文殊師利問經 [[KR6i0069]] (Liáng, 515–520 — a principal canonical source for the eighteen Indian sectarian schools), and a series of Tang–Sòng Mañjuśrī texts down to Fǎxián's late-Sòng *Fóshuō miàojíxiáng púsà suǒwèn dàshèng fǎluó jīng* 佛說妙吉祥菩薩所問大乘法螺經 [[KR6i0074]] (989–1000) trace the gradual standardization of Mañjuśrī's Chinese epithets — from older phonetic *Wénshūshīlì* to the standardized Sòng rendering *Miàojíxiáng*.

**The Yogācāra tail (KR6i0589–0596).** Bandhuprabha *et al.*'s *Fódì jīng lùn* 佛地經論 [[KR6i0589]] (Xuánzàng, 649), three Vasubandhu-style *upadeśa* commentaries translated by Bodhiruci [[KR6i0590]]–[[KR6i0592]] and Vimokṣaprajñā [[KR6i0593]], and a Dunhuang-derived *Dàshèng sìfǎ jīng* exegetical trio [[KR6i0594]]–[[KR6i0596]] sit at the catalog's end. They are structurally 釋經論 (commentaries on single sūtras), and in Taishō proper occupy Vol. 26; their inclusion here reflects the Kanripo principle of grouping sūtra commentaries with the parent sūtra division.

### Important persons

- [[竺法護]] (Dharmarakṣa, c. 239–316) — the single most prolific translator in this division (43 catalogued titles): *Xiánjié jīng*, the early Mañjuśrī texts, the Maitreya core, and a substantial Western-Jin Mahāyāna sūtra corpus.
- [[支謙]] (Zhī Qiān, fl. c. 222–253) — early Wu-period sūtra translator; 27 titles here, including the first Chinese *Vimalakīrti*.
- [[安世高]] (Ān Shìgāo, fl. 148–168) — Eastern Hàn pioneer; 26 short *jīng* of meditation and ethics surviving in this division.
- [[鳩摩羅什]] (Kumārajīva, 344–413) — fixed the canonical Chinese versions of *Vimalakīrti*, *Śūraṅgamasamādhi*, *Viśeṣacintābrahma-paripṛcchā*, and a clutch of other doctrinally central sūtras.
- [[曇無讖]] (Dharmakṣema, 385–433) — Northern Liáng translator of the earliest complete *Suvarṇaprabhāsa*.
- [[佛陀跋陀羅]] (Buddhabhadra, 359–429) — translator of the *Tathāgatagarbha-sūtra*, opening the textual base of East Asian Buddha-nature doctrine.
- [[求那跋陀羅]] (Guṇabhadra, 394–468) — translator of the four-fascicle *Laṅkāvatāra*, the lineage text of early Chán.
- [[菩提流支]] (Bodhiruci, fl. 508–537) — Northern Wei Yogācāra translator; ten-fascicle *Laṅkāvatāra*, *Anūnatva-apūrṇatva*, several Vasubandhu *upadeśa*.
- [[僧伽婆羅]] (Saṅghapāla, 460–524) — Liáng translator of the *Mañjuśrī-paripṛcchā*.
- [[那連提耶舍]] (Narendrayaśas, 517–589) — translator of the *Samādhirāja-sūtra*.
- [[闍那崛多]] (Jñānagupta, 523–600) — Sui-period sūtra retranslator.
- [[玄奘]] (Xuánzàng, 602–664) — canonical *Bhaiṣajyaguru*, the *Buddhabhūmi-śāstra*, and several authoritative re-translations.
- [[地婆訶羅]] (Divākara, 613–688) — translator of the *Ghanavyūha-sūtra*.
- [[實叉難陀]] (Śikṣānanda, 652–710) — seven-fascicle *Laṅkāvatāra*.
- [[義淨]] (Yìjìng, 635–713) — the seven-Buddha *Bhaiṣajyaguru* and the long *Suvarṇaprabhāsa-uttamarāja*.
- [[施護]] (Dānapāla, fl. 980–1017), [[法天]] (Dharmadeva, d. 1001), [[法賢]] (Fǎxián / Dharmabhadra, d. 1001) — Sòng Imperial Translation Bureau; substantial late-period additions to the division.
- [[智顗]] (Zhìyǐ, 538–597) and [[灌頂]] (Guàndǐng, 561–632) — Tiāntái commentaries on the *Vimalakīrti* and *Suvarṇaprabhāsa*.
- [[吉藏]] (Jízàng, 549–623) — Sānlùn commentaries across the *Vimalakīrti*, Maitreya, and *Suvarṇaprabhāsa* clusters.
- [[窺基]] (Kuījī, 632–682) — Fǎxiàng commentaries on the *Vimalakīrti* and *Mílè shàngshēng jīng*.
- [[宗密]] (Zōngmì, 780–841) — the *Yuánjué jīng* exegetical apparatus and the *Yúlánpén jīng* commentary that defined East Asian filial-piety Buddhism.
- [[知禮]] (Zhīlǐ, 960–1028) — Sòng Tiāntái revival, *Suvarṇaprabhāsa* sub-commentary.
- [[智旭]] (Zhìxù, 1599–1655) — Míng-Qīng synthesis; commentaries on *Bhaiṣajyaguru*, *Yúlánpén*, *Zhànchá jīng*, and the *Yuánjué jīng* tradition.
- [[元曉]] (Wǒnhyo, 617–686) and [[憬興]] (Gyeongheung, fl. late 7th c.) — Silla exegetes preserved in the Maitreya cluster.
- [[藏川]] (Zàngchuān, fl. c. 800–950) — compiler of the *Shíwáng jīng* corpus.

### Topics

- **The residual category as doctrinal repository.** Because the Taishō editors used *Jīngjí bù* as the bin for sūtras lacking a named cycle, the division accumulated isolates of unusual doctrinal density — single-text founders of major traditions (*Vimalakīrti*, *Tathāgatagarbha-sūtra*, *Yuánjué jīng*) sit beside short ritual texts. Reading the division requires constant attention to which texts are doctrinal pivots and which are devotional or apotropaic miniatures.
- **Tathāgatagarbha and the Laṅkāvatāra problem.** The three Chinese *Laṅkāvatāras* (KR6i0327–0329), the *Tathāgatagarbha-sūtra* [[KR6i0323]], the *Anūnatva-apūrṇatva-nirdeśa* [[KR6i0325]], and the *Ghanavyūha* [[KR6i0359]] together form the textual base from which East Asian Buddha-nature theory was synthesized — including the question of whether *ālayavijñāna* and *tathāgatagarbha* are identical, the doctrine that became canonical for Huáyán and Chán.
- **Samādhi literature.** The trio of *Śūraṅgamasamādhi*, *Samādhirāja*, and *Viśeṣacintā* offers a coherent Indian sūtra theory of the bodhisattva's contemplative attainments; this is distinct from — and historically prior to — the apocryphal *Lèngyán jīng* T945 with which the *Śūraṅgamasamādhi* is often confused.
- **Sinitic apocrypha and lay Buddhism.** *Yuánjué jīng*, *Zhànchá jīng*, *Yúlánpén jīng*, *Fùmǔ ēnzhòng jīng*, and the *Shíwáng jīng* corpus are the clearest cases in the Mahāyāna canon of texts produced in China and re-presented as Indian sūtras. They are also the texts with the broadest social reach: filial piety, calendrical festival, divination, and post-mortem judgement.
- **State-protective Buddhism.** The two Chinese *Suvarṇaprabhāsa* recensions, particularly Yìjìng's "Right Discourse on the Dharma of Kings" chapter, supplied the dharmic ideology of East Asian Buddhist statecraft from Sui through Nara and beyond.
- **Filial piety as Buddhist genre.** The Ullambana cluster, the Tang *fùmǔ ēn* texts, the *Shíwáng jīng*, and the *Dìzàng púsà běnyuàn jīng* tradition (its earliest witnesses sit in this division) together constitute Buddhism's most successful response to Confucian objections about filial debt.
- **Translation history through multi-recension witnesses.** Several core texts survive in two or three Chinese versions spanning four to seven centuries — *Vimalakīrti* (Zhī Qiān, Kumārajīva, Xuánzàng), *Laṅkāvatāra* (Guṇabhadra, Bodhiruci, Śikṣānanda), *Bhaiṣajyaguru* (Xuánzàng + Yìjìng + earlier witnesses), *Suvarṇaprabhāsa* (Dharmakṣema + Yìjìng + Bǎoguì compendium). This makes KR6i an unusually rich laboratory for translation-history research.
- **The Chinese exegetical density.** The *Vimalakīrti* alone carries some twenty Chinese commentaries spanning Sui to Qīng, and the *Yuánjué jīng* almost as many. The exegetical superstructure here is comparable in scale to that built around the *Lotus*, even though no individual non-Lotus sūtra reaches the *Lotus*'s extreme commentarial density.

### Timeline

- 148–168 — *Eastern Hàn short-sūtra layer* attributed to Ān Shìgāo (e.g. *Ānbān shǒuyì jīng* and related ethics / meditation miniatures): the oldest stratum of the division.
- 222–253 — Zhī Qiān's *Fóshuō wéimójié jīng* 佛說維摩詰經 [[KR6i0075]] — the first Chinese *Vimalakīrti*.
- 265–311 — *Fóshuō yúlán-pén jīng* 佛說盂蘭盆經 [[KR6i0364]], traditionally Dharmarakṣa, more likely Western Jin Sinitic apocryphon: founder of the East Asian Ghost Festival.
- 266–308 — *Fóshuō wénshū shīlì xiàn bǎozàng jīng* 佛說文殊師利現寶藏經 [[KR6i0062]] (Dharmarakṣa).
- 300 — *Xiánjié jīng* 賢劫經 [[KR6i0001]] (Dharmarakṣa, Yǒngkāng 1) — earliest precisely dated text in the division and source of the thousand-Buddhas of the present aeon.
- 317–420 — *Fóshuō mílè lái shí jīng* 佛說彌勒來時經 [[KR6i0036]]: the shortest Maitreya sūtra and probably the most archaic surviving recension.
- 402–405 — *Sīyì fàntiān suǒwèn jīng* 思益梵天所問經 [[KR6i0218]] (Kumārajīva).
- c. 402–409 — *Fóshuō shǒulèngyán sānmèi jīng* 佛說首楞嚴三昧經 [[KR6i0279]] (Kumārajīva): canonical *Śūraṅgamasamādhi*.
- 406 — *Wéimójié suǒshuō jīng* 維摩詰所說經 [[KR6i0076]] (Kumārajīva): the canonical Chinese *Vimalakīrti*.
- 406–420 — *Dàfāngděng rúláizàng jīng* 大方等如來藏經 [[KR6i0323]] (Buddhabhadra): textual base of East Asian Buddha-nature doctrine.
- 414–426 — *Jīnguāngmíng jīng* 金光明經 [[KR6i0301]] (Dharmakṣema): earliest complete *Suvarṇaprabhāsa*.
- 443 — *Léngqié ābáduōluó bǎo jīng* 楞伽阿跋多羅寶經 [[KR6i0327]] (Guṇabhadra): four-fascicle *Laṅkāvatāra*, lineage text of early Chán.
- 502–557 — expanded *Fóshuō fómíng jīng* 佛說佛名經 [[KR6i0017]]: enlarged liturgical Buddha-names corpus.
- 506–520 — *Wénshū shīlì wèn jīng* 文殊師利問經 [[KR6i0069]] (Saṅghapāla): canonical source for the eighteen Indian sectarian schools.
- 508–527 — *Fóshuō bù zēng bù jiǎn jīng* 佛說不增不減經 [[KR6i0325]] (Bodhiruci).
- 513 — *Rù léngqié jīng* 入楞伽經 [[KR6i0328]] (Bodhiruci): ten-fascicle *Laṅkāvatāra*.
- 557–559 — *Yuèdēng sānmèi jīng* 月燈三昧經 [[KR6i0276]] (Narendrayaśas): the *Samādhirāja*.
- 581–618 — *Zhànchá shàn'è yèbào jīng* 占察善惡業報經 [[KR6i0545]]: Sui-period Sinitic apocryphon and founder of the wooden-block divination liturgy.
- 649 — *Fódì jīng lùn* 佛地經論 [[KR6i0589]] (Xuánzàng): foundational Yogācāra *bhūmi* treatise.
- 650 — *Yàoshī liúlí guāng rúlái běnyuàn gōngdé jīng* 藥師琉璃光如來本願功德經 [[KR6i0048]] (Xuánzàng): canonical *Bhaiṣajyaguru*.

- c. 800–950 — *Fóshuō dìzàng púsà fāxīn yīnyuán shíwáng jīng* 佛說地藏菩薩發心因緣十王經 [[KR6i0583]] (Zàngchuān): Sinitic apocryphon and base of the ten-kings underworld tradition.
- 822–841 — *Yuánjué jīng dàshū* 圓覺經大疏 [[KR6i0555]] (Zōngmì): defining commentary on the *Yuánjué jīng*.
- 989–1000 — *Fóshuō miàojíxiáng púsà suǒwèn dàshèng fǎluó jīng* 佛說妙吉祥菩薩所問大乘法螺經 [[KR6i0074]] (Fǎxián / Dharmabhadra): a representative Sòng Imperial-Bureau translation, marking the shift to the *Miàojíxiáng* rendering of Mañjuśrī.
- 1500–1800 — *Huàzhū bǎomìng zhēn jīng* 化珠保命真經 [[KR6i0586]]: late-Míng *zhēn-jīng*-genre fusion of Buddhist form with Daoist *neidan* and popular-medical content — the latest substantive text in the division.
