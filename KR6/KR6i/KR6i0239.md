# Fóshuō Dàānbān shǒuyì jīng 佛說大安般守意經
*Sūtra of Great Mindfulness on Inhalation and Exhalation (Mahā-ānāpānasmṛti-sūtra)*
attributed to [[安世高]] (Ān Shìgāo, 譯)

## About the work

The *Dà-ānbān shǒuyì jīng* (T602) is a two-fascicle text of immense significance in early Chinese Buddhism — for centuries the principal Chinese exposition of *ānāpānasmṛti* (mindfulness of breathing) and the foundational text of the *chán* (*dhyāna*) movement of the Eastern Hàn 後漢 and Three Kingdoms. The transmitted text, however, is universally recognized in modern scholarship to be a heavily commentated composite, with Ān Shìgāo's underlying translation overlaid by interlinear and embedded commentary by [[康僧會]] Kāng Sēnghuì (and possibly [[支遁]] Zhī Dùn) that has become inseparable from the root text.

### Prefaces

[[僧祐]] Sēngyòu's *Chū sānzàng jì jí* (T2145, ch. 6) preserves a famous preface by [[康僧會]] Kāng Sēnghuì describing the lineage of the meditation practice from Ān Shìgāo through his disciples [[韓林]] Hán Lín, [[皮業]] Pí Yè, and [[陳慧]] Chén Huì, whose oral commentary Sēnghuì then incorporated. A second preface by [[謝敷]] Xiè Fū is also preserved.

### Abstract

A composite-witness text that reflects the very earliest layer of *dhyāna* practice in China. Modern Jan Nattier (2008, 2014) and Stefano Zacchetti (2002, 2010) have shown that Kongō-ji 金剛寺 (Japan) preserves an alternative manuscript redaction without commentary that is much closer to Ān Shìgāo's translation; T602 should therefore be read as a hybrid commentary-text, not a pure Hàn translation. The bracket 148–170 CE corresponds to Ān Shìgāo's documented translation activity at Luòyáng under Hàn Huándì 漢桓帝.
