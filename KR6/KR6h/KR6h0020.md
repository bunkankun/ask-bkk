# Zànlǐ dìzàng púsà chànyuàn yí 讚禮地藏菩薩懺願儀
*The Ritual of Praise, Salutation, Repentance, and Vow before the Bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha*
by [[智旭]] (Zhìxù / Ǒuyì 蕅益, 述)

## About the work

The *Zànlǐ dìzàng púsà chànyuàn yí* in 1 fascicle is the late-Míng repentance liturgy (*chànyuàn yí* 懺願儀) for the bodhisattva [[地藏]] Kṣitigarbha, composed by the late-Míng Buddhist master [[智旭]] Zhìxù (1599–1655) — better known by his sobriquet *Ǒuyì* 蕅益, the most influential Buddhist scholar of the late Míng. The Xuzangjing print signs the entry "明　菩薩沙彌古吳　智旭　述" — "set forth by Zhìxù, bodhisattva-novice of *Gǔ Wú* 古吳 [Sūzhōu], in the Míng dynasty". The text is structured as a complete *chànfǎ* 懺法 (repentance ritual) liturgy, with rubrics in interlinear notes giving the prescribed actions of the practitioner — purifying body, mouth, and mind; venerating the buddhas; visualising the bodhisattva; reciting confession formulas; and concluding with vows.

### Prefaces

The text opens directly with the liturgical instructions and the prescribed visualisation, without a separate preface.

### Abstract

The *chànyuàn yí* is part of the great late-Míng revival of Buddhist liturgical scholarship, a project to which [[智旭]] contributed several major works (the [[KR6f0050|*Wéishí xīn yào* 唯識新要]] on Yogācāra, his *Línglóng dàlùn* 楞嚴大論 on the *Śūraṅgama*, and his commentaries on the [[KR6h0016|*Dìzàng běnyuàn jīng*]] and other Mahāyāna sūtras). Like his contemporaries [[袾宏]] Yúnqī Zhūhóng and [[德清]] Hānshān Déqīng, [[智旭]] worked to reconstitute properly Buddhist ritual forms after the perceived corruptions of the late-Míng popular tradition; the *Chànyuàn yí* is one such reconstruction, prescribing a structured Kṣitigarbha repentance based on the doctrinal framework of the [[KR6h0016|*Běnyuàn jīng*]].

The dating window 1610–1655 reflects the bracket of [[智旭]]'s active scholarly life, conventionally given as 1610 (the year of his ordination) to 1655 (the year of his death). The work was widely adopted in Chinese, Korean, and Japanese (especially Ōbaku) Buddhist communities and remains in use as the standard Kṣitigarbha repentance liturgy in contemporary East Asian practice.
