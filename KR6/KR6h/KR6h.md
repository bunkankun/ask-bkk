### Scope and scholarly tradition

The Kanripo division KR6h corresponds to the *Dà jí bù* 大集部 (Mahāsaṃnipāta section) of the Taishō Tripiṭaka, occupying T13 (sūtras T0397–T0424 in the printed sequence, with omissions where the Kanripo selection differs). In the Taishō's own structural map (*div05* = 大集部) the section is built around T0397 *Dà fāngděng dà jí jīng* 大方等大集經 [[KR6h0001]], a sixty-fascicle compilation in which translations made independently in the third to seventh centuries were stitched together into a single "Great Assembly" frame. Around this central text the canon collects (a) earlier or alternative renderings of individual sections that survived as separate sūtras, (b) thematically affiliated cycles concerning the bodhisattvas Ākāśagarbha (虛空藏) and Kṣitigarbha (地藏), (c) the *Pratyutpanna-buddha-saṃmukhāvasthita-samādhi* (般舟三昧) and *Bodhisattva-buddhānusmṛti-samādhi* (菩薩念佛三昧) corpora, and (d) a handful of free-standing Mahāyāna sūtras and one Indic śāstra (T0524 = [[KR6h0035]]) whose textual ancestry lies in the same "great assembly" matrix.

The chronological span is wider than that of any other Mahāyāna sūtra division in the canon: it opens with Lokakṣema's late-Hàn renderings of the *Pratyutpanna-samādhi* (179 CE, [[KR6h0026]] / [[KR6h0027]]) — among the earliest datable Chinese Buddhist translations of any kind — and closes with Northern Sòng court-bureau retranslations under Dānapāla and Wéijìng (late tenth to early eleventh century, [[KR6h0004]] / [[KR6h0033]] / [[KR6h0034]]). The Kanripo selection also extends the section sideways into Míng-Qīng paratexts: Zhìxù's Pure-Land repentance liturgy ([[KR6h0020]]) and the three Qīng commentaries on the *Dìzàng běnyuàn jīng* by Língshèng ([[KR6h0017]] / [[KR6h0018]] / [[KR6h0019]]). The principal transmissional traditions a reader must keep in view are therefore three: the Indo-Iranian / Khotanese-mediated translation stream of the fifth century (Dharmakṣema 曇無讖, Buddhayaśas 佛陀耶舍, Dharmamitra 曇摩蜜多), the Suí-Táng Indic-Sanskritic stream (Jñānagupta 闍那崛多, Dharmagupta 達磨笈多, Xuánzàng 玄奘, Prabhākaramitra 波羅頗蜜多羅, Amoghavajra 不空), and the Northern-Sòng *Yìjīng yuàn* 譯經院 bureau stream (Dānapāla 施護, Wéijìng 惟淨, Jīn Zǒngchí 金總持).

### Important texts and text clusters

- **The *Dà jí jīng* compilation proper.** *Dà fāngděng dà jí jīng* 大方等大集經 [[KR6h0001]], Dharmakṣema's Northern-Liáng translation of the core sections (c. 414–426), as gathered into sixty fascicles by the Suí editor Sēngjiù 僧就 (c. 586) with the addition of later renderings by Narendrayaśas 那連提耶舍 and others. This is the structural anchor of the division; every other text either represents an earlier translation of one of its sections, a continuation in the same "great assembly" idiom, or a thematic offshoot.

- **The third-century "section sūtras" cycle.** Dharmarakṣa's 竺法護 four free-standing renderings — *Dà āi jīng* 大哀經 [[KR6h0002]], *Bǎonǚ suǒ wèn jīng* 寶女所問經 [[KR6h0003]], *Wúyán tóngzǐ jīng* 無言童子經 [[KR6h0005]], *Āchāmò púsà jīng* 阿差末菩薩經 [[KR6h0007]] — each corresponds to a discrete *pin* 品 (chapter) of the later compilation. They are the principal evidence for the pre-compilation state of the Mahāsaṃnipāta corpus and the bedrock of Western-language scholarship on the section.

- **The Ākāśagarbha (虛空藏) cluster.** Five recensions of a single cult-text plus one "great" expansion: [[KR6h0008]] (Amoghavajra, Táng), [[KR6h0009]] (Buddhayaśas, Yáo Qín), [[KR6h0010]] (anonymous Eastern Jìn), [[KR6h0011]] (Dharmamitra, Liú Sòng), [[KR6h0012]] (Jñānagupta, Suí), [[KR6h0013]] (Dharmamitra, visualization manual). The cluster is one of the most extensively-retranslated complexes in the Chinese canon and is the principal source for medieval East-Asian Ākāśagarbha devotion.

- **The Kṣitigarbha (地藏) cluster.** Two distinct strata: the *Daśacakra-kṣitigarbha* sūtras [[KR6h0014]] (anonymous, fifth century) and [[KR6h0015]] (Xuánzàng, 651) — eschatological texts on the "ten wheels" of dharma in a *mòfǎ* 末法 age; and the *Dìzàng běnyuàn jīng* 地藏菩薩本願經 [[KR6h0016]] (attributed to Śikṣānanda but almost certainly a Táng-period Chinese composition), which became the canonical scripture of East-Asian Kṣitigarbha cult and attracted the Qīng commentaries [[KR6h0017]] / [[KR6h0018]] / [[KR6h0019]] by Língshèng 靈椉 and the repentance liturgies [[KR6h0020]] / [[KR6h0021]]. Closing the cluster is the short Indic verse-text [[KR6h0022]] (Amoghavajra), which links Kṣitigarbha back to the Mahāsaṃnipāta frame.

- **The *Pratyutpanna-samādhi* / *Buddhānusmṛti-samādhi* cluster.** Lokakṣema's two parallel late-Hàn translations [[KR6h0026]] / [[KR6h0027]] (the latter the locus classicus for early Mahāyāna Buddha-visualization practice and a foundational text for the East-Asian Pure-Land tradition), the anonymous Hàn-period [[KR6h0028]] of the same source, the Suí re-translation as a section of the *Dà jí jīng* [[KR6h0025]] by Jñānagupta, and the closely-related *Púsà niànfó sānmèi jīng* of Gōngdézhí 功德直 [[KR6h0023]] together with its Suí re-translation by Dharmagupta [[KR6h0024]]. Together these works document four centuries of meditation-manual transmission within a single textual lineage.

- **The *Saṃghāṭa-sūtra* pair.** *Sēngjiāzhā jīng* 僧伽吒經 [[KR6h0032]] in the sixth-century Eastern-Wèi rendering of Upaśūnya 月婆首那 and its Sòng-period retranslation as *Dà jíhuì zhèngfǎ jīng* 大集會正法經 [[KR6h0034]] by Dānapāla 施護; together with Jīn Zǒngchí's *Sēngjiāzhā fǎyì jīng* 僧伽吒法義經 [[KR6h0033]]. The *Saṃghāṭa* is unusual in the Chinese canon for having very extensive Gilgit/Sanskrit manuscript witnesses, making it a flagship text for comparative textual study.

- **The *Vikurvāṇa-rāja-paripṛcchā* pair.** Kumārajīva's *Zìzài-wáng púsà jīng* 自在王菩薩經 [[KR6h0029]] and the Eastern-Wèi *Fènxùn-wáng wèn jīng* 奮迅王問經 [[KR6h0030]] of Prajñāruci and Tánlín — two translations of the same Indic source preserving distinct stages of the doctrinal vocabulary.

- **The *Ratnaketu-dhāraṇī*.** *Bǎoxīng tuóluóní jīng* 寶星陀羅尼經 [[KR6h0006]], Prabhākaramitra's 629–630 rendering of a long *dhāraṇī*-laden assembly sūtra, prefaced by Fǎlín 法琳 — important for the early-Táng transition from sūtra to esoteric idiom.

- **The single Indic śāstra.** *Bǎojì jīng sìfǎ yōubōtíshè* 寶髻經四法憂波提舍 [[KR6h0035]], attributed to Vasubandhu (天親菩薩) and translated by Vimokṣasena (毘目智仙), Prajñāruci, and Tánlín — the only commentarial / abhidharmic text in the division, and a witness to the Indian *upadeśa* tradition's engagement with the *Dà jí jīng* corpus.

- **The Northern-Sòng *Yìjīng yuàn* expansion.** *Hǎiyì púsà suǒ wèn jìngyìn fǎmén jīng* 海意菩薩所問淨印法門經 [[KR6h0004]] (Wéijìng 惟淨 *et al.*, c. 1004–1037), an eighteen-fascicle rendering of *Sāgaramati-paripṛcchā* that re-expands a single *pin* of the earlier compilation into an independent scripture — emblematic of the Sòng court-bureau's editorial policy of producing fresh, fuller translations of texts already transmitted in abbreviated form.

### Important persons

- [[曇無讖]] (Dharmakṣema, 385–433) — translator of the Northern-Liáng core of *Dà fāngděng dà jí jīng* [[KR6h0001]]; the founding translator of the Chinese Mahāsaṃnipāta corpus.
- [[僧就]] (Sēngjiù, fl. late sixth c.) — Suí editor who in c. 586 assembled the sixty-fascicle compilation [[KR6h0001]] from Dharmakṣema's translation, the *Sūryagarbha* and *Candragarbha* sections of Narendrayaśas, and other materials.
- [[竺法護]] (Dharmarakṣa, c. 233–c. 310) — Western-Jìn translator of the third-century "section sūtras" [[KR6h0002]] / [[KR6h0003]] / [[KR6h0005]] / [[KR6h0007]], the principal pre-compilation witnesses to the corpus.
- [[支婁迦讖]] (Lokakṣema, fl. 178–189) — late-Hàn translator of the *Pratyutpanna-samādhi* in two recensions [[KR6h0026]] / [[KR6h0027]]; founder of the Buddhānusmṛti / Pure-Land translation tradition in China.
- [[鳩摩羅什]] (Kumārajīva, 344–413) — Yáo-Qín translator of *Zìzài-wáng púsà jīng* [[KR6h0029]].
- [[佛陀耶舍]] (Buddhayaśas, fl. early fifth c.) — Yáo-Qín translator of the short *Ākāśagarbha* sūtra [[KR6h0009]].
- [[曇摩蜜多]] (Dharmamitra, 356–442) — Liú-Sòng translator of two of the *Ākāśagarbha* recensions [[KR6h0011]] / [[KR6h0013]].
- [[功德直]] (Gōngdézhí, fl. mid-fifth c.) — Liú-Sòng translator of *Púsà niànfó sānmèi jīng* [[KR6h0023]].
- [[月婆首那]] (Upaśūnya, fl. mid-sixth c.) — Eastern-Wèi translator of the *Saṃghāṭa-sūtra* [[KR6h0032]].
- [[瞿曇般若流支]] (Gautama Prajñāruci, fl. mid-sixth c.) — Eastern-Wèi translator working with [[曇林]] (Tánlín) on [[KR6h0030]] and on Vasubandhu's *upadeśa* [[KR6h0035]] (with [[毘目智仙]] Vimokṣasena).
- [[闍那崛多]] (Jñānagupta, 523–c. 600) — Suí translator of [[KR6h0012]], [[KR6h0025]] (the *Bhadrapāla* section of the compilation), and [[KR6h0031]].
- [[達磨笈多]] (Dharmagupta, d. 619) — Suí translator of *Púsà niànfó sānmèi fēn* [[KR6h0024]].
- [[波羅頗蜜多羅]] (Prabhākaramitra, 565–633) — early-Táng translator of *Bǎoxīng tuóluóní jīng* [[KR6h0006]].
- [[玄奘]] (Xuánzàng, c. 602–664) — Táng retranslator of the *Daśacakra* [[KR6h0015]] in 651, definitive for East-Asian Kṣitigarbha doctrine.
- [[實叉難陀]] (Śikṣānanda, 652–710) — Khotanese translator under whose name the *Dìzàng běnyuàn jīng* [[KR6h0016]] circulates; the attribution is doubted by modern scholarship.
- [[不空]] (Amoghavajra, 705–774) — Táng esoteric master and translator of [[KR6h0008]] and [[KR6h0022]], bringing the Mahāsaṃnipāta corpus into the orbit of mid-Táng Vajrayāna.
- [[施護]] (Dānapāla, d. 1018) — Northern-Sòng *Yìjīng yuàn* translator of *Dà jíhuì zhèngfǎ jīng* [[KR6h0034]], the *Saṃghāṭa* re-translation.
- [[惟淨]] (Wéijìng, 973–c. 1051) — Northern-Sòng court-bureau translator-monk who led the eighteen-fascicle rendering of *Sāgaramati-paripṛcchā* [[KR6h0004]].
- [[金總持]] (Jīn Zǒngchí, fl. late eleventh c.) — Sòng translator of [[KR6h0033]].
- [[智旭]] (Ouyi Zhìxù, 1599–1655) — Late-Míng Tiāntái-Pure-Land synthesizer whose Kṣitigarbha repentance liturgy [[KR6h0020]] is the principal Míng paratext in the division.
- [[靈椉]] (Língshèng, fl. mid-Qīng) — author of the three Qīng commentaries on the *Dìzàng běnyuàn jīng* [[KR6h0017]] / [[KR6h0018]] / [[KR6h0019]] — the densest commentarial reception of any single text in the division.

### Topics

- **The "great assembly" (*mahāsaṃnipāta*) frame.** The defining literary device of the section is the convocation of bodhisattvas from all directions before the Buddha for the exposition of dharma in the open space between two world-systems. It is at once a narrative scaffold and a doctrinal claim — that the dharmas to be taught in this section transcend any single buddha-field — and it allows the compiler to keep interpolating new texts indefinitely. Reading the division means watching this frame absorb successive materials over four centuries.

- **Compilation, interpolation, and the limits of Chinese canonicity.** [[KR6h0001]] is the locus classicus of the problem: Sēngjiù took Dharmakṣema's c. 414–426 base, slotted in Narendrayaśas's later *Sūryagarbha* and *Candragarbha* sections (c. 566), and re-arranged the whole into a sixty-fascicle book that has no exact Indic counterpart. The presence of multiple free-standing translations of constituent *pin* (Dharmarakṣa's, Buddhayaśas's, the Sòng-period *Hǎiyì* expansion) lets the reader see exactly which parts pre-existed the compilation and which were grafted in.

- **Ākāśagarbha and Kṣitigarbha as East-Asian bodhisattva cults.** The division is the principal scriptural reservoir for both. The Ākāśagarbha cluster ([[KR6h0008]]–[[KR6h0013]]) records a sustained fourth–eighth-century interest in this celestial bodhisattva; the Kṣitigarbha cluster ([[KR6h0014]]–[[KR6h0022]]) marks the shift, beginning with the *Daśacakra* sūtras, towards Kṣitigarbha's distinctively East-Asian role as saviour of beings in the lower destinies. The cult-trajectory of Kṣitigarbha — culminating in the *Dìzàng běnyuàn jīng* and its Qīng commentaries — is the longest sustained doctrinal arc in the division.

- **Buddhānusmṛti and Pratyutpanna-samādhi.** [[KR6h0026]] / [[KR6h0027]] / [[KR6h0023]] / [[KR6h0024]] / [[KR6h0025]] together form one of the canon's most important meditation-manual corpora, prescribing the practice of visualizing the buddhas of the present age in concentrated absorption. This is the textual genealogy on which much of the Pure-Land tradition draws, although the texts themselves do not yet operate within a strictly Pure-Land soteriology.

- **Eschatology and the *mòfǎ* (末法) age.** A pervasive concern across the section, most explicit in the *Daśacakra-kṣitigarbha* sūtras [[KR6h0014]] / [[KR6h0015]] but already present in Dharmakṣema's *Sūryagarbha* and *Candragarbha* materials inside [[KR6h0001]]. The "decline of the dharma" frame structures the soteriological urgency of the entire division.

- **Dhāraṇī and esoteric ritual.** Even before the rise of Vajrayāna proper, the *Dà jí* corpus carries a strong *dhāraṇī* element ([[KR6h0006]], [[KR6h0010]], [[KR6h0011]]) that opens the way for Amoghavajra's mid-Táng esoteric retranslations [[KR6h0008]] / [[KR6h0022]]. The section is thus a useful site for tracing the gradient between sūtra and tantra in the Chinese transmission.

- **Re-translation as canonical practice.** The Sòng *Yìjīng yuàn* deliberately produced fresh renderings of texts already preserved in earlier translations: [[KR6h0034]] retranslates the *Saṃghāṭa*, [[KR6h0004]] re-expands a single *pin* of the earlier compilation, [[KR6h0033]] adds a third *Saṃghāṭa* witness. This editorial habit is one of the distinctive contributions of the Northern-Sòng court-monastic translation programme and is unusually well-documented in this division.
