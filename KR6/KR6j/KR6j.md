## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR6j 密教部 (*Mìjiào bù*, Jp. *Mikkyō-bu*) is the Esoteric (Tantric) section of the Buddhist canon as organised by the Taishō *Shinshū Daizōkyō* 大正新脩大藏經 — Taishō volumes 18–21 in their canonical sequence, plus an extensive Japanese commentarial corpus drawn from later Taishō volumes (notably T39, T58, T59, T60, T61, T75, T77, T78, T79). It corresponds to division *div17* 密教部 in the CANWWW digital catalogue, which is the principal scholarly mapping of the Taishō to which Kanripo here adheres. The 762 entries gathered in KR6j make it one of the largest sub-divisions of the entire Kanripo Buddhist canon, comparable in scale only to the dhāraṇī- and ritual-heavy sections such as the 經集部 and the late-Sòng translation corpus.

The texts in KR6j fall into three temporally and doctrinally distinct strata. (1) **Pre-Esoteric dhāraṇī sūtras** translated in the Eastern Hàn through Suí period (c. 25 CE – 600 CE), often single-fascicle scriptures centred on a specific *dhāraṇī* (mantra-formula) and a single deity (Avalokiteśvara, Mañjuśrī, Bhaiṣajyaguru, etc.); these are the proto-Tantric substrate. (2) The **High-Tang Esoteric synthesis** (c. 716–774 CE) carried out at Cháng'ān and Luòyáng by the so-called "Three Tang Esoteric Patriarchs" — Śubhakarasiṃha 善無畏 ([[善無畏]], 637–735), Vajrabodhi 金剛智 ([[金剛智]], 671–741), and Amoghavajra 不空 ([[不空]], 705–774) — which gave East Asian Esoteric Buddhism its doctrinal core (the *Dàrì jīng* / *Vajraśekhara* / *Susiddhikara* triad) and its institutional shape under the *Kāiyuán* / *Tiānbǎo* / *Dàlì* reign-eras. (3) The **Sòng "new translation" bureau** (c. 982 – c. 1110), in which Dānapāla 施護 ([[施護]]), Devaśāntika / Tiānxīzāi 天息災 ([[天息災]]), Dharmadeva / Fǎtiān 法天 ([[法天]]), and Fǎxián 法賢 ([[法賢]]) — operating from the Yìjīngyuàn 譯經院 founded in 982 — rendered a large body of Indian *yoga-* and *anuttarayoga*-tantra material into Chinese, though by then the East Asian institutional tradition of Esoteric practice was in decline. To these three strata Kanripo adds a fourth, paratextual layer: Japanese Heian–Kamakura sub-commentary in the *kāití* 開題, *shìjì* 私記 (Jp. *shiki*), and *kǒuchuán* 口傳 (Jp. *kuden*) genres — Kūkai's 開題 corpus and the medieval Shingon scholastic glosses on Yīxíng's *Dàrìjīng shū*.

The principal commentarial pivot of the division is Yīxíng's 一行 ([[一行]]) twenty-fascicle *Dàrìjīng shū* 大日經疏 ([[KR6j0662]], T39n1796), composed at Luòyáng 724–727 under Śubhakarasiṃha's oral direction and the doctrinal foundation of every subsequent East Asian Esoteric exegesis. Kūkai 空海 ([[空海]]) brought this commentary to Japan in 806 and built the entire Shingon 真言 scholastic edifice on it — a transmission whose secondary literature occupies a substantial portion of the latter half of KR6j (the *Miàoyìn chāo* 妙印鈔 cluster KR6j0663–0671 and adjacent items). For the Tendai 天台 *Taimitsu* 台密 transmission of the same body of material — through Saichō 最澄, Ennin 圓仁 ([[圓仁]]), Enchin 圓珍 ([[圓珍]]), and Annen 安然 ([[安然]]) — the principal exegetical apparatus is dispersed elsewhere in KR6 and in the Japanese non-canonical literature; only a small number of *Taimitsu*-authored items (the *kāití* and *zhǐguī* 指歸 of Enchin) sit inside KR6j proper.

## Important texts and text clusters

The division is anchored by the two foundational sūtras of East Asian Esoteric Buddhism, the *Dàrì jīng* and the *Jīngāngdǐng jīng*, which together define the two-mandala (*liǎngjiè* 兩界 / Jp. *ryōkai*) system of doctrine and practice:

- *Dà Pílúzhēnà chéngfó shénbiàn jiāchí jīng* 大毘盧遮那成佛神變加持經 (*Mahāvairocana-sūtra*; *Dàrì jīng* 大日經) — [[KR6j0001]] (T18n0848). Translated 724–725 by [[善無畏]] and [[一行]]. The basis of the **Garbhadhātu** (*Tāizàngjiè* 胎藏界) lineage; locus classicus for the doctrines of the Three Mysteries (三密), *dharmakāya*-preached scripture (法身說法), and the *bodhi-mind / great-compassion / expedient-means* triad.
- *Jīngāngdǐng yīqiè rúlái zhēnshí shè dàshèng xiànzhèng dàjiàowáng jīng* 金剛頂一切如來真實攝大乘現證大教王經 (*Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṃgraha*; *Jīngāngdǐng jīng* 金剛頂經) — [[KR6j0024]] (T18n0865), Amoghavajra's three-fascicle rendering c. 753. The basis of the **Vajradhātu** (*Jīngāngjiè* 金剛界) lineage. The companion six-fascicle "short version" by Vajrabodhi is *Jīngāngdǐng yújiā zhōng lüèchū niànsòng jīng* 金剛頂瑜伽中略出念誦經 ([[KR6j0030]], T18n0866); the full thirty-fascicle Sòng rendering of the same *Tattvasaṃgraha* by Dānapāla is [[KR6j0041]]; Amoghavajra's important meta-text *Eighteen Assemblies* (*Shíbāhuì zhǐguī* 金剛頂經瑜伽十八會指歸) — the only Chinese-language overview of the *Vajraśekhara* tantra-corpus as a literary system — is [[KR6j0035]] (T18n0869).
- *Sūxīdì jiéluó jīng* 蘇悉地羯羅經 (*Susiddhikara-sūtra*) — [[KR6j0061]] (T18n0893), translated 724–735 by [[善無畏]]. The third pillar of the Tang Esoteric canon, principally a ritual-procedural scripture; the *Tendai* 台密 tradition gave it canonical status equal to the *Dàrì* and *Jīngāngdǐng*, generating a distinct three-sūtra (*sānbù* 三部) doctrinal framework as opposed to the Shingon two-mandala system. Its companion ritual manuals are [[KR6j0063]] and *Sūxīdì jiéluó gōngyǎng fǎ* 蘇悉地羯羅供養法 ([[KR6j0064]]).
- *Sūpóhū tóngzǐ qǐngwèn jīng* 蘇婆呼童子請問經 (*Subāhu-paripṛcchā*) — [[KR6j0065]] / [[KR6j0066]] (T18n0895a/b), again Śubhakarasiṃha. The principal *kriyā*-tantra ritual scripture; its concern with the *sādhana* failure-modes (*siddhi-vighna*) and ritual purification became the textual basis for the Esoteric handling of obstacles.

The principal exegetical apparatus of the *Dàrì jīng* is concentrated in a tight commentarial cluster:

- *Dà Pílúzhēnà chéngfó jīng shū* 大毘盧遮那成佛經疏 (*Dàrìjīng shū*) — [[KR6j0662]] (T39n1796) by [[一行]], 724–727. Twenty fascicles recording Śubhakarasiṃha's oral exegesis; the most consequential single commentary in the Esoteric tradition.
- *Dà Pílúzhēnà jīng gōngyǎng cìdì fǎ shū* 大毘盧遮那經供養次第法疏 — [[KR6j0670]], Yīxíng's ritual companion.
- *Dàrì jīng yìshì* 大日經義釋 — [[KR6j0734]], the redacted recension of Yīxíng's commentary that became canonical in Tendai *Taimitsu*.
- *Dàrì jīng yìshì yǎnmì chāo* 大日經義釋演密鈔 — [[KR6j0735]], the principal Liáo gloss.
- The medieval Japanese Shingon sub-commentary cluster on Yīxíng's *shū*: [[KR6j0663]] *Miàoyìn chāo* 妙印鈔, [[KR6j0664]] *Miàoyìn chāo kǒuzhuàn* 妙印鈔口傳, [[KR6j0665]] / [[KR6j0669]] *Zhùxīn-pǐn shū sījì* 住心品疏私記, [[KR6j0666]] *Yǎn'ào chāo* 演奧鈔, [[KR6j0667]] *Zhǐxīn chāo* 指心鈔, [[KR6j0668]] *Dàrìjīng shū chāo* 大日經疏鈔, [[KR6j0671]] *Gōngyǎng cìdì fǎ shū sījì* 供養次第法疏私記.

Kūkai's own writings in KR6j are confined to four *kāití* 開題 (Jp. *kaidai*, "title-expositions"): [[KR6j0002]] *Dàrìjīng kāití* 大日經開題, [[KR6j0025]] *Jīngāngdǐngjīng kāití* 金剛頂經開題, [[KR6j0026]] *Jiàowángjīng kāití* 教王經開題, and [[KR6j0119]] *Dà-fódǐng jīng kāití* 大佛頂經開題. His doctrinal *Sandai-bu* — the *Jūjūshinron* 十住心論, *Hizō-hōyaku* 秘藏寶鑰, *Kenmitsu-nikyō-ron* 顯密二教論, *Sokushin-jōbutsu-gi* 即身成佛義 — sits in Taishō vol. 77 (諸宗部) and is **not** in KR6j.

The third major textual block of KR6j is the **dhāraṇī-sūtra and ritual-manual corpus**, organised in deity-clusters:

- The **Uṣṇīṣa / 大佛頂 / 尊勝** cluster: [[KR6j0144]] *Fódǐng zūnshèng tuóluóní jīng* 佛頂尊勝陀羅尼經 (*Uṣṇīṣavijaya*) and the satellite items [[KR6j0148]]–[[KR6j0157]]; the *Yīzìdǐnglún* 一字頂輪 cluster (*Ekākṣaroṣṇīṣacakra*) at [[KR6j0124]] and [[KR6j0129]]–[[KR6j0135]].
- The **Avalokiteśvara dhāraṇī corpus** — the *Dàbēi zhòu* 大悲咒 / *Qiānshǒu jīng* 千手經 cluster [[KR6j0258]]–[[KR6j0274]] (anchor scripture [[KR6j0260]], Bhagavaddharma's 650s translation); the *Eleven-Faced* (*Ekādaśamukha*) cluster [[KR6j0275]]–[[KR6j0277]]; the *Bùkōng juànsuǒ* 不空羂索 (*Amoghapāśa*) cluster [[KR6j0300]]–[[KR6j0306]].
- The **Mañjuśrī ritual corpus** [[KR6j0395]]–[[KR6j0424]], the densest single deity-cluster in the division.
- The **Bhaiṣajyaguru** (*Yàoshī*) ritual corpus [[KR6j0093]]–[[KR6j0100]].
- The Esoteric **Pure-Land overlap** (Amitābha / *Wúliángshòu* mantric scriptures): [[KR6j0102]], [[KR6j0108]], [[KR6j0109]], [[KR6j0451]], [[KR6j0621]].

A fourth, doctrinally pivotal text-cluster is the **Lǐqù** 理趣 (*Adhyardhaśatikā Prajñāpāramitā*) **corpus**: Amoghavajra's exegetical *Lǐqù shì* 理趣釋 — *Dàlè jīngāng bùkōng zhēnshí sānmèiyé jīng bōrě bōluómìduō lǐqù shì* ([[KR6j0193]], T1003), the locus classicus of the famous "passion as enlightenment" hermeneutic; with its sub-commentaries [[KR6j0194]] and *Lǐqù shì bìyào chāo* 理趣釋祕要鈔 ([[KR6j0195]], 1356, by [[杲寶]]), and the structural variant [[KR6j0196]]. (The base scripture itself, *Bōrě bōluómìduō lǐqù jīng* 般若波羅蜜多理趣經 = T243, is canonically classified by Taishō under 般若部 and sits in [[KR6c]], not here.)

## Important persons

- [[善無畏]] (Śubhakarasiṃha, 637–735) — Indian master, arrived Cháng'ān 716; translated the *Dàrìjīng* and *Susiddhikara* and inaugurated the Tang Esoteric transmission. **First** of the Three Tang Patriarchs; 23 texts in KR6j.
- [[金剛智]] (Vajrabodhi, 671–741) — South Indian master, arrived Cháng'ān c. 720; translated the "short *Vajraśekhara*" ([[KR6j0030]]) and the *Yúqí jīng* ([[KR6j0031]]). **Second** Tang Patriarch; 25 texts in KR6j.
- [[不空]] (Amoghavajra, 705–774) — Sogdian-born disciple of Vajrabodhi; the single most prolific translator-author in the division (165 texts, c. 22% of the entire corpus). **Third** Tang Patriarch; honoured by three emperors (Xuánzōng, Sùzōng, Dàizōng) and the dominant institutional figure of mid-Tang Buddhism. Author of the canonical *Jīngāngdǐngjīng* ([[KR6j0024]]), the *Eighteen Assemblies* ([[KR6j0035]]), the *Lǐqù shì* ([[KR6j0193]]), and the bulk of the Tang ritual-manual corpus.
- [[一行]] (Yīxíng, 683–727) — Chinese disciple of Śubhakarasiṃha; calendrical astronomer and the principal Chinese exegete of the Tang Esoteric transmission. Author of the *Dàrìjīng shū* ([[KR6j0662]]) — the doctrinal foundation of every subsequent East Asian Esoteric commentary.
- [[施護]] (Dānapāla, fl. 982 – c. 1018) — Uḍḍiyāna master; co-founding translator at the Sòng Yìjīngyuàn 譯經院 from 982. The largest Sòng-era contributor: 48 texts, including the full thirty-fascicle Sòng rendering of the *Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṃgraha* ([[KR6j0041]]).
- [[法賢]] (Fǎxián / Dharmadeva, fl. 982 – c. 1001) — second of the Sòng Yìjīngyuàn translators (43 texts); responsible for a substantial body of late-Indian dhāraṇī material.
- [[法天]] (Fǎtiān / Dharmadeva-bis, fl. 973 – c. 1001) — Magadhan master; translated Indian *anuttarayoga* tantra-fragments into Chinese (23 texts).
- [[天息災]] (Tiānxīzāi / Devaśāntika, fl. 980 – c. 1000) — head of the Yìjīngyuàn at its founding; 6 texts.
- [[慈賢]] (Cíxián, fl. 11th c.) — Liáo-period Khitan master; 9 texts. Principal locus of post-Tang continental Esoteric transmission outside the Sòng bureau.
- [[空海]] (Kūkai, 774–835) — Japanese student of Huìguǒ 惠果 at Qīnglóngsì 青龍寺 (804–806); founder of the Japanese Shingon 真言 school; brought the entire Tang Esoteric corpus to Japan in 806. Author of [[KR6j0002]], [[KR6j0025]], [[KR6j0026]], [[KR6j0119]] (the four *kāití*) within this division; his doctrinal *Sandai-bu* is preserved outside KR6j.
- [[圓仁]] (Ennin, 794–864) — Japanese Tendai monk, founder of Tendai-side *Taimitsu* 台密 transmission; in China 838–847. Contributor to the *kāití* / *zhǐguī* literature.
- [[圓珍]] (Enchin, 814–891) — Japanese Tendai monk, in China 853–858; deepened the *Taimitsu* synthesis. Author of *Dà-Pílúzhēnà jīng zhǐguī* 大毘盧遮那經指歸 ([[KR6j0003]]).
- [[安然]] (Annen, c. 841 – c. 915) — *Taimitsu* doctrinal systematiser; principal author of the Heian Tendai esoteric synthesis (largely outside KR6j proper).
- [[杲寶]] (Gōhō, 1306–1362) — Kamakura-Nanbokuchō Shingon scholastic at Tō-ji 東寺; principal author of the *Lǐqù-shì bìyào chāo* and other late-medieval Shingon sub-commentaries.
- [[宥範]] (Yūhan, 1270–1352) — contemporary of Gōhō; Shingon scholastic, contributed the *Yǎn'ào chāo* 演奧鈔 ([[KR6j0666]]) and adjacent gloss-texts.
- [[菩提流志]] (Bodhiruci, d. 727) — Indian translator at Cháng'ān under Wǔ Zétiān and Xuánzōng; 15 dhāraṇī-sūtra translations in KR6j, bridging the pre-Tang dhāraṇī corpus and the High-Tang Esoteric synthesis.
- [[義淨]] (Yìjìng, 635–713), [[玄奘]] (Xuánzàng, 602–664), [[實叉難陀]] (Śikṣānanda, 652–710), [[菩提流支]] (Bodhiruci-the-earlier, fl. 508–537) — major Tang-and-earlier translators whose dhāraṇī-related output is collected in KR6j alongside their main Mahāyāna corpora (collected elsewhere in KR6).

## Topics

The principal interpretive and doctrinal issues running through KR6j are:

- **The two-mandala system** (*liǎngjiè* 兩界 / Jp. *ryōkai*): the doctrinal organisation of the Esoteric canon around the polar pair *Garbhadhātu* 胎藏界 (the *Dàrìjīng*'s mandala of compassion-as-cause) and *Vajradhātu* 金剛界 (the *Jīngāngdǐngjīng*'s mandala of wisdom-as-fruit). The Shingon school treats them as a non-dual pair; the Tendai *Taimitsu* tradition adds the *Susiddhikara* as a third co-equal element (*sānbù* 三部).
- **Dharmakāya-preached scripture** (法身說法): the doctrinal claim — first articulated systematically in Yīxíng's *Dàrìjīng shū* and developed in Kūkai's *kāití* — that the speaker of the Esoteric sūtras is not the historical Śākyamuni in *nirmāṇakāya* but the cosmic *dharmakāya* Mahāvairocana itself. The basis of the entire tradition's claim to supersede the exoteric Mahāyāna.
- **The Three Mysteries** (三密 *sānmì*): the practical-soteriological doctrine of body-, speech-, and mind-correspondence between the practitioner and the Buddha, mediated by *mudrā*, *mantra*, and *samādhi*. Locus classicus the *Dàrìjīng*'s first chapter.
- **The Eighteen Assemblies** (十八會) of the *Vajraśekhara*-corpus: Amoghavajra's *Shíbāhuì zhǐguī* ([[KR6j0035]]) is the only Chinese-language schematic of the *Vajraśekhara* tantra-cycle as a literary system, identifying the Chinese-translated *Tattvasaṃgraha* as the first of an eighteen-text tantra-cycle of which the bulk circulated in India / Tibet but not in Chinese.
- ***Abhiṣeka* and lineage transmission**: the consecration ritual that confers Esoteric authority. The institutional spine of the tradition from Vajrabodhi's transmission of *abhiṣeka* to Amoghavajra, through Huìguǒ to Kūkai, and through the Saichō / Ennin / Enchin lineage in Tendai *Taimitsu*.
- **Mantra and dhāraṇī efficacy**: the broad doctrinal question — present already in the pre-Tang dhāraṇī sūtras and refined in the *Lǐqù shì* — of how mantric sound, considered as the resonance of the *dharmakāya*, accomplishes soteriological work. The basis of the entire ritual-manual corpus.
- **"Passion as enlightenment"** (the *Lǐqù shì* hermeneutic): Amoghavajra's claim in [[KR6j0193]] that the human passions, when read at the Esoteric semantic level, are themselves enlightenment; the doctrinal hinge between the exoteric *Prajñāpāramitā* literature and the developed Esoteric tradition. The most controversial single text in the division.
- **Garbhadhātu mandala iconography and the *taizōkai* 胎藏界 visual programme**: the ritual layout of the Garbhadhātu mandala, codified in the *Dàrìjīng*'s middle chapters, became the basis for the entire East Asian Esoteric iconographic tradition — paintings, sculptural programmes, ritual diagrams, and the *abhiṣeka* hall layouts of Tō-ji and Mt. Kōya.
- **Tang-to-Japan transmission**: the institutional history of the transmission from Huìguǒ's *abhiṣeka* of Kūkai (805) and Saichō (804) onward — the single most consequential transfer of a Buddhist textual-ritual tradition between two East Asian polities, and the reason for the heavy Japanese commentarial layer in KR6j.
- **Sòng "new translations" and the decline of Chinese institutional Esotericism**: the Sòng Yìjīngyuàn (982–1082) rendered substantial new tantra-material into Chinese (the Dānapāla / Fǎxián / Fǎtiān corpus, 100+ texts in KR6j), but by then the Tang Esoteric institutional infrastructure had collapsed and these texts had no significant ritual reception in China itself. They became principally a Japanese scholastic resource.
- **The Heian–Kamakura *kāití* / *shìjì* genre**: the Japanese Shingon and Tendai scholastic apparatus on the Esoteric scriptures, codified in the *kāití* (title-exegesis), *shìjì* (sub-commentarial note), and *kǒuchuán* (oral-transmission record) genres. KR6j's largest paratextual layer.

## Timeline

- c. 25–600 — Pre-Esoteric dhāraṇī sūtras (Eastern Hàn through Suí). Approximately 90 texts in KR6j; representative items include *Qiānshǒu qiānyǎn dàbēi xīn tuóluóní jīng* 千手千眼陀羅尼經 ([[KR6j0260]], Bhagavaddharma, 650–661) at the late end of the bracket, and the Hàn–Suí 文殊 / 觀音 dhāraṇī corpus at the early end.
- 716–724 — Arrival of [[善無畏]] (716) and [[金剛智]] (c. 720) at Cháng'ān; inauguration of the Tang Esoteric transmission.
- 724–725 — *Dà Pílúzhēnà chéngfó shénbiàn jiāchí jīng* 大毘盧遮那成佛神變加持經 ([[KR6j0001]]) — Śubhakarasiṃha and Yīxíng's translation of the *Mahāvairocana-sūtra* at Luòyáng.
- 724–727 — *Dà Pílúzhēnà chéngfó jīng shū* 大毘盧遮那成佛經疏 ([[KR6j0662]]) — Yīxíng's twenty-fascicle commentary, the doctrinal foundation of the tradition.
- 724–735 — *Sūxīdì jiéluó jīng* 蘇悉地羯羅經 ([[KR6j0061]]) and *Sūpóhū tóngzǐ qǐngwèn jīng* 蘇婆呼童子請問經 ([[KR6j0065]]) — Śubhakarasiṃha's ritual-procedural scriptures.
- c. 720–741 — Vajrabodhi's *Jīngāngdǐng yújiā zhōng lüèchū niànsòng jīng* 金剛頂瑜伽中略出念誦經 ([[KR6j0030]]) and *Yúqí jīng* 瑜祇經 ([[KR6j0031]]).
- 746–774 — [[不空]] Amoghavajra's translation and compilation programme at Cháng'ān under three emperors; the canonical *Jīngāngdǐng yīqiè rúlái zhēnshí shè dàjiàowáng jīng* 金剛頂一切如來真實攝大乘現證大教王經 ([[KR6j0024]], c. 753); the *Shíbāhuì zhǐguī* 十八會指歸 ([[KR6j0035]]); the *Lǐqù shì* 理趣釋 ([[KR6j0193]]); and the bulk of the Tang ritual-manual corpus (165 texts).
- 805–806 — Huìguǒ's *abhiṣeka* of Kūkai (805) and Saichō's *abhiṣeka* under Shùnxiǎo 順曉 (804); transmission of the Esoteric corpus to Japan.
- 815–835 — [[空海]] Kūkai's *kāití* corpus at Tō-ji and Mt. Kōya: [[KR6j0002]] *Dàrìjīng kāití*, [[KR6j0025]] *Jīngāngdǐngjīng kāití*, [[KR6j0026]] *Jiàowángjīng kāití*, [[KR6j0119]] *Dà-fódǐng jīng kāití*.
- 838–847; 853–858 — Ennin's and Enchin's pilgrimages to Tang China and the consolidation of Tendai *Taimitsu*; [[KR6j0003]] Enchin's *Dà-Pílúzhēnà jīng zhǐguī*.
- 982 onward — Founding of the Sòng Yìjīngyuàn 譯經院 under [[天息災]], [[施護]], [[法天]], and [[法賢]]; sustained Sòng translation programme of late-Indian Esoteric material (174 texts in the 901–1300 bracket).
