# Shè wúài dàbēi xīn dà tuóluóní jīng… màntúluó yíguǐ 攝無礙大悲心大陀羅尼經計一法中出無量義南方滿願補陀落海會五部諸尊等弘誓力方位及威儀形色執持三摩耶幖幟曼荼羅儀軌
*Maṇḍala Manual of the Five Sections of Honoured Ones — Their Vows, Positions, Comportment, Form, Color, and Samaya Insignia — Of the South Pūrṇa Ocean Assembly of Potalaka, Drawing Out Boundless Meanings from the One Method of the Sūtra of the All-Encompassing Unobstructed Great-Compassion-Heart Great Dhāraṇī*
by [[不空]] (Bùkōng, Amoghavajra, 譯)

## About the work

A one-fascicle Tángmì *màntúluó yíguǐ* 曼荼羅儀軌 — a full maṇḍala manual — for the *Sahasrabhuja* cycle, organised around the **Potalaka assembly** (補陀落海會) of Avalokiteśvara and his five-section retinue. The colophon names **Amoghavajra** ([[不空]]) as the translator. The very long descriptive title is conventional in Tángmì *yíguǐ*: the editorial sub-title 但呪讚及作法宜視大儀軌內 ("for the spells, hymns, and ritual procedures consult the great manual") indicates the present text is the maṇḍala-iconography component to be paired with [[KR6j0272]] (the recitation-practice manual) and [[KR6j0269]] (the spell-text proper) as the third member of the Amoghavajra *Sahasrabhuja* *yíguǐ* triad.

### Abstract

The text describes the full maṇḍala of the **Potalaka oceanic assembly** of Avalokiteśvara: the central deity (Sahasrabhuja-Avalokiteśvara), the four directional bodhisattvas, the five-section retinue (五部諸尊 — Buddha section, Vajra section, Ratna section, Padma section, Karma section), each deity's *praṇidhāna* / *bāla* (弘誓力), spatial position (方位), comportment (威儀), iconographic form and color (形色), and *samaya* / insignia (三摩耶幖幟). The explicit "drawing-out-boundless-meanings-from-one-method" rubric (計一法中出無量義) is the standard Tangmí formula for *kalpa*-derived ritual elaboration: from the single core dhāraṇī of [[KR6j0260]] the manual generates the full Esoteric maṇḍala of the *Sahasrabhuja* deity. The text is essential reading for understanding the Tángmì iconography of the *Sahasrabhuja* and the South-Indian *Pūrṇa-girí* / Potalaka maṇḍala tradition that Amoghavajra had brought back to China in 746.
