# Jìngcí yàoyǔ 淨慈要語
*Essential Sayings on Pure Compassion*
by [[元賢]] (Yǒngjué Yuánxián, 述)

## About the work

A two-*juǎn* doctrinal-pastoral handbook composed by [[元賢]] **Yǒngjué Yuánxián** 永覺元賢 (1578–1657), one of the two leading late-Míng / early-Qīng **Cáodòng** 曹洞 Chán masters and abbot of the **Gǔshān Yǒngqiānsì** 鼓山湧泉寺 in **Fúzhōu** 福州 (Fújiàn). The title *jìngcí* 淨慈 — "Pure Compassion" — combines the central Pure Land idiom (*jìng* 淨, "pure" / Pure Land) with the central Mahāyāna idiom (*cí* 慈, *maitrī* / loving-kindness), and signals the work's principal concern: the integration of Pure Land devotional practice with the bodhisattva path of compassionate engagement with the world.

### Abstract

Yuánxián is one of the few **Cáodòng** Chán masters of the late-imperial period to produce a substantial Pure Land work. The Cáodòng tradition's distinctive doctrinal apparatus — the **five ranks** (*wǔwèi* 五位) inherited from Dòngshān Liángjiè 洞山良价, the meticulous attention to gradations of attainment, the relatively cautious attitude toward strong soteriological claims — sits in some tension with the more direct soteriological promise of Pure Land devotion, and Cáodòng masters of the late-imperial period had generally been less prominent in Pure Land scholarship than their Línjì counterparts. Yuánxián's *Jìngcí yàoyǔ* is therefore distinctive in **applying Cáodòng doctrinal categories to the analysis of Pure Land cultivation**, and in arguing that Pure Land devotion is fully consistent with the most rigorous Cáodòng Chán practice.

The two *juǎn* cover: doctrinal foundations (the Pure Land sūtras and their key passages, organised through Cáodòng analytic categories); the central thesis of *jìngcí*-integration (Pure Land practice as the natural expression of bodhisattva *cí* 慈; the integration of *zìlì* / *tālì* through the Cáodòng *zhèngpiān* 正偏 / "straight-bent" analysis); and practical instructions (the daily *yíguǐ* of *niànfó*; the deathbed practice; pastoral guidance for monastic and lay practitioners). The work is more discursive and pastoral than the polemical Pure Land texts of the late Míng (Yúnqī's *Yíbiàn*, Tiānrú's *Huòwèn*); Yuánxián writes from the position of an established Cáodòng master who is integrating Pure Land into his existing doctrinal-pastoral programme rather than from the position of a Pure Land partisan defending the tradition against Chán objections.

The text is preserved only in the *Xùzàngjīng* 卍續藏 (X1166). No preface fixes a precise composition date; the dating bracket adopted (1640–1657) covers Yuánxián's mature late-period abbacy at Gǔshān up to his death in 順治 14 (1657).

## Other points of interest

Yuánxián entered monastic life unusually late — at age 40, in 1617 — having spent the first half of his life as a Confucian-trained literatus (lay surname Cài 蔡, descendant of the Sòng Neo-Confucian scholar Cài Yuándìng 蔡元定). The *Jìngcí yàoyǔ* shows the marks of this Confucian training: the prose is literarily controlled, the doctrinal arguments are presented with literati-style attention to coherence and citation, and the work as a whole reads as the work of a scholar-monk rather than a devotional popular author. This combination of Cáodòng Chán authority, Confucian literary training, and Pure Land doctrinal commitment is unusual for the late-Míng / early-Qīng and gives the work a distinctive intellectual register.
