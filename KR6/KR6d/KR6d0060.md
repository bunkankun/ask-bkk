# Fǎhuá jīng dàyì 法華經大意
*The Great Purport of the Lotus Sūtra*
by [[湛然]] (Zhànrán / Jīngxī Zhànrán, 述)

## About the work

A single-*juan* synoptic exposition of the Lotus Sūtra by [[湛然]] Zhànrán (711–782), the ninth Tiāntái patriarch and architect of the mid-Táng Tiāntái revival. The work is the most accessible of Zhànrán's surviving Lotus Sūtra writings — markedly briefer than his *Fǎhuá xuányì shìqiān* ([[KR6d0007]], T1717, 20 fascicles) and *Fǎhuá wénjù jì* ([[KR6d0015]], T1719, 30 fascicles) — and provides a unified Tiāntái doctrinal framing of the Lotus suitable for use as an introductory text or as a study aid for students preparing to engage with the larger commentarial apparatus. Body attribution: *Pílíng shāmén Zhànrán shù* 毗陵沙門湛然　述 ("composed by the *śramaṇa* Zhànrán of Pílíng [Yíxīng / Wújiāng]").

The text begins with a remarkable bibliographic note: 當卷八千八百八十八言 此品四千一百七十二言 ("the present *juan* has 8,888 characters; this [first] chapter has 4,172 characters") — a precise word-count attestation that demonstrates the high level of Tiāntái scholastic textual self-awareness in the mid-Táng productive period.

### Prefaces

The text opens with Zhànrán's own framing: "Now, about to explain this entire wonderful canonical text, twenty-eight chapters: there are many [other] schools' [interpretations]; for the moment, let us provisionally return [the analysis] to the Tiāntái lineage. For each chapter I will use three gates of explanation: first, relate the great purport of each chapter; second, explain the name of each chapter; third, explain the inner text of each chapter, in brief sectional analysis."

### Abstract

Zhànrán's *Dàyì* applies a tripartite framework to each of the twenty-eight chapters of the Lotus Sūtra: (1) the *dàyì* 大意 (great purport, summarising the chapter's overall doctrinal commitment in the Tiāntái system); (2) the *shìmíng* 釋名 (explanation of the chapter title); and (3) the *shìnèiwén* 釋內文 (explanation of the inner text, with brief sectional analysis). The tripartite framework is parallel to but more compact than the *wǔzhòng xuányì* of [[智顗]] Zhìyǐ's *Xuányì* and serves as Zhànrán's most accessible Tiāntái treatment of the Lotus.

The work is consequently of substantial pedagogical value as a Tiāntái student's introduction to the Lotus Sūtra and the commentarial tradition. It bridges the gap between Zhìyǐ's foundational treatises (the *Xuányì* and the *Wénjù*) and Zhànrán's own detailed subcommentaries (the *Shìqiān* and the *Wénjù jì*), providing the synoptic doctrinal framework needed to navigate the larger works productively.

The opening cosmological-doctrinal exposition demonstrates Zhànrán's literary ambition: "Originally, vast vast — the nature-ocean conjoins motion and stillness yet congeals as the real. Distant distant — the awakening-mountain embraces principle and matter yet propagates the teaching. The teaching-ocean is inexhaustible; therefore [it] strikes against the myriad potentials in order [to manifest]." This baroque parallel-prose register is characteristic of Zhànrán's mature literary style.

The composition is dated to Zhànrán's mature productive period c. 750–782; no precise date is recoverable. The work was incorporated into the Tiāntái scholastic curriculum as the standard introductory text and remained widely used through the medieval and early-modern periods.

## Other points of interest

The precise character-count attestation in the opening of the *Dàyì* — 8,888 characters for the entire *juan*, 4,172 for the first chapter — is one of the more notable instances of mid-Táng Buddhist scholastic textual self-awareness, paralleling the contemporary Confucian *jīngxué* 經學 tradition's careful word-counting of canonical works. The figure suggests a high degree of editorial control over the text's transmission, with Zhànrán himself or his disciples maintaining systematic textual measurements as part of the work's authorised production.
