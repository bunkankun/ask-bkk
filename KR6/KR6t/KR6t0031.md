# Huāyánzōng yàoyì 華嚴宗要義
*The Essential Meaning of the Kegon School*
by [[凝然]] (述)

## About the work

A single-fascicle **Kegon doctrinal summary** by **Gyōnen** 凝然 (1240–1321), the most prolific Japanese Buddhist scholastic of the Kamakura period, composed at **Tōdaiji Kaidan-in** 東大寺戒檀院 on **the 28th day of the 10th month of Shōwa 3 = 1314 CE** for the Kantō-region patron **Zenmon Denka** 禪門殿下 of **Kōmyō-ji** 光明寺. The work is organised as a *shí mén* 十門 (ten-gate) compendium of Kegon doctrine: (1) *jiàozōng dàyì* 教宗大意 (great meaning of the school's teaching); (2) *jiàozōng míngyì* 教宗名義 (name and meaning of the school's teaching); (3) *lìjiào kāizōng* 立教開宗 (establishment of the teaching and opening of the school); (4) *yīchéng sānchéng* 一乘三乘 (the One Vehicle and the Three Vehicles); (5) *suǒlì fǎyì* 所立法義 (the dharmas and meanings established); (6) *běnjīng cìdì* 本經次第 (the order of the foundational scriptures); (7) *xiūzhèng xíngxiāng* 修證行相 (cultivation, realisation, and practice-characteristics); (8) *zhū zá fǎmén* 諸雜法門 (various other doctrinal gates); (9) *zhāngshū fēnliàng* 章疏分量 (the proportions of the commentarial corpus); (10) *zǔ chéng hóngzhuàn* 祖承弘傳 (patriarchal transmission and propagation).

### Abstract

Authorship and dating: The terminal colophon is exceptionally informative: "*At the time: **Shōwa 3, *jiǎ-yín* (= 1314), 10th month 28th day**, at the Tōdaiji **Kaidan-in**, I composed this for the **Zenmon Denka** ("Lord of the Zen Gate"), patron of the Kantō **Kōmyō-ji**. The Zen-Gate Lord, in the leisure-time of his loyal-and-diligent service, has thoroughly mastered all schools — exoteric and esoteric integrated, dharma-nature and dharma-characteristic together practised — and has questioned me about the Kegon Dharma. We conversed [over] two or three chapter-rolls, and I have further composed this brief chapter as a respectful response to his earnest aspiration. The Kegon-school śramaṇa **Gyōnen**, in his 75th year.*"

The Zenmon Denka of Kōmyō-ji is plausibly **Hōjō Sadatoki** 北條貞時 (1271–1311) or his successor in the Kamakura *bakufu*'s Zen-patronage establishment — both maintained close ties with Kōmyō-ji in modern Kamakura. The 1314 date is exactly Gyōnen's 75th year (1240 + 74 = 1314). The composition is therefore tightly fixed: **notBefore = 1314, notAfter = 1314.**

A second colophon records the work's transmission: "*Bunpō 2 (= 1318), 5th month, [the work was] received face-to-face from its author's own residence. With great joy: meeting the great teaching is a rare karmic occasion. **Yuán-zōng** śramaṇa **Fuhai Sonri** 普海存理, [bearing] the wisdom of the Round School, in his 6th monastic summer.*" Fuhai Sonri (圓宗 = Tendai *yuán-zōng*) received the work from Gyōnen four years after composition, in the year of his sixth monastic standing.

A third colophon — with a problematic era-name (recorded as "唐永二年癸未四月十四日" = "*Tang-yǒng* 2, *guǐwèi*, 4th month, 14th day"; *Tang-yǒng* is not a Japanese era and the era-name is likely a corruption of either 應永 *Ōei* or the Chinese-style *Yányòu* — but the *guǐwèi* year-cycle suggests **1403 CE**) — records a final copying by **Kakujō** 覺城 at **Yamamoto-ji** 山本寺 in Gōshū (Ōmi province), "*for self-recitation and dissemination.*"

Doctrinal content: the *shí mén* compendium is one of the most accessible introductions to Kegon doctrine produced in medieval Japan. The treatment of gate 6 (the order of the foundational scriptures) is particularly distinctive — Gyōnen lays out the *Avataṃsaka*'s textual history from the 60-juan **Buddhabhadra** version (T278) through the 80-juan **Śikṣānanda** version (T279) to the 40-juan **Prajñā** version (T293), and discusses their relative authority. Gate 10 (patriarchal transmission) is the principal medieval-Japanese **Kegon-school lineage** statement, naming **Du-shùn** 杜順 → **Zhì-yǎn** 智儼 → **Fa-zang** 法藏 → **Chéng-guān** 澄觀 → **Zōng-mì** 宗密 in China, and the Japanese transmission through **Shinjō** 審詳, **Rōben** 良辨, and the Tōdaiji Kegon establishment.

## Translations and research

- *No complete Western-language translation located.* The standard Western treatment of Gyōnen's Kegon corpus is in **Frédéric Girard**, *Une référence: le 'Compendium des doctrines essentielles' de Gyōnen* (in **Mireille Boutry**, ed., *Les écoles bouddhiques au Japon: hommages à Bernard Frank*, EFEO 1994).
- **Mochizuki**, *Bukkyō daijiten*, s.v. *Gyōnen* 凝然 and *Kegon-shū yōgi* 華嚴宗要義.

## Other points of interest

The work is a unique witness to **Gyōnen's encyclopedic late-period scholarship** and to the **Kamakura *bakufu*'s Buddhist patronage**. The composition for a Kantō Zen-school lay patron testifies to the *bakufu*'s policy of cultivating doctrinal scholarship across school boundaries, and to Gyōnen's status as the recognised authoritative scholar of the school at Tōdaiji.
