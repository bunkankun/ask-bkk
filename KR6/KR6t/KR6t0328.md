# Dāngmá màntúluó gōngshì 當麻曼荼羅供式
*Liturgical Service for the Taima Mandala Offering*
by [[證空]] Shōkū (記)

## About the work

A **single-fascicle ritual service-text** (供式 *gushiki*) for the veneration of the **Taima Mandala** 當麻曼荼羅 — the famous large-scale woven Pure-Land mandala (depicting Amitābha's Western Paradise with all the architectural and symbolic features of the *Guānjīng*) housed at **Taima-dera** 當麻寺 in Yamato province (modern Nara prefecture). By [[證空]] **Shōkū**. The Taima Mandala was traditionally said to have been woven by the legendary princess-nun **Chūjō-hime** 中将姫 in 763 from lotus-fibre threads, and was rediscovered (or doctrinally re-canonized) as a Pure-Land iconographic centre in the early Kamakura period under Shōkū's direct sponsorship.

### Abstract

The text is a **liturgical handbook**, not a doctrinal treatise: it sets out, step by step, the ritual choreography for a formal *kuyō* (供養 service) before the Taima Mandala. The opening lines: "*First, the **dōjō** (ritual space) is solemnly adorned. The offerings are of a hundred flavours, or alternatively of forty-eight kinds [corresponding to Amitābha's 48 vows], with one person sent to sprinkle the water. Next, the assembly stands in line, the junior-rank monks first, processing in silence three circumambulations, each holding a *kebako* [flower-basket]*" (先道場莊嚴 ... 大衆列立。下臈爲先。無言行道三匝 ...).

The ritual proceeds through (i) the **四智讃** *shichi-san* (praise of the *four wisdoms*, sung at the start); (ii) the **如來唄** *Nyorai-bai* (Tathāgata-praise hymn); (iii) the **散華** *sange* (scattering of flowers); (iv) the **法則** *hossoku* (Dharma-rule recitation); (v) the **舍利讃** *sharisan* (relic-praise); and (vi) the **聲明** *shōmyō* musical recitations of the relevant Pure-Land sūtras (*Amida-kyō*, sections of the *Wǎngshēng lùn*). The closing rite is the **三禮** *sanrei* (three-fold reverence) and the *Eikō* (廻向 merit-transfer dedication).

**Significance.** The Taima Mandala — together with the *Chion'in raigō-zu* and the *Yamakoshi Amida* — is one of the three most important medieval Japanese Pure-Land iconographic monuments. Shōkū's authoring of the *kuyō*-shiki canonized the Mandala as the **Seizan-line iconographic centre** and made Taima-dera a Seizan pilgrimage site of the first rank. Shōkū's affiliation with the Taima Mandala is one of the most distinctive features of the Seizan tradition (versus the Chinzei, which had no comparable iconographic centre); through Shōkū's promotion the Taima Mandala became the standard Pure-Land iconographic model for medieval Japanese painting and was reproduced in countless *Taima mandara fukusha* (copies) by Edo-period painters.

**Date.** Shōkū's Sai-no-yama and Taima-dera period, c. 1230s; no internal precise date.

## Other points of interest

The Taima Mandala's foundation-legend — that the princess **Chūjō-hime** wove it from lotus-fibre threads at the request of Amitābha — is one of the most famous Japanese-Buddhist hagiographic narratives and gave rise to a substantial *Chūjō-hime* literary corpus including Noh, jōruri, and kabuki adaptations. Shōkū's *kuyō-shiki* is the principal liturgical document by which the Mandala's veneration was formalized in Pure-Land tradition.
