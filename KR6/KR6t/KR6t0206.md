# Sì juǎn 四卷
*The Four Fascicles*
by [[興然]] (撰)

## About the work

A **Shingon ritual encyclopedia** by **Kōnen** 興然 (1121–1203), the late-Heian Kōyasan master of the **Chū'in-ryū** 中院流 transmission line, bearing the unadorned title *Yon-kan* — "The Four Fascicles." The minimal title (literally just the work's physical length) is characteristic of Kōyasan ritual notebooks that were used as internal lineage-specific working references rather than as broadly-circulated treatises.

### Abstract

Authorship and dating: the work is universally ascribed to Kōnen in the Shingon catalogs; composition window **ca. 1130–1203**, within his mature scholarly career on Kōyasan.

Doctrinal content: the table of contents (here marked as "personally abbreviated"  私略) lists the principal **specialised** *bessen-hō*:
- *Dharma-Wheel-Turning* (轉法輪).
- *Buddhalocanā*; *Cakravartin*; *Mahā-Buddhoṣṇīṣa*.
- *Rāgarāja as-procedurally-performed*; *Rāgarāja two-headed*; *Defilement-Love-King two-headed*.
- *Five-Ākāśagarbha*; *Maitreya*.
- *Perfect-Round Vajra* (圓滿金剛).
- *Seven-Star Cintāmaṇi-cakra*.
- *Eight-syllable Mañjuśrī with dwelling-stabilization*; *Six-syllable Mañjuśrī*; *Five-syllable Mañjuśrī*; *One-syllable Mañjuśrī*.
- *Tai-mu* (太無).
- *Great Adamantine Wheel*.
- *Permission/Authorisation* (許可) — i.e. the formal license to perform the rite.

The opening entry on the **Dharma-Wheel-Turning rite for subjugation purposes** is annotated: "*This rite, in full, is just as in the small folded-paper [version]; here personally abbreviated*" (此法全如小折紙。私略之). A parallel transmission of the same rite according to the **Anjō-ji** 安祥寺 lineage is also noted: "*This rite is as given in the small folded-paper [version]; here personally abbreviated*" (同法安祥寺傳。此法如出小折紙。私略之). This dual-transmission documentation is characteristic of Kōnen's careful Chū'in-ryū scholarship.

The work is one of the principal Kōyasan ritual references of the late 12th century and a foundational document of the **Chū'in-ryū**, the major Kōyasan branch of Shingon that became the dominant lineage of Kongōbu-ji in the medieval period.
