# Jùzhī guàndǐng yíshì 具支灌頂儀式
*The Fully-Equipped Abhiṣeka Ritual Procedure*
by [[元杲]] (撰)

## About the work

A single-fascicle **abhiṣeka ritual procedural manual** by **Gangō** 元杲 (914–995), the great mid-Heian Shingon master of **Daigo-ji** 醍醐寺 and **Tōji** 東寺. The work prescribes the **fully-equipped** (*jù-zhī* 具支 — i.e. "with all retainers and appointments") variant of the *abhiṣeka* ritual: the most elaborate and ceremonially imposing of the Shingon ordination procedures, requiring a substantial retinue of *shokushū* 職衆 (assisting officiants), banners, palanquin, ritual implements, and procession music.

### Abstract

Authorship and dating: Gangō was a senior disciple of **Junnyū** 淳祐 (see [[KR6t0174]]) and rose to become **Tōji chōja** 東寺長者 (head of Tōji) and to hold **abbots' rank at Hannya-ji** 般若寺. The composition window must lie within his mature scholarly career, **ca. 950–995**. The text is transmitted through a remarkable manuscript chain documented in successive colophons: **Eikyū 5 (1117)** copying campaign by *Engmei-in* 延命院 Sōzu; further copyings at **Kanshu-ji** 勸修寺 by **Kanchū** 寛丨, **Nipei 4 (1154)** by **Chikai** 智海, **Ōhō 2 (1162)** transmission to *Shinjōbō* 眞乘房 ācārya of *Uchiyama* 内山, **Kangen 3 (1245)** by **Shōyo** 聖譽, **Meitoku 4 (1393)** by **Jisshin** 實信 (age 29), **Ōei 31 (1424)** by **Okutsugu** 奧繼, and **Bunmei 6 (1474)** by **Kōen** 杲園 (age 21). The work was thus continuously in active liturgical use from the late 10th century through at least the 15th.

Doctrinal content: the work opens with the prerequisite **samaya-precept reception** (先三摩耶戒), then prescribes a Tōji-specific procession from the *Great Horse Road* (大馬道) to the front of the abhiṣeka hall, "*the ritual at the hall-front being as for the ordinary kechien kanjō*." For abhiṣekas performed elsewhere, the procession is to be adjusted as locally convenient. The bulk of the text describes the **ācārya's-room South-facing ritual** (阿闍梨房南面儀式): the assembling *shokushū* gather in the front courtyard of the ācārya's room arranged left-and-right by seniority (room-side as the senior side, upper-rank elders to the front); the *nyōhachi* 鐃拔 cymbal-player has special precedence regardless of seniority because of his role as *zanshu* 讃首 (chant-leader); the ācārya sits south-facing; the *gakō* 五股 (five-pronged *vajra*) is carried by the ācārya himself, while the **vajra-bell** 金剛鈴 and other ritual implements are carried by the *jikongō shū* 持金剛衆 (vajra-bearers); the *kekei* (花莒) is carried by the *zanshu*. Then "*the two banner-bearers go before, and the palanquin-and-canopy procession begins, with the cymbals striking three rolls of the* shichi-zatsu *during which the ācārya enters the palanquin*"; "*the* gōkaishi *holding the censer stands before the palanquin, the banner-bearers flanking him left and right*"; the third roll of cymbals leads directly into the procession's first chord (*chū-dan* 初段) and the *shokushū* proceed on foot following the palanquin in silence until reaching the dais.

The text is one of the most detailed extant procedural manuals for mid-Heian imperial-grade Shingon ordination, and it embodies the institutional culmination of Kūkai's *abhiṣeka* tradition as actually practiced at Tōji in the late 10th century.
