# Sìfēn bǐqiūnī jièběn 四分比丘尼戒本
*The Four-Part Bhikṣuṇī-Prātimokṣa* (Dharmaguptaka)
by [[佛陀耶舍]] (Buddhayaśas, 譯)

## About the work

A one-fascicle extract of the Dharmaguptaka *bhikṣuṇī-prātimokṣa* (348 rules) from the parent *Sìfēn lǜ* ([[KR6k0009]]) by **Buddhayaśas** ([[佛陀耶舍]]) and his team at Cháng'ān, 410–412 CE. After the canonisation of the *Sìfēn lǜ* as the official East Asian ordination Vinaya in 709 CE, this text became **the** *prātimokṣa* recitation-text for nuns throughout China, Korea, Japan, and Vietnam — and remains so today.

### Prefaces

Translator's colophon: *姚秦罽賓三藏佛陀耶舍譯*. The 348-rule structure is: 8 *pārājika* + 17 *saṃghāvaśeṣa* + 30 *naiḥsargika* + 178 *pāyantika* + 8 *pratideśanīya* + 100 *śaikṣa* + 7 *adhikaraṇa-śamatha*.

### Abstract

The Dharmaguptaka *bhikṣuṇī-prātimokṣa* in this Chinese rendering is the **single most influential nuns' rule-set in the world**: the contemporary *bhikṣuṇī*-saṅghas of China, Korea, Vietnam, and the recently re-established lineages in Sri Lanka, Thailand, and the Tibetan tradition (where Dharmaguptaka ordination has been admitted to bridge gaps in the *Mūlasarvāstivāda bhikṣuṇī*-lineage that died out) all use rule-sets descended from this text. Heirman's three-volume study (2002) is the standard scholarly treatment. Comparative work (Heirman 2002, Hirakawa 1982) shows the Dharmaguptaka *bhikṣuṇī-prātimokṣa* to share substantive content with the parallel Mahāsāṃghika ([[KR6k0008]]), Mahīśāsaka ([[KR6k0004]]), and Sarvāstivāda ([[KR6k0018]]) lines, but with a higher rule-count and distinctive formulations in several *pāyantika* and *śaikṣa* rules.
