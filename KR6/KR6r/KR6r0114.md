# Cháoxiǎn chánjiào kǎo 朝鮮禪教考
*An Examination of the Chán and Doctrinal Schools in Korea*

compiled by [[朴永善]] (Pak Yŏngsŏn / Bú Yǒngshàn, fl. late 19th c., 輯)

## About the work

A 1-juan late-Joseon Korean Buddhist historiographical compendium, compiled by **Pak Yŏngsŏn** 朴永善 of **Hanyang** 漢陽 (Seoul). The work is a chronological-annal summary of the introduction and development of Chinese-derived Buddhism (both **Chán** 禪 and **doctrinal** 教 schools) in the Korean peninsula, from the legendary first arrival in the 4th century down to the Sòng-Korean transmission via **Yǒngmíng Yánshòu**'s 永明延壽 disciples in the 10th c.

The work is undated in itself; it was incorporated into the *Wànzì Xùzàngjīng* (X87 No. 1622) when that compendium was prepared in the early 20th century, fixing a *terminus ante quem* of ca. 1905. The dating bracket is **1850 – 1900** (mid-to-late 19th-century late-Joseon scholarship).

### Abstract

The work follows a strictly **chronological** organisation, with Korean events dated by Chinese reign-eras for cross-referencing:

1. **Initial transmission** (post-Hàn-Mĭng-dì 4th-c. CE): the work opens by noting that Buddhism reached China in **Yǒngpíng 8** 永平八年乙丑 (= 65 CE) and from there was transmitted to **Koguryŏ** 高勾麗 in the **Fú-Qín** 苻秦 era (壬申 = 372 CE), with the arrival of **Hye-yang** 惠亮 and other early monks.
2. **Three Kingdoms transmissions** — coverage runs through **Koguryŏ**, **Paekche** 百濟, and **Silla** 新羅, anchored to the *Sānguó shǐ* 三國史 of **Kim Pusik** 金富軾.
3. **Statal sponsorship** — coverage of major royal patronages, the founding of **Hŭngnyun-sa** 興輪寺 and other state monasteries, the *kuksa* 國師 (national-preceptor) institution.
4. **Tang-Silla transmissions** — coverage of major monks who travelled to Táng China for advanced study (e.g., **Tao-yi** 道義 and the early **Sŏn** 禪 transmitters; the **Sŏn / Kyo** 禪敎 distinction begins to crystallise).
5. **Sòng-Koryŏ transmission** — the closing section centres on the late-10th-c. transmission from **Yǒng-míng Yán-shòu** 永明延壽 (904–975) of **Hángzhōu Huì-rì-sì** 慧日寺. Per the work, the **Koryŏ Kwangjong** 光宗 king sent an embassy to Yán-shòu with golden kāṣāya, purple-jade prayer-beads, and a golden water-vessel; Yán-shòu received them and dispatched **36 of his disciples to Koryŏ**, who became the founders of the major Koryŏ Chán-school lineages. The compiler closes with the editorial conclusion: "**The Korean Chán-schools all derive from the Hángzhōu Huì-rì master.**"

The work is the principal **late-Joseon synthetic account** of the origins of Korean Chán-school Buddhism, written from a **Sino-centric** perspective that emphasises continuity with the Chinese parent tradition.

- Robert E. Buswell Jr., *The Zen Monastic Experience: Buddhist Practice in Contemporary Korea* (Princeton: Princeton University Press, 1992).
- 김영태 (Kim Yŏng-tae), *韓國佛教史* — the standard modern Korean-language history.

## Other points of interest

The work's editorial argument that Korean Chán "all derives from Yǒngmíng Yánshòu" reflects the **late-Joseon Sŏn-Kyo unification** project — the historiographical retrieval of Yánshòu as the canonical Sòng precedent for the integration of Chán with the doctrinal schools, which had been a project of major late-Joseon Buddhist intellectuals such as **Hyujŏng** 休靜 (Sǒsan, 1520–1604) and his successors. The compilation thus does double work: it documents Korean Buddhist history *and* reaffirms the ideological position that Chán and Kyo are not in competition.
