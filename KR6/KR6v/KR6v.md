### Scope and scholarly tradition

KR6v 藏外部類 (*Zàngwài bù lèi*) is the Kanripo division of **Buddhist texts not transmitted in any traditional version of the Chinese Buddhist canon** — Dūnhuáng manuscripts, stone-inscriptions, regional and ethnic-canon materials (Western Xia 西夏, the Dàlǐ 大理 / Dàcháng-hé 大長和 kingdoms of Yúnnán), modern translations into Chinese from Pāli, Sanskrit, and Tibetan, and a parallel corpus of modern scholarly studies on canon-formation. The numerical core of the cataloged subset (KR6v0002–KR6v0104) reproduces the first nine volumes of Fāng Guǎngchāng's 方廣錩 *Zàngwài fójiào wénxiàn* 藏外佛教文獻 (Beijing: Zōngjiào wénhuà / Renmin chubanshe, 1995–2003), CBETA series **W** (`W01n0001`–`W09n0077`). Unlike most other KR6 divisions this corpus has **no matching CANWWW entry**: the standard files cover only the Taishō main canon (T01–T85) and the *Zhōnghuá dàzàngjīng* 中華大藏經 (the H-series in `div99.xml`), neither of which contains the post-Taishō texts gathered here. KR6v is therefore the principal Kanripo location for materials that the Taishō editors of the 1920s did not, or could not, place into the canon — either because the manuscripts had not yet been edited (the Dūnhuáng Chán scroll containing [[KR6v0002]] / [[KR6v0003]] / [[KR6v0004]] was identified by Tanaka Ryōshō in 1983; the Beijing Library *Běixīn* 1254 + 1255 fragments by Fāng himself), because they had never circulated in the Chinese-character canon (the Pāli-to-Chinese renditions of [[KR6v0057]]–[[KR6v0060]] by [[鄧殿臣]] Dèng Diànchén; the Tibetan-to-Chinese renditions of [[KR6v0044]], [[KR6v0101]], [[KR6v0102]]), or because they are themselves modern scholarly essays attached to text-editions as introductions ([[KR6v0022]], [[KR6v0043]], [[KR6v0056]], [[KR6v0062]], [[KR6v0069]], [[KR6v0085]], [[KR6v0086]], [[KR6v0090]], [[KR6v0091]], [[KR6v0103]], [[KR6v0104]]). The chronological span of the historic texts collected runs from **c. 150–250 CE** (Nāgārjuna's [[KR6v0033]] *Pratītyasamutpāda-hṛdaya-kārikā* and its *vṛtti* [[KR6v0034]]) to **1867** (the Qīng-Tóngzhì *Èryào jīngāng hébì* [[KR6v0089]]); the modern scholarly stratum then carries the division forward to 2003. The uncataloged remainder of the meta file (KR6v0105–KR6v0204) corresponds to a second body of material — Yán Juānyīng 顏娟英's *Běicháo fójiào shíkè tàběn jīngxuǎn* 北朝佛教石刻拓本精選 (Northern-Dynasties Buddhist stone-rubbing selections) — and [[KR6v0299]] is the Kanripo placement of Ennin 圓仁's 入唐求法巡禮行記 (*Nittō guhō junrei kōki*); these have not yet been cataloged and lie outside the per-text evidence base of this overview.

The scholarly tradition behind KR6v is almost entirely a **late-twentieth-century philological project**, the principal architect of which is [[方廣錩]] Fāng Guǎngchāng (1948–), head of the Dūnhuáng manuscript division at the National Library of China and later of the Buddhist-studies institute at the Shanghai Academy of Social Sciences. The *Zàngwài fójiào wénxiàn* series — programmatically launched in 1995 — was conceived as the *editio princeps* venue for Buddhist texts that the existing canons (Taishō, Zhōnghuá, *Wàn xù-zàng* 卍續藏 / Xuzangjing) had not edited or had edited badly, with critical apparatus, original-script *yuányàng* 原樣 transcriptions, and substantial Chinese-language introductions on dating, provenance, and authenticity. Fāng's own corpus of methodological essays in the series ([[KR6v0022]] on the *Chánzàng* and Dūnhuáng Chán manuscripts, [[KR6v0043]] on new *Jin-zàng* 金藏 materials, [[KR6v0062]] on the canonization of the Tiāntái corpus, [[KR6v0085]] on the Western-Xià Fāngtǎ excavations, [[KR6v0090]] on Dūnhuáng monastery-canons, [[KR6v0091]] on Huìlín's *yīnyì* and the Táng canon, [[KR6v0104]] on the apocryphal-revelation cult of Sēng Fǎ 僧法) constitutes a coherent programme for understanding the social and physical history of the Chinese Buddhist textual heritage outside its standard editions. Subsidiary editor-scholars whose work shapes the division include [[李際寧]] Lǐ Jìníng (the *Móyé fūrén pǐn* recension cluster, [[KR6v0018]]–[[KR6v0021]], and Jin-canon studies), [[侯沖]] Hóu Chōng (Yúnnán Buddhist ritual: [[KR6v0066]], [[KR6v0067]], [[KR6v0069]], [[KR6v0074]], [[KR6v0075]], plus the Sòng ritual manuals [[KR6v0063]], [[KR6v0064]], [[KR6v0065]], [[KR6v0088]]), [[陳明光]] Chén Míngguāng (the Bǎodǐng-shān 寶頂山 / Dàzú 大足 cliff-inscription cluster [[KR6v0045]]–[[KR6v0051]]), [[達照]] Dázhào (the *Jīngāngjīng* devotional cluster [[KR6v0071]]–[[KR6v0073]] and [[KR6v0093]]–[[KR6v0100]]), [[徐紹強]] Xú Shàoqiáng and [[華方田]] Huá Fāngtián (the Yogācāra and Mādhyamika Dūnhuáng commentaries), [[黃霞]] Huáng Xiá (the *Guānfó sānmèihǎi jīng běnxíng pǐn* cluster [[KR6v0038]]–[[KR6v0042]]), [[張總]] Zhāng Zǒng (the *Dìzàng púsà shízhāirì* cluster [[KR6v0077]]–[[KR6v0084]]), [[侯旭東]] Hóu Xùdōng (the *Final-Charge-from-the-Golden-Coffin* pair [[KR6v0054]] / [[KR6v0055]]), [[陳金華]] Chén Jīnhuá (the principal English-language Shanwuwei attribution study [[KR6v0056]]), [[西本照真]] Nishimoto Shōshin (Sānjiè-jiào: [[KR6v0092]], [[KR6v0103]]), and the Japanese collaborators [[末木文美士]] Suè-Mòki Fumihito ([[KR6v0086]]), [[大內文雄]] Ōuchi Fumio, and [[齊藤隆信]] Saitō Takanobu ([[KR6v0076]]). The reader of KR6v must keep three overlapping evidentiary frames in mind: (a) the **Dūnhuáng manuscript culture** that preserved the bulk of the medieval items; (b) the **regional and ethnic Buddhisms** (Western Xia, Dàlǐ, Yúnnán Āzhālì 阿吒力, post-medieval esoteric lineages in Hèběi and the southwest) that preserved a second category of items outside the metropolitan canon-print tradition; and (c) the **modern scholarly act** of editing and contextualizing both, which is itself part of the textual record of the division.

### Important texts and text clusters

- **The Zōngmì *Chánzàng* 禪藏 Dūnhuáng-fragment cluster.** Three short Chán treatises preserved together on a single scroll (P.2039 + Beijing Library *Běixīn* 1254 + 1255) and identified by Fāng Guǎngchāng as part of [[宗密]] Guīfēng Zōngmì's (780–841) lost *Chánzàng* anthology transmitted to Dūnhuáng: [[KR6v0002]] *Tiānzhúguó Pútídámó chánshī lùn* 天竺國菩提達摩禪師論 (with parallel-edited witnesses [[KR6v0024]] from P.2039 and [[KR6v0025]] from Beijing), [[KR6v0003]] *Chán cè wèndá* 禪策問答, and [[KR6v0004]] *Xī zhēng lùn* 息諍論. [[KR6v0023]] *Qīzǔ fǎbǎo jì xià juǎn* 七祖法寶記下卷 is the principal Dūnhuáng witness for the lost Northern-Chán "seven-patriarch" lineage record, and Fāng's modern essay [[KR6v0022]] is the methodological introduction to the cluster.
- **The Móyé / *Buddha's Mother* recension cluster.** [[KR6v0018]] through [[KR6v0021]] preserve four Dūnhuáng recensions of an apocryphal "Chapter on Lady Māyā" appended to the Chinese *Mahāparinirvāṇa-sūtra* (T374 / T375), edited side-by-side by [[李際寧]] Lǐ Jìníng to display the textual evolution of a popular eulogistic appendix that never entered the metropolitan canon.
- **The *Tiāngōng jīng* 天公經 recension cluster.** [[KR6v0015]] / [[KR6v0016]] / [[KR6v0017]] preserve three Dūnhuáng recensions of the *Sūtra of the Lord of Heaven*, a Suí–late-Táng apocryphon. The cluster overlaps directly with KR6u's [[KR6u0012]]; the KR6v editorial principle is to publish all manuscript witnesses, where the Taishō-rooted KR6u presents only the editorially-reconstructed text.
- **The *Guānfó sānmèihǎi jīng běnxíng pǐn* 觀佛三昧海經本行品 recension cluster (W03n0031 a–e).** [[KR6v0038]] through [[KR6v0042]] preserve five Dūnhuáng witnesses of a "Chapter Eight" — the *běnxíng pǐn* 本行品 (Original-Practice chapter) — circulating both as an excerpt of the canonical *Guānfó sānmèihǎi jīng* 觀佛三昧海經 (T15n0643) and under the alternative title *Fó shuō xiānghǎo jīng* 佛說相好經, edited by [[黃霞]] Huáng Xiá. The cluster is a paradigmatic case of canonical chapter circulating as an autonomous "scripture" in Dūnhuáng book-religion.
- **The *Dìzàng púsà shízhāirì* 地藏菩薩十齋日 cluster (W07n0064 a–h).** [[KR6v0077]] through [[KR6v0084]] are eight Dūnhuáng and post-Dūnhuáng recensions of the *Ten Fast-Days of the Bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha* — including the Dàzú stone-engraving witness [[KR6v0084]] — edited by [[張總]] Zhāng Zǒng. The text is the principal scriptural source for the lay-Buddhist monthly-fast calendar of medieval and post-medieval China.
- **The Buddha's-Final-Charge cluster.** [[KR6v0054]] *Fó zài jīnguān shàng zhǔlěi jīng* 佛在金棺上囑累經 and [[KR6v0055]] *Rúlái zài jīnguān zhǔlěi qīngjìng zhuāngyán jìngfú jīng* 如來在金棺囑累清淨莊嚴敬福經, edited by [[侯旭東]] Hóu Xùdōng, are the principal Northern-dynasties to early-Táng witnesses of the genre of "deathbed-injunction" apocrypha; they have evident overlap with [[KR6u0013]] in the apocrypha division.
- **The *Jìngmíng jīng* / Vimalakīrti commentary of [[道液]] Dàoyè.** [[KR6v0026]] *Jìngmíng jīng jíjiě Guānzhōng shū juǎn shàng* and [[KR6v0032]] *... juǎn xià* — together a complete Táng compendium-commentary on Kumārajīva's *Vimalakīrti-nirdeśa* (T14n0475), of c. 760–765, edited by [[黎明]] Lí Míng from Dūnhuáng witnesses.
- **The Sānjiè-jiào 三階教 dossier.** [[KR6v0053]] *Dàshèng wújìnzàng fǎ* 大乘無盡藏法 and [[KR6v0092]] *Fóxìng guān xiūshàn fǎ* 佛性觀修善法 are two key doctrinal works of [[信行]] Xìnxíng (540–594), the Suí founder of the Sānjiè 三階 movement, recovered from Dūnhuáng; [[KR6v0103]] *Sānjiējiào wénxiàn zōngshù* 三階教文獻綜述 is [[西本照真]] Nishimoto Shōshin's comprehensive 2003 survey of the entire surviving Sānjiè documentary record. Together with the parallel Sānjiè material in KR6u (especially [[KR6u0006]]), the cluster is the single largest concentration of primary Sānjiè-jiào evidence in any printed canonical edition.
- **The Nāgārjuna *Pratītyasamutpāda-hṛdaya* triad.** [[KR6v0033]] *Yīnyuán xīn lùn sòng* 因緣心論頌 (the Sanskrit *kārikā* in Chinese rendition), [[KR6v0034]] *Yīnyuán xīn lùn shì* 因緣心論釋 (the *vṛtti*), and [[KR6v0035]] *Yīnyuán xīn shìlùn kāijué jì* 因緣心釋論開決記 (a Tang Dūnhuáng sub-commentary) preserve a short [[龍樹]] Nāgārjuna verse-text and its layered commentaries, otherwise lost in the Chinese transmission.
- **The Tang Dūnhuáng commentaries on Indic śāstras.** [[KR6v0008]] *Ēpídámó jùshèlùn shíyì shū* 阿毗達磨俱舍論實義疏 is the Dūnhuáng witness of [[安慧]] Sthiramati's commentary on Vasubandhu's *Abhidharmakośa-bhāṣya* (T29n1558), one of the most consequential Yogācāra philological recoveries from the cave library. [[KR6v0006]] *Tiān qǐngwèn jīng shū* 天請問經疏 (by [[文軌]] Wénguǐ, c. 648–650) is a near-contemporary commentary on Xuánzàng's translation of the *Deva-paripṛcchā-sūtra* (T15n0592). [[KR6v0029]] [[韓清淨]] Hán Qīngjìng's 1949 *Yújiā shī dì lùn pī xún jì xù* and [[KR6v0061]] *Yúqiéshīdì kāishì fēnmén jì* (a Tang sub-commentary on Xuánzàng's *Yogācārabhūmi* T30n1579) document the Dūnhuáng-to-Republican-era continuity of the Chinese Yogācāra commentarial enterprise.
- **The Bǎodǐng-shān / Dàzú 大足寶頂山 stone-engraving cluster.** [[KR6v0045]] *Dàzàng Fó shuō shǒuhù dàqiān guótǔ jīng*, [[KR6v0046]] *Liùdào lúnhuí tú jìsòng*, [[KR6v0047]] *Fùmǔ ēn zhòng jīngbiàn jīngwén jìsòng*, [[KR6v0048]] *Dà fāngbiàn Fó bào'ēn jīngbiàn jīngwén jìsòng*, [[KR6v0050]] *Guānjīng biànxiàng jīngwén jìsòng*, [[KR6v0051]] *Dìyùbiàn jīngwén jìsòng* — six Southern-Sòng inscription-verse texts recovered from the Bǎodǐng-shān cliff-grottoes (Dàzú, Sichuan), edited by [[陳明光]] Chén Míngguāng. The cluster is the most important single body of inscription-witness Buddhist literature for the Southern Sòng popular cult.
- **The Yúnnán Āzhālì-jiào 阿吒力教 / Dàlǐ Kingdom esoteric ritual cluster.** [[KR6v0066]] *Guǎngshī wúzhē dàochǎng yí*, [[KR6v0067]] *Dàhēitiān shén dàochǎng yí*, [[KR6v0074]] *Hùguó sīnán chāo* 護國司南抄 (a 908 commentary by 玄鑒 Xuánjiàn of the Dàcháng-hé 大長和 kingdom on Liángbì's *Rénwáng hùguó bōrě bōluómìduō jīng shū*), and [[KR6v0075]] *Yàoshī guàndǐng zhāngjù yí* by 若愚 Ruòyú — the principal medieval-to-Míng witnesses of esoteric Buddhism transmitted in the Bái 白 kingdom regions of Yúnnán; [[KR6v0069]] is [[侯沖]] Hóu Chōng's 1998 programmatic essay on the historiographical value of this body of evidence.
- **The Liáng-cháo Fù Dàshì 傅大士 *Jīngāngjīng* praise-verses cluster (W09n0073 a–h).** [[KR6v0093]] through [[KR6v0100]] preserve eight recensions of the *Jīngāngjīng zàn jí* / *Liángcháo Fù dàshì sòng Jīngāngjīng* corpus of Dūnhuáng-to-Sòng *Vajracchedikā* devotional verse, edited by [[達照]] Dázhào; with the related earlier Vajracchedikā liturgy [[KR6v0071]] / [[KR6v0072]] (姚和尚's *Jīngāng wǔlǐ* 金剛五禮) and the Liáo 遼-period [[KR6v0073]] (通理's *Jīngānglǐ*), the *Vajracchedikā* devotional tradition is the single best-represented sub-corpus of the division.
- **The Pāli-canon Chinese translations of [[鄧殿臣]] Dèng Diànchén.** [[KR6v0057]] *Xiǎosòng* 小誦 (*Khuddakapāṭha*), [[KR6v0058]] *Jíxìng zìshuō* 即興自說 (*Udāna*), [[KR6v0059]] *Dàsuìdào běnshēng* 大隧道本生 (*Mahā-Ummagga Jātaka*), [[KR6v0060]] *Dà niànchù jīng* 大念處經 (*Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna-sutta*) — the principal late-twentieth-century Chinese-language renditions of *Khuddaka-nikāya* and *Dīgha-nikāya* materials, distributed in CBETA under the ZB rubric because they fall outside both the *Āgama* tradition canonized in the Taishō and the *Hànyì Nánchuán dàzàngjīng* 漢譯南傳大藏經 project.
- **The Tibetan-to-Chinese modern Mahāyāna translations.** [[KR6v0044]] *Rù púsà xíng lùn guǎngjiě* 入菩薩行論廣解 ([[寂天]] Śāntideva's *Bodhicaryāvatāra* with rGyal-tshab [[傑操]]'s commentary, translated by [[隆蓮]] Lónglián), [[KR6v0028]] *Dìyì zhèngdé jīng* 諦義證得經 (Umāsvāti's *Tattvārthādhigama-sūtra*, the foundational Jain śāstra — an "outside-Buddhism" outlier rendered into Chinese by Fāng Guǎngchāng himself), and the prose-and-verse pair [[KR6v0101]] / [[KR6v0102]] *Bǎojì běnyuán* 寶髻本緣 (the *Maṇicūḍa-avadāna* in long prose and Kṣemendra's verse recension, translated by 任遠 Rén Yuǎn) widen the division's reach beyond the Chinese transmission.
- **The Western-Xià Hàn-Chinese Buddhist corpus.** [[KR6v0005]] *Bā zhǒng cūzhòng fànduò* 八種粗重犯墮 (a Tantric Vinaya tract attributed to Aśvaghoṣa [[馬鳴菩薩]], recovered from a Western-Xià Tangut-era Hàn-Chinese manuscript) and [[KR6v0085]] [[方廣錩]]'s descriptive catalogue of the Hàn-Chinese Buddhist texts recovered from the Níngxià Xī Xià Fāngtǎ 西夏方塔 excavation document a Tangut-era Hàn-Chinese Buddhist textual stratum mostly invisible in the standard canon.
- **The Sòng-Yuán ritual-manual cluster.** [[KR6v0063]] *Léngyán jiěyuān shìjié dàochǎng yí* 楞嚴解冤釋結道場儀 ([[釋祖照]] Shì Zǔzhào, Northern Sòng 1111–1118), [[KR6v0064]] *Dìzàng cíbēi jiùkǔ jiànfú lìshēng dàochǎng yí* (Yuánzhào, Sòng), [[KR6v0065]] *Xiāoshì Jīngāngjīng kēyí* 銷釋金剛經科儀 ([[釋宗鏡]], Southern Sòng), and [[KR6v0088]] *Rúlái guǎngxiào shízhǒng bàoēn dàochǎng yí* (思覺, Southern Sòng) edited by 趙文煥 and [[侯沖]] — a coherent body of Sòng *dàochǎng yí* 道場儀 ("sacred-assembly ritual manuals") for repentance, suffering-rescue, and *Vajracchedikā*-recitation, never transmitted in the metropolitan canon but of central importance for the social history of Sòng monastic ritual.

### Important persons

The persons that shape the division fall into three groups: (a) authors of the historic texts; (b) modern editor-translators; and (c) one cohort of contemporary scholar-essayists whose programmatic articles are themselves *texts of the division*.

- [[方廣錩]] — Fāng Guǎngchāng (1948–), the principal editor of the *Zàngwài fójiào wénxiàn* series and author of the methodological-essay stratum of KR6v ([[KR6v0022]], [[KR6v0043]], [[KR6v0062]], [[KR6v0085]], [[KR6v0090]], [[KR6v0091]], [[KR6v0104]]); his identifications of the Beijing-Library Chán-scroll fragments and his sociology of the Dūnhuáng monastery-canons set the agenda of the division.
- [[李際寧]] — Lǐ Jìníng, second-rank editor of the series, responsible for the Móyé *Buddha's Mother* recension cluster ([[KR6v0018]]–[[KR6v0021]]) and the [[KR6v0043]] essay on new *Jīnzàng* 金藏 materials.
- [[侯沖]] — Hóu Chōng, the principal modern authority on Yúnnán Āzhālì-jiào esoteric Buddhism and editor of the Sòng and Dàlǐ ritual-manual cluster ([[KR6v0066]], [[KR6v0067]], [[KR6v0074]], [[KR6v0075]]; collaboratively [[KR6v0063]], [[KR6v0064]], [[KR6v0088]]; programmatic essay [[KR6v0069]]).
- [[陳明光]] — Chén Míngguāng, editor of the Southern-Sòng Bǎodǐng-shān cliff-inscription cluster ([[KR6v0045]]–[[KR6v0051]]).
- [[達照]] — Dázhào, editor of the *Vajracchedikā* devotional cluster ([[KR6v0071]]–[[KR6v0073]], [[KR6v0093]]–[[KR6v0100]]).
- [[徐紹強]] — Xú Shàoqiáng, editor of the Dūnhuáng Yogācāra commentarial materials ([[KR6v0014]], [[KR6v0037]], [[KR6v0061]]).
- [[華方田]] — Huá Fāngtián, editor of the Nāgārjuna *Pratītyasamutpāda* triad ([[KR6v0033]]–[[KR6v0035]]) and the Vajracchedikā commentary [[KR6v0036]].
- [[黃霞]] — Huáng Xiá, editor of the *Guānfó sānmèihǎi jīng běnxíng pǐn* recension cluster ([[KR6v0038]]–[[KR6v0042]]).
- [[張總]] — Zhāng Zǒng, editor of the *Dìzàng púsà shízhāirì* cluster ([[KR6v0077]]–[[KR6v0084]]).
- [[侯旭東]] — Hóu Xùdōng, editor of the Final-Charge-from-the-Golden-Coffin pair ([[KR6v0054]], [[KR6v0055]]).
- [[西本照真]] — Nishimoto Shōshin, the principal modern authority on Sānjiè-jiào and editor of [[KR6v0092]] and author of [[KR6v0103]].
- [[陳金華]] — Chén Jīnhuá (Chen Jinhua), author of [[KR6v0056]], the principal study of the Śubhakarasiṃha-attribution problem within the division.
- [[鄧殿臣]] — Dèng Diànchén (1937–2002), the late-twentieth-century Chinese Pāli translator responsible for the *Khuddaka-nikāya* / *Dīgha-nikāya* renditions ([[KR6v0057]]–[[KR6v0060]]) collected here.
- [[末木文美士]] — Suè-Mòki Fumihito, author of [[KR6v0086]] on the formation of Japanese Buddhist bibliography centered on the *Tōiki dentō mokuroku*.
- [[宗密]] — Guīfēng Zōngmì (780–841), Tang Chán-Huáyán master whose lost *Chánzàng* anthology is the inferred original collection from which the [[KR6v0002]] / [[KR6v0003]] / [[KR6v0004]] cluster derives; also direct author of [[KR6v0070]] *Zhù Xīnyào fǎmén*.
- [[信行]] — Xìnxíng (540–594), founder of the Sānjiè 三階 movement; author of [[KR6v0053]] and [[KR6v0092]].
- [[道宣]] — Dàoxuān (596–667), Tang Vinaya master and author of *Sìfēn lǜ shānfán bǔquē xíngshì chāo* (T1804); his Dūnhuáng-witness *fēnmén túlù* outline is preserved as [[KR6v0007]].
- [[道液]] — Dàoyè, mid-eighth-century Tang Vimalakīrti commentator; author of the two-fascicle [[KR6v0026]] / [[KR6v0032]] *Guānzhōng* compendium-commentary.
- [[文軌]] — Wénguǐ, mid-seventh-century Tang Hossō-affiliated commentator; author of [[KR6v0006]].
- [[安慧]] — Sthiramati (c. 510–570), the principal South-Indian Yogācāra commentator on the *Abhidharmakośa*; author of the [[KR6v0008]] *jùshèlùn shíyì shū*.

### Topics

- **The *Zàngwài fójiào wénxiàn* editorial programme.** The constitutive scholarly act of KR6v is the editorial decision to publish, with critical apparatus and *yuányàng* 原樣 (original-form) transcription, every surviving manuscript witness of texts that did not enter the metropolitan canon, including parallel witnesses of the same work edited side-by-side. This produces the conspicuous "recension-cluster" structure of much of the division (the Móyé cluster, the *Tiāngōng jīng* cluster, the *Guānfó sānmèihǎi jīng běnxíng pǐn* cluster, the *Dìzàng púsà shízhāirì* cluster, the Fù Dàshì *Jīngāngjīng* cluster). Fāng's introductions are the principal methodological statement of the programme.
- **The recovered Chán-Dūnhuáng corpus and Zōngmì's *Chánzàng*.** KR6v is the principal canon-edition location for the textual fall-out from Tanaka Ryōshō and Fāng Guǎngchāng's work on the Dūnhuáng Chán manuscripts and the inferred reconstruction of [[宗密]] Zōngmì's lost *Chánzàng* anthology — alongside the better-known [[KR6u0022]] *Tóutuó jīng* (which is itself paralleled by the recension [[KR6v0009]]).
- **Sānjiè-jiào 三階教 as a primary-evidence dossier.** The Suí Sānjiè movement is in modern reception almost entirely a Dūnhuáng-recovery phenomenon: the foundational scriptures and treatises are now available, in critical edition, in the combined corpus of KR6u and KR6v ([[KR6v0053]], [[KR6v0092]], [[KR6v0103]]); together with [[KR6u0006]] this is the largest single-canon Sānjiè-jiào dossier in print.
- **Regional and ethnic Buddhisms outside the metropolitan canon.** The Yúnnán Āzhālì-jiào cluster ([[KR6v0066]], [[KR6v0067]], [[KR6v0069]], [[KR6v0074]], [[KR6v0075]]), the Western-Xià Hàn-Chinese stratum ([[KR6v0005]], [[KR6v0085]]), the Liáo-dynasty *Vajracchedikā* liturgy [[KR6v0073]], and the Sòng-Yuán *dàochǎng yí* ritual manuals together constitute the strongest single block of evidence in any canonical edition for Buddhism practised outside the central Chinese metropolitan tradition.
- **Stone-engraving as text-transmission.** The Bǎodǐng-shān / Dàzú cliff-inscription cluster ([[KR6v0045]]–[[KR6v0051]]), the *Dìzàng púsà shízhāirì* Dàzú witness [[KR6v0084]], and the Western-Xià Fāngtǎ excavated materials [[KR6v0085]] illustrate the principle — central to Fāng Guǎngchāng's methodology — that the textual record of medieval Chinese Buddhism cannot be reconstructed from manuscript / print evidence alone.
- **Pāli and Tibetan translation into Chinese as a twentieth-century project.** The Dèng Diànchén Pāli renditions and the Lónglián / Rén Yuǎn / Fāng Guǎngchāng Sanskrit-and-Tibetan renditions document the late-twentieth-century PRC philological project of completing the Chinese Buddhist canon with materials from the southern (Theravāda) and northern (Indo-Tibetan) traditions. The Jain outlier [[KR6v0028]] *Tattvārthādhigama-sūtra* extends this principle beyond Buddhism into broader Indic religious philology.
- **Apocrypha as a continuous Chinese tradition from the Suí to the Qīng.** Where KR6u truncates at the late Tang, KR6v carries the apocrypha tradition forward through the Sòng (the Bǎodǐng inscriptions, the Sòng ritual manuals), Yuán (the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* commentary [[KR6v0044]]), Míng (the [[KR6v0075]] *Yàoshī guàndǐng zhāngjù yí*), and into the Qīng ([[KR6v0089]] 1867 *Èryào jīngāng hébì*). The apparent break between the Tang and Sòng apocrypha-corpora is editorial, not historical.
- **Modern Buddhology as part of the canon-record.** A unique feature of the *Zàngwài fójiào wénxiàn* programme — and therefore of KR6v — is the inclusion of contemporary scholarly articles ([[KR6v0022]], [[KR6v0043]], [[KR6v0056]], [[KR6v0062]], [[KR6v0069]], [[KR6v0085]], [[KR6v0086]], [[KR6v0090]], [[KR6v0091]], [[KR6v0103]], [[KR6v0104]]) as full numbered entries on a par with edited primary texts. Reading these alongside the edited witnesses gives the division a unique meta-historical layer: it is simultaneously a text-edition and a record of how those texts are being edited.
