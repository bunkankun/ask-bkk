# Juéàn Kěxiāng chánshī yǔlù 絕岸可湘禪師語錄

*Sayings-Record of Chán Master Juéàn Kěxiāng* — a one-juan late-Sòng / early-Yuán *yǔlù* of **Juéàn Kěxiāng** [[可湘]] 絕岸可湘 (1206 – Zhìyuán 27 / 1290, *shì* 85), principal dharma-heir of [[師範]] Wújùn Shīfàn in the Pòān line. Compiled by six *sìfǎ ménrén* 嗣法門人 led by the principal heir [[妙恩]] Duànyá Miàoēn, then abbot of the Kāiyuán chánsì at Quánzhōu.

## About the work

One-juan *yǔlù* in *Xuzangjing* X70 n1384. Eight successive abbacy records: Jiāxīngfǔ Liúhóng Xīngshèng 流虹興聖 (entered Bǎoyòu 1.10.28 = 20 November 1253), Wēnzhōu Yànshān Néngrén 鴈山能仁, Yuèzhōu Jiǔyán Huìyún 九巖慧雲, Tiāntái Hùguó Guǎngēn 護國廣恩, Lín'ānfǔ Chóngēn Yǎnfú 崇恩演福, Wēnzhōu Jiāngxīn Lóngxiáng Xīngqìng 江心龍翔興慶, Fúzhōu Xuěfēng Chóngshèng 雪峯崇聖 (from Xiánchún 8 / 1272 for ten years), and a *zàizhù Xuěfēng* 再住雪峯 record. Closing with *niāngǔ*, *sònggǔ*, *fǎyǔ*, *zàn*, *sòng*, *xiǎo fóshì*, and a *bá*. The byline of the opening juan gives "住泉州開元禪寺嗣法門人 妙恩 等編" — principal editor being Miàoēn while he was abbot of the Kāiyuánsì at Quánzhōu.

### Abstract

Per DILA A003948 and the *xù* tradition: Kěxiāng (*hào* Juéàn 絕岸, also Xuěfēng Xiāng 雪峯湘), surname Gě 葛, native of Nínghǎi 寧海 in Táizhōu. He received decisive dharma-transmission from Wújùn Shīfàn at Jìngshān and subsequently held the eight abbacies listed above, his tenure at Xuěfēng Chóngshèng in Fúzhōu being the longest and the one that gave him the alternate style Xuěfēng Xiāng. After a decade at Xuěfēng he retired to Bǎoshòusì at Hángzhōu, dying in Zhìyuán 27 (1290). His dharma-heirs included Yúnsǒu Cóng 雲叟從 and Duànyá Miàoēn 斷崖妙恩 — the latter, abbot of Quánzhōu Kāiyuánsì and himself posthumously honored as "Guǎngmíng Tōnghuì Pǔjì chánshī" 廣明通慧普濟禪師, being the principal compiler of this *yǔlù*. The *yǔlù* was therefore compiled posthumously, c. 1290–1300, at Quánzhōu.

## Translations and research

*No substantial secondary literature located* in English. Kě-xiāng is treated in passing in Japanese surveys of Sòng-Yuán Línjì (Ishii Shūdō), and in Chinese studies of the Fújiàn Chán tradition around Xuě-fēng.
