# Páng jūshì yǔlù 龐居士語錄

*Sayings-Record of Layman Páng* — a three-juan Táng *yǔlù* of the celebrated lay Chán master [[龐蘊|Páng Yùn]] 龐蘊 (*hào* Páng jūshì 龐居士, c. 740–808) — one of the most beloved figures of classical Chán literature and a paradigm of the serious lay practitioner. Compiled by [[于頔|Yú Dí]] 于頔, the Táng scholar-official who was Páng Yùn's patron and interlocutor.

## About the work

A three-juan Táng-era Chán lay-master's *yǔlù*, X69 n1336. Non-commentary; *commentedTextid* omitted.

[[龐蘊|Páng Yùn]] 龐蘊 (*hào* Dàoxuán 道玄; c. 740–808): the archetypal Chinese Chán lay practitioner. Reportedly cast his entire family wealth into the river to free himself of attachment, then lived a family life of manual labour (weaving bamboo baskets) while sustaining serious Chán practice with his wife and his daughter [[靈照|Língzhào]] 靈照. Páng trained under [[希遷|Shítóu Xīqiān]] 石頭希遷, then with [[道一|Mǎzǔ Dàoyī]] 馬祖道一 (see [[KR6q0267]]). His deathbed *gāozuò* 高座 encounter with his daughter Língzhào has been one of the most-cited Chán stories throughout the tradition.

Compiler [[于頔|Yú Dí]] 于頔: Táng scholar-official; lay patron of Páng Yùn. Lifedates approximately contemporary with Páng.

The Páng Yùn corpus is a foundational source for the ideal of serious lay Chán practice that has continued through the tradition — directly invoked by later lay-*yǔlù* such as [[徐昌治|Xú Chāngzhì]]'s *Wúyī dàorén lù* [[KR6q0192]] in the late Míng.

Dating: *notBefore* c. 790 (Páng's mature teaching-years); *notAfter* c. 830 (Yú Dí's compilation-horizon in the early 9th century).
