# Xuějiāo Xìn chánshī yǔlù 雪嶠信禪師語錄
*Recorded Sayings of Chán Master Xuě-jiāo Xìn*
by [[弘歇]] (等編)

Ten-juan *yǔlù* of **Xuějiāo Yuánxìn** [[圓信]] 雪嶠圓信 (**1571 – 1647-09-24**, age 77). One of the **three principal *fǎsì*** of **Lóngchí Huànyǒu Zhèngchuán** [[正傳]] (cf. [[KR6q0601]]) — alongside [[圓悟]] **Mìyún Yuánwù** and [[圓修]] **Tiānyǐn Yuánxiū**. *Biéhào* **Yǔfēng lǎorén** 語風老人, **Qīngshīwēng** 青獅翁 ("Old Man of the Blue Lion"), **Xuěshīzǐ** 雪獅子 ("Snow Lion"), lay name **Zhū** 朱 (i.e. **Zhū Xuětíng** 朱雪庭). Native of **Yínxiàn** 鄞縣 (Níngbō, Zhèjiāng). Compiled by *fǎsì* **Hóngxiē** [[弘歇]] (with **Hóngzhū** 弘珠) *děng biān*.

The *yǔlù* documents Yuánxìn's three principal abbacies and his late-life teaching. *notBefore* ≈ **1610** (post-mature-master period under Huànyǒu); *notAfter* = **1647** (death). Catalog source-edition is the **Qiánlóng Edition** of the canon Vol. 153 No. 1638.

**Contents.** Juan 1: preface (by **Yú Yī** 魚一); *zhòng hùfǎ qǐng kāitáng shū* 眾護法請開堂疏; *mùlù*; *Dōngtǎ chánsì yǔlù* 住東塔禪寺語錄; *Yuèzhōu Yúnménsì yǔlù* 越州雲門寺語錄. Juan 2: *Kāixiān chánsì yǔlù*; *pǔshuō*. Juan 3: *xiǎocān*; *wǎncān*; *cháhuà*; *shìzhòng*; *kāishì*. Juan 4: *fǎyǔ* (示錢相國塞菴居士, 示錢元冲居士, etc.). Juan 5–10: continuing materials, *jìzàn*, *fóshì*, *xíngshí*. The frequent *yīnshì* 音釋 (phonetic glosses) at chapter ends indicates careful textual treatment for canonical inclusion.

## Other points of interest

Xuějiāo Yuánxìn is one of the most distinctive Línjì-Yángqí Chán figures of the late Míng — known for his unconventional, eccentric self-presentation (the **Xuěshīzǐ** "Snow Lion" persona), his *Qīngshīwēng* "Blue Lion Old Man" sobriquet, and his prominent role as teacher of **Tuìān Duànyú Zhì** [[智|Tuìān Duànyú Zhì]] (cf. [[KR6q0455]]). His Yúnménsì abbacy at Shàoxìng (越州) places him at one of the most prestigious Chán mountain-temples; his Kāixiānsì 開先 abbacy at Lúshān adds Jiāngxī to his circulation. Together with [[KR6q0601]] (his teacher Huànyǒu) and [[KR6q0603]] / [[KR6q0604]] (his co-disciples Tiānyǐn / Mìyún), [[KR6q0602]] forms the core textual evidence for the late-Míng Línjì-Yángqí lineage-restoration.
