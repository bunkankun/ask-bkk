# Wèilín Dàopèi chánshī huánshān lù 為霖道霈禪師還山錄

Four-juan late-career *yǔlù* of **Wèilín Dàopèi** [[道霈]] 為霖道霈 (1615–1702), titled *Huánshān lù* 還山錄 ("Record of Returning to the Mountain") — marking Dàopèi's late-life return to Gǔshān after itinerant teaching in Fújiàn. *Xuzangjing* X72 no. 1440. Recorded (*lù* 錄) by four of his attendants: [[興燈]] Xīngdēng, [[心亮]] Xīnliàng, [[法雲]] Fǎyún, and [[性朗]] Xìnglǎng — each marked as *shìzhě* 侍者 in the editorial byline.

### Abstract

The text is bracketed by two prefaces and closed with a postface, which between them record its dual Chinese-Japanese publication history. The body carries a self-preface (*fēijiāsǒu* 非家叟 = Dàopèi) dated the *dēngjié* 燈節 of Kāngxī 27 / 1688; the four-juan core was thus finalised in early 1688 at Gǔshān. The Xuzangjing printing, however, preserves Japanese prefatorial material: an opening *xù* 序 by **Dúān Xuánguāng** 獨菴玄光 (Dokuan Genkō, 1630–1698; a Japanese Rinzai monk of Edo with Ōbaku connections), dated 元祿癸酉十月二日 (31 October 1693); and a closing *hòuxù* 後序 by **Jūzān** 壽山祝 (a Chinese-born Sōtō monk then resident at Sa-shū Gyokuryū-san Fuku-shō-sen-ji 薩州玉龍山福昌禪寺 in Kyūshū, formerly a personal disciple of Dàopèi's in Fújiàn), dated 元祿癸酉九月廿八日 (27 October 1693). Dokuan's preface narrates how Jūzān, en route through Kyoto on business to the Edo *dàfǔ*, brought the manuscript of the *Huánshān lù* to Kyoto for cutting, and how he himself (Dokuan) met Jūzān at Settsu-ga 攝津賀 *lǚguǎn* 旅館 and, in conversation, recognised the Chinese Sōtō monk as a Fújiàn acquaintance from forty years earlier — "his hair had gone white and his face wrinkled" — and was asked to write the preface. Jūzān's closing postscript, dated three days earlier, is a substantial statement of Shòu-chāng-lineage orthodoxy against contemporary Japanese Chán rivals, positioning Dàopèi's Gǔshān as the continuation of authentic *zǔyìn* 祖印.

The content, organised across four juan, includes a long retrospective self-memoir ***Lǚbó huànjī*** 旅泊幻蹟 (in *juan* 4) — the source for DILA's biographical dates — tracing Dàopèi's life from his 1615 birth at Jiànān Dōngbáiyún 東白雲 through his long Gǔshān residency. Further material ranges through *shàngtáng*, *xiǎocān*, stupa-management records, registry-book prescriptions ("*chángzhù zhī yǒu jiāotóu bù* 常住之有交頭簿" — the established custom of a hand-over register for monastic property succession, which Dàopèi reformed in Kāngxī *Yǐchǒu* 乙丑 / 1685 by launching a new register), and memorial prose. The sections dated to Shùnzhì *Dīngyǒu* 丁酉 (1657) preserve Dàopèi's first-person record of his master [[元賢]]'s death and the immediate administrative transition at Gǔshān.

Date bracket: 1657 (first-person testimony to Yuánxián's death and the succession) through the 1693 Japanese reprinting. The Chinese *nèishū* 內書 is dated 1688 but covers material accumulated across Dàopèi's thirty-year abbacy from 1660.

## Translations and research

*No book-length Western-language study located.* Dokuan Genkō's opening preface is of independent interest for Japanese Zen historiography (Dokuan was a leading voice in late-17th-century Japanese disputes over Ōbaku and Chinese Chán authenticity). The Jū-zān postscript gives rare documentation of Fújiàn-Kyūshū Sōtō transmission networks in the late 17th century.

## Other points of interest

The *Lǚbó huànjī* 旅泊幻蹟 in *juan* 4 — Dàopèi's first-person "dream-travel-vestiges" memoir — is one of the most detailed surviving late-Míng / early-Qīng Chán-abbatial autobiographies and the principal source for DILA's biographical record. The Dokuan / Jūzān Japanese-publication frame documents the specific Fújiàn → Kyūshū Sōtō transmission corridor of the 1660s–1690s, parallel to and competing with the simultaneous Ōbaku-ji 黃檗寺 transmission from the same Fújiàn region.
