# Gāofēng Yuánmiào chánshī yǔlù 高峰原妙禪師語錄

Two-juan Yuán-dynasty *yǔlù* of **Gāofēng Yuánmiào** [[原妙]] 高峰原妙 (Jiāxī 2.3.23 / 16 April 1238 – Yuánzhēn 1.12.1 / 13 January 1296), Yángqí-branch Línjì master, dharma-heir of [[祖欽]] Xuěyán Zǔqīn and teacher of Zhōngfēng Míngběn 中峰明本. *Xuzangjing* X70 n1400. Compiled "by the participating disciples" (*cānxué ménrén biān*) — names not given in the extant text — and re-cut at Língyǐn by Hónglǐ 弘禮 in the Wànlì period. Preface by [[袾宏|Yúnqī Zhūhóng]] 雲棲袾宏 (1535–1615) dated Wànlì 27.foshi-huanxi-ri (Wànlì 27 = 1599, monks' "Buddha-rejoicing day", the close of summer retreat) — Zhūhóng's preface records that he had treasured Yuánmiào's text for thirty years, wished the *dàzàng* had absorbed it earlier, and encountered criticism from Pure Land devotees in his *Liánshè* community ("How is it that having turned all streams to the direction of the Pure Land you are now so eagerly praising this collection?"). Juan 1 contains *fǎyǔ* from the hermitage at Shuāngjìān 雙髻庵 in Húzhōu (from Xiánchún *jiǎxū* spring / 1274), *shēngzuò* records, *shìzhòng* 示眾 talks. Juan 2 contains *xiǎocān*, *jìsòng*, and the *xíngzhuàng* with Yuánmiào's precise birth and death dates.

### Abstract

Per DILA A-entry for 原妙 and the *xíngzhuàng*: Yuánmiào (*hào* Gāofēng 高峰 "High-Peak"; posthumous title Pǔmíng Guǎngjì chánshī 普明廣濟禪師) was a native of Wújiāng 吳江 (Sūzhōu). He received dharma-transmission from Xuěyán Zǔqīn after an extended crisis-and-breakthrough training pattern that became celebrated in later Línjì pedagogy (the famous "great-doubt" instruction to focus on *wànfǎ guī yī, yī guī hé chù* "the ten-thousand dharmas return to one — to where does the one return?"). His most famous feature is the self-imposed seclusion at Shīguān 死關 ("Death-Barrier") on Xīfēng 西峰 of Tiānmùshān from 1279, where he refused all callers and lived for the last eighteen years of his life; his teaching was conducted through letters and occasional hermitage *shìzhòng*. His dharma-heir Zhōngfēng Míngběn 中峰明本 (1263–1323) transmitted the line into the Yuán state-approved Chán of the early fourteenth century. The *yǔlù* was deeply prized in the Míng Chán revival (particularly by Zhūhóng) and in later Japanese Rinzai; the Língyǐn re-cutting sponsored by Hónglǐ was the basis of its wide Qīng-era circulation.

The companion text *Gāofēng Yuánmiào chánshī chányào* ([[KR6q0334]]) distils Yuánmiào's teachings into a single-juan "essentials" manual, explicitly modelled by its compiler Qiáozǔ 喬祖 on the "gāng 綱 : mù 目 :: lǐng 領 : lǚ 縷" schema — "if the rope is thrown out but the net-cords are not gathered, the net will not spread; if the collar is lifted but the threads are not pulled, the robe will not shake clean." The two texts together form the extant standard corpus.

Yuán-miào's *yǔlù* and *chán-yào* are staples of Japanese Rinzai and Korean Sŏn training. English-language scholarship: Jeffrey Broughton, *The Chan Whip Anthology: A Companion to Zen Practice* (Oxford University Press, 2015) — a translation of Yún-qī Zhū-hóng's *Chán-guān cè-jìn* 禪關策進 (compiled 1600), which cites Yuán-miào extensively. Morten Schlütter, *How Zen Became Zen* (Kuroda, 2008), discusses Yuán-miào in the context of the Yángqí-branch continuation of Dàhuì's *kàn-huà* program. Korean work on Taego Bou (whose master Shí-wū Qīng-gǒng was a dharma-brother of Zhōng-fēng in the Xuě-yán line) regularly references Gāo-fēng.
