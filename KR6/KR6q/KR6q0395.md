# Wúhuàn chánshī yǔlù 無幻禪師語錄
*Recorded Sayings of Chán Master Wú-huàn*
by [[性沖]] (說), [[慧廣]] (編集)

## About the work

Two-juan recorded-sayings of the late-Míng **Línjì Yángqí 臨濟楊岐** Chán master **Wúhuàn Gǔzhàn Xìngchōng** [[性沖]] (1540–1612), direct dharma-heir of [[如空|Wúqù Rúkōng]] 無趣如空 (1491–1580) and principal teacher at **Chēxīān** 車溪庵 in the Sūzhōu region (*jìjì* 寄跡 for over twenty years) and at **Jìngshān** 徑山 near Hángzhōu. Compiled (*biānjí* 編集) by his direct disciple **Nánmíng Huìguǎng** [[慧廣]] (1576–1620) and printed through the patronage of **Chéng Shàngfǔ** 程尚甫. Preserved in the *Jiāxīng zàng* as J25 no. B156. The text is framed by three prefaces — by **Zhànrán Yuánchéng** 湛然圓澄 (1561–1626, dated Wànlì 42 = 1614), by **Càí Shànjì** 蔡善繼 (*Xiàxiūshēng* 夏休生) of Wúxìng, and by **Yúnqī Zhūhóng** 雲棲袾宏 (1535–1615) — and closed by Xìngchōng's *xíngzhuàng* 行狀 signed by the editor Huìguǎng and fellow disciples (*ménrén Huìguǎng děng qǐshǒu shù* 門人慧廣等稽首述). This is the companion volume to the *yǔlù* of Xìngchōng's master in [[KR6q0394]]. Non-commentary; *commentedTextid* omitted.

### Abstract

**Author.** Xìng-chōng, styled *Gǔ-zhàn* 古湛 ("Ancient Deep Stillness") and epithet *Wú-huàn* 無幻 ("Without Illusion"), was a native of **Hé-jùn Xiù-shuǐ** 禾郡秀水 (= Jiā-xìng Xiù-shuǐ, Zhèjiāng), lay surname **Zhāng** 張, lay name **Qiáo** 橋 (given from his mother's dream of a rooster flying up from a bridge). His early Buddhist formation came under the layman **Fāng Yàn-shān** 方硯山, who directed him to read the scriptures; when Xìng-chōng concluded that "the Tripiṭaka and twelve-fold corpus are but fish-traps and rabbit-snares" (*sān-zàng shí-èr-bù dà-yuē zhǐ-shì quán-tí ěr* 三藏十二部大約秪是筌蹄耳), Fāng sent him to [[如空|Wú-qù Rú-kōng]] with the endorsement that this was "a Línjì child in truth, an authentic figure of the Way." On first meeting Rú-kōng rebuked him — "remembering this tangle fouls a single pure-white inch of you" (*jì cǐ yī luò-suǒ wū rǔ yī piàn qīng-bái dì* 記此一絡索汙汝一片清白地) — and set him to investigation. Xìng-chōng could not take the tonsure immediately because of his mother; he served her while practising at Yuán-míng-sì 圓明寺 and was *yìnkě*'d only after she died. On his announced intention to retire to Jìng-shān, Rú-kōng pronounced the cryptic: "your karmic connection lies at the water's edge" (*zǐ yuán zài shuǐ biān* 子緣在水邊). The transmission verse was delivered in connection with a striking incident: Rú-kōng was about to burn his accumulated verses and *jì-sòng* 偈頌; Xìng-chōng begged to rescue and anthologise them, and brought the completed anthology back as a gift — presenting it as "some farmland and its original title-deed, requesting the Master's signature." Rú-kōng opened it: "This is mine — where is yours?" Xìng-chōng replied, "Master, do not grasp at the market." Rú-kōng threw down the manuscript; Xìng-chōng turned to leave, then submitted four verses of his own. Rú-kōng conferred robe-dharma with the verse *Shī chuán niān-huā zōng, shì wǒ wēi-xiào fǎ, qīn shǒu zhǎn fù rǔ, chí-fèng biàn chén-shā* 師傳拈花宗／示我微笑法／親手展付汝／持奉遍塵剎. After Rú-kōng's 1580 death Xìng-chōng stayed at Jìng-shān three years, then sealed himself into door-closed *bì-guān* at Yuán-míng-sì for another three. On opening the gate he accepted the invitation of the monk **Zhèng-chuān** 證川 of **Chē-xī-ān** and made his principal residence there for more than twenty years (ca. 1589–1611), fulfilling the "water's edge" prediction (Chē-xī = "stream-cart" referring to the adjacent stream). In spring Wàn-lì 38 *gēng-xū* (1610) Jìng-shān established a chán-period and requested him as leader; he refused; the following spring (1611) he accepted reluctantly, served half a year, fell ill with *pí-jí* 脾疾 (spleen disorder), and returned to Chē-xī; on Wàn-lì 39.12.4 (= 6 January **1612**) he arranged final matters, sat upright, and died, aged 72 with 30 *là* (ordained years). His relic-bones were enshrined in a stūpa at Jìng-shān. Dharma-heir and compiler: Huì-guǎng.

**Contents.** Juan 1 (*shàng*): three prefaces → **住車溪庵語** (*Zhù Chēxīān yǔ* — record of his Chēxī abbacy, *xiǎocān* 小參, *niānxiāng* 拈香 dedications including the anniversary-*jìchén* 忌辰 of Rúkōng, *jīyuán* 機緣 dialogues with *Xiùzuòzhǔ* 秀座主 and other interlocutors) → **徑山語** (*Jìngshān yǔ* — the brief but dense half-year teaching at Jìngshān in 1611, opening with his pronouncement that he had come because "Wúqù lǎorén left an unfinished *gōngàn* that this old man came out to conclude") → **示禪人** (*shì chánrén* — dharma-instructions to named and unnamed practitioners) → **機緣** (*jīyuán* — extended encounter-dialogues) → **拈古** (*niāngǔ* — comments on classical cases) → **頌古** (*sònggǔ* — twenty-five verses on set cases, from Huánglóng's abbacy-selection to Lǔzǔ 魯祖's wall-facing, Mùzhōu's 喪考妣, the *Qīngzhōu bùshān* 青州布衫, Zhàozhōu's *chīchá qù* 喫茶去, Yúnmén's *rìlǐ kànshān* 日裏看山, the Sixth Patriarch's *búhuì fófǎ* 不會佛法, Déshān's alms-bowl, Yǎngshān's *yīèrsān* verse, and others) → **讚** (*zàn* — encomia on Guānyīn images of several types: 水月, 魚籃, etc.). Juan 2 (*xià*): **偈頌** (*jìsòng* — extensive verse corpus), opening with the remarkable *Jí Wúqù héshàng yǔ jì ǒuchéng sān shǒu* 集無趣和尚語偈偶成三首 (three verses spontaneously produced while anthologising Rúkōng's sayings — the very anthology that triggered Rúkōng's robe-transmission); then *Cānchán jì shíyī shǒu* 參禪偈 (11 pieces), *Shì chánrén liù shǒu* (6), occasional verses of place and travel (*Shānlóu jì Huàn* 山樓寄幻, *Dēng Wūzhānshān* 登烏瞻山, *Jìngshān huí zhì Yuánmíng chányuàn guānjū èr shǒu* 徑山回至圓明禪院關居二首, *Zháxī dàozhōng* 霅溪道中), exchanges with lay interlocutors (including the jūshì **Shěnyuán** 靜源, **Chōngxuán Jì** 沖玄計 jūshì, **Méicūn** 梅村, **Xīguīzǐ** 西歸子), seasonal meditations (*Shānjū shí shǒu* 山居十首, *Xuězhōng jíshì* 雪中即事), and extended *niànfó sòng* 念佛頌, *zhǐxīn sòng* 指心頌, and the *Mùniú tú sòng* 牧牛圖頌 (verses on the 10-frame Ox-Herding picture sequence). The juan closes with the *xíngzhuàng*.

**Lay dedications in the prefaces.** Càí Shànjì 蔡善繼 (1565–1625) of Wúxìng 吳興, a prominent Míng jūshì known by his *hào* *Xiàxiūshēng* 夏休生, writes the middle preface and emphasises the extraordinary coincidence that father-and-son *yǔlù* — Rúkōng's [[KR6q0394]] and Xìngchōng's present [[KR6q0395]] — appeared simultaneously, a pairing he compares to the rare **Udumbara-flower re-blooming** and to the Yuán-dynasty **Gāofēng / Zhōngfēng 高峰 / 中峰 父子** double-transmission. Càí invokes **Yúnqī Zhūhóng** 雲棲袾宏 as the figure who alone could adjudicate the rarity; and in the third preface, Yúnqī himself (*lǎobìng sēng Zhūhóng hénán jǐn zhì* 老病僧袾宏和南謹識) modestly declines to expound, saying only that without having met Xìngchōng in person he nonetheless reads the *yǔlù* "as though seeing the master's face."

**Dating.** notBefore 1580 (Rúkōng's death; Xìngchōng's independent teaching career begins). notAfter 1615 (Yúnqī Zhūhóng's death on Wànlì 43.7.4 = 27 August 1615, terminus for the third preface and hence for the received text as framed). The Zhànrán Yuánchéng preface is explicitly dated to Wànlì 42 *jiǎyín* (1614) 夾鍾月 (second lunar month) and gives the tightest verifiable pre-publication terminus. The Chēxīān teaching occupies ca. 1589–1611; the Jìngshān half-year is mid-1611; compilation by Huìguǎng falls between 1612 and the 1614 preface.

*No substantial Western-language secondary literature located on Xìng-chōng specifically.* He appears in Jiang Wu, *Enlightenment in Dispute: The Reinvention of Chan Buddhism in Seventeenth-Century China* (Oxford, 2008) only as background to the Míng-Qīng transition Línjì revival. The trilateral lay-clerical frame of the present *yǔlù* (Càí Shàn-jì, Yún-qī Zhū-hóng, Zhàn-rán Yuán-chéng) makes it an excellent primary text for studies of the late-Wàn-lì Jiā-xìng / Sū-zhōu Buddhist network; see Chün-fang Yü, *The Renewal of Buddhism in China: Zhuhong and the Late Ming Synthesis* (Columbia, 1981) for Yún-qī's circle, and the collected studies in Timothy Brook, *Praying for Power* (Harvard, 1993) for the lay-donor pattern. For the Tiān-mù-shān Línjì sub-lineage that Xìng-chōng preserved, see Natasha Heller, *Illusory Abiding* (Harvard, 2014).

## Other points of interest

- The anecdote of Xìngchōng's **rescue and anthologisation of Rúkōng's verses** — preserved in both the 行狀 at the end of juan 2 and the compressed *Jí Wúqù héshàng yǔ jì ǒuchéng sān shǒu* 集無趣和尚語偈偶成三首 at the head of juan 2 — is the internal explanation for why Rúkōng's own *yǔlù* ([[KR6q0394]]) survives at all: its edited form is likely traceable back to this same editorial effort by Xìngchōng. The two *yǔlù* are thus not just parallel father-and-son records but textually co-dependent.
- Xìngchōng's half-year **Jìngshān abbacy** (1611) is dated in the *xíngzhuàng* and provides a rare dated glimpse into the state of Jìngshān teaching on the eve of the **Mìyún Yuánwù** 密雲圓悟 / **Hànyuè Fǎzàng** 漢月法藏 generation that would reanimate Línjì Chán in the Tiānqǐ and Chóngzhēn eras.
- The *yǔlù* closes with a full **Mùniú tú sòng** 牧牛圖頌 — Xìngchōng's rendering of the ten-stage Ox-Herding sequence — a late-Míng revival of the classic Pǔmíng 普明 / Kuòān 廓庵 meditative programme.
- The three-preface opening by a **Cáodòng** figure (Yuánchéng, self-identifying here as 27th-generation Cáodòng, not as his more usual Yúnmén persona), a **Pure Land / syncretist** (Yúnqī), and a **lay literatus** (Càí) gives an unusually rich map of Xìngchōng's cross-sectarian reception.
