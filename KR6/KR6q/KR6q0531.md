# Défēng chánshī Bōrě yǔlù 德風禪師般若語錄
*Recorded Sayings of Chán Master Dé-fēng of Bō-rě [-sì]*
by [[如惺]] (等編)

Six-juan *yǔlù* of **Défēng** [[德風]] (b. **1622-10-08**, death unknown), Línjì-Yángqí dharma-heir of **Bǎichī Xíngyuán** 百癡行元 (1611–1662), thus a co-disciple of **Liánfēng Sù** [[素]] (cf. [[KR6q0530]]). Native of **Shàngyáng / Chuānshā** 上洋川沙 (Sōngjiāng / Shànghǎi area), lay surname **Gù** 顧. The text bears Défēng's name only in the title position; the catalog persons-list registers only [[如惺]] as compiler. The author's name in the inner title is given as 德風禪師, with the editorial note "侍者如惺等編" — Rúxīng was Défēng's personal attendant.

**Abbacies.** Principally **Wǔhú Bōrěsì** 鵡湖般若寺 (in Wújiāng / Sūzhōu region — *Wǔhú* 鵡湖 is the local name for an inlet of Tàihú), entered **順治戊戌 12/12** = **1659-01-04** (the formal *rùyuàn*), with the abbacy invitation accepted on **丁酉正月初三** = **1657-02-15**; later **Hùguó chányuàn** 護國禪院. The *yǔlù*'s subtitle 般若語錄 reflects Bōrěsì as the principal preaching site.

The preface is by lay disciple **Shīfēng jūshì Shěn Zōngjìng** 獅峰居士沈宗敬 dated **康熙二十八年歲次己巳八月朔** = **1689-09-14**. *notBefore* = **1657** (acceptance of Wǔhú abbacy); *notAfter* = **1689** (preface). Printed as Jiāxīng Canon J38 B411.

**Contents.** Juan 1: prefaces, *shàngtáng* records from his Bōrě and Hùguó tenures. Juan 2: *sònggǔ*, *Lèdào gē* 樂道歌. Juan 3–4: *wèndá jīyuán*, mountain-dwelling poems (*shānjū jì*), boat-dwelling poems (*chuánjū*), and a long sequence of *shì*-verses to disciples and lay friends. Juan 5–6: continuation, including the autobiographical *xíngyóu* 行繇 — the principal source for his life — and a closing *jǐngshì* poem on the brevity of worldly things, signed by lay donors who funded the printing.

## Other points of interest

The first-person *xíngyóu* in juan 6 is unusual for a *yǔlù* — most parallel collections have a third-person *xíngzhuàng* by a disciple. Défēng's account narrates his sequential testing under five separate masters before final transmission from Bǎichī, giving an unusually detailed picture of mid-17th-century mendicant *cānfǎng* circuits in the lower Yangtze (Lónghuá → Lùzhí → Nánxiáng → Qīzhēn → Yúnjiān).
