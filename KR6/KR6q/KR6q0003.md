# Jǐngdé chuándēng lù 景德傳燈錄

*Jǐngdé-Era Record of the Transmission of the Lamp*

compiled by [[道原]] (Dàoyuán) of Yǒng'ānyuàn 永安院, presented to the throne in 1004 (Sòng *Jǐngdé* 景德 1) and published 1011 after editorial revision by [[楊億]] (Yáng Yì) and others

## About the work

The most authoritative and most influential of the Sòng-era Chán lineage records, in 30 juan. Traces transmission from the seven buddhas of the past, through 28 Indian patriarchs, the six Chinese patriarchs, and the descending lines of Chán masters, to the late tenth century — a total of 1,701 figures, of whom 951 receive substantive entries with recorded dialogue. The work effectively defined the canonical shape of Chán historical self-representation for the rest of the imperial period; subsequent lamp records (the *Tiānshèng guǎngdēng lù* 天聖廣燈錄, *Jiànzhōng jìngguó xùdēng lù* 建中靖國續燈錄, *Liándēng huìyào* 聯燈會要, *Jiātài pǔdēng lù* 嘉泰普燈錄, and the synthesising *Wǔdēng huìyuán* 五燈會元 of 1252) all build on its template and supplement rather than supersede it.

### Abstract

Compiled by Dàoyuán 道原, a Chán monk of Yǒng'ānyuàn 永安院 in the Línjì 臨濟 line, and presented to the throne in *Jǐngdé* 1 (1004) — hence the era-name title. The Sòng emperor [[真宗]] Zhēnzōng ordered the work submitted for editorial revision to a commission led by [[楊億]] Yáng Yì (974–1020), [[李維]] Lǐ Wéi, and [[王曙]] Wáng Shǔ; their revisions polished the literary style and harmonised the dialogue conventions. The revised text was officially printed in 1011 (some sources give 1009 for the imperial *Dàzàng* 大藏 inclusion) and entered the canon. Yáng Yì's preface — a major literary document in its own right — sets out the work's ambition to consolidate the disparate Táng and Five Dynasties Chán transmission narratives into a single authoritative genealogy.

The structural innovation of the *Jǐngdé chuándēng lù* is to organise the entries hierarchically by lineage generation rather than chronologically, so that the family-tree of Chán transmission can be read down its branches. The standard literary unit — biographical headnote, genealogical position, recorded dialogue, transmission verse — became the model for all subsequent lamp records and, ultimately, for the *Wǔdēng huìyuán* 五燈會元 ([[KR6q0012]]) synthesis.

The Bǎotáng 保唐 and other Sìchuān lines that the *Lìdài fǎbǎo jì* 曆代法寶記 ([[KR6q0001]]) had defended are absent or marginalised, indicating both an editorial decision and the pace of those lines' decline by the year 1000. Conversely, the orthodox Línjì and Cáodòng lines whose Sòng-era ascendancy was already evident at the time of compilation receive their definitive lineage formulation here.

The text was carved into the imperially-sponsored *Dàzàng* canon and reproduced in all major subsequent canonical editions: 磧砂藏, 高麗藏, 南藏, 甲, 明, etc. — the unusually long edition list in the catalog metadata reflects this comprehensive canonical presence.

## Translations and research

A complete English translation of juan 1–3 has been published by Sōhaku Ogata (Ogata Shōhaku 緒方宗博), *The Transmission of the Lamp* (Wakefield, NH: Longwood, 1990). Randolph S. Whitfield has translated additional juan in a multi-volume project (Books on Demand, ongoing). No fully consolidated authoritative complete English translation exists.

The standard scholarly Chinese edition is Gù Hóngyì 顧宏義, *Jǐngdé chuándēng lù yìzhù* 景德傳燈錄譯註 (4 vols., Shànghǎi shūdiàn 上海書店出版社, 2010), which provides full punctuation, modern Chinese paraphrase, and detailed annotation. Earlier punctuated editions: Yú Gǔ 余谷, *Jǐngdé chuándēng lù* (Sìchuān bāshǔ shūshè 四川巴蜀書社, 2000).

Foundational Japanese scholarship by Yanagida Seizan 柳田聖山 (e.g. *Zen no goroku* 禪の語錄 series, Chikuma Shobō, 1971ff.). Western scholarship: Albert Welter, *Monks, Rulers, and Literati: The Political Ascendancy of Chan Buddhism* (OUP, 2006), and *The Linji Lu and the Creation of Chan Orthodoxy* (OUP, 2008), are essential context for the *Jǐngdé chuándēng lù*'s place in the Sòng Chán institutional settlement; T. Griffith Foulk, "The 'Ch'an School' and Its Place in the Buddhist Monastic Tradition" (PhD diss., U Michigan, 1987), and Morten Schlütter, *How Zen Became Zen* (UH Press, 2008), supply the institutional history.

## Other points of interest

The *Jǐngdé chuándēng lù*'s 1004 imperial submission and 1011 publication mark the formal consolidation of Chán's institutional position within the Northern Sòng state-Buddhist establishment, and the text is correspondingly central to debates about when and how Chán became the dominant Buddhist tradition of late-imperial China. The lineage diagrams it codified — particularly the Five Houses (五家) framework — became the standard organising scheme for all subsequent Chán historiography and remain so in scholarly literature today. Of the 1,701 figures listed, only 951 have substantive entries; the remainder are recorded as "*name only*" (有名無語), a useful index of which mid-tenth-century lines had already lost their living transmission by 1000.
