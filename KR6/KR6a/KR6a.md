### Scope and scholarly tradition

KR6a corresponds exactly to the 阿含部 *Āhán bù* of the Taishō canon (Vol. 1 and the first two-thirds of Vol. 2, T01n0001–T02n0151), the section in which the Chinese tradition collected its translations of the Indic *sūtra-piṭaka* — the discourse-collections common, in differing recensions, to all early Buddhist schools. Its spine is the four great Āgamas — *Dīrghāgama*, *Madhyamāgama*, *Saṃyuktāgama*, *Ekottarikāgama* — corresponding broadly to the Pāli *Dīgha-*, *Majjhima-*, *Saṃyutta-*, and *Aṅguttara-nikāya*. Around that spine the cataloguers gathered ~150 shorter translations: stand-alone parallels of individual sūtras, alternative translations of the long parinirvāṇa narrative, cosmological texts on the structure of the *lokadhātu*, biographical accounts of the seven past Buddhas, dialogues with brahmans, and a small group of Abhidharma-style treatises that frame the discourse material analytically. The CANWWW structural index  makes the architecture explicit: most of the parallel sūtras and parinirvāṇa variants are linked from inside the *Dīrghāgama* table of contents itself, marking the four Āgamas as the conceptual anchor against which the rest of the section is to be read.

The chronological range of KR6a is the largest of any subdivision of the Buddhist canon: from the second-century Later Hàn translations of Ān Shìgāo 安世高 down to the early eleventh-century court-translation projects of the Northern Sòng. Three school recensions are represented within the four Āgamas — the *Dīrghāgama* (Dharmaguptaka), *Madhyamāgama* and *Saṃyuktāgama* (Sarvāstivāda / Mūlasarvāstivāda), *Ekottarikāgama* (Mahāsāṃghika or a closely related school) — so that KR6a is not a single canonical *Sūtra-piṭaka* but a cross-sectarian composite assembled across eight centuries. Modern scholarship on this material is dominated by the comparative-Āgama project descended from Anesaki Masaharu and continued by Akanuma Chizen, with more recent contributions by Mizuno Kōgen, Tilmann Vetter, and especially Jan Nattier, whose redatings of attributed translators (notably stripping spurious Hàn-period attributions) the per-text notes follow throughout.

### Important texts and text clusters

The four Āgamas are the irreducible core of the division and account for close to half its total extent (183 of ~400 fascicles):

- *Cháng Āhán jīng* 長阿含經 [[KR6a0001]] — Dharmaguptaka *Dīrghāgama*, 22 fascicles, translated 413 by Buddhayaśas 佛陀耶舍 and Zhú Fóniàn 竺佛念 under the Later Qín. Thirty long discourses including the *Mahāpadāna*, *Mahāparinirvāṇa*, *Aggañña* and *Cakkavatti* sūtras.
- *Zhōng Āhán jīng* 中阿含經 [[KR6a0026]] — Sarvāstivāda *Madhyamāgama*, 60 fascicles, the largest single text in KR6a, retranslated 397–398 by Gōután Sēngjiātípó 瞿曇僧伽提婆 (Saṅghadeva) at Jiànkāng on the basis of an earlier Dào'ān 道安 / Saṅghadeva collaboration of 384.
- *Zá Āhán jīng* 雜阿含經 [[KR6a0099]] — Mūlasarvāstivāda *Saṃyuktāgama*, 50 fascicles, translated 435–443 by Qiúnàbátuóluó 求那跋陀羅 (Guṇabhadra) at Jiànkāng. Roughly 1,362 short connected sūtras, the principal Chinese witness to the *saṃyukta*-style sūtra collection. The textual order of the received recension is famously dislocated and was reconstructed in modern scholarship by Lǚ Chéng 呂澂 and Yìnshùn 印順.
- *Biéyì Zá Āhán jīng* 別譯雜阿含經 [[KR6a0100]] — anonymous "alternative-translation" *Saṃyuktāgama* in 16 fascicles, dated to the Sānqín period (c. 350–431); preserves a partial parallel recension of uncertain school affiliation, indispensable for comparative work on T99.
- *Zēngyī Āhán jīng* 增壹阿含經 [[KR6a0126]] — *Ekottarikāgama*, 51 fascicles, translated 384–398. The catalogue ascription oscillates between Dharmanandi 曇摩難提 (initial draft, 384) and Saṅghadeva (revision, 397); the received text shows clear Mahāsāṃghika or proto-Mahāyāna inflections and has been intensively studied for that reason.

Around this spine, several smaller clusters recur:

- **Parinirvāṇa cycle.** Three independent translations of the long *Mahāparinirvāṇa* narrative — *Fó bānníhuán jīng* [[KR6a0005]] (Bái Fǎzǔ 白法祖, c. 290–305), *Bānníhuán jīng* [[KR6a0006]] (anonymous, Eastern Jìn), and Fǎxiǎn 法顯's *Dà bānnièpán jīng* [[KR6a0007]] (413–418) — sit in deliberate parallel to the *Yóuxíng jīng* 遊行經 inside the *Dīrghāgama*. The female parinirvāṇa narrative *Fó shuō Dà'àidào bānníhuán jīng* [[KR6a0148]] and *Fómǔ bānníhuán jīng* [[KR6a0149]] complete the cycle.
- **Past-Buddha biography.** *Qī fó jīng* [[KR6a0002]], *Pípóshī fó jīng* [[KR6a0003]] and *Qī fó fùmǔ xìngzì jīng* [[KR6a0004]] expand the *Mahāpadāna* material from the *Dīrghāgama*'s opening; the first two are late tenth-century Sòng retranslations by Fǎtiān 法天.
- **Cosmology.** *Dà lóutàn jīng* [[KR6a0023]] (Fǎlì 法立 / Fǎjù 法炬, Western Jìn), *Qǐshì jīng* [[KR6a0024]] (Shénàjuéduō 闍那崛多 / Jñānagupta, Suí), and *Qǐshì yīnběn jīng* [[KR6a0025]] (Dámójíduō 達摩笈多 / Dharmagupta, Suí) are three successive renderings of the *Lokaprajñapti* / *Aggañña* cosmological material, each with substantive variants. They are the principal Chinese witnesses to early Buddhist cosmology and bear on later Chán and Tiāntái discussions of world-systems.
- **Brahman-dialogue group.** A cluster of Sòng-period retranslations by Shīhù 施護 (Dānapāla) — *Dà Jiāngù póluómén yuánqǐ jīng* [[KR6a0008]], *Níjūtuó fànzhì jīng* [[KR6a0011]], among others — gives access to brahman-encounter discourses that are partial parallels to the *Mahāgovinda*, *Aggañña*, and *Udumbarīkasīhanāda* sūtras.
- **Late Sòng court-translation revival.** Roughly 25 KR6a texts dated 970–1017 stem from the Northern Sòng *Yìjīng yuàn* 譯經院 under Shīhù, Fǎtiān, Fǎxián 法賢 (Dharmadeva), and Fǎhù 法護 (Dharmapāla). These small-scale retranslations of *Āgama* parallels are systematically valuable as a fresh Indic vantage on material that had long since stabilized in earlier Chinese versions.
- **Mahāyāna-inflected outliers.** A handful of texts catalogued in KR6a are doctrinally Mahāyāna; the most important is *Yāngjuémóluó jīng* 央掘魔羅經 [[KR6a0120]], Guṇabhadra's *tathāgatagarbha* recension of the Aṅgulimāla legend — a key source for the early Chinese reception of buddha-nature teaching.
- **Analytic / Abhidharma annexes.** *Fēnbié gōngdé lùn* 分別功德論 [[KR6a0159]] (5 fascicles, anonymous Hàn–Jìn) is a commentarial treatise on the introductory section of the *Ekottarikāgama* and the principal indigenous bridge between *sūtra* and *abhidharma* in this division.

### Important persons

- [[佛陀耶舍]] (Buddhayaśas, fl. early 5c.) — Kashmiri reciter; translator of the *Dīrghāgama* and of the *Sìfēn lǜ* in the Vinaya division.
- [[竺佛念]] (Zhú Fóniàn, fl. late 4c.) — Liángzhōu-trained Chinese collaborator on the *Dīrghāgama* and many of Dào'ān's Cháng'ān projects; key oral interpreter for an entire generation.
- [[瞿曇僧伽提婆]] (Saṅghadeva, fl. 383–398) — Kashmiri Sarvāstivādin; sole authority for the standard recensions of both the *Madhyamāgama* and the *Ekottarikāgama*.
- [[求那跋陀羅]] (Guṇabhadra, 394–468) — central Indian master active at Jiànkāng under Liú Sòng; translator of the *Saṃyuktāgama* and of *Yāngjuémóluó jīng*; pivotal figure in the early Chinese reception of *tathāgatagarbha* doctrine.
- [[法顯]] (Fǎxiǎn, c. 337–c. 422) — pilgrim-translator whose *Dà bānnièpán jīng* anchors the Hīnayāna parinirvāṇa cluster in KR6a; his Indic travels supplied the Sanskrit exemplars used here.
- [[安世高]] (Ān Shìgāo, fl. c. 148–170) — Parthian missionary, the earliest substantial translator into Chinese; ~20 short *Āgama*-parallel sūtras in KR6a are traditionally attributed to him, though many attributions have been narrowed by Nattier and others.
- [[支謙]] (Zhī Qiān, fl. c. 222–253) — Yuèzhī-descended Wú-court translator; produced numerous short *Āgama* sūtras, often in elegant literary register that contrasts with the more workmanlike Hàn versions.
- [[竺法護]] (Zhú Fǎhù / Dharmarakṣa, c. 233–c. 311) — Western Jìn translator of the broadest range; supplied multiple individual sūtra parallels and (with Fǎlì) the cosmological *Dà lóutàn jīng*.
- [[法炬]] (Fǎjù, fl. c. 290–306) — prolific Western Jìn translator of short sūtras, frequently in collaboration with Fǎlì 法立; one of the largest individual contributors to KR6a by text count.
- [[竺曇無蘭]] (Zhú Tánwúlán, fl. c. 381–395) — Eastern Jìn translator of numerous short Āgama parallels.
- [[闍那崛多]] (Jñānagupta, 523–c. 600) — Gandhāran translator at the Suí court; produced *Qǐshì jīng* and the parallel *Fó wèi Shǒujiā zhǎngzhě shuō yèbào chābié jīng* [[KR6a0080]] (with Gōután Fǎzhì 瞿曇法智).
- [[達摩笈多]] (Dharmagupta, d. 619) — Southern Indian translator at the Suí *Fānjīng guǎn*; produced the parallel *Qǐshì yīnběn jīng*.
- [[施護]] (Dānapāla, fl. 980–1017) — Uḍḍiyāna monk and senior translator at the Sòng *Yìjīng yuàn*; the dominant figure in the late KR6a retranslation programme (~13 texts).
- [[法天]] (Fǎtiān, d. 1001) — Magadhan translator at the Sòng court; rendered the past-Buddha biographies KR6a0002–0003 among others.
- [[法賢]] (Fǎxián / Dharmadeva, fl. late 10c.) — Sòng-period translator of Indic origin (distinct from the Eastern-Jìn pilgrim Fǎxiǎn 法顯, despite the homophone); ~10 short *Āgama* sūtras.
- [[白法祖]] (Bái Fǎzǔ, fl. 290–306) — Western Jìn translator of *Fó bānníhuán jīng* and *Dà'àidào bānníhuán jīng*.
- [[慧簡]] (Huìjiǎn, fl. 457) — Liú Sòng translator of *Fómǔ bānníhuán jīng* and a small group of related short sūtras.

### Topics

The interpretive issues that recur across KR6a entries cluster around five problems:

1. **Sectarian recension.** Each of the four Āgamas was transmitted by a different school, and the comparative question — what does each Chinese Āgama tell us about the doctrinal commitments of its Indic *nikāya* — runs through nearly every per-text note. *Ekottarikāgama* in particular is read by some scholars as bearing proto-Mahāyāna features, by others as straightforwardly Mahāsāṃghika.

2. **Pāli–Chinese parallels.** The main scholarly use of the section since Anesaki and Akanuma has been comparative: matching Chinese Āgama sūtras to their Pāli *Nikāya* counterparts (or to Sanskrit fragments from Central Asia and Gilgit) and analysing the divergences. The CANWWW *Structural Division* index in `div01.xml` gives the parallel-text mapping at the level of individual sub-sūtras.

3. **Authenticity of Hàn-period attributions.** A large number of short sūtras are traditionally attributed to Ān Shìgāo, Zhī Qiān, or 失譯 ("translator unknown"). Modern scholarship — Zürcher, Nattier, Harrison, Karashima — has substantially redrawn the attribution map, often pushing texts forward by centuries. The per-text notes in this division reflect that revision (e.g., several "Hàn" attributions are bracketed to a Six-Dynasties or later notBefore).

4. **Parinirvāṇa narrative variants.** The multiple Chinese versions of the long parinirvāṇa story (KR6a0005, 0006, 0007, plus the *Yóuxíng jīng* embedded in the *Dīrghāgama*) provide one of the richest case-studies in early Buddhist textual history, with significant doctrinal divergences between the recensions on the Buddha's last instructions, the four guarantees, and the schismatic prediction.

5. **The cosmological tradition.** *Dà lóutàn jīng*, *Qǐshì jīng*, and *Qǐshì yīnběn jīng* descend from a single Indic *Lokaprajñapti* but show three increasingly detailed Chinese expansions; they are the indispensable backdrop for the cosmological material in later Chinese Buddhist scholasticism (Tiāntái, Huáyán, the *Cāntóngqì* tradition's Buddhist appropriation, and the popular cosmologies of the *Nièpán-jīng* commentaries in KR6c).

### Timeline

Composition / translation dates, oldest first; for translation literature the date is the date of the Chinese rendering (the underlying Indic material is in every case older).

- 25–220 — *Fēnbié gōngdé lùn* 分別功德論 [[KR6a0159]] — anonymous Hàn-attributed commentary on the *Ekottarikāgama* preface (later strata may be Three-Kingdoms / Jìn).
- c. 148–170 — Ān Shìgāo's short *Āgama* parallels (~20 texts), the earliest Chinese sūtra translations preserved in KR6a.
- c. 222–253 — Zhī Qiān's *Āgama* renderings (~12 texts) at the Wú court at Jiànyè.
- c. 290–311 — Western Jìn cluster: *Fó bānníhuán jīng* [[KR6a0005]], *Dà lóutàn jīng* [[KR6a0023]], *Dà'àidào bānníhuán jīng* [[KR6a0148]], and the bulk of Zhú Fǎhù 竺法護, Fǎlì 法立, and Fǎjù 法炬's translations.
- 384–385 — first draft of *Zēngyī Āhán jīng* 增壹阿含經 by Dharmanandi at Cháng'ān (later revised; see KR6a0126).
- 397–398 — Saṅghadeva's *Zhōng Āhán jīng* 中阿含經 [[KR6a0026]] and revised *Ekottarikāgama* 增壹阿含經 [[KR6a0126]] at Jiànkāng — the foundational Eastern-Jìn moment for the section.
- 413 — Buddhayaśas / Zhú Fóniàn's *Cháng Āhán jīng* 長阿含經 [[KR6a0001]] at Cháng'ān under the Later Qín.
- 413–418 — Fǎxiǎn's *Dà bānnièpán jīng* 大般涅槃經 [[KR6a0007]], on his return from India.
- c. 350–431 — anonymous *Biéyì Zá Āhán jīng* 別譯雜阿含經 [[KR6a0100]], Sānqín period; window deliberately wide.
- 435–443 — Guṇabhadra's *Zá Āhán jīng* 雜阿含經 [[KR6a0099]] at Jiànkāng.
- 435–468 — Guṇabhadra's *Yāngjuémóluó jīng* 央掘魔羅經 [[KR6a0120]] and a substantial cluster of short sūtras.
- 457 — Huìjiǎn's *Fómǔ bānníhuán jīng* 佛母般泥洹經 [[KR6a0149]].
- 581–600 — Jñānagupta's *Qǐshì jīng* 起世經 [[KR6a0024]] under the Suí.
- 590–619 — Dharmagupta's *Qǐshì yīnběn jīng* 起世因本經 [[KR6a0025]], the parallel Suí cosmological recension.
- 973–1001 — opening phase of the Sòng court-translation programme: Fǎtiān's *Qī fó jīng* 七佛經 [[KR6a0002]] and the past-Buddha cluster.
- 982–1017 — main Sòng programme under Shīhù: brahman-dialogue parallels (KR6a0008, KR6a0011, etc.) and a wide range of short sūtras; closes the Chinese translation history of the Āgama section.
