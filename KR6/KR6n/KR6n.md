### Scope and scholarly tradition

KR6n collects the Chinese canon's holdings for the Yogācāra (瑜伽 *yújiā*) doctrinal stream — the abhidharmic-idealist tradition that descends from the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra* and the works of Asaṅga 無著菩薩 and Vasubandhu 世親菩薩 in fourth- and fifth-century North India, and that becomes the East Asian Fǎxiàng 法相 / Cí'ēn 慈恩 school after Xuánzàng 玄奘 reimports it in the mid-seventh century. The doctrinal core is established by the CANWWW Taishō *Yújiā bù* (`div30`, Taishō Vol. 30–31, Nos. 1579–1627): the *Yogācārabhūmi* and its summaries, the *Madhyāntavibhāga* and *Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra* of the so-called Maitreya corpus, the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* of Asaṅga, the *Triṃśikā* and *Viṃśatikā* of Vasubandhu, the *Abhidharmasamuccaya*, the *Ratnagotravibhāga*, the Hundred-Dharmas list, Dignāga's 陳那菩薩 *Ālambanaparīkṣā*, and the Yogācāra–tathāgatagarbha hybrids preserved at T1610–1611 and T1626–1627.

The division as catalogued in Kanripo, however, extends well beyond the Taishō *Yújiā bù* proper: it folds in the Cí'ēn-school commentarial canon of Vols. 42–45 (Kuījī's 窺基 *Chéng wéishí lùn shùjì* and *Fǎyuàn yìlín zhāng*; Huìzhāo 慧沼 and Zhìzhōu 智周 sub-commentaries), the late-Táng to Wǔdài Dūnhuáng Yogācāra fragments of Taishō Vol. 85, the Japanese Hossō 法相 scholastic apparatus of Vols. 65–70, and a sizeable late-Míng / early-Qīng Fǎxiàng-revival corpus in *Xùzàngjīng* Vols. 39 and 46–55. Three transmissional layers must therefore be kept distinct when reading the division: the **Old-Yogācāra** layer of Paramārtha 真諦 (c. 546–569), which is closer to a Valabhī recension and preserves the *trisvabhāva* and *amalavijñāna* in idiosyncratic form; the **New-Yogācāra** layer of Xuánzàng (646–664), based on Nālandā materials and Dharmapāla's 護法菩薩 reading; and the **East Asian scholastic** layer (Táng Cí'ēn school, Korean Hyŏnyu monks at Cháng'ān, Heian/Kamakura Hossō, Ming Cíbēi-Cí'ēn revivalists), in which the imported Indian texts become objects of an autonomous and very long-lived commentarial industry. The chronological span runs from c. 414 (Dharmakṣema's 曇無讖 *Bodhisattvabhūmi* [[KR6n0003]]) to the late seventeenth century (Ouyi Zhixu's 智旭 *Xiàngzōng bā yào zhíjiě* [[KR6n0145]]) — roughly twelve centuries of continuous Yogācāra scholasticism in Chinese.

### Important texts and text clusters

- *Yúqié shī dì lùn* 瑜伽師地論 [[KR6n0001]] — Xuánzàng's 646–648 translation of the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra* in 100 *juǎn*; the encyclopaedic root text of the entire division, conventionally attributed to Maitreya 彌勒菩薩 but in fact a multi-author stratified compilation of the Asaṅga circle. The exegetical aid *Yúqié shī dì lùn shì* 瑜伽師地論釋 [[KR6n0002]] of Jinaputra 最勝子 accompanies it.
- *Chéng wéishí lùn* 成唯識論 [[KR6n0016]] — Xuánzàng and Kuījī's 659 redaction of the ten Indian *Triṃśikā*-bhāṣyas around Dharmapāla's reading; the doctrinal charter of the Cí'ēn school. The commentarial pyramid stacked over it — Kuījī's *Shùjì* 述記 [[KR6n0026]], *Zhǎngzhōng shūyào* 掌中樞要 [[KR6n0029]], Huìzhāo's *Liǎoyì dēng* 了義燈 [[KR6n0030]], Zhìzhōu's *Yǎnmì* 演祕 [[KR6n0032]], and Taehyŏn's 太賢 *Xuéjì* 學記 [[KR6n0041]] — defines Táng-period Fǎxiàng orthodoxy.
- *Wéishí sānshí lùn sòng* 唯識三十論頌 [[KR6n0022]] and *Wéishí èrshí lùn* 唯識二十論 [[KR6n0055]] — Vasubandhu's *Triṃśikā* and *Viṃśatikā*, translated by Xuánzàng in 648 and 661; the textual nucleus around which both Indian and Chinese consciousness-only argumentation organises itself. Paramārtha's earlier renderings as *Zhuǎn shí lùn* 轉識論 [[KR6n0024]], *Wéishí lùn* 唯識論 [[KR6n0053]], and *Dàshèng wéishí lùn* 大乘唯識論 [[KR6n0054]] preserve the Old-Yogācāra reception.
- The *Shè dàshèng lùn* 攝大乘論 cluster — Asaṅga's *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*, surviving in three Chinese translations (Buddhaśānta 531–534 [[KR6n0058]], Paramārtha 563–564 [[KR6n0059]], Xuánzàng 648–649 [[KR6n0060]]) plus the *Bhāṣya* in three further versions [[KR6n0061]] [[KR6n0063]] [[KR6n0064]] [[KR6n0065]] and a thicket of Sui–Táng commentaries and Dūnhuáng *zhāng* fragments [[KR6n0066]]–[[KR6n0070]]. The cluster is the principal vehicle by which the *ālayavijñāna* doctrine enters China, and the Paramārtha line generates the Shèlùn 攝論 lineage that the Cí'ēn school then displaces.
- The *Madhyāntavibhāga* cluster — Paramārtha's *Zhōngbiān fēnbié lùn* 中邊分別論 [[KR6n0071]] and Xuánzàng's *Biàn zhōngbiān lùn* 辯中邊論 [[KR6n0072]] plus its verse [[KR6n0073]] and Kuījī's *Shùjì* [[KR6n0075]]; with the closely related *Shíbā kōng lùn* 十八空論 [[KR6n0074]] and *Sān wúxìng lùn* 三無性論 [[KR6n0079]] of the Paramārtha corpus.
- *Dàshèng zhuāngyán jīng lùn* 大乘莊嚴經論 [[KR6n0080]] — the *Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra* with Vasubandhu-circle commentary, Prabhākaramitra 630–633; together with *Xiǎnyáng shèngjiào lùn* 顯揚聖教論 [[KR6n0077]], Asaṅga's digest of the *Yogācārabhūmi* in Xuánzàng's 645–646 rendering.
- *Dàshèng āpídámó jí lùn* 大乘阿毘達磨集論 [[KR6n0081]] and its expanded *Zájí lùn* 雜集論 [[KR6n0082]] — Asaṅga's *Abhidharmasamuccaya* and Sthiramati's bhāṣya; the Yogācāra abhidharma proper. Read in China together with the *Dàshèng wǔyùn lùn* 大乘五蘊論 [[KR6n0094]], the *Guǎng wǔyùn lùn* 大乘廣五蘊論 [[KR6n0095]], and the *Bǎifǎ míngmén lùn* 大乘百法明門論 [[KR6n0096]] of Vasubandhu, the four works form the standard East Asian curriculum in Yogācāra dharma-taxonomy.
- The tathāgatagarbha-Yogācāra hybrids — *Fóxìng lùn* 佛性論 [[KR6n0087]], the *Ratnagotravibhāga* (*Jiūjìng yīshèng bǎoxìng lùn* 究竟一乘寶性論) [[KR6n0088]], and the two recensions of *Dàshèng fǎjiè wú chābié lùn* 大乘法界無差別論 [[KR6n0089]] [[KR6n0090]] — bridge the division to the *tathāgatagarbha* literature of KR6q and were a central battleground in the East Asian one-vehicle / five-natures controversy.
- The *Ālambanaparīkṣā* cluster — Dignāga's *Guān suǒyuányuán lùn* 觀所緣緣論 [[KR6n0112]] with Dharmapāla's commentary [[KR6n0113]], and a late-Míng wave of revival commentaries [[KR6n0114]]–[[KR6n0117]] — anchors the epistemological side of the division.
- The Cí'ēn doctrinal compendia — Kuījī's *Dàshèng fǎyuàn yìlín zhāng* 大乘法苑義林章 [[KR6n0124]], Huìzhāo's *Quànfā pútí xīn jí* 勸發菩提心集 [[KR6n0127]] and *Néng xiǎn zhōngbiān huìrì lùn* 能顯中邊慧日論 [[KR6n0128]], Zhìzhōu's *Dàshèng rùdào cìdì* 大乘入道次第 [[KR6n0129]] — codify the Cí'ēn school's doctrinal positions, including the five-gotras doctrine and the polemic against Wǒnch'ǔk's 圓測 readings.
- The *Bā shí guījǔ* 八識規矩 cluster ([[KR6n0131]]–[[KR6n0140]]) — Xuánzàng's (apocryphally attributed) verses on the eight consciousnesses, with Putai's 普泰 1530s Yuán-Míng-transition commentary [[KR6n0131]] and a dense Wànlì-Chongzhen-era stratum from Mingyu 明昱, Zhixu 智旭, Deqing 德清, and others. These short verse cycles, together with the *Xiàngzōng bā yào* 相宗八要 anthology [[KR6n0143]]–[[KR6n0145]], are the late-Míng Fǎxiàng revival's basic curriculum and the principal channel by which Cí'ēn doctrine survives into the Qīng.
- *Dàshèng yìzhāng* 大乘義章 [[KR6n0122]] — Jìngyǐng Huìyuǎn's 慧遠 580–592 doctrinal encyclopaedia: not a pure Yogācāra work, but a Sui-era Shèlùn-tradition synthesis included here because it remains the most systematic pre-Xuánzàng treatment of the Yogācāra dharma-list in Chinese.

### Important persons

- [[彌勒菩薩]] — the traditional speaker of the *Yogācārabhūmi* and the so-called Maitreya corpus (*Madhyāntavibhāga*, *Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra*, etc.); historicity disputed in modern scholarship, but the canonical attribution shapes every East Asian reading of the division.
- [[無著菩薩]] (Asaṅga, c. 4th c.) — author of the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*, the *Xiǎnyáng shèngjiào lùn*, and the *Abhidharmasamuccaya*; the architect of the systematic Yogācāra in this division.
- [[世親菩薩]] (Vasubandhu, c. 4th–5th c.; also rendered 天親菩薩) — author of the *Triṃśikā*, *Viṃśatikā*, *Pañcaskandhaka*, *Bǎifǎ míngmén lùn*, the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha-bhāṣya*, and *Dàshèng chéngyè lùn* [[KR6n0086]]; the most heavily commented author in the division.
- [[安慧菩薩]] (Sthiramati, 6th c.) — author of the *Abhidharmasamuccaya-vyākhyā* underlying T1606 and of the *Madhyāntavibhāga-ṭīkā* underlying parts of T1599; the principal "Old-Yogācāra" exegete reaching China through Paramārtha.
- [[護法菩薩]] (Dharmapāla, c. 530–561) — Nālandā commentator whose reading of the *Triṃśikā* is the spine of the *Chéng wéishí lùn* and whose *Ālambanaparīkṣā-vṛtti* [[KR6n0113]] founds the Cí'ēn epistemological tradition.
- [[陳那菩薩]] (Dignāga, c. 480–540) — author of *Guān suǒyuányuán lùn* [[KR6n0112]] and, by implication, of the epistemological reorientation that drives the *Ālambanaparīkṣā* and *Buddhabhūmi-vyākhyāna* portions of the corpus.
- [[親光菩薩]] (Bandhuprabha) — credited author of the *Buddhabhūmi-śāstra* materials underlying the *Chéng wéishí lùn* synthesis.
- [[最勝子]] (Jinaputra) — Indian compiler of *Yúqié shī dì lùn shì* [[KR6n0002]], the canonical exegetical key to the *Yogācārabhūmi*.
- [[真諦]] (Paramārtha, 499–569) — translator of the bulk of the Old-Yogācāra layer (T1584, T1587, T1589, T1593, T1595, T1599, T1610, T1616–1620); the rival transmissional lineage that the Xuánzàng school later partly supersedes.
- [[玄奘]] (Xuánzàng, 602–664) — translator of the New-Yogācāra core (T1579, T1585, T1586, T1590, T1594, T1597, T1600–1606, T1612, T1614 and many others); the single most important figure for the division and the founder of its Chinese school.
- [[窺基]] (Kuījī, 632–682) — Xuánzàng's principal disciple and the doctrinal architect of the Cí'ēn / Fǎxiàng school; author of the *Shùjì*, *Zhǎngzhōng shūyào*, *Yúqié shī dì lùn lüèzuǎn* [[KR6n0008]], *Fǎyuàn yìlín zhāng* [[KR6n0124]], and the *Wéishí èrshí lùn shùjì* [[KR6n0057]].
- [[慧沼]] (Huìzhāo, 650–714) — second of the Cí'ēn "three masters" (慈恩三祖); author of the *Liǎoyì dēng* [[KR6n0030]] and of the principal polemic against Wǒnch'ǔk in *Néng xiǎn zhōngbiān huìrì lùn* [[KR6n0128]].
- [[智周]] (Zhìzhōu, 668–723) — third of the Cí'ēn three masters; author of the *Yǎnmì* [[KR6n0032]] and *Dàshèng rùdào cìdì* [[KR6n0129]]; closes the Táng commentarial canon of the school.
- [[圓測]] (Wǒnch'ǔk, 613–696) — Korean monk at Cháng'ān, lifelong rival of Kuījī; his independent reading of the *Chéng wéishí lùn* is preserved largely in fragments and citations, and his disagreements with the Cí'ēn line drive much of the *Liǎoyì dēng* polemic.
- [[太賢]] (Taehyŏn, fl. mid-8th c.) — Silla Fǎxiàng systematiser; author of *Chéng wéishí lùn xuéjì* [[KR6n0041]], one of the longest sub-commentaries on the *Shùjì*.
- [[遁倫]] (Tullyun, late 7th – early 8th c.) — Korean monk; compiler of *Yúqié lùn jì* 瑜伽論記 [[KR6n0007]], the standard exegetical companion to the *Yogācārabhūmi* throughout East Asia.
- [[義淨]] (Yìjìng, 635–713) — translator of *Chéng wéishí bǎoshēng lùn* [[KR6n0056]], *Liùmén jiàoshòu xí dìng lùn* [[KR6n0084]], and *Guān suǒyuán lùn shì* [[KR6n0113]]; supplies the post-Xuánzàng Indian materials that round out the *Triṃśikā* / *Ālambanaparīkṣā* commentary set.
- [[達摩笈多]] (Dharmagupta, d. 619), [[佛陀扇多]] (Buddhaśānta), [[般若流支]] (Prajñāruci), [[提雲般若]] (Devaprajña), [[曇無讖]] (Dharmakṣema), [[求那跋摩]] (Guṇavarman) — the chain of pre-Xuánzàng translators through whom the Yogācāra corpus arrives in China between the early fifth and early seventh centuries.
- [[慧遠]] (Jìngyǐng Huìyuǎn, 523–592) — Sui doctrinal encyclopaedist; the *Dàshèng yìzhāng* [[KR6n0122]] is the most influential pre-Xuánzàng Chinese systematisation of Yogācāra topics.
- [[普泰]] (Pǔtài, fl. 1530s) — late-Míng Fǎxiàng pioneer; the *Bā shí guījǔ bǔzhù* [[KR6n0131]] is the catalyst of the late-Míng *Bā shí guījǔ* commentarial wave.
- [[王肯堂]] (Wáng Kěntáng, 1549–1613) — Wànlì-era *jūshì* physician-Buddhist; author of *Chéng wéishí lùn zhèngyì* [[KR6n0045]], a key bridge from the late-Míng *jūshì* scholarship into Fǎxiàng exegesis.
- [[通潤]] (Tōngrùn, 1565–1624) — Yúfēng 玉峯 master; author of *Chéng wéishí lùn jíjiě* [[KR6n0044]] and one of the leading Fǎxiàng-revivalist preachers of the Wànlì–Tianqi period.
- [[明昱]] (Míngyù, late 16th – early 17th c.) — late-Míng Fǎxiàng commentator on *Bā shí guījǔ*, *Bǎifǎ*, and *Guān suǒyuányuán lùn*; the single most prolific late-Míng author within the division.
- [[智旭]] (Ouyi Zhìxù, 1599–1655) — late-Míng Tiāntái–Pure Land master whose *zhíjiě* 直解 commentaries on the *Bā shí guījǔ*, *Bǎifǎ*, *Chéng wéishí lùn*, *Guān suǒyuányuán lùn*, and *Xiàngzōng bā yào* ([[KR6n0047]], [[KR6n0051]], [[KR6n0106]], [[KR6n0115]], [[KR6n0137]], [[KR6n0145]]) integrate Cí'ēn doctrine into the Ming syncretic synthesis.
- [[德清]] (Hānshān Déqīng, 1546–1623) — one of the "four great masters of the late Míng"; his *Bā shí guījǔ tōngshuō* [[KR6n0135]] gives the eight-consciousnesses schema its Chán-inflected late-Míng reading.

### Topics

- **Vijñaptimātra / cittamātra** (唯識 *wéishí*). The thesis that what appears as an external object is a transformation of consciousness; argued textually through Vasubandhu's *Viṃśatikā* and *Triṃśikā* and exegetically through the entire *Chéng wéishí lùn* commentarial pyramid.
- **The eight consciousnesses** (八識 *bā shí*), with particular attention to the *ālayavijñāna* (阿賴耶識) as substrate and *kliṣṭamanas* (染汚意) as the sixth-to-seventh-consciousness link. Paramārtha's idiosyncratic positing of a ninth, *amalavijñāna* 阿摩羅識, is one of the principal Old-Yogācāra peculiarities. The *Bā shí guījǔ* tradition turns the eight-consciousness schema into a Ming pedagogical genre.
- **The three natures** (三性 *sān xìng*, *trisvabhāva*): *parikalpita* (遍計所執), *paratantra* (依他起), *pariniṣpanna* (圓成實). The cross-cutting **three non-natures** (三無性) doctrine is foregrounded in Paramārtha's *Sān wúxìng lùn* [[KR6n0079]] and is the principal hermeneutic key for reconciling the Yogācāra ontology with the Prajñāpāramitā emptiness teaching.
- **Five gotras / five natures** (五姓各別 *wǔxìng gèbié*). The Yogācārabhūmi taxonomy of five fixed spiritual lineages, against which Wǒnch'ǔk and the East Asian one-vehicle traditions argue. The Cí'ēn defence of the five-gotras position is the principal polemical thread of Huìzhāo's *Néng xiǎn zhōngbiān huìrì lùn* [[KR6n0128]].
- **The Hundred Dharmas** (百法 *bǎifǎ*). Vasubandhu's 5-category × 100-item taxonomy of dharmas (T1614); becomes the standard East Asian Buddhist abhidharmic primer and generates a long commentarial chain from Kuījī's *Dàshèng bǎifǎ míngmén lùn shū* [[KR6n0098]] through the Dūnhuáng *Kāizōng yìjì* [[KR6n0107]] to the Wànlì-era revivalist commentaries [[KR6n0103]]–[[KR6n0106]].
- **Stages of the Yoga practitioner** (瑜伽師地 *yúqié shī dì*) — the seventeen-bhūmi structure of the *Yogācārabhūmi*, organising the entire division.
- **Yogācāra–tathāgatagarbha synthesis**. T1610 *Fóxìng lùn*, T1611 *Bǎoxìng lùn*, T1626–1627 *Fǎjiè wú chābié lùn*, and T1612 *Wǔyùn lùn* sit at the seam between Yogācāra and the *tathāgatagarbha* literature of KR6q; the question of whether the *tathāgatagarbha* is reducible to the *ālayavijñāna* shapes both the Old-Yogācāra reception in China and the *Awakening of Faith* tradition.
- **Epistemology of perception (所緣緣)**. Through Dignāga's *Ālambanaparīkṣā* [[KR6n0112]] and its commentaries, the question of what constitutes a valid object of perception becomes a Yogācāra theme; it is taken up again in the Wànlì–early Qīng commentary wave of [[KR6n0114]]–[[KR6n0117]].
- **Old- vs. New-Yogācāra** (舊譯 *jiùyì* / 新譯 *xīnyì*). The Paramārtha-line readings of the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* and *Triṃśikā* differ systematically from Xuánzàng's: on *amalavijñāna*, on the status of the *paratantra*, and on the relation between *vāsanā* and the *ālaya*. The division as a whole preserves both layers in parallel — the cataloguing principle is doctrinal subject matter, not translatorial school.
- **Cí'ēn school polemics**. Internal to the Táng layer: Kuījī vs. Wǒnch'ǔk on the reading of T1585; Huìzhāo's continuation of that polemic; Zhìzhōu's consolidation. These polemics define the canonical shape of the Fǎxiàng curriculum.
- **East Asian transmission and revival**. The division is the textual basis for three successive scholastic traditions: Táng Cí'ēn (mid-7th to early 8th c., centred at Cháng'ān, with Silla and Goguryeo participation), Heian–Kamakura Hossō (Vols. 65–70 of the Taishō, c. 800–1500), and late-Míng / early-Qīng Fǎxiàng revival (c. 1530–1660, *Xùzàngjīng* Vols. 46–55).

### Timeline

- 414–426 — *Púsà dìchí jīng* 菩薩地持經 [[KR6n0003]] — Dharmakṣema's translation of the *Bodhisattvabhūmi*; the earliest stratum of Yogācāra in China, predating the systematic transmission by a century.
- 431 — *Púsà shànjiè jīng* 菩薩善戒經 [[KR6n0004]] [[KR6n0005]] — Guṇavarman's second translation of the *Bodhisattvabhūmi*.
- 508–511 — *Jiūjìng yīshèng bǎoxìng lùn* 究竟一乘寶性論 [[KR6n0088]] — Ratnamati's translation of the *Ratnagotravibhāga*; brings the tathāgatagarbha synthesis into Chinese.
- 531–534 — *Shè dàshèng lùn* 攝大乘論 [[KR6n0058]] — Buddhaśānta's translation of the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*; the first systematic Yogācāra text in Chinese.
- 538–543 — *Wéishí lùn* 唯識論 [[KR6n0053]] — Prajñāruci's translation of the *Viṃśatikā*; the entry of the consciousness-only argument.
- 541 — *Yè chéngjiù lùn* 業成就論 [[KR6n0085]] — Vimokṣaprajñā's translation of Vasubandhu on action.
- 557–569 — Paramārtha's translation programme: *Juédìng zàng lùn* [[KR6n0006]], *Zhuǎn shí lùn* [[KR6n0024]], *Xiǎnshí lùn* [[KR6n0025]], *Wúxiāng sīchén lùn* [[KR6n0111]], *Jiějuǎn lùn* [[KR6n0118]], *Shíbā kōng lùn* [[KR6n0074]], *Sān wúxìng lùn* [[KR6n0079]], *Fóxìng lùn* [[KR6n0087]], *Dàshèng wéishí lùn* [[KR6n0054]] — the Old-Yogācāra layer in roughly a decade at Liáng Jiànkāng and Chén-period Guǎngzhōu.
- 563–564 — *Shè dàshèng lùn* and *Shì* [[KR6n0059]] [[KR6n0061]] — Paramārtha's *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* and *bhāṣya*; the Shèlùn school's foundational pair.
- 580–592 — *Dàshèng yìzhāng* 大乘義章 [[KR6n0122]] — Jìngyǐng Huìyuǎn's pre-Xuánzàng doctrinal encyclopaedia.
- 580–592 — *Dìchí lùn yìjì* 地持論義記 [[KR6n0013]] — Huìyuǎn's commentary on the *Bodhisattvabhūmi*.
- 605–610 — *Shè dàshèng lùn shì lùn* 攝大乘論釋論 [[KR6n0063]] — Dharmagupta's revision of the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha-bhāṣya*.
