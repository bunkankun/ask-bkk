## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR6m` 中觀部 (*Zhōngguān bù*, "Madhyamaka section") is the Kanripo mirror of the Madhyamaka — Sānlùn 三論 — section of the Chinese Buddhist canon: the Indic Mādhyamaka *śāstras* by Nāgārjuna 龍樹, Āryadeva 提婆 / 聖天, Bhāviveka 清辯, Sthiramati 安慧, Dharmapāla 護法 and others, together with the East-Asian commentarial superstructure they generated, from Sēngzhào's 僧肇 *Zhào lùn* in the early-fifth century down through the late-Ming "*Wù bù qiān*" controversy of the 1590s and the Heian–Edo Japanese Sānlùn scholasticism. In CANWWW  the division's primary translations sit in `div20` between T30N1564 and T30N1578 (interleaved with the abhidharma-period Indic *śāstras* of vol. 30); the Suí–Tang exegetical apparatus runs in `div16` 論疏部 (T1824–T1827, T1852–T1860); and the Sòng–Yuán–Míng sub-commentaries are gathered in `div28` 續論疏部. Kanripo `KR6m` integrates these three CANWWW divisions into one division of 51 catalogued works, organised around the doctrinal core of the *Sān lùn* 三論 ("Three Treatises") that Kumārajīva's Cháng'ān workshop translated in 404–409 and that became, through Jízàng 吉藏 (549–623), the textual basis of an independent Chinese-Buddhist exegetical school.

The corpus falls into six historically distinct strata. (1) The **early-fifth-century Cháng'ān translations** of Kumārajīva (鳩摩羅什, 344–413) under Hòu-Qín 後秦 patronage created the *Three Treatises* foundation: *Bǎi lùn* [[KR6m0012]] (404), *Shí'èr-mén lùn* [[KR6m0008]] (408–409) and *Zhōng lùn* [[KR6m0001]] (409), framed by the Huìyuǎn–Kumārajīva *Dàyì* correspondence [[KR6m0036]] (c. 405–413) and Sēngzhào's *Zhào lùn* [[KR6m0038]] (c. 404–414). (2) **Northern-dynasty and Northern-Liáng retranslations** brought further Indic Mādhyamaka material into Chinese: the Bodhiruci-circle *Bǎizì lùn* [[KR6m0017]], the Prajñāruci translations *Shùnzhōng lùn* [[KR6m0003]] and *Yīshūlújiā lùn* [[KR6m0018]], and the Hexi-corridor *Dàzhàngfū lùn* [[KR6m0022]] of Dào Tài 道泰. (3) The **early-seventh-century Tang renewal** added Bhāviveka's *Prajñāpradīpa* as *Bōrědēng lùn shì* [[KR6m0004]] (Prabhākaramitra, 630–632), then Xuánzàng's 玄奘 cluster of 649–650: the *Karatalaratna* as *Dàshèng zhǎngzhēn lùn* [[KR6m0023]], the Āryadeva root-text *Guǎngbǎi lùn běn* [[KR6m0014]], and Dharmapāla's *Catuḥśataka* commentary as *Dàshèng guǎngbǎi lùn shìlùn* [[KR6m0015]]. (4) The **Suí–Tang Sānlùn exegetical synthesis** under Jízàng 吉藏 produced the most coherent doctrinal treatment of the corpus in any East-Asian language — the *Sānlùn xuányì* [[KR6m0026]] (597–599), *Èrdì yì* [[KR6m0034]], *Dàshèng xuánlùn* [[KR6m0031]], and the three running sub-commentaries [[KR6m0006]] [[KR6m0009]] [[KR6m0013]]. (5) The **late-Northern-Sòng translation programme** under Dānapāla 施護 retranslated three short Nāgārjuna verse-treatises ([[KR6m0019]], [[KR6m0020]], [[KR6m0021]]), and the Wéijìng 惟淨 group rendered Sthiramati's *Madhyamakavṛtti* as *Dàshèng zhōngguān shìlùn* [[KR6m0005]] (1009–1023) — the only Chinese witness to that commentarial layer. (6) The **late-imperial Chinese reception** is dominated by the Míng-period *Zhàolùn* commentaries of Hānshān Déqīng 憨山德清 [[KR6m0046]] and by the *Wù bù qiān* polemical controversy of 1590–1617 [[KR6m0047]] [[KR6m0048]] [[KR6m0049]], the most articulate late-imperial Chinese-Buddhist case of explicit doctrinal critique of an established canonical text.

## Important texts and text clusters

### The Three Treatises (三論) and the Cháng'ān foundation

The doctrinal core of the division: three Indic Mādhyamaka *śāstras* translated at Cháng'ān 長安 in 404–409 under [[姚興]] Yáo Xìng's patronage and grouped from [[僧叡]] Sēngruì onward as a single canonical triad.

- *Zhōng lùn* 中論 [[KR6m0001]] (T1564, 409) — Kumārajīva's four-fascicle Chinese rendering of Nāgārjuna's *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* in 27 *pǐn* 品 of negative dialectic, embedded in Piṅgala's (Fànzhì Qīngmù 梵志青目) prose commentary. The principal Chinese witness to the *Mūlamadhyamaka*-commentary layer otherwise lost in Sanskrit; the locus classicus of the *sāndì jì* 三諦偈 (24.18) that became the textual foundation of Tiāntái 天台 doctrine.
- *Shí'èr-mén lùn* 十二門論 [[KR6m0008]] (T1568, 408–409) — Nāgārjuna's twelve-gate companion treatise, condensing the *Mūlamadhyamaka* method into a dozen short investigations.
- *Bǎi lùn* 百論 [[KR6m0012]] (T1569, 404) — Āryadeva's 提婆 *Hundred-Verse Treatise*, the polemical companion to the *Mūlamadhyamaka*, with the prose commentary of *Vasu* (Pósǒu 婆藪).
- *Jiūmóluóshí fǎshī dàyì* 鳩摩羅什法師大義 [[KR6m0036]] (T1856, c. 405–413) — the doctrinal correspondence between [[慧遠]] Lúshān Huìyuǎn and Kumārajīva, the principal early-fifth-century epistolary exchange on Mahāyāna doctrine; preserves Huìyuǎn's queries on *dharmakāya*, *prajñā*, and *nirmāṇakāya* with Kumārajīva's Mādhyamaka responses.

### Sēngzhào and the *Zhào lùn* tradition

The first wholly Chinese articulation of Mādhyamaka and one of the most influential Buddhist works composed in any East-Asian language. The commentarial cluster runs continuously from the Liú-Sòng to the late Míng — a transmission depth matched by only a handful of works in the entire Buddhist canon.

- *Zhào lùn* 肇論 [[KR6m0038]] (T1858, c. 404–414) — Sēngzhào's four-treatise corpus (*Wù bù qiān lùn* 物不遷論, *Bù zhēn kōng lùn* 不真空論, *Bōrě wú zhī lùn* 般若無知論, *Nièpán wú míng lùn* 涅槃無名論), the foundational Chinese-Buddhist articulation of *kōng* 空 in *xuánxué*-inflected idiom. With the pseudepigraphic *Bǎozàng lùn* 寶藏論 [[KR6m0037]] (T1857, attributed but actually Tang, c. 700–900; cf. Sharf 2002).
- The early sub-commentary tradition: [[惠達]] Huìdá's *Zhàolùn shū* [[KR6m0041]] (X866, 5th c., the earliest surviving commentary); Yuánkāng's 元康 Tang *Zhàolùn shū* [[KR6m0039]] (T1859, c. 650–680); Wéncái's 文才 Yuán *Zhàolùn xīnshū* [[KR6m0040]] (T1860, 1300–1320) with its companion *Yóurèn* [[KR6m0045]].
- The Northern-Sòng *Zūnshì–Xiǎoyuè* cluster: Zūnshì's 遵式 *Zhù Zhàolùn shū* [[KR6m0044]] (X870, 1010–1032) and *Zhàolùn shū kē* [[KR6m0042]] (X868, 1050–1100); Xiǎoyuè's 曉月 *Jiākē Zhàolùn xù zhù* [[KR6m0043]] (X869, 1050–1100).
- The late-Míng commentarial flowering: Hānshān Déqīng's 憨山德清 *Zhàolùn lüèzhù* [[KR6m0046]] (X873, 1606–1623) — the principal Chinese late-imperial reading of the *Zhào lùn* and a foundational document of the *Wànlì* Buddhist revival.

### The *Wù bù qiān* controversy (1590–1617)

A late-Míng doctrinal-philosophical controversy over the proper reading of the second treatise of the *Zhào lùn* — among the principal cases in late-imperial Chinese Buddhism of explicit critique of an established canonical text, and a striking parallel from within Buddhism to the *kǎozhèng* 考證 sensibility of contemporary Confucian scholarship.

- *Wù bù qiān zhèngliàng lùn* 物不遷正量論 [[KR6m0048]] (X879, c. 1590–1617) — Yuèchuān Zhènchéng's 月川鎮澄 (1547–1617) sustained polemical critique of Sēngzhào's *Wù bù qiān lùn*, charging that the formulation 物各性住 unwittingly adopts a substantialist position in tension with the Mahāyāna doctrine of intrinsic emptiness (*xìngkōng* 性空).
- *Wù bù qiān zhèngliàng zhèng* 物不遷正量證 [[KR6m0047]] (X878) by Dàohéng 道衡 and *Wù bù qiān lùn biànjiě* 物不遷論辯解 [[KR6m0049]] (X880) by Zhēnjiè 真界 — the principal contemporary refutations defending the orthodox reading.

### Jízàng's Sānlùn corpus and the Suí scholastic synthesis

Jízàng 吉藏 (549–623), the founder of the Sānlùn 三論 school, produced at Jiāxiángsì 嘉祥寺 the most coherent doctrinal articulation of the Three-Treatise tradition in any East-Asian language. The cluster of Suí-period works is the single most consequential body of original East-Asian Mādhyamaka thought.

- *Sānlùn xuányì* 三論玄義 [[KR6m0026]] (T1852, 597–599) — the most-studied programmatic statement of Sānlùn doctrine; the canonical introduction to the school for every subsequent commentator (with the Heian–Kamakura–Edo Japanese sub-commentary stratum [[KR6m0027]] [[KR6m0028]] [[KR6m0029]] [[KR6m0030]]).
- *Dàshèng xuánlùn* 大乘玄論 [[KR6m0031]] (T1853, 605–615) — Jízàng's mature five-fascicle synthesis of the Mahāyāna doctrinal categories.
- *Èrdì yì* 二諦義 [[KR6m0034]] (T1854, 597–615) — the principal Sānlùn treatment of the two-truths doctrine, foundational for the entire Chinese reception of Mādhyamaka.
- *Sānlùn lüèzhāng* 三論略章 [[KR6m0051]] (X876) — Jízàng's compact introduction to the *Three Treatises*.
- The three running sub-commentaries: *Zhōngguān lùn shū* 中觀論疏 [[KR6m0006]] (T1824) on the *Zhōng lùn*, *Shí'èr-mén lùn shū* 十二門論疏 [[KR6m0009]] (T1825) on the *Shí'èr-mén lùn*, and *Bǎi lùn shū* 百論疏 [[KR6m0013]] (T1827) on the *Bǎi lùn* — a unified scholastic apparatus on the entire *Three-Treatise* corpus.

### Other Indic Mādhyamaka treatises in Chinese

The wider Indic Mādhyamaka corpus, brought into Chinese in waves over six centuries:

- *Shùnzhōng lùn* 順中論 [[KR6m0003]] (T1565, 538–543, Prajñāruci 瞿曇般若流支) — Asaṅga's 無著 commentary on Nāgārjuna's *Mūlamadhyamaka*, the unique Indic case of a Yogācāra master commenting on a Mādhyamaka root text.
- *Bōrědēng lùn shì* 般若燈論釋 [[KR6m0004]] (T1566, 630–632, Prabhākaramitra 波羅頗蜜多羅) — Bhāviveka's 清辯 *Prajñāpradīpa*, the principal Svātantrika-Mādhyamaka commentary on the *Mūlamadhyamaka*; Bhāviveka appears here under his alternative Chinese name 分別明菩薩.
- *Dàshèng zhōngguān shìlùn* 大乘中觀釋論 [[KR6m0005]] (T1567, 1009–1023, [[惟淨]] Wéijìng et al.) — Sthiramati's 安慧 commentary on the *Mūlamadhyamaka*, the unique Chinese witness to that layer of Mādhyamaka exegesis (with the second part preserved only in the Tripiṭaka Koreana K1482).
- *Guǎngbǎi lùn běn* 廣百論本 [[KR6m0014]] (T1570, 650, Xuánzàng) and *Dàshèng guǎngbǎi lùn shìlùn* 大乘廣百論釋論 [[KR6m0015]] (T1571, 650, Xuánzàng) — Āryadeva's *Catuḥśataka* root verses with Dharmapāla's 護法 commentary; the principal Chinese witness to Dharmapāla's Mādhyamaka exegesis. With Wéngui's 文軌 single-fascicle Tang sub-commentary [[KR6m0016]] (T2800) preserved among the Dūnhuáng manuscripts.
- *Bǎizì lùn* 百字論 [[KR6m0017]] (T1572, 508–535, Bodhiruci 菩提流支) — a short Āryadeva treatise.
- *Yīshūlújiā lùn* 壹輸盧迦論 [[KR6m0018]] (T1573, 538–543, Prajñāruci) — a single-verse Nāgārjuna treatise (*Eka-śloka-śāstra*) with auto-commentary.
- *Dàzhàngfū lùn* 大丈夫論 [[KR6m0022]] (T1577, 414–426, Dào Tài 道泰) — *Devala*-bodhisattva's two-fascicle bodhisattva-conduct treatise, translated in Hexi during the Northern Liáng.
- *Dàshèng zhǎngzhēn lùn* 大乘掌珍論 [[KR6m0023]] (T1578, 649, Xuánzàng) — Bhāviveka's *Karatalaratna* ("Jewel in the Hand"), the unique Chinese witness to this short Svātantrika-Mādhyamaka treatise (no Tibetan parallel survives). With the Tang sub-commentary *Zhǎngzhēn lùn shū* [[KR6m0025]] (X788) and the Heian Japanese *Zhǎngzhēn liàng dào* [[KR6m0024]] (T2258).

### Dānapāla's Sòng Mādhyamaka programme

The late-Northern-Sòng *Yìjīngyuàn* 譯經院 retranslation programme under Emperor Tàizōng 太宗 added three short Nāgārjuna verse-treatises in fresh translations by Dānapāla 施護 (Shīhù):

- *Dàshèng pòyǒu lùn* 大乘破有論 [[KR6m0019]] (T1574, 982–1000) — short refutation of substance-views.
- *Liùshí sòng rúlǐ lùn* 六十頌如理論 [[KR6m0020]] (T1575) — the *Yuktiṣaṣṭikā*, one of Nāgārjuna's six core *yukti* treatises.
- *Dàshèng èrshí sòng lùn* 大乘二十頌論 [[KR6m0021]] (T1576) — short twenty-verse treatise.

### Other Suí–Tang Sānlùn material

- *Sānlùn yóuyì yì* 三論遊意義 [[KR6m0035]] (T1855, c. 580–610) — the *Qī fǎshī* 磧法師's compact pre-Jízàng Sānlùn manual; one of the few surviving non-Jízàng witnesses to early Sānlùn doctrine.
- *Dàshèng sìlùn xuányì* 大乘四論玄義 [[KR6m0050]] (X784, 620–680, by 均正 Jūnzhèng) — the Tang *Sìlùn xuányì*, expanding the Sānlùn frame to include the *Dà zhì dù lùn* as a fourth foundation; the principal Tang Sānlùn-school work outside Jízàng's corpus.

### The Heian–Kamakura–Edo Japanese Sānlùn stratum

The transmission of Sānlùn to Japan in the Nara period generated a distinct scholastic tradition that survived through to the Edo, of which the Taishō preserves a substantial corpus:

- *Zhōng lùn shū jì* 中論疏記 [[KR6m0007]] (T2255, c. 780–814) — Anchō's 安澄 sub-commentary on Jízàng's *Zhōngguān lùn shū*; the principal Heian-period Sānlùn work.
- *Shí'èrmén lùn shū wénsī jì* 十二門論疏聞思記 [[KR6m0011]] (T2257, Heian) — Zōkai's 藏海 sub-commentary on Jízàng's *Shí'èr-mén lùn shū*.
- *Zhōngguān lùn èrshíqī pǐn biéshì* 中觀論二十七品別釋 [[KR6m0002]] (T2256, 1527, Kaiken 快憲) — a Muromachi-period systematic re-treatment of the 27 *pǐn* of the *Zhōng lùn*.
- The Chinkai 珍海 (1091–1152) Heian cluster: *Sānlùn xuánshū wényì yào* [[KR6m0027]] (T2299), *Dàshèng xuán wèndá* [[KR6m0032]] (T2303), *Yīchéng yì sījì* [[KR6m0033]] (T2304).
- The later sub-commentaries on Jízàng's *Sānlùn xuányì*: Chōzen's 澄禪 *Sānlùn xuányì jiǎnyōu jí* [[KR6m0028]] (T2300, 1280, Kamakura), Teikai's 貞海 *Sānlùn xuányì chāo* [[KR6m0029]] (T2301), and Bunshō's 聞證 Edo-period *Sānlùn xuányì yòuméng* [[KR6m0030]] (T2302, 1686).

### Fǎzàng's Huayan reading of the *Shí'èr-mén lùn*

- *Shí'èr-mén lùn zōngzhì yìjì* 十二門論宗致義記 [[KR6m0010]] (T1826, 690–712, by [[法藏]] Fǎzàng) — the principal Huáyán-school reading of a Sānlùn root text; documents the late-Tang absorption of Sānlùn doctrine into the Huáyán scholastic frame after the Sānlùn school proper had ceased to be an independent institutional tradition.

## Important persons

### The Indic Mādhyamaka authors

- [[龍樹菩薩]] (Lóngshù púsà / Nāgārjuna, c. 150–250) — root author of the Mādhyamaka tradition; in `KR6m` credited with eight texts including the *Zhōng lùn* root verses [[KR6m0001]], the *Shí'èr-mén lùn* [[KR6m0008]], the *Yīshūlújiā lùn* [[KR6m0018]], the Sòng-translated *Dàshèng pòyǒu lùn* [[KR6m0019]], the *Liùshí sòng rúlǐ lùn* [[KR6m0020]], and the *Dàshèng èrshí sòng lùn* [[KR6m0021]]. The principal authorial figure of the entire division.
- [[提婆菩薩]] / [[聖天菩薩]] (Típó púsà / Shèngtiān / Āryadeva, c. 170–270) — Nāgārjuna's principal disciple; author of the *Bǎi lùn* [[KR6m0012]], *Bǎizì lùn* [[KR6m0017]], and the *Catuḥśataka* root verses [[KR6m0014]]. The *Shèngtiān* form is Xuánzàng's seventh-century re-rendering; *Típó* is the Kumārajīva-era transliteration.
- [[梵志青目]] (Fànzhì Qīngmù / Piṅgala, c. 4th c.) — author of the prose commentary on the *Mūlamadhyamaka* embedded in [[KR6m0001]]; the unique Chinese witness to this layer of early Mādhyamaka exegesis.
- [[清辯菩薩]] / [[分別明菩薩]] (Qīngbiàn / Fēnbiémíng / Bhāviveka, c. 500–570) — founder of the Svātantrika branch of Mādhyamaka; author of the *Prajñāpradīpa* (= [[KR6m0004]]) and the *Karatalaratna* (= [[KR6m0023]]). The two Chinese names are alternative renderings of the same Sanskrit name.
- [[護法菩薩]] (Hùfǎ púsà / Dharmapāla, c. 530–561) — Yogācāra master at Nālandā; author of the *Catuḥśataka* commentary [[KR6m0015]], in which his characteristic Yogācāra doctrinal positions are read into the Mādhyamaka root text.
- [[安慧菩薩]] (Ānhuì púsà / Sthiramati, c. 510–570) — Yogācāra master; author of the *Madhyamakavṛtti* preserved only in Chinese as [[KR6m0005]].
- [[無著菩薩]] (Wúzhuó púsà / Asaṅga, c. 4th c.) — founder of the Yogācāra school; author of the unusual Mādhyamaka commentary preserved as *Shùnzhōng lùn* [[KR6m0003]].
- [[婆藪開士]] (Pósǒu kāishì / Vasu, c. 4th c.) — author of the prose commentary on the *Bǎi lùn* embedded in [[KR6m0012]].
- [[提婆羅菩薩]] (Tipóluó / Devala, c. 4th–5th c.) — author of the *Dàzhàngfū lùn* [[KR6m0022]].

### The translators

- [[鳩摩羅什]] (Jiūmóluóshí / Kumārajīva, 344–413) — the most consequential Buddhist translator in Chinese history; created the *Three Treatises* foundation of the division at Cháng'ān in 404–409 ([[KR6m0001]] [[KR6m0008]] [[KR6m0012]]) and conducted the *Dàyì* correspondence with Huìyuǎn ([[KR6m0036]]).
- [[菩提流支]] (Pútíliúzhī / Bodhiruci, fl. 508–535) and [[瞿曇般若流支]] (Qútán Bōrěliúzhī / Prajñāruci, fl. 538–543) — the two principal Northern-dynasty translators of Mādhyamaka material ([[KR6m0017]], [[KR6m0003]], [[KR6m0018]]). The two are sometimes confused in the catalog tradition; they are distinct individuals.
- [[道泰]] (Dào Tài, fl. 414–426) — Northern-Liáng translator of [[KR6m0022]]; one of the principal Hexi-corridor translators of the early fifth century.
- [[波羅頗蜜多羅]] (Bōluópōmìduōluó / Prabhākaramitra, 565–633) — early-Tang translator; rendered Bhāviveka's *Prajñāpradīpa* as [[KR6m0004]] in 630–632 at Cháng'ān.
- [[玄奘]] (Xuánzàng, 602–664) — translated three Mādhyamaka treatises in 649–650 immediately following his return from India ([[KR6m0014]] [[KR6m0015]] [[KR6m0023]]); the principal Tang witness to Bhāviveka and Dharmapāla.
- [[施護]] (Shīhù / Dānapāla, fl. 980–1017) — Indian translator-monk at the Sòng *Yìjīngyuàn*; rendered three short Nāgārjuna verse-treatises ([[KR6m0019]] [[KR6m0020]] [[KR6m0021]]).
- [[惟淨]] (Wéijìng, c. 973–1051) — Chinese imperial-translator at the *Yìjīngyuàn*; led the Sòng team that rendered Sthiramati's commentary as [[KR6m0005]].

### The Chinese Sānlùn masters

- [[僧肇]] (Sēngzhào, 374–414) — Kumārajīva's principal Cháng'ān disciple; author of the *Zhào lùn* [[KR6m0038]], the foundational Chinese Mādhyamaka work and the most-commented-on Chinese-Buddhist treatise after the *Awakening of Faith*. Pseudo-attributed author of the *Bǎozàng lùn* [[KR6m0037]].
- [[慧遠]] (Huìyuǎn of Lúshān 廬山, 334–416) — early-Eastern-Jìn Buddhist patriarch at Lúshān; questioner in the Kumārajīva *Dàyì* correspondence [[KR6m0036]].
- [[吉藏]] (Jízàng / Jiāxiáng dàshī 嘉祥大師, 549–623) — founder of the Sānlùn school as an independent doctrinal tradition; author of seven works in `KR6m` ([[KR6m0006]] [[KR6m0009]] [[KR6m0013]] [[KR6m0026]] [[KR6m0031]] [[KR6m0034]] [[KR6m0051]]) including the *Sānlùn xuányì* and the three running sub-commentaries on the *Three Treatises*. The single most consequential exegetical figure in the division.
- [[磧法師]] (Qī fǎshī, fl. c. 580–610) — pre-Jízàng Suí-period Sānlùn teacher; author of [[KR6m0035]].
- [[均正]] (Jūnzhèng, 7th c.) — Tang Sānlùn-school master; author of [[KR6m0050]] *Dàshèng sìlùn xuányì*.
- [[元康]] (Yuánkāng, fl. c. 650–680) — Tang scholar-monk; the principal Tang commentator on the *Zhào lùn* ([[KR6m0039]]).
- [[文軌]] (Wéngui, fl. c. 650–700) — Tang sub-commentator on the *Catuḥśataka*; author of [[KR6m0016]] preserved among the Dūnhuáng manuscripts.

## Topics

- **The *Three Treatises* as a canonical triad.** The grouping of *Zhōng lùn*, *Bǎi lùn*, and *Shí'èr-mén lùn* as a single canonical corpus (三論) is a Chinese — not Indian — formation: it originates with [[僧叡]] Sēngruì's preface to the *Zhōng lùn* and is consolidated by Jízàng. The Indian Mādhyamaka tradition itself recognises no such triad. The doctrinal-architectural decision to read Nāgārjuna and Āryadeva as a single canonical voice, mediated through Kumārajīva's translation idiom, is the foundational move of East-Asian Mādhyamaka.
- **The Sānlùn school as institutional tradition.** Whether Sānlùn 三論 was an independent Buddhist "school" (*zōng* 宗) in any robust institutional sense is one of the running historiographic questions of East-Asian Buddhology. The institutional thread runs from Sēngzhào and Sēngruì through [[僧朗]] Sēnglǎng, [[僧詮]] Sēngquán, and [[法朗]] Fǎlǎng to its high point in Jízàng (549–623), after which it ceased to exist as an independent Chinese-Buddhist tradition — though it survived in Japan as one of the six Nara schools.
- **The two truths (二諦).** The Sānlùn doctrine of *èrdì* — *súdì* 俗諦 (conventional truth) and *zhēndì* 真諦 (ultimate truth) — is the principal doctrinal vehicle of Chinese Mādhyamaka and the subject of Jízàng's most extensive single treatment in [[KR6m0034]]. The Sānlùn formulation insists that the two truths are not parallel descriptions of two ontological levels but rather two pedagogical registers for a single non-dual reality.
- **The eight negations (八不).** The Mādhyamaka formula of "neither arising nor ceasing, neither permanent nor annihilated, neither one nor different, neither coming nor going" 不生不滅不常不斷不一不異不來不去, drawn from the dedicatory verses of the *Zhōng lùn*, became in the Sānlùn tradition the condensed doctrinal formula of the *zhōngdào* 中道 ("middle way") and the principal mnemonic of Mādhyamaka instruction in East Asia.
- **Mādhyamaka–Yogācāra relations.** The presence in `KR6m` of three Yogācāra-authored commentaries on Mādhyamaka root texts — Asaṅga on the *Mūlamadhyamaka* ([[KR6m0003]]), Sthiramati on the same ([[KR6m0005]]), and Dharmapāla on the *Catuḥśataka* ([[KR6m0015]]) — documents the Indic and Sino-Indic dialectic between the two great Mahāyāna scholastic traditions. The Tang doctrinal controversy between the Sānlùn / Tiāntái Mādhyamaka reading and the Cí'ēn 慈恩 Yogācāra reading is one of the central debates of Tang Buddhism.
- **Svātantrika versus Prāsaṅgika.** The Indian Mādhyamaka split between Bhāviveka's *svatantra* (positive-syllogistic) method and Buddhapālita's / Candrakīrti's *prasaṅga* (purely-refutative) method enters Chinese only through Bhāviveka — Buddhapālita and Candrakīrti are unknown to the Chinese tradition, the *Prasannapadā* never having been translated. The Chinese reception of Mādhyamaka is therefore implicitly a Svātantrika tradition, and the Tibetan-school distinction is not a live category in the East Asian sources.
- **Kumārajīva's translation idiom.** The doctrinal-philosophical vocabulary that Kumārajīva forged at Cháng'ān in 404–409 to render Indic Mādhyamaka — *kōng* 空 for *śūnya*, *jiǎ* 假 for *prajñapti*, *zhōng* 中 for *madhyama*, *èrdì* 二諦 for *satya-dvaya* — became the operative Buddhist Chinese throughout East Asia and shaped the conceptual vocabulary of every subsequent Chinese-Buddhist tradition, Mādhyamaka or otherwise.
- **The Huìyuǎn–Kumārajīva correspondence.** The *Dàyì* [[KR6m0036]] preserves the principal early-fifth-century epistolary witness to the doctrinal questions a Chinese Buddhist of the late Eastern Jin would raise about Mahāyāna — the nature of the *dharmakāya*, the operation of *prajñā*, the post-mortem persistence of awareness — and to Kumārajīva's Mādhyamaka responses. The text is the foundational document for the early-Chinese-Buddhist intellectual encounter with Indian Mādhyamaka.
- **Sēngzhào and *xuánxué*.** Sēngzhào's *Zhào lùn* [[KR6m0038]] is the principal case in early Chinese Buddhism of the deliberate use of *xuánxué* 玄學 idiom and Lao-Zhuang vocabulary to render Mādhyamaka doctrine. The interpretive question of whether this constitutes successful translation, problematic syncretism, or genuine doctrinal innovation runs from Liebenthal (1948, 1968) and Robinson (1967) through the present.
- **The *Wù bù qiān* controversy.** The 1590–1617 polemical engagement of Zhènchéng with Sēngzhào ([[KR6m0048]] [[KR6m0047]] [[KR6m0049]]) is one of the principal cases in late-imperial Chinese Buddhism of explicit doctrinal critique of an established canonical text — a stance more familiar from late-imperial Confucian *kǎozhèng* 考證 scholarship than from the conventional Buddhist commentarial tradition, and a significant marker of late-Ming Buddhist intellectual sophistication.
- **The Sòng retranslation programme.** Dānapāla's late-Northern-Sòng translations ([[KR6m0019]]–[[KR6m0021]]) document the imperial-court attempt under Tàizōng and Zhēnzōng to systematically retranslate the late-Indic Buddhist treatise corpus into Chinese — a programme that gave Chinese its only versions of several core Nāgārjuna verse-treatises (the *Yuktiṣaṣṭikā* in particular) but never displaced the Kumārajīva translations as the operative basis of Chinese Mādhyamaka.
- **Pseudepigraphic transmission.** The *Bǎozàng lùn* [[KR6m0037]] is a Tang-period pseudepigraphic *śāstra* canonised under Sēngzhào's name and transmitted as authentic for over a millennium. Sharf 2002 has shown its importance for the formation of pre-Sòng Chan literature, and the work is a case-study in the way the East-Asian canon absorbed and re-attributed later texts to early-Buddhist authorities.
- **East-Asian transmission.** Sānlùn was carried to Japan by [[慧灌]] Hyegwan in the early seventh century and became one of the six Nara schools (*Sanron-shū* 三論宗); the Japanese reception is documented in `KR6m` by the Heian–Kamakura–Edo commentarial stratum ([[KR6m0007]] [[KR6m0011]] [[KR6m0027]]–[[KR6m0030]] [[KR6m0032]] [[KR6m0033]] [[KR6m0002]]), which preserves a continuous interpretive tradition of Jízàng's Suí-period scholarship into the Edo period.
