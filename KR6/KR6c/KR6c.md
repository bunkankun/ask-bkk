# Bōrě bù 般若部 — Prajñāpāramitā literature

## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR6c` is the Kanripo mirror of the Taishō *Bōrě bù* 般若部 — the canonical Buddhist division gathering every Chinese translation of the Indic *Prajñāpāramitā* sūtra-corpus together with the major Indic *śāstra*-commentaries on it and the bulk of the East-Asian exegetical literature it generated. In CANWWW the division is `div02` (般若部), running from `T05N0220` (Xuánzàng's 600-fascicle *Dà bōrě bōluómìduō jīng*) through `T08N0261` and the related *śāstra*-cluster in `T25`. The Kanripo `KR6c` directory expands the Taishō *Bōrě bù* by adding the very large Wàn xùzàng 卍續藏 (X) commentarial corpus, so that 229 works are catalogued here in total — by some margin the largest single sūtra-class division in the entire Chinese Buddhist canon.

The chronological span is roughly 179 CE (Lokakṣema's *Dào-xíng bōrě* [[KR6c0010]], the earliest surviving Chinese rendering of an *Aṣṭasāhasrikā* recension and one of the earliest substantial Mahāyāna translations into Chinese) to the late nineteenth century (a long tail of Qīng-era *Jīngāng jīng* and *Xīnjīng* commentaries). The textual core is Indic: a small handful of Sanskrit *Prajñāpāramitā*-sūtras retranslated repeatedly across seven centuries, attended by an Indic *śāstra*-stratum (Nāgārjuna's *Dàzhìdù lùn*, Asaṅga's and Vasubandhu's Yogācāra commentaries on the *Vajracchedikā*) that mediates the sūtras to scholastic readers. Around this Indic core grew an enormous East-Asian commentarial superstructure — Suí-Táng Sānlùn and Yogācāra scholastic *shū* 疏, Tiāntái doctrinal classification, Sòng Tiāntái and Huáyán *jì* 記, Míng-Qīng Cáodòng and Chán philosophical readings, Japanese Shingon esoteric commentaries — making the division also the principal arena in which every major exegetical school of pre-modern East-Asian Buddhism articulated its reading of emptiness.

## Important texts and text clusters

### The large Prajñāpāramitā recensions

- *Dàbōrě bōluómìduō jīng* 大般若波羅蜜多經 [[KR6c0001]] — Xuánzàng's 600-fascicle integrated translation (660–663) of sixteen *huì* 會 (assemblies) covering the full Indic *prajñā*-corpus from the *Śatasāhasrikā* (100,000-verse) down to the *Vajracchedikā* and *Hṛdaya*. The single most extensive text in the Chinese Buddhist canon and the canonical reference-recension after the seventh century.
- *Móhē bōrě bōluómì jīng* 摩訶般若波羅蜜經 [[KR6c0004]] — Kumārajīva's 27-fascicle rendering of the *Pañcaviṃśatisāhasrikā* (the "Larger Prajñāpāramitā", 25,000-verse). Up to Xuánzàng the standard Chinese form of the large *prajñā* and the base-text of Nāgārjuna's *Dàzhìdù lùn*.
- *Fàngguāng bōrě jīng* 放光般若經 [[KR6c0002]] and *Guāngzàn jīng* 光讚經 [[KR6c0003]] — the two Xī-Jìn (286 / 291) renderings of the *Pañcaviṃśatisāhasrikā* by Mokṣala 無羅叉 and Dharmarakṣa 竺法護 respectively; foundational documents of early Chinese Mahāyāna and the textual base for the *gé-yì* 格義 exegesis of the third and fourth centuries.
- *Dàoxíng bōrě jīng* 道行般若經 [[KR6c0010]] — Lokakṣema's 179 CE translation of the *Aṣṭasāhasrikā* (8,000-verse Prajñāpāramitā), the earliest surviving Chinese Prajñāpāramitā text and a key witness for the linguistic state of Late-Hàn Buddhist Chinese.
- *Xiǎopǐn bōrě bōluómì jīng* 小品般若波羅蜜經 [[KR6c0013]] — Kumārajīva's 408 retranslation of the *Aṣṭasāhasrikā*, the standard "Smaller Prajñāpāramitā" of the medieval Chinese tradition.
- *Dàmíngdù jīng* 大明度經 [[KR6c0011]], *Móhē bōrě chāo jīng* 摩訶般若鈔經 [[KR6c0012]], *Shèngtiānwáng bōrě bōluómì jīng* 勝天王般若波羅蜜經 [[KR6c0019]] — further independent Wú, Qián-Qín, and Chén-era renderings of medium and large *prajñā* recensions, valued in subsequent tradition as comparative witnesses.

### The Mahāprajñāpāramitāśāstra cluster

- *Dàzhìdù lùn* 大智度論 [[KR6c0005]] — the 100-fascicle Mahāyāna *śāstra* attributed in the Chinese tradition to Nāgārjuna 龍樹 and translated by Kumārajīva in 402–405. Commentary on the *Pañcaviṃśatisāhasrikā* [[KR6c0004]] but in practice an encyclopaedic compendium of pre-Yogācāra Mahāyāna doctrine; the principal Chinese source for the Sānlùn 三論 tradition and an irreplaceable doctrinal substrate for Tiāntái, Huáyán, and Chán.
- A small but doctrinally significant secondary commentarial cluster grew up around the *Dàzhìdù lùn*: [[KR6c0006]] *Dàzhìdù lùn shū* by Huìyǐng 慧影 (the only surviving substantive Chinese commentary on the work), [[KR6c0007]] *Dàpǐn jīng yóuyì* by Jízàng 吉藏, [[KR6c0008]] *Dà huìdù jīng zōngyào* by the Silla master Wǒnhyo 元曉, and [[KR6c0009]] *Dàpǐn jīng yìshū* (again Jízàng).

### The Vajracchedikā (Diamond Sūtra) cluster

The single largest cluster in the division, comprising six translations, an Indic-śāstra layer, and an exegetical tradition running to the end of the Qīng dynasty:

- Six Chinese translations: Kumārajīva's *Jīngāng bōrě bōluómì jīng* [[KR6c0023]] (the canonical liturgical form), Bodhiruci's [[KR6c0024]], Paramārtha's [[KR6c0026]], Dharmagupta's *Jīngāng néngduàn* [[KR6c0027]], Xuánzàng's *Néngduàn jīngāng bōrě bōluómìduō jīng* [[KR6c0029]] (also embedded as the ninth *huì* of [[KR6c0001]]), and Yijing's *Fóshuō néngduàn jīngāng* [[KR6c0028]].
- Indic Yogācāra-school *śāstra*-tradition: Asaṅga's *Jīngāng bōrě lùn* [[KR6c0030]] (T1510), Vasubandhu's *Jīngāng bōrě bōluómì jīng lùn* in Bodhiruci's [[KR6c0032]] (T1511) and Yijing's [[KR6c0031]] translations, and the *Jīngāngxiān lùn* [[KR6c0033]] (T1512). Yijing also translated Vasubandhu's *Lùnshì* and *Lùnsòng* [[KR6c0034]] [[KR6c0035]], and the Mādhyamika-school *Pò qǔzhuó bùhuài jiǎmíng lùn* of Gunada 功德施 survives as [[KR6c0036]].
- Major Suí-Táng commentaries: Zhìyǐ 智顗's *Jīngāng bōrě jīng shū* [[KR6c0037]], Jízàng's *Jīngāng bōrě shū* [[KR6c0038]], Kuījī 窺基's *Zànshù* [[KR6c0039]] and *Luèshū* [[KR6c0044]], Tang Xuān-zōng's imperial preface-and-commentary [[KR6c0100]] with Dào-yīn 道氤's *Xuānyǎn* [[KR6c0105]], the Huáyán-school *Zuǎnyào* [[KR6c0041]] of Zōngmì 宗密 with Zǐxuán 子璿's *Kāndìng jì* [[KR6c0042]], and the prosimetric *Fù Dàshì sòng* [[KR6c0104]] traditionally attributed to Fù Xī 傅翕 (the *Liáng* Buddhist layman 497–569).
- A continuous Sòng–Yuán–Míng–Qīng Chinese-commentarial corpus of nearly seventy further works (KR6c0040–KR6c0099, KR6c0116) covering every major school: Tiāntái *cǎiwēi* studies of Zǐxuán-school inheritor Zōngjìng 宗鏡 [[KR6c0052]] [[KR6c0053]], Yuán-era *kēyí* ritual exegesis (the *Xiāoshì Jīngāng jīng kēyí* [[KR6c0055]]), late-Míng philosophical readings by Hānshān Déqīng 憨山德清 [[KR6c0062]], Zēng-fèng 曾鳳儀 [[KR6c0059]], and Zhìxù 智旭 [[KR6c0067]] [[KR6c0068]], and a still-larger Qīng commentary tradition.
- Japanese commentary: Kūkai 空海's *Jīngāng bānruò bōluómì jīng kāití* [[KR6c0101]] — the foundational Shingon reading of the *Vajracchedikā*.

### The Heart Sūtra (Hṛdaya) cluster

The second great cluster — roughly seventy-five works (KR6c0127–KR6c0201) covering every Chinese translation of the *Prajñāpāramitā-hṛdaya* and the major Chinese, Korean, and Japanese commentarial tradition:

- Chinese translations: Kumārajīva's *Móhē bānruò bōluómì dàmíng zhòu jīng* [[KR6c0127]] (T250, the earliest surviving form), Xuánzàng's canonical *Bānruò bōluómìduō xīnjīng* [[KR6c0128]] (T251, 649 — the standard liturgical text of East-Asian Buddhism), Dharmacandra 法月's *Pǔbiàn zhìzàng* [[KR6c0129]], Prajñā 般若 and Lìyán 利言's long-recension [[KR6c0130]], and the Sòng-era expansions by Fǎyuè 法月 (T254 / [[KR6c0131]]), Fǎchéng 法成 (T255 / [[KR6c0132]], translated from Tibetan), and Dānapāla 施護 (T257 / [[KR6c0136]]). Sanskrit-transliterated witnesses survive as [[KR6c0133]] (Bùkōng) and [[KR6c0134]] [[KR6c0135]].
- Major Tang commentaries: Kuījī 窺基's *Yōuzàn* [[KR6c0137]], Wǒnch'ǔk 圓測's *Zàn* [[KR6c0138]], Fǎzàng 法藏's *Lüèshū* [[KR6c0139]], Jìngmài 靖邁's *Shū* [[KR6c0142]], the *Huányuán shù* [[KR6c0197]] and the *Xiézhù* [[KR6c0198]]. Sòng *zhāng-shū* tradition: Shīhù 師護 [[KR6c0140]], Zhīyuán 智圓 [[KR6c0148]] [[KR6c0150]].
- A very large Míng–Qīng commentary stratum (KR6c0151–KR6c0195) representing every late-imperial school: Línjì-Chán, Tiāntái, Cáodòng (Hánshì 圅昰's philosophically substantial *Bōrě xīnjīng lùn* [[KR6c0184]]; Yuánxián 元賢, Hónglì 弘麗, Hóngzàn 弘贊's *Tiānzú* and *Guànyì*), and gentry Confucian-Buddhist syncretists.
- Japanese commentary: Kūkai's *Bānruò xīnjīng mìjiàn* [[KR6c0199]] — foundational Shingon Heart-Sūtra reading — with two later sub-commentaries [[KR6c0200]] [[KR6c0201]].

### The Renwáng-jīng (Humane King Sūtra) cluster

- Two translations: T245 [[KR6c0202]] (canonically attributed to Kumārajīva but most likely a late-fifth- or sixth-century Chinese composition; see Orzech 1998) and Amoghavajra's revised T246 *Rénwáng hùguó bōrě bōluómìduō jīng* [[KR6c0203]] (765–766) which became the canonical text used in the Tang and Heian *Rénwáng-huì* 仁王會 state-protective ritual programme.
- Principal commentaries: Tiāntái Zhìyǐ 智顗 (recorded by Guàndǐng 灌頂)'s *Hùguó bōrě jīng shū* [[KR6c0204]] with the Sòng sub-commentary *Shénbǎo jì* [[KR6c0205]]; Jízàng's *Rénwáng bōrě jīng shū* [[KR6c0206]]; Wǒnch'ǔk's *Rénwáng jīng shū* [[KR6c0207]]; and — most consequentially for state Buddhism — Liángbì 良賁's *Rénwáng hùguó bōrě bōluómìduō jīng shū* [[KR6c0208]] (composed at Amoghavajra's request shortly after T246 and the canonical Tang esoteric reading of the sūtra). Ming-Qing recensions in [[KR6c0209]]–[[KR6c0215]] document the continuing imperial use of the work; Kūkai's *Rénwáng jīng kāití* [[KR6c0218]] is the Shingon counterpart.

### The Adhyardhaśatikā / Lǐqù-jīng cluster

The *Adhyardhaśatikā Prajñāpāramitā* (the *Lǐqù-jīng* 理趣經) is the only *prajñā* sūtra whose canonical East-Asian reception is straightforwardly Tantric. Four Chinese renderings document its progressive Tantricisation: Bodhiruci II's *Shíxiāng bānruò bōluómì jīng* [[KR6c0117]] (Mahāyāna register), Vajrabodhi 金剛智's *Jīngāngdǐng yújiā lǐqù bānruò jīng* [[KR6c0118]] (explicit *Vajraśekhara*-affiliation), Amoghavajra 不空's *Dàlè jīngāng bùkōng zhēnshí sānmóyē jīng* [[KR6c0120]] (the canonical Tang and Japanese *Lǐqù-jīng*, the doctrinal substrate of Shingon practice), and the Sòng *Biànzhào bānruò bōluómì jīng* [[KR6c0119]]. The Sòng *Zuìshàng gēnběn dàlè* expansion is [[KR6c0125]]. A small Shingon commentarial cluster — Kūkai's *Lǐqù jīng kāití* [[KR6c0121]] and *Zhēnshí jīng wénjù* [[KR6c0122]], Kakuban-circle *Zhǒngzǐ shì* [[KR6c0123]], the *Dàlè jīng xiǎnyì chāo* [[KR6c0124]] — preserves its Heian-Kamakura reception.

### Wisdom-Mother (Fómǔ) and short-prajñā Sòng translations

A late-Northern-Sòng cluster (980–1018), produced under imperial patronage by the Indian translators Tiānxīzāi 天息災, Fǎxián 法賢, and especially Dānapāla 施護, brings into the canon the late-Indic Pāla-era *Prajñāpāramitā* short-recension and tantric-prajñā literature: the *Fómǔ chūshēng sānfǎzàng* [[KR6c0014]] (the Sòng *Aṣṭasāhasrikā* recension), the *Fómǔ bǎodézàng* [[KR6c0015]] (= *Ratnaguṇasaṃcayagāthā*), the late-Indic *śāstra*-pair [[KR6c0017]] [[KR6c0018]] (*Triśatikāyāḥ Prajñāpāramitāyāḥ kārikāsaptati* of Asaṅga / Vasubandhu), the *Jiǔsòng jīngyì lùn* [[KR6c0016]], and a sequence of short Tantric-prajñā texts [[KR6c0219]]–[[KR6c0225]] (the *Yībǎibā míng tuóluóní jīng*, *Liǎoyì jīng*, *Wǔshísòng jīng*, *Dìshì bōrě xīn jīng*, *Xiǎozì bōrě bōluómìduō jīng*, *Guānxiǎng fómǔ púsà jīng*, *Kāijué zìxìng jīng*). Together they document the last continuous wave of Indic *prajñā* literature in Chinese.

### Late synthetic and Mañjuśrī-related works

- *Wénshūshīlì suǒshuō Móhē bōrě bōluómì jīng* [[KR6c0020]] and *Wénshūshīlì suǒshuō bōrě bōluómì jīng* [[KR6c0021]] — Liáng-era translations of the *Saptaśatikā Prajñāpāramitā*, attaching the *prajñā*-tradition to the Mañjuśrī cult.
- *Dàshèng lǐqù liù bōluómìduō jīng* 大乘理趣六波羅蜜多經 [[KR6c0226]] — Prajñā 般若's late-Táng (788–798) translation of a *prajñā*-frame six-pāramitā synthesis, an important late-Tang doctrinal source.
- *Dà bōrě jīng guānfǎ* [[KR6c0227]], *Dà bōrě jīng gāngyào* [[KR6c0228]], *Dà bōrě guān* [[KR6c0229]] — Sòng and Qīng synthetic / structural guides to T220 [[KR6c0001]], representing the long East-Asian project of making the 600-fascicle *Mahāprajñāpāramitā* navigable.

## Important persons

### Translators

- [[支婁迦讖]] (Lokakṣema, fl. 168–189) — Late-Hàn pioneer; *Dàoxíng bōrě jīng* [[KR6c0010]], the earliest substantial Mahāyāna text in Chinese.
- [[支謙]] (Zhī Qiān, fl. 220–253) — Wú-era translator; *Dàmíngdù jīng* [[KR6c0011]].
- [[竺法護]] (Dharmarakṣa, c. 233 – c. 310) — Xī-Jìn translator; *Guāngzàn jīng* [[KR6c0003]].
- [[無羅叉]] (Mokṣala, fl. 291) — *Fàngguāng bōrě jīng* [[KR6c0002]].
- [[鳩摩羅什]] (Kumārajīva, 344–413) — The single most important translator in the division: *Móhē bōrě bōluómì jīng* [[KR6c0004]], *Xiǎopǐn bōrě* [[KR6c0013]], *Dàzhìdù lùn* [[KR6c0005]], *Jīngāng bōrě bōluómì jīng* [[KR6c0023]], *Móhē bānruò bōluómì dàmíng zhòu jīng* [[KR6c0127]], the attributed *Rénwáng-jīng* [[KR6c0202]].
- [[菩提流支]] (Bodhiruci, fl. 508–537) — Yuán-Wèi Yogācāra translator; *Jīngāng bōrě bōluómì jīng* [[KR6c0024]] [[KR6c0025]] and Vasubandhu's *Jīngāng bōrě lùn* [[KR6c0032]].
- [[真諦]] (Paramārtha, 499–569) — Chén-era Yogācāra translator; *Jīngāng bōrě bōluómì jīng* [[KR6c0026]].
- [[達摩笈多]] (Dharmagupta, d. 619) — Suí translator; *Jīngāng néngduàn bōrě bōluómì jīng* [[KR6c0027]].
- [[玄奘]] (Xuánzàng, 602–664) — The other pivotal translator of the division: *Dàbōrě bōluómìduō jīng* [[KR6c0001]], the canonical *Bānruò bōluómìduō xīnjīng* [[KR6c0128]], *Néngduàn jīngāng bōrě bōluómìduō jīng* [[KR6c0029]], the Lùnshì and Lùnsòng [[KR6c0034]] [[KR6c0035]] of Vasubandhu.
- [[義淨]] (Yijing, 635–713) — *Fóshuō néngduàn jīngāng bōrě bōluómìduō jīng* [[KR6c0028]] and Vasubandhu's *Lùn* in his recension [[KR6c0031]].
- [[金剛智]] (Vajrabodhi, 671–741) — Tang Tantric translator; *Jīngāngdǐng yújiā lǐqù bānruò jīng* [[KR6c0118]].
- [[不空]] (Amoghavajra, 705–774) — The mid-Tang esoteric architect; canonical *Lǐqù-jīng* [[KR6c0120]] and the revised *Rénwáng-jīng* [[KR6c0203]].
- [[般若]] (Prajñā, fl. 781–810) — Late-Tang Indian translator; long-recension *Heart Sūtra* [[KR6c0130]] and *Dàshèng lǐqù liù bōluómìduō jīng* [[KR6c0226]].
- [[施護]] (Dānapāla, fl. 980–1017) — Northern-Sòng imperial translator; the principal architect of the late-Indic prajñā cluster [[KR6c0014]] [[KR6c0136]] [[KR6c0219]]–[[KR6c0224]].
- [[法賢]] (Fǎxián, d. 1001) and [[法天]] (Fǎtiān, d. 1001) — Dānapāla's Sòng colleagues, responsible for further pieces of the same programme.
- [[法成]] (Fǎchéng, c. 755–c. 849) — Tibetan-Chinese Sino-Tibetan translator working at Dūnhuáng; T255 [[KR6c0132]].

## Topics

- **Śūnyatā 空 and prajñāpāramitā 般若波羅蜜多.** The division is the canonical home of the Mahāyāna doctrine of universal emptiness — every *dharma* is empty of an intrinsic nature (*svabhāva*) — and of the *bodhisattva*-soteriology by which the realisation of emptiness through *prajñā* effects the perfection of all six *pāramitā*s. Every text in the division addresses this doctrine in some register, from the encyclopaedic exposition of the *Dàzhìdù lùn* [[KR6c0005]] through the four-corner negation of the *Vajracchedikā* [[KR6c0023]] to the 260-character compression of the *Heart Sūtra* [[KR6c0128]].
- **Form and emptiness (sè-kōng 色空).** The *Heart Sūtra* formula *sè jí shì kōng, kōng jí shì sè* — "form is itself emptiness, emptiness is itself form" — is the most-cited single sentence in East-Asian Buddhism and the focus of an enormous commentarial debate, with virtually every Heart-Sūtra commentary in KR6c0137–KR6c0201 staking a doctrinal position on the relation between the two terms (Yogācāra *vijñaptimātra*, Sānlùn two-truths, Huáyán *lǐ-shì*, Tiāntái *yī-niàn sān-qiān*, Chán *jiàn-xìng*, Cáodòng *substance-function*).
- **The relation of sūtra to śāstra.** The presence in the division of the Indic *śāstra*-stratum — Nāgārjuna's *Dàzhìdù lùn*, Asaṅga's and Vasubandhu's *Jīngāng bōrě lùn*-cluster — sets up a perennial East-Asian exegetical question about the proper relation between *prajñā*-sūtra and Mādhyamika or Yogācāra *śāstra*-commentary. Sānlùn 三論, Fǎxiàng 法相, and Huáyán 華嚴 commentaries each take a distinctive position; the disagreement runs through the Tang and recurs in Sòng-Míng.
- **Vinayana of the bodhisattva-path.** The *Dàzhìdù lùn* in particular provides the most extensive surviving Indic-source account of the *bodhisattva* career — six *pāramitā*s, ten *bhūmis*, the *anuttarasamyaksaṃbodhi* — and is the textual base for medieval Chinese *bodhisattva*-doctrine.
- **State Buddhism.** The *Rénwáng-jīng* [[KR6c0202]] [[KR6c0203]] is one of the three royal state-protective scriptures (with the *Suvarṇaprabhāsa* and the *Mahāmāyūrī*) and the canonical text of an enormous Tang-Heian-Kamakura state-Buddhist political theology, with Liángbì's [[KR6c0208]] as its principal scholastic commentary.
- **Tantricisation of the prajñā tradition.** The progressive Tantric reinterpretation of the *Adhyardhaśatikā* through Vajrabodhi [[KR6c0118]] and Amoghavajra [[KR6c0120]], the embedding of *dhāraṇī* within the Heart Sūtra in Prajñā's recension [[KR6c0130]], and the late-Indic Sòng cluster [[KR6c0219]]–[[KR6c0224]] together document the gradual incorporation of *prajñā* into the Indic and East-Asian Tantric programme. The Heian Shingon reception (Kūkai's commentaries) gives this tradition its definitive East-Asian articulation.
- **The Mahāprajñāpāramitā as canonical reference work.** The 600-fascicle *Dàbōrě bōluómìduō jīng* [[KR6c0001]] sits at the centre of the division as a single integrated witness to the Indic *prajñā*-corpus, but its sheer scale generates a continuing problem of accessibility: how does one read 600 fascicles? The Sòng-Qīng synthetic guides [[KR6c0227]] [[KR6c0228]] [[KR6c0229]] are the formal record of the East-Asian project of making the great sūtra usable.
