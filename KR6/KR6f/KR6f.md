# Bǎojī bù 寶積部 — Ratnakūṭa Section

## Scope and scholarly tradition

`KR6f` is the Kanripo mirror of the Taishō *Bǎojī bù* 寶積部 — the canonical Buddhist division that gathers the *Mahā-ratnakūṭa* "Heap of Jewels" compendium together with the parallel and independent Chinese translations of the sūtras absorbed into it, plus the Sukhāvatī / Pure-Land sūtra corpus that the Taishō editors classed in the same volume-pair, plus the entire East-Asian commentarial superstructure that grew out of both. In CANWWW the division is `div03` (`div03.xml`, `寶積部`); it runs from `T11N0310` Bodhiruci's 120-fascicle compendium through `T12N0373` and is followed in the Taishō architecture by the *Nièpán bù* 涅槃部 (div19, T374–T396, which is grouped in Kanripo as `KR6g`). `KR6f` extends the Taishō *Bǎojī bù* by absorbing the related *śāstra* and *upadeśa* corpus from Taishō volumes 26 (T1523–T1525) and 40 (T1819), the entire *jīngshūbù* commentarial cluster on the Sukhāvatī and Śrīmālā sūtras from Taishō volume 37 (T1744–T1762), the Japanese Pure-Land sub-commentaries from volume 57 (T2208), the Dūnhuáng fragments from volume 85 (T2759–T2763), and a small *Xùzàngjīng* 卍續藏經 tail (X264, X351–X353) — 104 works in total.

The chronological span runs from the late-second-century translations of Lokakṣema 支婁迦讖 ([[KR6f0004]] *Akṣobhyatathāgatasya-vyūha* of c. 178–189, [[KR6f0042]] *Kāśyapa-parivarta* of c. 178–189) and An Shigao 安世高 ([[KR6f0040]], [[KR6f0048]], c. 148–170) through to the mid-seventeenth-century Ming Pure-Land synthesis of Zhìxù 智旭 ([[KR6f0093]] *Ēmítuójīng yàojiě* of c. 1620–1655), with a thin late tail of Kamakura-period Japanese sub-commentaries on the *Guānjīngshū* extending the run to the early fourteenth century. The textual core of the division is Indic and the principal transmissional traditions a reader must keep in mind are four: (i) the Hàn–Wú southern lineage of Lokakṣema, Zhī Qiān 支謙 and Saṅghavarman 康僧鎧 that produced the earliest Chinese renderings of the *Akṣobhya-vyūha*, the *Kāśyapa-parivarta* and the *Larger Sukhāvatī*; (ii) the Western-Jìn Cháng'ān lineage of Dharmarakṣa 竺法護, by some margin the most productive single translator in the division (twelve attributed works, from [[KR6f0006]] and [[KR6f0009]] through [[KR6f0041]]); (iii) the Yáo-Qín / Liú-Sòng lineage of Kumārajīva 鳩摩羅什, Guṇabhadra 求那跋陀羅 and Kālayaśas 畺良耶舍, which gave the canonical Chinese forms of the *Smaller Sukhāvatī*, the *Śrīmālā-sūtra*, and the *Visualisation-of-Amitāyus*; and (iv) the Northern-Sòng *Yìjīngyuàn* 譯經院 of Shīhù 施護, Fǎhù 法護 (the Northern-Sòng Dharmapāla, distinct from the Western-Jìn Dharmarakṣa), Fǎxián 法賢 and Rìchēng 日稱, which produced a long final stratum of retranslations of *Ratnakūṭa* assemblies (T310 assemblies 6, 35, 36, 39, 47 all retranslated 980–1058). Around this Indic core grew the East-Asian commentarial layers: the Suí *Dìlùn*-school readings of Jìngyǐng Huìyuǎn 淨影慧遠 and the early Sānlùn readings of Jízàng 吉藏; the foundational Tiāntái Pure-Land works of Zhìyǐ 智顗; the great Táng Pure-Land patriarchal corpus of Shàndǎo 善導, Huìjìng 慧淨, Kuījī 窺基 and Cí'ēn 慈恩; the Sillan tradition of Wǒnhyo 元曉 and Jǐngxīng 璟興; the Northern-Sòng Tiāntái synthesis of Zhīlǐ 知禮 and Yuánzhào 元照; and a Kamakura Japanese sub-commentarial tradition centred on Shàndǎo's *Four-Fascicle Commentary* (源信, 良忠, 顯意, 證忍). The division is thus the principal canonical arena for the constitution of East-Asian Pure-Land Buddhism as a doctrinal and ritual tradition, while simultaneously housing the *tathāgatagarbha*-class *Śrīmālā* literature and the most ambitious editorial-translational project of the Táng — Bodhiruci II's 120-fascicle assembly of the *Mahā-ratnakūṭa*.

## Important texts and text clusters

### The Mahā-ratnakūṭa compendium and its independent assemblies

- *Dà bǎojī jīng* 大寶積經 [[KR6f0001]] — Bodhiruci II 菩提流志's 120-fascicle compendium of 49 *huì* 會 (assemblies), assembled at the *Chóngfúsì* 崇福寺 in Cháng'ān 706–713 under imperial patronage of Zhōngzōng 中宗 and Ruìzōng 睿宗; some assemblies are Bodhiruci's new translations, others are re-edited Dharmarakṣa, Zhī Qiān, Xuánzàng or Yìjìng translations incorporated into the larger compendium. The work is one of the three great Mahāyāna sūtra-compendia of the East-Asian canon alongside the [[KR6e0001|*Avataṃsaka*]] and the *Mahāprajñāpāramitā*, and the textual core of `KR6f`.
- **Independent translations of *Ratnakūṭa* assemblies**. T311–T359 (here [[KR6f0002]]–[[KR6f0051]], with [[KR6f0060]]–[[KR6f0064]] for the Sukhāvatī line) are the freestanding parallel and predecessor translations of the 49 assemblies, transmitted independently before and alongside Bodhiruci's compilation. Reading any individual assembly in T310 against its independent Chinese parallels is the standard philological method for the corpus: e.g. T310 assembly 5 (*Larger Sukhāvatī*) has at least five extant Chinese parallels [[KR6f0060]]–[[KR6f0064]]; assembly 48 (*Śrīmālā*) is [[KR6f0045]]; assembly 43 (*Kāśyapa-parivarta*) is [[KR6f0042]] (Lokakṣema), [[KR6f0043]] (anonymous Western-Jìn), [[KR6f0044]] (Shīhù).

### The Akṣobhya, Kāśyapa-parivarta and Bodhisattvapiṭaka clusters

- *Ākṣobhya-tathāgatasya-vyūha* — [[KR6f0004]] *Ēchù fóguó jīng* 阿閦佛國經 (Lokakṣema, c. 178–189); the foundational Chinese statement of the Akṣobhya / *Abhirati* Pure-Land model, parallel to but earlier than the Sukhāvatī cycle.
- *Kāśyapa-parivarta* — [[KR6f0042]] *Fó shuō yírì móní bǎo jīng* 佛說遺日摩尼寶經 (Lokakṣema, c. 178–189), [[KR6f0043]] *Móhēyǎn bǎoyán jīng* 摩訶衍寶嚴經 (anonymous Western-Jìn), [[KR6f0044]] *Dà-Jiāyè wèn Dàbǎojī zhèngfǎ jīng* 大迦葉問大寶積正法經 (Shīhù, Northern Sòng) — three Chinese versions of the same Indic text spanning eight centuries, a key witness for Mahāyāna textual evolution and the source of the *Ratnakūṭa* corpus's name.
- *Bodhisattvapiṭaka* — [[KR6f0008]] *Fó shuō Dàshèng pútísàzàng zhèngfǎ jīng* 佛說大乘菩薩藏正法經 (Fǎhù 法護, Northern-Sòng, 1004–1058) and the closely-related [[KR6f0014]] *Fǎjìng jīng* 法鏡經 (Ān Xuán 安玄, c. 181–188) and [[KR6f0015]] *Yùjiā Luóyuè wèn púsàxíng jīng* 郁迦羅越問菩薩行經 (Dharmarakṣa) — the *Ugraparipṛcchā* / *Bodhisattva-vinaya* literature that defined the early-Mahāyāna laity's path.

### The Śrīmālā cluster

- *Shèngmán shīzǐhǒu yīchéng dàfāngbiàn fāngguǎng jīng* 勝鬘師子吼一乘大方便方廣經 [[KR6f0045]] — Guṇabhadra 求那跋陀羅's Liú-Sòng translation (435–443) of the *Śrīmālādevī-siṃhanāda-sūtra*, one of the principal Mahāyāna *tathāgatagarbha* sūtras and the foundation of mature East-Asian Buddha-nature theology; later incorporated into the *Mahā-ratnakūṭa* as assembly 48.
- **Śrīmālā commentaries**. The Suí–Táng subcommentarial cluster [[KR6f0052]]–[[KR6f0059]]: Jízàng's *Shèngmán bǎokū* [[KR6f0052]], the *Shèngmánjīng yìshū* [[KR6f0053]] attributed to Shōtoku Taishi 聖德太子 (the foundational Japanese Buddhist commentary, alongside his Lotus and *Vimalakīrti* *shū*), the *Shèngmánjīng yìjì* [[KR6f0057]] of Jìngyǐng Huìyuǎn 淨影慧遠, the *Shèngmánjīng shùjì* [[KR6f0058]] of Cí'ēn-school Kuījī 窺基 / Yìlìng 義令, and the Japanese-Tang *Shèngmánjīngshū yì sīchāo* [[KR6f0059]] of Míngkōng 明空.

### The Sukhāvatī / Pure-Land sūtra corpus

- **The Larger *Sukhāvatī-vyūha*** (Taishō's "five existing translations" cluster). [[KR6f0060]] *Wúliàngshòu jīng* 無量壽經 (Saṅghavarman 康僧鎧 by traditional attribution, 252 — modern scholarship questions the attribution but it is preserved here as canonical); [[KR6f0061]] *Wúliàng qīngjìng píngděngjué jīng* 無量清淨平等覺經 (attrib. Lokakṣema, c. 178–189); [[KR6f0062]] *Ēmítuó sānyé sānfó sàlóufótán guòdùréndào jīng* (Zhī Qiān, 222–253); [[KR6f0063]] *Dàshèng wúliàngshòu zhuāngyán jīng* (Fǎxián 法賢, Northern Sòng); [[KR6f0064]] *Dà Ēmítuó jīng* (Wáng Rìxiū 王日休's 1160 collated edition). The fivefold Chinese transmission is the most exhaustive multi-version corpus for any single Indic sūtra and the principal data-base for the modern philological reconstruction of the *Sukhāvatī*-textual-history.
- **The *Visualization Sūtra*** — [[KR6f0071]] *Guān wúliàngshòu fó jīng* 觀無量壽佛經 (Kālayaśas 畺良耶舍, Liú-Sòng, 424–442); the source of the *Sixteen Visualisations* (十六觀) and *Nine Grades of Rebirth* (九品往生) doctrines that became foundational for all later Pure-Land soteriology. Modern scholarship has raised the question of Central-Asian or Chinese composition (no Sanskrit original survives); the traditional Indic-translation attribution is preserved.
- **The Smaller *Sukhāvatī-vyūha*** — [[KR6f0082]] *Ēmítuó jīng* 阿彌陀經 (Kumārajīva, Yáo-Qín, 402–412), the single most widely-recited Buddhist text in East Asia, and its Táng retranslation [[KR6f0085]] *Chēngzàn jìngtǔ fó shèshòu jīng* 稱讚淨土佛攝受經 (Xuánzàng, 645–664).
- **Pure-Land *dhāraṇī* and short ritual texts** — [[KR6f0094]]–[[KR6f0099]]: the *báyīqiè yèzhàng* rebirth-*dhāraṇī*, anonymous *Ēmítuófó shuōzhòu*, the *Ēmítuó gǔyīnshēngwáng* *dhāraṇī-sūtra*, [[KR6f0097]] *Guānshìyīn púsà shòujì jīng* (Dharmodgata, Liú-Sòng 420–479), [[KR6f0098]] *Rúhuàn sānmèi wúliàngyìn fǎmén jīng* (Shīhù, Northern Sòng) and the anonymous *Hòuchū Ēmítuófó jì* — the ritual-liturgical apparatus that grew up around the three principal sūtras.

### Pure-Land sūtra commentaries

- ***Larger Sukhāvatī* commentaries** — [[KR6f0065]] Huìyuǎn (Jìngyǐng) *Wúliàngshòujīng yìshū* (Suí, 580–592); [[KR6f0066]] Jízàng *Wúliàngshòujīng yìshū* (Suí, 595–623); [[KR6f0067]] Wǒnhyo 元曉 *Liǎngjuǎn Wúliàngshòujīng zōngyào* (Silla, 660–686); [[KR6f0068]] Jǐngxīng 璟興 *Wúliàngshòujīng liányì shùwénzàn* (Silla, 670–700); and the Dūnhuáng-fragment [[KR6f0069]] *Wúliàngshòujīng yìjì juànxià*.
- ***Visualization-Sūtra* commentaries** — [[KR6f0072]] Huìyuǎn (Suí); [[KR6f0073]] Zhìyǐ's *Guān wúliàngshòu fó jīng shū*, the foundational Tiāntái Pure-Land reading; [[KR6f0074]] Zhīlǐ 知禮's *Miàozōngchāo* (Northern Sòng, 1020–1028), the canonical Tiāntái subcommentary that defined the Sòng "Mountain-School" (山家) Pure-Land position; [[KR6f0075]] Jízàng (Sānlùn, Suí–Táng); [[KR6f0076]] **Shàndǎo 善導's *Four-Fascicle Commentary*** (660–681) — the most consequential single Pure-Land commentary in East-Asian history, the doctrinal foundation of Japanese Pure-Land Buddhism via Hōnen 法然 and Shinran 親鸞; [[KR6f0077]] Yuánzhào 元照 (Northern Sòng); and the Dūnhuáng fragment [[KR6f0070]].
- **Japanese sub-commentaries on Shàndǎo's *Four-Fascicle Commentary*** — [[KR6f0078]] *Jìngtǔ yíduān* (Kenni 顯意, 1283), [[KR6f0079]] *Guānjīngyì xiánwèn yúdáchāo* (Shōnin 證忍, 1285), [[KR6f0080]] *Guānjīngyì zhuōyí qiǎodá yánhéchāo* (Kenni, 1285–1304), and [[KR6f0081]] *Guānjīngshū chuántōngjì* (Ryōchū 良忠, 1257–1275); the Japanese exegetical layer that mediated Shàndǎo to medieval Kamakura Pure-Land sectarianism.
- ***Smaller Sukhāvatī* commentaries** — [[KR6f0086]] Zhìyǐ; [[KR6f0087]] Huìjìng 慧淨 (Táng); [[KR6f0088]] Kuījī 窺基 *Ēmítuójīng shū*; [[KR6f0089]] Kuījī *Tōngzàn shū*; [[KR6f0090]] Wǒnhyo (Silla); [[KR6f0091]] Zhìyuán 智圓 (Northern Sòng); [[KR6f0092]] Yuánzhào (Northern Sòng); [[KR6f0093]] **Zhìxù 智旭 *Ēmítuójīng yàojiě*** (Ming, 1620–1655), the canonical Ming Pure-Land commentary.
- **Pure-Land scripture-lexicon and ritual apparatus** — [[KR6f0083]] Shinzui 信瑞 *Jìngtǔ sānbùjīng yīnyì jí* (Japan, 1248), the standard glossary of the *Three Pure-Land Sutras*; [[KR6f0084]] Genshin 源信 *Ēmítuójīng lüèjì* (Japan, 1014), early Tendai Pure-Land synthesis.

### The Sukhāvatī treatise lineage

- *Wúliàngshòujīng yōubōtíshě* 無量壽經優波提舍 [[KR6f0100]] — Vasubandhu 婆藪槃豆's *Sukhāvatī-vyūhopadeśa*, translated by Bodhiruci I 菩提流支 (Northern-Wèi, 529–535); the foundational *śāstra* of East-Asian Pure-Land Buddhism, source of the *Wǔniànmén* 五念門 ("Five-Fold Recollection").
- *Wúliàngshòujīng yōupótíshě yuànshēngjì zhù* 無量壽經優婆提舍願生偈註 [[KR6f0101]] — **Tánluán 曇鸞's *Wǎngshēnglùn zhù* 往生論註** (Northern-Wèi, 530–542); the foundational doctrinal commentary of East-Asian Pure-Land Buddhism, source of the *zìlì / tālì* 自力／他力 ("self-power / other-power") distinction and the principal ancestor of the Tánluán → Dàochuò 道綽 → Shàndǎo → Hōnen → Shinran patriarchal lineage.

### Ratnakūṭa-class commentaries

- [[KR6f0102]] *Dàbǎojījīng lùn* 大寶積經論 (Bodhiruci I, Northern Wèi, 508–537) — Indian *śāstra* commentary on the *Ratnakūṭa* core.
- [[KR6f0103]] *Mílèpúsà suǒwèn jīng lùn* 彌勒菩薩所問經論 (Bodhiruci I) — Maitreya-paripṛcchā *śāstra*.
- [[KR6f0104]] *Dàbǎojījīng shù* 大寶積經述 (Xú È 徐鍔, Táng, 706–720) — court-official's "Account" of Bodhiruci II's compendium and its translation programme, the principal contemporary record.

## Important persons

- [[菩提流志]] (Bodhiruci II / Pútíliúzhì, Táng) — South-Indian Brahmin convert and translator; principal compiler of [[KR6f0001]] the 120-fascicle *Mahā-ratnakūṭa* (706–713); also responsible for [[KR6f0011]] (Mañjuśrī's Buddha-land), [[KR6f0028]] (Sumati assembly), [[KR6f0032]] (Mañjuśrī's Inconceivable Buddha-realm). The most consequential Buddhist editorial figure of the Táng.
- [[菩提流支]] (Bodhiruci I, Northern Wèi, fl. 508–537) — Indian translator at the Northern Wèi court of Luòyáng; translator of [[KR6f0100]] Vasubandhu's *Sukhāvatī-vyūhopadeśa*, [[KR6f0102]] *Mahā-ratnakūṭa-śāstra*, and [[KR6f0103]] *Maitreya-paripṛcchā-śāstra* — the Indian-treatise nucleus of the division. Not to be confused with the homophonous Tang Bodhiruci II.
- [[曇鸞]] (Tánluán, 476–542) — Northern-Wèi monk, the foundational Pure-Land doctrinal author through [[KR6f0101]] *Wǎngshēnglùn zhù*; first articulator of the *zìlì / tālì* distinction; first patriarch in the standard Chinese Pure-Land patriarchal lineage.
- [[竺法護]] (Dharmarakṣa / Zhú Fǎhù, c. 230–c. 316) — Yuèzhī-descent Western-Jìn translator at Dūnhuáng and Cháng'ān; twelve works in `KR6f` ([[KR6f0006]], [[KR6f0007]], [[KR6f0009]], [[KR6f0010]], [[KR6f0015]], [[KR6f0016]], [[KR6f0026]], [[KR6f0029]], [[KR6f0030]], [[KR6f0034]], [[KR6f0035]], [[KR6f0037]], [[KR6f0041]]), the largest single attribution and the principal vector of *Ratnakūṭa* assembly material into pre-Kumārajīva Chinese.
- [[支婁迦讖]] (Lokakṣema, Later Hàn, c. 167–186) — earliest Kushan / Yuèzhī translator at Luòyáng; produced [[KR6f0004]] *Akṣobhya-vyūha* and [[KR6f0042]] *Kāśyapa-parivarta*, the oldest stratum of Mahāyāna sūtra-literature surviving in Chinese.
- [[安世高]] (An Shigao, Later Hàn, fl. 148–170) — Parthian translator at Luòyáng; produced [[KR6f0040]] *Dàshèng fāngděng yàohuì jīng* and [[KR6f0048]] *Bǎojī sānmèi Wénshūshīlì púsà wèn fǎshēn jīng*, the earliest precisely-datable Mahāyāna materials in the division (despite the school-affiliational complexity of An Shigao's larger output).
- [[鳩摩羅什]] (Kumārajīva, 344–413) — translator of [[KR6f0082]] the *Smaller Sukhāvatī* and [[KR6f0027]] the Sumati assembly; through the *Ēmítuójīng* he is the principal vector of Pure-Land devotional liturgy throughout East Asia.
- [[康僧鎧]] (Saṅghavarman, Cáo-Wèi, fl. mid-3rd c.) — translator (by traditional attribution) of [[KR6f0060]] the *Larger Sukhāvatī*, the foundational scripture of East-Asian Pure-Land Buddhism. Modern scholarship questions the attribution (some scholars assign the translation to Buddhabhadra or Bǎoyún), but it is preserved as canonical here.
- [[支謙]] (Zhī Qiān, Sān-Guó Wú, fl. 222–253) — translator of [[KR6f0062]] *Ēmítuó sānyé sānfó* — one of the five extant Chinese *Larger Sukhāvatī* translations and the earliest dated.
- [[畺良耶舍]] (Kālayaśas, Liú-Sòng, fl. 424–442) — Central-Asian monk-translator; translator of [[KR6f0071]] the *Visualization-of-Amitāyus Sūtra*, the source of the *Sixteen Visualisations* and *Nine Grades of Rebirth*.
- [[求那跋陀羅]] (Guṇabhadra, 394–468) — Central-Indian Liú-Sòng translator; translator of [[KR6f0045]] the *Śrīmālādevī-siṃhanāda-sūtra*, the foundational *tathāgatagarbha* sūtra. Also responsible for [[KR6f0094]] the rebirth-*dhāraṇī*.
- [[婆藪槃豆菩薩]] (Vasubandhu) — Indian *śāstra*-author; the foundational treatise [[KR6f0100]] *Sukhāvatī-vyūhopadeśa* attributed to him (Indian-school attribution questioned in modern scholarship but preserved as canonical) is the doctrinal anchor of the entire Pure-Land *śāstra* tradition.
- [[善導]] (Shàndǎo, 613–681) — third Pure-Land patriarch; [[KR6f0076]] his *Four-Fascicle Commentary* on the *Visualization Sūtra* is the most consequential Pure-Land commentary in East-Asian history; the doctrinal foundation of the entire Japanese Pure-Land tradition via Hōnen.
- [[智顗]] (Zhìyǐ, 538–597) — Tiāntái 天台 founder; commentator on both the *Smaller Sukhāvatī* [[KR6f0086]] and the *Visualization Sūtra* [[KR6f0073]], establishing the Tiāntái school's Pure-Land reading and the framework within which Zhīlǐ's [[KR6f0074]] Sòng synthesis was elaborated.
- [[慧遠]] (Jìngyǐng Huìyuǎn 淨影慧遠, 523–592) — Suí *Dìlùn*-school exegete (not to be confused with the Lúshān Huìyuǎn 廬山慧遠 of the Eastern Jìn); author of [[KR6f0057]] *Shèngmánjīng yìjì*, [[KR6f0065]] *Wúliàngshòujīng yìshū*, [[KR6f0072]] *Guān wúliàngshòu fó jīng yìshū* — the foundational Suí scholastic readings of the central sūtras.
- [[吉藏]] (Jízàng, 549–623) — Sānlùn 三論 master at Cháng'ān; [[KR6f0052]] *Shèngmán bǎokū*, [[KR6f0066]] *Wúliàngshòujīng yìshū*, [[KR6f0075]] *Guān wúliàngshòu jīng yìshū* — the Sānlùn alternative to the *Dìlùn* / Tiāntái synthesis.
- [[元曉]] (Wǒnhyo, 617–686) — Sillan master; [[KR6f0067]] *Liǎngjuǎn Wúliàngshòujīng zōngyào* and [[KR6f0090]] *Ēmítuójīng shū* — the principal Korean Pure-Land contributions.
- [[璟興]] (Jǐngxīng / Gyǒnghǔng, Silla, fl. 670–700) — Sillan exegete; [[KR6f0068]] *Wúliàngshòujīng liányì shùwénzàn*.
- [[窺基]] (Kuījī, 632–682) — Cí'ēn 慈恩-school *Faxiang* / Yogācāra patriarch; [[KR6f0058]] *Shèngmánjīng shùjì*, [[KR6f0088]] *Ēmítuójīng shū*, [[KR6f0089]] *Tōngzàn shū* — the Faxiang reading of the principal sūtras.
- [[玄奘]] (Xuánzàng, 602–664) — Táng pilgrim-translator; [[KR6f0085]] his Tang-period retranslation of the *Smaller Sukhāvatī*, alternative to Kumārajīva's canonical version.
- [[不空]] (Amoghavajra, 705–774) — Esoteric-school translator; [[KR6f0011]] *Dàshèng Wénshūshīlì púsà fóchà gōngdé zhuāngyán jīng* and [[KR6f0018]] *Sānshíwǔfó míng lǐchàn wén* — the Esoteric layer in the division.
- [[施護]] (Shīhù / Dānapāla, Northern Sòng, fl. 980–1017) — Northern-Sòng *Yìjīngyuàn* translator; six works ([[KR6f0013]], [[KR6f0023]], [[KR6f0038]], [[KR6f0044]], [[KR6f0098]] and others) — the principal Sòng-period vector of *Ratnakūṭa* assembly retranslation.

## Topics

- **Pure-Land soteriology.** The corpus is the principal canonical foundation of East-Asian Pure-Land Buddhism. Four overlapping textual strata generate it: the sūtra-core (the *Three Pure-Land Sutras* [[KR6f0060]], [[KR6f0071]], [[KR6f0082]]); the *śāstra* layer ([[KR6f0100]]–[[KR6f0101]]); the great Chinese commentaries (Zhìyǐ, Huìyuǎn, Jízàng, Shàndǎo, Zhīlǐ, Yuánzhào, Zhìxù); and the Japanese sub-commentarial extension (Genshin, Hōnen's masters Ryōchū, Kenni, Shōnin). The doctrinal apparatus they articulate — the *Forty-Eight Vows* of Dharmākara, the *Sixteen Visualisations*, the *Nine Grades of Rebirth*, the *Five-Fold Recollection*, the *zìlì / tālì* distinction, the *Original Vow* (本願) doctrine — is the single most influential body of Mahāyāna soteriological thought in East Asia.
- **The *Mahā-ratnakūṭa* as cosmic compendium.** Bodhiruci II's 120-fascicle [[KR6f0001]] is one of three great Mahāyāna sūtra-compendia (alongside the [[KR6e0001|*Avataṃsaka*]] and the *Mahāprajñāpāramitā*); each presents itself as an overarching architectural scheme — 49 assemblies, 9 assemblies, 16 assemblies respectively — within which the diversity of Mahāyāna doctrine is to be read as a unified whole. The 49-assembly *Ratnakūṭa* scheme is the most heterogeneous of the three and the most editorially constructed (Bodhiruci combining new translations with re-edited older ones), making the question of the compendium's coherence an interpretive problem central to the division.
- **The *Larger Sukhāvatī* textual problem.** Five extant Chinese translations of the same Indic recension ([[KR6f0060]]–[[KR6f0064]]) plus a Sanskrit witness plus a Tibetan version — the most exhaustively-transmitted single Mahāyāna sūtra in any tradition, and the principal philological laboratory for the comparative study of Chinese Buddhist translation. The Fujita Kōtatsu / Inagaki / Gómez / Schopen line of modern scholarship reads the variants as evidence of textual evolution; the East-Asian devotional tradition reads them as parallel canonical witnesses.
- **The *tathāgatagarbha* through the *Śrīmālā*.** [[KR6f0045]] and its subcommentarial cluster [[KR6f0052]]–[[KR6f0059]] are one of the principal canonical pathways for the entry of *tathāgatagarbha* doctrine into East-Asian Buddhism, parallel to but distinct from the more philosophically-technical entry via the *Laṅkāvatāra* and the *Ratnagotra-vibhāga*. The dialogic form (Queen Śrīmālā as principal female *bodhisattva*-speaker) and the relative accessibility of the prose have made it canonically important for women's Buddhist practice in East Asia.
- **The Lokakṣema stratum and the dating of the earliest Chinese Mahāyāna.** [[KR6f0004]] *Akṣobhya-vyūha* and [[KR6f0042]] *Kāśyapa-parivarta* are among the very earliest precisely-datable Mahāyāna sūtras in any language. Nattier's redatings of Lokakṣema's corpus (1990, 2003, 2008) and Harrison's parallel work have made the Hàn-period stratum of `KR6f` a central locus for the modern philological reconstruction of nascent Mahāyāna.
- **Translation, retranslation, and editorial compilation.** The division's transmission history exhibits every major mode of Chinese Buddhist text-production: solo translation under foreign masters (Lokakṣema, Saṅghavarman, Kumārajīva), team translation under Indian guidance and Chinese editorship (Dharmarakṣa, Guṇabhadra, Xuánzàng), large-scale editorial compilation of pre-existing material (Bodhiruci II's *Mahā-ratnakūṭa*, Wáng Rìxiū's *Dà Ēmítuó jīng* [[KR6f0064]]), institutional state-sponsored translation (the Northern-Sòng *Yìjīngyuàn* of Shīhù and Fǎhù), and the Chinese-composition / Central-Asian-recension problem (the *Visualization Sūtra* [[KR6f0071]]). Reading the corpus is therefore also reading the methodological diversity of Chinese Buddhist textual production across nine centuries.
- **Cross-cultural transmission to Korea and Japan.** Sillan Wǒnhyo's and Jǐngxīng's Pure-Land commentaries [[KR6f0067]], [[KR6f0068]], [[KR6f0090]], the Japanese-attributed *Shèngmánjīng yìshū* of Shōtoku [[KR6f0053]], Genshin's *Lüèjì* [[KR6f0084]], Shinzui's *Yīnyì jí* [[KR6f0083]], and the Kamakura Pure-Land subcommentaries [[KR6f0078]]–[[KR6f0081]] make this division the principal canonical site for the Korea- and Japan-ward transmission of East-Asian Pure-Land Buddhism. Shàndǎo's [[KR6f0076]] *Four-Fascicle Commentary* in particular is the single text without which medieval Japanese Pure-Land Buddhism (Hōnen 法然 → Shinran 親鸞) is unintelligible.
- **Tiāntái versus Faxiang versus Sānlùn readings.** The same principal sūtras are read in `KR6f` by Tiāntái (Zhìyǐ, Zhīlǐ), Faxiang / Cí'ēn (Kuījī), and Sānlùn (Jízàng) — making the division a microcosm of the Suí–Táng scholastic schools' divergent readings of identical canonical material. The Tiāntái synthesis through Zhīlǐ's [[KR6f0074]] *Miàozōngchāo* becomes the dominant East-Asian reading by the Sòng.
- **Pseudepigraphy and attribution problems.** Several principal works in the division carry attributions challenged by modern scholarship: the Saṅghavarman *Larger Sukhāvatī* [[KR6f0060]], the Kālayaśas *Visualization Sūtra* [[KR6f0071]] (Chinese-composition hypothesis), Vasubandhu's *Upadeśa* [[KR6f0100]] (Indian-school authorial uncertainty), Shōtoku Taishi's *Shèngmánjīng yìshū* [[KR6f0053]]. The traditional attributions are preserved as canonical but the philological problems are flagged in the per-text Abstracts.
