# Huángdì jiàngdàn rì yú Líndédiàn jiǎng Dà fāngguǎng fó huáyán jīng xuán yì yī bù 皇帝降誕日於麟德殿講大方廣佛華嚴經玄義一部
*A Lecture on the Profound Meaning of the Great, Vast Buddha-Flower-Garland Scripture, Delivered at the Lín-dé Hall on the Day of the Emperor's Birth*
by [[靜居]] Jìngjū (撰)

## About the work

This one-fascicle text is a court Buddhist lecture (*jiǎng* 講) delivered by the otherwise obscure Tang monk [[靜居]] Jìngjū of the Dà-ān-guó-sì 大安國寺 at the Lín-dé Hall of the Dà-míng-gōng 大明宮 (the imperial palace complex of the late Tang) on the occasion of an unspecified Tang emperor's birthday. The work is set out as a structured doctrinal sermon on the *Huáyán jīng*'s overall structure: the seven places, nine assemblies, 39 chapters across 80 fascicles. Each chapter is briefly summarised, with the cosmic setting, principal interlocutors, and main doctrinal theme. The whole functions as a compact lay-introduction to the *Avataṃsaka*, but addressed to the imperial audience.

### Prefaces

The work has no separate preface; it opens with the title-line and Jìngjū's signature: "皇帝降誕日於麟德殿講大方廣佛華嚴經玄義一部 / 大安國寺沙門靜居謹上" ("Lecture on the Profound Meaning of the *Dà fāngguǎng fó huáyán jīng*, delivered at the Lín-dé Hall on the day of the emperor's birth, in one fascicle. Respectfully presented by the *śramaṇa* Jìngjū of the Dà-ān-guó Monastery"). The text begins immediately: "This sūtra has 7 places, 9 assemblies, 39 chapters, [in] 80 fascicles. The first assembly at the *bodhi-maṇḍa* contains 6 chapters: (1) the *Shìzhǔ miào yán pǐn* in 5 fascicles; (2) the *Rúláixiàn xiàng pǐn* in 1 fascicle; (3) the *Pǔ-xián sānmèi pǐn*; (4) the *Shìjiè chéngjiù pǐn*, two chapters together in 1 fascicle; (5) the *Huā-zàng shìjiè pǐn* in 3 fascicles; (6) the *Pílúzhēnà pǐn* in 1 fascicle. The second assembly at the *Pǔ-guāng-míng diàn* contains 6 chapters [namely] the *Rúlái...*" — and so on through the entire sūtra.

### Abstract

The dating bracket adopted here (859 – 888) is based on the style of the work, the reference to the Líndédiàn 麟德殿 (which functioned as a court lecture-hall in the late Tang, particularly under Yìzōng and Xīzōng), and the institutional setting of the Dàānguósì 大安國寺 — an imperial-foundation monastery active throughout the late Tang and a frequent venue for court Buddhist lectures. No closer dating is internally attested. The text is one of a small number of court-Buddhist *jiǎng* lectures preserved as independent works (most others are referenced only in the *Sòng gāosēng zhuàn* and the *Tang huì yào* 唐會要 without surviving in primary form).

The work's significance is twofold. First, it documents the Tang imperial practice of *huáng-dì jiàng-dàn* 皇帝降誕 ("Emperor's Birth Day") court Buddhist services — a ritual genre attested also in some of [[澄觀]] Chéngguān's writings — in which Buddhist masters delivered lectures to the assembled court on auspicious imperial occasions. Second, it preserves a compact summary of the doctrinal structure of the *[[KR6e0010|new (80-fascicle) Huáyán]]* from a perspective intermediate between the elaborate Huáyán-school scholasticism (Fǎzàng / Chéngguān) and the lay-philosophical reading of [[李通玄]] Lǐ Tōngxuán: a court-pedagogical reading that integrated the basic structural-doctrinal apparatus of the school's mature commentary tradition into an accessible sermon-format. The Taishō text is established on the Korean *Tripiṭaka Koreana* (麗) collated against the *yuán* 原 (original-block) witness.

## Other points of interest

- The Líndédiàn 麟德殿 was one of the principal halls of the Dàmínggōng palace complex, used for major court ceremonies and audiences; its use as a venue for Buddhist lectures attests to the continuing — if increasingly contested — imperial sponsorship of Buddhism in the late Tang, despite the great Huìchāng 會昌 persecution of 845.
- The work's *yī bù* 一部 ("in one *bù*", i.e. comprising one assembly / unit) in the title is a standard formula of Tang imperial-record bibliographic notation.
