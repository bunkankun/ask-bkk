# Shí dì jīng lùn 十地經論
*Treatise on the Daśabhūmika-sūtra* (Skt. *Daśabhūmika-sūtra-vyākhyāna* / *Daśabhūmika-vibhāṣā*)
by [[天親菩薩]] (Vasubandhu, 造) and [[菩提流支]] (Bodhiruci, *et al.*, 等譯)

## About the work

The *Shí dì jīng lùn* in 12 fascicles is [[天親菩薩]] Vasubandhu's great commentary on the *Daśabhūmika-sūtra* — the foundational *śāstra* of the Yogācāra-Mahāyāna interpretation of the bodhisattva path-stages — translated into Chinese by [[菩提流支]] Bodhiruci and his Northern-Wèi-period collaborators in 508 – 511 CE. The work is the founding text of the Chinese *Dìlùn* 地論 school (so named after this *Lùn* / 論 / *śāstra*); through its disciple [[慧光]] Huìguāng's lineage it became the doctrinal precursor to the Sui-Tang Huáyán 華嚴 synthesis.

### Prefaces

The Taishō print is preceded by a substantial preface by [[崔光]] Cuī Guāng (450–523), the senior Northern Wèi court official and a leading lay Buddhist scholar of his time. The preface — one of the great preface-pieces of the Northern-Dynasties Chinese Buddhist tradition — is one of the principal contemporary witnesses to the institutional context of the translation.

In summary, Cuī Guāng's preface argues: (i) The *Shí dì jīng* 十地經 is "the profound park of spiritual awakening, the wondrous dwelling of the numinous wisdom; the ground-and-vehicle of a hundred-million good [practices], the framework of the myriad measures [of the Path]; in principle it embraces the secret of the multitudinous treasuries, in meaning it crowns the depths of the various scriptures." (ii) The treatise was authored by the Indian *mahāsattva* Vasubandhu (Skt. *Vasubandhu*; transliterated as 婆藪槃豆 *Pósǒu pándòu*; rendered into Chinese as 天親 *Tiānqīn*, "Heavenly-Kin"), described as "tracing his elevation in the era of *images* (*xiàng* 像, the second of the three-period Buddhological scheme), drawing his inheritance from the foulness of the latter age" — i.e. a post-Buddha *ārya* who composed his commentary because he found the parent sūtra "concise in word but rich in meaning, near in language but far in import." (iii) The translation was undertaken on imperial command in *Yǒngpíng* 永平 1 (508 CE), 4th month, first day, when Northern Wèi Xiàomíngdì 孝明帝 commanded the *Tripiṭaka* master *Pútíliúzhī* 菩提流支 (Northern Indian, Chinese rendering 道希 *Dàoxī*), the *Tripiṭaka* master *Lènà Mótí* 勒那摩提 (Central Indian, Chinese rendering 寶意 *Bǎoyì*), and the translation-assistant *śramaṇa* [[佛陀扇多]] Buddhaśānta (伏陀扇多 / 佛陀扇多, Northern Indian) — together with more than ten further scholar-monks — to translate the treatise at the *Tài-jí Zǐ-tíng* 太極紫庭 of the Wèi imperial palace. (iv) Translation was completed in *Yǒngpíng* 4 (511 CE), early summer.

The preface is followed by the standard work-attribution: "天親菩薩造 / 後魏北印度三藏菩提流支等譯" — "composed by Vasubandhu Bodhisattva, translated by the Northern-Indian *Tripiṭaka* Bodhiruci *et al.* of the Later Wèi" — with apparatus showing variant readings (流 / 留 in different witnesses; 等 / 奉詔 in different witnesses).

The first fascicle opens with a verse-eulogy followed by the doctrinal exposition of the first *bhūmi*-stage.

### Abstract

The translation is precisely datable through Cuī Guāng's preface and the *Lìdài sānbǎo jì* ([[KR6r0011]]) to *Yǒngpíng* 1 to 4 (508 – 511 CE). The bracket adopted here reflects this exact dating. The doctrinal-historical importance of the work is foundational: it provides Vasubandhu's authoritative Yogācāra reading of the bodhisattva path-stages of the *Daśabhūmika*, introducing the doctrinal apparatus of *ālayavijñāna* (storehouse-consciousness), the *six characteristics* (*liù xiàng* 六相, drawn out by [[慧光]] Huìguāng and developed in mature Huáyán doctrine), and the systematic Yogācāra analysis of the bodhisattva career.

The translation enterprise itself is one of the most significant institutional events in Chinese Buddhist history: the joint work of [[菩提流支]] Bodhiruci and [[勒那摩提]] Ratnamati produced two distinct translations from the same Sanskrit Vorlage, which were subsequently collated and unified by [[慧光]] Huìguāng, founding the *Dìlùn* 地論 school. The school split into two branches — the *northern lineage* 北道派, which followed Bodhiruci's reading and emphasised the *ālayavijñāna* as the conditioned ground of consciousness, and the *southern lineage* 南道派, which followed Ratnamati's reading (under Huìguāng's guidance) and emphasised the *tathāgatagarbha* as the unconditioned ground. This split into the *northern* and *southern* *Dìlùn* lineages is one of the foundational schisms of Chinese Buddhist intellectual history, the resolution of which (in the late Northern-Dynasties / early Suí period) prepared the ground for the Tang Huáyán school's distinctive synthesis.

The Taishō text (T1522) is established on a particularly rich apparatus including the Korean *Tripiṭaka Koreana*, the Sòng (宋), Yuán (元), Míng (明), Palace (宮), Southern Míng (南藏), Shèng (聖), Shèng-bǐng (聖丙), Shèng-yǐ (聖乙), and Chí (知) witnesses.

## Other points of interest

- Cuī Guāng's preface is one of the most distinguished pieces of Chinese Buddhist preface-literature; its parallel-prose style and its institutional documentation make it a major piece of evidence for the elite Buddhist culture of the early-sixth-century Northern Wèi court.
- The split of the *Dìlùn* school into northern and southern lineages — one of the foundational schisms in Chinese Buddhist intellectual history — is documented through this work and its subsequent commentaries.
