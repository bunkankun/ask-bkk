# Sānjiè Tú 三界圖
*Diagram of the Three Realms*

## About the work

The *Sānjiè Tú* 三界圖 (CBETA [[T85n2840]]) is an anonymous, undated 1-juan Abhidharma schematic document presenting in tabular form the distribution of afflictions (*kleśa* 煩惱) across the three realms of existence (*sān jiè* 三界: 欲界, 色界, 無色界) and their elimination through the sixteen-moment path of insight (*jiàndào* 見道) and through cultivation (*xiūdào* 修道). The text appears in Taisho Vol. 85, which collects Dunhuang manuscripts and fragmentary texts; no author or date attribution is given. The CBETA edition is the sole witness.

### Prefaces

No preface. The text opens directly with the tabular presentation of afflictions by realm.

### Abstract

The *Sānjiè Tú* presents the canonical Sarvāstivāda account of the 98 latent afflictions (*anuśaya* 九十八隨眠), tabulated by realm and truth-category in grid form. The three realms (欲界, 色界, 無色界) each carry their respective sets of *anuśaya* across the five categories associated with the Four Noble Truths and a final category of cultivation-eliminated afflictions, producing:
- 欲界: 32 *anuśaya* eliminated by insight (*jiàn duàn* 見斷) + 4 eliminated by cultivation (*xiū duàn* 修斷)
- 色界: 28 *anuśaya* eliminated by insight + 3 by cultivation
- 無色界: same as 色界

Total: 98. Following the table, a prose section explains the sixteen-*ākāra* (行相) contemplations of the Four Noble Truths and the path of knowledge that eliminates them (sixteen moments: *dharmajñāna-kṣānti, dharmajñāna*, etc. for each of the four truths and their counterparts). The text ends with a placeholder `<img:>` marking the location of an actual diagram (now lost from the CBETA digitization).

This schema is a fundamental tool of *Kōsha*-based Abhidharma pedagogy, derived from the *Abhidharmakośa* chapter 5 (分別隨眠品, [[KR6l0023]]). Its format — a simple reference chart rather than a discursive commentary — suggests it functioned as a teaching aid or mnemonic diagram (圖) for students. The T85 context (Dunhuang manuscripts) places its likely origin in the 7th–9th centuries CE.
