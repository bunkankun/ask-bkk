# Zǒngjí lèi 總集類 — General Anthologies

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR4h is the **總集類** (*Zǒngjí lèi*, "general anthologies") of the **集部** (*Jíbù*, Literature branch) — the third of the five *Sìkù* sub-categories of belles-lettres, sitting alongside *Chǔcí* 楚辭, *biéjí* 别集 (collected works of individuals), *shīwén píng* 詩文評 (literary criticism), and *cí/qǔ* 詞曲. As Wilkinson notes in §30.3 (General Anthologies), the *Sìkù* editors used *zǒngjí* in contrast to *biéjí* — *biéjí* gathers the writings of one author, *zǒngjí* the writings of many. Together, these two categories carry the bulk of the *Jíbù*, which by the eighteenth century constituted thirty-seven percent of the *Sìkù quánshū* — far the largest of the four branches (Wilkinson §30.1, Table 37). Although poetry (*shī* 詩) is never given a *Sìkù* category of its own, it is in practice the first and most prestigious genre within both *biéjí* and *zǒngjí*; rhapsodies (*fù* 賦), parallel prose (*piánwén* 駢文), ancient-style prose (*gǔwén* 古文), examination essays, *yuèfǔ* 樂府 songs, regulated verse (*lǜshī* 律詩), quatrains (*juéjù* 絕句), and palace verse (*gōngcí* 宮詞) all follow.

The division's chronological span runs from the early-sixth-century *Wén xuǎn* 文選 [[KR4h0001]] of Xiāo Tǒng 蕭統, Crown Prince Zhāomíng of the Liáng, down to the great Qiánlóng-era imperially-sponsored anthologies (*Yùxuǎn TángSòng wén chún* [[KR4h0147]], *Sì shū wén* [[KR4h0150]] etc.) and on to twentieth-century critical compilations (*Quán Táng wén* [[KR4h0168]], *Táng shī sānbǎi shǒu* [[KR4h0169]], *Quán Sòng cí* [[KR4h0170]], *Dūnhuáng biànwén xuǎnzhù* [[KR4h0171]]). Two transmissional axes run through the whole. The first is the **canon-defining axis**: the *Wén xuǎn* establishes a genre-based literary canon in the early Liáng, the *Wényuàn yīnghuá* 文苑英華 [[KR4h0022]] of 982–987 extends it through the Táng under the *Wén xuǎn*'s own genre grid, the *Sòng wénjiàn* 宋文鑑 [[KR4h0040]] of Lǚ Zǔqiān (1177–1179) does the same for the Northern Sòng, the *Yuán wénlèi* 元文類 [[KR4h0081]] of Sū Tiānjué for the Yuán, the *Míng wénhéng* 明文衡 [[KR4h0102]] and *Míng wén hǎi* 明文海 [[KR4h0152]] for the Míng, and the Kāngxī-era *Quán Táng shī* 全唐詩 [[KR4h0140]] and its parallels close the loop with imperially-determined comprehensive recensions of period-defining corpora. The second is the **pedagogical-canonical axis**: the *gǔwén* 古文 line that runs from Lǚ Zǔqiān's *Gǔwén guānjiàn* 古文關鍵 [[KR4h0041]] through Lóu Fǎng's *Chónggǔ wénjué* [[KR4h0044]], Zhēn Déxiù's *Wénzhāng zhèngzōng* [[KR4h0046]], Xiè Fāngdé's *Wénzhāng guǐfàn* [[KR4h0059]], and Máo Kūn's *TángSòng bā dà jiā wén chāo* [[KR4h0115]] — the textbook tradition that supplied the civil-service examination its prose models and that culminates, beyond the borders of this division, in Yáo Nài's *Gǔwénci lèizuǎn* and Wú Chǔcái's *Gǔwén guānzhǐ* (Wilkinson §30.3.1, Box 174–175).

## Important texts and text clusters

- *Wén xuǎn* 文選 [[KR4h0001]] — Xiāo Tǒng's c. 526–531 anthology of pre-Liáng literature in 37(–39) genres is the genre-defining ancestor of every later *zǒngjí*; from the Táng onward "the *Wén xuǎn* thoroughly done, half a *xiùcái* won" (Wilkinson §30.3.1). The Tang annotators recast it as the Lǐ Shàn 李善 commentary [[KR4h0002]] and the *Liù chén zhù* [[KR4h0003]] (Lǐ Shàn plus the Five Officials), and a separate Yuán-period critical study of its principal poets [[KR4h0004]] survives.
- *Yùtái xīnyǒng* 玉臺新詠 [[KR4h0005]] — Xú Líng's mid-sixth-century anthology of love poetry, the principal Liáng counter-tradition to the *Wén xuǎn*'s ethical-aesthetic balance (Wilkinson §30.1, view (2)); read in the Qīng through Jì Róngshū's textual study [[KR4h0006]].
- The **Táng small-anthology cluster** ([[KR4h0008]]–[[KR4h0020]]) — *Héyuè yīnglíng jí* [[KR4h0009]] (Yīn Fán, 753), *Guóxiù jí* [[KR4h0010]], *Zhōngxìng jiānqì jí* [[KR4h0012]], *Jíxuán jí* [[KR4h0013]], *Sōnglíng jí* [[KR4h0014]], *Cáidiào jí* [[KR4h0019]], et al. — together preserve the bulk of what survives from middle and late Táng poetry outside the *Quán Táng shī*'s later recovery efforts; they are the principal pre-Sòng source for Táng poetic taste and circle-formation.
- *Wényuàn yīnghuá* 文苑英華 [[KR4h0022]] — Lǐ Fǎng's 982 imperial committee work in 1,000 *juàn*, conceived as the continuation of the *Wén xuǎn* and the chief Northern-Sòng vessel for Táng and Five-Dynasties prose and poetry; Péng Shūxià's *Biànzhèng* [[KR4h0023]] is its textual companion.
- *Táng wéncuì* 唐文粹 [[KR4h0024]] — Yáo Xuàn's 1011 hundred-*juàn* distillation of *Wényuàn yīnghuá* into *gǔwén* canon, retroactively defining the prose canon of the Táng for later generations.
- *Yuèfǔ shījí* 樂府詩集 [[KR4h0034]] — Guō Màoqiàn's late-Northern-Sòng compilation in 100 *juàn* is the indispensable anthology of *yuèfǔ* from Hàn through Táng and the principal source for early Chinese musical-literary history; later supplemented by Méi Dǐngzuò's *Gǔ yuèyuàn* 古樂苑 [[KR4h0119]].
- *Sòng wénjiàn* 宋文鑑 [[KR4h0040]] — Lǚ Zǔqiān's 1177–1179 imperially-commissioned anthology of Northern Sòng prose; the foundational *zǒngjí* for the period, paralleling for the Sòng what the *Wényuàn yīnghuá* had been for the Táng.
- **Gǔwén pedagogical cluster** — *Gǔwén guānjiàn* [[KR4h0041]] (Lǚ Zǔqiān), *Chónggǔ wénjué* [[KR4h0044]] (Lóu Fǎng), *Wénzhāng zhèngzōng* [[KR4h0046]] and *Xù Wénzhāng zhèngzōng* [[KR4h0047]] (Zhēn Déxiù), *Wénzhāng guǐfàn* [[KR4h0059]] (Xiè Fāngdé), and *TángSòng bā dà jiā wén chāo* [[KR4h0115]] (Máo Kūn). Between them they domesticated the prose of Hán Yù 韓愈, Liǔ Zōngyuán 柳宗元, Ōuyáng Xiū 歐陽修, Sū Shì 蘇軾 and the rest of the Eight Masters into the staple curriculum of Sòng-through-Qīng civil-service candidates.
- *Jiānghú xiǎojí* / *Jiānghú hòují* [[KR4h0053]]–[[KR4h0054]] — Chén Qǐ's Hángzhōu-publisher anthologies of Southern Sòng *jiānghú* poets, the principal vehicle for the Yǒngjiā Four Spirits 永嘉四靈 and the *jiānghú* poetic movement.
- *Zhōngzhōu jí* 中州集 [[KR4h0069]] — Yuán Hàowèn's anthology with poet-biographies of the Jīn dynasty, the single most important source for Jīn-dynasty literary history and the model later extended in Wáng Shìzhēn's *Yùdìng Zhōngzhōu jí zēngbǔ* [[KR4h0144]].
- *Yíngkuí lǜsuǐ* 瀛奎律髓 [[KR4h0074]] — Fāng Huí's late-Sòng / early-Yuán anthology of *lǜshī* organised by theme, foundational to subsequent reading of Táng and Sòng regulated verse and the textual fount for the Jiāng-xī-school 江西派 reception of Dù Fǔ 杜甫.
- *Yuán wénlèi* 元文類 [[KR4h0081]] — Sū Tiānjué's mid-fourteenth-century anthology of Yuán-dynasty prose, the parallel to *Sòng wénjiàn* for the Yuán.
- *Táng shī pǐnhuì* 唐詩品彙 [[KR4h0095]] — Gāo Bǐng's 1393 ninety-juàn anthology and its later *Shíyí*, the work that codified the four-period periodisation of Táng poetry (*chūshèngzhōngwǎn* 初盛中晚) that would dominate critical reception for half a millennium.
- **Méi Dǐngzuò 梅鼎祚 *Wénjì* series** — *Pángà wénjì* [[KR4h0120]], *XīHàn wénjì* [[KR4h0121]], *DōngHàn wénjì* [[KR4h0122]], *XīJìn wénjì* [[KR4h0123]], *Sòng wénjì* [[KR4h0124]], *NánQí wénjì* [[KR4h0125]], *Liáng wénjì* [[KR4h0126]], *Chén wénjì* [[KR4h0127]], *BěiQí wénjì* [[KR4h0128]], *HòuZhōu wénjì* [[KR4h0129]], *Suí wénjì* [[KR4h0130]], *Shì wénjì* [[KR4h0131]] — the late-Míng dynasty-by-dynasty prose project that compiled, in twelve linked volumes, the surviving prose of every dynasty from high antiquity through the Suí. This was the principal pre-modern attempt at a "complete prose" of the early empire, and Yán Kějūn 嚴可均's *Quán shànggǔ Sāndài Qín Hàn Sānguó Liùcháo wén* in the nineteenth century built directly on it (Wilkinson §30.3.2).
- *Yùdìng Quán Táng shī* 御定全唐詩 [[KR4h0140]] — Péng Dìngqiú's 1705–1707 Kāngxī-commissioned compilation in 900 *juàn*, with c. 48,900 poems by c. 2,200 authors, the most authoritative pre-modern corpus of any single dynasty's poetry; supplemented by *Yùdìng Quán Táng shī lù* [[KR4h0161]].
- **Kāngxī imperially-sponsored anthology cluster** — *Yùxuǎn gǔwén yuānjiàn* [[KR4h0138]], *Yùdìng lìdài fùhuì* [[KR4h0139]], *Yùdìng Pèiwénzhāi yǒngwù shīxuǎn* [[KR4h0141]], *Yùdìng lìdài tíhuà shīlèi* [[KR4h0142]], *Yùxuǎn Sòng Jīn Yuán Míng sìcháo shī* [[KR4h0143]], *Yùxuǎn Táng shī* [[KR4h0145]]; later, in the Qiánlóng reign, *Yùxuǎn TángSòng wén chún* [[KR4h0147]] and *Yùxuǎn TángSòng shī chún* [[KR4h0148]]. Together they constitute the most ambitious imperial-anthology programme in Chinese history and the basis for nearly every subsequent claim about the corpus of TángSòngYuánMíng literature.
- *Míng shī zōng* 明詩綜 [[KR4h0156]], *Sòng shī chāo* [[KR4h0157]], *Yuán shī xuǎn* [[KR4h0160]] — Zhū Yízūn 朱彝尊 and the *Sòng shī chāo* compilers under Wú Zhīzhèn, the Qīng dynasty-by-dynasty poetry compilations that brought the *Wénjì* programme into the late-imperial period for poetry.
- *Quán Táng wén* 全唐文 [[KR4h0168]], *Táng shī sānbǎi shǒu* 唐詩三百首 [[KR4h0169]], *Quán Sòng cí* 全宋詞 [[KR4h0170]], *Dūnhuáng biànwén xuǎnzhù* [[KR4h0171]] — the late-imperial and twentieth-century closures of the anthology tradition: the *Quán Táng wén* of 1814 as the prose counterpart of the *Quán Táng shī*, Sūn Zhū's *Tángshī sānbǎi shǒu* of 1763 as the most influential late-imperial pedagogical canon, Táng Guīzhāng's *Quán Sòng cí* (1940/65) as the modern-philological completion of the Sòng *cí* corpus, and the *Dūnhuáng biànwén xuǎnzhù* as the manuscript-recovery supplement.

## Important persons

- [[蕭統]] (Xiāo Tǒng, 501–531) — Crown Prince Zhāomíng of the Liáng, patron-editor of [[KR4h0001]] and the figurehead of the court editorial circle that produced the foundational Chinese literary anthology.
- [[徐陵]] (Xú Líng, 507–583) — compiler of [[KR4h0005]], heir to the Liáng court anthology tradition and the principal early advocate of "literature for entertainment and self-expression" (Wilkinson §30.1).
- [[李善]] (Lǐ Shàn, c. 630–689) — the foundational Táng commentator on the *Wén xuǎn* ([[KR4h0002]]), whose annotation became the textual base for every subsequent edition.
- [[殷璠]] (Yīn Fán, fl. 753) — compiler of [[KR4h0009]] and architect of the *shīpǐn* 詩品 critical idiom in mid-Táng anthology practice.
- [[李昉]] (Lǐ Fǎng, 925–996) — chief compiler of [[KR4h0022]] under Sòng Tàizōng, also chief editor of the contemporary *Tàipíng yùlǎn* and *Tàipíng guǎngjì* — the principal Northern Sòng imperial-encyclopedia editor.
- [[郭茂倩]] (Guō Màoqiàn, fl. 1085–1099) — compiler of [[KR4h0034]] and the single most important pre-modern editor of the *yuèfǔ* tradition.
- [[呂祖謙]] (Lǚ Zǔqiān, 1137–1181) — compiler of [[KR4h0040]] and [[KR4h0041]], the Jīnhuá-school philosopher who simultaneously founded the Sòng *zǒngjí* and the *gǔwén* pedagogical anthology.
- [[真德秀]] (Zhēn Déxiù, 1178–1235) — compiler of [[KR4h0046]] and [[KR4h0047]], who recast *gǔwén* selection on overtly Cheng-Zhu Neo-Confucian moral lines.
- [[陳起]] (Chén Qǐ, fl. 1225) — Hángzhōu publisher and compiler of [[KR4h0053]] and [[KR4h0054]], the principal disseminator of late-Southern-Sòng *jiānghú* poetry.
- [[元好問]] (Yuán Hàowèn, 1190–1257) — compiler of [[KR4h0069]], the single most important figure in the preservation of Jīn-dynasty literary culture.
- [[方回]] (Fāng Huí, 1227–1305/1307) — compiler of [[KR4h0004]] and [[KR4h0074]], the late-Sòng / early-Yuán critic who founded the *Jiāngxī*-genealogy reading of regulated verse.
- [[蘇天爵]] (Sū Tiānjué, 1294–1352) — compiler of [[KR4h0081]] and the principal Yuán-dynasty literary-historian editor.
- [[高棅]] (Gāo Bǐng, 1350–1423) — compiler of [[KR4h0095]] and architect of the four-period (*chūshèngzhōngwǎn*) periodisation of Táng poetry.
- [[茅坤]] (Máo Kūn, 1512–1601) — compiler of [[KR4h0115]] and the principal late-Míng codifier of the "Eight Great Masters of Táng and Sòng" canon.
- [[梅鼎祚]] (Méi Dǐngzuò, 1549–1615) — compiler of the *Wénjì* series ([[KR4h0120]]–[[KR4h0131]]), *Gǔ yuèyuàn* [[KR4h0119]], and *HànWèi Liùcháo bǎisānjiā jí* [[KR4h0134]]; with twelve titles in this division he is by some distance the most-represented single anthologist.
- [[馮惟訥]] (Féng Wéinè, 1513–1572) — compiler of [[KR4h0107]], the foundational Míng compilation of pre-Táng poetry to which subsequent recoveries respond.
- [[鍾惺]] (Zhōng Xīng, 1574–1624) — compiler of [[KR4h0133]] and a principal figure of the late-Míng Jìnglíng 竟陵 school of poetic criticism.
- [[朱彝尊]] (Zhū Yízūn, 1629–1709) — compiler of [[KR4h0156]] and a principal architect of the Qīng *kǎozhèng* approach to Míng-poetry transmission.
- [[王士禛]] (Wáng Shìzhēn, 1634–1711) — compiler of [[KR4h0153]], [[KR4h0154]], and [[KR4h0155]], the dominant Qīng-period anthologist of Táng poetry and the principal advocate of the *shényùn* 神韻 aesthetic.
- [[聖祖玄燁]] (Kāngxī Emperor, 1654–1722) — imperial patron of [[KR4h0138]]–[[KR4h0146]] and [[KR4h0161]], and the directing intelligence of the most ambitious imperial-anthology programme in Chinese history.
- [[彭定求]] (Péng Dìngqiú, 1645–1719) — chief compiler under imperial commission of [[KR4h0140]], the definitive corpus of Táng poetry.
- [[高宗弘曆]] (Qiánlóng Emperor, 1711–1799) — imperial patron of [[KR4h0147]], [[KR4h0148]], and [[KR4h0151]], extending the Kāngxī programme into the high-Qiánlóng era.

## Topics

- **The *Wén xuǎn* genre grid.** Wilkinson §30.3.1, Box 174, lists the thirty-seven-to-thirty-nine genres recognised by Xiāo Tǒng's editorial circle — *fù* 賦, *shī* 詩, *sāo* 騷, *qī* 七, *zhào* 詔, *cè* 策, *biǎo* 表, *shàngshū* 上書, *qǐ* 啟, *jiān* 牋, *zòujì* 奏記, *shū* 書, *xí* 檄, *duìwèn* 對問, *shèlùn* 設論, *cí* 辭, *xù* 序, *sòng* 頌, *zàn* 贊, *fúmìng* 符命, *shǐlùn* 史論, *shǐshùzàn* 史述贊, *lùn* 論, *liánzhū* 連珠, *zhēn* 箴, *míng* 銘, *lěi* 誄, *āi* 哀, *bēiwén* 碑文, *mùzhì* 墓誌, *xíngzhuàng* 行狀, *diàowén* 弔文, *jìwén* 祭文, and others. This grid was reproduced (with minor adjustments) by *Wényuàn yīnghuá*, *Sòng wénjiàn*, *Yuán wénlèi*, *Míng wénhéng*, and Méi Dǐngzuò's *Wénjì* series; the rival grid (Yáo Nài's *Gǔwénci lèizuǎn*, Box 175, with thirteen classes) belongs largely to the eighteenth-century *gǔwén* tradition outside this division.
- **The 古文 (*gǔwén*) movement and the examination canon.** From Lǚ Zǔqiān's *Gǔwén guānjiàn* through Lóu Fǎng, Zhēn Déxiù, Xiè Fāngdé, and Máo Kūn to the eighteenth-century Tóngchéng school, *zǒngjí* compilation tracked — and shaped — the prose curriculum of the civil-service examination. The "Eight Great Masters of Táng and Sòng" canon (Hán Yù, Liǔ Zōngyuán, Ōuyáng Xiū, Sū Xún, Sū Shì, Sū Zhé, Wáng Ānshí, Zēng Gǒng) is a product of this anthologising tradition, not a fact about Chinese literary history independent of it.
- **Periodisation of Táng poetry.** Gāo Bǐng's *Táng shī pǐnhuì* [[KR4h0095]] formalises the *chūshèngzhōngwǎn* four-period model, which structures the *Yùdìng Quán Táng shī* and almost every MíngQīng Táng-poetry anthology — even when, as in the Kāngxī Emperor's preface to *Quán Táng shī*, the imposed periodisation is itself overtly rejected.
- **The *yuèfǔ* tradition.** Guō Màoqiàn's [[KR4h0034]] organises *yuèfǔ* into the twelve canonical sub-genres (*jiāomiào gēcí* 郊廟歌辭, *yānshè gēcí* 燕射歌辭, *gǔchuī qūcí* 鼓吹曲辭, *héngchuī qūcí* 橫吹曲辭, *xiānghé gēcí* 相和歌辭, *qīngshāng qūcí* 清商曲辭, *wǔqū gēcí* 舞曲歌辭, *qínqū gēcí* 琴曲歌辭, *zámiào gēcí* 雜謠歌辭, *jìndài qūcí* 近代曲辭, *zágē yáocí* 雜歌謠辭, *xīn yuèfǔ cí* 新樂府辭), a classification taken up by Méi Dǐngzuò's [[KR4h0119]] and still followed by modern editions.
- **Imperial sponsorship.** Three peaks: Sòng Tàizōng's *Wényuàn yīnghuá* programme (980s), Lǚ Zǔqiān's *fèngchì* compilation of *Sòng wénjiàn* (1170s), and the Kāngxī–Qiánlóng programme that runs from *Yùxuǎn gǔwén yuānjiàn* (1685) through *Yùdìng Quán Táng shī* (1707) to *Yùxuǎn TángSòng shī chún* (1750). All three were politically motivated as much as scholarly — anthology as imperial legitimation through cultural inheritance.
- **The local and regional anthology.** *Huìjī duōyīng zǒngjí* [[KR4h0028]], *Yánlíng jí* [[KR4h0036]], *Wúdū wéncuì* [[KR4h0057]] / *xùjí* [[KR4h0116]], *Tiāntái qiánjí* [[KR4h0048]], *Chìchéng jí* [[KR4h0049]], *Wǎnlíng qúnyīng jí* [[KR4h0080]], *Guǎngzhōu sì xiānshēng shī* [[KR4h0096]], *Mǐnzhōng shízǐ shī* [[KR4h0098]], *QuánShǔ yìwén zhì* [[KR4h0109]], *Yǒngshàng qíjiù shī* [[KR4h0162]], *Zuìlǐ shī xì* [[KR4h0163]], *Póyáng wǔjiā jí* [[KR4h0165]] — Wilkinson §30.3.3 treats these "Anthologies of Specific Places" as a distinct sub-type, important sources for local literary culture.
- **The dynastic-complete anthology programme.** Méi Dǐngzuò's *Wénjì* series in the late Míng, then Yán Kějūn's *Quán shànggǔ Sāndài Qín Hàn Sānguó Liùcháo wén* (in the wake of and partly outside this division), then the Kāngxī *Quán Táng shī* and Qiánlóng-era successors, then the twentieth-century *Quán Sòng wén*, *Quán Yuán wén*, *Quán Sòng cí*, *Quán Yuán shī* — a continuous ambition, from the sixteenth century forward, to compile the "complete" surviving literature of each dynasty.
- **Examination-essay anthologies.** *Lùnxué shéngchǐ* [[KR4h0056]], *Wénzhāng guǐfàn* [[KR4h0059]], *Zēngzhù Tángcè* [[KR4h0064]], *Shí xiānshēng àolùn zhù* [[KR4h0066]], *Wén biān* [[KR4h0106]], *Jīngyì mófàn* [[KR4h0105]], and ultimately the Qiánlóng-imperial *Sì shū wén* [[KR4h0150]] — anthologies that targeted the *bāgǔ wén* 八股文 and decree-essay sub-genres of the examination system, a sub-corpus that the *Sìkù* editors largely tolerated rather than celebrated.
- **Recovery of lost early poetry.** A tradition of *jì* 紀 and *xuǎn* 選 that aimed to reconstruct early corpora — *Gǔshī jì* [[KR4h0107]] of Féng Wéinè, with Féng Shù's *Shījì kuāngmiù* [[KR4h0108]] as its critical companion, and Méi Dǐngzuò's *HànWèi Liùcháo bǎisānjiā jí* [[KR4h0134]] — paving the way for nineteenth-century philological projects.
