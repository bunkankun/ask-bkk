# Zǒngjí lèi 總集類 — General Anthologies

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR4h is the **總集類** (*Zǒngjí lèi*, "general anthologies") of the **集部** (*Jíbù*, Literature branch) — the third of the five *Sìkù* sub-categories of belles-lettres, sitting alongside *Chǔcí* 楚辭, *biéjí* 别集 (collected works of individuals), *shīwén píng* 詩文評 (literary criticism), and *cí/qǔ* 詞曲. As Wilkinson notes in §30.3 (General Anthologies), the *Sìkù* editors used *zǒngjí* in contrast to *biéjí* — *biéjí* gathers the writings of one author, *zǒngjí* the writings of many. Together, these two categories carry the bulk of the *Jíbù*, which by the eighteenth century constituted thirty-seven percent of the *Sìkù quánshū* — far the largest of the four branches (Wilkinson §30.1, Table 37). Although poetry (*shī* 詩) is never given a *Sìkù* category of its own, it is in practice the first and most prestigious genre within both *biéjí* and *zǒngjí*; rhapsodies (*fù* 賦), parallel prose (*piánwén* 駢文), ancient-style prose (*gǔwén* 古文), examination essays, *yuèfǔ* 樂府 songs, regulated verse (*lǜshī* 律詩), quatrains (*juéjù* 絕句), and palace verse (*gōngcí* 宮詞) all follow.

The division's chronological span runs from the early-sixth-century *Wén xuǎn* 文選 [[KR4h0001]] of Xiāo Tǒng 蕭統, Crown Prince Zhāo-míng of the Liáng, down to the great Qiánlóng-era imperially-sponsored anthologies (*Yù-xuǎn Táng-Sòng wén chún* [[KR4h0147]], *Sì shū wén* [[KR4h0150]] etc.) and on to twentieth-century critical compilations (*Quán Táng wén* [[KR4h0168]], *Táng shī sānbǎi shǒu* [[KR4h0169]], *Quán Sòng cí* [[KR4h0170]], *Dūnhuáng biànwén xuǎnzhù* [[KR4h0171]]). Two transmissional axes run through the whole. The first is the **canon-defining axis**: the *Wén xuǎn* establishes a genre-based literary canon in the early Liáng, the *Wényuàn yīnghuá* 文苑英華 [[KR4h0022]] of 982–987 extends it through the Táng under the *Wén xuǎn*'s own genre grid, the *Sòng wén-jiàn* 宋文鑑 [[KR4h0040]] of Lǚ Zǔ-qiān (1177–1179) does the same for the Northern Sòng, the *Yuán wénlèi* 元文類 [[KR4h0081]] of Sū Tiān-jué for the Yuán, the *Míng wénhéng* 明文衡 [[KR4h0102]] and *Míng wén hǎi* 明文海 [[KR4h0152]] for the Míng, and the Kāngxī-era *Quán Táng shī* 全唐詩 [[KR4h0140]] and its parallels close the loop with imperially-determined comprehensive recensions of period-defining corpora. The second is the **pedagogical-canonical axis**: the *gǔwén* 古文 line that runs from Lǚ Zǔ-qiān's *Gǔwén guān-jiàn* 古文關鍵 [[KR4h0041]] through Lóu Fǎng's *Chóng-gǔ wén-jué* [[KR4h0044]], Zhēn Dé-xiù's *Wénzhāng zhèngzōng* [[KR4h0046]], Xiè Fāng-dé's *Wénzhāng guǐfàn* [[KR4h0059]], and Máo Kūn's *Táng-Sòng bā dà jiā wén chāo* [[KR4h0115]] — the textbook tradition that supplied the civil-service examination its prose models and that culminates, beyond the borders of this division, in Yáo Nài's *Gǔwénci lèizuǎn* and Wú Chǔcái's *Gǔwén guān-zhǐ* (Wilkinson §30.3.1, Box 174–175).

## Important texts and text clusters

- *Wén xuǎn* 文選 [[KR4h0001]] — Xiāo Tǒng's c. 526–531 anthology of pre-Liáng literature in 37(–39) genres is the genre-defining ancestor of every later *zǒngjí*; from the Táng onward "the *Wén xuǎn* thoroughly done, half a *xiùcái* won" (Wilkinson §30.3.1). The Tang annotators recast it as the Lǐ Shàn 李善 commentary [[KR4h0002]] and the *Liù chén zhù* [[KR4h0003]] (Lǐ Shàn plus the Five Officials), and a separate Yuán-period critical study of its principal poets [[KR4h0004]] survives.
- *Yùtái xīnyǒng* 玉臺新詠 [[KR4h0005]] — Xú Líng's mid-sixth-century anthology of love poetry, the principal Liáng counter-tradition to the *Wén xuǎn*'s ethical-aesthetic balance (Wilkinson §30.1, view (2)); read in the Qīng through Jì Róng-shū's textual study [[KR4h0006]].
- The **Táng small-anthology cluster** ([[KR4h0008]]–[[KR4h0020]]) — *Hé-yuè yīnglíng jí* [[KR4h0009]] (Yīn Fán, 753), *Guóxiù jí* [[KR4h0010]], *Zhōngxìng jiānqì jí* [[KR4h0012]], *Jíxuán jí* [[KR4h0013]], *Sōnglíng jí* [[KR4h0014]], *Cáidiào jí* [[KR4h0019]], et al. — together preserve the bulk of what survives from middle and late Táng poetry outside the *Quán Táng shī*'s later recovery efforts; they are the principal pre-Sòng source for Táng poetic taste and circle-formation.
- *Wényuàn yīnghuá* 文苑英華 [[KR4h0022]] — Lǐ Fǎng's 982 imperial committee work in 1,000 *juàn*, conceived as the continuation of the *Wén xuǎn* and the chief Northern-Sòng vessel for Táng and Five-Dynasties prose and poetry; Péng Shū-xià's *Biànzhèng* [[KR4h0023]] is its textual companion.
- *Táng wén-cuì* 唐文粹 [[KR4h0024]] — Yáo Xuàn's 1011 hundred-*juàn* distillation of *Wényuàn yīnghuá* into *gǔwén* canon, retroactively defining the prose canon of the Táng for later generations.
- *Yuè-fǔ shī-jí* 樂府詩集 [[KR4h0034]] — Guō Mào-qiàn's late-Northern-Sòng compilation in 100 *juàn* is the indispensable anthology of *yuèfǔ* from Hàn through Táng and the principal source for early Chinese musical-literary history; later supplemented by Méi Dǐng-zuò's *Gǔ yuèyuàn* 古樂苑 [[KR4h0119]].
- *Sòng wén-jiàn* 宋文鑑 [[KR4h0040]] — Lǚ Zǔ-qiān's 1177–1179 imperially-commissioned anthology of Northern Sòng prose; the foundational *zǒngjí* for the period, paralleling for the Sòng what the *Wényuàn yīnghuá* had been for the Táng.
- **Gǔwén pedagogical cluster** — *Gǔwén guān-jiàn* [[KR4h0041]] (Lǚ Zǔ-qiān), *Chóng-gǔ wén-jué* [[KR4h0044]] (Lóu Fǎng), *Wénzhāng zhèngzōng* [[KR4h0046]] and *Xù Wénzhāng zhèngzōng* [[KR4h0047]] (Zhēn Dé-xiù), *Wénzhāng guǐfàn* [[KR4h0059]] (Xiè Fāng-dé), and *Táng-Sòng bā dà jiā wén chāo* [[KR4h0115]] (Máo Kūn). Between them they domesticated the prose of Hán Yù 韓愈, Liǔ Zōng-yuán 柳宗元, Ōuyáng Xiū 歐陽修, Sū Shì 蘇軾 and the rest of the Eight Masters into the staple curriculum of Sòng-through-Qīng civil-service candidates.
- *Jiānghú xiǎojí* / *Jiānghú hòují* [[KR4h0053]]–[[KR4h0054]] — Chén Qǐ's Hángzhōu-publisher anthologies of Southern Sòng *jiānghú* poets, the principal vehicle for the Yǒng-jiā Four Spirits 永嘉四靈 and the *jiānghú* poetic movement.
- *Zhōngzhōu jí* 中州集 [[KR4h0069]] — Yuán Hào-wèn's anthology with poet-biographies of the Jīn dynasty, the single most important source for Jīn-dynasty literary history and the model later extended in Wáng Shì-zhēn's *Yù-dìng Zhōng-zhōu jí zēngbǔ* [[KR4h0144]].
- *Yíngkuí lǜsuǐ* 瀛奎律髓 [[KR4h0074]] — Fāng Huí's late-Sòng / early-Yuán anthology of *lǜshī* organised by theme, foundational to subsequent reading of Táng and Sòng regulated verse and the textual fount for the Jiāng-xī-school 江西派 reception of Dù Fǔ 杜甫.
- *Yuán wénlèi* 元文類 [[KR4h0081]] — Sū Tiān-jué's mid-fourteenth-century anthology of Yuán-dynasty prose, the parallel to *Sòng wén-jiàn* for the Yuán.
- *Táng shī pǐn-huì* 唐詩品彙 [[KR4h0095]] — Gāo Bǐng's 1393 ninety-juàn anthology and its later *Shíyí*, the work that codified the four-period periodisation of Táng poetry (*chū-shèng-zhōng-wǎn* 初盛中晚) that would dominate critical reception for half a millennium.
- **Méi Dǐng-zuò 梅鼎祚 *Wénjì* series** — *Pángà wén-jì* [[KR4h0120]], *Xī-Hàn wénjì* [[KR4h0121]], *Dōng-Hàn wénjì* [[KR4h0122]], *Xī-Jìn wénjì* [[KR4h0123]], *Sòng wénjì* [[KR4h0124]], *Nán-Qí wénjì* [[KR4h0125]], *Liáng wénjì* [[KR4h0126]], *Chén wénjì* [[KR4h0127]], *Běi-Qí wénjì* [[KR4h0128]], *Hòu-Zhōu wénjì* [[KR4h0129]], *Suí wénjì* [[KR4h0130]], *Shì wénjì* [[KR4h0131]] — the late-Míng dynasty-by-dynasty prose project that compiled, in twelve linked volumes, the surviving prose of every dynasty from high antiquity through the Suí. This was the principal pre-modern attempt at a "complete prose" of the early empire, and Yán Kějūn 嚴可均's *Quán shàng-gǔ Sān-dài Qín Hàn Sānguó Liù-cháo wén* in the nineteenth century built directly on it (Wilkinson §30.3.2).
- *Yù-dìng Quán Táng shī* 御定全唐詩 [[KR4h0140]] — Péng Dìng-qiú's 1705–1707 Kāngxī-commissioned compilation in 900 *juàn*, with c. 48,900 poems by c. 2,200 authors, the most authoritative pre-modern corpus of any single dynasty's poetry; supplemented by *Yù-dìng Quán Táng shī lù* [[KR4h0161]].
- **Kāngxī imperially-sponsored anthology cluster** — *Yù-xuǎn gǔ-wén yuān-jiàn* [[KR4h0138]], *Yù-dìng lì-dài fù-huì* [[KR4h0139]], *Yù-dìng Pèi-wén-zhāi yǒng-wù shī-xuǎn* [[KR4h0141]], *Yù-dìng lì-dài tí-huà shī-lèi* [[KR4h0142]], *Yù-xuǎn Sòng Jīn Yuán Míng sì-cháo shī* [[KR4h0143]], *Yù-xuǎn Táng shī* [[KR4h0145]]; later, in the Qiánlóng reign, *Yù-xuǎn Táng-Sòng wén chún* [[KR4h0147]] and *Yù-xuǎn Táng-Sòng shī chún* [[KR4h0148]]. Together they constitute the most ambitious imperial-anthology programme in Chinese history and the basis for nearly every subsequent claim about the corpus of Táng-Sòng-Yuán-Míng literature.
- *Míng shī zōng* 明詩綜 [[KR4h0156]], *Sòng shī chāo* [[KR4h0157]], *Yuán shī xuǎn* [[KR4h0160]] — Zhū Yízūn 朱彝尊 and the *Sòng shī chāo* compilers under Wú Zhī-zhèn, the Qīng dynasty-by-dynasty poetry compilations that brought the *Wén-jì* programme into the late-imperial period for poetry.
- *Quán Táng wén* 全唐文 [[KR4h0168]], *Táng shī sānbǎi shǒu* 唐詩三百首 [[KR4h0169]], *Quán Sòng cí* 全宋詞 [[KR4h0170]], *Dūnhuáng biànwén xuǎnzhù* [[KR4h0171]] — the late-imperial and twentieth-century closures of the anthology tradition: the *Quán Táng wén* of 1814 as the prose counterpart of the *Quán Táng shī*, Sūn Zhū's *Tángshī sānbǎi shǒu* of 1763 as the most influential late-imperial pedagogical canon, Táng Guī-zhāng's *Quán Sòng cí* (1940/65) as the modern-philological completion of the Sòng *cí* corpus, and the *Dūnhuáng biànwén xuǎnzhù* as the manuscript-recovery supplement.

## Important persons

- [[蕭統]] (Xiāo Tǒng, 501–531) — Crown Prince Zhāo-míng of the Liáng, patron-editor of [[KR4h0001]] and the figurehead of the court editorial circle that produced the foundational Chinese literary anthology.
- [[徐陵]] (Xú Líng, 507–583) — compiler of [[KR4h0005]], heir to the Liáng court anthology tradition and the principal early advocate of "literature for entertainment and self-expression" (Wilkinson §30.1).
- [[李善]] (Lǐ Shàn, c. 630–689) — the foundational Táng commentator on the *Wén xuǎn* ([[KR4h0002]]), whose annotation became the textual base for every subsequent edition.
- [[殷璠]] (Yīn Fán, fl. 753) — compiler of [[KR4h0009]] and architect of the *shīpǐn* 詩品 critical idiom in mid-Táng anthology practice.
- [[李昉]] (Lǐ Fǎng, 925–996) — chief compiler of [[KR4h0022]] under Sòng Tài-zōng, also chief editor of the contemporary *Tàipíng yùlǎn* and *Tàipíng guǎngjì* — the principal Northern Sòng imperial-encyclopedia editor.
- [[郭茂倩]] (Guō Mào-qiàn, fl. 1085–1099) — compiler of [[KR4h0034]] and the single most important pre-modern editor of the *yuèfǔ* tradition.
- [[呂祖謙]] (Lǚ Zǔ-qiān, 1137–1181) — compiler of [[KR4h0040]] and [[KR4h0041]], the Jīnhuá-school philosopher who simultaneously founded the Sòng *zǒngjí* and the *gǔwén* pedagogical anthology.
- [[真德秀]] (Zhēn Dé-xiù, 1178–1235) — compiler of [[KR4h0046]] and [[KR4h0047]], who recast *gǔwén* selection on overtly Cheng-Zhu Neo-Confucian moral lines.
- [[陳起]] (Chén Qǐ, fl. 1225) — Hángzhōu publisher and compiler of [[KR4h0053]] and [[KR4h0054]], the principal disseminator of late-Southern-Sòng *jiānghú* poetry.
- [[元好問]] (Yuán Hào-wèn, 1190–1257) — compiler of [[KR4h0069]], the single most important figure in the preservation of Jīn-dynasty literary culture.
- [[方回]] (Fāng Huí, 1227–1305/1307) — compiler of [[KR4h0004]] and [[KR4h0074]], the late-Sòng / early-Yuán critic who founded the *Jiāng-xī*-genealogy reading of regulated verse.
- [[蘇天爵]] (Sū Tiān-jué, 1294–1352) — compiler of [[KR4h0081]] and the principal Yuán-dynasty literary-historian editor.
- [[高棅]] (Gāo Bǐng, 1350–1423) — compiler of [[KR4h0095]] and architect of the four-period (*chū-shèng-zhōng-wǎn*) periodisation of Táng poetry.
- [[茅坤]] (Máo Kūn, 1512–1601) — compiler of [[KR4h0115]] and the principal late-Míng codifier of the "Eight Great Masters of Táng and Sòng" canon.
- [[梅鼎祚]] (Méi Dǐng-zuò, 1549–1615) — compiler of the *Wénjì* series ([[KR4h0120]]–[[KR4h0131]]), *Gǔ yuèyuàn* [[KR4h0119]], and *Hàn-Wèi Liù-cháo bǎi-sān-jiā jí* [[KR4h0134]]; with twelve titles in this division he is by some distance the most-represented single anthologist.
- [[馮惟訥]] (Féng Wéi-nè, 1513–1572) — compiler of [[KR4h0107]], the foundational Míng compilation of pre-Táng poetry to which subsequent recoveries respond.
- [[鍾惺]] (Zhōng Xīng, 1574–1624) — compiler of [[KR4h0133]] and a principal figure of the late-Míng Jìng-líng 竟陵 school of poetic criticism.
- [[朱彝尊]] (Zhū Yízūn, 1629–1709) — compiler of [[KR4h0156]] and a principal architect of the Qīng *kǎo-zhèng* approach to Míng-poetry transmission.
- [[王士禛]] (Wáng Shì-zhēn, 1634–1711) — compiler of [[KR4h0153]], [[KR4h0154]], and [[KR4h0155]], the dominant Qīng-period anthologist of Táng poetry and the principal advocate of the *shén-yùn* 神韻 aesthetic.
- [[聖祖玄燁]] (Kāngxī Emperor, 1654–1722) — imperial patron of [[KR4h0138]]–[[KR4h0146]] and [[KR4h0161]], and the directing intelligence of the most ambitious imperial-anthology programme in Chinese history.
- [[彭定求]] (Péng Dìng-qiú, 1645–1719) — chief compiler under imperial commission of [[KR4h0140]], the definitive corpus of Táng poetry.
- [[高宗弘曆]] (Qiánlóng Emperor, 1711–1799) — imperial patron of [[KR4h0147]], [[KR4h0148]], and [[KR4h0151]], extending the Kāngxī programme into the high-Qiánlóng era.

## Topics

- **The *Wén xuǎn* genre grid.** Wilkinson §30.3.1, Box 174, lists the thirty-seven-to-thirty-nine genres recognised by Xiāo Tǒng's editorial circle — *fù* 賦, *shī* 詩, *sāo* 騷, *qī* 七, *zhào* 詔, *cè* 策, *biǎo* 表, *shàng-shū* 上書, *qǐ* 啟, *jiān* 牋, *zòu-jì* 奏記, *shū* 書, *xí* 檄, *duì-wèn* 對問, *shè-lùn* 設論, *cí* 辭, *xù* 序, *sòng* 頌, *zàn* 贊, *fú-mìng* 符命, *shǐ-lùn* 史論, *shǐ-shù-zàn* 史述贊, *lùn* 論, *liánzhū* 連珠, *zhēn* 箴, *míng* 銘, *lěi* 誄, *āi* 哀, *bēi-wén* 碑文, *mù-zhì* 墓誌, *xíng-zhuàng* 行狀, *diào-wén* 弔文, *jì-wén* 祭文, and others. This grid was reproduced (with minor adjustments) by *Wényuàn yīnghuá*, *Sòng wén-jiàn*, *Yuán wénlèi*, *Míng wénhéng*, and Méi Dǐng-zuò's *Wén-jì* series; the rival grid (Yáo Nài's *Gǔwénci lèizuǎn*, Box 175, with thirteen classes) belongs largely to the eighteenth-century *gǔwén* tradition outside this division.
- **The 古文 (*gǔwén*) movement and the examination canon.** From Lǚ Zǔ-qiān's *Gǔwén guān-jiàn* through Lóu Fǎng, Zhēn Dé-xiù, Xiè Fāng-dé, and Máo Kūn to the eighteenth-century Tóngchéng school, *zǒngjí* compilation tracked — and shaped — the prose curriculum of the civil-service examination. The "Eight Great Masters of Táng and Sòng" canon (Hán Yù, Liǔ Zōng-yuán, Ōuyáng Xiū, Sū Xún, Sū Shì, Sū Zhé, Wáng Ān-shí, Zēng Gǒng) is a product of this anthologising tradition, not a fact about Chinese literary history independent of it.
- **Periodisation of Táng poetry.** Gāo Bǐng's *Táng shī pǐnhuì* [[KR4h0095]] formalises the *chū-shèng-zhōng-wǎn* four-period model, which structures the *Yù-dìng Quán Táng shī* and almost every Míng-Qīng Táng-poetry anthology — even when, as in the Kāngxī Emperor's preface to *Quán Táng shī*, the imposed periodisation is itself overtly rejected.
- **The *yuèfǔ* tradition.** Guō Mào-qiàn's [[KR4h0034]] organises *yuèfǔ* into the twelve canonical sub-genres (*jiāo-miào gē-cí* 郊廟歌辭, *yān-shè gē-cí* 燕射歌辭, *gǔ-chuī qū-cí* 鼓吹曲辭, *héngchuī qū-cí* 橫吹曲辭, *xiānghé gē-cí* 相和歌辭, *qīng-shāng qū-cí* 清商曲辭, *wǔ-qū gē-cí* 舞曲歌辭, *qín-qū gē-cí* 琴曲歌辭, *zámiào gē-cí* 雜謠歌辭, *jìndài qū-cí* 近代曲辭, *zá-gē yáo-cí* 雜歌謠辭, *xīn yuè-fǔ cí* 新樂府辭), a classification taken up by Méi Dǐng-zuò's [[KR4h0119]] and still followed by modern editions.
- **Imperial sponsorship.** Three peaks: Sòng Tài-zōng's *Wényuàn yīnghuá* programme (980s), Lǚ Zǔ-qiān's *fèng-chì* compilation of *Sòng wén-jiàn* (1170s), and the Kāngxī–Qiánlóng programme that runs from *Yù-xuǎn gǔ-wén yuān-jiàn* (1685) through *Yù-dìng Quán Táng shī* (1707) to *Yù-xuǎn Táng-Sòng shī chún* (1750). All three were politically motivated as much as scholarly — anthology as imperial legitimation through cultural inheritance.
- **The local and regional anthology.** *Huì-jī duō-yīng zǒng-jí* [[KR4h0028]], *Yán-líng jí* [[KR4h0036]], *Wú-dū wéncuì* [[KR4h0057]] / *xùjí* [[KR4h0116]], *Tiān-tái qiánjí* [[KR4h0048]], *Chì-chéng jí* [[KR4h0049]], *Wǎnlíng qúnyīng jí* [[KR4h0080]], *Guǎngzhōu sì xiānshēng shī* [[KR4h0096]], *Mǐnzhōng shízǐ shī* [[KR4h0098]], *Quán-Shǔ yìwén zhì* [[KR4h0109]], *Yǒng-shàng qí-jiù shī* [[KR4h0162]], *Zuì-lǐ shī xì* [[KR4h0163]], *Pó-yáng wǔ-jiā jí* [[KR4h0165]] — Wilkinson §30.3.3 treats these "Anthologies of Specific Places" as a distinct sub-type, important sources for local literary culture.
- **The dynastic-complete anthology programme.** Méi Dǐng-zuò's *Wén-jì* series in the late Míng, then Yán Kě-jūn's *Quán shàng-gǔ Sān-dài Qín Hàn Sānguó Liù-cháo wén* (in the wake of and partly outside this division), then the Kāngxī *Quán Táng shī* and Qiánlóng-era successors, then the twentieth-century *Quán Sòng wén*, *Quán Yuán wén*, *Quán Sòng cí*, *Quán Yuán shī* — a continuous ambition, from the sixteenth century forward, to compile the "complete" surviving literature of each dynasty.
- **Examination-essay anthologies.** *Lùnxué shéngchǐ* [[KR4h0056]], *Wénzhāng guǐfàn* [[KR4h0059]], *Zēngzhù Tángcè* [[KR4h0064]], *Shí xiānshēng àolùn zhù* [[KR4h0066]], *Wén biān* [[KR4h0106]], *Jīngyì mófàn* [[KR4h0105]], and ultimately the Qiánlóng-imperial *Sì shū wén* [[KR4h0150]] — anthologies that targeted the *bāgǔ wén* 八股文 and decree-essay sub-genres of the examination system, a sub-corpus that the *Sìkù* editors largely tolerated rather than celebrated.
- **Recovery of lost early poetry.** A tradition of *jì* 紀 and *xuǎn* 選 that aimed to reconstruct early corpora — *Gǔshī jì* [[KR4h0107]] of Féng Wéi-nè, with Féng Shù's *Shījì kuāngmiù* [[KR4h0108]] as its critical companion, and Méi Dǐng-zuò's *Hàn-Wèi Liù-cháo bǎi-sān-jiā jí* [[KR4h0134]] — paving the way for nineteenth-century philological projects.
