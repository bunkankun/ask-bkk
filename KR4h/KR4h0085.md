# Yùshān míngshèng jí 玉山名勝集
 *Famed Scenes of Jade Mountain (Collection)*
by [[顧瑛]]

## About the work

A **8-*juǎn* + 1 *juǎn wài-jí*** topographical-and-occasional anthology, edited by **Gù Yīng** ([[顧瑛]], 1310–1369, *zì* Zhòng-yíng 仲瑛, *hào* **Yùshān zhǔrén** 玉山主人), the famous late-Yuán Kūnshān 崑山 patron of literature. The work organises into **named-spots** the prose, verse, *jì* and *xù* composed by the dozens of contemporary literary visitors who frequented Gù's **Yùshān cǎo-táng** 玉山草堂 estate, with each section headed by the personal name(s) of the person(s) who composed the spot's inscription (*tí-é*) and ordered chronologically thereafter. The spots include: **玉山堂** *Yù-shān-táng*, **玉山佳處** *Yù-shān jiā-chù*, **種玉亭** *Zhòng-yù-tíng*, **小蓬萊** *Xiǎo Péng-lái*, **碧梧翠竹堂** *Bì-wú cuì-zhú-táng*, **湖光山色樓** *Hú-guāng shān-sè-lóu*, **讀書舍** *Dú-shū-shè*, **可詩齋** *Kě-shī-zhāi*, **醉雪齋** *Zuì-xuě-zhāi*, **白雲海** *Bái-yún-hǎi*, **來龜軒** *Lái-guī-xuān*, **雪巢** *Xuě-cháo*, **春草池** *Chūn-cǎo-chí*, **絳雪亭** *Jiàng-xuě-tíng*, **浣華館** *Huàn-huá-guǎn*, **柳塘春** *Liǔ-táng-chūn*, **漁莊** *Yú-zhuāng*, **書畫舫** *Shū-huà-fǎng*, **春暉樓** *Chūn-huī-lóu*, **秋華亭** *Qiū-huá-tíng*, **淡香亭** *Dàn-xiāng-tíng*, **君子亭** *Jūn-zǐ-tíng*, **釣月軒** *Diào-yuè-xuān*, **拜石壇** *Bài-shí-tán*, **寒翠所** *Hán-cuì-suǒ*, **芝雲堂** *Zhī-yún-táng*, **金粟影** *Jīn-sù-yǐng* (etc.).

### Tiyao

Your servants respectfully submit: the *Yù-shān míng-shèng jí* in 8 *juǎn* + 1 *wài-jí juǎn* — the Yuán **Gù Yīng** edited it. Yīng has the *Yù-shān pú-gǎo* 玉山璞稿 ([[KR4d0574]]), already catalogued. The grandness of his estate's ponds and pavilions was unrivaled in the southeast; one age's prevailing talents mostly came to visit and feast. He thus gathered the resulting poetry and prose into this collection, each section keyed to a topographical name (*dì-míng*). [Categories then listed: as above.]

For each spot, the editor places first the inscription's author (*tí-é zhī rén*), then Gù Yīng's own commemorative verse, with the prefaces, records, poems and *cí*-pieces appended thereafter. Of the late Yuán's renowned literati, **eight or nine in ten** appear in this volume.

If we consider: the convivial assembly of *Jīn-gǔ* 金谷 and *Lán-tíng* 蘭亭 began the tradition of feast-and-poetry; the topographical multitude of *Wǎng-chuān* 輞川 and *Yún-xī* 雲谿 began the tradition of poetic topography. Yet for sheer number of guests and abundance of compositions, **nothing surpasses the present collection**. Although it appears in a declining age — with men using literary refuge as an escape — the *wén-cǎi fēng-liú* (literary brilliance and refined manner) illumines the whole epoch; even centuries later, one seems still to behold them. To preserve this book is one of the eternal *jiā-huà* (fine talks) of the Garden of Letters.

Reverently submitted, fourth month of Qiánlóng 46 (1781). Editor-in-Chief Jǐ Yún (note: SKQS source typesetter's slip — 紀的 in the source for 紀昀), Lù Xīxióng, Sūn Shìyì. General Collator Lù Fèichí.

### Abstract

**Date.** The estate flourished from **Zhì-zhèng** 1 (1341), when Gù Yīng (then 32 *suì*) retired from worldly affairs to devote himself to the **Yùshān cǎo-táng** project. Most of the recorded gatherings cluster in **Zhì-zhèng 8–16 (1348–1356)**; after the **Long-Spear** rebellions of Zhāng Shì-chéng disrupted Wú-zhōng in 1356 and especially after the **Hóng-wǔ** relocation to Línháo 臨濠 in 1370, the gatherings ended. The compilation must have been substantially completed in the late 1340s–early 1350s; the printing window is c. 1350s–1369 (Gù's death).

**The compiler.** [[顧瑛]] was the central late-Yuán *jiāng-nán* literary patron; his guest-circle included **Yáng Wéi-zhēn** 楊維楨 (the most prominent late-Yuán poet), **Ní Zàn** 倪瓚 (painter and recluse), **Zhāng Yǔ** 張雨 (Daoist poet-priest), **Kē Jiǔ-sī** 柯九思, **Huáng Jìn** 黃溍, [[周砥]] Zhōu Dǐ, **Zhèng Yuán-yòu** 鄭元祐 ([[KR4h0087]]), and many others. Gù's wealth (he had handed his entire patrimony to his son **Yīng-chén** 元臣 at age 40) and his refusal of office made him the late Yuán's exemplary *yǐn-jūn* (recluse-host).

**Significance.** (1) The work is the **principal documentary record of the Yùshān cǎo-táng literary circle** — the most active literary salon of the late Yuán southeast and the model for subsequent Míng-Qīng *yǐn-jūn* literary patronage. (2) Composition-by-topographical-spot is an unusual organizational principle; comparable models are the Táng *Wǎng-chuān jí* 輞川集 (of Wáng Wéi 王維 and Péi Dí 裴迪 — the standard precedent the prefaces invoke). (3) The companion volume **[[KR4h0086]]** *Cǎotáng yǎ-jí* 草堂雅集 (13 *juǎn*) covers the same circle's compositions but organised **by author**; the two anthologies are deliberately complementary. (4) The work is a major documentary witness to **late-Yuán Wú-zhōng visual-and-literary culture**: many of the named spots have surviving paintings by Ní Zàn, Wáng Méng 王蒙, etc.

## Other points of interest

The work's organizational principle — *anthology-by-place* — was distinctive enough that the Qīng SKQS editors flagged it explicitly. The model anticipates the Míng-Qīng tradition of garden-anthologies (cf. **[[KR4h0104]]** *Hǎi-dài huì-jí* — a 16th-c. Shāndōng garden-circle anthology with similar structure). The work and its companion *Cǎo-táng yǎ-jí* together constitute the single most concentrated documentary witness to late-Yuán Wú-zhōng literary culture — comparable in scope to the Sòng *Liù-yī jū-shì jí* 六一居士集 for Sòng salon culture or Táng *Yuán-shū* 元書 for Táng *jiē-shè* (poetry-society) culture.
