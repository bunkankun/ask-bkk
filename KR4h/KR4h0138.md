# Yù-xuǎn gǔ-wén yuān-jiàn 御選古文淵鑒
 *Imperially Selected Mirror-of-the-Abyss of Ancient Prose*
by [[聖祖玄燁]], [[徐乾學]]

## About the work

The **Kāngxī emperor's imperially-selected 64-*juǎn* anthology of Chinese classical prose** running from the *Zuǒ-zhuàn* and the *Guó-yǔ* (Spring-and-Autumn, *juǎn* 1–8) down to the late Southern Sòng (Wén Tiān-xiáng, Xiè Fáng-dé, Fāng Féng-chén, etc., *juǎn* 64). Compiled and annotated by **Xú Qián-xué** ([[徐乾學]], 1631–1694, *Nèi-gé xué-shì* at the time) "by imperial command" (*fèng-chì*). The work bears the **imperial preface dated Kāngxī 24, 12th month (1685)** and is the most ambitious of the Kāngxī-era literary projects in classical-prose anthology — a *jīn-kē yù-lǜ* (golden-statute, jade-rule) model for the *gǔ-wén* canon. The imperial preface explicitly positions the work in the lineage of **Xiāo Tǒng's** *Wén-xuǎn*, **Yáo Xuàn's** *Táng wén-cuì*, and **Lǚ Zǔ-qiān's** *Sòng wén-jiàn*, criticising the latter two for their dynastic restriction; the *Yuān-jiàn* is to be a **trans-dynastic** anthology, holding the whole tradition from Spring-Autumn through Sòng to a single elevated *zhèng* (regular) standard. The *Sìkù tíyào* praises the work as bringing together five anthological-critical precedents: **Zhēn Dé-xiù's** *Wén-zhāng zhèng-zōng* (selection by didactic-moral standard), **Lǐ Shàn's** *Wén-xuǎn* commentary (philological note), **Lóu Fǎng's** *Gǔ-wén biāo-zhù* (critical comment), **Wáng Tíng-zhèn's** *Gǔ-wén jí-chéng* (broad gathering), and the **Wǔ-chén** *Wén-xuǎn zhù* (multiple-commentator format) — while exceeding all of them in scope and discrimination.

### Tiyao

Your servants respectfully submit: the *Yù-xuǎn gǔ-wén yuān-jiàn* in 64 *juǎn* — in Kāngxī 24 (1685), the **Sage-Ancestor Benevolent Emperor** [Kāngxī] *yù-xuǎn* (imperially selected); the *Nèi-gé xué-shì* **Xú Qián-xué** and others *fèng-chì biān-zhù* (compiled-and-annotated by command).

What is recorded: **upward from *Chūn-qiū Zuǒ-zhuàn*, downward to Sòng**. The example of **Zhēn Dé-xiù's** *Wén-zhāng zhèng-zōng* is followed — but the *ruì-jiàn jīng-shēn* (imperial vision is deep), the *bié-cái zhèng-dāng* (selection is correct-and-proper); not the same as Dé-xiù's *jū-yū* (restricted-and-pedantic) approach. Material is annotated under the example of **Lǐ Shàn's** *Wén-xuǎn zhù* — but *kǎo-zhèng míng-què* (textual evidence is clear), *xiáng-lüè dé-yí* (length-and-detail is well-judged); not the same as Shàn's *fán-suì* (busy-fragmentary) approach. Each piece is reviewed under the example of **Lóu Fǎng's** *Gǔ-wén biāo-zhù* — but *pī-dǎo kuǎn-yào* (criticism opens the essential), *kāi-fā jīng-wēi* (revealing fine-subtle points); not the same as Fǎng's *jiǎn-lüè* (brief-cursory). Earlier critics' comments are recorded in full under the example of **Wáng Tíng-zhèn's** *Gǔ-wén jí-chéng* — but *sōu-luó gāi-bèi* (gathering is complete), *qù-qǔ jǐn-yán* (selection is rigorous); not the same as Tíng-zhèn's *wú-zá* (tangled-and-disorderly) approach. Each *chén-jiā* (court editor)'s remarks list their names following the example of the **Wǔ-chén zhù** of *Wén-xuǎn* — but *sù-chéng shèng-xùn* (each long-trained in imperial instruction), the words seen at the source; not the same as the Wǔ-chén's *shū-lòu* (sparse-and-leaking) approach.

As for the **imperial discrimination of *jiǎ-yǐ* rankings** (which is *first-class* / *second-class*), *qīn-huī kuí-zǎo* (personally wielding the cinnabar pen), *bié bǎi-jiā zhī gōng-zhuō* (distinguishing the hundred schools' skill-and-clumsiness), *qióng sān-zhǔn zhī jīng-wēi* (penetrating the three norms' fine-subtleties) — **from the existence of *zǒng-jí* to the present, no emperor has been known to do this**. There is no precedent to follow. This is because:

The *shèng-rén* heart has no place it does not penetrate; the *shèng-rén* way has no completion it lacks. Not only is the *gōng* deep and *dé* great, *shàng yì Táng Yú* (surpassing Yáo and Shùn) — even the *yǐ-lǎn zhī yú* (overflow of the imperial reading) and *pǐn-tí wén-yì* (literary criticism) is the *jīn-fú* (golden gong-stick) of the *cí-yuàn* (rhetorical garden) and the *yù-lǜ* (jade norm) of the *rú-lín* (forest of Confucians). Even **Emperor Yáo's** *huàn hū wén-zhāng* (brilliantly displaying patterned writing) — what could be added? Reverently submitted, fifth month of Qiánlóng 46 (1781). Editor-in-Chief Jǐ Yún, Lù Xī-xióng, Sūn Shì-yì. General Collator Lù Fèi-chí.

### Abstract

**Date.** The imperial preface is dated **Kāngxī 24, 12th month (1685)** — this is the standard publication date. The compilation was carried out over several preceding years by Xú Qián-xué's editorial team; the imperial selection and annotation were finalised by 1685.

**Significance.** (1) The *Yù-xuǎn gǔ-wén yuān-jiàn* is the **single most authoritative classical-prose anthology of the early Qīng** — the imperial successor to **Xiāo Tǒng's** *Wén-xuǎn*, **Yáo Xuàn's** *Táng wén-cuì*, and **Lǚ Zǔ-qiān's** *Sòng wén-jiàn*. As an imperially-selected anthology, it set the **canon of literary models** for the Kāngxī- and Qiánlóng-period examination essay (*bā-gǔ wén*) and *gǔ-wén* practice. (2) The Kāngxī emperor's stated reasoning for trans-dynastic selection (rather than dynasty-by-dynasty) is a **distinctive critical contribution**: literature, unlike ritual / institutional / legal matters, has a *yuán-liú shēn-cháng* (deep, long source-flow) crossing dynasties, and to limit selection by dynasty is to miss the literary continuity. (3) The structural plan — *zhèng-jí* (primary collection), *bié-jí* (separate collection), *wài-jí* (outer collection, drawn from the *zhū-zǐ* Masters) — provides a three-tier hierarchy of canonicity that organises the literary tradition into a unified system. (4) The annotation format — combining philological exegesis (after Lǐ Shàn), critical comment (after Lóu Fǎng), and earlier-critics' assembled remarks (after Wáng Tíng-zhèn) — establishes the **imperial-anthology format** that the Qiánlóng emperor would later use for the *Yù-xuǎn Táng-Sòng wén-chún* 御選唐宋文醇 [[KR4h0147]] and the *Yù-xuǎn Táng-Sòng shī-chún* 御選唐宋詩醇 [[KR4h0148]]. (5) Xú Qián-xué's role as the principal editorial annotator establishes the **early-Qīng imperial-scholar partnership** that would define imperial cultural projects through the Qiánlóng reign.

**Editorial method.** The compilation runs strictly chronologically by dynasty: Zhōu / Spring-and-Autumn (*Zuǒ-zhuàn*, *Guó-yǔ*, *Gōng-yáng*, *Gǔ-liáng*, *Zhàn-guó cè*; *juǎn* 1–8); Qín (juǎn 9); Hàn (*juǎn* 10–20, divided Western and Eastern Hàn); Three Kingdoms (*juǎn* 21–22); Jìn (juǎn 23–24); Liú-Sòng / Qí / Liáng / Chén (*juǎn* 25–26); Northern Wèi / Northern Qí / Northern Zhōu / Suí (*juǎn* 27–28); Táng (*juǎn* 29–40); Five Dynasties (*juǎn* 41); Sòng (*juǎn* 42–64). Each entry-author has comments by named editorial *chén-jiā* (court-editor) hands, plus imperial *jiǎ-yǐ pǐn-tí* (first-/second-class rankings).

## Other points of interest

The Kāngxī emperor's imperial preface contains the strongest statement of **literary self-confidence** in any pre-modern Chinese imperial document: that he, the emperor, will personally rank (*jiǎ-yǐ pǐn-tí*) the *gōng-zhuō* (skill-and-clumsiness) of the hundred schools, exceeding what any prior emperor has attempted in literary anthology. This positioning — emperor as supreme literary judge — is the **founding gesture of the Qīng imperial literary canon** that the Qiánlóng emperor would later complete in the *Sì-kù quán-shū* project. The imperial *jiǎ-yǐ* rankings in the *Yuān-jiàn* serve as the **basic litmus** for the Kāngxī canonical view of the *gǔ-wén* tradition.
