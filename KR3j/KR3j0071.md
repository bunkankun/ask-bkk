# Yì lín huì kǎo 藝林彙考
*Gathered Investigations of the Garden of Arts*

by [[沈自南]] (Shěn Zìnán, *zì* Liúhóu 留侯, *fl.* mid-seventeenth century; of Wújiāng 吳江; Shùnzhì *rénchén* [1652] *jìnshì*; magistrate of Pénglái 蓬萊 county, Shāndōng)

## About the work

A 40-juan early-Qīng *kǎozhèng* compendium organized as a categorical encyclopedia of names-and-things, surviving in 5 sections of an originally planned 24 sections (the remaining 19 were never printed or are lost). The five extant sections are: *Dòng yǔ* 棟宇 (architecture, 10 juan with 10 sub-categories), *Fú shì* 服飾 (clothing, 10 juan with 8 sub-categories), *Yǐn shí* 飲食 (food and drink, 7 juan with 6 sub-categories), *Chēng hào* 稱號 (kinship and social titles, 12 juan with 11 sub-categories), and *Zhí wù* 植物 (plants, 1 juan, incomplete — only the *qióng huā* 瓊花 entries survive). The author's prefaces ("title-records") for the first four sections explain his method: gather citations from across the entire transmitted corpus that contain *biànzhèng* (rectifying-arguments) on each term, with explicit source-attribution, organized for ready reference. The book is one of the more methodologically self-conscious early-Qīng evidential lexicons and the *Sìkù* editors single it out for praise: "the organization is not matched by recent *lèishū* — we therefore record it specifically in the *Zákǎo* class and do not place it with the other *lèishū*."

### Tiyao

We respectfully submit that *Yì lín huì kǎo* in forty juan was compiled by Shěn Zìnán of our dynasty. Zìnán's *zì* was Liúhóu. A Wújiāng man. Shùnzhì *rénchén* (1652) *jìnshì*; served as magistrate of Pénglái county, Shāndōng.

The book has five sections: *Dòng yǔ* 棟宇, *Fú shì* 服飾, *Yǐn shí* 飲食, *Chēng hào* 稱號, *Zhí wù* 植物. At the front, Chén Jiàn 陳鑑 of Xiùshuǐ 秀水 wrote a *Tí jì* 題記: "this book originally had twenty-four sections — the juan-count is very large — but at the time of printing only this much was prepared; what is here is sufficient to cover what bears on human affairs."

[The juan-count and sub-category structure of each section is itemized: *Dòng yǔ* in 10 sub-categories — *gōng diàn* 宮殿, *fǔ shǔ* 府署, *tíng tái* 亭臺, *mén píng* 門屛, *miào shì* 廟室, *sì guàn* 寺觀, *zhái shè* 宅舍, *wú xù* 廡序, *liáng lì* 梁欐, *gōu tú* 溝塗 — and so on for *fú shì*, *yǐn shí*, *chēng hào*, with detailed sub-categorical structure preserved in the source.]

For the first four sections (*dòng yǔ*, *fú shì*, *yǐn shí*, *chēng hào*) Zìnán wrote section-prefaces; the *zhí wù* section alone has no preface — evidence that the manuscript was incomplete. What he cites is broadly substantial and well-anchored. So Chén Jiàn's *tí jì* further records Wāng Fèn's 汪份 words: "What *Huì kǎo* contains are all books with rectifying-arguments — reading them is enough to enlarge knowledge and dispel doubt. Also, what it selects always carries the book-title, so that one already familiar with a book can recognize it at a glance, and one who wishes to consult the original can pursue the reference. Its arrangement is in every way beyond what recent *lèishū* can match." This judgment is just; we therefore specifically record it in the *Záxiè kǎo* class and do not place it alongside the other *lèishū*.

Respectfully revised and submitted, fifth month of the forty-sixth year of Qiánlóng [1781].

### Abstract

Shěn Zìnán 沈自南 (*fl.* mid-seventeenth century; lifedates unrecoverable), *zì* Liúhóu 留侯, of Wújiāng 吳江 (Sūzhōu). Shùnzhì *rénchén* (1652) *jìnshì*. Served as magistrate of Pénglái 蓬萊 county in Shāndōng — his only known office. The *Yì lín huì kǎo* is his sole substantial transmitted work; the remaining 19 sections of the originally planned 24-section encyclopedia are lost and were probably never printed.

The work occupies an unusual methodological position between the late-Míng *lèishū* tradition and emerging Qīng *kǎozhèng* lexicology. Formally it is a categorical encyclopedia (extended *lèishū* structure with detailed sub-categories); methodologically it is a *kǎozhèng* compendium (citation-based rectifying arguments only, with strict source-attribution). The *Sìkù* editors classify it under *Zákǎo* rather than *Lèishū* precisely on this methodological ground. The work is broadly contemporary with — but methodologically distinct from — the major early-Qīng works of Gù Yánwǔ and Huáng Shēng, and represents an interesting middle path.

The *Chēng hào* section in particular is among the more substantial premodern Chinese kinship-and-social-title lexicons, with 12 juan covering 11 detailed sub-categories from palace women to monastic clergy. It anticipates Qīng kinship-anthropological studies and is occasionally cited in modern Chinese sociology of the pre-modern family.

Dating. The 1652 *jìnshì* date and the publication terminus around 1690 (Chén Jiàn's *Tí jì* dating to *Kāngxī xīnmèi* 1751 is the *Sìkù* editors' republishing date, not the original publication) bracket Shěn's productive scholarly period. The *notBefore* of 1652 is the *jìnshì*; the *notAfter* of 1690 is a conservative *terminus ante quem* for the surviving sections.

The standard transmitted text is the SKQS recension; the original 1690s printing survives in scattered library collections.

## Translations and research

*No substantial Western-language secondary literature located.* The work is occasionally cited in Chinese-language studies of early-Qīng lexicography and of the Qīng *lèi-shū* tradition. Modern reprints in the *Sì-kù quán-shū* facsimile.

## Other points of interest

The *Sìkù* editors' categorical decision to place this work in *Zákǎo* rather than *Lèishū* — on the explicit ground of methodological rigor and source-attribution — is a small but significant case study in the eighteenth-century editorial reception of the late-Míng / early-Qīng evidential tradition. Wāng Fèn's defense of the book in Chén Jiàn's preface — "what it cites always carries the book-title" — captures precisely what distinguishes Qīng evidential method from earlier *lèishū* practice.

The incomplete state of the work (5 of 24 sections only) is one of the more striking instances of a major early-Qīng project never completed; the remaining 19 sections must be presumed lost.
