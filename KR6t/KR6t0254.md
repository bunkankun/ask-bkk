# Yuán-tōng Dà-yīng Guó-shī yǔ-lù 圓通大應國師語録
*Recorded Sayings of National Master Entsū Daiō*
*by [[南浦紹明]] Nanpo Jōmyō (語); compiled by [[祖照]] Soshō (等編)*

## About the work

A two-fascicle **Recorded Sayings collection** of [[南浦紹明]] **Nanpo Jōmyō** (1235–1308), Kamakura-period Japanese **Rinzai-Zen** master, posthumous title **Entsū Daiō Kokushi** 圓通大應國師. Compiled by his disciple **Soshō** 祖照 and others.

### Abstract

Nanpo Jōmyō received Línjì-school transmission from **Xūtáng Zhìyú** 虛堂智愚 (1185–1269) at Jìngshān-sì 徑山寺 during his 1259–1267 China sojourn — establishing in Japan the **Xūtáng line** (the *Yōrin Hōō chu-tang ha* 楊岐法應虛堂派) that would, through his disciple **Shūhō Myōchō** 宗峰妙超 (Daitō Kokushi) and Shūhō's disciple **Kanzan Egen** 關山慧玄, become the dominant medieval-and-modern Japanese Rinzai-Zen lineage known as the **Ō-tō-kan-ha** 應燈關派 (after the three founding masters: *Daiō* Nanpo, *Daitō* Shūhō, *Kanzan* Egen).

The *yulu* in two fascicles preserves Nanpo's *jōdō* sermons, *hōgo* dharma-talks, encounter-dialogues, and occasion-verses, organized by his abbacies at **Kōtoku-ji** 興德寺 in Hakata, **Sōfuku-ji** 崇福寺, and **Kenchō-ji** 建長寺. The compilation was done by **Soshō** 祖照 and unnamed disciples, presumably soon after Nanpo's death in 1308.

Significance: the **textual foundation of the Ō-tō-kan-ha lineage** — the principal medieval Japanese Rinzai-Zen line — and through it of Daitoku-ji, Myōshin-ji, and the modern Rinzai sub-schools. One of the most historically influential *yulu* of Japanese Buddhism.
