# Jiào-shí zhēng 教時諍
*Dispute on the Teaching-Times*
by [[安然]] (撰)

## About the work

A single-fascicle **doctrinal-historical survey of the nine-school doctrinal-classification (*jiào-shí*) controversy** by **Annen** 安然 (841–c.915). The work is the shorter and earlier of two paired Annen treatises on the *jiào-shí* dispute ([[KR6t0093]] = T75n2395A; [[KR6t0094]] = T75n2395B = *Jiào-shí zhēng lùn* — the longer expanded version), and a major doctrinal source for understanding how Japanese Buddhists conceptualized the relative status of the nine principal schools of Heian Buddhism.

### Abstract

**Authorship.** The header reads "*Composed by Preceptor Annen.*" **Date.** Annen's mature systematic period, **876–915 CE**.

The work opens by setting out the polemical landscape: "*Our country, the Great Japan, has nine schools of teaching. The dispute on persons and dharmas extends across three countries — India, China, and Japan.*" Annen then surveys the **five distinct enumerations of "nine schools"** that had been offered in Heian Buddhist literature:

1. **Saichō's annual-apportionment memorial enumeration** (傳教大師請年分表): Kegon 3 persons, Tendai-Hokke 2, Risshū 2, Hossō 3, with the addition of the Hīnayāna *Jōjitsu* (*Tattvasiddhi*) school and the Hīnayāna *Kushasha* (*Abhidharmakośa*) school, the "*Abhidharma School*" — for a total of 9 if grouped differently. The fundamental schema lists eight schools plus one.

2. The second variant lists slightly differently.

(Annen lists three more variants in turn, but the work's brief one-fascicle scope means the discussion is condensed.)

Annen then identifies the **two foundational Shingon-school transmissions** he recognizes: "*The Shingon school has two different versions: (1) the* **Three-Stream Blood-Lineage of Shingon** *composed in the* Enryaku era *by Saichō Dengyō Daishi; (2) the* **Three-Certificate Shingon Transmission** *received in the* Jōwa era *by Ennin Jikaku Daishi.*"

The work proceeds to expound the **dispute concerning the** *Mahāvairocana-sūtra*'s **temporal position** in the Buddha's preaching career — was it preached *before* the *Lotus* (and therefore subordinate to it as a provisional teaching), *concurrently* with the *Lotus* (and therefore equal), or *after* the *Lotus* (and therefore superior)? Annen's answer — articulated more fully in [[KR6t0094]] and especially [[KR6t0095]] — is the canonical **Taimitsu position**: the *Mahāvairocana-sūtra* is preached *eternally* by the *dharma-kāya* Vairocana, transcending the temporal frame altogether, and is therefore at once **prior to, contemporary with, and posterior to** the *Lotus*. This is the doctrinal foundation of the Taimitsu *jiào-pàn* (doctrinal-classification) tradition.

The text closes simply: "*Jiào-shí zhēng, one fascicle.*"

## Other points of interest

The work provides one of the most valuable witnesses to the early Heian Buddhist **doctrinal-classification debate**: which schools were officially recognized, in what order, with what hierarchical relations? Annen's "*five variant enumerations*" preserves the doctrinal diversity of the period before the Heian-late establishment of the *kenmitsu* eight-school orthodoxy (*kenshū*: Sanron, Hossō, Kegon, Tendai, Kusha, Jōjitsu, Risshū, Shingon).
