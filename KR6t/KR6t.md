### Scope and scholarly tradition

KR6t 諸宗部類 (*Zhūzōng bù lèi*) is the Kanripo division of **Japanese sectarian Buddhist writings** — the "various schools" section of the canon, comprising the doctrinal-summae, ritual manuals, oral-transmission compendia (*kuden* 口傳 / *kōden* 講傳), school-history apologetics, scriptural commentaries, hymns (*wasan* 和讃), lecture-liturgies (*kōshiki* 講式), Siddham primers, and chant-notation anthologies produced by the Japanese Buddhist schools from the late Nara period (mid-8th century) through the Edo scholastic revival (late 18th century). In Taishō terms the division covers vols 70–84 (T2306–T2731). The matching CANWWW classification is  續諸宗部 (*Xù zhūzōng bù*, "Continued Various Schools"), which the canon editors created precisely to house the Japanese sectarian corpus distinct from the Chinese 諸宗部 (div31, T44–T48). The chronological span runs from 754 — the year of Jianzhen's 鑑真 *kaidan* foundation at Tōdai-ji, after which Bun'an's [[KR6t0044]] *Jièlǜ chuánlái jì* situates the Japanese Ritsu lineage — to 1788 (Edo Sōtō *Gokeshu sanshōyōro-mon* [[KR6t0282]]). With 442 texts on disk this is one of the two largest divisions in the entire Kanripo corpus and the single largest body of pre-modern Japanese-Buddhist textual scholarship gathered under one heading.

Structurally KR6t is organized by **school of attribution** rather than by genre, and follows the conventional Heian–Edo sectarian roster (the "eight schools plus Zen, Pure Land, Nichiren, and Shin"): T70 = **Sanron** 三論 ([[KR6t0001]]–[[KR6t0004]]); T71 = **Hossō** 法相 ([[KR6t0005]]–[[KR6t0021]]); T72–73 = **Kegon** 華嚴 ([[KR6t0022]]–[[KR6t0043]]); T74 = **Ritsu** 律 ([[KR6t0044]]–[[KR6t0058]]) and **Tendai exoteric** 顯天台 ([[KR6t0059]]–[[KR6t0082]]); T75–76 = **Tendai esoteric** (*Taimitsu* 台密, [[KR6t0083]]–[[KR6t0110]]); T77–79 = **Shingon esoteric** (*Tōmitsu* 東密 plus Shingi-Shingon 新義真言, [[KR6t0111]]–[[KR6t0248]]); T80–82 = **Zen** 禪 (both Rinzai 臨濟 and Sōtō 曹洞, [[KR6t0249]]–[[KR6t0313]]); T83 = **Jōdo** 淨土 and **Shinshū** 真宗 ([[KR6t0314]]–[[KR6t0390]]); T84 = the residual genres — **Yūzū-nembutsu** and Pure-Land miscellany ([[KR6t0391]]–[[KR6t0398]]), **Nichiren** ([[KR6t0399]]–[[KR6t0411]]), **Siddham / Bonji** 悉曇 ([[KR6t0412]]–[[KR6t0422]]), **Shōmyō** 聲明 (Buddhist chant, [[KR6t0423]]–[[KR6t0432]]), and **Kōshiki** 講式 ([[KR6t0433]]–[[KR6t0442]]). The principal scholarly traditions a reader must keep in view are accordingly six: (a) the **Nara six schools** (Sanron, Hossō, Kegon, Ritsu, plus the institutionally minor Jōjitsu 成實 and Kusha 倶舍), whose Heian-Kamakura systematization fills T70–T74; (b) the **two Heian Mikkyō** traditions (Tendai Taimitsu and Shingon Tōmitsu) which together account for ca. one-third of the entire division; (c) the **Kamakura sectarian foundings** of 1175–1290 (Hōnen 法然, Eisai 榮西, Dōgen 道元, Shinran 親鸞, Nichiren 日蓮) plus the parallel **Kamakura revivals** of the older schools (Eison 叡尊 / Kakujō 覺盛 Ritsu revival, Jōkei 貞慶 Hossō, Myōe 明惠 / Kōben 高辨 Kegon); (d) the **late-Kamakura encyclopedic synthesis** (Gyōnen 凝然's *Hassū kōyō* programme, Kōshū 光宗's *Keiran shūyō shū*); (e) the **Muromachi 五山 literature** as the principal Zen-monastic *bungaku* of T80–82; and (f) the **Edo scholastic revival** of the late 17th–18th centuries — Hōtan 鳳潭, Jōgon 淨嚴, Fujaku 普寂, Jiun Sonja 慈雲尊者 — whose critical editions, polemical sub-commentaries, and modern philological projects set the immediate stage for the Meiji-era reorganization of Japanese Buddhist scholarship.

### Important texts and text clusters

- **The Tenchō 7 (830) imperial commissions (*Tenchō roku-pon-shū* 天長六本宗書).** Three of the six surviving sectarian statements commissioned by Emperor Junna 淳和 fall in KR6t: Gomyō's 護命 Hossō *Dàchéng fǎxiāng yánshén zhāng* [[KR6t0005]], Bun'an's 豐安 Ritsu *Jièlǜ chuánlái jì* [[KR6t0044]] (sole surviving fascicle), and Gishin's 義眞 Tendai *Tiāntái Fǎhuá zōng yìjí* [[KR6t0064]]. Kūkai's contribution — the *Jūjūshin-ron* [[KR6t0125]] — is also dated to this commission, making it the single most consequential year in the division.
- **Kūkai's foundational Shingon corpus.** *Mìmì màntúluó shízhùxīn lùn* (*Jūjūshin-ron*) [[KR6t0125]] and its three-fascicle abridgement *Mìcáng bǎoyuè* (*Hizō hōyaku*) [[KR6t0126]] establish the ten-stages *kemmitsu* doctrinal hierarchy; *Biàn xiǎnmì èrjiào lùn* [[KR6t0127]] is the founding polemical statement of the exoteric / esoteric distinction; *Jíshēn chéngfó yì* [[KR6t0128]] and *Hōngzì yì* [[KR6t0136]] expound the *sokushinjōbutsu* doctrine and the bīja-as-cosmos theology; the disciple-redacted *Yù yígào* [[KR6t0137]] is the foundational institutional charter of Tōji and Kōya-san. The cluster is the doctrinal-textual centre of Japanese Shingon.
- **Saichō's Tendai-establishment dossier.** *Shǒuhù guójiè zhāng* [[KR6t0060]] is the principal nine-fascicle refutation of Tokuitsu's 徳一 Hossō challenge ([[KR6t0164]]); *Xiǎnjiè lùn* [[KR6t0074]] and *Shānjiā xuéshēng shì* [[KR6t0075]] are Saichō's 818–820 memorials to Emperor Saga establishing the Mahāyāna-only *kaidan* at Hiei-zan; Kōjō's 光定 *Chuánshù yīxīn jiè wén* [[KR6t0077]] is the principal canonical biographical source for Saichō and the precept-platform institution; Ennin's 圓仁 *Xiǎnyáng dàjiè lùn* [[KR6t0078]] is the mature scriptural defense.
- **The Heian Taimitsu (Tendai-esoteric) cluster.** Ennin's Garbha/Vajra/Susiddhi ritual notebooks [[KR6t0083]]–[[KR6t0087]] and Annen's 安然 magisterial seven-fascicle *Tāizàngjiè dàfǎ duìshòu jì* [[KR6t0088]], *Jīngāngjiè dàfǎ duìshòu jì* [[KR6t0089]], and *Sūxīdì duìshòu jì* [[KR6t0090]] are the foundational texts of Hiei-zan esoteric Buddhism. Annen's *Zhēnyánzōng jiàoshí yì* [[KR6t0095]] is the canonical Taimitsu *jiàopàn* (panjiao) treatise asserting the unity-of-Buddha-time-place-teaching against Tōmitsu hierarchical claims. Kōkei 皇慶's *Suíyào jì* [[KR6t0107]] and Chōen 長宴's *Sìshí tiè jué* [[KR6t0108]] establish the **Tani-ryū** 谷流 sub-lineage; Seinen 靜然's eighty-two-fascicle *Xínglín chāo* [[KR6t0109]] is the deity-indexed ritual encyclopedia; Kōshū 光宗's 116-fascicle *Xīlán shíyè jí* (*Keiran shūyōshū*) [[KR6t0110]] is the single largest medieval Japanese Buddhist text and the principal source for *kenmitsu* / *hongaku* / Sannō-cult late-medieval Tendai.
- **The Heian-to-Kamakura Tōmitsu (Shingon-esoteric) cluster.** Jichie 實慧's *Āzì guān yòngxīn kǒujué* [[KR6t0138]] founds the *Aji-kan* meditation tradition; Seizon 成尊's *Zhēnyán fùfǎ zuǎnyào chāo* [[KR6t0139]] codifies the eight-patriarch lineage narrative; Ningai 仁海's *Xiǎoyě liù tiè* [[KR6t0179]] codifies the **Ono-ryū** 小野流 ritual canon, while the **Hirosawa-ryū** 廣澤流 ritual lineage runs through Saisen 濟暹's commentaries [[KR6t0140]]–[[KR6t0142]] and the *Daigo-ji* / *Ninna-ji* materials in [[KR6t0181]]–[[KR6t0214]]. Kakuban 覺鑁's [[KR6t0216]]–[[KR6t0233]] foundational corpus, especially *Wǔlún jiǔzì míng mìmìshì* [[KR6t0220]] and *Jíshēn chéngfó yìzhāng* [[KR6t0217]], establish the Shingon-Pure-Land synthesis and the doctrinal foundation of **Shingi-Shingon** 新義真言. Raiyu 頼瑜's [[KR6t0234]]–[[KR6t0242]] are the Kamakura Shingi-Shingon scholastic synthesis. Yūkai 宥快's [[KR6t0160]]–[[KR6t0162]] are the Muromachi Kogi-Shingon scholastic response.
- **Hossō and Kegon Heian-Kamakura systematization.** Zenju 善珠's *Fǎyuàn yìjìng* [[KR6t0013]] and *Wéishí fēnliàng jué* [[KR6t0017]] inaugurate Japanese Yogācāra commentary. Jōkei 貞慶's *Xīnyào chāo* [[KR6t0007]] (Yogācāra-Mikkyō synthesis) and Ryōhen 良遍's *Guānxīn juémèng chāo* [[KR6t0008]] are the principal Kamakura Hossō pedagogical works. On the Kegon side, Juryō 壽靈's *Wǔjiào zhāng zhǐshì* [[KR6t0033]] founds Japanese Kegon sub-commentary on Fa-zang's *Wǔjiào zhāng*, with Myōe 高辨 (明惠) contributing [[KR6t0026]]–[[KR6t0028]] and [[KR6t0442]] and Gyōnen 凝然 contributing the monumental 52-fascicle *Wǔjiào zhāng tōnglù jì* [[KR6t0035]] and the more accessible *Huāyánzōng yàoyì* [[KR6t0031]]. Hōtan 鳳潭's Edo polemical *Kuāngzhēn chāo* [[KR6t0040]] is the late-Tokugawa Kegon revival.
- **The Kamakura Ritsu revival.** Kakujō 覺盛's *Púsà jièběn zōngyào záwén jí* [[KR6t0049]] is the doctrinal foundation of the Tōshōdai-ji *jisei-jukai* 自誓受戒 self-ordination movement of 1236; Eison 叡尊's *Púsà jièběn zōngyào fǔxíng wénjí* [[KR6t0053]] and *Yīnglǐzōng jiètú shìwén chāo* [[KR6t0054]] are the foundational texts of the Saidai-ji Shingon-Ritsu 西大寺真言律宗 line; Gyōnen's *Lǜzōng gāngyào* [[KR6t0045]] is the school's manifesto.
- **The Kamakura Zen-school foundations.** Eisai 榮西's *Xīngchán hùguó lùn* [[KR6t0249]] (1198) is the founding apologetic of Japanese Rinzai. Dōgen 道元's *Pǔquàn zuòchán yí* (*Fukan zazengi*) [[KR6t0286]], *Xuédào yòngxīn jí* (*Gakudō yōjinshū*) [[KR6t0287]], *Zhèngfǎ yǎnzàng* (*Shōbōgenzō*) [[KR6t0288]] and *Yǒngpíng qīngguī* (*Eihei shingi*) [[KR6t0290]] are the foundational corpus of Sōtō. Keizan 瑩山's *Chuánguāng lù* (*Denkōroku*) [[KR6t0291]] and *Yíngshān qīngguī* (*Keizan shingi*) [[KR6t0295]] are the Sōji-ji line foundation. The **Gozan 五山 abbots' recorded sayings** ([[KR6t0250]]–[[KR6t0267]] and [[KR6t0268]]–[[KR6t0285]]) — including the *Daiō* [[KR6t0254]], *Daitō* [[KR6t0272]], *Musō* [[KR6t0261]], *Ikkyū*-line (*Kaiankokugo* [[KR6t0280]]), and the **Edo Rinzai revivalists** (Hakuin's *Sokkō roku* tradition in [[KR6t0280]]–[[KR6t0282]]) — constitute one of the largest *yǔlù* clusters in any East Asian canon.
- **The Kamakura Pure-Land foundings.** Hōnen 源空's *Xuǎnzé běnyuàn niànfó jí* (*Senchakushū*) [[KR6t0314]] (1198, embargoed during his lifetime) is the founding text of Jōdoshū. The Chinzei 鎮西 line continues through Benchō 辨阿 ([[KR6t0316]], [[KR6t0319]]); the **Seizan** 西山 line through Shōkū 證空 ([[KR6t0326]]–[[KR6t0332]]) and Jōon 淨音 ([[KR6t0333]]–[[KR6t0334]]); the **Chōrakuji** 長樂寺 line through Ryūkan 隆寛 ([[KR6t0388]]–[[KR6t0389]]); and the **Tanenkō** *tanen* line through Seikaku 聖覺 ([[KR6t0324]], [[KR6t0386]]). Genshin 源信's *Wǎngshēng yàojí* (*Ōjō yōshū*) [[KR6t0393]] (985) is the Heian foundational Pure-Land *summa* and the basis of medieval Japanese hell-iconography. Yōkan 永觀's *Wǎngshēng shíyīn* [[KR6t0394]] and *Wǎngshēng jiǎngshì* [[KR6t0436]] are the Eikan-dō Heian Pure-Land tradition. Gyōnen's *Jìngtǔ fǎmén yuánliú zhāng* [[KR6t0398]] is the outsider doctrinal-historical survey.
- **The Shinshū foundational corpus.** Shinran 親鸞's six-chapter magnum opus *Xiǎn jìngtǔ zhēnshí jiàoxíngzhèng wénlèi* (*Kyōgyōshinshō*) [[KR6t0352]] is the founding doctrinal text of Jōdo Shinshū; *Jìngtǔ wénlèi jùchāo* [[KR6t0353]], *Yútū chāo* (*Gutoku-shō*) [[KR6t0354]], and the *Sanjō wasan* — *Jìngtǔ hézàn* [[KR6t0356]], *Jìngtǔ gāosēng hézàn* [[KR6t0357]], *Zhèngxiàng mòfǎ hézàn* [[KR6t0358]] — together constitute Shinran's mature output. The letter-collection *Mòdēng chāo* (*Mattōshō*) [[KR6t0370]] (comp. Jūkaku 從覺) and *Tànyì chāo* (*Tannishō*) [[KR6t0372]] are principal sources for Shinran's late thought, the latter being **the most-read text of Japanese Buddhism** in the modern period. Kakunyo 覺如's *Kǒuchuán chāo* (*Kuden-shō*) [[KR6t0374]] and *Hongan-ji shōnin denne* [[KR6t0375]] establish the Hongan-ji-line normative tradition. **Rennyo** 蓮如's *Liánrú shàngrén yùwén* (*Ofumi*) [[KR6t0379]] and the *Yùyīdàijì wénshū* [[KR6t0380]] are the principal vehicle by which Shinshū became Japan's largest sectarian movement.
- **Nichiren's gosho cluster.** *Lìzhèng ānguó lùn* (*Risshō ankoku ron*) [[KR6t0399]] (1260), *Kāimù chāo* [[KR6t0400]] (1272 Sado), *Guānxīn běnzūn chāo* [[KR6t0403]] (1273), *Zhuànshí chāo* [[KR6t0401]] (1275), and *Bàoēn chāo* [[KR6t0402]] (1276) are the **Five Major Writings** (*go-sho* 五大部) of Nichiren Buddhism. *Yùyì kǒuchuán* (*Ongi kuden*) [[KR6t0410]] is the doctrinally-central but text-historically disputed *Lotus*-commentary attributed to Nichiren via Nikkō 日興.
- **Siddham (悉曇), Shōmyō (聲明), and Kōshiki (講式).** Kūkai's *Fànzì xītán zìmǔ shìyì* [[KR6t0412]] founds the Japanese Siddham tradition; Annen's eight-fascicle *Xītán zàng* [[KR6t0413]] is the magisterial encyclopaedia; Myōkaku 明覺's *Xītán yàojué* [[KR6t0417]] is the late-Heian comparative-philological project; Jōgon 淨嚴's *Xītán sānmì chāo* [[KR6t0421]] and Jiun's 慈雲 *Bongaku shinryō sōmokuroku* [[KR6t0422]] are the Edo critical revival that bridges to modern Japanese Sanskrit studies. The *Shōmyō* corpus [[KR6t0423]]–[[KR6t0432]] preserves one of the world's oldest continuously-transmitted notated-music traditions, with Gyōnen's *Shēngmíng yuánliú jì* [[KR6t0431]] the standard history. The *Kōshiki* corpus [[KR6t0433]]–[[KR6t0442]] — including Genshin's *Èrshíwǔ sānmèi shì* [[KR6t0434]], Yōkan's *Wǎngshēng jiǎngshì* [[KR6t0436]], Kakuban's *Àirǎn wáng jiǎngshì* [[KR6t0437]], Jōkei's *Guānyīn* and *Mílè* *jiǎngshì* [[KR6t0439]]–[[KR6t0440]], and Myōe's *Sìzuò jiǎngshì* [[KR6t0442]] — is the principal liturgical-literary form of medieval Japanese aristocratic Buddhism.

### Important persons

- [[最澄]] — Saichō (767–822), founder of Japanese Tendai; author of *Shǒuhù guójiè zhāng* [[KR6t0060]], *Xiǎnjiè lùn* [[KR6t0074]], *Shānjiā xuéshēng shì* [[KR6t0075]], and (attributed) the *Yàoshī rúlái jiǎngshì* [[KR6t0433]]; the principal early-Heian institutional founder.
- [[空海]] — Kūkai (774–835), founder of Japanese Shingon; the division's single most prolific author (over 20 attributed works), with the foundational doctrinal corpus [[KR6t0125]]–[[KR6t0137]] plus the founding Siddham primer [[KR6t0412]]; the most influential single figure in the division.
- [[圓仁]] — Ennin (794–864), third Tendai zasu; Taimitsu ritual founder ([[KR6t0083]]–[[KR6t0087]]) and *kaidan* defender ([[KR6t0078]]); pilgrim to Tang (839–847).
- [[圓珍]] — Enchin (814–891), fifth Tendai zasu; author of [[KR6t0065]]–[[KR6t0066]] and the lineage-founder of Tendai-ji-mon-ha 寺門派.
- [[光定]] — Kōjō (779–858), close disciple of Saichō; author of *Chuánshù yīxīn jiè wén* [[KR6t0077]], the principal canonical insider source for the Saichō biography and the *kaidan* institution.
- [[安然]] — Annen (841–c. 915), Tendai-Mikkyō systematist; author of the principal Taimitsu doctrinal-ritual corpus [[KR6t0088]]–[[KR6t0096]] and the magisterial Siddham encyclopaedia *Xītán zàng* [[KR6t0413]]; the single most prolific Heian Mikkyō scholar.
- [[圓行]] — see [[KR6s0110]]; pilgrim of the *nittō hakke* — primary work in KR6s.
- [[實慧]] — Jichie (786–847), Kūkai's senior disciple; author of *Āzì guān yòngxīn kǒujué* [[KR6t0138]] and second abbot of Tōji and Kōya-san.
- [[源信]] — Genshin (Eshin Sōzu 惠心僧都, 942–1017), Hiei-zan scholar-saint; author of *Wǎngshēng yàojí* [[KR6t0393]] and the *Èrshíwǔ sānmèi* dossier [[KR6t0434]]–[[KR6t0435]]; principal antecedent of Hōnen.
- [[皇慶]] — Kōkei (977–1049), founder of Tendai Tani-ryū 谷流; author of *Suíyào jì* [[KR6t0107]] and source of Chōen's [[KR6t0108]] *kuden* compendium.
- [[長宴]] — Chōen (1016–1081), Tani-ryū systematist; compiler of *Sìshí tiè jué* [[KR6t0108]], the largest Heian Taimitsu *kuden* corpus.
- [[仁海]] — Ningai (951–1046), founder of Shingon Ono-ryū 小野流; author of the seven-fascicle ritual encyclopaedia *Xiǎoyě liù tiè* [[KR6t0179]].
- [[永觀]] — Yōkan (Eikan, 1033–1111), Eikan-dō Pure-Land patriarch; author of *Wǎngshēng shíyīn* [[KR6t0394]] and *Wǎngshēng jiǎngshì* [[KR6t0436]].
- [[覺鑁]] — Kakuban (Kōgyō Daishi 興教大師, 1095–1143), founder of Shingi-Shingon 新義真言; author of the foundational corpus [[KR6t0216]]–[[KR6t0233]], in particular *Wǔlún jiǔzì míng mìmìshì* [[KR6t0220]] (Shingon-Pure-Land synthesis).
- [[榮西]] — Eisai (Yōsai, 1141–1215), founder of Japanese Rinzai-Zen; author of the founding apologetic *Xīngchán hùguó lùn* [[KR6t0249]].
- [[源空]] — Hōnen (Genkū, 1133–1212), founder of Jōdoshū; author of the founding doctrinal text *Xuǎnzé běnyuàn niànfó jí* (*Senchakushū*) [[KR6t0314]] and the *Hēigǔ shàngrén yǔdēng lù* [[KR6t0317]] corpus.
- [[聖覺]] — Seikaku (1167–1235), Honen's principal Tendai-trained disciple; author of *Wéixìn chāo* [[KR6t0386]] (the doctrinal ancestor of Shinran's *shinjin*) and *Dàyuán tányì wénshū chāo* [[KR6t0324]].
- [[隆寛]] — Ryūkan (1148–1227), Hōnen's Chōrakuji-line successor; author of *Yīniàn duōniàn fēnbié shì* [[KR6t0388]] (immediate ancestor of Shinran's mature view).
- [[親鸞]] — Shinran (1173–1263), founder of Jōdo Shinshū; author of *Kyōgyōshinshō* [[KR6t0352]], the *Sanjō wasan* [[KR6t0356]]–[[KR6t0358]], and the principal Shinshū doctrinal corpus.
- [[證空]] — Shōkū (Seizan Shōkū 西山證空, 1177–1247), founder of the Jōdoshū Seizan branch; author of [[KR6t0326]]–[[KR6t0332]].
- [[辨阿]] — Benchō (Shōkō Benchō 聖光辨阿, 1162–1238), founder of the Jōdoshū Chinzei branch; author of [[KR6t0316]] and [[KR6t0319]].
- [[貞慶]] — Jōkei (Gedatsu Shōnin 解脱上人, 1155–1213), Hossō reformer at Kasagi-dera; author of *Xīnyào chāo* [[KR6t0007]] (Yogācāra-Mikkyō synthesis), the *Kōshiki* corpus [[KR6t0439]]–[[KR6t0440]], and the principal anti-*senju nenbutsu* polemicist of his generation.
- [[高辨]] — Myōe (Kōben 高辨, 1173–1232), Kegon reformer at Kōzan-ji; author of [[KR6t0026]]–[[KR6t0028]] and the *Sìzuò jiǎngshì* [[KR6t0442]]; the principal Kamakura Kegon-school spokesman and Hōnen's most learned critic.
- [[道元]] — Dōgen (1200–1253), founder of Japanese Sōtō-Zen; author of *Fukan zazengi* [[KR6t0286]], *Gakudō yōjinshū* [[KR6t0287]], *Shōbōgenzō* [[KR6t0288]], and *Eihei shingi* [[KR6t0290]].
- [[覺盛]] — Kakujō (1194–1249), founder of the Tōshōdai-ji Ritsu revival; author of *Púsà jièběn zōngyào* corpus [[KR6t0049]]–[[KR6t0051]].
- [[叡尊]] — Eison (Shien 思圓, 1201–1290), founder of the Saidai-ji Shingon-Ritsu 真言律 line; author of [[KR6t0053]]–[[KR6t0054]] and one of the principal Kamakura reformers across both ritual and welfare-institution dimensions.
- [[日蓮]] — Nichiren (1222–1282), founder of Nichiren Buddhism; author of *Lìzhèng ānguó lùn* [[KR6t0399]], *Kāimù chāo* [[KR6t0400]], *Zhuànshí chāo* [[KR6t0401]], *Bàoēn chāo* [[KR6t0402]], *Guānxīn běnzūn chāo* [[KR6t0403]], and the rest of the gosho cluster [[KR6t0404]]–[[KR6t0409]].
- [[頼瑜]] — Raiyu (1226–1304), Shingi-Shingon scholastic; author of [[KR6t0234]]–[[KR6t0242]].

### Topics

- **The "one-vehicle vs. three-vehicles" controversy (一乘三乘) and the *gosei kakubetsu* 五姓各別 doctrine.** The Tokuitsu–Saichō debate of 818–820 — *Shǒuhù guójiè zhāng* [[KR6t0060]] vs. [[KR6t0164]] — is the founding doctrinal dispute of Japanese Buddhism; its echoes run through Genshin's *Yīchéng yàojué* [[KR6t0068]] and the entire late-Heian and Kamakura Tendai-Hossō relationship. The dispute determines who in Japan can be saved (all sentient beings vs. only those with the "right disposition") and is the deep doctrinal substrate of every later Japanese sectarian controversy.
- **The Mahāyāna *kaidan* (大乘戒壇) question.** Whether the *Brahmajāla* bodhisattva precepts constitute a complete ordination (Saichō / Ennin / Annen position) or whether they require the *Dharmagupta-vinaya* *prātimokṣa* as well (the Nara Ritsu position, and the Kamakura Eison-Kakujō revival) is the institutional-canon-law question that splits Japanese monastic Buddhism. KR6t [[KR6t0044]]–[[KR6t0058]] (Ritsu) and [[KR6t0074]]–[[KR6t0082]] (Tendai *kaidan* defense) are the principal evidentiary corpus.
- ***Kemmitsu* 顯密 and Mikkyō supremacy.** Kūkai's foundational distinction (*Biàn xiǎnmì èrjiào lùn* [[KR6t0127]]) of an "apparent" (顯, doctrinal-conceptual) and an "esoteric" (密, mantra-mudra-mandala) teaching, and the hierarchical placement of the latter above the former, is the deep structural axis of Heian-to-Kamakura Japanese Buddhism. Annen's *Jiàoshí yì* [[KR6t0095]] is the Taimitsu adaptation; Kakuban's [[KR6t0220]] is the Shingon-Pure-Land synthesis; Yūkai vs. Raiyu ([[KR6t0160]] vs. [[KR6t0234]]) is the Kogi-vs-Shingi continuation.
- ***Hongaku* 本覺 / original-enlightenment theory.** The mature Tendai doctrine of inherent Buddhahood — that all beings are already Buddha and that practice is the disclosure rather than the construction of this fact — is articulated through the *kuden* (oral-transmission) literature of Hiei-zan systematized in Kōshū's *Keiran shūyō shū* [[KR6t0110]] and earlier in the Tani-ryū *kuden* corpus of Kōkei / Chōen [[KR6t0107]]–[[KR6t0108]]. *Hongaku* is the immediate doctrinal background to Dōgen's *shūshō ichi nyo*, Nichiren's *Lotus-mandala*, and the Pure-Land tradition's *ki-hō ichinyo*.
- **The Kamakura "*senju*" 選擇 (exclusive selection) gesture.** Hōnen's *Senchakushū* [[KR6t0314]] gesture — to *select* a single practice (the *Nembutsu*) as exclusively efficacious for the *mappō* age — is replicated structurally by Shinran (the *Eighteenth Vow* and *shinjin*), Dōgen (*zazen* alone, *shikan taza*), and Nichiren (the *daimoku* alone) as a shared Kamakura grammar of *single-practice + mappō-historical-reasoning*. The internal Pure-Land arguments — *ichinen* vs. *tanen* ([[KR6t0386]]–[[KR6t0390]]), Chinzei vs. Seizan ([[KR6t0316]]–[[KR6t0334]]), exclusive *nembutsu* vs. broad-spectrum devotion (Jōkei [[KR6t0439]]–[[KR6t0440]] vs. Hōnen) — are the principal late-Kamakura doctrinal disputes.
- **Esoteric / exoteric coordination (台密 vs. 東密).** Taimitsu (Saichō / Ennin / Annen) holds the *Lotus Sūtra* and the *Mahāvairocana / Vajraśekhara* corpus to be doctrinally equivalent ("*kenmitsu itchi* 顯密一致"); Tōmitsu (Kūkai / Kakuban) holds Mikkyō to be doctrinally superior to the *Lotus*. The 12th-century Shōshin's *Tiāntái Zhēnyán èrzōng tóngyì zhāng* [[KR6t0070]] is the canonical Hiei-zan statement; the *Daigo-ji* / *Kōya-san* corpus from [[KR6t0179]] onwards is the Tōmitsu institutional position. This dispute is the principal Heian-Kamakura intra-Buddhist polemical axis below the sectarian level.
- **The *kuden* 口傳 / *kanjō* 灌頂 epistemic regime.** From the late 10th century onwards, both the Mikkyō schools and Tendai *hongaku* discourse organize their authoritative teachings as *oral transmissions* (*kuden*) given at *kanjō* (initiation) ceremonies, with the written text reserved as a mnemonic apparatus for what is properly only transmittable face-to-face. This regime — visible in [[KR6t0107]]–[[KR6t0110]], [[KR6t0179]], [[KR6t0203]]–[[KR6t0215]], [[KR6t0235]]–[[KR6t0242]] — is the principal medieval Japanese reorganization of canonical authority.
- **The *Mappō* 末法 framework.** The doctrine that the post-1052-CE world is in the final ("Latter-day Dharma") age, and that this age demands a new and simplified soteriology, is the shared historical-theological reasoning of every Kamakura sectarian founding. Nichiren's *Senji-shō* [[KR6t0401]] is the most systematic statement; Hōnen's *Senchakushū* [[KR6t0314]] is its Pure-Land application.
- **The Shingon-Pure-Land synthesis.** Kakuban's identification of Mahāvairocana with Amitābha (*Mìmì niànfó* 祕密念佛, *Wǔlún jiǔzì míng mìmìshì* [[KR6t0220]]) is the principal medieval Japanese attempt to reconcile esoteric ritualism with single-practice Pure-Land soteriology; it constitutes the doctrinal pillar of Shingi-Shingon and influences later Shingon-Risshū (Eison) and Edo Shingon-Pure-Land (Jōgon, Fujaku).
- **The Gozan 五山 *yǔlù* / *bungaku* tradition.** The recorded sayings of the abbots of the *Gozan* monasteries of Kamakura and Kyoto ([[KR6t0250]]–[[KR6t0285]]) form one of the largest *yǔlù* clusters in East Asia, with Daiō [[KR6t0254]], Musō [[KR6t0261]], Daitō [[KR6t0272]], and Ikkyū-line *Kaiankokugo* [[KR6t0280]] the principal literary monuments; this corpus is the immediate background to medieval Japanese poetry, painting, and political-religious history.
- **Japanese Siddham (悉曇) philology as a continuous tradition.** From Kūkai's *Zìmǔ shìyì* [[KR6t0412]] through Annen's *Xītán zàng* [[KR6t0413]] (880), Myōkaku's late-Heian *Xītán yàojué* [[KR6t0417]], Shinpan 信範's [[KR6t0419]], the Edo critical revival of Jōgon [[KR6t0421]] and Jiun [[KR6t0422]], to the Meiji handover to academic Sanskrit studies, Japanese Siddham constitutes the world's longest continuously-transmitted Sanskrit-philological tradition outside India.
- **The *shōmyō* 聲明 chant tradition.** The Ōhara 大原 / Tendai chant lineage anthologized in [[KR6t0423]]–[[KR6t0432]] preserves one of the oldest continuously-transmitted notated musical traditions in the world. Gyōnen's *Shēngmíng yuánliú jì* [[KR6t0431]] supplies the indigenous history from Cáo Zhí 曹植 through Saichō to the Ryōnin 良忍 Ōhara revival.
- **The *kōshiki* 講式 liturgical-literary form.** The lecture-liturgy ([[KR6t0433]]–[[KR6t0442]]) — a vernacular-Sino-Japanese composition designed for elite assembly-recitation in honour of a specific devotional figure or event — is the principal medieval Japanese aristocratic Buddhist literary form; Genshin's *Èrshíwǔ sānmèi* [[KR6t0434]] founds the deathbed-confraternity strand, Yōkan's *Wǎngshēng jiǎngshì* [[KR6t0436]] the Pure-Land devotional strand, and Myōe's *Sìzuò jiǎngshì* [[KR6t0442]] the Kegon Śākyamuni-centred strand.
