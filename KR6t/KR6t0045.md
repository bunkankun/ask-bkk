# Lǜ-zōng gāng-yào 律宗綱要
*The Essential Outline of the Vinaya School*
by [[凝然]] (述)

## About the work

A two-fascicle **comprehensive outline of the Vinaya school** by **Gyōnen** 凝然 (1240–1321), the encyclopedic Tōdaiji scholastic. While Gyōnen is best known for his Kegon work [[KR6t0035]] *Wǔ-jiào zhāng tōng-lù jì* and for his eight-school doctrinal-historical *Hasshū kōyō* 八宗綱要, he was equally productive in the **Vinaya school** (where his masterwork is the fifty-fascicle *Bommō kaihon-sho nichijū-shō* [[KR6k0081]] on the *Fànwǎng jīng*). *Lǜ-zōng gāng-yào* is his concise systematic treatment of the entire Ritsu-school doctrinal apparatus.

### Abstract

Authorship and dating: The work bears the standard Gyōnen attribution "*Tōdaiji śramaṇa Gyōnen has expounded this*" (東大寺沙門凝然述) at the head. No internal date is given. The work's mature exposition of the Vinaya school's full doctrinal apparatus places it in Gyōnen's mature productive years; the closing passages discuss the **Kamakura Vinaya revival** under Kakujō 覺盛 (1194–1249), Eison 叡尊 (1201–1290), and their successor lineage — making the *terminus a quo* approximately 1290 (Eison's death). **notBefore = 1280, notAfter = 1321** is conservative.

Doctrinal content: the work opens with an elevated philosophical preface: "*The great storehouse of the *vinaya* contains the ten thousand dharmas without limit. The vast practice of *śīla* embraces a hundred million crossings, hard to fathom. The five abodes of confused delusion are by precept-practice suddenly extinguished. The two-fold birth-death entanglement is by the *prātimokṣa* eternally severed.*" Gyōnen then sets out the **two main divisions** of his exposition: the *substance* (體) and *function* (用) of the precepts, and their *fivefold* (五乘 = ratha, "vehicle"-classified) doctrinal apparatus.

The closing fascicle systematically treats the **Kamakura Vinaya revival**: the founding-figure **Kakujō** 覺盛 of Tōshōdai-ji ([[KR6t0049]], [[KR6t0050]], [[KR6t0051]] author); his disciple **Eison** 叡尊 of Saidai-ji; their successor **Ninshō** 忍性 (1217–1303); the **Sennyū-ji** 泉涌寺 line through **Gankai** 願行, **Kakua** 覺阿, and **Chigen** 知元. Gyōnen identifies the Vinaya school as a "*revived Dharma in middle [period] (律法中興)*" through these masters, "*spreading throughout the south and north capitals, the five-kinai and seven-circuit provinces, according to the karmic occasions and timely propagation.*"

The work is at once a doctrinal compendium and a **Vinaya-school lineage manifesto** — Gyōnen's own statement of the Kamakura Ritsu-school's institutional and doctrinal legitimacy, written from the Tōdaiji perspective.

## Other points of interest

The work is the **principal medieval Japanese statement of Vinaya-school doctrinal-historical identity** and the foundational reference for understanding the Kamakura Ritsu revival as a self-conscious institutional and doctrinal project. It complements Gyōnen's other school-outline works (the eight-school *Hasshū kōyō*; the Kegon-school [[KR6t0031]] *Yào-yì*) and forms part of his **encyclopedic project of school-by-school summarisation**.
