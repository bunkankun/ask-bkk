# Qiú-wén-chí biǎo-bái 求聞持表白
*Ritual Statement for the Gumonji Practice*
by [[覺鑁]] Kakuban (撰)

## About the work

A **single-fascicle ritual statement** (表白 *hyōbyaku* — a *kōshiki*-adjacent liturgical-prefatory text) by **Kakuban** 覺鑁 [[覺鑁]] (1095–1144), for use in the **Gumonji-hō** 求聞持法 — the **Vajra-Wisdom-Memory** esoteric practice descending from Kūkai. The CANWWW catalog spells the author's name 覺鎫 (a graphic variant). The *Gumonji-hō* is one of the **most distinguished esoteric practices** of Japanese Shingon, claimed by Kūkai himself to have been the practice through which he attained his initial breakthrough.

### Abstract

The *Gumonji-hō* — short for *Kokūzō Gumonji-hō* 虚空藏求聞持法, "Ākāśagarbha's Wisdom-Memory Practice" — is a mantric-meditative practice in which the practitioner, sealed in solitary retreat for typically **100 consecutive days**, recites the mantra of **Ākāśagarbha** Bodhisattva one million times. The traditional claim — going back to the **Kokūzō-gyō** sūtra and Kūkai's autobiographical account — is that completion of the practice confers **perfect memory** (求聞持 *gu-mon-ji*, "seeking-hearing-holding"): the ability to remember whatever one hears or sees, perfectly and permanently.

Kakuban's *hyōbyaku* is the **ritual statement** to be recited at the **commencement** of the 100-day retreat. It addresses the **doctrinal frame** of the practice, the **practitioner's purpose**, the **dedication of merit**, and the **petition for divine support**. The text is one of the principal sources for the **late-Heian Shingon practice culture** as it was institutionalized in Kakuban's Negoro-ji reform movement.

**Date.** Composition during Kakuban's mature career, *c.* **1120–1144**.
