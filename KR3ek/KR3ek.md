# KR3ek — Wàikē 外科 (External Medicine / Surgery)

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3ek is the surgical sub-division of KR3e 醫家類, itself part of the Masters (KR3 子部). The genre covers what Chinese medicine has called *wàikē* 外科 ("external medicine"): the suppurative, ulcerative, cutaneous, traumatic, and contact-transmissible diseases that present on the body's surface — *yōng* 癰 (carbuncle), *jū* 疽 (deep abscess), *dīng* 疔 (felon / boil), *chuāngyáng* 瘡瘍 (ulcers), *luǒlì* 瘰癧 (scrofula), *bèijū* 背疽 (back carbuncles), *rǔyán* 乳岩 (breast tumour), *zhì lòu* 痔瘻 (hemorrhoidal fistula), and the *máfēng* 麻風 / *lì* 癘 (leprosy) and *méi chuāng* 黴瘡 (later: syphilis) complexes — together with the operative techniques used to treat them (lancing, cautery, *biānlián* 砭鐮, plaster, ligature, the corrosive *shēngdān* / *jiàngdān* preparations, and a full pharmacy of *gāodānwánsǎn* 膏丹丸散 dosage forms). The genre's chronological centre of gravity is the Sòng–Míng–Qīng arc; the corpus collected here runs from the 5th-century *Liú Juānzǐ guǐyí fāng* [[KR3ek040]] — the earliest extant Chinese surgical monograph — to Republican-period (民國) syntheses such as Zhāng Shòuyí's [[KR3ek034]] *Yángkē gāngyào* (1917–27) and Zhāng Juérén's [[KR3ek018]] *Wàikē shísān fāng kǎo* (1939–48).

The social history of the genre is its principal contextual key, and is given a programmatic discussion in Hinrichs & Barnes 2013 (chapter 5, especially the section on the Yuán court surgeon Qí Dézhī 齊德之 — author of [[KR3ek005]] *Wàikē jīngyì*). Through most of the late-imperial period, learned literati-physicians (*rúyī* 儒醫) regarded *wàikē* as a low-status manual specialty fit for *chuāngyī* 瘡醫 ("sore-doctors") rather than for scholars, and Confucian medical ethics actively discouraged the cutting of living bodies. A central polemical move running through this corpus is the surgical writer's attempt to re-classify *wàikē* as a learned discipline: from Chén Zìmíng's [[KR3ek012]] *Wàikē jīngyào* slogan *yōngjū bì běn yú nèi* 癰疽必本於內 ("abscesses and carbuncles invariably have their root in the interior"), through Qí Dézhī's [[KR3ek005]] importation of classical pulse-diagnosis into surgical practice, Wāng Jī's [[KR3ek045]] *bǔtǔ* internalisation, Xuē Jǐ's [[KR3ek022]] [[KR3ek024]] [[KR3ek039]] [[KR3ek038]] *wēnbǔ* (warming-tonification) doctrine, Chén Shígōng's [[KR3ek014]] ethical-and-operative *zhèngzōng*, Wáng Hóngxù's [[KR3ek017]] radical interiorisation of the *yīnyáng* differential, to Gāo Bǐngjūn's [[KR3ek042]] application of Wēnbìng 溫病學 theory to surgical aetiology — the corpus reads as a sustained argument for surgery as a learned art rather than a craft. The same period also yields specialist monographs on the diseases that fell hardest on the surgically-treated population: leprosy ([[KR3ek035]], [[KR3ek038]], [[KR3ek041]]), syphilis ([[KR3ek048]]), scrofula ([[KR3ek044]], [[KR3ek033]]).

## Important texts and text clusters

The division's principal works fall into recognisable clusters that an outline of the field has to keep distinct:

- **The 5th-century foundation.** [[KR3ek040]] *Liú Juānzǐ guǐyí fāng* 劉涓子鬼遺方 — the earliest extant Chinese specialist surgical monograph; in 5 *juǎn*, with the 499 CE colophon of [[龔慶宣]] over earlier military-medical material attributed to [[劉涓子]]. The opening *xùlùn* 序論 ("ghost-bequeathed prescriptions" foundation myth) is one of the most famous origin narratives of any Chinese medical genre.

- **The Sòng–Yuán doctrinal turn.** [[KR3ek003]] *Wèijì bǎoshū* 衛濟寶書 (Northern Sòng), [[KR3ek037]] *Jíyàn bèijū fāng* 集驗背疽方 ([[李迅]], c. 1180–96) — Sòng forerunners; and the two foundational *nèiwài jiānzhì* (combined internal-and-external treatment) texts: [[KR3ek012]] *Wàikē jīngyào* 外科精要 ([[陳自明]], 1263) and [[KR3ek005]] *Wàikē jīngyì* 外科精義 ([[齊德之]], 1330s). Chén Zìmíng codifies the principle that surgical lesions have an internal root; Qí Dézhī forces pulse-diagnosis into the surgical case. Together these two works set the doctrinal grammar that every subsequent late-imperial wàikē writer either inherits or reacts to.

- **Míng synthesis — the move into the literati canon.** The Xīnān school's [[KR3ek045]] *Wàikē lǐlì* ([[汪機]], 1531); the [[薛己]] *wēnbǔ* corpus — [[KR3ek024]] *Wàikē xīnfǎ*, [[KR3ek039]] *Lìzhāi wàikē fāhuī*, [[KR3ek022]] *Wàikē shūyào* (printed posthumously 1571), [[KR3ek038]] *Lìyáng jīyào* (1554); [[KR3ek021]] *Wàikē qǐxuán* ([[申拱辰]], 1604); and the great late-Míng synthesis [[KR3ek014]] *Wàikē zhèngzōng* ([[陳實功]], 1617). Chén Shígōng's *Wàikē zhèngzōng* is the founding text of the so-called **正宗派** *zhèngzōng pài* — the operative-surgical school that retains knife and cautery — and includes the first systematic Chinese clinical description of *rǔyán* 乳岩 (breast cancer), the *wǔ shàn qī è* 五善七惡 prognostic rubric, and the *wǔ jiè shí yào* 五戒十要 (surgeon's ethical code).

- **The Míng standards-of-practice and illustrated tradition.** [[KR3ek027]] *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng · Yángyī* ([[王肯堂]], 1602–08), the surgical division of Wáng Kěntáng's enormous *zhǔnshéng* "standards" project; [[KR3ek032]] *Xīnkè túxíng zhěncáng wàikē* (late Wànlì / Chóngzhēn), an 80-diagram illustrated primer that anticipates the topographical lesion-mapping of the imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn*; [[KR3ek020]] *Wànshì mìchuán wàikē xīnfǎ* (the Wàn 萬全 lineage, Jiājìng – Lóngqìng).

- **The Qīng canonical triad.** Three works defined high-Qīng surgical practice and are routinely treated as a single argumentative complex: [[KR3ek011]] *Wàikē dà chéng* ([[祁坤]], 1665), the early-Qīng Imperial Physician's grand synthesis; [[KR3ek009]] *Yīzōng jīnjiàn · Wàikē xīnfǎ yàojué* ([[吳謙]] ed., 1742), the imperially commissioned surgical curriculum that re-mediated Qí Kūn's work through his grandson [[祁宏源]] and became the *de facto* Qīng standard for two centuries; and [[KR3ek017]] *Wàikē quánshēng jí* ([[王洪緒]], 1740, expanded by [[馬培之]] in 1883), the founding text of the **全生派** *quánshēng pài* — the radically internal, anti-operative school that opposes Chén Shígōng's *zhèngzōng pài* and reduces all surgical pathology to the single *yīnyáng* differential of red *yōng* / white *jū*. The doctrinal debate *zhèngzōng* vs. *quánshēng* is the central methodological axis of late-imperial wàikē.

- **The Qīng wàikē encyclopedia.** [[KR3ek013]] *Yángyī dàquán* 瘍醫大全 ([[顧世澄]], 1760–73), 40 *juǎn* — the largest single-author pre-modern Chinese surgical compendium, conceived as a wàikē counterpart to the imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* and sitting deliberately between the *zhèngzōng* and *quánshēng* poles.

- **The Wēnbìng / *xīndé* / Mènghé Qīng line.** [[KR3ek042]] *Yángkē xīndé jí* ([[高秉鈞]], 1805) is the third member of the canonical Qīng triad (with the *Zhèngzōng* and *Quánshēng*), founding the **心得派** *xīndé pài* and applying Sūzhōu Wēnbìng theory systematically to external pathology. The Jiāngnán Mènghé 孟河 lineage continues through [[KR3ek023]] *Wàikē zhèngzhì mìyào* ([[王旭高]], Gāo Bǐngjūn's nephew) and culminates in [[KR3ek008]] *Wàikē chuánxīn jí* ([[馬培之]], 1892–93) — the formulary of the late-Qīng's most prestigious wàikē physician, summoned in 1880 to treat the Empress Dowager Cíxǐ 慈禧.

- **Other Qīng practitioner / lineage texts.** [[KR3ek001]] *Wàikē zhèngzhì quánshū* ([[許克昌]], 1808–31, a Hézhōu popularisation of the *quánshēng* school); [[KR3ek002]] *Wàikē shífǎ* ([[程國彭]], 1732); [[KR3ek015]] *Wàikē xuǎnyào* ([[唐黌]], 1776); [[KR3ek026]] *Wàikē bèiyào* ([[易鳳翥]]); [[KR3ek028]] *Tóngyuán yīshū · Wàikē* ([[鄭玉壇]], late Qiánlóng); [[KR3ek016]] *Wàikē fāngwài qífāng* ([[凌奐]], late Qīng); [[KR3ek043]] *Fābèi duìkǒu zhìjué lùn* (1840); [[KR3ek036]] *Yángkē jiéjìng*; [[KR3ek010]] *Wàikē yījìng* ([[高思敬]], late Qīng – early Republican); and the texts attributed to [[傅山]] — [[KR3ek031]] *Fùshì wàikē* and [[KR3ek019]] *Qīngnáng mìjué* — together with the related [[KR3ek025]] *Qīngnáng quánjí mìzhǐ* (1886) and [[KR3ek029]] *Dòngtiān àozhǐ* ([[陳士鐸]]).

- **Specialist disease monographs.** Leprosy / *máfēng* / *lì*: [[KR3ek035]] *Jiěwéi yuánsǒu* ([[沈之問]], Jiājìng) and [[KR3ek038]] *Lìyáng jīyào* (Xuē Jǐ, 1554) are the two principal Míng witnesses to Angela Leung's reconstructed reconceptualisation of leprosy as a transmissible disease; [[KR3ek041]] *Fēngmén quánshū* ([[肖曉亭]], 1796–1845) is the most developed Qīng monograph in the same line. Scrofula / *luǒlì*: [[KR3ek044]] *Lìkē quánshū* ([[梁希曾]], 1909) and [[KR3ek033]] *Tánlì fǎmén* ([[李子毅]], 1918). Hernia: [[KR3ek049]] *Shànqì zhèngzhì lùn* and [[KR3ek050]] *Shànzhèng jījù*.

- **Edo Sino-Japanese surgical writing.** Three Edo monographs that participate in the Chinese wàikē tradition while channelling early Dutch (*lán* 蘭) surgical knowledge eastward: [[KR3ek048]] *Bairai shinsho* / *Méilì xīnshū* ([[片倉元周]] Katakura Genshū, 1786–87), the most influential Edo treatise on syphilis and leprosy, incorporating three explicitly Dutch surgical-plaster recipes (*Diachylon*, *Basilicum*, etc.) via the Seikendō school; and [[KR3ek049]] and [[KR3ek050]] by [[大橋尚因]] (Ōhashi Shōin, 1778) on hernia.

- **Republican-period syntheses.** [[KR3ek034]] *Yángkē gāngyào* ([[張壽頤]], 1917–27); [[KR3ek018]] *Wàikē shísān fāng kǎo* ([[張覺人]], 1939–48); [[KR3ek030]] *Wàikē xué huà yì* (late Qīng / early Republican, anon.); [[KR3ek047]] *Chúnyìlú jí (chuāngyáng)* (Qīng anthology). [[KR3ek046]] *Yōngjū shénmì yànfāng* ([[陶華]], with [[薛己]] editorial collation, mid-Míng) closes the Míng end of the long Héjiān / Xīnān popular-formulary tradition.

## Important persons

- [[劉涓子]] (Liú Juānzǐ, *fl.* late Jìn / early LiúSòng) and [[龔慶宣]] (Gōng Qìngxuān, 499 colophon) — the eponymous attribution and the actual recension-editor of the earliest extant Chinese surgical monograph [[KR3ek040]].
- [[陳自明]] (Chén Zìmíng, *fl.* mid-13th c.) — Southern-Sòng author of [[KR3ek012]] *Wàikē jīngyào*, codifier of the *yōngjū bì běn yú nèi* doctrine that frames the rest of the division.
- [[齊德之]] (Qí Dézhī, *fl.* 1330s) — Yuán court surgeon (御藥院外科太醫), author of [[KR3ek005]] *Wàikē jīngyì*; explicitly cited in Hinrichs & Barnes 2013 as a representative of the Yuán-period polemic against unlettered *chuāngyī*.
- [[汪機]] (Wāng Jī, 1463–1539) — founder of the Xīnān 新安 medical school, author of [[KR3ek045]] *Wàikē lǐlì* (1531); the first Míng systematic absorption of *wàikē* into literati internal-medicine *bǔtǔ* doctrine.
- [[薛己]] (Xuē Jǐ, 1487–1559) — Sūzhōu imperial physician, the single most prolific Míng wàikē author. Four works in this division ([[KR3ek022]], [[KR3ek024]], [[KR3ek038]], [[KR3ek039]]) plus extensive editorial work on Chén Zìmíng [[KR3ek012]] and Táo Huá [[KR3ek046]]. Founder of the *wēnbǔ* surgical doctrine.
- [[陳實功]] (Chén Shígōng, c. 1555–1636) — Nántōng surgeon, author of [[KR3ek014]] *Wàikē zhèngzōng* (1617). Founder of the *zhèngzōng pài*; the most consequential Míng operative-surgical writer.
- [[王肯堂]] (Wáng Kěntáng, 1549–1613) — Wànlì *jìnshì* and one of the most learned encyclopaedists of the late Míng; author of [[KR3ek027]] *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng · Yángyī*, the surgical division of his *Zhèngzhì zhǔnshéng* "standards" project.
- [[祁坤]] (Qí Kūn, *fl.* Shùnzhì – Kāngxī) — Qīng Imperial Physician, author of [[KR3ek011]] *Wàikē dà chéng* (1665); the principal source of the imperial *Yīzōng jīnjiàn* surgical division through his grandson [[祁宏源]].
- [[吳謙]] (Wú Qiān, *fl.* early Qiánlóng) — chief editor of the imperial [[KR3ek009]] *Yīzōng jīnjiàn · Wàikē xīnfǎ yàojué* (1742); director of the *Tàiyīyuàn*.
- [[王洪緒]] (Wáng Hóngxù, c. 1669 – c. 1749) — Sūzhōu lay practitioner; founder of the *quánshēng pài* with [[KR3ek017]] *Wàikē quánshēng jí* (1740), the great late-Qīng polemical counterweight to the *zhèngzōng* school.
- [[顧世澄]] (Gù Shìchéng, *fl.* Qiánlóng) — Wúhú / Yángzhōu hereditary physician, author of the 40-*juǎn* encyclopaedia [[KR3ek013]] *Yángyī dàquán* (1760–73).
- [[高秉鈞]] (Gāo Bǐngjūn, 1755–1827) — Wúxī physician; founder of the *xīndé pài* with [[KR3ek042]] *Yángkē xīndé jí* (1805); the third member of the Qīng canonical triad and the principal channel by which Wēnbìng theory entered surgical practice.
- [[馬培之]] (Mǎ Péizhī, 1820–1903) — late-Qīng Mènghé synthesiser, summoned 1880 to treat the Empress Dowager Cíxǐ; author of [[KR3ek008]] *Wàikē chuánxīn jí* and editor of the 1883 expanded recension of Wáng Hóngxù's [[KR3ek017]].
- [[沈之問]] and [[肖曉亭]] — the principal authors of the Míng and Qīng leprosy monographs ([[KR3ek035]] and [[KR3ek041]]); together with Xuē Jǐ's [[KR3ek038]], the documentary backbone of Angela Leung's *Leprosy in China*.
- [[片倉元周]] (Katakura Genshū / Kakuryō, 1751–1822) — Edo physician of the Tanba 丹波 lineage; author of [[KR3ek048]] *Bairai shinsho* / *Méilì xīnshū* (1786–87), notable for incorporating Dutch surgical-plaster recipes received at the *Seikendō*.
- [[傅山]] (Fù Shān, 1607–1684) — early-Qīng polymath; not primarily a surgeon, but two later texts in this division are attributed to him ([[KR3ek019]] *Qīngnáng mìjué*, [[KR3ek031]] *Fùshì wàikē*) and represent the pseudepigraphic "green-bag" surgical tradition.

## Topics

- **The 癰 / 疽 *yīnyáng* differential.** The single most pervasive theoretical axis: *yōng* (heat, surface, redness, painful, *yáng*) versus *jū* (cold, deep, leathery, *yīn*). Programmatically schematised in [[KR3ek040]] *Liú Juānzǐ guǐyí fāng*; restated in every subsequent generation; reduced by Wáng Hóngxù [[KR3ek017]] to the sole diagnostic criterion needed in *wàikē*.

- ***Zhèngzōng* 正宗 vs. *quánshēng* 全生 — operative versus internalist surgery.** The central methodological controversy of the Qīng. The *zhèngzōng pài* (Chén Shígōng [[KR3ek014]]) retains active operative intervention (knife, cautery, corrosive *shēngdān*); the *quánshēng pài* (Wáng Hóngxù [[KR3ek017]]) rejects most operative methods in favour of internal *yīnyáng* prescriptions. Practical Qīng practice (Gù Shìchéng [[KR3ek013]], Gāo Bǐngjūn [[KR3ek042]]) sits between the two, and the imperial [[KR3ek009]] consolidates a middle position.

- ***Nèiwài jiānzhì* 內外兼治 — surgery's claim to literati status.** The standing argument, from Chén Zìmíng [[KR3ek012]] through Qí Dézhī [[KR3ek005]], Wāng Jī [[KR3ek045]], Xuē Jǐ [[KR3ek022]] [[KR3ek024]] [[KR3ek039]], and Qí Kūn [[KR3ek011]], that surgical lesions are internally rooted and that the surgeon's first duty is *nèituō* 內托 (internal supportive tonification), not topical intervention. This argument runs against the *chuāngyī* / *yángyī* practitioner tradition criticised in Hinrichs & Barnes 2013.

- **The five-good and seven-bad prognostic rubric (*wǔ shàn qī è* 五善七惡).** Articulated in Chén Shígōng [[KR3ek014]] and absorbed wholesale into the imperial [[KR3ek009]]; a standardised differential of curable from fatal cases that remained in active use through the Qīng.

- **Wēnbìng 溫病學 and *huǒdú* 火毒 aetiology.** Gāo Bǐngjūn [[KR3ek042]] systematically imports the Sūzhōu Wēnbìng theory of *huǒdú* / *shīrè* accumulation into surgical pathology, decoupling *yōng* / *jū* from a pure structural anatomy and reading them as expressions of channel-organ heat.

- **Contagion theory and chronic skin disease.** [[KR3ek035]], [[KR3ek038]], [[KR3ek041]], [[KR3ek048]] form the principal Chinese (and Sino-Japanese) corpus on leprosy and syphilis as transmissible diseases, with both inherited (*fùmǔ jīngxuè*) and contact (*qìhuì*) routes; the locus classicus for Angela Leung's *Leprosy in China* (Columbia, 2009) and for Hinrichs & Barnes 2013 on Chinese pre-modern infectious-disease thinking.

- **Topographical organisation and the illustrated tradition.** Most late-Míng and Qīng wàikē texts arrange disease "from head to foot," and the illustrated tradition culminates in the imperial [[KR3ek009]] illustrated lesion charts. [[KR3ek032]] *Zhěncáng wàikē* anticipates this organisation in the late Míng; [[KR3ek013]] *Yángyī dàquán* applies it on an encyclopaedic scale.

- **Sino-Japanese transmission and the reception of Dutch (*lán* 蘭) surgery.** Katakura's [[KR3ek048]] (1786–87) is the most explicit case: Dutch surgical-plaster recipes received at the Seikendō are absorbed into a Chinese-language wàikē framework alongside the standard *quánshēng* and *zhèngzōng* materials, then re-exported to China in the 19th century.

- **Lineage transmission, *chuánxīn* 傳薪, and the case-record (*yīàn* 醫案) as evidence.** Late-Qīng works in this division (Mǎ Péizhī [[KR3ek008]], the Mènghé and Wáng Xùgāo [[KR3ek023]] line, Xǔ Kèchāng [[KR3ek001]]) treat surgical knowledge as private lineage capital, transmitted from master to disciple in formulary digests rather than published in full; the published recensions are typically posthumous or pupil-edited.

- **c. 1100–1170** — *Wèijì bǎoshū* 衛濟寶書 [[KR3ek003]] — short Northern-Sòng surgical formulary by the Eastern-Studio Recluse.
- **c. 1180–1196** — *Jíyàn bèijū fāng* 集驗背疽方 ([[李迅]]) [[KR3ek037]] — Sòng specialist monograph on back carbuncles; principal source for Chén Zìmíng.
- **1263** — *Wàikē jīngyào* 外科精要 ([[陳自明]]) [[KR3ek012]] — Southern-Sòng codification of the *yōngjū bì běn yú nèi* doctrine; transmitted in [[薛己]]'s 1547 expanded recension.
- **c. 1330–1335** — *Wàikē jīngyì* 外科精義 ([[齊德之]]) [[KR3ek005]] — Yuán court-surgeon's importation of pulse-diagnosis into surgery.
- **c. 1378–1382** — *Xiānchuán wàikē jíyàn fāng* 仙傳外科集驗方 ([[趙宜真]]) [[KR3ek004]] — YuánMíng transition; Daoist *xiānchuán* surgical formulary derived from [[楊清叟]].
- **c. 1378–1395** — *Mìchuán wàikē fāng* 秘傳外科方 ([[趙宜真]]) [[KR3ek007]] — companion to [[KR3ek004]].
- **c. 1430–1450** — *Yōngjū shénmì yànfāng* 癰疽神秘驗方 ([[陶華]], with [[薛己]] editorial collation) [[KR3ek046]] — mid-Míng popular-formulary witness.
- **1498** — *Wàikē jíyàn fāng* 外科集驗方 ([[周文采]]) [[KR3ek006]] — mid-Míng Hóngzhì compilation.

- **1665** — *Wàikē dà chéng* 外科大成 ([[祁坤]]) [[KR3ek011]] — Imperial Physician's grand early-Qīng synthesis.
- **c. 1670–1723** — *Qīngnáng mìjué* 青囊秘訣 ([[傅山]] attributed) [[KR3ek019]] — second pseudepigraphic Fù Shān text.
- **c. 1690–1790** — *Dòngtiān àozhǐ* 洞天奧旨 ([[陳士鐸]]) [[KR3ek029]] — Daoist-inflected Kāngxī surgical formulary, widely circulated through the Qiánlóng period.
- **1732** — *Wàikē shífǎ* 外科十法 ([[程國彭]]) [[KR3ek002]] — short, programmatic Yōngzhèng manual.
- **1739–1742** — *Yīzōng jīnjiàn · Wàikē xīnfǎ yàojué* 醫宗金鑑·外科心法要訣 ([[吳謙]] ed.) [[KR3ek009]] — imperially commissioned surgical curriculum; the Qīng standard text.
- **1740** — *Wàikē quánshēng jí* 外科全生集 ([[王洪緒]]) [[KR3ek017]] — founding text of the *quánshēng* school.
- **c. 1742–1900** — *Yángkē jiéjìng* 瘍科捷徑 ([[楊成博]]) [[KR3ek036]] — mid-to-late Qīng practitioner's handbook.
- **c. 1742–1911** — *Chúnyìlú jí (chuāngyáng)* 純懿廬集（瘡瘍） [[KR3ek047]] — anonymous Qīng anthology of surgical writing.
- **1760–1773** — *Yángyī dàquán* 瘍醫大全 ([[顧世澄]]) [[KR3ek013]] — the 40-*juǎn* Qīng surgical encyclopaedia.
- **1776** — *Wàikē xuǎnyào* 外科選要 ([[唐黌]]) [[KR3ek015]] — Qiánlóng condensed teaching text.
- **1778** — *Shànqì zhèngzhì lùn* 疝氣證治論 ([[大橋尚因]]) [[KR3ek049]] — Edo hernia monograph.
- **1778** — *Shànzhèng jījù* 疝症積聚 ([[大橋尚因]]) [[KR3ek050]] — companion Edo hernia treatise.
- **c. 1780–1795** — *Tóngyuán yīshū · Wàikē* 彤園醫書·外科 ([[鄭玉壇]]) [[KR3ek028]] — late-Qián-lóng synthesis.
- **1786–1787** — *Méilì xīnshū* / *Bairai shinsho* 黴癘新書 ([[片倉元周]]) [[KR3ek048]] — Edo specialist on syphilis and leprosy, incorporating Dutch surgical recipes.
- **c. 1796–1845** — *Fēngmén quánshū* 瘋門全書 ([[肖曉亭]]) [[KR3ek041]] — late-Qián-lóng / early-Dào-guāng leprosy monograph.
- **1805** — *Yángkē xīndé jí* 瘍科心得集 ([[高秉鈞]]) [[KR3ek042]] — founding text of the *xīndé* school; third member of the Qīng canonical triad.
- **1808–1831** — *Wàikē zhèngzhì quánshū* 外科證治全書 ([[許克昌]]) [[KR3ek001]] — late-Qīng *quánshēng*-school popularisation; with the prior unfinished draft of [[畢法]].
- **c. 1810–1844** — *Wàikē bèiyào* 外科備要 ([[易鳳翥]]) [[KR3ek026]] — Dàoguāng practitioner's handbook.
- **c. 1820–1862** — *Wàikē zhèngzhì mìyào* 外科證治秘要 ([[王旭高]]) [[KR3ek023]] — Mènghé / Wúxī continuation of Gāo Bǐngjūn's *xīndé* lineage.
- **1840** — *Fābèi duìkǒu zhìjué lùn* 發背對口治訣論 ([[謝應材]]) [[KR3ek043]] — specialist tract on back and nape carbuncles.
- **c. 1850–1930** — *Wàikē xué huà yì* 外科學話義 [[KR3ek030]] — late-Qīng / early-Republican anonymous didactic.
- **c. 1862–1893** — *Wàikē fāngwài qífāng* 外科方外奇方 ([[凌奐]]) [[KR3ek016]] — late-Qīng formulary outside the "regular" tradition.
- **c. 1883–1916** — *Wàikē yījìng* 外科醫鏡 ([[高思敬]]) [[KR3ek010]] — late-Qīng / Republican-transition synthesis.
- **1886** — *Qīngnáng quánjí mìzhǐ* 青囊全集秘旨 ([[黃廷爵]]) [[KR3ek025]] — late-Qīng "green-bag" surgical anthology.
- **1892–1893** — *Wàikē chuánxīn jí* 外科傳薪集 ([[馬培之]]) [[KR3ek008]] — formulary of the late-Qīng Mènghé master.
- **1909–1910** — *Lìkē quánshū* 癧科全書 ([[梁希曾]]) [[KR3ek044]] — late-Qīng scrofula monograph.
- **1917–1927** — *Yángkē gāngyào* 瘍科綱要 ([[張壽頤]]) [[KR3ek034]] — Republican-era ulcer-medicine framework.
- **1918** — *Tánlì fǎmén* 痰癧法門 ([[李子毅]]) [[KR3ek033]] — Republican-era specialist on phlegm-node disorders.
- **1939–1948** — *Wàikē shísān fāng kǎo* 外科十三方考 ([[張覺人]]) [[KR3ek018]] — the latest work in the division; Republican-era critical investigation of the *shísān fāng* surgical-formula tradition.
