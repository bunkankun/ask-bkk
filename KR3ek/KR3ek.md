# KR3ek — Wài-kē 外科 (External Medicine / Surgery)

## Scope and scholarly tradition

KR3ek is the surgical sub-division of KR3e 醫家類, itself part of the Masters (KR3 子部). The genre covers what Chinese medicine has called *wài-kē* 外科 ("external medicine"): the suppurative, ulcerative, cutaneous, traumatic, and contact-transmissible diseases that present on the body's surface — *yōng* 癰 (carbuncle), *jū* 疽 (deep abscess), *dīng* 疔 (felon / boil), *chuāng-yáng* 瘡瘍 (ulcers), *luǒ-lì* 瘰癧 (scrofula), *bèi-jū* 背疽 (back carbuncles), *rǔ-yán* 乳岩 (breast tumour), *zhì lòu* 痔瘻 (hemorrhoidal fistula), and the *má-fēng* 麻風 / *lì* 癘 (leprosy) and *méi chuāng* 黴瘡 (later: syphilis) complexes — together with the operative techniques used to treat them (lancing, cautery, *biān-lián* 砭鐮, plaster, ligature, the corrosive *shēng-dān* / *jiàng-dān* preparations, and a full pharmacy of *gāo-dān-wán-sǎn* 膏丹丸散 dosage forms). The genre's chronological centre of gravity is the Sòng–Míng–Qīng arc; the corpus collected here runs from the 5th-century *Liú Juān-zǐ guǐ-yí fāng* [[KR3ek040]] — the earliest extant Chinese surgical monograph — to Republican-period (民國) syntheses such as Zhāng Shòu-yí's [[KR3ek034]] *Yáng-kē gāng-yào* (1917–27) and Zhāng Juérén's [[KR3ek018]] *Wài-kē shí-sān fāng kǎo* (1939–48).

The social history of the genre is its principal contextual key, and is given a programmatic discussion in Hinrichs & Barnes 2013 (chapter 5, especially the section on the Yuán court surgeon Qí Dé-zhī 齊德之 — author of [[KR3ek005]] *Wài-kē jīng-yì*). Through most of the late-imperial period, learned literati-physicians (*rú-yī* 儒醫) regarded *wài-kē* as a low-status manual specialty fit for *chuāng-yī* 瘡醫 ("sore-doctors") rather than for scholars, and Confucian medical ethics actively discouraged the cutting of living bodies. A central polemical move running through this corpus is the surgical writer's attempt to re-classify *wài-kē* as a learned discipline: from Chén Zìmíng's [[KR3ek012]] *Wài-kē jīng-yào* slogan *yōng-jū bì běn yú nèi* 癰疽必本於內 ("abscesses and carbuncles invariably have their root in the interior"), through Qí Dé-zhī's [[KR3ek005]] importation of classical pulse-diagnosis into surgical practice, Wāng Jī's [[KR3ek045]] *bǔ-tǔ* internalisation, Xuē Jǐ's [[KR3ek022]] [[KR3ek024]] [[KR3ek039]] [[KR3ek038]] *wēn-bǔ* (warming-tonification) doctrine, Chén Shí-gōng's [[KR3ek014]] ethical-and-operative *zhèng-zōng*, Wáng Hóng-xù's [[KR3ek017]] radical interiorisation of the *yīn-yáng* differential, to Gāo Bǐng-jūn's [[KR3ek042]] application of Wēn-bìng 溫病學 theory to surgical aetiology — the corpus reads as a sustained argument for surgery as a learned art rather than a craft. The same period also yields specialist monographs on the diseases that fell hardest on the surgically-treated population: leprosy ([[KR3ek035]], [[KR3ek038]], [[KR3ek041]]), syphilis ([[KR3ek048]]), scrofula ([[KR3ek044]], [[KR3ek033]]).

## Important texts and text clusters

The division's principal works fall into recognisable clusters that an outline of the field has to keep distinct:

- **The 5th-century foundation.** [[KR3ek040]] *Liú Juān-zǐ guǐ-yí fāng* 劉涓子鬼遺方 — the earliest extant Chinese specialist surgical monograph; in 5 *juǎn*, with the 499 CE colophon of [[龔慶宣]] over earlier military-medical material attributed to [[劉涓子]]. The opening *xù-lùn* 序論 ("ghost-bequeathed prescriptions" foundation myth) is one of the most famous origin narratives of any Chinese medical genre.

- **The Sòng–Yuán doctrinal turn.** [[KR3ek003]] *Wèi-jì bǎo-shū* 衛濟寶書 (Northern Sòng), [[KR3ek037]] *Jí-yàn bèi-jū fāng* 集驗背疽方 ([[李迅]], c. 1180–96) — Sòng forerunners; and the two foundational *nèi-wài jiān-zhì* (combined internal-and-external treatment) texts: [[KR3ek012]] *Wài-kē jīng-yào* 外科精要 ([[陳自明]], 1263) and [[KR3ek005]] *Wài-kē jīng-yì* 外科精義 ([[齊德之]], 1330s). Chén Zìmíng codifies the principle that surgical lesions have an internal root; Qí Dé-zhī forces pulse-diagnosis into the surgical case. Together these two works set the doctrinal grammar that every subsequent late-imperial wài-kē writer either inherits or reacts to.

- **Míng synthesis — the move into the literati canon.** The Xīn-ān school's [[KR3ek045]] *Wài-kē lǐ-lì* ([[汪機]], 1531); the [[薛己]] *wēn-bǔ* corpus — [[KR3ek024]] *Wài-kē xīn-fǎ*, [[KR3ek039]] *Lì-zhāi wài-kē fā-huī*, [[KR3ek022]] *Wài-kē shū-yào* (printed posthumously 1571), [[KR3ek038]] *Lì-yáng jī-yào* (1554); [[KR3ek021]] *Wài-kē qǐ-xuán* ([[申拱辰]], 1604); and the great late-Míng synthesis [[KR3ek014]] *Wài-kē zhèng-zōng* ([[陳實功]], 1617). Chén Shí-gōng's *Wài-kē zhèng-zōng* is the founding text of the so-called **正宗派** *zhèng-zōng pài* — the operative-surgical school that retains knife and cautery — and includes the first systematic Chinese clinical description of *rǔ-yán* 乳岩 (breast cancer), the *wǔ shàn qī è* 五善七惡 prognostic rubric, and the *wǔ jiè shí yào* 五戒十要 (surgeon's ethical code).

- **The Míng standards-of-practice and illustrated tradition.** [[KR3ek027]] *Zhèng-zhì zhǔn-shéng · Yáng-yī* ([[王肯堂]], 1602–08), the surgical division of Wáng Kěn-táng's enormous *zhǔn-shéng* "standards" project; [[KR3ek032]] *Xīn-kè tú-xíng zhěn-cáng wài-kē* (late Wàn-lì / Chóng-zhēn), an 80-diagram illustrated primer that anticipates the topographical lesion-mapping of the imperial *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn*; [[KR3ek020]] *Wàn-shì mì-chuán wài-kē xīn-fǎ* (the Wàn 萬全 lineage, Jiā-jìng – Lóng-qìng).

- **The Qīng canonical triad.** Three works defined high-Qīng surgical practice and are routinely treated as a single argumentative complex: [[KR3ek011]] *Wài-kē dà chéng* ([[祁坤]], 1665), the early-Qīng Imperial Physician's grand synthesis; [[KR3ek009]] *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn · Wài-kē xīn-fǎ yào-jué* ([[吳謙]] ed., 1742), the imperially commissioned surgical curriculum that re-mediated Qí Kūn's work through his grandson [[祁宏源]] and became the *de facto* Qīng standard for two centuries; and [[KR3ek017]] *Wài-kē quán-shēng jí* ([[王洪緒]], 1740, expanded by [[馬培之]] in 1883), the founding text of the **全生派** *quán-shēng pài* — the radically internal, anti-operative school that opposes Chén Shí-gōng's *zhèng-zōng pài* and reduces all surgical pathology to the single *yīn-yáng* differential of red *yōng* / white *jū*. The doctrinal debate *zhèng-zōng* vs. *quán-shēng* is the central methodological axis of late-imperial wài-kē.

- **The Qīng wài-kē encyclopedia.** [[KR3ek013]] *Yáng-yī dà-quán* 瘍醫大全 ([[顧世澄]], 1760–73), 40 *juǎn* — the largest single-author pre-modern Chinese surgical compendium, conceived as a wài-kē counterpart to the imperial *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn* and sitting deliberately between the *zhèng-zōng* and *quán-shēng* poles.

- **The Wēn-bìng / *xīn-dé* / Mèng-hé Qīng line.** [[KR3ek042]] *Yáng-kē xīn-dé jí* ([[高秉鈞]], 1805) is the third member of the canonical Qīng triad (with the *Zhèng-zōng* and *Quán-shēng*), founding the **心得派** *xīn-dé pài* and applying Sūzhōu Wēn-bìng theory systematically to external pathology. The Jiāng-nán Mèng-hé 孟河 lineage continues through [[KR3ek023]] *Wài-kē zhèng-zhì mì-yào* ([[王旭高]], Gāo Bǐng-jūn's nephew) and culminates in [[KR3ek008]] *Wài-kē chuán-xīn jí* ([[馬培之]], 1892–93) — the formulary of the late-Qīng's most prestigious wài-kē physician, summoned in 1880 to treat the Empress Dowager Cí-xǐ 慈禧.

- **Other Qīng practitioner / lineage texts.** [[KR3ek001]] *Wài-kē zhèng-zhì quán-shū* ([[許克昌]], 1808–31, a Hézhōu popularisation of the *quán-shēng* school); [[KR3ek002]] *Wài-kē shí-fǎ* ([[程國彭]], 1732); [[KR3ek015]] *Wài-kē xuǎn-yào* ([[唐黌]], 1776); [[KR3ek026]] *Wài-kē bèi-yào* ([[易鳳翥]]); [[KR3ek028]] *Tóng-yuán yī-shū · Wài-kē* ([[鄭玉壇]], late Qián-lóng); [[KR3ek016]] *Wài-kē fāng-wài qí-fāng* ([[凌奐]], late Qīng); [[KR3ek043]] *Fā-bèi duì-kǒu zhì-jué lùn* (1840); [[KR3ek036]] *Yáng-kē jié-jìng*; [[KR3ek010]] *Wài-kē yī-jìng* ([[高思敬]], late Qīng – early Republican); and the texts attributed to [[傅山]] — [[KR3ek031]] *Fù-shì wài-kē* and [[KR3ek019]] *Qīng-náng mì-jué* — together with the related [[KR3ek025]] *Qīng-náng quán-jí mì-zhǐ* (1886) and [[KR3ek029]] *Dòng-tiān ào-zhǐ* ([[陳士鐸]]).

- **Specialist disease monographs.** Leprosy / *má-fēng* / *lì*: [[KR3ek035]] *Jiě-wéi yuán-sǒu* ([[沈之問]], Jiā-jìng) and [[KR3ek038]] *Lì-yáng jī-yào* (Xuē Jǐ, 1554) are the two principal Míng witnesses to Angela Leung's reconstructed reconceptualisation of leprosy as a transmissible disease; [[KR3ek041]] *Fēng-mén quán-shū* ([[肖曉亭]], 1796–1845) is the most developed Qīng monograph in the same line. Scrofula / *luǒ-lì*: [[KR3ek044]] *Lì-kē quán-shū* ([[梁希曾]], 1909) and [[KR3ek033]] *Tán-lì fǎ-mén* ([[李子毅]], 1918). Hernia: [[KR3ek049]] *Shàn-qì zhèng-zhì lùn* and [[KR3ek050]] *Shàn-zhèng jī-jù*.

- **Edo Sino-Japanese surgical writing.** Three Edo monographs that participate in the Chinese wài-kē tradition while channelling early Dutch (*lán* 蘭) surgical knowledge eastward: [[KR3ek048]] *Bairai shinsho* / *Méi-lì xīn-shū* ([[片倉元周]] Katakura Genshū, 1786–87), the most influential Edo treatise on syphilis and leprosy, incorporating three explicitly Dutch surgical-plaster recipes (*Diachylon*, *Basilicum*, etc.) via the Seikendō school; and [[KR3ek049]] and [[KR3ek050]] by [[大橋尚因]] (Ōhashi Shōin, 1778) on hernia.

- **Republican-period syntheses.** [[KR3ek034]] *Yáng-kē gāng-yào* ([[張壽頤]], 1917–27); [[KR3ek018]] *Wài-kē shí-sān fāng kǎo* ([[張覺人]], 1939–48); [[KR3ek030]] *Wài-kē xué huà yì* (late Qīng / early Republican, anon.); [[KR3ek047]] *Chún-yì-lú jí (chuāng-yáng)* (Qīng anthology). [[KR3ek046]] *Yōng-jū shén-mì yàn-fāng* ([[陶華]], with [[薛己]] editorial collation, mid-Míng) closes the Míng end of the long Hé-jiān / Xīn-ān popular-formulary tradition.

## Important persons

- [[劉涓子]] (Liú Juān-zǐ, *fl.* late Jìn / early Liú-Sòng) and [[龔慶宣]] (Gōng Qìng-xuān, 499 colophon) — the eponymous attribution and the actual recension-editor of the earliest extant Chinese surgical monograph [[KR3ek040]].
- [[陳自明]] (Chén Zìmíng, *fl.* mid-13th c.) — Southern-Sòng author of [[KR3ek012]] *Wài-kē jīng-yào*, codifier of the *yōng-jū bì běn yú nèi* doctrine that frames the rest of the division.
- [[齊德之]] (Qí Dé-zhī, *fl.* 1330s) — Yuán court surgeon (御藥院外科太醫), author of [[KR3ek005]] *Wài-kē jīng-yì*; explicitly cited in Hinrichs & Barnes 2013 as a representative of the Yuán-period polemic against unlettered *chuāng-yī*.
- [[汪機]] (Wāng Jī, 1463–1539) — founder of the Xīn-ān 新安 medical school, author of [[KR3ek045]] *Wài-kē lǐ-lì* (1531); the first Míng systematic absorption of *wài-kē* into literati internal-medicine *bǔ-tǔ* doctrine.
- [[薛己]] (Xuē Jǐ, 1487–1559) — Sūzhōu imperial physician, the single most prolific Míng wài-kē author. Four works in this division ([[KR3ek022]], [[KR3ek024]], [[KR3ek038]], [[KR3ek039]]) plus extensive editorial work on Chén Zìmíng [[KR3ek012]] and Táo Huá [[KR3ek046]]. Founder of the *wēn-bǔ* surgical doctrine.
- [[陳實功]] (Chén Shí-gōng, c. 1555–1636) — Nán-tōng surgeon, author of [[KR3ek014]] *Wài-kē zhèng-zōng* (1617). Founder of the *zhèng-zōng pài*; the most consequential Míng operative-surgical writer.
- [[王肯堂]] (Wáng Kěn-táng, 1549–1613) — Wàn-lì *jìn-shì* and one of the most learned encyclopaedists of the late Míng; author of [[KR3ek027]] *Zhèng-zhì zhǔn-shéng · Yáng-yī*, the surgical division of his *Zhèng-zhì zhǔn-shéng* "standards" project.
- [[祁坤]] (Qí Kūn, *fl.* Shùn-zhì – Kāng-xī) — Qīng Imperial Physician, author of [[KR3ek011]] *Wài-kē dà chéng* (1665); the principal source of the imperial *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn* surgical division through his grandson [[祁宏源]].
- [[吳謙]] (Wú Qiān, *fl.* early Qián-lóng) — chief editor of the imperial [[KR3ek009]] *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn · Wài-kē xīn-fǎ yào-jué* (1742); director of the *Tài-yī-yuàn*.
- [[王洪緒]] (Wáng Hóng-xù, c. 1669 – c. 1749) — Sūzhōu lay practitioner; founder of the *quán-shēng pài* with [[KR3ek017]] *Wài-kē quán-shēng jí* (1740), the great late-Qīng polemical counterweight to the *zhèng-zōng* school.
- [[顧世澄]] (Gù Shì-chéng, *fl.* Qián-lóng) — Wú-hú / Yáng-zhōu hereditary physician, author of the 40-*juǎn* encyclopaedia [[KR3ek013]] *Yáng-yī dà-quán* (1760–73).
- [[高秉鈞]] (Gāo Bǐng-jūn, 1755–1827) — Wú-xī physician; founder of the *xīn-dé pài* with [[KR3ek042]] *Yáng-kē xīn-dé jí* (1805); the third member of the Qīng canonical triad and the principal channel by which Wēn-bìng theory entered surgical practice.
- [[馬培之]] (Mǎ Péi-zhī, 1820–1903) — late-Qīng Mèng-hé synthesiser, summoned 1880 to treat the Empress Dowager Cí-xǐ; author of [[KR3ek008]] *Wài-kē chuán-xīn jí* and editor of the 1883 expanded recension of Wáng Hóng-xù's [[KR3ek017]].
- [[沈之問]] and [[肖曉亭]] — the principal authors of the Míng and Qīng leprosy monographs ([[KR3ek035]] and [[KR3ek041]]); together with Xuē Jǐ's [[KR3ek038]], the documentary backbone of Angela Leung's *Leprosy in China*.
- [[片倉元周]] (Katakura Genshū / Kakuryō, 1751–1822) — Edo physician of the Tanba 丹波 lineage; author of [[KR3ek048]] *Bairai shinsho* / *Méi-lì xīn-shū* (1786–87), notable for incorporating Dutch surgical-plaster recipes received at the *Seikendō*.
- [[傅山]] (Fù Shān, 1607–1684) — early-Qīng polymath; not primarily a surgeon, but two later texts in this division are attributed to him ([[KR3ek019]] *Qīng-náng mì-jué*, [[KR3ek031]] *Fù-shì wài-kē*) and represent the pseudepigraphic "green-bag" surgical tradition.

## Topics

- **The 癰 / 疽 *yīn-yáng* differential.** The single most pervasive theoretical axis: *yōng* (heat, surface, redness, painful, *yáng*) versus *jū* (cold, deep, leathery, *yīn*). Programmatically schematised in [[KR3ek040]] *Liú Juān-zǐ guǐ-yí fāng*; restated in every subsequent generation; reduced by Wáng Hóng-xù [[KR3ek017]] to the sole diagnostic criterion needed in *wài-kē*.

- ***Zhèng-zōng* 正宗 vs. *quán-shēng* 全生 — operative versus internalist surgery.** The central methodological controversy of the Qīng. The *zhèng-zōng pài* (Chén Shí-gōng [[KR3ek014]]) retains active operative intervention (knife, cautery, corrosive *shēng-dān*); the *quán-shēng pài* (Wáng Hóng-xù [[KR3ek017]]) rejects most operative methods in favour of internal *yīn-yáng* prescriptions. Practical Qīng practice (Gù Shì-chéng [[KR3ek013]], Gāo Bǐng-jūn [[KR3ek042]]) sits between the two, and the imperial [[KR3ek009]] consolidates a middle position.

- ***Nèi-wài jiān-zhì* 內外兼治 — surgery's claim to literati status.** The standing argument, from Chén Zìmíng [[KR3ek012]] through Qí Dé-zhī [[KR3ek005]], Wāng Jī [[KR3ek045]], Xuē Jǐ [[KR3ek022]] [[KR3ek024]] [[KR3ek039]], and Qí Kūn [[KR3ek011]], that surgical lesions are internally rooted and that the surgeon's first duty is *nèi-tuō* 內托 (internal supportive tonification), not topical intervention. This argument runs against the *chuāng-yī* / *yáng-yī* practitioner tradition criticised in Hinrichs & Barnes 2013.

- **The five-good and seven-bad prognostic rubric (*wǔ shàn qī è* 五善七惡).** Articulated in Chén Shí-gōng [[KR3ek014]] and absorbed wholesale into the imperial [[KR3ek009]]; a standardised differential of curable from fatal cases that remained in active use through the Qīng.

- **Wēn-bìng 溫病學 and *huǒ-dú* 火毒 aetiology.** Gāo Bǐng-jūn [[KR3ek042]] systematically imports the Sūzhōu Wēn-bìng theory of *huǒ-dú* / *shī-rè* accumulation into surgical pathology, decoupling *yōng* / *jū* from a pure structural anatomy and reading them as expressions of channel-organ heat.

- **Contagion theory and chronic skin disease.** [[KR3ek035]], [[KR3ek038]], [[KR3ek041]], [[KR3ek048]] form the principal Chinese (and Sino-Japanese) corpus on leprosy and syphilis as transmissible diseases, with both inherited (*fù-mǔ jīng-xuè*) and contact (*qì-huì*) routes; the locus classicus for Angela Leung's *Leprosy in China* (Columbia, 2009) and for Hinrichs & Barnes 2013 on Chinese pre-modern infectious-disease thinking.

- **Topographical organisation and the illustrated tradition.** Most late-Míng and Qīng wài-kē texts arrange disease "from head to foot," and the illustrated tradition culminates in the imperial [[KR3ek009]] illustrated lesion charts. [[KR3ek032]] *Zhěn-cáng wài-kē* anticipates this organisation in the late Míng; [[KR3ek013]] *Yáng-yī dà-quán* applies it on an encyclopaedic scale.

- **Sino-Japanese transmission and the reception of Dutch (*lán* 蘭) surgery.** Katakura's [[KR3ek048]] (1786–87) is the most explicit case: Dutch surgical-plaster recipes received at the Seikendō are absorbed into a Chinese-language wài-kē framework alongside the standard *quán-shēng* and *zhèng-zōng* materials, then re-exported to China in the 19th century.

- **Lineage transmission, *chuán-xīn* 傳薪, and the case-record (*yī-àn* 醫案) as evidence.** Late-Qīng works in this division (Mǎ Péi-zhī [[KR3ek008]], the Mèng-hé and Wáng Xù-gāo [[KR3ek023]] line, Xǔ Kèchāng [[KR3ek001]]) treat surgical knowledge as private lineage capital, transmitted from master to disciple in formulary digests rather than published in full; the published recensions are typically posthumous or pupil-edited.

## Timeline

- **499 CE** — *Liú Juān-zǐ guǐ-yí fāng* 劉涓子鬼遺方 [[KR3ek040]] — earliest extant Chinese surgical monograph; recension of fifth-century battlefield-medical material by Gōng Qìng-xuān.
- **c. 1100–1170** — *Wèi-jì bǎo-shū* 衛濟寶書 [[KR3ek003]] — short Northern-Sòng surgical formulary by the Eastern-Studio Recluse.
- **c. 1180–1196** — *Jí-yàn bèi-jū fāng* 集驗背疽方 ([[李迅]]) [[KR3ek037]] — Sòng specialist monograph on back carbuncles; principal source for Chén Zìmíng.
- **1263** — *Wài-kē jīng-yào* 外科精要 ([[陳自明]]) [[KR3ek012]] — Southern-Sòng codification of the *yōng-jū bì běn yú nèi* doctrine; transmitted in [[薛己]]'s 1547 expanded recension.
- **c. 1330–1335** — *Wài-kē jīng-yì* 外科精義 ([[齊德之]]) [[KR3ek005]] — Yuán court-surgeon's importation of pulse-diagnosis into surgery.
- **c. 1378–1382** — *Xiān-chuán wài-kē jí-yàn fāng* 仙傳外科集驗方 ([[趙宜真]]) [[KR3ek004]] — Yuán-Míng transition; Daoist *xiān-chuán* surgical formulary derived from [[楊清叟]].
- **c. 1378–1395** — *Mì-chuán wài-kē fāng* 秘傳外科方 ([[趙宜真]]) [[KR3ek007]] — companion to [[KR3ek004]].
- **c. 1430–1450** — *Yōng-jū shén-mì yàn-fāng* 癰疽神秘驗方 ([[陶華]], with [[薛己]] editorial collation) [[KR3ek046]] — mid-Míng popular-formulary witness.
- **1498** — *Wài-kē jí-yàn fāng* 外科集驗方 ([[周文采]]) [[KR3ek006]] — mid-Míng Hóng-zhì compilation.
- **1522–1566** — *Jiě-wéi yuán-sǒu* 解圍元藪 ([[沈之問]]) [[KR3ek035]] — Jiā-jìng-era leprosy monograph; key Míng witness to contagion theory.
- **1528** — *Lì-zhāi wài-kē fā-huī* 立齋外科發揮 ([[薛己]]) [[KR3ek039]] — Xuē Jǐ's first major surgical work.
- **c. 1528–1559** — *Wài-kē xīn-fǎ* 外科心法 ([[薛己]]) [[KR3ek024]] — programmatic statement of the *wēn-bǔ* surgical doctrine.
