# Yáng-yī dà-quán 瘍醫大全
*Complete Compendium of Ulcer Medicine*
by [[顧世澄]] (Gù Shìchéng, also Gù Chéng 顧澄; *zì* Liàn-jiāng 練江; *hào* Jìng-zhāi 靜齋, *fl.* Qián-lóng, 清) — third-generation hereditary physician of Wú-hú 蕪湖 (Ān-huī), practising in Yáng-zhōu 揚州.

## About the work

A forty-*juǎn* surgical encyclopedia, the most comprehensive single Qīng *wài-kē* reference and the largest pre-modern Chinese surgical compendium overall. Compilation completed in Qián-lóng 25 (1760), additional prefaces added in 1762 and 1773 (the printing date). Gù was the third generation of a hereditary medical family, preserving his grandfather Níng-huá 寧華's and father Qīng-yán 青岩's secret prescriptions alongside his own and an exhaustive citation of the prior surgical literature. The work was conceived as a *wài-kē* counterpart to the imperial [[KR3ek009]] *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn* (1742) and was financially sponsored by fellow Huái-region physicians.

### Abstract

Three prefaces frame the work in the Kanripo digitisation: that of Wāng Lì-dé 汪立德 (1773), the author's own *zì-xù* dated *Qián-lóng yǐ-mào* (1760), and that of Qiáo Guāng-liè 喬光烈 (1762), plus a fifteen-item *fán-lì* 凡例. Gù explicitly describes the work as inspired by the imperial *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn* compilation effort and conceived as a defining *wài-kē* synthesis bridging *nèi-jīng* doctrinal foundations, pulse-doctrine, channel topography, and topographically organised surgical cases.

The forty *juǎn* divide as follows: *juǎn* 1–7, classical theory, pulses, channels, principles of inner pattern; *juǎn* 8–35, topographically ordered ailments from crown to sole, each with woodcut illustration, classical formula, master commentary, and family secret recipes; *juǎn* 36–40, paediatric and obstetric surgery, including a smallpox section drawn from Niè Jiǔ-wú 聶九吾 and Zhū Chún-hè 朱純嘏, trauma (knife / fire / falls), animal bites and toxic ingestions, with a closing *Hán mén* 寒門 (cold-damage gateway) capsule for emergency reference. Every lesion is illustrated, making the work the largest single-author illustrated Chinese surgical compendium.

The methodological centre of the work is its systematic completion of the *nèi-wài jiān-zhì* programme inaugurated by Chén Zìmíng's [[KR3ek012]] *Wài-kē jīng-yào* (1263), codified by Chén Shí-gōng's [[KR3ek014]] *Wài-kē zhèng-zōng* (1617), and given imperial sanction in the *Yī-zōng jīn-jiàn* (1742). Where Chén Shí-gōng pulled the *wài-kē* tradition toward operative surgery and Wáng Hóng-xù's [[KR3ek017]] *Quán-shēng jí* (1740) pulled it sharply toward internal tonification, Gù Shì-chéng's synthesis sits comprehensively in the middle. The *Yáng-yī dà-quán* remained the standard Qīng *wài-kē* desk reference into the Republican period.

Gù's lifedates are not documented; he speaks of forty-plus years of practice in his 1760 preface, suggesting birth in the early 18th century.

## Other points of interest

The work has been valued by historians of Chinese surgical iconography for its over one thousand woodcut lesion illustrations — among the largest single illustrated corpora in pre-modern Chinese medicine.
