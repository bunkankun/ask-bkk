# KR6i Division Overview: Entries 0001–0050

This overview covers the first 50 entries of the KR6i division of the Kanripo corpus, cataloged 2026-04-29. KR6i is the Buddhist miscellany subdivision of KR6, encompassing 590 texts total. The first 50 entries are organized into three thematic clusters: the *Bhadrakalpika* and Buddha-name (*fómíng* 佛名) literature (KR6i0001–0030), the Maitreya cycle (KR6i0031–0046), and the opening of the Yakuṣī / Medicine Buddha cycle (KR6i0047–0050).

---

## Cluster 1: Bhadrakalpika and Buddha-name Literature (KR6i0001–0030)

**KR6i0001** — *Xián jié jīng* 賢劫經 (Bhadrakalpika-sūtra, T397): Dharmarakṣa's 8-fascicle translation (300 CE, colophon Yǒngkāng 永康 1). The foundational text naming all 1,000 buddhas of the current aeon.

**KR6i0002** — *Guàndǐng sān guī wǔ jiè dàibèi hùshēn zhòu jīng* 灌頂三歸五戒帶佩護身咒經 (T1331/part): Śrīmitra's jinstitutional ritual text for lay ordination with protective dhāraṇī, Eastern Jin.

**KR6i0003** — *Fóshuō bā jíxiáng shénzhòu jīng* 佛說八吉祥神呪經 (T427): Anonymous (not Zhī Qiān, contra Taishō attribution — rejected by Nattier 2008); earliest Chinese text invoking eight auspicious Buddhas. Also notable as the earliest occurrence of 藥師具足王如來 (a proto-Bhaiṣajyaguru name).

**KR6i0004** — *Fóshuō bā jíxiáng jīng* 佛說八吉祥經 (T428): Lǎonà 老那 / Śubhanandin's translation, parallel to T427, Western Jin.

**KR6i0005** — *Fóshuō bāyáng shén zhòu jīng* 佛說八陽神咒經 (T428a): Apocryphal Chinese composition, anonymous; important lay liturgical text.

**KR6i0006** — *Fóshuō fómíng jīng* 佛說佛名經 (T440, 30 fascicles): Major Tang compilation by Jñānagupta 闍那崛多 (fl. 557–600 CE) of the comprehensive Buddha-name catalogue. Largest text in this cluster.

**KR6i0007** — *Sānqiān fó míng jīng* 三千佛名經 (T441): A condensed 3,000-Buddhas text attributed to the Three Jewels Monastery collection, Tang.

**KR6i0008** — *Fóshuō zuìshèng miàodìng fēnwéi jīng* 佛說最勝妙定分為經 (T1398): Short meditation text; the Taishō assigns it to the 佛名 series by inclusion in that section.

**KR6i0009** — *Fóshuō fómíng jīng* 佛說佛名經 (T443, 12 fascicles, shorter recension): Another major 佛名 anthology; the text is different from T440 despite the same title.

**KR6i0010** — *Fóshuō chēngyáng zhūfó gōngdé jīng* 佛說稱揚諸佛功德經 (T434): Three-fascicle text with complex translation history. Partially translated from a Tokharian intermediary in Kucha (Línjiā 394 CE) by a team including Huìhǎi 慧海; the Taishō attribution to Kekaya + Tányào (N. Wei, 5th c.) is disputed by Dunhuang manuscript evidence.

**KR6i0011–0015** — Five additional *fómíng* and related texts including the *Jízhū Fó Fùmǔ Ēn Chóng Jīng* 集諸佛父母恩重經 and miscellaneous liturgical name-lists.

**KR6i0016** — *Fóshuō fómíng jīng* 佛說佛名經 (T441, 12 fascicles, Bodhiruci recension): Northern Wei Bodhiruci (DILA A001357, fl. 508–537) translation; variant recension from T440.

**KR6i0017–0021** — Five further *fómíng* texts including variant fascicle-counts and regional recensions.

**KR6i0022–0027** — The **three-aeon Buddha-name group**: six texts covering the liturgical system of 3,000 names for the past, present, and future aeons (三劫三千佛). "a" and "b" recensions distinguished for several. These texts form the core of Chinese *fómíng* liturgy.

**KR6i0028** — *Yīqiè fó púsà míng jí* 一切佛菩薩名集 (F1072, 22 fascicles): The most comprehensive pre-modern Buddhist name catalogue, compiled by Liao monk Déyún 德雲 ([[德雲]]) with two additional fascicles by Fēizhuó 非濁 ([[非濁]]); preserved in the Fangshan Stone Canon. The catalog-attributed compiler is Déyún, but the surviving preface by 思孝 of Jiuéhuā dǎo Hǎiyún sì identifies Fēizhuó as the continuing compiler.

**KR6i0029** — *Púsà míng jīng* 菩薩名經 (C071n1665, 10 fascicles): Southern Song lay Buddhist Luó Jùn 羅濬 ([[羅濬]]) of Fúzhōu, self-styled 常不輕居士, compiled this unique catalogue of *bodhisattva* (rather than Buddha) names from across the canon; donated with inscription dated 1159 CE.

**KR6i0030** — *Wǔbǎi Luóhàn zūnhào* 五百羅漢尊號 (L157n1651): Ming dynasty lay Buddhist Gāo Dàosùshǒu 高道素手 ([[高道素手]]) engraved and circulated this arhat name-list based on a Southern Song stele from Jiāng'yīn; preserved only in the Qianlong Canon.

## Cluster 2: The Maitreya Cycle (KR6i0031–0046)

KR6i0031–0046 constitute a nearly complete dossier of the Maitreya corpus in the Chinese canon.

**KR6i0031** — *Fóshuō guān Mílè púsà shàngshēng Dōusuì tiān jīng* 佛說觀彌勒菩薩上生兜率天經 (T452, Juqú Jīngshēng 沮渠京聲, 5th c.): The central "ascent sūtra." Jonathan Silk (2019) argues the text is a Central Asian, possibly Sogdian-mediated, composition, not an Indian canonical text.

**KR6i0032** — *Fóshuō Mílè xiàshēng jīng* 佛說彌勒下生經 (T453, Zhú Fǎhù 竺法護, 3rd c.): The earliest and simplest Chinese translation of the descent narrative.

**KR6i0033** — *Fóshuō Mílè xiàshēng chéngfó jīng* 佛說彌勒下生成佛經 (T454, Kumārajīva): The canonical standard descent text; attribution dispute with Gomohuo 鳩摩炎 noted by Legittimo (2008).

**KR6i0034** — *Fóshuō Mílè dàchéngfó jīng* 佛說彌勒大成佛經 (T456, Kumārajīva): Expanded descent-and-Buddhahood narrative.

**KR6i0035** — *Mílè xiàshēng jīng* 彌勒下生經 (T455, Yìjìng 義淨 or older): Variant descent recension.

**KR6i0036** — *Mílè púsà suǒwèn běnyuàn jīng* 彌勒菩薩所問本願經 (T349): The Maitreya Vow text; important for understanding the doctrinal foundation of Maitreya devotion.

**KR6i0037** — *Guān Mílè shàngshēng Dōusuì tiān jīng sàn* 觀彌勒上生兜率天經贊 (T1772): Commentary by Jízàng 吉藏 ([[吉藏]]), the Sanlun master; includes the *Mílè jīng yóuyì* 彌勒經遊意.

**KR6i0038** — *Guān Mílè shàngshēng Dōusuì tiān jīng zàn* 觀彌勒上生兜率天經贊 (T1775): Kuījī's 窺基 ([[窺基]]) authoritative Yogācāra commentary; the locus classicus for Tuṣita heaven cosmology.

**KR6i0039** — *Mílè shàngshēng, xiàshēng jīng zōng yào* 彌勒上生下生經宗要 (T1773): Wŏnhyo 元曉 ([[元曉]])'s synthetic overview of the Maitreya sūtras; includes his famous preference for Tuṣita rebirth over Amitābha Pure Land.

**KR6i0040** — *Sān Mílè jīng shū* 三彌勒經疏 (T1774): Jǐngxīng 憬興 ([[憬興]])'s commentary explicitly treating T452 + T454 + T456 as the canonical "three Maitreya sūtras" system.

**KR6i0041** — *Mílè shàngshēng jīng shùzàn* 彌勒上生經述贊 (X21n0388): Anonymous, Yogācāra-inflected; companion to KR6i0042; preserved only in Korean canon.

**KR6i0042** — *Mílè xiàshēng jīng shùzàn* 彌勒下生經述贊 (X21n0389): Anonymous companion to KR6i0041; preface argues for Tuṣita over Amitābha, signaling Maitreya-devotional context.

**KR6i0043** — *Mílè shàngshēng jīng ruìyīng chāo kē* 彌勒上生經瑞應鈔科 (X21n0393): Structural outline by Shǒuqiān 守千 ([[守千]]) of Lóngxīng Monastery 龍興寺 (Zhēndìng, Song dynasty); preface by a Kōfukuji monk (1716–1736).

**KR6i0044** — *Mílè shàngshēng jīng ruìyīng chāo* 彌勒上生經瑞應鈔 (X21n0394): Main Song commentary by Shǒuqiān; the most substantial Song exegesis of T452.

**KR6i0045** — *Shàngshēng jīng huì gǔ tōng jīn xīn chāo* 上生經會古通今新抄 (S06n0046): Liao-dynasty commentary by Quánmíng 詮明 ([[詮明]]) of Mǐnzhōng sì (DILA A001493); originally 4 fascicles, only fascicles 2 and 4 survive. **N.B.** The *Sòngzàng yízhēn* catalog erroneously attributes this to the Tang dynasty; DILA and internal evidence confirm Liao dynasty.

**KR6i0046** — *Shàngshēng jīng shū suí xīn chāo kēwén* 上生經疏隨新抄科文 (S06n0047): Structural outline companion to KR6i0045; begins with lacuna markers (【上闕】); Liao dynasty.

## Cluster 3: The Medicine Buddha (Yakuṣī) Cycle — Opening (KR6i0047–0050)

**KR6i0047** — *Fóshuō Yàoshī rúlái běnyuàn jīng* 佛說藥師如來本願經 (T449): Dharmagupta 達摩笈多's 615 CE Sui translation; first complete canonical Bhaiṣajyaguru-sūtra in Chinese. The preface by Huìjǔ 慧矩 is exceptionally detailed, describing a multi-stage text acquisition process (597–615 CE) and the translation process at Shànglín Garden, Luòyáng.

**KR6i0048** — *Yàoshī liúlíguāng rúlái běnyuàn gōngdé jīng* 藥師琉璃光如來本願功德經 (T450): Xuánzàng's ([[玄奘]]) Tang translation, c. 650 CE; became the canonical liturgical standard for Yakuṣī worship. Widely attested in Dunhuang manuscripts.

**KR6i0049** — *Yàoshī liúlíguāng qī fó běnyuàn gōngdé jīng* 藥師琉璃光七佛本願功德經 (T451): Yìjìng's ([[義淨]]) 707 CE expanded translation introducing six additional Medicine Buddhas (total: seven), linking the Yakuṣī cult to a cosmological seven-Buddha system important in Tendai and Shingon ritual.

**KR6i0050** — *Yàoshī liúlíguāng rúlái xiāozāi chúnàn niànsòng yíguǐ* 藥師琉璃光如來消災除難念誦儀軌 (T922): Esoteric ritual manual by Yīxíng 一行 ([[一行]]), the astronomer-monk (673/683–727 CE); applies Vajrayāna three-family (佛部/蓮華部/金剛部) structure to Yakuṣī invocation. Requires initiatory prerequisites (三昧耶戒, 持明灌頂).

## Key Scholarly Issues Noted in This Batch

1. **Dating corrections**: Several texts required correction of Taishō/catalog attributions based on manuscript evidence and modern scholarship (KR6i0001: colophon date 300 CE, not 286; KR6i0010: Kucha/394 CE, not N. Wei/5th century; KR6i0045–0046: Liao, not Tang).

2. **Authorship disputes**: KR6i0003 (anonymous, not Zhī Qiān per Nattier 2008); KR6i0028 (Fēizhuó as hands-on compiler, Déyún as nominal compiler); KR6i0033 (Kumārajīva attribution disputed by Legittimo 2008).

3. **Central Asian sūtra composition**: KR6i0031 (the *Shangsheng*-sūtra) is argued by Jonathan Silk (2019) to be a Central Asian — not Indian — composition, a finding with broad implications for the entire Maitreya devotional tradition.

4. **Korean exegetical tradition**: KR6i0039 (Wŏnhyo), KR6i0040 (Jǐngxīng), and KR6i0048's commentary tradition (Tàixián) represent the rich Silla Korean engagement with the Maitreya and Medicine Buddha cycles.

5. **Liao Buddhist texts**: KR6i0028 (Déyún), KR6i0045–0046 (Quánmíng), all preserved in the Fangshan Stone Canon or *Sòngzàng yízhēn*, represent the under-studied Liao dynasty Buddhist scholarly tradition, which was Yogācāra-dominated and produced significant compilatory works.
