# Fózǔ sānjīng zhǐnán 佛祖三經指南
*A Compass to the Three Sūtras of the Buddha-Patriarchs*
expounded by [[道霈]] (Dàopèi = Wéilín, 述)

## About the work

X37 No. 675 in three fascicles is a comprehensive late-Míng / early-Qīng exposition of the **"Three Sūtras of the Buddha-Patriarchs"** (佛祖三經) — the [[KR6i0483|*Sìshí'èrzhāng jīng* 四十二章經]], the [[KR6i0476|*Bā dàrén jué jīng* 八大人覺經]], and the *Yíjiào jīng* 遺教經 — by the late Míng Cáodòng Chán master [[道霈]] (Dàopèi = Wéilín 為霖, 1615–1702). The work is preserved in the Manji *Zoku-zōkyō* (CBETA X37 No. 675).

### Abstract

Dàopèi's *Zhǐ-nán* ("compass," "guide") provides a unified expository treatment of the three sūtras as a single integrated curriculum. The work is structured in three fascicles, one per sūtra, each containing the full text of the sūtra followed by Dàopèi's running commentary. The unifying interpretive framework is the late-Míng synthesis of Cáo-dòng Chán with Tiāntái doctrinal categories and Pure Land devotion that characterised Dàopèi's teaching cycle: the *Sì-shí'èr-zhāng jīng* is read as the foundational *śrāvaka*-and-bodhisattva digest, the *Bā dà-rén jué jīng* as the *bodhisattva-yāna* contemplation-handbook, and the *Yí-jiào jīng* as the Buddha's final synthetic injunction to the *saṅgha*. The three together are presented as the **complete monastic introduction** to Buddhist practice — the canonical curriculum that every novice was expected to master.

The composition reflects the late-imperial consolidation of the *Sān-jīng* curriculum that had been promoted from the early Sòng onward (under the patronage of [[真宗皇帝|Sòng Zhēn-zōng]] and the editors of the imperial canons). Dàopèi's *Zhǐ-nán* provided the integrated commentary that the Sòng promotion had not produced, and it became one of the standard introductions to monastic Buddhism in the Cáo-dòng tradition during the Qīng. Dàopèi was a prolific commentator, with extensive works on the *Avataṃsaka*, the *Mahā-parinirvāṇa*, the *Yuán-jué jīng*, and many other texts; the *Fó-zǔ sān-jīng zhǐ-nán* is one of his most accessible compositions, designed for the ordinary monastic reader rather than the doctrinal specialist.
