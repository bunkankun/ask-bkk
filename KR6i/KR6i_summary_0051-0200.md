# KR6i Division Overview: Entries 0051–0200

This overview covers entries 51 to 200 of the KR6i division of the Kanripo corpus, cataloged 2026-04-29. The 150 texts in this batch span the major Mahāyāna sūtra families and their commentarial traditions: the *Guàndǐng* (Consecration) cycle, the closing of the Yakuṣī commentaries, the Mañjuśrī cycle, the Vimalakīrti translation and commentary tradition, the bodhisattva-question literature, the Ānanda-Kāśyapa narratives, the kings/elders/children sūtra cycle, and the women's-narrative cluster. The texts span from Eastern Hàn (148 CE) through the Northern Sòng (1054 CE), encompassing twelve centuries of Chinese Buddhist translation history.

---

## Cluster 1: Guàndǐng + Yakuṣī Commentaries Closing (KR6i0051–0058)

**KR6i0051** — *Fóshuō guàndǐng jīng* 佛說灌頂經 (T1331): the great twelve-fascicle composite of dhāraṇī, ritual, and divinatory texts attributed to Śrīmitra 帛尸梨蜜多羅 ([[帛尸梨蜜多羅]]; fl. 317–343); fascicles 11–12 are fifth-century Chinese compositions.

**KR6i0052** — Independent Fángshān stone-canon recension of the proto-Yakuṣī (T1331/12): the Huìjiǎn 慧簡 composition of 457 CE.

**KR6i0053** — *Běnyuàn Yàoshī jīng gǔjī* 本願藥師經古迹 (T1770) by the Silla Yogācāra master Tàixián 太賢 ([[太賢]]): the principal Korean commentary on the Yakuṣī cycle, summarizing all three Chinese translations.

**KR6i0054, 0055** — Two Dunhuang anonymous Yakuṣī commentaries (T2766, T2767), the former on Huìjiǎn's recension, the latter on Dharmagupta's.

**KR6i0056** — *Yàoshī jīng zhíjiě* 藥師經直解 (X0381): the late-Ming Tiantai commentary by Língyào 靈耀, printed in Edo Japan (1686).

**KR6i0057** — *Yàoshī sānmèi xíngfǎ* 藥師三昧行法 (X1483) by the late-Ming Tiantai master Shòudēng 受登: a *xíngfǎ* (ritual-meditation manual) on the Yakuṣī cult.

**KR6i0058** — *Cíbēi Yàoshī bǎochàn* 慈悲藥師寶懺 (X1484): the anonymous Ming-Qīng standardized Yakuṣī repentance liturgy still in active use.

## Cluster 2: The Mañjuśrī Cycle (KR6i0059–0074)

A nearly-complete corpus of Chinese Mañjuśrī sūtras spanning the Eastern Hàn through Sòng. Major texts:

**KR6i0059** — *Wénshūshīlì wèn púsà shǔ jīng* (T458) by Lokakṣema 支婁迦讖, the earliest Chinese Mañjuśrī sūtra (Eastern Hàn, ca. 178–189 CE).

**KR6i0060–0062** — Three major Mañjuśrī sūtras by Dharmarakṣa 竺法護 ([[竺法護]]; W. Jìn): *Wénshū huǐguò jīng* (T459, repentance), *Wénshūshīlì jìnglǜ jīng* (T460, pure-Vinaya), and *Wénshūshīlì xiàn bǎozàng jīng* (T461, treasure-store).

**KR6i0063** — *Dàfāngguǎng bǎoqiè jīng* (T462) by Guṇabhadra 求那跋陀羅, parallel to T461.

**KR6i0064** — *Wénshūshīlì bān nièpán jīng* (T463) by Niè Dàozhēn 聶道真: the canonical source for Mañjuśrī's connection to Wǔtáishān 五臺山.

**KR6i0065–0068** — The four-translation *Gayāśīrṣa-sūtra* family: Kumārajīva (T464), Bodhiruci I (T465), Vinītaruci (T466), and Bodhiruci II (T467).

**KR6i0069** — *Wénshū wèn jīng* (T468) by Saṅghapāla 僧伽婆羅, important for the eighteen-sects (*nikāya-bheda*) chapter.

**KR6i0070** — *Wénshū wèn jīng zìmǔ pǐn* (T469) by Amoghavajra 不空: the Sanskrit-alphabet (*akṣaramātṛkā*) chapter as an esoteric ritual text.

**KR6i0071–0072** — Two parallel translations of the *Mañjuśrī-vihāra-sūtra*: Bodhiruci I (T470) and Jñānagupta (T471).

**KR6i0073–0074** — Two late Sòng Mañjuśrī sūtras by Tiānxīzāi 天息災 / Fǎxián 法賢 (T472, T473), using *Miàojíxiáng* 妙吉祥 as the Mañjuśrī-translation idiom.

## Cluster 3: The Vimalakīrti Cycle (KR6i0075–0105)

**Three canonical translations** (KR6i0075–0077):
- T474 *Fóshuō Wéimójié jīng* by Zhī Qiān 支謙 (Wú, 222–252 CE) — the earliest Chinese version.
- T475 *Wéimójié suǒshuō jīng* by Kumārajīva 鳩摩羅什 (Yáo Qín, 406 CE) — the canonical classical version.
- T476 *Shuō wúgòuchēng jīng* by Xuánzàng 玄奘 (Tang, 650 CE) — the literal Yogācāra translation.

**Major commentaries** (KR6i0078–0085):
- T1775 *Zhù Wéimójié jīng* by Sēngzhào 僧肇 (the earliest, preserving Kumārajīva's lecture-record).
- T1776 *Wéimó yìjì* by Jìngyǐng Huìyuǎn 慧遠 (Sui Dìlùn / Yogācāra).
- T1777 *Wéimó jīng xuán shū* + T1778 *Wéimó jīng lüè shū* + T1779 *Chuíyù jì* — the Tiantai canonical commentary by Zhīyǐ 智顗 / Zhànrán 湛然 / Zhīyuán 智圓.
- T1780 *Jìngmíng xuán lùn* + T1781 *Wéimó jīng yìshū* by Jízàng 吉藏 (Sānlùn).
- T1782 *Shuō wúgòuchēng jīng shū* by Kuījī 窺基 (Cí'ēn / Yogācāra).

**Dunhuang Vimalakīrti commentary cluster** (KR6i0086–0098): mostly anonymous fragmentary commentaries (T2768–T2778) from the Hexi corridor, including the *Guānzhōng jíjiě* by Dào Yè 道掖.

**Recovered-from-Japan major commentaries** (KR6i0099–0102): the original Zhīyǐ 28-fascicle *Wéimó jīng wén shū* (X0338) with Zhànrán's *Shū jì* (X0340), Jízàng's *Lüè shū* (X0343), and Dào Xiān's *Sī jì* (X0345).

**Late-Ming literary commentaries** (KR6i0103–0105): Yáng Qǐyuán 楊起元's literary *Píng zhù* (X0347), Yúyǒu Chuándēng 傳燈's *Wéimó wúwǒ shū* (X0348), and an anonymous outline (X0349).

## Cluster 4: Bodhisattva-Question Sūtras (KR6i0106–0117)

A diverse cluster of "*púsà suǒwèn jīng*" 菩薩所問經 sūtras featuring various bodhisattva interlocutors:

- **KR6i0106–0108** — The *Mūrdharāja* (Crowned-King) sūtra family: Dharmarakṣa, Yuèpó Shǒunà 月婆首那, Jñānagupta — featuring Vimalakīrti's son Dǐngwáng / Shànsī tóngzǐ.
- **KR6i0109** — *Yuèshàngnǚ jīng* (T480) by Jñānagupta: the Candrottarā-dārikā wise-girl sūtra.
- **KR6i0110–0111** — Two parallel translations of the *Lokadhara-paripṛcchā* (T481, T482).
- **KR6i0112** — *Sānmàntuó báduóluó púsà jīng* (T483): early Chinese Samantabhadra sūtra by Niè Dàozhēn.
- **KR6i0113–0117** — Five short bodhisattva-question texts spanning Kumārajīva (T484), Jñānagupta (T485), Nàtí (T486, T487), and the Sòng monk Fǎxián (T488).

## Cluster 5: Disciple Cycle — Ānanda, Mahākāśyapa, and Bodhisattva Groups (KR6i0118–0135)

Texts on the Buddha's principal disciples:

- **KR6i0118** — Twenty-fascicle *Sarvanīvaraṇa-viṣkambhī-paripṛcchā* (T489) by Sòng Fǎhù 法護.
- **KR6i0119–0120** — Texts on the eight and six great bodhisattvas (T490, T491).
- **KR6i0121–0125** — The Ānanda cycle: T492a-b (Ān Shìgāo's "auspicious matters"), T493 (Zhī Qiān's "four matters"), T494 (Tánwúlán's "seven dreams"), T495 (Fǎjiān's "discrimination").
- **KR6i0126–0128** — The Mahākāśyapa cycle: T496 (Dharmarakṣa, *jātaka*), T497 (Guṇabhadra, alms-from-poor-woman), T498 (Sòng Shīhù, *Prathamabhāga*).
- **KR6i0129** — Anonymous Pāli-parallel *Acela Kassapa* sūtra (T499).
- **KR6i0130–0135** — Short monastic-conduct sūtras: T500 (Fǎjù, Rāhula's patience), T501–T505 (various translators), all concerning the conduct of *bhikṣu*s.

## Cluster 6: The Kings Cycle (KR6i0136–0154)

Nineteen short narratives on Indic kings and their relations to the Buddha:

- **KR6i0136** — *Jiāntuó guówáng jīng* (T506) by Ān Shìgāo: the earliest royal narrative.
- **KR6i0137–0139** — The Ajātaśatru cycle: Zhī Qiān's *Wèishēngyuàn* (T507), Fǎjù's *wǔnì-questions* (T508), Fǎjù's *vyākaraṇa* (T509).
- **KR6i0140** — *Cǎihuā wéi wáng shàngfó* (T510): the flower-picker's prophecy by Tánwúlán.
- **KR6i0141** — *Píngshā wáng wǔyuàn* (T511) by Zhī Qiān: King Bimbisāra's five vows.
- **KR6i0142** — *Jìngfàn wáng bān nièpán* (T512) by Jǔqú Jīngshēng 沮渠京聲: King Śuddhodana's parinirvāṇa, foundational for Buddhist filial-piety arguments.
- **KR6i0143** — *Liúlí wáng* (T513) by Dharmarakṣa: King Vaidūrya's massacre of the Śākyas.
- **KR6i0144–0149** — Six royal narratives all by Jǔqú Jīngshēng (T514, T517–T519): admonitions, kings of the Mallas, Caṇḍrāvatī, Madra, etc.
- **KR6i0145–0146** — Prasenajit cycle: Xuánzàng's T515, Shīhù's T516 (parallel translations).
- **KR6i0150–0153** — Anonymous (*shīyì*) royal narratives (T520–T523): the *Sāla* kingdom, *Brahmadatta*, *Pǔdá*, the Five Kings.
- **KR6i0154** — Amoghavajra's *Yōutián wáng zhènglùn* (T524): the major Tang Buddhist political-theory treatise.

## Cluster 7: Elder's-Son and Children Sūtras (KR6i0155–0181)

The "*zhǎngzhě zǐ*" cycle:

- **KR6i0155–0156** — Ān Shìgāo's *zhǎngzhě zǐ* texts (T525, T526).
- **KR6i0157–0158** — Two parallel translations of the *Shì tóngzǐ* (Boy Shì) sūtra by Zhī Fǎdù and Bái Fǎzǔ.
- **KR6i0159** — Anonymous *Ājiūliú* (T529).
- **KR6i0160–0163** — Four Zhī Qiān sūtras on elders and bodhisattvas (T530, T531, T532, T533).
- **KR6i0164–0166** — The Candraprabha-kumāra (Yuèguāng tóngzǐ) cycle: Dharmarakṣa T534, T535; Guṇabhadra T536. Contains the famous prophecy that Candraprabha will be reborn as a king of *Zhīnà* (China).
- **KR6i0167** — Niè Chéngyuǎn's *Yuènán* sūtra (T537).
- **KR6i0168–0169** — Tánwúlán's *Hēdiāo* (T538) and anonymous *Lúzhì* (T539).
- **KR6i0170–0171** — Two recensions of Guṇabhadra's *Jyotiṣka* sūtra (T540a, T540b).
- **KR6i0172–0173** — Jǔqú Jīngshēng's *Fódà Sēngdà* (T541) and *Yéqí* (T542).
- **KR6i0174** — Sòng Zhìjíxiáng's three-fascicle *Jùlì zhǎngzhě* (T543).
- **KR6i0175** — Northern Wèi Fǎchǎng's *Biànyì zhǎngzhě zǐ* (T544).
- **KR6i0176** — Sui Narendrayaśas's two-fascicle *Déhù zhǎngzhě* (T545); contains the East-Asian-king prophecy.
- **KR6i0177–0181** — Sòng-period child-bodhisattva sūtras: Tiānxīzāi's *Jīnyào tóngzǐ* (T546), Prajñā-Lìyán's *Dà huāyán Nàluóyán* (T547), Fǎxián's *Jīnguāngwáng* (T548), Shīhù's four-fascicle *Guāngmíng tóngzǐ* (T549), Wéijìng's twelve-fascicle *Jīnsè tóngzǐ* (T550).

## Cluster 8: Women's Narratives (KR6i0182–0200)

The "women's sūtras" cycle:

- **KR6i0182–0183** — Two recensions of the Mātaṅga-girl narrative (T551, T552).
- **KR6i0184–0185** — Two recensions of the Āmrapālī-Jīvaka narrative (T553, T554).
- **KR6i0186–0187** — Two recensions of *Wǔmǔ zǐ* (T555a, T555b) by Zhī Qiān.
- **KR6i0188** — Zhī Qiān's *Qīnǚ jīng* (T556): seven women debating Buddhist doctrine.
- **KR6i0189–0190** — The Nāga-girl narrative: Zhī Qiān's *Lóngshī nǚ* (T557) and Dharmarakṣa's *Lóngshī púsà běnqǐ* (T558).
- **KR6i0191–0193** — The "old woman" cluster (T559, T560, T561) by Zhī Qiān, Guṇabhadra, and an anonymous translator.
- **KR6i0194** — Dharmarakṣa's *Wúgòu xiánnǚ* (T562).
- **KR6i0195** — Dharmakṣema's *Fùzhōng nǚ tīng* (T563): pre-natal bodhisattva.
- **KR6i0196** — Dharmamitra's *Zhuǎn nǚshēn jīng* (T564): the controversial doctrine of female-body transformation.
- **KR6i0197–0198** — Two parallel translations of the *Strīvivartavyākaraṇa* (T565, T566) by Dharmarakṣa and Dharmayaśas.
- **KR6i0199** — Dharmarakṣa's *Fànzhìnǚ Shǒuyì* (T567).
- **KR6i0200** — Bodhiruci II's *Yǒudé nǚ* (T568).

## Key Scholarly Issues Noted in This Batch

1. **Dating corrections and refinements**:
   - KR6i0051–0052: The proto-Yakuṣī text in the *Guàndǐng* (T1331/12) is securely dated to 457 CE per Sēngyòu, despite its inclusion in the Śrīmitra-attributed compilation.
   - KR6i0099, KR6i0100, KR6i0101: Tiantai and Sānlùn Vimalakīrti commentaries lost in China and recovered from Japanese transmission, showing the importance of inter-East-Asian textual circuits.

2. **Multi-translation traditions**: Several texts have three or more Chinese translations spanning centuries, providing rich material for translation-philology:
   - The *Vimalakīrti* (T474, T475, T476).
   - The *Gayāśīrṣa-sūtra* (T464, T465, T466, T467).
   - The *Strīvivartavyākaraṇa* (T565, T566).
   - The *Mūrdharāja* (T477, T478, T479).
   - The Candraprabha-Sundaya cycle (T534, T535, T536).

3. **East Asian Buddhist political ideology**: Three texts contain prophecies that figure prominently in Chinese imperial Buddhist legitimation: KR6i0164 (Yuèguāng tóngzǐ as future Chinese king), KR6i0176 (Déhù zhǎngzhě's son as eastern king), and KR6i0154 (Amoghavajra's *Wángfǎ zhènglùn*, the major Tang political-theory text).

4. **Female bodhisattva tradition**: Eighteen texts in the women's cycle (KR6i0182–0200) preserve the Mahāyāna canonical engagement with women's bodhisattva-capacity. Texts split between those that present transformation requirements (T564) and those that reject them (T556, T557, T558, T568) — a long-running debate.

5. **Pre-Sui anonymous translations**: A substantial proportion of texts (about 12 in this batch) are catalogued as *shīyì* 失譯 (translator unknown) and date broadly to the pre-Sui period (200–600 CE). These represent the unattributed substrate of the Chinese Buddhist canon.

6. **Sòng *Yìjīng yuàn* corpus**: About 15 texts in this batch are products of the Sòng imperial translation institute (982–1054 CE) under Tiānxīzāi/Fǎxián, Shīhù, Fǎhù, Wéijìng, and Zhìjíxiáng. This concentration testifies to the breadth of late-period Indic material that was transmitted to Sòng China before the Indian Buddhist tradition's decline.

7. **Skipped entry**: KR6i0086 (a placeholder *Wéimó jīng yìshū* with no source files) is logged in TODO.md.
