# Bā jíxiáng jīng 八吉祥經
*Sūtra of the Eight Auspicious Ones*
by [[僧伽婆羅]] (Saṅghapāla, 譯)

## About the work

The *Bā jíxiáng jīng* is a brief single-fascicle sūtra enumerating the eight directional auspicious Buddhas (*bā jíxiáng* 八吉祥), belonging to the T427–T431 cluster of closely related parallel translations. It is distinguished from the others in the group by its relatively clean and idiomatic Chinese style, reflecting the quality of Saṅghapāla's Liang-dynasty translations. The translator colophon reads: 梁扶南三藏僧伽婆羅譯 ("Translated by the Funan Tripiṭaka master Saṅghapāla of the Liang"). Saṅghapāla was a monk of Funan (modern Cambodia/Vietnam) origin who worked in the Liang capital Jiànkāng (建康, modern Nanjing).

### Prefaces

No preface survives in the received text. The body begins: 如是我聞... The Taishō groups T430 with T427–T429 and T431 as parallel translations of the same Indic original.

### Abstract

Saṅghapāla 僧伽婆羅 ([[僧伽婆羅]]; ca. 460–524 CE), the Funan-born monk who came to Jiànkāng and worked under imperial patronage of the Liang Wudi (梁武帝, r. 502–549), is known primarily for his translations of Śrāvakayāna sūtras and *āranyaka*-type texts, as well as his rendering of the *Āryamaṅjuśrīmūlakalpa*-related materials. His version of the *Bā jíxiáng jīng* (T430) is the Liang representative of the five-translation series. The term *jíxiáng* 吉祥 corresponds to the Sanskrit *maṅgala* and resonates with indigenous Chinese concepts of auspiciousness, which likely contributed to the repeated translation of this text. The approximate date of Saṅghapāla's translations is ca. 506–520 CE, during his active period at Jiànkāng.
