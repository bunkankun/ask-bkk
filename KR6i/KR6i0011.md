# Fóshuō miè shífāng míng jīng 佛說滅十方冥經
*Sūtra of Extinguishing the Darkness of the Ten Directions*
by [[竺法護]] (Dharmarakṣa, 譯)

## About the work

The *Miè shífāng míng jīng* is a brief single-fascicle Mahāyāna sūtra in which the Buddha illuminates the darkness of the ten directions and teaches a bodhisattva assembly about the qualities of the Tathāgata and the path to liberation. The title — "extinguishing the darkness of the ten directions" (*miè shífāng míng* 滅十方冥) — emphasizes the Buddha's radiant wisdom dispelling ignorance throughout the cosmos. The translator colophon reads: 西晉月氏國三藏竺法護譯 ("Translated by the Yuèzhī Tripiṭaka master Dharmarakṣa of the Western Jin"). The text is one of the many short Mahāyāna sūtras Dharmarakṣa ([[竺法護]]) produced during the Western Jin period.

### Abstract

This brief sūtra belongs to the cluster of early Mahāyāna texts translated by Dharmarakṣa (ca. 239–316 CE) that emphasize the luminous, all-pervading wisdom of the Buddha and the methods by which bodhisattvas can emulate it. The "ten directions" (*shífāng* 十方) framework is characteristic of Mahāyāna spatialization of the Buddha's field of activity. The *CSZJJ* records this text among Dharmarakṣa's translations; its exact date within his active period (265–313 CE) is not specified.
